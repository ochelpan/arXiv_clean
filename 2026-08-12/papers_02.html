Summary. This paper analyzes how monitoring an open quantum oscillator affects its escape time statistics, comparing quantum predictions against classical expectations. By using both density matrix and trajectory methods, the authors show that while measurement protocols can generate nonclassical states, the deviation from classical behavior diminishes as the energy threshold increases. Main problem. The study investigates the first-passage time statistics for open quantum systems undergoing continuous monitoring, focusing on the crossover between quantum and classical behavior.

Main result. The analysis provides a framework to generate nonclassical states via measurement protocols.

Method. The authors employ both ensemble-averaged density matrix dynamics and trajectory-resolved stochastic pure-state dynamics to model the system evolution under repeated projective measurements.

Model / system. The physical model is a harmonic oscillator driven by classical additive noise, which serves as an open quantum system. The dynamics are governed by master equations incorporating stroboscopic projective measurements at intervals $\theta$.

Key observables. First-passage time distribution (FPTD), nonclassical signatures like persistent Wigner negativity, and the Kullback–Leibler divergence quantifying the quantum-classical difference.

Important parameters / regimes. The measurement threshold energy ($E_B$ or $N_B$), the measurement interval ($\theta$), and the initial state energy level ($N_0$).

Paper structure. Abstract

The quantum first-passage problem involves stochastic trajectories conditioned on measurement outcomes. The timing statistics of such trajectories remain largely unexplored in open quantum systems. Here, we investigate the first-passage time to an energy threshold for a ubiquitous model: a harmonic oscillator driven by classical additive noise. We find that projective measurements and energy quantization lead to substantial differences between the quantum and classical first-passage-time distributions at low thresholds, while these differences gradually diminish as the threshold energy increases. We treat the problem using both ensemble-averaged conditioned density-matrix dynamics and trajectory-resolved stochastic pure-state dynamics. The two descriptions yield indistinguishable timing statistics. Quantization effects appear in the ensemble-level phase-space distributions of the surviving states and vanish at larger threshold energies. Individual trajectories reveal emergent quantum signatures from the repeated measurements, such as persistent Wigner negativity. Our results provide a framework for using first-passage processes to create measurement-induced nonclassical resource states and to study the quantum-classical crossover of monitored systems.

Abstract

Physical conservation laws, such as those of energy and momentum, are generally believed to hold exactly and locally in spacetime, including in quantum phenomena. Yet Aharonov, Popescu, and Rohrlich (APR) recently argued, on the basis of a thought experiment, that individual quantum events, unlike ensemble averages, may occasionally violate local conservation laws. Their argument relies on the wave phenomenon known as "superoscillations", which APR themselves discovered more than 30 years ago. Here we provide experimental evidence for such a violation. We extract photons from a small superoscillatory region near the core of an optical vortex and show that their mean transverse momentum is statistically incompatible with a general bound implied by local momentum conservation. The derivation of this bound requires only the theoretically well-supported assumption that the extraction mechanism does not alter the photons' mean transverse momentum. We also detect photons with high transverse momentum at a rate significantly exceeding that predicted by a model assuming local momentum conservation. Because this violation can be established only counterfactually and through postselection, it does not conflict with relativistic causality. Our results may represent the first example of a distinct form of quantum nonlocality that does not explicitly rely on entanglement.

Summary. This theoretical work quantifies how measuring a collective property, such as the trajectory of one particle, constrains the entire system's underlying dynamics near a glass transition. It proves that this constraint introduces a predictable baseline suppression (the Schur shield) to fluctuations. Therefore, any measured deviation in cooperative behavior must be interpreted relative to this calculated baseline, providing a rigorous method for extracting true collective signals.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical framework connecting path conditioning (a concept relevant to quantum measurement theory) directly to measurable thermodynamic quantities like susceptibility provides a powerful tool for interpreting complex many-body dynamics observed in experiments.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how conditioning on a shared, collective observation (like a tagged particle's trajectory) modifies the underlying dynamics and fluctuations of a system approaching a glass transition.

Main result. Conditioning introduces a calculable, nonpositive baseline correction (the Schur shield) to the measured four-point susceptibility ($\chi_4$), meaning the true cooperative signal must be measured as an excess over this baseline rather than relative to zero.

Method. The analysis employs Girsanov path transformations and stochastic process theory applied to a guiding Brownian model, allowing for exact computation of conditional joint laws.

Model / system. The system models a liquid near its glass transition, characterized by heterogeneous dynamics. The core mechanism involves the influence of $z$ neighboring particles (the 'cage') on a single tagged particle whose motion generates the shared observation record.

Key observables. Four-point susceptibility ($\chi_4$), covariance operators ($C$, $D$), and the Schur shield ($D - C$).

Important parameters / regimes. The number of neighbors, $z$; the time window $[0, T]$; and the relative magnitude of local pair correlations versus collective sums.

Assumptions / limitations. The results are nonasymptotic and dimension-free at a fixed observation window; extending to bulk $\chi_4$ across different tagged stars is noted as an open problem.

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically illustrates how one shared output constrains the population, represented by a translucent shield compressing the source-visible collective direction. Figure 2 details how eigenmode variances split upon conditioning.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theory using Girsanov transformations to derive the penalty term and the Schur shield for the conditional law. It then analyzes pair correlations and compares local vs. collective observer sums, culminating in a physical interpretation of $\chi_4$ as an excess over the calculated baseline.

Abstract

As a liquid approaches its glass transition, its dynamics turns heterogeneous: mobile and immobile regions coexist, and the four-point susceptibility $χ_4$ that quantifies this heterogeneity grows sharply. Interpreting that growth is subtle, because the collective signals experiments record, such as a tagged particle's trajectory, an overlap function, or a mean field, are generated by the same particles they describe. Here we compute exactly what conditioning on such a shared record does to the population that produced it, for a broad class of stochastically observed systems; the guiding example is a tagged particle and the cage of $z$ neighbors that drives its force history. Using a Girsanov path transformation, we prove that the conditioning multiplies the independent joint law of the $z$ trajectories by exactly one term: a centered-square penalty along the single collective direction the record can see. Any fixed pair of particles stays nearly independent, with covariance falling as $O(z^{-1})$ and mutual information as $O(z^{-2})$, the property known as propagation of chaos, yet the $z(z-1)$ weak pair correlations add coherently into a finite suppression of collective fluctuations, the Schur shield $D - C = -C^2(aI + C)^{-1} \preceq 0$. An exactly solvable Brownian model calibrates the construction. The physical consequence is a calculable baseline for dynamical heterogeneity: conditioning itself contributes a computable, nonpositive amount to the susceptibility of a conditioned ensemble, so the genuine cooperative signal is the excess of the measured $χ_4$ over this baseline rather than over zero, a comparison that existing simulation data can already perform.

Abstract

Quantum circuits with measurements and unitary feedback (MF) can prepare long-range entangled states in constant depth, but a systematic construction of the MF preparation circuit for a given target state remains underexplored. We develop such a scheme for one-dimensional states, based on the notion of pushable defects: virtual-bond operators of a matrix product state that can be pushed through the tensor at the price of a physical feedback unitary. We show that the set of pushable defects, together with their pushing relations classifies finite-depth MF-preparable states and dictates their preparation circuits. To each class of the target state $|A\rangle$, we associate a state $|B\rangle$ from which $|A\rangle$ can be prepared using a 1-round MF circuit; in particular, $|A\rangle$ is preparable from a product state using a circuit with 1 round of MF whenever $|B\rangle$ is preparable by a finite-depth local unitary (FDLU) circuit. For a general target state, the scheme is obtained by iterating this procedure until the associated state is FDLU-preparable. For open-boundary matrix product states, the scheme is complete: it constructs a preparation circuit whenever finite-depth MF preparation with left-conditioned feedback corrections is possible. Pushable defects and pushing relations thus emerge as a unifying principle for quantum state preparation via measurements and feedback. This characterization further reveals an intrinsic connection between MF circuits and non-invertible symmetries: states with certain classes of pushing relations are related to a product state by Tambara-Yamagami duality operators, or by continuous cosine symmetry operators with fusion rules $L_α L_{α'} = L_{α+α'} + L_{α-α'}$, together with their generalizations up to (not necessarily transversal) gates.
