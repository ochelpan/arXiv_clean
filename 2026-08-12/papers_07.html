Summary. This paper presents a finite-key security proof for Quantum Key Distribution against information leakage from imperfect physical receivers. By using phase-error estimation, the authors quantify how side-channel emissions compromise security in both post-measurement and basis-choice leakage scenarios. This provides a vital tool for making QKD protocols robust enough for real-world implementation.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses practical limitations in quantum communication hardware (leaky detectors), providing rigorous security proofs that are crucial for deploying QKD systems outside idealized laboratory settings.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To develop a simple, finite-key security proof for Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) that remains secure even when the physical receivers leak information about measurement outcomes or basis choices to an eavesdropper.

Main result. The authors provide a loss-tolerant security proof based on phase-error estimation, showing how leakage introduces only quantifiable penalties to the key rate for both post-measurement and a priori basis leakage scenarios.

Method. The analysis establishes equivalence between the actual leaky protocol and a virtual scenario, allowing the use of phase-error estimation techniques derived from bounding the distinguishability (fidelity) of leaked side-channel states.

Model / system. The study focuses on prepare-and-measure (P&M) QKD schemes, modeling information leakage via isometries acting on the received quantum state. The analysis uses concepts like phase-error operators and fidelity bounds derived from the leaky receiver's side channels.

Key observables. Phase-error rate ($\delta_\gamma$), X-basis error rate, and the key secret length $\ell$.

Important parameters / regimes. Leakage overlap parameter ($\gamma$), detection probability ($p_{ ext{det}}$), and correlation length ($L_c$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that leakage states can be bounded by fidelity constraints, and it models the system using an isometry framework to relate actual measurements to virtual ones.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the modeling of information leakage across three scenarios (actual, fictitious, virtual). Figure 2 compares the secret-key rate vs. distance for post-measurement versus a priori basis choice leakage.

Paper structure. The paper develops the security proof by first establishing the mathematical equivalence between the physical protocol and an idealized virtual one. It then derives specific bounds relating the phase error to the X-basis error, accounting separately for post-measurement and a priori basis leakage mechanisms.

Abstract

Practical quantum key distribution (QKD) receivers may leak information about their measurement outcomes and settings to the channel (e.g., through detector backflashes or back-reflected Trojan-horse light) that could compromise the protocol's security. Here we present a simple finite-key security proof based on phase-error estimation for prepare-and-measure QKD in the presence of either a priori information leakage about the basis choices and/or a posteriori information leakage about the measurement outcomes. The proof requires only a bound on the distinguishability of the side-channel states. Furthermore, the analysis is modular and compatible with existing security proofs that address detector and source imperfections, making it applicable to a wide range of practical QKD implementations.

Summary. This paper generalizes Hopfield networks by placing neurons on the $ ext{SU}(d)$ manifold ($ ext{CP}^{d-1}$), achieving a significant boost in memory capacity over standard models. It employs advanced techniques, including Lie algebra and replica analysis, to show that recall is governed by alignment along top eigenvectors. The results are validated using physical dynamics (LLG) and suggest connections to quantum glassy systems.

Why it may be interesting. This work bridges concepts from quantum information (SU(d) representations, complex structures) with condensed matter physics (spin glasses, disordered systems) via neural network modeling. The connection between geometric constraints and enhanced capacity is highly relevant for understanding physical implementations of memory storage.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To develop generalized Hopfield networks using continuous variables on Riemannian manifolds to significantly enhance memory storage capacity beyond traditional vector models.

Main result. The use of $ ext{SU}(d)$ symmetry leads to a strong, order-of-magnitude enhancement in critical capacity ($\alpha_c$) compared to standard spherical networks. Furthermore, physical dynamics (LLG) successfully reproduce these high capacities.

Method. The analysis combines replica techniques with Lie algebraic methods to linearize the geometric constraints of the manifold. Retrieval is analyzed using energy minimization principles derived from generalized Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert dynamics.

Model / system. The model is a Hopfield network where neurons and memories reside on symmetric spaces, specifically $ ext{SU}(d)$ associated with the manifold $ ext{CP}^{d-1}$. The system's energy functional governs memory storage and retrieval.

Key observables. Critical capacity ($\alpha_c$), Memory Overlap ($O_\mu$), and the success rate of image recovery during dynamics.

Important parameters / regimes. The dimension $d$ of the special unitary group, which dictates the manifold structure; the load parameter $\alpha$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on mapping the non-linear geometry onto an auxiliary Hilbert space using Lie algebra techniques. The LLG dynamics are assumed to govern physical recall.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate image encoding/decoding protocols for SU(3) and show capacity scans demonstrating high $\alpha_c$ values, alongside visualizations of winner-takes-it-all behavior during retrieval.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the generalized network on Riemannian manifolds, derives the energy functional using Lie algebra, analyzes the enhanced capacity via replica methods, demonstrates physical recall using LLG dynamics (including image examples), and concludes by quantizing the system to relate it to Sachdev-Ye glassy models.

Abstract

Generalized Hopfield networks are introduced where memories and neurons are continuous variables that lie on a Riemannian manifold. We explicitly focus on symmetric spaces associated with the special unitary groups SU(d), and use both numerical and analytical (replica) techniques to demonstrate an almost order of magnitude enhancement in critical capacity over the vector networks starting with d=3 and further rapidly growing with d. To circumvent the non-linear geometric constraints, we use a Lie algebraic method [following V. Galitski, Phys. Rev. A 84, 012118 (2011)] to exactly describe the classical neural network in terms of linear algebra in an auxiliary Hilbert space. It is shown that in contrast to the traditional Hopfield networks, memory recall in SU(d) Hopfields corresponds to neuron alignment along a top eigenvector of a spiked matrix, which is less susceptible to random matrix crosstalk than other models with continuous neuron variables. Physical platforms to realize SU(d) Hopfields are briefly discussed and physical (in addition to algorithmic) recall mechanism is demonstrated, where memory recovery occurs naturally through generalized Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert dynamics. To illustrate SU(3) memory recall, we introduce a color (RGB) image encoding/decoding protocol and explicitly run image recovery on corrupted cues. Finally, we quantize the generalized Hopfields which are shown to reduce to Sachdev-Ye glassy type of models. Their many-body spectra generally feature two types of dark and memory bands, where the latter exhibits chaotic Wigner-Dyson level statistics that hides Hebbian data.

Summary. Main result. The authors develop an ensemble-based loopy message-passing framework that accurately predicts percolation thresholds by incorporating loop correlations, validated by a diagnostic coefficient (GECC).

Method. They derive and utilize a progressively refined, high-order message-passing recursion based on generalized-edge statistics to account for arbitrary connectivity patterns in random graph ensembles.

Model / system. The study focuses on percolation theory applied to random clustered graph ensembles (e.g., P2P, Facebook networks). The framework models network connectivity using 'generalized edges' that characterize overlap patterns among node neighborhoods.

Key observables. Percolation threshold ($q_c$), Generalized Edge Closure Coefficient (GECC), and the conditional finite-cluster-size distribution ($ ilde{\pi}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The order of approximation ($n$, up to $n=2$ is detailed) and structural features like degree classes, distance, and shared neighbors.

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes that the complex connectivity can be systematically approximated by progressively including higher-order neighborhood statistics (generalized edges).

It then reduces this high-dimensional theory to a tractable, second-order approximation (Eq. 9), culminating in testing its validity using real-world networks and introducing GECC as the primary diagnostic tool.

Abstract

Predicting the percolation threshold of highly clustered networks from local statistics remains difficult, because short loops break the independence assumption underlying tree-like message passing. Existing remedies address loopy connectivity either through prescribed local motifs in random-graph ensembles or through a single network's realized topology, leaving an ensemble-level treatment of arbitrary connectivity patterns absent. Here, we develop a loopy message-passing framework for random clustered graph ensembles based on generalized-edge statistics, which characterize overlap patterns among the neighborhoods of different nodes. This yields a progressively refined approximation scheme based on neighborhoods of increasing size around each node. The low-order approximations recover previous equations for random network ensembles, and the new result that yields refined threshold prediction is developed by the second-order approximation. We show that the effectiveness of this framework depends not only on short-cycle density but also on the internal consistency of generalized edges. To diagnose this effectiveness, we introduce the generalized-edge closure coefficient (GECC) to quantify this consistency. Because GECC is computed entirely from local statistics and does not rely on any percolation calculation, it serves as an a priori diagnostic for the reliability of the approximation. Using synthetic and real networks, the threshold is evaluated via the second-order and lower-order approximations. Comparisons with Monte Carlo simulations show that GECC captures key structural features that strongly affect the percolation threshold. These results establish ensemble-based loopy message passing as an efficient route for predicting the percolation threshold in large clustered networks.

Abstract

Quantum computing is a promising way to study problems that are difficult for classical methods, but current quantum hardware still faces limits in scale, noise, and fidelity. Running quantum algorithms on physical machines can also be costly. Quantum circuit simulators therefore remain important because they let researchers design and test algorithms on classical computers before using quantum hardware. Most high-performance simulators provide GPU backends, while few offer native support for NPUs. This gap limits the computing platforms available for quantum-algorithm research. We developed Aicir to provide a full-stack quantum circuit simulator with a native Huawei Ascend NPU backend. Aicir connects circuit construction, several state representations, measurement, differentiation, variational algorithms, quantum machine learning, and quantum architecture search through one programming model. It also supports noise simulation, tensor-network and matrix-product-state engines, and distributed state simulation. On the NPU, paired real tensors, fixed-rank gate views, and hardware-specific formulas keep the tested simulation paths on the device. The same representation lets Aicir partition a state across $2^{p}$ NPUs while retaining reverse-mode differentiation. We validated native execution with CPU fallback disabled and checked distributed communication and gradients on 2, 4, and 8 NPUs. For the tested fused layered circuits, Aicir's CPU runtime is within $0.97$--$1.28\times$ that of Qiskit Aer and $0.76$--$1.10\times$ that of Cirq. These results place its CPU execution in the same range as established simulators for this workload, while the NPU tests establish correct native execution rather than CPU-to-NPU speedup.

Summary. This paper introduces a novel Cubic-Equivariant Neural Density Functional Theory (DFT) designed to calculate properties of 3D lattice fluids. It trains a neural network on complete density profiles to approximate the one-body direct-correlation functional, enforcing cubic symmetry exactly. The results show that this neural approach significantly improves predictions for bulk thermodynamics and planar wall adsorption compared to existing methods.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical lattice fluids, the use of equivariant neural networks to enforce fundamental symmetries in a many-body functional is highly relevant for developing machine learning potentials or force fields applicable to quantum systems where symmetry constraints are paramount.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To develop a computationally tractable method for calculating equilibrium structure and thermodynamics by approximating the excess free-energy functional within Density Functional Theory (DFT).

Main result. The neural functional significantly improves the accuracy for the homogeneous equation of state and the density profile at a planar hard wall compared to existing methods.

Method. A fully convolutional neural network is trained to learn the one-body direct-correlation functional $c^{(1)}[ ho]$ directly from complete 3D density profiles, enforcing exact cubic equivariance via kernel projection.

Model / system. The study models three-dimensional lattice fluids using parallel hard cubes on a simple cubic lattice. The framework is based on classical DFT, which approximates the many-body problem variationally.

Key observables. Homogeneous equation of state, density profile at a planar hard wall, and anisotropic pair distribution around a fixed particle.

Important parameters / regimes. Packing fractions ($\eta$) ranging from $\approx 0.063$ to $0.6$; the cubic point group symmetry ($G=O_h$).

Assumptions / limitations. The method is restricted to enforcing only discrete cubic lattice symmetry, and obtaining smooth 3D histograms for reliable training targets remains a bottleneck for continuum fluids.

The paper introduces the DFT framework, details the construction of the cubic-equivariant neural functional using GCMC data for training, compares its predictions (equation of state, wall profiles) against established functionals (Lafuente-Cuesta), and discusses limitations regarding continuum extension.

Abstract

We construct a neural classical density functional that acts directly on unrestricted three-dimensional density profiles. As a computationally tractable test bed, we consider parallel hard cubes of side length three on a simple cubic lattice. A fully convolutional network learns the one-body direct-correlation functional $c^{(1)}[ρ]$ from data obtained with grand-canonical Monte Carlo simulations in randomized external potentials. Complete profiles are used during both training and inference; a stochastic Bernoulli mask on the output sites makes full-profile training effective without explicitly extracting and storing overlapping local density windows. Averaging the first-layer kernels over all 48 rotations and reflections of the cubic point group additionally imposes exact cubic equivariance without data augmentation. We compare the learned functional with independent simulation data and with the lattice fundamental-measure functional of Lafuente and Cuesta. The neural functional markedly improves the homogeneous equation of state and the density profile at a planar hard wall. For the anisotropic pair distribution around a fixed particle, both functionals reproduce the principal packing shells, with their relative accuracy depending on crystallographic direction. These results demonstrate neural density-functional calculations on complete three-dimensional profiles while also identifying accurate full-dimensional training data, thermodynamic consistency, and structural correlations as the central challenges for extensions to continuum fluids.

Summary. The authors introduce an advanced continuum model that accounts for lattice relaxation effects in moiré TMD heterobilayers. They demonstrate that these intrinsic structural deformations are necessary to generate robust topological bands characterized by non-zero Chern numbers. This establishes a powerful new theoretical tool linking fundamental material structure to exotic quantum electronic states.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous theoretical framework connecting first-principles DFT calculations to measurable many-body observables like topological gaps and superfluid stiffness in moiré systems, which is highly relevant for understanding emergent quantum phases.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To correct continuum models for moiré TMD heterobilayers by incorporating intrinsic lattice relaxation effects, which are crucial for accurately predicting their electronic topology.

Main result. The pseudomagnetic field alone is sufficient to induce topological gaps with Chern numbers $\pm 1$, and the inclusion of full relaxation enhances these gaps while preserving the topological nature across wide parameter regimes.

Method. Developing an extended continuum Hamiltonian that decomposes lattice relaxation into modified moiré potential, a pseudoelectric (scalar) potential, and a pseudomagnetic (vector) potential. Calculations utilize Fourier expansions and neural-network variational Monte Carlo methods.

Model / system. The system is Moiré transition metal dichalcogenide (TMD) heterobilayers (e.g., WSe2/WS2), modeled by an effective continuum Hamiltonian incorporating strain effects ($\hat{H}_\epsilon$). The analysis compares rigid vs. relaxed electronic structures.

Key observables. Chern numbers ($C_3, C_4$), charge gap ($\Delta_c$), superfluid stiffness ($ar{D}_s$), Berry curvature, and quantum metric.

Important parameters / regimes. Twist angle ($ heta$) and lattice mismatch ($\delta$); the relative strength of interaction energy ($E_C$) to bandwidth ($W_1$).

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first identifying the failure of rigid models, then developing the three-channel continuum Hamiltonian, demonstrating that the pseudomagnetic field drives topology, and finally confirming these results using many-body calculations and comparing different stacking symmetries (3R vs. 2H).

Abstract

Moiré transition metal dichalcogenide (TMD) heterobilayers are commonly modeled by a continuum theory that yields topologically trivial bands, in contrast to their homobilayer counterparts which host topological bands and fractional Chern insulators (FCI). We show this conclusion is an artifact of neglecting the pseudomagnetic field generated by lattice relaxation, an effect intrinsic to every moiré material. We develop a continuum model that resolves relaxation into three channels: a modified moiré potential with higher Fourier harmonics, a pseudoelectric (scalar deformation) potential, and a pseudomagnetic (vector) potential. Using WSe$_2$/WS$_2$ as a prototype, we find that the pseudomagnetic field alone gaps the third and fourth valence bands with Chern numbers $\pm 1$ over a broad range of twist angle and lattice mismatch, while the moiré potential correction and pseudoelectric potential narrow the bandwidth and enhance the bandgaps, which survive many-body interactions using neural-network variational Monte Carlo calculations. Relaxation also smoothens the Berry curvature and quantum metric relative to the rigid model, moving the band closer to the ideal Chern limit, beneficial for the quantum anomalous Hall effect, FCI states, and flat-band superconductivity when filled to higher bands. Our work establishes a new framework that connects first-principles calculations, through the continuum model, to many-body observables. Using this framework, we show moiré heterobilayers as a new class of topological materials whose topology is driven entirely by intrinsic lattice relaxation.

Abstract

Controlling the ordered phases of correlated electron systems remains a central challenge in quantum materials design. Divergences in the electronic density of states, known as Van Hove singularities (VHSs), are one obvious route to such control. It is clear from recent work that the exact functional form of these divergences can profoundly affect which phases are realized; a full picture, however, remains elusive. In this work, we use both the hot-spot parquet renormalization group and the truncated-unity functional renormalization group to theoretically study the emergent correlated states of a two-dimensional square-lattice Hubbard model with VHSs at or near the Fermi level. By varying a single hopping parameter, $t_3$, we are able to change the strength of the VHS divergence in the density of states from logarithmic (for $t_3 < t_{3c}$) to power-law (for $t_3 = t_{3c}$). Further increase of $t_3$ ($t_3 > t_{3c}$) causes each original Van Hove point to split into two, both of the conventional logarithmic type. We show that which of these regimes we are in strongly influences the predicted ordered states. We also study the dependence on doping, and find that the ferromagnetic state that occurs at Van Hove filling in these models is unstable to very small shifts in the Fermi level, often giving way to distinct ordered states depending on whether the model is electron- or hole-doped. These results highlight the importance of tuning VHS properties to control ordered states in correlated materials, and offer design rules to engineer these phases in novel systems.

Abstract

Coordination is a fundamental primitive in communication and information theory, in which distributed systems must collectively generate correlated behavior rather than merely exchange messages. In quantum networks, the nature of entanglement, quantum measurements, and nonclassical correlations introduces coordination possibilities unavailable classically. This article reviews recent advances in coordination over classical and quantum communication networks, focusing on empirical and strong coordination in multi-user settings. The review highlights connections between coordination theory and applications such as distributed quantum systems, quantum internet architectures, and future communication networks.

Abstract

We study a scale-invariant, dipole-conserving two-parameter family of fracton Hamiltonians $H_{α,β}$.
