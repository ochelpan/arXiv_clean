Exchange-induced suppression of superconductivity in a nano-skyrmion lattice - superconductor hybrid

Authors: Dongfei Wang, Wenbin Li, Eric Mascot, Roland Wiesendanger
Type: both · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.09207v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5 · driven-dissipative phase transition 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper reports on the suppression of superconductivity when a nano-skyrmion lattice is coupled to a superconductor in an Fe/Ir(111)/Nb(110) hybrid. Using STS and tight-binding theory, the authors show that strong local exchange coupling within the skyrmions quenches the superconducting gap entirely. This provides critical microscopic constraints for engineering topological superconductivity using magnetic nanostructures.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses how engineered magnetic textures can fundamentally alter superconducting pairing symmetries and magnitudes, which is crucial for designing novel topological superconductors relevant to quantum computing architectures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how coupling non-collinear spin textures, specifically nano-skyrmion lattices, affects the superconducting state in magnet-superconductor hybrid systems.

Main result. The research demonstrates that increasing exchange coupling within the skyrmion lattice leads to a progressive and complete quenching of superconductivity, which contrasts with some prior theoretical expectations.

Method. The work combines experimental measurements using Scanning Tunneling Spectroscopy (STS) on fabricated heterostructures with theoretical predictions derived from tight-binding Bogoliubov-de Gennes (BdG) calculations.

Model / system. The physical system is a hybrid structure: Fe islands forming a nano-skyrmion lattice deposited on an Ir(111) film, which itself sits atop an Nb(110) superconducting substrate. The theoretical model incorporates hopping, pairing potential, and strong onsite s-d exchange coupling.

Key observables. Superconducting gap size ($\Delta$), local density of states (LDOS) ratio at zero bias vs. coherence peaks, and the spatial mapping of superconductivity suppression across the skyrmion lattice.

Important parameters / regimes. Exchange coupling strength ($J$), superconducting gap energy ($\Delta \approx 1.2-1.5$ meV), and spin-orbit coupling ($\alpha$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the observed quenching is due to strong local exchange coupling rather than simple Zeeman splitting, and theoretical parameters are representative rather than a direct fit.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate sample preparation (Fe/Ir/Nb), visualization of the skyrmion lattice via spin-polarized STM, spatially resolved STS maps showing gap closing across the Fe island, and theoretical DOS calculations tracking gap filling with increasing exchange coupling.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from characterizing the superconducting substrate and the magnetic structure to performing quantitative spectroscopy (STS) that reveals local gap suppression. This experimental evidence is then rigorously supported by tight-binding calculations showing spectral weight transfer leading to quenching as a function of exchange coupling strength.

Abstract

Engineered magnet-superconductor hybrids have recently been identified as promising platforms for the investigation of topological superconductivity. Beyond ferro- and antiferromagnetic structures, coupling non-collinear spin textures, such as skyrmion lattices, to superconductors offers an exciting route for creating and manipulating unconventional superconducting states. In this work, by preparing monolayer Fe on Ir(111) thin films grown epitaxially on a Nb(110) surface, we realize a hybrid system of a nano-skyrmion lattice proximitized to a superconducting substrate. Scanning tunneling spectroscopy shows that superconductivity becomes suppressed by the Fe nano-skyrmion lattice, with both the superconducting gap and coherence peaks disappearing. Tight-binding calculations reveal that with increasing exchange coupling, the gap is progressively filled up and eventually superconductivity gets quenched. These results reveal microscopic constraints for designing topologically non-trivial states based on magnet-superconducting heterostructures.

Long-range exchange interaction controls the fine structure of excited trion states in semiconductor quantum dots

Authors: M. M. Glazov, E. L. Ivchenko
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.09554v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: interference shaping light 1/5 · quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops a microscopic theory detailing how the long-range electron-hole exchange interaction dictates the fine structure of excited trion states in semiconductor quantum dots. By deriving an effective Hamiltonian and applying it to specific band models, the authors predict anisotropic splittings that can be measured using polarization-resolved optical spectroscopy.

Why it may be interesting. This work is highly relevant for quantum optics and open quantum systems as it provides detailed theoretical predictions linking fundamental many-body interactions (exchange) to measurable spectroscopic signatures (fine structure splitting) in solid-state emitters.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study develops a microscopic theory to explain the fine structure observed in excited trion states confined within semiconductor quantum dots.

Main result. The long-range exchange interaction (LRE) is shown to be responsible for lifting degeneracies and creating rich, anisotropic fine structures in these excited trion states. This provides a unified theoretical framework for interpreting polarization-resolved optical spectroscopy.

Method. The authors derive an effective long-range exchange Hamiltonian using second quantization formalism applied to two different band structure models (simple $\Gamma_6 imes \Gamma_7$ and heavy-hole).

Model / system. The system is negatively charged trions ($ ext{X}^-$) confined in semiconductor quantum dots, analyzed under the strong confinement regime. The theory considers the interplay between electron-electron and electron-hole exchange interactions.

Key observables. Fine structure splitting of excited trion energy levels; polarization-dependent optical spectra (e.g., $\sigma^+$ vs. $\sigma^-$); anisotropic splittings.

Important parameters / regimes. Long-range exchange parameters derived from the Hamiltonian; confinement potential anisotropy ($\sigma_x, \sigma_y, \sigma_z$ symmetry).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes strong confinement (size quantization $\gg$ Coulomb interaction $\gg$ fine structure splitting) and neglects spin-orbit coupling in some parts.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the effect of LRE interaction on both ground exciton and trion states in the heavy-hole model, demonstrating state mixing under anisotropic confinement.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by deriving the general LRE Hamiltonian from electrodynamics, applying it to specific two-band models (Model I and Model II), calculating the resulting energy shifts, and finally interpreting these results via polarization-resolved optical spectroscopy.

Abstract

We develop a microscopic theory of the long-range electron-hole exchange interaction in charged excitons (trions) confined in semiconductor quantum dots. While the ground-state singlet trion remains degenerate in the spin component of unpaired charge carrier by time-reversal symmetry, excited trion states exhibit a rich fine structure resulting from the interplay of electron-electron and electron-hole exchange interactions. We derive the effective long-range exchange Hamiltonian for both the spin-$3/2$ heavy-hole and a simple spin-$1/2$ valence band models. The long-range exchange interaction mixes singlet and triplet trion configurations, giving rise to anisotropic fine-structure splittings and related polarization-dependent optical spectra determined by the quantum-dot shape. Analytical expressions are obtained for the long-range exchange parameters. The developed theory establishes a unified microscopic description of the fine structure of excited trions in semiconductor quantum dots and provides a framework for interpreting polarization-resolved optical spectroscopy of charged excitonic complexes.

Magnetically induced Circular Photogalvanic Effect in Symmetric Two-dimensional Materials

Authors: Peng Liu, Fanhao Jia, Ruixue Li, Yuan Li, Igor Zutic, Gaofeng Xu
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.09309v1

Summary. This paper proposes the Magneto-Circular Photogalvanic Effect (MCPGE), showing that magnetic coupling can induce helicity-dependent photocurrents in materials lacking inversion symmetry. By modeling monolayer SbH on a magnetic substrate, the authors use advanced calculations involving Berry curvature and excitonic responses to prove this effect, opening new avenues for optoelectronic device design.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a clear mechanism to engineer optoelectronic response by controlling symmetry breaking using magnetism, which is highly relevant for designing novel quantum light-matter interaction devices.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is to generate a helicity-dependent photocurrent (CPGE) in materials that possess inversion symmetry, overcoming the typical requirement of broken inversion symmetry.

Main result. The authors demonstrate that applying magnetic proximity effects can break the necessary symmetries, leading to a measurable Magneto-Circular Photogalvanic Effect (MCPGE) in an otherwise symmetric material like monolayer SbH.

Method. The mechanism is elucidated using a second-order response formalism derived from an effective Hamiltonian incorporating exchange and Rashba SOC terms. Optical absorption is analyzed via the Bethe-Salpeter equation.

Model / system. The model system is a monolayer (ML) SbH placed on a magnetic substrate with adjustable magnetization, treated as a 2D material exhibiting proximity magnetism. The Hamiltonian includes bare band structure, exchange interaction ($H_{ ext{ex}}$), and Rashba SOC ($H_R$).

Key observables. Photocurrents dependent on light helicity ($eta_{ij}(\omega)$ tensor components), Berry curvature ($\Omega_v^z$), and excitonic absorption spectra ($\alpha_{\pm}(\omega)$).

Important parameters / regimes. Substrate magnetization direction ($\hat{n}$), in-plane magnetic field angle ($ heta$), and out-of-plane component ($\phi$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on a two-band model (lower CB/higher VB) for calculating the CPGE tensor, and uses approximations inherent in solving the Bethe-Salpeter equation.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate band structure shifts (isoenergy contours) under magnetic fields, the evolution of Berry curvatures ($\Omega_v^z$), and the dependence of the CPGE tensors on the angle ($ heta$) of the applied field.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the concept of MCPGE in symmetric materials. It then details the theoretical framework using an effective Hamiltonian for ML SbH on a magnetic substrate. The core analysis involves calculating photocurrents via Berry curvature and analyzing optical responses through BSE, culminating in demonstrating how magnetic asymmetry enables the effect.

Abstract

Photocurrents that depend on the helicity of the incident light can be generated in both bulk and low-dimensional materials lacking inversion symmetry, known as the circular photogalvanic effect (CPGE). We propose that by employing a magnetic effect, the limitation on the inversion symmetry broken materials can be overcome, such that helicity-dependent photocurrent can be generated in a symmetric material, i.e., a magneto-circular photogalvanic effect (MCPGE). As a proof of principle, we elucidate the mechanism of such an MCPGE through an effective Hamiltonian of a monolayer SbH on a magnetic substrate with an adjustable magnetization. Moreover, the associated response in optical absorption is analyzed, both single-particle and excitonic, through a Bethe-Salpeter equation to describe the Coulomb interaction in excitons. Our result broadens the mechanism of CPGE and opens new opportunities for optoelectronic devices.

Revealing Hidden Unconventional Pairing through Nonreciprocal Transport

Authors: Wen-Bo Dai, Ming Gong, Xianxin Wu, Chui-Zhen Chen, X. C. Xie
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.09751v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5 · quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. The paper introduces nonreciprocal transport as a novel, symmetry-selective method to determine unconventional superconducting pairing symmetries. By measuring how conductance changes upon reversing current flow, researchers can isolate signatures arising only from symmetry-breaking components of the order parameter. This technique is highly valuable for characterizing complex materials where conventional measurements are ambiguous.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, experimentally accessible diagnostic tool for characterizing complex superconducting pairing states without needing direct spectroscopic measurements of the gap structure itself.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is identifying the pairing symmetry of Cooper pairs in unconventional superconductors when the signal is obscured by a dominant s-wave component.

Main result. Nonreciprocal transport measurements provide a symmetry-resolved probe that can distinguish between different unconventional pairing symmetries (e.g., $s+id$ vs. spin-triplet) because pure s-wave states yield zero nonreciprocity.

Method. The authors develop a framework using the difference in conductance ($\Delta G$) when source and detector terminals are exchanged in multi-terminal devices, which is sensitive to symmetry breaking components of the superconducting order parameter.

Model / system. The study models superconductors (like iron-based or noncentrosymmetric materials) using an effective Hamiltonian incorporating both conventional s-wave pairing ($H_s$) and an unconventional component ($\lambda V_{un}$).

Key observables. Nonreciprocal charge conductance ($\Delta G_c$) and nonreciprocal spin conductance ($\Delta G_s$), which exhibit characteristic angular dependencies based on the underlying symmetry.

Important parameters / regimes. The magnitude of the unconventional pairing component ($\lambda$), the angle $\varphi$ (momentum space), and the ratio of disorder/roughness strength ($W/\mu$).

Assumptions / limitations. The method relies solely on symmetry considerations, making it robust against a dominant s-wave background; calculations often use the Andreev approximation.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate how nonreciprocity arises from specific angular dependencies ($ ext{Im}[\psi_{un}(\varphi_k)]$ for $s+id$, or $d$-vector components for spin-triplet) and show comparisons between different pairing symmetries and external perturbations like interface roughness.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the theoretical framework by defining nonreciprocity ($\Delta G$), demonstrates its sensitivity to symmetry breaking (comparing s-wave vs. unconventional), details how charge and spin channels probe distinct symmetries, and finally verifies the method's robustness against disorder and edge effects.

Abstract

Identifying the pairing symmetry of Cooper pairs is a fundamental step toward understanding the microscopic mechanisms of unconventional superconductors. However, experimental identification remains a formidable challenge, particularly when unconventional pairing is obscured by a dominant $s$-wave component that masks its spectroscopic signatures. Here, we develop a symmetry-resolved framework to identify superconducting pairing symmetry through nonreciprocal conductance upon exchanging source and detector terminals in multiterminal devices. We show that nonreciprocal transport arises from symmetry-breaking components of the superconducting order parameter and exhibits a characteristic angular dependence that encodes the momentum-space structure of the pairing gap. In particular, time-reversal-breaking singlet pairing induces nonreciprocal charge transport, while spin-triplet pairing generates nonreciprocal spin responses, providing distinct transport fingerprints of the underlying order. We demonstrate this mechanism using representative models of iron-based and noncentrosymmetric superconductors and outline experimental protocols for multiterminal measurements. Our results advance the theoretical understanding of nonreciprocal transport in superconductors, and establish it as a symmetry-selective probe for identifying hidden unconventional pairing in a wide range of superconducting materials.

Rogue Wave Statistics from a Sparse Coherent Structure Decomposition

Authors: Yuchen He, Amin Chabchoub, Zhan Wang
Type: both · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.09584v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: methods for driven-dissipative 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces a novel framework to predict rogue wave statistics by decomposing the measured wave field into a sparse ensemble of localized, soliton-like packets. By analyzing the log-normal distribution of these extracted structures, the authors derive an extreme value theory that successfully predicts the heavy tails in wave exceedance probabilities, validating their approach against laboratory hydrodynamic experiments.

Why it may be interesting. The methodology—using signal processing techniques like matching pursuit to decompose a complex random field into fundamental coherent structures whose statistical properties govern extreme events—is highly relevant for understanding nonlinear dynamics in optics or plasma physics where such decomposition methods are applicable.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Accurately predicting rogue wave statistics, which are extreme events in strongly nonlinear random wave fields.

Main result. The framework successfully predicts the heavy-tailed probability of exceedance for rogue waves by modeling the field as a sparse superposition of coherent soliton-like packets. This prediction shows good agreement with laboratory hydrodynamic experiments.

Method. An iterative matching pursuit algorithm is used to decompose the complex wave envelope into a sparse ensemble of localized solitons, whose statistical properties (log-normal amplitude distribution) are then fed into an Extreme Value Theory framework.

Model / system. The study analyzes rogue waves in deep-water random wave fields, using laboratory hydrodynamic experiments conducted in a wave flume. The underlying model treats the complex wave envelope as a sparse superposition of coherent soliton packets.

Key observables. Exceedance probability $P(> H)$, soliton amplitudes ($a$), number ($N$), and phase ($\phi$).

Important parameters / regimes. Sea state parameters like steepness ($\epsilon$) and peakedness ($\gamma$); the statistical moments ($\mu, \sigma$) derived from the extracted solitons.

Assumptions / limitations. The complex wave envelope can be accurately represented as a sparse superposition of localized soliton-like packets; the iterative decomposition process yields a physically robust representation despite inherent non-uniqueness.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the successful reconstruction of the original wave envelope and phase using the extracted solitons, show the uniform distribution of soliton emergence times in 3D parameter space, and compare the predicted heavy-tailed exceedance probabilities against experimental data for various sea states.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the limitation of conventional statistical models. It then details the novel decomposition method (matching pursuit) to extract sparse solitons from measured wave fields. Finally, it applies Extreme Value Theory using the statistics of these extracted solitons to predict and validate rogue wave probabilities against experimental data across multiple sea states.

Abstract

While conventional rogue wave statistical models rely on linear or weakly nonlinear descriptions of random seas, we demonstrate experimentally that moderately or strongly nonlinear wave fields can be represented by sparse ensembles of coherent soliton-like packets. These packets exhibit log-normal amplitude distributions together with uniformly distributed phases and peak emergence times. This sparse coherent structure framework naturally leads to an extreme value description in which the probability of exceedance is governed by the tail of the coherent-structure amplitude distribution. The prediction is validated against laboratory hydrodynamic experiments across a variety of unidirectional sea states, showing good agreement with the experimental observations and comparing favourably with conventional statistical prediction models while retaining analytical simplicity. Our results provide a direct physics-based link between sparse coherent structures and rogue wave probabilities, with broader implications for nonlinear wave physics in optics, cold gases, and plasmas.

Authors: Priya Singh, Konrad Kwatek, Tatiana Zajarniuk, Tomasz Cetner, Jan Mizeracki, Shiv J. Singh
Type: experiment · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.08083v1

Summary. This experimental work investigates how high-pressure synthesis modifies the superconducting performance of $ ext{PrFeAsO}_{1-x} ext{F}_x$. The findings show that while high pressure improves crystal structure and raises $ ext{T}_c$ at optimal doping, this enhancement is highly sensitive to fluorine content; excessive doping leads to detrimental impurity phase formation.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on condensed matter superconductivity, the systematic study of phase stability and structural modification under extreme conditions (high pressure) provides insights into how external thermodynamic variables can tune electronic properties in correlated materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how high-pressure/high-temperature synthesis (HP-HTS) affects the structural, superconducting, and magnetic properties of fluorine-doped iron pnictide superconductors ($ ext{PrFeAsO}_{1-x} ext{F}_x$).

Main result. HP-HTS enhances superconductivity ($ ext{T}_c$ increase) and microstructure in underdoped/optimal compositions by improving fluorine incorporation, but this benefit is lost in overdoped samples due to impurity phase segregation.

Method. The research combines synthesis (ambient vs. high pressure), structural characterization (XRD, lattice parameter refinement), microstructural analysis (SEM), and transport measurements ($ ho(T)$, $M(H)$) across different doping levels.

Model / system. The physical system is the iron-based superconductor $ ext{PrFeAsO}_{1-x} ext{F}_x$, which exhibits superconductivity dependent on fluorine substitution ($x$) and processing conditions. The study maps out the superconducting dome by testing underdoped, optimal, and overdoped regimes.

Key observables. Superconducting transition temperature ($ ext{T}_c$), critical current density ($ ext{J}_c$), lattice parameters ($a$, $c$), fluorine incorporation level ($x_{ ext{act}}$), and phase purity (secondary phases like $ ext{PrOF}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Doping concentration ($x=0.2, 0.3, 0.5$), synthesis pressure (0.5 GPa), temperature (950°C), and the interplay between fluorine content and phase stability.

Assumptions / limitations. The effectiveness of HP-HTS is assumed to be governed by achieving optimal fluorine incorporation without exceeding the solubility limit, which dictates structural integrity and superconducting performance.

Figures summary. Figures detail XRD patterns comparing synthesis methods, track lattice parameter changes with doping, show SEM images contrasting microstructures (porosity/connectivity), and plot $ ext{T}_c$ derived from both resistivity and magnetization measurements versus fluorine concentration.

Paper structure. The paper systematically compares samples synthesized under ambient pressure versus high pressure. It analyzes structural improvements (phase purity, densification) first, followed by quantitative measurements of superconducting properties ($ ext{T}_c$, $ ext{J}_c$) across the doping spectrum to establish composition-dependent limits.

Abstract

Motivated by recent reports of enhanced superconducting performance in several families of iron-based superconductors (IBS) processed by high-pressure (HP) synthesis, we investigate the influence of high gas pressure and high-temperature synthesis (HP-HTS) process on the structural, microstructural, electrical transport, and magnetic properties of Pr-based oxypnictide PrFeAsO1-xFx (Pr1111) using the processing conditions of 0.5 GPa for 1 h previously optimized for other IBS families. Representative underdoped (x = 0.2), optimal doped (x = 0.3), and overdoped (x = 0.5) compositions from the ambient-pressure electronic phase diagram of Pr1111 are selected to evaluate its composition-dependent effects of HP-HTS. The results demonstrate that HP-HTS enhances fluorine incorporation, improves phase formation, and produces a denser microstructure with improved grain connectivity in the underdoped and optimal doped compositions. Magnetic measurements reveal increased in the superconducting transition temperature (Tc) of ~1 K for x = 0.2 and ~6 K for x = 0.3, whereas only a marginal improvement in the critical current density is observed. Electrical resistivity measurements of the underdoped composition show a slight increase in Tc accompanied by a broader resistive transition, indicating residual structural inhomogeneity. In contrast, the overdoped composition exhibits increased impurity phase segregation, accompanied by suppression of superconductivity. These results demonstrate that the effectiveness of HP-HTS in Pr1111 is strongly composition dependent and governed by the interplay among fluorine incorporation, phase stability, and microstructural evolution, highlighting the need for further optimization of the HP-HTS processing conditions.

Summary. This paper reports on the creation and characterization of polycrystalline diamond films suitable for use as dielectric heat spreaders in advanced semiconductor packaging (BEOL). Using TDTR measurements, the authors determined that these low-temperature grown films exhibit extremely high thermal conductivities, demonstrating their potential to improve thermal management in microelectronic devices.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on materials science/thermal engineering, the study's quantitative measurement of phonon transport in crystalline solids is relevant to understanding thermal properties in complex quantum systems or interfaces where heat dissipation is critical.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Developing thermally conductive dielectric materials for Back-End-of-Line (BEOL) thermal management that can operate within low temperature budgets (<400°C).

Main result. The authors successfully fabricated polycrystalline diamond films with effective room temperature thermal conductivities of 73 and 86 W m-1 K-1, which are significantly higher than conventional dielectrics.

Method. Characterization involved Raman spectroscopy to check crystal quality, SEM for morphology, and Time-Domain Thermoreflectance (TDTR) measurements to quantify thermal conductivity.

Model / system. The physical system is polycrystalline diamond films grown directly on an Si substrate at low temperatures. The analysis models the heat transport using an isotropic model due to the nearly-isotropic grain structure observed.

Key observables. Room temperature effective thermal conductivities ($\sim 73$ and $\sim 86 ext{ W m}^{-1} ext{ K}^{-1}$), sharp diamond Raman peaks, and large grain sizes visible via SEM.

Important parameters / regimes. Low growth temperature (<400°C), film thicknesses (760 nm and 1000 nm), and the comparison to conventional dielectric materials' thermal conductivity.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes an isotropic thermal model ($\kappa_r = \kappa_z$) because sensitivity analysis showed comparable in-plane and cross-plane heat transport sensitivities.

Figures summary. Figures show TDTR measurement setups, Raman spectra confirming diamond purity, and SEM images illustrating the large grain size of the films.

Paper structure. The paper follows a standard experimental structure: introducing the thermal management problem in BEOL, detailing characterization methods (Raman, SEM, TDTR), presenting results showing high conductivity values, and concluding with the potential application of these low-temperature grown diamond layers.

Abstract

Back-end-of-line (BEOL) thermal management requires electrically insulating heat-spreading dielectric that can be integrated within thermal budgets below 400 C. Here, we report polycrystalline diamond films grown directly on Si at a substrate temperature below 400C. Two films with average thickness of 760 and 1000 nm were characterized by Raman spectroscopy, scanning electron microscopy (SEM), and time-domain thermoreflectance (TDTR). Raman spectra show a sharp diamond peak with minor signatures of non-diamond carbon, while SEM reveals lateral growth and large grain size. Temperature dependent TDTR measurements were performed from room temperature to 100C. Sensitivity analysis indicates that the sensitivity of cross-plane thermal conductivity is comparative to the in-plane thermal conductivity. Accordingly, the films were analyzed using an isotropic thermal model by considering the nearly-isotropic grain structure, yielding room temperature effective thermal conductivity of 73 and 86 W m-1 K-1, respectively. These values are about two orders of magnitude higher than those of conventional dielectric materials and demonstrate the potential of diamond films grown at low temperatures as dielectric heat-spreading layers.

Abstract

Using first-principles calculations, we predict a new perovskite compound SrAsO$_3$ . The undoped cubic phase has pronounced soft-phonon instabilities, which are gradually suppressed upon K doping the Sr site. The cubic phase becomes dynamically stable for K-doping levels above approximately 60%, and the stabilized K-doped phases are metallic with predicted conventional phonon mediated superconductivity. Moreover, the inclusion of nonlocal exchange interactions broadens the electronic bandwidth, enhances the electron-phonon coupling (EPC) strength, and increases the superconducting transition temperature ($T_c$) of these doped compounds. In particular, the HSE06 hybrid exchange-correlation functional corrected EPC constant $λ$ reaches 1.41 for Sr$_{0.4}$K$_{0.6}$AsO$_3$, corresponding to a predicted $T_c$ of 44.3 K. These results suggest that SrAsO$_3$ is a BaBiO$_3$-like superconducting perovskite driven by strong electron-phonon coupling.
