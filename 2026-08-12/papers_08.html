Emergent Problem-Graph Alignment in RL-Discovered Entanglement Topologies for QAOA

Authors: Tobias Rohe, Federico Harjes Ruiloba, Markus Baumann, Gerhard Stenzel, Leo Sünkel, Thomas Gabor, Claudia Linnhoff-Popien
Type: both · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.07686v1

Summary. This work uses Reinforcement Learning to train an agent that discovers optimal entanglement topologies for QAOA optimization on MaxCut problems. By limiting the agent's observation to only performance feedback (AR), it learns to select sparse gate connections that align closely with the true problem graph structure. This reveals a critical trade-off: topology choice dictates convergence speed, suggesting the variational landscape itself contains structural information about the underlying physics.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a novel computational paradigm for circuit design in quantum algorithms, showing how machine learning can probe underlying structural information of Hamiltonians without explicit knowledge, which is relevant for hardware-efficient ansatz design.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Investigating if a Reinforcement Learning (RL) agent can discover effective entanglement topologies for QAOA-based MaxCut optimization without direct access to the problem graph structure.

Main result. The RL agent consistently discovers sparse, problem-aligned topologies that outperform full graph connectivity when the optimization budget is limited. This suggests the variational optimization landscape implicitly encodes structural information about the problem Hamiltonian.

Method. An outer loop uses a Masked PPO agent to sequentially place IsingZZ entangling gates (topology discovery), while an inner loop optimizes QAOA parameters using the resulting topology, returning the approximation ratio as a sparse terminal reward.

Model / system. The system is based on the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) applied to MaxCut, which maps to an Ising Hamiltonian. The RL agent learns to select the entanglement topology $T$ that governs the circuit structure.

Key observables. Approximation Ratio (AR), Overlap Ratio ($|T \cap E|/|T|$), Meyer–Wallach Entanglement Measure ($Q$).

Important parameters / regimes. Optimization budget (e.g., 50 vs. 500 gradient steps) and circuit depth ($p=1$ vs. $p>1$).

Assumptions / limitations. The agent's observation space is intentionally limited to placed edges and the current AR, withholding explicit problem graph structure information.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate training dynamics showing how edge count and overlap ratio evolve over time; tables compare performance metrics across different optimization budgets and circuit depths.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the QAOA framework, defines the RL formulation (MDP), details the agent's interaction with the inner VQA optimizer, presents results comparing sparse vs. full topologies under varying resource constraints, and concludes by discussing the trainability-expressibility trade-off.

Abstract

In the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA), the entanglement topology, where qubit pairs are connected by two-qubit gates, is conventionally set equal to the edge set of the problem graph. This coupling ties circuit design to explicit problem knowledge and may not yield the most trainable circuit under limited optimization budgets. We investigate whether a reinforcement learning (RL) agent can discover more effective entanglement topologies for QAOA-based MaxCut optimization without direct access to the problem graph. A Masked Proximal Policy Optimization agent sequentially places IsingZZ gates to construct a circuit topology, while a variational inner loop optimizes the resulting QAOA parameters and returns the approximation ratio as a sparse terminal reward. The agent's observation contains only the edges placed so far and the current approximation ratio; graph structure can only be inferred indirectly through the optimization reward. On Erdős--Rényi instances with up to $10$~qubits, the agent consistently converges to topologies that are strict subsets of the problem graph, achieving overlap ratios approaching $1.0$, despite receiving no explicit information about the graph structure in its observations. These sparse, problem-aligned topologies outperform the full graph topology and several structural baselines when the optimization budget is limited ($50$~gradient steps), but are overtaken by denser topologies given sufficient optimization budget. Our results reveal a trainability--expressibility trade-off governed by topology density and suggest that the variational optimization landscape implicitly encodes structural information about the problem Hamiltonian.

Summary. This theoretical study investigates the emergence of superconductivity in the dilute 3D electron gas by analyzing electronic self-energy fluctuations. The authors demonstrate that the superconducting pairing mechanism arises from virtual excitations involving excitons, leading to a distinct one-dimensional fluctuation channel. This provides an unexpected pathway for plasmon-driven superconductivity observable through pseudogaps and characteristic temperature dependencies.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides an exotic mechanism for superconductivity in a clean electron gas, linking it to quasi-1D excitonic dynamics rather than phonons or simple Fermi liquid theory, which is highly relevant to understanding unconventional superconductors.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The central challenge is understanding how superconductivity emerges in dilute electronic systems, specifically the 3D homogeneous electron gas (3DHEG), without relying on conventional phonon mechanisms.

Main result. Superconductivity in the 3DHEG can be driven by superconducting fluctuations mediated by virtual excitons, revealing a second-order phase transition at specific densities and low temperatures.

Method. The study employs first-principles calculations of the electron self-energy using the functional GWGamma (FGWGamma) method, analyzing singular behavior in fluctuation quantities.

Model / system. The model is the three-dimensional homogeneous electron gas (3DHEG). The analysis focuses on decomposing the renormalization function Z(K) into contributions from normal quasi-particle physics, excitons, and superconducting fluctuations.

Key observables. Renormalization function divergence (signaling phase transition), pseudogap in density of states D(omega), linear-in-T inverse electron lifetime ($ au^{-1}$), and the exciton spectral shape evolution.

Important parameters / regimes. Density parameter $r_s \approx 8$ and low temperatures, e.g., $T \approx 10^{-4}E_F$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that superconducting fluctuations are effectively one-dimensional (1D) due to phase-space restrictions imposed by the excitonic mechanism.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the divergence of Z(K) near $r_s \approx 8$, showing pseudogaps in D(omega), and comparing the theoretical self-energy contributions from standard 3D fluctuations versus the novel exciton-induced 1D fluctuations.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by establishing the problem (superconductivity in dilute systems), applying advanced many-body techniques (FGWGamma) to find divergences, identifying that excitons mediate these fluctuations via a specific 1D channel ($\Sigma^{(b)}_{sc}$), and finally determining critical parameters like $ au^{-1}$ through frequency extrapolation.

Abstract

Understanding how superconductivity can emerge in dilute electronic systems remains a central challenge in condensed matter physics. By performing first-principles calculations of the electron self-energy Σ(k,iw_n) in the low-density three-dimensional electron gas, we identify a sharp divergence at r_s ~ 8 and T ~ 10^{-4}E_ F, signaling a second-order phase transition. This critical behavior originates from one-dimensional superconducting fluctuations mediated by virtual excitations of an excitron---a quasi-1D electronic composite formed by an electron and longitudinal electron-hole pairs. Although the superconducting mechanism itself is plasmon-mediated, the excitron channel provides a unique window into its fluctuation dynamics. Near the transition, we observe a pseudogap and a linear-in-T inverse electron lifetime, reminiscent of phenomena in high-T_c materials. These results reveal an unexpected route by which plasmon-driven superconductivity manifests in the dilute 3D electron gas through quasi-1D excitron dynamics.

Summary. The paper develops fuzzy geometry as a novel nonperturbative probe to calculate the bound state spectrum of massive Quantum Field Theories. By analyzing systems on fuzzy tori and spheres, the authors demonstrate how spectral features evolve continuously during dimensional crossover from 1D to 2D. This establishes a robust theoretical framework for studying QFTs in regimes inaccessible by standard methods.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful theoretical tool—fuzzy geometries—to study complex many-body physics (bound states) in QFTs without relying solely on computationally intensive lattice methods, making it relevant for understanding critical phenomena and continuum limits.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is performing nonperturbative spectroscopy of bound states (like mesons and glueballs) in massive Quantum Field Theories (QFTs), especially when moving beyond one spatial dimension.

Main result. Fuzzy geometries are established as a robust, nonperturbative method to probe the spectrum of massive QFTs, successfully tracking excitations through dimensional crossover from 1D to 2D while reproducing known theoretical mass ratios.

Method. The authors use regularization based on noncommutative 'fuzzy' geometry (fuzzy torus/sphere) to calculate energy spectra and analyze dynamical structure factors. They employ finite-size scaling and continuum extrapolation techniques.

Model / system. The study uses the Ising QFT as an illustrative model, analyzing its bound states in $(1+1)$D and $(2+1)$D dimensions. The physics is mapped onto spinful electrons confined to a fuzzy torus or sphere geometry.

Key observables. Low-lying energy spectrum ($E_n$), mass ratios ($m_i/m_1$), normalized dynamical structure factor (DSF), and critical fields ($h_c^x$).

Important parameters / regimes. The aspect ratio of the torus ($r=L_y/L_x$) is crucial for dimensional crossover; scaling variables like $D$ and $\mu$ define different physical regimes.

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes that fuzzy geometries provide a valid, nonperturbative regularization scheme allowing continuous tracking across dimensions. The analysis also relies on specific symmetry filtering ($ ext{A}_1$) to isolate relevant spectral components.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the spectrum evolution (e.g., Fig. 4) as the torus aspect ratio changes; they show mass ratios approaching plateaus in scaling windows (Fig. 2); and they detail spectral weight transfer during avoided crossings on the sphere (Fig. S5).

Paper structure. The paper systematically develops the methodology by first establishing benchmarks in 1D, then analyzing dimensional crossover using varying aspect ratios on the torus, and finally applying the technique to the fuzzy sphere geometry while performing finite-size scaling analysis.

Abstract

Mesons and glueballs are paradigmatic bound states of confining quantum field theories (QFTs), but their nonperturbative spectroscopy in the continuum remains challenging beyond one spatial dimension. Here we perform such spectroscopy for the Ising QFT using a recently developed regularization based on noncommutative ``fuzzy'' geometry. On a thin fuzzy torus, we reproduce the universal low-lying $\mathbb E_8$ meson masses of the magnetically perturbed $(1{+}1)\mathrm{D}$ Ising QFT. By increasing the torus aspect ratio, we continuously track the second-lightest $\mathbb E_8$ meson as the system effectively crosses over from 1D to 2D. On the 2D fuzzy torus and sphere, we find a subthreshold scalar level and above-threshold response features consistent with previous estimates of glueball masses. The same excitations are revealed away from equilibrium using quench dynamics. Our results establish fuzzy geometries as nonperturbative spectroscopic probes of massive QFTs, including their bound-state evolution through dimensional crossover.

Summary. This work constructs Genuinely Unextendible Product Bases (GUPBs) using mathematical structures derived from Maximum Distance Separable (MDS) error-correcting codes. These bases define complementary subspaces that are proven to host genuinely multipartite bound entangled states. The paper provides both an explicit construction and rigorous tools, like specialized entanglement witnesses, to certify this non-local entanglement.

Why it may be interesting. The direct link established between classical error-correcting codes (MDS codes) and certifying genuine multipartite entanglement provides a powerful, algebraic tool for quantum information theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the open problem of constructing Genuinely Unextendible Product Bases (GUPBs) for $N \ge 3$ parties and establishing a deep connection between these bases and multipartite entanglement.

Main result. An explicit family of GUPBs is constructed using classical Maximum Distance Separable (MDS) codes, which in turn yields an algebraic route to certifying genuinely multipartite bound entangled states.

Method. The construction relies on the rigidity imposed by MDS codes on product tilings, combined with Fourier mode deletion and a 'stopper' state to prove genuine unextendibility via orthogonality arguments.

Model / system. The system involves multipartite quantum states in $(\mathbb{C}^{p})^{\otimes N}$, analyzed through orthogonal product bases ($\mathcal{U}_C$) whose complementary subspace is shown to host bound entanglement.

Key observables. GUPBs, genuinely entangled subspaces (GES), GME witnesses, and the discrimination constant $\kappa_{\ell, X|Y}$.

Important parameters / regimes. $N \ge 3$ parties; prime field $\mathbb{F}_p$ with $p \ge N^2$; finite tensor powers ($\otimes^\ell$).

Assumptions / limitations. The construction requires the existence of suitable MDS codes and relies on algebraic properties derived from orthogonality relations.

Figures summary. Not specified by notes, but the analysis involves projector operators ($\Pi_G$, $P_U$) and discrimination bounds $\kappa_{\ell, X|Y}$.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the GUPB construction using MDS codes (Chunk 1), to proving the entanglement properties of the complementary subspace via orthogonality conditions (Chunk 2), then detailing distinguishability criteria and witnesses (Chunk 3), and finally analyzing stability under tensor products (Chunk 4).

Abstract

The existence of genuinely unextendible product bases (GUPBs), incomplete orthogonal sets of fully product states whose orthogonal complements contain no product vector across any bipartition, has remained an open problem. Here we construct GUPBs for any number $N\geq3$ of parties using classical maximum distance separable (MDS) codes. The MDS property imposes a rigidity on the induced product tiling across every bipartition; combined with Fourier mode deletion and a stopper state, this rigidity enforces genuine unextendibility. Consequently, the orthogonal complement of each GUPB is a genuinely entangled subspace whose normalized projector is invariant under partial transposition across every bipartition, yielding an explicit family of multipartite bound entangled states. We further construct GME witnesses that detect these states even though no fully decomposable witness can do so. Moreover, the resulting indistinguishability persists under arbitrary finite tensor powers and measurements separable across any bipartition. These results establish a direct connection between error-correcting codes and multipartite entanglement and provide an algebraic route to certifying genuinely multipartite bound entanglement.

Summary. This paper introduces a novel method to automatically refine interatomic potentials by leveraging Lee-Yang theory. It treats phase transitions as singularities in the partition function, using the modulus of this function as a differentiable target during optimization. The technique successfully improves predictions for both simple models and metallic systems by enforcing consistency with known phase diagrams.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical condensed matter, the reliance on complex analysis (Lee-Yang zeros) and differentiable optimization techniques makes this methodology highly relevant for developing machine learning force fields that require rigorous thermodynamic constraints.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Developing a general, automated framework for refining interatomic force fields by guiding the optimization process using information derived from known phase diagrams.

Main result. The proposed method successfully refines potentials (L-J and EAM) such that the predicted Lee-Yang zeros accurately map onto target coexistence curves, significantly improving predictions of thermodynamic properties like enthalpy and heat capacity.

Method. It uses Lee-Yang theory to treat phase transitions as singularities in the partition function. The refinement minimizes a loss function based on the modulus of the partition function evaluated at points near the predicted Lee-Yang zeros in the complex temperature plane.

Model / system. The method is applied to classical molecular simulations, specifically refining parameters for Lennard-Jones potentials (gas-liquid and solid-liquid) and Embedded-Atom Method potentials (Cu metal).

Key observables. Partition function modulus ($\vert ilde{Z} \vert$), phase transition temperatures ($T_{coex}$), enthalpy $H(T)$, and isobaric heat capacity $C_p(T)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The optimization targets are defined by the Lee-Yang circle theorem, requiring input of a known phase diagram boundary.

Assumptions / limitations. The core assumption is that minimizing $\vert ilde{Z} \vert$ at points on the Lee-Yang circle correctly guides the force field parameters toward the true thermodynamic equilibrium state.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the theoretical concept of Lee-Yang zeros forming a circle in the complex plane, show optimization results comparing initial vs. refined phase diagrams (L-J), and validate improved predictions for $H(T)$ and $C_p(T)$ against experimental data (Cu).

Paper structure. The paper introduces the framework using Lee-Yang theory to guide force field refinement. It details the mathematical construction of the loss function based on the partition function modulus, validates it on L-J systems for multiple transitions, and extends its applicability to metallic potentials like Cu.

Abstract

We propose a general framework for automatic force field refinement guided by phase diagrams, grounded in Lee-Yang phase transition theory. The central idea is to directly use the partition function modulus as a phase-diagram-guided optimization target. Evaluating the modulus at points close to the real axis, where the Lee-Yang zeros are mostly associated with the phase transition, is more physically meaningful and avoids the numerical difficulty of explicitly solving for the zeros. This approach requires no system-specific order parameters or response properties for characterizing phase transition points, making it universal across various discontinuous phase transitions and material systems. We validate the method on refining parameters of a Lennard-Jones potential covering both gas-liquid and solid-liquid transitions, and a Cu embedded-atom method potential based on experimental melting curves. The refined force fields reproduce the target phase diagrams with significant improvement across all systems. For Cu, the refinement simultaneously improves predictions of enthalpy and heat capacity, which are observables beyond the optimization target. These results establish Lee-Yang theory as a practical tool for contemporary force field development.

Abstract

In this work, we use algebraic geometric techniques to construct codes over binary extension fields $\mathbb{F}_{2^s}$, thus discovering new protocols for the distillation of qubit magic states, where our focus is on the regime of practical qubit-based quantum computing architectures. When optimized for magic state production per unit time, or logical spacetime volume, we find that our protocols outperform the state-of-the-art in almost every situation, both at input error rates $10^{-3}$ (direct injection), and $10^{-6}$ (allowing some cultivation pre-injection).

Abstract

Hexagonal MnTe is a prototypical semiconducting altermagnet whose properties are heavily influenced by intrinsic disorder, yet how the resulting diverse transport regimes shape its magnetotransport behavior remains to be clarified alongside the role of relativistic spin-orbit coupling (SOC). Our work thus underscores the necessity of exploring magnetotransport across diverse conducting regimes to comprehensively understand altermagnetic properties.

Abstract

This paper argues that the Gibbs paradox can be resolved without using the concept of identical particles in quantum mechanics. The molecules in different regions of the gas can be distinguished, so there is no need to introduce the N! factor. For each molecule, the volume of its free movement space is local at every instant. Moreover, collisions are the primary way of interaction between gas molecules and the fundamental driving force for reaching equilibrium. The potential energy during collisions cannot be ignored. Based on the local free space assumption and collision potential energy, this paper uses the canonical ensemble method to rederive the entropy increase formula for gas mixtures. It includes parameters such as molecular mass, effective radius, and collision characteristic time, which vary with the type of gas molecules. This solves the problem that the entropy increase of gas mixtures is independent of gas properties, that is, it truly resolves the Gibbs paradox instead of providing a new conceptual explanation.

Abstract

The boundary quantity that captures the growth of black-hole interiors quantified by holographic complexity remains unknown beyond 2d dilaton gravity. We provide a critical analysis of a partition-function construction of Krylov spread complexity for thermofield-double states that provides a dimension-independent boundary reconstruction from semiclassical holographic partition functions, while developing the present dynamical and bulk construction for the BTZ saddle. In the double-scaled Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev model, where exact and semiclassical results can be compared, we show that the classical limit is reliable only when taken after the complexity has been reconstructed; taking this limit at the level of individual Lanczos coefficients discards essential information. Applying the construction to a large-central-charge two-dimensional conformal field theory above the Hawking-Page temperature dual to a Bañados-Teitelboim-Zanelli black hole, we find an intermediate departure from early-time quadratic growth followed by behavior compatible with a return toward asymptotically quadratic growth, rather than the linear late-time behavior of the volume and the standard finite-functional complexity = anything class. We then match this boundary behavior to a generalized complexity = anything bulk object built from an infinite series of extrinsic-curvature invariants. The construction provides a systematic route from black-hole thermodynamics to Krylov dynamics and can naturally be extended to higher-dimensional holographic black holes.

Abstract
