A Time-Frequency Framework for GKP Codes

Authors: Franz Luef, Eduard Ortega
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10802v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops a time-frequency framework using Gabor analysis to analyze lattice GKP codes. It proves that the logical information can be uniquely and stably recovered from a finite block of phase-space coefficients. Furthermore, it provides a method to extract the continuous displacement syndrome by analyzing the phase relationships between these coefficients, offering a theoretical blueprint for syndrome measurement.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful mathematical tool for understanding continuous variable quantum error correction, linking abstract phase-space representations (Gabor analysis) directly to measurable syndrome information in a stable, quantifiable manner.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To develop a rigorous time-frequency framework for lattice Gottesman-Kitaev-Preskill (GKP) codes, specifically addressing how to stably reconstruct the finite logical information from localized phase-space coefficients.

Main result. The authors prove that the normalized block map is an isometry, establishing a unique and stable determination of the logical vector from sampled coefficients, and derive a stable estimator for the continuous displacement syndrome from phase relations between coefficient blocks.

Method. The work employs the vector-valued Zak transform and multi-window Gabor analysis to map ideal codewords to finite blocks of adjoint-lattice coefficients, allowing for reconstruction theorems and syndrome estimation.

Model / system. The system is based on lattice GKP codes, which encode quantum information into highly structured states within the infinite-dimensional Hilbert space of oscillators, analyzed using modulation spaces and Gabor frames.

Key observables. The finite block of adjoint-lattice Gabor coefficients, and the phase relations between translated coefficient blocks used to estimate the displacement syndrome.

Important parameters / regimes. The lattice structure ($\Lambda_D$), the regularization parameter ($\epsilon$), and the noise level ($\eta$) governing stability bounds.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is conditional on access to the complex amplitudes (coefficients) and relies on asymptotic convergence and weak-* approximations for finite-energy states.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the Zak transform framework, then developing the adjoint-lattice coefficient model and proving the isometry, followed by deriving the block-correlation estimator for syndrome extraction, and finally quantifying the convergence of this estimator for finite-energy states.

Abstract

We develop a time--frequency framework for lattice GKP codes in which ideal codewords are realized in the modulation space $M^\infty$ and identified, through a vector-valued Zak transform, with a finite logical fibre over the continuous syndrome torus. Multi-window Gabor analysis then represents the logical vector by a finite block of adjoint-lattice coefficients. We prove that the normalized block map is an isometry, obtain an explicit recovering projection, and derive stable logical reconstruction. We further construct normalizable GKP approximants as lattice-envelope Gabor multipliers and establish weak-$*$ convergence and asymptotically isometric encoding. Finally, we recover displacement syndromes from phase relations between translated coefficient blocks and quantify their stability under additive perturbations.

Certified reduced-basis emulation of conformal field theories on the fuzzy sphere

Authors: Virgil V. Baran
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11675v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work develops a highly efficient computational framework using reduced-basis emulation combined with exact diagonalization to study CFTs on the fuzzy sphere. By accurately mapping the energy landscape across coupling parameters, the authors precisely locate critical points for the Ising and O(2) models. This methodology significantly reduces the search cost for understanding quantum critical phenomena in strongly interacting systems.

Why it may be interesting. The use of RBM to map out critical lines in parameter space is a powerful computational technique applicable to any quantum system whose Hamiltonian depends smoothly on multiple parameters, bridging the gap between exact diagonalization and full phase diagram mapping.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To gain numerical access to three-dimensional Conformal Field Theories (CFTs) by efficiently testing for criticality across the vast parameter space of microscopic couplings.

Main result. The combination of exact diagonalization and a certified reduced-basis emulator successfully reproduces known CFT results for the Ising and O(2) models, locating critical lines with high precision.

Method. The authors combine exact diagonalization on symmetry-adapted bases with a certified reduced-basis emulator (RBM) to reconstruct energy and observable data continuously across coupling variations, drastically reducing computational cost.

Model / system. The study focuses on fuzzy-sphere regularizations of 3D CFTs, specifically analyzing the $N_f=2$ Ising model and the $N_f=3$ O(2) Wilson-Fisher CFT. These models are realized via Hamiltonians involving interacting fermions in the Lowest Landau Level.

Key observables. Ground-state energy ($E_{GS}$), scaling dimensions ($\Delta O$), and the ratio $\chi = E_{3/2}/E_0$ used to locate critical points.

Important parameters / regimes. Microscopic couplings (e.g., $h$ for Ising, $D$ and $V_0$ for O(2)), and system size $N$.

Assumptions / limitations. The fuzzy-sphere construction preserves SO(3) symmetry, and the RBM assumes the Hamiltonians are exactly affine in the couplings.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the critical lines for the Ising and O(2) models in coupling planes, showing agreement between emulator results and established DMRG values. Other figures show error analysis and energy/order-parameter surfaces.

Paper structure. The paper develops the computational framework by first detailing the exact-L ED approach, then introducing the RBM for efficient parameter scanning, and finally applying this combined method to determine and map the critical lines for both the Ising and O(2) models using multiple diagnostic criteria.

Abstract

Fuzzy-sphere regularization gives numerical access to three-dimensional conformal field theories (CFTs), but locating one requires testing for criticality across the parameter space of microscopic couplings. We reduce the search cost by about two orders of magnitude, combining an exact diagonalization based on states having definite total angular momentum with a certified reduced-basis emulator that reconstructs energies, observables and their derivatives continuously across the couplings from a handful of exact solutions per system size. The combination reproduces the published ground-state-energy analysis of the fuzzy-sphere Ising model and the published conformal data of a three-flavor fuzzy-sphere realization of the O(2) Wilson--Fisher CFT, including quantities defined by parameter derivatives. Applied to the O(2) model with no CFT input, the ground-state-energy criterion locates the critical coupling to within half a percent of a density matrix renormalization group value obtained on systems more than twice the size, and maps the critical line of the model across the coupling plane. Ground-state criteria locate the critical line robustly, but the point they select along it is weakly determined, and the computed dimensions come closest to the bootstrap elsewhere along the line.

Competing Interlayer Loop Currents and Superconductivity in the Bilayer $t$-$J_\perp$-$V$ Model

Authors: Luciano Zinni, Fabricio Gómez, Jun Zhan, Matías Bejas, Xianxin Wu, Andreas P. Schnyder, Andrés Greco
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10926v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical study models high-Tc superconductivity in bilayer nickelates using the $t$-$J_{\perp}$-$V$ model. It finds that an out-of-plane bond-order phase instability generates loop currents that compete with superconductivity. This competition results in a characteristic dome-shaped superconducting phase diagram, suggesting a quantum critical point governs optimal pairing.

Why it may be interesting. The explicit theoretical modeling of competing orders (charge/orbital vs. superconducting) in a strongly correlated lattice model provides a rich playground for understanding unconventional superconductivity mechanisms, which is a major focus in condensed matter theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the competition between out-of-plane loop currents (LCs) and superconductivity (SC) in bilayer nickelates, a system relevant to high-Tc superconductivity.

Main result. The out-of-plane bond-order phase ($z$-BOP) instability drives the formation of time-reversal symmetry breaking loop currents, leading to a dome-shaped superconducting region optimal near the $z$-BOP quantum critical point.

Method. The study employs a controlled large-$N$ expansion, analyzing the self-consistent gap equations for both the superconducting gap ($\Delta_\perp$) and the complex $z$-BOP gap ($\phi$).

Model / system. The system is modeled by the strongly correlated one-orbital ($ ext{d}_{x^2-y^2}$) bilayer $t$-$J_{\perp}$-$V$ model for nickelates. The Hamiltonian includes hopping, spin exchange ($J_{\perp}$), and Coulomb interactions ($V$).

Key observables. Superconducting gap ($\Delta_\perp$), $z$-BOP gaps ($\phi_{r\perp}, \phi_{A\perp}$), induced in-plane currents, orbital magnetic moment, and the phase diagram structure.

Important parameters / regimes. Doping ($\delta$), interlayer exchange coupling ($J_{\perp}$), and the ratio of hopping parameters ($t_{\perp}/t$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the $z$-BOP gap is purely imaginary ($\phi = i \phi_{A\perp}$), and that Coulomb interactions are less critical than the spin/hopping terms for the primary instabilities.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the evolution of the real and imaginary $z$-BOP gaps versus doping, showing the dominance of the imaginary component ($\phi_{A\perp}$), and map out the phase diagram showing LC, SC, and coexisting regions.

Paper structure. The paper systematically derives the coupled gap equations for SC and $z$-BOP order parameters, analyzes the resulting magnetic flux and current patterns, and maps the resulting phase diagram as a function of doping.

Abstract

The recent discovery of high-$T_c$ superconductivity in pressurized and thin-film bilayer nickelates, featuring a strong interlayer exchange coupling, and their potential similarities with cuprate superconductors, has made this a very active topic in condensed matter physics. In the present paper we study the strongly correlated one-orbital ($d_{x^2-y^2}$) bilayer $t$-$J_\perp$-$V$ model for nickelates, where $V$ denotes the Coulomb interactions, using a controlled large-$N$ expansion at and beyond the mean-field level. Focusing on the out-of-plane spin exchange interaction ($J_\perp$), we find that it triggers both out-of-plane $s$-wave superconductivity and an out-of-plane bond-order phase ($z$-BOP) instability. The $z$-BOP gives rise to a complex $z$-axis hopping dominated by its imaginary component, which drives out-of-plane currents and induces in-plane ones, spontaneously forming on the vertical plaquettes a loop-current state that breaks time-reversal symmetry. Competition between this loop-current phase and superconductivity yields a dome-shaped superconducting region, with optimal superconductivity occurring near the $z$-BOP quantum critical point. The resulting phase diagram features a pure loop-current region, a low-doping coexistence phase, a pure superconducting state at higher doping, and a correlated metallic state.

Consensus in Effective Model Inference for Disordered Ising Systems

Authors: Ashveed Ashraf
Type: theory · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10731v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work introduces 'consensus' to measure how robustly different models agree on the effective physics of disordered systems. By analyzing the variance of inferred parameters, the authors show that the best temperature for agreement is distinct from the best temperature for accuracy due to model limitations. This provides a crucial diagnostic tool for interpreting results from complex, disordered physical models.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous framework for quantifying model uncertainty and disagreement (consensus) in the presence of quenched disorder is highly relevant for interpreting complex simulations in condensed matter theory, especially when data is limited or noisy.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper quantifies the reproducibility of inferred physical descriptions (effective parameters) from finite, noisy data generated by disordered systems, using the concept of 'consensus'.

Main result. The temperature at which independent learners agree most closely (consensus minimum) is generally different from the temperature where their shared answer is most faithful (MSE minimum) due to inherent model misspecification bias.

Method. A teacher-student framework is employed where multiple students independently infer a single effective coupling from finite subsets of equilibrium configurations generated by a disordered Ising system.

Model / system. The system is the two-dimensional random-bond Ising model on a square lattice with quenched couplings drawn from a uniform distribution. The inference process involves fitting a single uniform effective coupling to the local field statistics.

Key observables. Consensus Width (variance), Mean-Squared Error (MSE), Sampling Noise variance, Quenched-Disorder variance, Residual Complexity (RC).

Important parameters / regimes. Disorder strength ($\delta$), Temperature ($T$), Finite sample size ($M$), and the number of students ($N_s$).

Assumptions / limitations. The students are restricted to fitting a single uniform effective coupling, which introduces a systematic misspecification bias that persists even in the infinite-data limit.

Figures summary. Figures compare the inferred coupling $ar{J}$ across different kernels, showing consistency at certain temperatures, and illustrate Residual Complexity (RC), which is zero for the isotropic kernel but non-zero for the identity kernel, indicating lost structure.

Paper structure. The paper develops the consensus concept by decomposing the total variance into sampling and disorder contributions. It then analyzes the bias introduced by the limited hypothesis class, comparing the optimal temperature for consensus agreement versus optimal faithfulness (MSE).

Abstract

When independent learners are trained on data from the same disordered system, finite and noisy samples can lead them to different descriptions of the underlying physics. We quantify the reproducibility of such descriptions through consensus, the degree of agreement among independently trained models. In a teacher-student framework on the two-dimensional random-bond Ising model, an ensemble of students each infers a single uniform effective coupling from a finite set of equilibrium configurations drawn from one quenched bond realization. Aggregating the inferred couplings across students and realizations yields the consensus distribution, whose width quantifies the agreement among learners. The variance of this distribution separates exactly into a finite-sampling contribution, bounded below by inverse of Fisher information, and a quenched-disorder contribution. Sum of these two variance is minimized near critical region, so the consensus width has a minimum near the pseudocritical temperature that sharpens with increasing disorder. The two minima arise from different mechanisms: the sampling term inherits the critical peak of the Fisher information, while the disorder term is temperature-independent at leading order and acquires its critical minimum only at fourth order in the disorder strength, through the covariance between the linear and cubic responses to the bond disorder. Because the students fit a uniform coupling to a heterogeneous lattice, the inferred coupling also carries a misspecification bias that survives in the infinite-data limit. This bias displaces the minimum of the mean-squared error above the consensus minimum, so the temperature at which independent learners agree most closely is not the temperature at which their shared answer is most faithful.

Disordered ground states in one-dimensional exactly solvable fluids

Authors: Igor Travěnec, Ladislav Šamaj
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11394v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Frenkel-Kontorova 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical paper analyzes the ground states of 1D fluids with soft repulsive interactions. It establishes that disordered ground states only exist for potentials that are concave ($ u \ge 1$) at a critical pressure $p_i$. These disordered states are non-hyperuniform and exhibit correlation functions that predict behavior over a wider range of temperatures and pressures.

Why it may be interesting. The finding that disordered ground states can be precisely characterized by non-zero susceptibility and extended to finite temperatures provides a theoretical bridge between zero-temperature quantum/classical ground states and finite-temperature liquid behavior.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the existence and properties of disordered ground states in one-dimensional (1D) exactly solvable fluids.

Main result. Disordered ground states exist only for concave potentials ($ u \ge 1$) at a specific incompressibility pressure ($p_i$), characterized by non-zero isothermal susceptibility and non-hyperuniformity.

Method. The analysis uses the isothermal-isobaric ensemble, employing Laplace transforms and the steepest descent method to analyze the $T o 0$ limit of the equation of state and pair correlation function.

Model / system. The system consists of 1D fluids of classical hard-core particles interacting via a soft, repulsive, finite-range potential $\phi(x)$. The interactions are simplified to nearest-neighbor interactions when the interaction range $a'$ is small relative to the particle diameter $a$.

Key observables. Equation of state, mean nearest-neighbor distance ($l_0$), pair correlation function ($g_0(x)$), and isothermal susceptibility ($\chi_T$).

Important parameters / regimes. The potential exponent $ u$ (determining concavity/convexity), the incompressibility pressure $p_i$, and the temperature $T$ (analyzed in the $T o 0$ limit).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the pairwise interactions reduce to nearest-neighbor interactions ($a' \le 2a$) and relies on the exact solvability of the model in the isothermal-isobaric ensemble.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the pair correlation function $g(x)$ for different pressures around $p_i$, showing transitions between disordered, $a$-periodic, and $a'$-periodic behavior for the square shoulder potential.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the ground state behavior by examining the potential's concavity ($ u$ vs. 1), identifying the critical pressure $p_i$, and deriving the specific forms of $l_0$ and $g_0(x)$ for the resulting ordered and disordered phases.

Abstract

One-dimensional fluids of classical hard-core particles of diameter $a$, interacting in pairs via a soft repulsive (monotonically decreasing) potential of finite range $\varphi(x)=\varepsilon \left[ (a'-x)/(a'-a)\right]^{1/ν}$ $(a\le x\le a')$ with real positive parameters $\varepsilon$ and $ν$, are studied in an isothermal-isobaric ensemble. If $a'\le 2a$, the pairwise interactions are reduced to nearest-neighbour interactions, which allows for an exact solution of the thermal equilibrium. We focus on the $T\to 0$ ground states, specifically on the equation of state for the mean distance between nearest neighbours $l_0$ and the pair correlation function $g_0(x)$. If $\varphi(x)$ is concave $(ν\ge 1)$, there exists an ``incompressibility'' pressure $p_i=\varepsilon/(a'-a)$ such that the ground state is an equidistant chain of particles with spacing $l_0=a'$ for $0p_i$. If $ν>1$ (strict concavity), the ground state at $p=p_i$ is disordered with $l_0=\left[ νa +(ν-1)a'\right]/(2ν-1)$ and $g_0(x)$ being a superposition of weighted Dirac delta functions over discrete positions. If $ν=1$ (linear ramp), the ground state at $p=p_i$ is disordered with $l_0=(a+a')/2$ and the continuous $g_0(x)$ is a superposition of Heaviside step functions multiplied by polynomials in $x$. The isothermal susceptibility at $T=0$ is nonzero for concave $\varphi(x)$ ($ν\ge 1$) at $p=p_i$ and, therefore, the corresponding disordered ground states are non-hyperuniform, i.e., they resemble disordered fluids at nonzero temperatures. It turns out that pair correlation functions of disordered ground states, which occur only at a single pressure $p_i$, extend their predictive power to thermodynamic states at nonzero temperatures over a wider range of pressures around $p_i$.

EFI Pairs Without One-Way Puzzles: Oracle Separations from Communication Complexity

Authors: Atul Mantri
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11901v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper tackles the relationship between two key quantum cryptographic assumptions: EFI pairs and one-way puzzles. By constructing a specific oracle, the authors prove that one can have quantum states that are hard to distinguish classically without relying on the existence of one-way puzzles. The work rigorously bounds the quantum advantage using communication complexity and random matrix theory.

Why it may be interesting. It provides deep theoretical insights into the computational assumptions underpinning quantum cryptography, showing that certain quantum advantages might vanish when the input/output structure is restricted to classical information.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates whether the existence of EFI pairs (a quantum cryptographic primitive) necessitates the existence of one-way puzzles, a fundamental open question in quantum cryptography.

Main result. The authors construct a scenario relative to a specific oracle where EFI pairs exist without one-way puzzles, demonstrating that quantum polynomial time offers no advantage on tasks with purely classical inputs and outputs.

Method. The security proof relies on reducing the distinguishing advantage problem to classical communication complexity, specifically the Vector-in-Subspace problem, and uses random matrix theory for the final coherent query bound.

Model / system. The model involves analyzing the indistinguishability of two quantum states (an EFI pair) relative to an oracle that provides classical query answers about quantum samplers, all within a 'Coherent-last access' computational model.

Key observables. Distinguishing advantage ($ ext{Advantage}$), trace distance ($ ext{TD}$), and the bias of the quantum state separation.

Important parameters / regimes. The parameter $\lambda$ governing the state preparation, the dimension $n$ of the subspace, and the number of queries $L$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is restricted to the 'Coherent-last access' model, and the paper concludes by stating conjectures to remove the restriction on superposition queries.

Figures summary. No figures were explicitly described in the notes, but the analysis relies on bounding sums over different cell size regimes and using various mathematical lemmas (e.g., Lemma 6.15).

Paper structure. The paper establishes the problem setup using EFI pairs and one-way puzzles, then introduces the oracle model. The core is the security proof, which proceeds by reducing the advantage to communication complexity (Theorem C) and bounding the coherent query advantage using random matrix theory (Theorem D).

Abstract

EFI pairs (Brakerski, Canetti, and Qian, ITCS 2023) and one-way puzzles (Khurana and Tomer, STOC 2024) are the leading candidates for the minimal assumption of quantum cryptography. The first are efficiently preparable quantum states, statistically far yet computationally indistinguishable; the second are classical puzzles, easy to sample and hard to solve. One-way puzzles imply EFI pairs, and whether the converse holds is open. We construct a single classical oracle relative to which one-way puzzles do not exist, even with an unbounded verifier, while an EFI pair survives every distinguisher that queries the oracle classically throughout and holds advice about it, making its one superposition query at the end. The oracle answers every question about the output probabilities of quantum samplers, which removes the puzzles, and hides a Haar-random half-dimensional subspace. To prove security we reduce it to communication complexity. An adversary whose knowledge of the subspace arrives as classical query answers can be simulated inside a two-party protocol against the party holding it, so it does no better than the best classical protocol for Vector-in-Subspace (Klartag and Regev, STOC 2011), whatever the oracle computes. That argument does not cover the superposition query, which we bound instead using tools from random matrix theory. The same attack gives a classical simulation of any quantum party in a classical-message protocol with no entanglement shared in advance, so relative to the oracle there is no proof of quantumness either. Quantum polynomial time therefore offers no advantage on any task with classical inputs and outputs, while the two quantum states stay indistinguishable. We state conjectures on removing the restriction on superposition queries.

Fingerprints of Excitonic Collective Modes in the Two-Dimensional Electron Gas

Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5

Summary. This work uses TDDFT to explore collective charge excitations in the 2DEG, finding that excitonic modes appear at low densities, distinct from simple plasmons. The theory predicts that these modes signal an instability towards a charge-density-wave phase when the electron density drops sufficiently low. The findings offer concrete, measurable fingerprints for experimental detection in tunable 2D electron systems.

Why it may be interesting. The detailed study of collective excitations and instability mechanisms in a fundamental model system like the 2DEG provides crucial theoretical benchmarks for understanding strongly correlated electron behavior in low-dimensional materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the existence and experimental signatures of excitonic collective charge excitations in the Two-Dimensional Electron Gas (2DEG) at low densities.

Main result. New excitonic modes emerge beyond traditional plasmons at low densities, and the system is predicted to become unstable towards a charge-density-wave (CDW) phase when the Wigner-Seitz radius exceeds a critical value.

Method. The research employs Time-Dependent Density Functional Theory (TDDFT) within the framework of the Casida equation, comparing results across various approximations for the exchange-correlation (xc) kernel.

Model / system. The physical system is the two-dimensional homogeneous electron gas (2D HEG), a model system whose properties are tunable by varying the electron density via the Wigner-Seitz radius ($r_s$).

Key observables. Loss function ($ ext{Im} \epsilon^{-1}(q, \omega)$), static linear density response ($\chi(q, \omega=0)$), and induced density oscillations ($\Delta n(r)$).

Important parameters / regimes. Wigner-Seitz radius ($r_s$), wavevector ($q$), and frequency ($\omega$). The critical instability is predicted around $r_s \gtrsim 14.455$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on approximations for the xc kernel, comparing RPA, ALDA, DPGT, and DPGT+NCT, with the most detailed results using the DPGT+NCT parametrization.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the development of asymmetric peak structures in the loss function and strong Friedel-like oscillations in the static response function as $r_s$ increases, particularly near $q=2k_F$.

Paper structure. The paper systematically compares the predictions of various theoretical approximations (RPA vs. DPGT+NCT) for collective excitations, focusing on how excitonic effects manifest in the loss function and the static density response, culminating in the prediction of a CDW instability.

Abstract

The two-dimensional homogeneous electron gas (2D HEG) is a prototype system of fundamental interest that can be realized experimentally over a wide range of densities. Here, we investigate its collective charge excitations at low densities, using time-dependent density functional theory. We demonstrate that, beyond traditional plasmons, new collective excitonic modes emerge for a Wigner-Seitz radius larger than $r_{\mathrm s} \approx 1$. These excitonic modes leave characteristic fingerprints in experimentally accessible quantities, namely, asymmetric peak structures in the loss function and very strong Friedel-like oscillations in the static linear density response that increase when the system approaches a regime of instability. Indeed, at low enough densities the collective modes cross the zero energy axis, indicating an instability of the paramagnetic 2D HEG towards the formation of a charge-density-wave phase with excitonic origin. These findings provide valuable insights for the experimental detection of excitonic collective modes in tunable 2D electron systems and contribute to the fundamental understanding of many-body effects in low-density electron gases.

Hierarchy of Rényi Coherent Information in Stabilizer Codes

Authors: Akash Vijay, Luis Colmenarez, Jong Yeon Lee
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11930v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper analyzes the Rényi coherent information in quantum error correction codes, proving that under specific noise conditions (independent Bernoulli faults), this information measure is nondecreasing with the Rényi index $n$. It also provides an operational interpretation by linking the information measure to the fidelity of quantum channel recovery via syndrome postselection.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides rigorous mathematical tools to analyze the performance limits of quantum error correction codes under realistic noise models by establishing concrete, computable hierarchies for entanglement measures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses two shortcomings of Rényi coherent information ($I_c^{(n)}$): its lack of monotonicity in the Rényi index $n$ and its lack of clear operational meaning compared to the von Neumann case.

Main result. For Pauli noise from independent Bernoulli events, the Rényi coherent information is proven to be nondecreasing in $n$. Furthermore, an operational meaning is established via postselection on matching syndromes, linking saturation of $I_c^{(n)}$ to perfect channel recovery.

Method. The authors employ information-theoretic tools, including Rényi entropy calculations, correlation inequalities (like Harris's), and analyzing the structure of syndrome-conditioned quantum channels.

Model / system. The study focuses on stabilizer codes subjected to Pauli noise, considering both independent Bernoulli noise and general stochastic Pauli noise models.

Key observables. Rényi coherent information ($I_c^{(n)}$), the Rényi deficit ($\Delta_n$), and the recovery fidelity ($F_n$).

Important parameters / regimes. The Rényi index $n$, the noise model (Bernoulli vs. general stochastic Pauli noise), and the code structure.

Assumptions / limitations. The initial hierarchy proof relies on the assumption that the Pauli noise is generated by independent Bernoulli events; counterexamples show failure when this assumption is violated.

Figures summary. Figure 1(a) illustrates the nondecreasing Rényi hierarchy for a code under depolarizing noise, while Figure 1(b) depicts the postselection gadget using one data block and $n-1$ auxiliary blocks.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by first proving the nondecreasing hierarchy for independent Bernoulli noise, then defining the operational meaning via matched-syndrome postselection, and finally analyzing the conditions (like $n$-balance) under which these bounds and equivalences hold.

Abstract

Rényi coherent information, a computable proxy for the von Neumann coherent information, is widely used to study mixed-state phases of matter and decodability transitions in noisy quantum error-correcting codes. However, being a difference of two Rényi entropies, it need not be monotonic in the Rényi index, and lacks the operational meaning of its von Neumann counterpart. Here we address both issues for stabilizer codes. First, for Pauli noise generated by independent Bernoulli events, we prove that the Rényi-$n$ coherent information is nondecreasing in $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$. This follows from a general theorem: if independent random bits are mapped linearly to a fine label $T$ and a coarse label $C$, then the Rényi entropy difference $H_n(C)-H_n(T)$ is nondecreasing in $n$. For stabilizer codes, $T$ is the joint syndrome--logical class and $C$ is the syndrome, and the difference is the Rényi-$n$ coherent information up to a constant. The same theorem covers classical linear codes and independent detector error models. Second, for arbitrary stochastic Pauli noise, we give the Rényi-$n$ coherent information an operational meaning via postselection on matching syndromes between one data block and $n-1$ auxiliary blocks. We determine when this defines a quantum channel and show that saturation of the Rényi-$n$ coherent information is equivalent to asymptotically perfect recovery of the postselected channel. Moreover, the Rényi-$n$ coherent information also upper-bounds the ordinary coherent information achievable after any syndrome-conditioned recovery.

Irrationality Measure Controls Long-Wavelength Charge Fluctuations in Quasiperiodic Systems

Authors: Junmo Jeon, Shiro Sakai
Type: theory · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10948v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Frenkel-Kontorova 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work establishes that the long-wavelength charge fluctuations in quasiperiodic systems are fundamentally controlled by the arithmetic properties of the irrational modulation frequency $eta$. It derives a scaling law linking the fluctuation exponent to both the frequency's irrationality measure and the smoothness of the system's charge profile. This provides a deep theoretical link between number theory and condensed matter physics.

Why it may be interesting. The explicit connection between number theory (irrationality measure) and physical transport/fluctuation properties in quasiperiodic lattices provides a powerful, non-trivial diagnostic tool for characterizing the underlying order.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper determines the fundamental principle governing long-wavelength charge fluctuations in quasiperiodic systems, linking it directly to the arithmetic properties of the irrational modulation frequency.

Main result. The scaling of infrared charge fluctuations is governed by a relationship involving the system's regularity exponent ($r$) and the irrationality measure ($\mu(eta)$) of the frequency, specifically $\alpha + 1 = 2r / (\mu(eta) - 1)$.

Method. The analysis uses Fourier decomposition and scaling arguments to relate the integrated low-momentum weight (charge fluctuations) to the arithmetic properties of the quasiperiodicity.

Model / system. The study focuses on translation-covariant quasiperiodic systems, exemplified by the Aubry-André model, described by Hamiltonians $H( heta)$ modulated by an irrational frequency $eta$.

Abstract

Abstract

Quantum-inspired classical algorithms have dequantized several quantum machine learning routines by replacing quantum linear-algebra subroutines with classical counterparts. However, the sampler based on quantum singular value transformation (QSVT) for learning with optimized random features is not covered by existing dequantization frameworks, because the matrix to be inverted is not itself available through sampling access. In this work, we develop a classical algorithm to address this type of quantum-advantage candidate.
