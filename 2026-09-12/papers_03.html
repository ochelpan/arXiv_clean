Microwave-controlled interactions and stripe formation of static-field-shielded polar molecules

Summary. This theoretical study models polar molecules whose interactions are controlled by microwave fields and static electric fields. By deriving an effective interaction potential, the authors predict that the system can enter a supersolid stripe phase even with small microwave ellipticity. This work advances the understanding of quantum gas control and exotic phase realization in ultracold molecular systems.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the control of quantum many-body systems using tailored light fields, providing a mechanism to realize exotic phases like supersolids in molecular BECs, which is highly relevant to modern AMO research.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates microwave-controlled interactions and the resulting stripe phase formation in a system of polar molecules trapped in external fields.

Main result. The research demonstrates that even small microwave ellipticity can induce a supersolid stripe phase in a quasi-two-dimensional molecular Bose-Einstein condensate.

Method. The study employs perturbation theory to derive effective interaction potentials, which are then used in the extended Gross-Pitaevskii equation (GPE) and analyzed via Bogoliubov-de Gennes (BdG) equations.

Model / system. The system consists of polar molecules subjected to a static electric field and an elliptically polarized microwave field. The interactions are modeled using derived effective potentials, leading to a mean-field description for the gas dynamics.

Key observables. Collective excitation spectrum, ground state density, total loss rate coefficient, and the formation of the stripe phase.

Important parameters / regimes. Static field strength (F), microwave ellipticity ($\xi$), detuning ($\Delta$), and Rabi frequency ($\Omega$).

Assumptions / limitations. The effective interaction approach is validated against coupled-channel calculations, and the GPE approach assumes the applicability of mean-field theory.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the dependence of loss rates and scattering ratios on field parameters, and show the excitation spectrum of the condensate as a function of ellipticity, revealing the transition to a stripe phase.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by deriving the effective interaction potential via perturbation theory, analyzing the resulting loss mechanisms, and finally applying the effective interaction within the GPE framework to calculate and map out the excitation spectrum, revealing the stripe phase transition.

Abstract

We study polar molecules where short-range losses are suppressed by a shielding scheme involving a static electric field and an elliptically polarized microwave field. Using perturbation theory, we derive the effective interaction potential and validate it against coupled channel calculations. We identify a parameter regime where two-body losses are strongly suppressed and the extended mean-field description of dilute molecular Bose-Einstein condensates is justified. We calculate the collective excitations and show that intriguingly, supersolidity in quasi-two-dimensional confinement emerges as a stripe phase even at small values of microwave ellipticity.

Walking Floquet code circuits for zero-overhead leakage reduction

Summary. This paper presents novel, zero-overhead walking circuits for the honeycomb Floquet code to combat leakage errors in quantum computation. By dynamically removing leakage while preserving code distance, the circuits show superior error correction capabilities compared to existing methods. The work highlights the importance of entropic effects, predicting a rapid 'waterfall' improvement in error rates at finite physical error levels.

Why it may be interesting. The focus on leakage mitigation and the observation of non-standard error scaling regimes (waterfall) provide advanced insights into the practical limits of fault-tolerant quantum computation beyond simple asymptotic bounds.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the significant challenge of leakage errors in quantum error correction (QEC), which induce correlated errors and degrade code performance.

Main result. The proposed zero-overhead walking circuits for the honeycomb Floquet code can correct as many leakage errors as Pauli errors, and exhibit a pronounced 'waterfall regime' where performance surpasses distance-limited scaling predictions.

Method. The authors introduce and analyze two specific walking circuits (swirling and sliding) designed to periodically remove leakage while dynamically protecting logical qubits using anticommuting two-body measurements.

Model / system. The study focuses on the honeycomb Floquet code (hFC) implemented on a hexagonal lattice, analyzed under several physically motivated leakage noise models (e.g., depolarizing, skip-gate leakage).

Key observables. Logical Error Rate ($p_L$), Code Distance, Qubit Lifetime, and the scaling exponent ($\alpha$) of the error rate.

Important parameters / regimes. Physical error rate ($p$), leakage noise strength, and the comparison between asymptotic low-error regimes and finite-error 'waterfall' regimes.

Assumptions / limitations. The circuits are designed to be 'zero-overhead,' meaning they do not require additional qubits, gates, or time steps for leakage removal.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the hFC on the lattice, the four leakage noise models considered, and plots showing the dependence of the logical error rate on the physical error rate, highlighting the waterfall regime.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the hFC and the leakage problem, details the construction of the zero-overhead circuits (swirling/sliding), analyzes performance under various leakage models via numerical simulation, and compares results against the walking surface code.

Abstract

Leakage, occurring when a qubit undetectably exits the computational subspace, poses a significant challenge for quantum error correction by inducing correlated errors in space and time. These correlations reduce both the code threshold and the effective code distance. To address this challenge, we introduce two zero-overhead walking circuits for the honeycomb Floquet code (hFC), termed the swirling and sliding circuits, which periodically remove leakage while dynamically protecting logical qubits via a schedule of anticommuting two-body measurements. Unlike previous dynamic circuits for the hFC, ours preserves the distance to Pauli errors. We further study their performance under leakage and find that, for two leakage noise models, they may correct as many leakage errors as Pauli errors, improving on the more widely known walking surface code. Through numerical simulation, we observe that finite-error-rate performance under leakage is strongly influenced by entropic effects, producing a pronounced waterfall regime in which the logical error rate decreases much more rapidly with physical error rate than the expected distance-limited scaling. In this regime, even when asymptotic predictions suggest otherwise, the hFC can outperform the same-distance walking surface code in error rates and sub-threshold logical error scaling.

Summary. This paper presents a novel, gradient-based algorithm (gradBEA) to optimize the single-particle basis used in Matrix Product States for fermionic systems. By minimizing a block entropy area cost function, the method iteratively refines the state representation, achieving superior convergence and accuracy compared to previous local optimization techniques. It provides a powerful, generalizable tool for simulating complex quantum many-body physics.

Why it may be interesting. The development of a non-local, gradient-based optimization technique for tensor network representations is a powerful algorithmic advance for simulating strongly correlated quantum systems, potentially improving upon standard DMRG limitations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core problem is efficiently finding the ground state eigenstates of many-body Hamiltonians for interacting fermions, which is computationally intractable due to the exponential growth of the Hilbert space.

Main result. The proposed gradient-based method (gradBEA) achieves significantly faster convergence and yields lower ground state energies compared to established local optimization protocols.

Method. It introduces a systematic block entropy area based mode optimization algorithm that uses the gradient of an entanglement cost function to drive an iterative, non-local update of the single-particle basis within the Matrix Product State framework.

Model / system. The method is applied to interacting fermions, modeled using Matrix Product States (MPS). Specific examples include spinless 'tt'V' models on a square lattice, the Hubbard model, and the $ ext{Fe}_4 ext{S}_4$ cluster.

Key observables. The primary observables are the ground state energy ($E_0/t$) and the entanglement measure, the block entropy area ($B_\mu$).

Important parameters / regimes. The optimization depends on the entanglement measure ($\mu$-Rényi entropy $S^{(i)}_\mu$) and the parameters ($\vec{ heta}$) governing the unitary rotations ($U^{(s)}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The optimization is performed iteratively by following the steepest descent path defined by the gradient, and the method is restricted to optimizing rotations within the spanned single-particle space.

Figures summary. Figure 1 compares the ground state energy convergence of gradBEA against local methods for the $ ext{tt}' ext{V}$ model. Figure 2 illustrates the iterative loop and the tensor diagram used in the calculation.

Paper structure. The paper details the systematic derivation of the gradient of the block entropy area, outlines the three-step iterative optimization protocol (DMRG simulation, gradient calculation, time evolution via PS-TDVP), and validates the method on various lattice and molecular models.

Abstract

We introduce a systematic block entropy area based mode optimization algorithm for many-body quantum states of interacting fermions represented by matrix product states. From the gradient of a global cost function, the block entropy area, a long-ranged, non-interacting effective disentangler Hamiltonian is formed. We then simulate the time-dependent Schrödinger equation driven by the disentangler Hamiltonian by employing the time-dependent variational principle based on projector splitting, and minimize the cost function. The combination of the density matrix renormalization group with this gradient-based entanglement minimization forms an efficient low-rank iterative ground-state algorithm that also provides an optimized single-particle basis for matrix product state representation. We demonstrate the method on two-dimensional lattice models of interacting fermions and the Fe${_4}$S${_4}$ cluster, and show its robustness and superiority over earlier protocols using nearest-neighbor mode rotations and reorderings.

Condensation temperature and magnetic phases of trapped coherently coupled Bose gases

Summary. This theoretical study analyzes the phase transitions and critical temperature of a Bose gas coupled coherently in a harmonic trap. By using semiclassical and HFB-Popov methods, the authors show that coherent coupling modifies $T_c$, and that repulsive interactions stabilize the system's response to this coupling. The results provide a detailed phase diagram mapping the transition from magnetic to thermal states.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the interplay between coherent quantum coupling, spatial confinement, and interatomic interactions on fundamental quantum phase transitions in ultracold atomic systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the condensation temperature and magnetic phase transitions in a Bose gas subjected to coherent coupling (Rabi coupling) within a harmonic trap.

Main result. Coherent coupling enhances the critical temperature, and repulsive interactions substantially suppress the variation of this temperature with increasing coupling strength.

Method. The study employs the semiclassical description for the noninteracting case and the Hartree–Fock–Bogoliubov–Popov (HFB-Popov) framework for interactions.

Model / system. The system is a Bose gas confined in a 3D harmonic trap, modeled with a Hamiltonian incorporating a coherent coupling term ($\sigma_x$). The analysis covers both noninteracting and interacting regimes.

Key observables. Critical temperature ($T_c$), condensate fraction ($N_0/N$), and the magnetic phase diagram (Ferromagnetic $ o$ Paramagnetic $ o$ Thermal gas).

Important parameters / regimes. Coherent coupling strength ($\Omega$), particle number ($N$), and interaction strength ($g$).

Assumptions / limitations. The semiclassical approximation ($k_B T \gg \hbar\omega$) is used for the density of states, and the analysis incorporates finite-size corrections.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the excitation spectrum showing level splitting, the density of states ($ ho_{3D}, ho_{2D}$) for varying coupling, and the normalized $T_c$ and condensate fraction versus temperature.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from noninteracting analysis using semiclassical methods to incorporating finite-size corrections. It then extends the analysis to include repulsive interactions using the HFB-Popov framework to map the full phase diagram.

Abstract

We investigate condensation and finite-temperature magnetic phase transitions in a coherently (Rabi) coupled Bose gas confined in a three-dimensional harmonic trap. For the noninteracting system, we derive analytical expressions for the critical temperature and condensate fraction using a semiclassical description of the single-particle spectrum and density of states. At fixed particle number, coherent coupling enhances the critical temperature relative to the uncoupled system, and this enhancement decreases with increasing particle number $N$. The finite-size correction, in contrast, lowers the transition temperature, with its effect diminishing for larger $N$. We then incorporate repulsive interactions within the Hartree--Fock--Bogoliubov--Popov framework to investigate the finite-temperature phase diagram in the temperature--Rabi coupling plane. The system undergoes successive transitions from a ferromagnetic to a paramagnetic condensate and, at higher temperature, to a thermal gas. The transition boundaries obtained from the vanishing of the condensate fraction show excellent agreement with the analytical Hartree-Fock predictions. We find that the interactions substantially suppress the variation in the condensation temperature with an increase in the coherent coupling.

Engineering Quantum Links: Noise and Quantum-State-Degradation Metrics over Metropolitan Fiber Network

Authors: Marcello Caleffi, Laura d'Avossa, Angela Sara Cacciapuoti
Type: both · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11359v1

Summary. This paper develops an engineering framework for quantum networking by characterizing noise and state degradation over real metropolitan fiber links. The authors adapt classical communication metrics like SINR and BER to the quantum domain, measuring impairments across polarization, time, and frequency. This work transforms quantum networking from a theoretical exercise into a quantifiable engineering design problem for the Quantum Internet.

Why it may be interesting. It provides concrete, measurable metrics (like the quantum link budget) that bridge fundamental quantum optics theory with practical, large-scale engineering deployment challenges in quantum communication.

Detailed structure

Main result. The quantum link can be characterized by a small set of measurable parameters, transforming quantum networking from a pure physics demonstration into a solvable engineering design problem.

Method. The authors developed a unified framework by creating quantum analogs of classical metrics (SINR and BER) and applying them to real-world measurements on a deployed fiber loop.

Model / system. The study uses a 7.3 km metropolitan fiber loop in the QuantumInternet.it testbed, comparing quantum signals (C-band) to classical traffic (O-band). The analysis models noise using a photon-counting SINR analog and state degradation across polarization, time, and frequency using BER analogs.

Key observables. Dark-count rate, interference count from classical traffic, channel-induced degradation, and temporal drift for polarization, time, and frequency.

Important parameters / regimes. The link budget is built from measurable parameters, including the ratio of signal to noise/interference counts, and the spectral characteristics of Raman noise.

Assumptions / limitations. The approach assumes the use of a shared physical medium, allocating classical traffic to the O-band and quantum signals to the C-band.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates that quantum state degradation is captured by two complementary descriptions: the SINR analog (noise side) and the BER analog (error side, resolved across polarization, time, and frequency).

Paper structure. The paper builds the foundation by first defining the necessary metrics (SINR/BER analogs), then experimentally measuring noise (dark counts, crosstalk) and state degradation (polarization, time, frequency drift) on a deployed loop, culminating in a comprehensive quantum link budget.

Abstract

Deploying quantum networks over existing network infrastructures requires the same engineering foundations that underpin classical communications: quantitative models of the channel's noise and of the impairments it imposes on the transmitted information. In this work, we build such a foundation on experimental measurements, grounding the quantum-network counterparts of the two cornerstone metrics of classical link characterization - namely, the SINR and the BER - on a 7.3 km deployed metropolitan-scale fiber-loop interconnecting two campuses of the University of Naples Federico II within the national QuantumInternet.it testbed. On the noise side, we adopt a photon-counting quantum analog of the SINR - in which dark counts constitute the intrinsic noise and the photons generated by classical traffic (through either spontaneous Raman scattering or inter-fiber crosstalk) constitute the interference - and we quantify each contribution directly on the deployed loop. On the bit-error side, we consider the main degrees-of-freedom available to encode a quantum state within an optical photon - namely, polarization, time, and frequency - and we quantify for each degree the channel-induced degradation and its drift over time. These results show that a quantum fiber link, like its classical counterpart, can be captured by a small set of measurable parameters, turning quantum networking over deployed fiber from a physics demonstration into an engineering design problem. Together, they provide the key ingredients of a quantum link budget for the Quantum Internet.

Summary. This paper develops a generalized entropy formula for tensor network models representing TQFTs with continuous gauge groups. Abstract

We derive an entropy formula for recently proposed tensor network models which prepare diffeomorphism invariant states of topological quantum field theories with non-compact and/or continuous gauge groups. We show that our entropy formula generalizes the notion of ``topological entanglement entropy'' to incorporate the infinite number of particle-like excitations in such theories. When our networks are endowed with gauge group $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$, we can interpret them as models of three-dimensional gravity with small Newton's constant and possibly non-invertible metrics.

Massive quantum divergence on the Cauchy horizon of a black hole

Summary. This paper analyzes quantum fluctuations of a massive scalar field inside a charged black hole, specifically focusing on the Cauchy horizon. The key finding is that quantum effects cause divergences in the stress-energy tensor on the CH, suggesting a stronger singularity than classical theory predicts. This work probes the limits of semi-classical gravity in extreme astrophysical environments.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on general relativity, the study of divergences and backreaction in extreme spacetime geometries shares conceptual parallels with understanding instabilities or strong coupling regimes in quantum many-body systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the behavior of quantum massive scalar fields near the Cauchy horizon (CH) of a charged Reissner-Nordström black hole, focusing on quantum divergences and their implications for spacetime structure.

Main result. The vacuum polarization and stress-energy tensor components diverge on the CH for massive fields, suggesting a strong singularity via backreaction, unlike the classical prediction.

Method. The authors employ a semi-classical approach, calculating renormalized expectation values of quantum fields using techniques like the extended coordinate method in curved spacetime.

Model / system. The system is a quantum massive scalar field propagating in the interior of a charged, spherically-symmetric Reissner-Nordström black hole spacetime.

Key observables. Vacuum polarization ($\langle \hat{\Phi}^2 angle$), components of the renormalized quantum stress-energy tensor ($\langle \hat{T}_{\mu u} angle$), and energy fluxes in Eddington-Finkelstein and Kruskal coordinates.

Important parameters / regimes. Black hole charge ($Q$), field mass ($\mu$), coupling constant ($\xi$), and the choice of quantum state (Hartle-Hawking vs. Unruh).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is restricted to the RN background and relies on the semi-classical approximation where quantum effects source classical spacetime curvature.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the divergence of vacuum polarization and stress-energy tensor components near the CH for varying field masses and charges, and show the dependence of fluxes on parameters.

Paper structure. The paper analyzes the quantum field behavior by comparing massive and massless cases, calculating divergences in the vacuum polarization and stress-energy tensor components, and discussing the implications for the stability and singularity structure of the Cauchy horizon.

Abstract

We investigate a quantum massive scalar field in the interior of a charged and spherically-symmetric (Reissner-Nordström) black hole. We examine the behaviour for varying values of the black hole charge, field mass and coupling constant when the field is in two quantum states: Hartle-Hawking (representing a black hole in thermal equilibrium) and Unruh (representing a black hole evaporating via the emission of Hawking radiation). We show that the vacuum polarization as well as the angular components of the quantum stress-energy tensor diverge on the Cauchy horizon, in stark contrast to what happens for massless fields. We also calculate the energy fluxes in Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates $\{u,v\}$. We show that these fluxes on the Cauchy horizon do not generically vanish. This implies, in particular, that in regular, Kruskal coordinates $\{U,V\}$ the ingoing flux diverges like $V^{-2}$ on the Cauchy horizon (where $V=0$). This divergence suggests that its backreaction via the semiclassical Einstein equations would yield a strong singularity, as opposed to its weaker, classical counterpart. Interestingly, there are exceptions, in which the energy fluxes in Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates vanish: (i) in the extremal limit (where the black hole is maximally charged); (ii) certain fine-tuned regions of parameter space, where the fluxes change sign.

Abstract

We study quantum droplets in a symmetric two-component Bose mixture with interspecies $p$-wave interactions and a two-dimensional transverse optical lattice. The lattice drives a crossover from an anisotropic three-dimensional gas to weakly coupled one-dimensional tubes. We calculate the ground-state energy and quantum depletion at the Gaussian level and derive their limiting forms. The interspecies $p$-wave interaction modifies only the spin branch. In the parameter range studied, increasing its strength lowers the equilibrium density across the crossover, consistently with a weakening of the induced binding.

Abstract

We investigate the ground-state and finite-temperature properties of the kagome antiferromagnetic Heisenberg model with three inequivalent couplings, dubbed the 3J model, which is designed for the candidate Dirac quantum spin liquid (QSL) material YCu$_3$(OH)$_6$Br$_2$[Br$_{1-x}$(OH)$_x$] (see, e.g., Zeng et al., 2024). Employing large-scale density-matrix renormalization group (DMRG) supplemented by neural quantum states (NQS) simulations, we identify an intermediate QSL phase between two magnetically ordered phases. We also find that this QSL is separated from the kagome spin liquid ground state at the isotropic limit. To establish a direct comparison with experiments, we compute the specific heat of the model by means of advanced exponential (XTRG) and tangent-space (tanTRG) thermal tensor-network methods. In the magnetically ordered phase, the specific heat over temperature exhibits a shoulder at a temperature that is a fraction of the coupling strength $J_{hex}$, which disappears in the QSL phase. These universal behaviors are consistent with the experimentally observed specific heat in 3J materials for both ordered and QSL candidate samples. Our work thus connects microscopic models with experimentally measurable signatures, exemplifying an integrated approach (see Meng et al., 2026) to understanding QSL phenomena in frustrated quantum magnets, with 3J materials serving as a representative case and providing a foundation for future studies.

Summary. This work uses two-color high-harmonic spectroscopy on a 1D Mott insulator to observe ultrafast, sub-cycle dynamics of doublon-holon pairs. By measuring the chirp and temporal asymmetry of emitted harmonics, the authors confirm that the process is governed by d-h recombination and quantify the subsequent electronic dephasing. This provides a novel experimental window into non-equilibrium many-body physics in strongly correlated materials.

Why it may be interesting. The use of HHG spectroscopy to probe correlated electron dynamics provides a powerful, non-perturbative tool for studying non-equilibrium quantum phenomena, bridging concepts from quantum optics and condensed matter physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to directly measure and understand strongly driven, sub-optical-cycle dynamics within correlated materials, specifically focusing on doublon-holon recombination.

Main result. The experiment reveals coherent doublon-holon dynamics characterized by a positive chirp in high-harmonic emission, and quantifies ultrafast dephasing through harmonic order-dependent temporal asymmetry.

Method. The research employs two-color high-harmonic spectroscopy to probe the time-domain emission profile of even harmonics, complemented by semiclassical modeling of doublon-holon trajectories.

Important parameters / regimes. Sub-optical-cycle timescales, the two-color phase delay, and the characteristic dephasing length scale ($\sim 0.5$ lattice sites).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on semiclassical models for d-h trajectories, which neglect spinon dynamics and non-local interactions; the weak second harmonic is assumed to only perturb the process.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the experimental technique using two-color HHG to access sub-cycle dynamics, presents the semiclassical model for d-h motion, and then correlates the observed harmonic chirp and temporal asymmetry with the predicted recombination dynamics and subsequent dephasing.

Abstract

Solid-state high-harmonic spectroscopy is becoming an emerging tool for probing nonequilibrium many-body dynamics. Yet, direct measurements of strongly driven, sub-optical-cycle dynamics in correlated materials during high-harmonic emission remain largely unexplored. Here, we measure high-harmonic emission chirp in a prototypical one-dimensional Mott insulator, which encodes strongly driven doublon-holon dynamics at sub-optical-cycle timescales. We observe a positive chirp for above band gap harmonics, indicating that high harmonics are dominated by doublon-holon recombinations. We further show a harmonic order-dependent dephasing, which can be understood through different doublon-holon excursion distances associated with each harmonic. These results reveal coherent doublon-holon dynamics and their ultrafast dephasing in Mott insulators, which is relevant to other nonequilibrium light-induced phenomena, such as Floquet engineering.

Orientational order on non-orientable domains

Summary. This work analyzes how placing orientational spin systems on non-orientable manifolds, like the Klein bottle, fundamentally changes their statistical mechanics compared to standard surfaces like the torus. The key finding is that non-orientability causes 'topological caging,' which limits orientational fluctuations to a finite value, a robust effect seen in both passive and active matter models.

Why it may be interesting. The concept of topological constraints fundamentally altering equilibrium dynamics (caging) without requiring an explicit external field is a rich theoretical topic relevant to understanding complex phase behavior in condensed matter systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how the non-orientable topology of a domain, specifically the Klein bottle, alters the statistical properties and orientational fluctuations of many-body systems compared to standard orientable domains like the torus.

Main result. Non-orientability eliminates global rotational soft modes and induces 'topological caging' in passive systems, leading to finite saturation of orientational fluctuations, a state absent on the torus.

Method. The authors employ stochastic microscopic equations, mean-field theory, and spin-wave approximations, analyzing the system's dynamics by mapping topological constraints onto non-local symmetry relations on an orientable double-cover.

Model / system. The models include the passive XY spin model and active self-propelled particle systems (like the active Ising model) whose orientations are constrained on a Klein bottle geometry, contrasting with the torus geometry.

Key observables. Global orientation fluctuation $\langle\delta\Theta^2(t) angle$, polar order parameter $|M|$, and the spin polarization distribution $P(m_x, m_y)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The comparison between the torus and Klein bottle topologies, and the role of the non-local symmetry relations imposed by non-orientability.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies heavily on the spin-wave approximation for small angle variations and the mathematical mapping of topological constraints to symmetry relations on a double-cover.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the double-cover representation of the Klein bottle and compare the time evolution of orientation fluctuations ($\langle\delta\Phi^2 angle$) on both the torus and the Klein bottle, showing saturation on the latter.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the topological constraints via the double-cover, then analyzing the dynamics of passive XY models to show topological caging, and finally extending these findings to active particle models like the active Ising spin model.

Abstract

We study the statistical properties of passive and active many-body systems with orientational degrees of freedom on non-orientable domains. By rephrasing topological constraints as non-local symmetry relations on an orientable double-cover, we show that non-orientability eliminates global rotational soft modes without acting like an external field. In a passive XY model, this results in topological caging, where orientational fluctuations that exhibit conventional diffusive behavior on a torus saturate on a Klein bottle to a finite value that we compute exactly in the thermodynamic limit. In models of active self-propelled particles with orientational degrees of freedom, topological caging persists despite continuously changing interaction neighborhoods. In an active Ising spin model, non-orientability enforces the coexistence of ordered anti-parallel domains with vanishing global polar order, a state that is absent on orientable domains.
