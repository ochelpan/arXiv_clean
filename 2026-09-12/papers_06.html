A Unified Theory of Collective Magnon and Orbiton Excitations in Altermagnets

Authors: Bishal Das, Chanchal K. Barman, Aftab Alam
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10708v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper unifies the theoretical description of collective excitations (magnons and orbitons) in altermagnets by developing an extended spin-orbital model. It demonstrates that the observed spectral splitting arises from two distinct physical origins: lattice asymmetry and orbital ordering. The methodology combines mean-field theory with Monte Carlo simulations to provide a comprehensive understanding of the coupled spin-orbital dynamics.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a detailed, unified theoretical framework for understanding coupled spin-orbital excitations in magnetic materials, which is highly relevant to modern condensed matter physics research on emergent magnetism.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is developing a unified theoretical framework to describe the collective excitations (magnons and orbitons) in altermagnets, which arise from two distinct microscopic mechanisms: lattice asymmetry and orbital ordering.

Main result. The authors successfully develop a unified model showing that the characteristic chiral-splitting in the excitation spectra originates from two complementary contributions: one from lattice asymmetry and one from orbital-order-induced exchange anisotropy.

Method. The study employs an extended Kugel'-Khomskiĭ spin-orbital model analyzed using a self-consistent mean-field spin-wave orbital-wave formalism, complemented by classical Monte Carlo simulations.

Model / system. The system is an altermagnet modeled on a decorated square lattice, described by an extended anisotropic Heisenberg Hamiltonian coupling spin and orbital degrees of freedom. The model incorporates both inequivalent non-magnetic environments and correlation-driven orbital ordering.

Key observables. Magnon and orbiton excitation spectra, chiral-splitting magnitude, and finite-temperature population densities ($\varrho_A, \varrho_B$).

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature ($T$), lattice coupling constants ($J, K$), and the degree of orbital/spin ordering.

Assumptions / limitations. The mean-field approach assumes the decoupling of spin and orbital sectors, and the analysis often neglects full quantum fluctuations in favor of mean-field or classical Monte Carlo treatments.

Figures summary. The notes detail the derivation of symmetry relations for Bogolyubov coefficients and the calculation of temperature-dependent population densities for magnon and orbiton modes.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining the physical problem and model Hamiltonian, to applying mean-field theory to derive excitation spectra, analyzing symmetry properties using Bogolyubov transformations, and finally investigating finite-temperature behavior using both analytical methods and Monte Carlo simulations.

Abstract

Altermagnetism has recently emerged as a distinct collinear magnetic phase exhibiting momentum-dependent spin-splitting despite vanishing net magnetization, as a consequence of inequivalent non-magnetic environments. Lately, it has been proposed that strong electronic correlations may yield spontaneous altermagnetism due to orbital ordering even for equivalent non-magnetic environments. While previous studies have largely focused on the electronic structure, a unified understanding of the collective excitations associated with these two different microscopic mechanisms stabilizing altermagnetism remains absent. Here, we develop an extended Kugel'-Khomskiĭ spin-orbital model on a decorated square lattice that simultaneously incorporates inequivalent non-magnetic environments and correlation-driven orbital ordering within a common theoretical framework. Employing a self-consistent mean-field spin-wave orbital-wave formalism, we demonstrate the emergence of mutually unhybridized but interdependent magnon and orbiton excitations exhibiting characteristic chiral-splitting. We show that the splitting originates from two distinct microscopic contributions: a lattice-dependent term arising from inequivalent non-magnetic environments and an orbital-order-induced exchange-anisotropy term that survives even for equivalent non-magnetic environments. The proposed framework therefore unifies the collective excitations associated with both types of altermagnetism. We further investigate the finite-temperature evolution of the coupled spin-orbital system, revealing the breakdown of spin-wave and orbital-wave approximations through spurious 1st-order transitions, while complementary classical Monte Carlo simulations recover the expected continuous 2nd-order behaviour. Our work establishes a unified microscopic framework for understanding collective magnon and orbiton excitations in altermagnets.

Summary. This paper constructs a family of Absolutely Maximally Entangled (AME) quantum states for dimensions $q$ that are odd prime powers. The construction is deeply rooted in algebraic coding theory, utilizing Hermitian self-dual MDS codes over finite fields. These states are significant because they represent extreme entanglement useful for quantum error correction and defining optimal quantum resources.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous construction of highly entangled states like AME states provides fundamental resources for quantum error correction and quantum computation, pushing the boundaries of known entanglement bounds.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is the construction of Absolutely Maximally Entangled (AME) states, which represent an extreme form of multipartite entanglement.

Main result. The authors construct stabilizer AME$(2q, q)$ states for every odd prime power $q=p^e$, and derive corresponding AME$(2q-1, q)$ states.

Method. The construction heavily relies on generating Hermitian self-dual Maximum Distance Separable (MDS) codes using bordered-circulant matrices over finite fields $\mathbb{F}_{q^2}$.

Model / system. The system is a pure quantum state $|\Psi angle \in (\mathbb{C}^d)^{\otimes N}$ defined by its entanglement properties, specifically requiring its reduced density matrix to be maximally mixed for small subsets of parties.

Key observables. The entanglement structure, quantified by the maximal mixing of reduced density matrices, and the associated quantum code parameters (e.g., $[2q, q, q+1]_{q^2}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The dimension $q$ must be an odd prime power ($q=p^e, p ext{ odd prime}$). The state parameters are $(N, d) = (2q, q)$ and $(2q-1, q)$.

Assumptions / limitations. The construction relies on specific algebraic properties within finite fields ($\mathbb{F}_{q^2}$) and the existence of certain matrices satisfying specific structural equations.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the existence status of AME states for various parameters, marking the newly constructed cases. Table I collects explicit examples of parent states and their one-party projections.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by defining the AME property, linking it to MDS codes via specific matrices ($A_q$), deriving the explicit construction for $AME(2q, q)$, and then showing the reduction to $AME(2q-1, q)$ via measurement.

Abstract

Absolutely maximally entangled (AME) states represent an extreme form of multipartite entanglement: every reduced system containing at most half of the parties is maximally mixed. These states provide perfect tensors and optimal quantum error-correcting codes, yet their existence is known only in restricted parameter regimes. For every odd prime power $q=p^e\ge3$, we construct a stabilizer $\mathrm{AME}(2q,q)$ state whose normalized one-party projection yields a stabilizer $\mathrm{AME}(2q-1,q)$ state. A closed-form $q\times q$ bordered-circulant matrix $A_q$ over $\mathbb{F}_{q^2}$ generates a Hermitian self-dual maximum distance separable (MDS) code $[2q,q,q+1]_{q^2}$, which lies outside the extended Reed-Solomon classes. In suitable bases, the amplitude tensors define normalized $q$-unitary complex Hadamard matrices of order $q^q$ with $p$th-root phases. Additional constructions yield $\mathrm{AME}(q+3,q)$ states and families at intermediate particle numbers through explicit rescalings of selected submatrices. We also provide nine explicit parent matrices and the corresponding one-party projections.

Summary. This theoretical paper proves a significant rigidity result for finite-mode Gaussian states, showing that the complex concept of full inseparability is mathematically equivalent to Genuine Multipartite Entanglement (GME). The authors achieve this by employing advanced techniques from functional analysis and quantum information theory, demonstrating that the structure of Gaussian states prevents entanglement from being activated by mixing over multiple potential separable cuts.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides deep structural constraints on entanglement for Gaussian states, which are highly relevant in quantum optics and continuous-variable quantum information processing, by showing that complex mixed-state entanglement measures collapse to simpler, fixed-partition tests.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates whether full inseparability (entanglement across every bipartition) and Genuine Multipartite Entanglement (GME) coincide for finite-mode Gaussian states, especially when considering convex decompositions over multiple partitions.

Main result. For finite-mode Gaussian states, the authors prove that full inseparability and GME are equivalent, demonstrating that partition mixing adds no new exact Gaussian states.

Method. The proof combines spectral selection techniques with holomorphic rigidity arguments, relating local product structures in the state's spectral range to global separability conditions.

Model / system. The study focuses on finite-mode Gaussian states of bosonic modes distributed among multiple parties. Separability is analyzed using the trace-norm-closed convex hull of product projectors ($ ext{Sep}_F$).

Key observables. Covariance matrices, entanglement depth, fixed-partition separability criteria (LMIs), and the existence of product vectors in the square-root range of the state.

Important parameters / regimes. Finiteness of modes and parties; the exponent $s$ used in Gaussian powers ($ ho^s$); the specific structure of the Gaussian state.

Assumptions / limitations. The target density operator is assumed to be an exact finite-mode Gaussian state. The analysis is restricted to finite numbers of parties and modes.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates convex partition rigidity for a three-party system, showing Gaussian members of $ ext{Sep}_F$ are constrained to a single fixed-partition set. Figure 2 outlines the proof roadmap involving Gaussian powers and product-range rigidity.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the general rigidity theorem for Gaussian states ($ ext{G} \cap ext{Sep}F = ext{G} \cap igcup ext{Sep}\pi$), then applies this to the bipartition case to prove the equivalence between full inseparability and GME, relying on spectral selection and holomorphic rigidity.

Abstract

For general mixed states, entanglement across every bipartition need not imply genuine multipartite entanglement (GME), because a biseparable decomposition may switch the separable cut from term to term. We prove that this convex ambiguity disappears for Gaussian states of finitely many bosonic modes. More generally, for any finite family of partitions, a Gaussian density operator in the trace-norm-closed convex class generated by states separable across those partitions is already separable across one fixed partition in the family. Only the target is Gaussian; a valid decomposition may be continuous and may contain arbitrary non-Gaussian states. Thus full inseparability and GME coincide, Gaussian k-separability and k-producibility reduce to fixed-partition tests, and party-wise tensor powers cannot activate GME from a biseparable Gaussian state. The proof combines a spectral selector with a holomorphic rigidity argument that converts one product vector in the square-root range of a Gaussian state into a block-local covariance certificate. The result shows that partition mixing, a generic mixed-state mechanism, adds no new exact finite-mode Gaussian states.

Generative Replay Mitigates Sample Starvation in Quantum Architecture Search

Authors: Akash Kundu, Amit Kumar Jaiswal, Sebastian Feld, Prayag Tiwari
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11248v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

Summary. This paper addresses the sample starvation problem in Quantum Architecture Search (QAS) by introducing GenQAS. This method enhances reinforcement learning by using a learned model to generate synthetic, yet physically informed, circuit transitions. The results demonstrate that this generative replay significantly boosts the efficiency and success rate of discovering optimal quantum circuits for chemistry and condensed matter simulations.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a concrete, scalable ML technique to overcome fundamental data scarcity issues in quantum algorithm design, which is crucial for simulating complex molecular or material properties.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Reinforcement learning for Quantum Architecture Search (QAS) suffers from sample starvation as the search space grows, making it difficult to find optimal quantum circuits.

Main result. The proposed GenQAS framework significantly improves the success probability of finding low-error quantum circuits across various qubit counts, showing up to a 7.0x improvement at 12 qubits.

Method. It introduces GenQAS, a tensor network-guided RL framework that augments standard replay buffers by using a learned local transition model to generate synthetic, on-demand circuit transitions.

Model / system. The system involves constructing parameterized quantum circuits (PQC) to approximate the ground state of chemical Hamiltonians (e.g., $ ext{BeH}_2$, transverse field Ising model) using qubits.

Key observables. Success probability, energy error, and the useful-transition density ($ ho$).

Important parameters / regimes. Qubit count (6 to 15), generation ratio ($G_r$), and the depth of the circuit.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the synthetic transitions generated by the learned model remain sufficiently consistent with the real environment to support stable off-policy learning.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate how GenQAS converts passive replay into active experience generation, showing the limitations of passive replay versus the enhanced training signal from generative replay.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the sample starvation problem in QAS, then details the GenQAS methodology combining MPS warm-starts with generative replay, and finally presents quantitative results across multiple chemical and Ising model benchmarks.

Abstract

Reinforcement learning (RL) can automate quantum architecture search, but its scalability is limited when useful circuit trajectories become rare in the rapidly expanding search space. Existing replay mechanisms reuse observed transitions; the proposed learned model produces additional predicted one step transitions from real state-action seeds. Here we introduce GenQAS, a tensor network-guided RL framework that combines a fixed matrix product state warm-start with prioritized generative replay. A learned local transition model generates synthetic circuit transitions on demand and mixes them with real experience during Double Deep Q-Network updates. Under a random exploration analysis, near ground state circuits occupy a rapidly shrinking region of the accessible state space. We investigate whether real data anchored synthetic replay can improve the effective training signal in this regime. Across chemical Hamiltonian benchmarks from 6 to 12 qubits, GenQAS improves fixed-budget success probability and identifies compact circuits at competitive energy error. At 12 qubits, it improves final success probability by up to $7.0\times$ over passive replay. On a 15-qubit transverse field Ising model, GenQAS increases success probability from $12\%$ to $21\%$. In a noisy 6-qubit BeH$_2$ transfer experiment, generative replay reduces the steps to chemical accuracy by $92.7\%$. These results show that generative replay can mitigate sample starvation in quantum architecture search and support more resource efficient circuit discovery.

Summary. This work elucidates the mechanism behind record high Curie temperatures in (Fe,Ni)GeTe2 by demonstrating that the magnetism originates from strain-stabilized Fe6GeTe2 nano-precipitates. Using a combination of advanced spectroscopy and DFT, the authors show that site-specific electronic filling dictates the magnetic ground state, providing a roadmap for engineering 2D magnetic metals.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a detailed, atomistic understanding of how structural phase separation and site-selective electronic filling can stabilize magnetic phases at record high temperatures, offering a blueprint for designing novel magnetic materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to unravel the origin of record high Curie temperature ferromagnetism in the metallic van der Waals magnet (Fe,Ni)GeTe2, which is complicated by structural complexity and mixed magnetic contributions.

Main result. The high-$T_C$ phase is attributed to a strain-stabilized Fe6GeTe2 nano-precipitate, whose electronic structure features localized moments on interior Fe atoms and itinerant carriers on outer Fe atoms.

Method. The research combines advanced experimental techniques like ARPES, XMCD, and STEM with first-principles DFT calculations to map out site-specific electronic and magnetic properties.

Model / system. The system is the ternary chalcogenide vdW magnet (Fe,Ni)GeTe2, which exhibits phase separation into distinct Fe-rich and Ni-rich structural domains.

Abstract

The discovery of high-temperature ferromagnetism in the metallic van der Waals (vdW) system Fe$_N$GeTe$_2$ has brought two-dimensional (2D) magnets into technologically relevant temperature scales. Specifically at N = 5, dilution of magnetic moments by nickel substitution counterintuitively achieves a record high Curie temperature of 478~K. Unraveling the origin of this nickel-substitution-induced enhancement is complicated by the compound's structural complexity, coexistent itinerant and local magnetic contributions, and mesoscopic compositional domains. Through coordinated structural and electronic characterization, we identify that the high-T$_C$ magnetic phase arises from a strain-stabilized Fe$_6$GeTe$_2$ nano-precipitate. Combining first-principles calculations and spin- and angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES), we uncover a site-specific electronic landscape in which interior iron atoms primarily host localized moments while the outer iron atoms neighboring the tellurium layers produce spin-polarized itinerant carriers that cross the vdW gap. The large energy cost associated with homogeneous nickel substitution is found to favor the spontaneous precipitation of the crystallographically and electronically ``clean'' high-T$_C$ phase. Finally, we compare metal-rich vdW magnets with binary magnetic alloys, and discuss the unifying roles of nano-precipitates in stabilizing otherwise unattainable bulk phases. Our work provides mechanistic insights into the record-high T$_C$ ferromagnetism in (Fe,Ni)$_{5+δ}$GeTe$_2$, establishing a rigorous foundation for the atomic engineering of vdW magnetic metals informed by direct electronic signatures.

Summary. This theoretical work introduces 'hyperspin' to overcome the limitations of studying non-collinear magnets. It proposes a new class, Hyperspin Altermagnets (HAMs), which are characterized by a conserved 'hyperspin' quantum number rather than the standard spin. This framework provides a deeper understanding of magnetic order beyond simple collinear models.

Why it may be interesting. The concept of 'hyperspin' provides a powerful, generalized mathematical tool to classify magnetic order beyond simple spin conservation, which is relevant for understanding complex quantum many-body systems where standard symmetries fail.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the limitations in characterizing non-collinear magnets, which lack well-defined spin quantum numbers, by introducing the concept of 'hyperspin'.

Main result. It classifies non-collinear magnets exhibiting alternating spin-split bands in k-space as Hyperspin Altermagnets (HAMs), distinguished from collinear Altermagnets (AMs) by the existence of a conserved 'hyperspin' quantum number.

Method. The analysis involves examining the commutation relations between the Hamiltonian and generalized spin operators ($\hat{S}$ vs. $\hat{J}$) to identify conserved quantities that dictate the magnetic behavior.

Model / system. The study models magnetic systems, comparing collinear AMs with non-collinear HAMs, often built by merging multiple Antiferromagnetic (AFM) subsystems under $\Theta au$ symmetry.

Key observables. Alternating spin-split bands in k-space, the existence of a conserved hyperspin quantum number ($\hat{J}_y$), and exotic spin current generation effects.

Important parameters / regimes. The symmetry $\Theta au$ is crucial for the emergence of HAMs; the Hamiltonian is analyzed in k-space using matrix diagonalization.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the applicability of a generalized Hamiltonian and relies on symmetry constraints ($\Theta au$) to define the new conserved quantity.

Figures summary. Figure 1 compares AMs and HAMs regarding band structures and spin current generation. Figure 2 illustrates building a HAM by merging two AFM lattices related by $\Theta au$. Table I summarizes the symmetry requirements for AMs, AFMs, and HAMs.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the problem with non-collinear magnets, then develops the concept of hyperspin ($\hat{J}$) via commutation relation analysis. It applies this framework to construct and analyze HAMs, deriving the k-space Hamiltonian and demonstrating the resulting collinear spin splitting.

Abstract

The behavior of spin quantum in k-space is key to identifying altermagnets (AMs) as the third kind of fundamental collinear magnetism. In contrast, non-collinear magnets,though abundant in nature,lack well-defined spin quantum numbers, and the resulting spin textures are often highly complex, which limits their potential for next-generation spintronic applications. Here we propose hyperspin, which lives in a higher-dimensional space, to address these drawbacks. Through analyzing the commutation relations between spin and Hamiltonian for a class of non-collinear magnets, we reveal it is a hyperspin, rather than the usual spin, that commutes with Hamiltonian. Unexpectedly, these non-collinear magnets should also show collinear spin-split bands in k-space like collinear AMs. We therefore classify such non-collinear magnets as hyperspin altermagnets (HAMs), as opposed to the usual collinear AMs. Our theory elucidates the fundamental physics of AMs and HAMs and provides a framework for exploring the wide range of non-collinear magnets that may possess other kinds of conserved quantities.

Abstract

We construct the first fully spatially local fault-tolerant quantum computer based on topological codes in fewer than four spatial dimensions. Our construction is a two-dimensional architecture that uses only geometrically local quantum and classical operations, bounded-speed classical communication and computation, and a constant density of quantum and classical resources. The core component is a new time-translation-invariant cellular-automaton decoder for the surface code. This decoder preserves logical information for a time stretched-exponential in the code distance and operates continuously during state injection, stabilizer-state preparation, lattice surgery, and transversal readout. We also prove that every translation-invariant topological Pauli stabilizer code is locally decodable under phenomenological noise.

Abstract

The execution of useful quantum algorithms on fault-tolerant processors requires more than a mapping from logical gates to encoded operations: the spatial organization, non-Clifford resource supply, and execution schedule must also be determined while keeping physical overhead within practical limits. Although the theoretical hierarchy from logical circuits to fault-tolerant operations is well established, these implementation choices are often specified and optimized separately. Here we develop a low-cost algorithm-to-execution framework for surface-code quantum computing. From hierarchical algorithm descriptions, it constructs dependency-preserving logical schedules and an executable workload capturing logical interactions, operation parallelism, and time-resolved non-Clifford demand, thereby linking logical computation to surface-code organization, resource-state preparation, and fault-tolerant execution in a traceable workflow. Circuit-specific surface-code layouts reduce routed-latency estimates for all twenty benchmarks; thirteen also reduce space-time volume because communication savings outweigh added spatial overhead. These results show that low-cost fault-tolerant execution depends on computation scheduling and organization, not aggregate logical resource counts alone.

Abstract

Abstract

The application of a time-varying strain field drives a superconducting order parameter out of equilibrium. How the order parameter relaxes back to equilibrium depends both on the structure of the superconducting gap and on the nature of quasiparticle scattering. We report the discovery of an ultrasonic attenuation peak inside the superconducting state of the kagome superconductor CsV$_3$Sb$_5$. This peak is the natural consequence of the order parameter relaxation time matching the ultrasonic drive frequency near $T_{\rm c}$. From the measured frequency dependence of the peak, we extract a microscopic scattering time of $τ_N = 25$ ps. This timescale is two orders of magnitude longer than the elastic scattering time as determined by resistivity measurements, but is comparable to the inelastic scattering time determined by thermal transport. Within the conventional framework of order-parameter relaxation, this implies that elastic scattering is ineffective at relaxing the superconducting condensate, consistent with a sign-preserving $s$-wave state obeying Anderson's theorem.
