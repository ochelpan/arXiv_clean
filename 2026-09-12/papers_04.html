Recurrence and transience of random walks with drift $ρx^α/t^β$

Summary. This paper rigorously analyzes the recurrence and transience of a random walk whose drift term is time and position-dependent ($ ho x^\alpha/t^eta$). By employing advanced stochastic methods like martingale theory, the authors establish complete classification criteria for the process's long-term behavior under weaker assumptions than previously required. This provides a powerful mathematical tool for understanding diffusion processes in complex, non-stationary environments.

Why it may be interesting. While the core subject is random walk theory, the rigorous mathematical techniques involving martingale analysis and boundary crossing probabilities are highly relevant to analyzing non-equilibrium dynamics and stochastic processes in condensed matter physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper determines the recurrence or transience of a random walk process whose conditional drift depends on both time and position in the form $ ho x^\alpha/t^eta$.

Main result. It establishes comprehensive recurrence and transience criteria for the process across various parameter regimes of $\alpha$ and $eta$, improving upon previous results by weakening assumptions.

Method. The analysis employs martingale theory, supermartingale arguments, and the construction of specific stopping times to analyze the long-term behavior of the process.

Model / system. The model is a discrete-time, $\mathbb{R}^+$-valued random walk $(X_t)_{t\in\mathbb{Z}^+}$ whose conditional drift is governed by the term $ ho x^\alpha/t^eta$. The analysis focuses on the process's tendency to return to a bounded region or escape to infinity.

Key observables. Recurrence (hitting a point infinitely often) and Transience (diverging to infinity), analyzed via the process's position $X_t$ and related quantities like $Y_t = X_t^2/t$.

Important parameters / regimes. The parameters are $\alpha$ and $eta$ governing the drift, with critical regimes defined by $2eta - \alpha = 1$ and the general scope being $-1 < \alpha < 1$ and $eta \ge 0$.

Assumptions / limitations. The proofs rely on hypotheses regarding the boundedness of conditional second moments of increments (H1) and lower bounds on these moments (H2), and the process is analyzed without assuming the Markov property.

Paper structure. The paper develops the argument by first establishing recurrence criteria using supermartingale properties (Theorem 1.1), followed by proving transience criteria using bounds on hitting probabilities and constructing sequences of stopping times (Theorem 1.2).

Abstract

Menshikov and Volkov [Electron. J. Probab. 13 (2008)] studied recurrence and transience of a class of Markovian random walks on $\mathbb R_+$ whose conditional drift depends on both time and position and is of order $ρx^αt^{-β}$ with $ρ>0$. The case on the critical line $2β-α=1$, with $α\in(-1,1)\setminus\{0\}$, remained open. We prove recurrence in this remaining case. Furthermore, we establish recurrence and transience criteria that complete the classification for $-1<α<1$ and $β\ge 0$, without assuming the Markov property and under weaker assumptions on the increments than those imposed by Menshikov and Volkov.

Universal sampling of spin systems across quenched disorder

Authors: Jing Liu, Yeyuan Wu, Ying Tang, Pan Zhang
Type: theory · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11336v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 2/5 · analog quantum simulation 2/5 · methods for driven-dissipative 2/5

Summary. This paper presents a novel, amortized neural variational framework using Transformers to calculate thermodynamic properties of disordered spin systems. By training once on the disorder ensemble rather than per realization, it overcomes the prohibitive computational cost of traditional methods. This breakthrough enables accurate, scalable simulations of complex, frustrated many-body systems previously inaccessible to variational inference.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical spin systems, the methodology of using deep generative models (Transformers) to efficiently sample complex, high-dimensional probability distributions conditioned on system parameters (disorder) is highly relevant for simulating quantum many-body states or quantum field theories where disorder is present.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is evaluating quenched thermodynamic observables in disordered spin systems, which requires averaging over the disorder realization itself, a task difficult for standard variational methods.

Main result. The authors introduce a universal neural variational framework that amortizes inference across the entire disorder ensemble, allowing for zero-shot sampling on unseen disorder realizations.

Method. They employ an encoder-decoder Transformer architecture where the encoder processes the disorder couplings $\{J\}$ to generate site embeddings, which condition a causal Transformer decoder to sample the spin configuration $s$.

Model / system. The work focuses on disordered spin systems, specifically the 2D Edwards-Anderson model and the random-bond Ising model, governed by a Hamiltonian involving quenched random couplings $J_{ij}$.

Key observables. Variational free energy, Binder cumulant crossings, and ground-state energies.

Important parameters / regimes. Inverse temperature ($eta$), system size ($L$), and the nature of the disorder (bimodal vs. Gaussian couplings).

Assumptions / limitations. The framework assumes that the disorder couplings can be processed by the encoder to create a universal representation $\{h_i\}$ that governs the sampling process.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the conceptual flow of the disorder-conditioned neural Boltzmann sampling framework, showing training dynamics, thermal generalization, and extrapolation across system sizes.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the computational bottleneck of per-instance training for disordered systems. It then introduces the amortized Transformer architecture, details its training on various disorder types and temperatures, and validates its performance by calculating key physical observables like the Binder cumulant.

Abstract

Statistical physics extracts macroscopic laws by averaging over the many microscopic degrees of freedom of a system. Disordered systems demand a second and far harder average, one over the quenched randomness itself. The classic analytical routes, the replica and cavity methods, become uncontrolled outside mean-field or tree-like limits, and conventional numerical algorithms like parallel tempering require expensive, independent equilibration for every disorder realization. In this work, we introduce a universal neural variational framework that amortizes inference across the disorder ensemble, eliminating both the need for per-instance Markov chain equilibration and the cost of retraining instance-specific variational ansatzes. Built on an encoder-decoder Transformer architecture, after training once, it produces an explicit approximation to the Boltzmann distribution given previously unseen disorder realizations without further optimization. We validate this framework on 2D Edwards-Anderson models, and apply it to the random-bond Ising model, successfully capturing the Binder cumulant crossings near the Nishimori multicritical point. These results shift the object of variational inference from the single instance to the disorder ensemble, opening a route to frustrated many-body systems where instance-by-instance computation is prohibitive.

Summary. This work develops a systematic, analytical framework to translate the structure of continuum quantum operators into the discrete language of qubits using Walsh-Pauli representations. By employing generating functions, the authors determine the spectrum and resource cost associated with operators like the momentum operator, even when subjected to nonlinear deformations such as GUP kinematics. Why it may be interesting. It provides a rigorous, analytical tool to estimate the quantum resources needed to simulate complex physical Hamiltonians (like those involving GUP) by analyzing the spectral structure of their momentum components.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses how the structure of a continuum operator, such as the momentum operator, is faithfully represented and characterized within a finite qubit register.

Main result. A generating-function framework is developed to analytically determine the Walsh-Pauli spectrum of analytic functions of the JLP momentum operator, revealing exact support and parity selection rules.

Model / system. The model centers on the Jordan-Lee-Preskill (JLP) construction, which discretizes continuum degrees of freedom onto a finite qubit register. A controlled application is the Generalized Uncertainty Principle (GUP) kinematics, treated as a nonlinear deformation of momentum.

Key observables. Walsh-Pauli spectrum, Pauli-weight structure, Walsh compressibility, and estimated quantum circuit resource costs (e.g., CNOT gates).

Important parameters / regimes. The number of qubits ($n_q$) defining the finite register size, and the deformation parameters characterizing the GUP.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is restricted to diagonal functions of a single JLP momentum register, and the method provides an upper bound for circuit costs.

Paper structure. The paper builds from establishing the continuum-to-qubit mapping, developing the generating-function formalism for the spectrum, deriving selection rules (support/parity), and finally applying the framework to analyze physical deformations like GUP kinematics.

Abstract

How does continuum operator structure appear in a finite qubit register? We address this question for analytic functions of diagonal operators represented in a binary basis, focusing on the Jordan--Lee--Preskill (JLP) momentum operator, by developing an operator-level generating-function framework for their Walsh-Pauli spectra. The construction determines the spectrum analytically and reveals exact support and parity selection rules, together with a nontrivial intra-shell hierarchy induced by the binary encoding. This provides a systematic characterization of Walsh compressibility and its relation to Pauli locality. As a controlled application, we consider generalized uncertainty-principle (GUP) kinematics as a tunable nonlinear odd deformation of momentum, showing how higher-order momentum terms redistribute spectral weight into progressively higher Pauli-weight sectors

Quantifying Symmetry Breaking

Authors: Koji Yamaguchi, Hiroyasu Tajima
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11926v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5 · Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5 · analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work develops a comprehensive resource theory approach to quantify symmetry breaking in mixed quantum states. It establishes that the optimal conversion rate is governed not by a single measure, but by an entire continuous family of Quantum Fisher Information matrices. This reveals a fundamental difference between pure-state and mixed-state resource quantification.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a rigorous, quantitative framework for understanding how symmetry constraints dictate the fundamental limits of quantum information processing, which has implications for characterizing non-equilibrium processes in many-body systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to provide a complete, operational characterization for quantifying symmetry breaking in general mixed quantum states within the i.i.d. asymptotic limit.

Main result. It establishes a single-letter formula for the optimal conversion rate between arbitrary mixed states, determined by an entire one-parameter family of Quantum Fisher Information (QFI) matrices.

Method. The analysis uses quantum resource theories, extending quantum local asymptotic normality (QLAN) to complex unitary models and characterizing convertibility via the continuous QFI family.

Model / system. The study concerns quantum systems in finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces subjected to compact Lie group symmetries, analyzing the conversion of many copies of input states ($ ho^{\otimes n}$) to output states ($ ho'^{\otimes m}$).

Key observables. Asymmetry measure, Quantum Fisher Information (QFI) matrices (interpolating SLD and RLD), and the generalized quantum geometric tensor (QGT).

Important parameters / regimes. The asymptotic regime (i.i.d.), the parameter $q$ defining the QFI family, and the group structure of the symmetry $G$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is restricted to the i.i.d. asymptotic regime, and the proof relies on extending QLAN to arbitrary rank unitary models.

Figures summary. Figure 1 summarizes the findings, contrasting the single-measure characterization for pure-state conversion (using QGT) with the full QFI family required for general mixed-state conversion.

Paper structure. The paper builds from the general problem of resource quantification, develops the necessary mathematical tools (like extended QLAN), derives the single-letter formula using the continuous QFI family, and then specializes the results to pure-state distillation and asymmetry activation.

Abstract

Quantifying properties of quantum states through the limits of their manipulation is a central goal of quantum resource theories. For symmetry breaking, the quantum geometric tensor governs asymptotic pure-state conversion, but a complete characterization for general mixed states has remained elusive. Here we fully resolve this problem for finite-dimensional systems under compact Lie group symmetries in the i.i.d. asymptotic regime. Specifically, we establish a single-letter formula for the optimal conversion rate between arbitrary states, with vanishing trace-distance error, in the resource theory of asymmetry. The rate is determined by a one-parameter family of quantum Fisher information (QFI) matrices that interpolates between the symmetric- and right-logarithmic-derivative QFIs. No state-independent finite subset of this family suffices in general, even for $U(1)$ symmetry, revealing a qualitative distinction from pure-state conversion. Our formula further yields an exact formula for pure-state distillation rates in terms of the generalized quantum geometric tensor, characterizes asymptotically reversible interconversion, and identifies bound asymmetry for quantum clocks. Complementarity among different members of the QFI family also uncovers an activation mechanism for quantum clocks. Our proof relies on two developments of independent interest. First, we extend quantum local asymptotic normality to unitary models with arbitrary rank and spectral degeneracy. Second, we characterize convertibility between quantum Gaussian shift models in terms of the same one-parameter family of QFIs. Together, these results provide an operational characterization of symmetry breaking for general quantum states in the i.i.d. asymptotic regime and reveal how distinct QFI constraints give rise to irreversibility and activation.

Timelike Entanglement from Spacetime Density Matrices: A Lattice Realization

Summary. This work establishes a lattice realization for quantifying timelike entanglement in QFT using spacetime density matrices. By analyzing the spectrum of the reduced density matrix, the authors show that entanglement entropy moments precisely match predictions from Lorentzian twist-operator correlation functions. This provides a powerful, microscopic tool for studying quantum correlations across time separations.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous lattice foundation for timelike entanglement provides a concrete, computable framework for understanding non-local quantum correlations in time, which is crucial for understanding dynamics in open or time-dependent quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the concept of timelike entanglement in Quantum Field Theory (QFT) by analyzing reduced spacetime density matrices, extending entanglement measures beyond standard equal-time spatial cuts.

Main result. The authors provide a microscopic lattice realization for timelike entanglement, showing that Rényi entropy moments derived from the reduced density matrix match Lorentzian twist-operator correlation functions, even in regimes where reality is not guaranteed by causal disconnection.

Method. The analysis uses Gaussian diagonalization techniques applied to the generally non-Hermitian reduced spacetime density matrix, determining its spectrum via a characteristic polynomial, and relating its moments to correlation functions using the real-time replica construction.

Model / system. The study is performed on a free real scalar field, modeled on a lattice of coupled harmonic oscillators. The formalism is extended to analyze boundary effects (Periodic, Dirichlet, Neumann) and massive integrable QFTs.

Key observables. Reduced spacetime density matrix ($T_{A_0 B_t}$), Rényi entropy ($S_n$), von Neumann entropy ($S$), and Lorentzian twist-operator correlation functions (four-point and two-point functions).

Important parameters / regimes. Time separation ($t$), lattice size ($L$), mass scale ($\xi \sim m^{-1}$), and boundary conditions (DD, NN, PBC).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is initially restricted to the vacuum state of a free scalar field, and the methods rely on extending Gaussian diagonalization to non-Hermitian operators.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the schematic setup for the reduced density matrix on a circle and a strip, and show the evolution of Rényi entropies for various boundary conditions (DD, NN, DN) as a function of time separation.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by defining the reduced spacetime density matrix, calculating its spectrum and Rényi moments, establishing the connection to Lorentzian twist-operator correlators, and finally testing this identification against various boundary conditions and limits.

Abstract

We investigate timelike entanglement in quantum field theory using spacetime density matrices and provide a microscopic lattice realization. For a two-dimensional free real scalar field, we extend Gaussian diagonalization methods to the generally non-Hermitian reduced spacetime density matrix and determine its complete nonzero spectrum in the generic regular case, together with all integer Rényi moments. The real-time replica construction identifies these moments with Lorentzian branch-point twist-operator correlation functions. We test this identification against the full four-point function on a circle, boundary two-point functions with Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions, and massive form-factor predictions, finding quantitative agreement in both magnitude and phase across distinct causal regimes. The boundary setup exhibits a finite causally connected window in which every integer Rényi entropy is real, showing that reality is not equivalent to causal disconnection. These results provide a microscopic lattice foundation for timelike entanglement and for Lorentzian twist-operator methods beyond equal-time regions.

It details advanced spectroscopic techniques like VCD, ROA, and circularly polarized Raman scattering. The work is crucial for harnessing the unique spin-lattice coupling enabled by these phonons in emerging fields like spintronics and quantum information.

Why it may be interesting. The focus on spin-lattice coupling and the ability to probe chirality using optical methods makes it highly relevant for understanding quantum phenomena in condensed matter systems, bridging optics and lattice dynamics.

Model / system. The study covers chiral and achiral materials, ranging from crystalline solids like $\alpha$-quartz to 2D perovskites and quantum magnets. Chirality can be inherent or induced transiently via interactions with photons or magnons.

Abstract

Chiral phonons are quantized vibrations where the atomic motion in a solid breaks improper rotation symmetries. In many cases, chiral phonons possess angular momenta and are therefore selective to circularly polarized light. Both fundamental and applied research efforts on chiral phonons have been gaining increasing attention owing to their importance in a variety of fields including spintronics, spin-selective chemical reactions, thermal transport, quantum information processing and biosensing, where the bi-directional spin-lattice coupling enabled by chiral phonons can be harnessed in new ways, and potentially lead to new functionalities. Thus far, the studies of chiral phonons across diverse materials platforms have evolved largely independently within these fields, but the experimental techniques are often interrelated. In this perspective, we present a detailed description, as well as advantages and disadvantages of the current approaches for experimentally measuring chiral phonons in chiral and achiral materials. We conclude with a discussion of new methods for measuring chiral phonons. Ultimately, this work seeks to offer an experimental guide for systematically investigating the properties of chiral phonons in various materials systems and applications.

Robust Spin-1 Haldane Topology under Itinerant Doping

Summary. This paper theoretically investigates how the spin-1 Haldane topological phase persists when holes are doped into the system. Using DMRG and Bosonization, the authors map out the phase diagram, showing that the topological order remains robust while the system develops a metallic character. The study reveals complex doping-dependent physics, including the formation of a reentrant ferromagnetic phase at high doping.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a detailed theoretical understanding of how topological order (Haldane SPT) can survive and reorganize in the presence of mobile charge carriers, which is crucial for understanding doped quantum magnets and correlated materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the robustness and evolution of the spin-1 Haldane symmetry-protected topological (SPT) phase when the system is subjected to itinerant hole doping.

Main result. The doped Haldane SPT remains stable over a broad doping range, characterized by a metallic low-energy description, and its phase diagram reveals a reentrant ferromagnetic pocket at high doping.

Method. The research employs advanced numerical techniques, primarily Density-Matrix Renormalization Group (DMRG) calculations, supplemented by Bosonization analysis to characterize the electronic and spin correlations.

Model / system. The model consists of two t-J chains coupled by a ferromagnetic (FM) Hund exchange interaction, allowing for the study of charge and spin degrees of freedom under doping ($\delta$).

Key observables. String Order Parameter (SOP), bulk spin gap ($\Delta_s$), entanglement spectrum, and various correlation functions like hole correlations ($g_{hh}(r)$) and defect-spin correlations ($G_{dd}(r)$).

Important parameters / regimes. Doping ($\delta$), Hund coupling ($J_H$), and the AFM leg exchange coupling ($J$) are the key tuning parameters defining the phase diagram.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on extrapolating finite-size DMRG results to the thermodynamic limit and uses empirical fits to map out the boundaries of the ferromagnetic phase.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the global phase diagram in the $(J_H, \delta)$ plane, showing the coexistence of the doped Haldane SPT and FM phases, alongside plots detailing the evolution of topological and correlation diagnostics across doping levels.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the system's behavior by first establishing the model and key diagnostics (DMRG/Bosonization). It then maps the global phase diagram, detailing how low doping involves short-range charge correlations, while high doping shows a crossover to collective spin incorporation, culminating in the characterization of the reentrant FM boundary.

Abstract

We study itinerant hole doping of the spin-$1$ Haldane symmetry-protected topological (SPT) phase in two $t$--$J$ chains coupled by ferromagnetic (FM) Hund exchange. Density-matrix renormalization group calculations show that string order, a finite spin gap, and the Haldane entanglement structure coexist over a broad doping range. Combined with entanglement-entropy scaling consistent with a single gapless charge mode, these results support a metallic doped Haldane SPT with a $C1S0$ low-energy description. At low doping, short-range charge correlations reduce the density of spin-$1/2$ defect rungs, whereas at larger doping their positions become nearly uncorrelated while their spins are collectively incorporated into the correlated spin sector. The doped Haldane SPT terminates in a fully spin-polarized FM phase, which forms a reentrant pocket at high doping in the global phase diagram. Dilute-limit analyses trace the two FM boundaries to spin--charge factorization at weak Hund coupling and to rung-triplet binding with reduced pair mobility at strong Hund coupling. Our results show how mobile carriers reorganize the Haldane spin structure while preserving its characteristic signatures.

A Geometric Theory of Quantum Entanglement

Summary. This paper unifies multiple entanglement measures—including Entanglement Distance (ED) and Quantum Fisher Information (QFI)—by showing they are all rooted in the geometry of the projective Hilbert space. It demonstrates that this geometric measure quantifies the total resourcefulness for local quantum sensing, providing a rigorous link between information geometry and quantum metrology.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a powerful, unified geometric tool (ED) that connects abstract concepts of entanglement to concrete, measurable quantities in quantum metrology (QFI), which is highly relevant for designing quantum sensing protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to geometrically characterize and quantify quantum entanglement, establishing mathematical equivalences between various measures like Entanglement Distance (ED), Meyer-Wallach, and Quantum Fisher Information (QFI).

Main result. It proves that ED is mathematically equivalent to the sum of local linear entropies and is directly proportional to the total QFI available for local unitary estimation, bridging geometry and quantum metrology.

Method. The analysis heavily utilizes the Fubini-Study metric on the projective Hilbert space and relates entanglement measures to the variance of local observable generators.

Model / system. The work considers general finite-dimensional multipartite systems, analyzing specific dynamics using the Kitagawa-Ueda (KU) model for spin squeezing, and also examining the Haar-random state ensemble.

Key observables. Entanglement Distance (ED), Quantum Fisher Information (QFI), Spin Squeezing Parameter ($\xi^2$), and local linear entropies.

Important parameters / regimes. The number of qubits ($N$), time evolution parameter ($t$), and the local generator structure ($\mathfrak{su}(d_\mu)$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the assumption that the geometric approach is robust for extending measures to infinite-dimensional (CV) systems, though it notes a gap between theoretical saturation and experimental accessibility for random states.

Figures summary. Figure 1 compares the evolution of the Spin Squeezing Parameter ($\xi^2$), ED, and normalized Entropy of Entanglement for the KU Model, showing ED's distinct behavior.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the geometric framework using the Fubini-Study metric, derives the equivalence between ED and local entropy sums, applies this framework to the KU model to show metrological advantage, and finally analyzes the global average entanglement for Haar-random states.

Abstract

Entanglement Distance (ED) was originally proposed as a geometric measure of entanglement derived from the Fubini-Study metric on the projective Hilbert space. Independently, the Meyer-Wallach and Scott measures quantify multipartite entanglement via linear entropy. In this work, we demonstrate that these two seemingly distinct frameworks are mathematically identical for pure states of arbitrary finite dimensions. We prove that ED arises naturally as the trace of the Fubini-Study metric tensor over the local subalgebra of observables. Crucially, this geometric unification yields a direct operational interpretation: the global entanglement of a pure state is exactly proportional to the total Quantum Fisher Information (QFI) available for local unitary estimation. This bridges abstract information geometry with quantum metrology, demonstrating that ED dynamically quantifies resourcefulness for distributed quantum sensing, identifying Heisenberg-limited sensitivity in regimes where standard variance-based witnesses fail.

Abstract

Much work has been done in the last two decades on the topic of quantum state transfer in a quantum spin network. One can model such a system of interacting qubits using an undirected graph, and studying vertex-to-vertex dynamics. This setup has recently been relaxed to allow for dynamics between linear combinations of two vertex states, i.e.\ from $\mathbf u = \mathbf e_a + s \mathbf e_b$ to $\mathbf μ=\mathbf e_α + r \mathbf e_β$, where $r=s$ is either $-1$ (which corresponds to pair state transfer) or $+1$ (which corresponds to plus state transfer), or more recently $r=s$ is taken to be any real number (which corresponds to $s$-pair state transfer). Here, we broaden the investigation of $s$-pair state transfer to \textit{perfect $(s,r)$-state transfer}, which is perfect state transfer from $\mathbf u = \mathbf e_a + s \mathbf e_b$ to $\mathbf μ=\mathbf e_α + r \mathbf e_β$ (up to some dilation) where $r,s\in \mathbb C$. We identify infinite families of graphs with perfect $(s,r)$-state transfer and provide characterizations of cases when $|r|= |s|$ and when $|r|\neq |s|$, showing situations when the degree of entanglement between vertex states is preserved, and when it is not preserved. The latter is particularly important as it represents perfect state transfer from an entangled pair of qubits to another one where the degree of entanglement need not be the same\mdash in fact, it can be set up so as to ``boost'' (increase) entanglement. We provide an algorithm that finds the vector with two nonzero entries that maximizes the fidelity of transfer for a fixed time $t$ starting from a given $s$-pair state $\mathbf u$. Finally, we provide a sensitivity analysis, with respect to readout time errors, of perfect $(s,r)$-state transfer.

Abstract

Altermagnets constitute a distinct class of magnetic materials that combine compensated magnetic order with spin-polarized electronic bands, hence carrying characteristics of both antiferromagnets and ferromagnets. Piezomagnetism - the linear relationship between lattice strain and a net magnetic moment - has emerged as smoking-gun evidence of altermagnetism that distinguishes it from antiferromagnetism. Here, we uncover a large piezomagnetic response in MnTe2, a cubic altermagnet with a noncollinear spin arrangement and weak spin-orbit coupling. We combine dilatometry with nuclear magnetic resonance, a bulk local probe, to observe signatures of both direct and inverse piezomagnetism consistent with symmetry considerations and first-principles calculations. The dilatometry measurements reveal a shear deformation proportional to an applied magnetic field, while magnetic resonance detects a ferromagnetic moment induced by shear strain. The respective results for the piezomagnetic coupling strength are in good agreement, and they are captured by first-principles results. These findings for simple binary MnTe2 position this material as a model altermagnet, demonstrate the utility of nuclear magnetic resonance in investigations of piezomagnetism, and open new avenues for multimodal strain and magnetic-field control in spintronic and memory applications.
