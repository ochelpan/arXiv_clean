A hierarchy of exact lower bounds on the Laughlin spectral gap

Authors: Michael G. Scheer, Eslam Khalaf
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18854v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper presents a sophisticated mathematical framework to derive rigorous lower bounds on the spectral gap for models describing strongly correlated electrons, specifically those related to the Fractional Quantum Hall Effect. It employs a bootstrap approach based on semidefinite programming applied to the Hamiltonian squared. The work provides a general, powerful technique for converting computationally derived bounds into mathematically proven results.

Why it may be interesting. The general methodology—turning numerical SDP bounds into rigorous proofs—is highly valuable for any theoretical physicist working on strongly correlated systems where exact spectral gap calculations are notoriously difficult.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is to derive exact, rigorous lower bounds on the spectral gap of the Laughlin spectral gap Hamiltonian.

Main result. The authors develop a general method using semidefinite programming (SDP) and advanced mathematical techniques to establish these bounds, achieving results numerically exact up to solver precision.

Method. The approach utilizes Knabe's observation ($H^2 \succeq \epsilon H$) and decomposes the Hamiltonian squared ($H^2$) into parts that are bounded analytically or via finite SDPs. Novel algorithms are introduced to convert numerical SDP bounds into rigorous mathematical proofs.

Model / system. The study concerns the spectral gap of the first Haldane pseudopotential Hamiltonian, which models interacting electrons in the lowest Landau level on a sphere with $N_\Phi$ flux quanta, relevant to the Fractional Quantum Hall Effect.

Key observables. The spectral gap ($\epsilon$), the lower bound on the gap, and the Hamiltonian itself.

Important parameters / regimes. Flux quanta ($N_\Phi$), filling fraction ($ u$), and cutoff parameters ($r', r$) that determine the size of the SDP.

Assumptions / limitations. The method relies on mapping the problem onto a structure suitable for $H^2 = K_1 + K_2$ decomposition, and later steps involve assuming the existence of limits and using symmetry averaging over SU(2).

Figures summary. Not specified.

Paper structure. The paper builds from the general principle of using SDPs on the spectral gap, applies this to the specific pseudopotential Hamiltonian via symmetry averaging, and culminates in developing rigorous algorithms to prove the derived numerical bounds in the thermodynamic limit.

Abstract

We develop a bootstrap approach to deriving lower bounds on the spectral gap of the first Haldane pseudopotential Hamiltonian on the sphere with $N_Φ$ flux quanta for arbitrary $N_Φ$, including in the thermodynamic limit $N_Φ\to \infty$. Our approach can in principle be generalized to pseudopotentials describing other model wavefunctions such as the Moore-Read state or Read-Rezayi sequence. Our approach is based on Knabe's observation that if $H$ is a Hamiltonian with ground state energy $0$ then the spectral gap is at least $ε> 0$ if and only if $H^2 \succeq εH$. To show this inequality, we split $H^2$ into two parts, one that can be analytically bounded and another that can be bounded using a finite semidefinite program (SDP). Crucially, the size of this program is determined only by cutoff parameters, and the program depends on $N_Φ$ only parametrically, allowing us to obtain numerically exact (up to solver precision) lower bounds on the gap. In order to turn these numerical bounds into mathematically rigorous bounds, we introduce an algorithm that can produce nearly-optimal exact feasible points for large rational SDPs. We additionally provide an algorithm that establishes the rank stability of the semidefinite program in the limit $N_Φ\to \infty$ which is needed to certify the bound in the thermodynamic limit. Beyond establishing the Laughlin gap and revealing a potential method for deriving spectral gap lower bounds for other pseudopotentials, our work provides a general method for turning numerical semidefinite programming bounds into rigorous mathematical proofs, which may have wide applicability.

Exact Microscopic One-Body Reduction in the Symmetric Exclusion Process

Authors: Olivier Bénichou, Aurélien Grabsch
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18626v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: non-equilibrium universality 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work solves a major problem in non-equilibrium statistical mechanics by showing that the complex, many-body correlation dynamics of the Symmetric Exclusion Process (SEP) simplify exactly. The infinite hierarchy of equations collapses onto a single, linear equation equivalent to a single random walker's persistence problem. This establishes a fundamental, microscopic one-body description for the system's behavior.

Why it may be interesting. The finding that a complex, interacting many-body system reduces exactly to a one-body problem before any scaling limit is taken provides a powerful, general organizing principle for understanding non-equilibrium dynamics in interacting particle systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of determining the correlation profile in the Symmetric Exclusion Process (SEP), which is governed by an intractable, infinite many-body hierarchy of equations.

Main result. They prove that the complex many-body problem reduces exactly to a simple, genuine one-body problem equivalent to a persistence problem for a single auxiliary random walker, even at finite time.

Method. The authors employ advanced techniques involving the stirring representation, Bethe-ansatz calculations, and the use of Poisson transforms to derive a closed, linear equation for the correlation profile.

Model / system. The system studied is the Symmetric Exclusion Process (SEP), a fundamental model for interacting particle transport on a lattice where particles undergo symmetric random walks subject to hard-core exclusion.

Key observables. The correlation profile $\omega_r(t; \lambda)$ and the $n$-particle propagators $\langle \eta_{r_1}(t) \cdots \eta_{r_n}(t) angle$.

Important parameters / regimes. Time ($t$), spatial lattice positions ($r$), and the coupling parameter $\lambda$. The analysis distinguishes between finite-time microscopic regimes and large-scale diffusive limits.

Assumptions / limitations. The derivation relies on extending the microscopic Bethe-ansatz calculation and involves complex mathematical manipulations like contour deformations and expansions in the large-scale limit.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the SEP as a stirring process, showing how the particle configuration is derived from the initial labeling of particles.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first identifying the many-body hierarchy, then developing the microscopic tools (Bethe ansatz, determinantal representations) to derive a closed, linear equation, and finally demonstrating that this equation is equivalent to a single random walker's persistence problem.

Abstract

The symmetric exclusion process (SEP) is a paradigmatic model of interacting particle transport. Its current--density correlations are governed by an infinite many-body hierarchy of equations. Only recently, this hierarchy was found to collapse at large scales onto a single closed equation. Is this remarkable simplification created by coarse graining, or does it reflect a deeper property already present in the microscopic dynamics? Here we show that the second scenario is realized. At arbitrary time on the lattice, the entire hierarchy collapses onto a single linear equation which is exactly equivalent to a persistence problem for a single auxiliary random walker. Thus an interacting many-body problem reduces exactly to a genuine one-body problem before any scaling limit is taken. This reduction determines the complete finite-time correlation profile, including during nonequilibrium relaxation. More broadly, the microscopic operator uncovered here opens a route to more general correlation functions in the SEP, and may point to a general organizing principle for nonequilibrium interacting particle systems.

On the Statistical Mechanics of Attention

Authors: Jerry Yao-Chieh Hu, Yu-An Chen, Maojiang Su, Han Liu
Type: theory · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.17347v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work applies statistical mechanics, specifically using free-energy formalisms derived from Hopfield models, to analyze the Transformer attention mechanism. It treats attention as an emergent physical process governed by energy minimization, allowing researchers to map out phase diagrams. This provides a rigorous theoretical framework for understanding when and why attention systems fail or become unreliable.

Why it may be interesting. The deep connection between attention mechanisms (a core component of modern AI) and established concepts from spin-glass theory and associative memory provides a powerful, physics-based lens for understanding AI reliability.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to rigorously characterize the statistical mechanics of the Transformer attention mechanism to understand its operational regimes and critical thresholds for reliable inference.

Main result. It establishes that attention emerges naturally from the free-energy minimization principle of a modern Hopfield model, allowing for the identification of first-order phase transitions between glassy and robust-inference states.

Method. The authors employ a free-energy formalism, utilizing concepts from statistical mechanics like Tsallis entropy and Fenchel-Young duality, to analyze the energy landscape of the system.

Model / system. The core model is the Transformer attention mechanism, which is mapped onto a Modern Hopfield Model (MHM). This model's dynamics are governed by a joint energy function involving query states and memory patterns.

Key observables. Free-energy landscape ($\Gamma_\alpha$), phase structure, coexistence points ($eta_{coexist}$), and spinodal points ($eta_{sp}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Attention temperature ($eta$), memory load, and the entropy parameter ($\alpha$) used in the Tsallis entropy generalization.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis often restricts attention to the deterministic regime (zero variance) and relies on assumptions of uniform distribution and well-separation for quantitative validation.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the derivation from microscopic fluctuations to macroscopic energy landscapes, and later show numerical validation of theoretical formulas for coexistence and spinodal points in two-key and general k-support systems.

Paper structure. The paper builds from introducing the MHM and free-energy formalism, deriving key relationships via duality, and then validating the resulting theoretical predictions (coexistence/spinodal points) through numerical calculations on simplified memory systems.

Abstract

We revisit the statistical mechanics of Transformer attention via modern Hopfield models. We present a free-energy formalism to characterize parameter regimes and critical thresholds for robust attention inference. The resulting free-energy landscape incorporates randomness in query states and attention weights. Attention then emerges as the equation of motion of the corresponding statistical-mechanical system (i.e., modern Hopfield model) through the standard free-energy minimization principle. This allows us to identify first-order phase transitions in attention between glassy and robust-inference phases. We further extend to attentions with stochastic queries and keys. Together, our results provide a rigorous statistical-mechanics framework for characterizing phase structure and inference reliability across a family of Transformer attention mechanisms.

Quantum Cryptography and Autoformalization in Lean

Summary. This work develops a novel, machine-verified framework in the Lean proof assistant to rigorously prove the security of Quantum Key Distribution protocols like BB84. By formalizing the entire process, including finite-size effects and LOCC constraints, the authors eliminate ambiguity in security assumptions. This establishes a reproducible, automated standard for security proofs in quantum cryptography.

Why it may be interesting. It demonstrates a powerful synergy between theoretical quantum information security and advanced formal verification tools (Lean), setting a new standard for rigorously proving quantum protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of establishing end-to-end, machine-verified security theorems for Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) protocols, specifically closing gaps in existing proofs due to mismatched assumptions.

Main result. The authors provide the first end-to-end, machine-verified composable finite-key security proof for the entanglement-based qubit BB84 protocol against coherent attacks.

Method. The core method involves formalizing the entire protocol and its security analysis within the Lean proof assistant, utilizing LLM-based autoformalization and a specialized Local Operations and Classical Communication (LOCC) language.

Model / system. The focus is on the theoretical security analysis of the entanglement-based qubit BB84 protocol, modeling quantum operations using Kraus maps and defining security via the diamond norm distance between real and ideal quantum channels.

Key observables. Security bound ($\epsilon_{ ext{QKD}}$), key rate (finite-size penalty), and the distinguishability between the real and ideal quantum channels.

Important parameters / regimes. Finite block size, coherence of attacks, and the robustness bound ($\delta_{ ext{rob}}$) related to the abort probability.

Assumptions / limitations. The security proof is fundamentally built upon the assumption of Local Operations and Classical Communication (LOCC).

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the AI-assisted proving workflow (AI agents $ o$ Lean kernel checking $ o$ manual review). Figure 2 details the machine-checked BB84 development using the LOCC construction.

Paper structure. The paper develops a formal framework in Lean to model quantum protocols, applies this framework to the BB84 protocol to prove finite-key security, and discusses the integration of autoformalization pipelines to accelerate discovery.

Abstract

Quantum key distribution provides secure communication by relying on physics principles and mathematical reasoning. Security proofs across the field can contain gaps, often between individually valid theorems integrated under mismatched assumptions. We produce an end-to-end security theorem formalized in the Lean proof assistant, eliminating hidden hypotheses by building only on Lean's standard axioms. Our proof covers composable security of the entanglement-based qubit BB84 protocol against coherent attacks by the postselection technique, accounting for finite block size, and is written in a language that enforces local operations and classical communication to help ensure a faithful representation of the protocol. The proof itself was enabled by LLM-based autoformalization, and with the proof complete, we used the same pipeline as a discovery machine, reducing the finite-size penalty on the key rate. Our work sets a reproducible standard for security arguments in quantum cryptography and shows how formal proof engines can accelerate discovery across quantum information science.

Theory of Trotter Error with Variable-Weight Schedules

Authors: Henrique Ennes
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18202v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces 'scheduled trotterization,' a variable-weight modification to standard quantum simulation algorithms. It rigorously proves that by adaptively scheduling the local pulse weights, the global error in approximating time evolution can be improved from $O(N^{-1})$ to $O(N^{-2})$. This offers a powerful theoretical tool for designing more accurate quantum simulation protocols.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous, mathematically advanced framework for optimizing quantum simulation algorithms, directly impacting the achievable accuracy in simulating complex quantum dynamics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of accurately simulating quantum time evolution using product formulas (Trotterization) when the available local generators are limited.

Main result. By introducing a variable-weight, scheduled trotterization scheme, the global approximation error can be significantly reduced from the standard $O(N^{-1})$ scaling to $O(N^{-2})$ for exact synthesis.

Method. The analysis uses the geometry of the Lie group, expressing the global error as a product of transported local defects analyzed via the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula.

Model / system. The model involves approximating a target unitary evolution $U = \exp(itH)$ using a sequence of local pulses generated by a finite set of available Hamiltonians $\{H_j\}$ in $SU(d)$.

Key observables. Global endpoint error ($\|U - W\|$); local defects; internal and external remainder terms ($R_{ ext{int}}, R_{ ext{ext}}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Number of steps ($N$); error scaling ($\delta, \epsilon$); local covering parameter (for imperfect synthesis).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the validity of BCH expansions under small norm conditions and assumes the target Hamiltonian lies in the real span of the available generators.

Figures summary. Figure 1 compares fixed-weight ($O(N^{-1})$) vs. variable-weight ($O(N^{-2})$) scaling. Figure 2 illustrates the geometric path difference between ideal and simulated evolution.

Paper structure. The paper develops the error analysis inductively, first establishing the general error bound using BCH expansions, then deriving the improved $O(N^{-2})$ bound for exact synthesis by defining an adaptive schedule, and finally extending the framework to imperfect synthesis.

Abstract

Trotterization is a widely used algorithm for simulating quantum evolutions and consists of approximating a target unitary through a local linear decomposition of its generator that is repeated over $N$ steps. We introduce {scheduled trotterization}, a variable-weight modification in which the coefficients of the local linear decomposition are allowed to change from step to step. Using the geometry of the Lie group of unitary operators, we express the global endpoint error as a product of transported local defects, whose leading terms are obtained by two applications of the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula, one inside each local step and one between different transported defects, separating the internal Trotter error from the external accumulation error. While we recover recent tighter bounds on the global approximation error when the weights are held fixed, the new framework allows us to propose a weight-varying schedule that significantly decreases the global error. For exact infinitesimal synthesis, we prove that this improves the usual fixed-weight $O(N^{-1})$ behavior to $O(N^{-2})$, without changing the basic pulse skeleton. We also extend the framework to imperfect infinitesimal synthesis, as arises in quantum gate synthesis problems, where admissible coefficients may only approximate the desired local corrections. In this case, the error is controlled by a local covering parameter measuring the quality of the available coefficient set. Numerical experiments for random $\SU(2)$ targets further support the predicted theoretical improvements. The results suggest a novel perspective for the analysis of Trotter errors, which can be treated as schedulable quantities to be coherently canceled at the endpoint.

Towards synergy between theory and experiments: First-passage processes under resetting with stochastic return protocols

Authors: Ashutosh Dubey, Arnab Pal
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18814v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper analyzes search processes in a 1D harmonic trap subjected to stochastic resetting with a finite-time return phase. By deriving the MFPT and using the CV criterion, the authors map out the parameter space where this stochastic return mechanism significantly enhances search efficiency over simpler instantaneous return models. The results offer concrete, experimentally testable guidelines for optimizing search protocols.

Why it may be interesting. The analysis of non-Markovian or finite-time return dynamics (stochastic return) provides a crucial refinement to standard resetting models, directly bridging theoretical predictions to measurable optical trap experiments.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates first-passage processes under stochastic resetting with stochastic return (SR) protocols to understand how this mechanism controls search efficiency compared to instantaneous return (IR).

Main result. The most beneficial regime for SR occurs when the potential strength in the search phase ($k_1$) is large and the potential strength in the return phase ($k_2$) is small. The work provides experimentally testable predictions.

Method. The authors analytically derive the Mean First-Passage Time (MFPT) and utilize the coefficient-of-variation (CV) criterion to construct phase diagrams comparing SR efficiency against the underlying dynamics.

Model / system. The model considers a one-dimensional diffusing particle confined in a harmonic trap, undergoing a search phase (stiffness $k_1$) followed by a return phase (stiffness $k_2$) after stochastic resetting.

Key observables. Mean First-Passage Time (MFPT), Search efficiency enhancement/suppression (via CV criterion), Comparison between SR and IR efficiency.

Important parameters / regimes. Potential stiffnesses ($k_1, k_2$), resetting rate ($r$), and the target location ($L$).

Assumptions / limitations. The dynamics are modeled using an overdamped Langevin process (Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process) in both phases, and the return phase allows for target detection.

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically illustrates three search scenarios: direct success, target detection during return, or return to origin followed by a new search. Figure 2 shows a phase-space diagram mapping parameter regimes where SR is beneficial.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the theoretical framework for SR dynamics, derives the MFPT, applies the CV criterion to map the parameter space, and finally tests these theoretical predictions against parameters reported in existing experimental studies.

Abstract

Stochastic resetting with stochastic return (SR) has recently emerged as an effective and practically relevant strategy for controlling first-passage phenomena and optimizing search processes by significantly reducing completion times in a wide class of stochastic systems. While most theoretical studies assume instantaneous return (IR), in realistic systems the return to the resetting position typically occurs over a finite time. We consider a 1-d diffusing particle in a harmonic trap with stiffnesses $k_1$ and $k_2$ during search and return, respectively. The particle searches for the target and, upon resetting at rate $r$, enters the return phase, continuing until it reaches either the origin or the absorbing target at $L>0$. Notably, the model permits target detection throughout the return phase, distinguishing it from IR scenarios. We analytically derive the MFPT for our model and determine parameter regions where SR enhances or suppresses search efficiency over the underlying dynamics, constructing the corresponding phase diagram using the coefficient of variation criterion. We further identify regimes in which SR is more efficient than IR using the SR criterion. This behavior is tested across different parameter sets taken from experimental studies by Goerlich et al., Biroli et al., and Besga et al. We map the parameter values reported in existing experimental studies onto our theoretical phase diagram, thereby identifying the regimes in which SR outperforms IR. In particular, we find that the most relevant region of the phase space, where SR is most beneficial corresponds to larger values of the potential strength in the search phase and lower values of potential in the return phase. Our results provide experimentally testable predictions and bridge the gap between theoretical resetting models and optical trap mediated finite-time return dynamics.

$\mathsf{EFI}\Leftrightarrow\mathsf{1PRS}$

Summary. This paper proves a fundamental equivalence in quantum cryptography, showing that EFI pairs are mathematically equivalent to expanding single-copy pseudorandom state generators (1PRS). By removing non-uniformity from previous work, the authors provide the first uniform characterization of this relationship. This result establishes a deep quantum analogue to classical cryptographic theorems linking one-way functions and pseudorandomness.

Why it may be interesting. While highly abstract, the rigorous mathematical tools developed for bounding quantum entropy and proving computational indistinguishability are foundational to understanding the limits of quantum information processing.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To prove the uniform equivalence between Efficiently Formed Information (EFI) pairs and expanding single-copy pseudorandom state generators (1PRS).

Main result. The authors establish the first uniform meta-complexity characterization showing that EFI pairs exist if and only if expanding 1PRS exist, providing a quantum analogue to classical cryptographic equivalences.

Method. The proof adapts the random-start technique and uses entropy chain rules to remove non-uniformity from prior constructions, relating the existence of quantum states to computational hardness assumptions.

Model / system. The analysis is conducted using quantum states (pure states) within the framework of quantum cryptography primitives like EFI pairs and 1PRS.

Key observables. Von Neumann entropy, algorithmic entropy, trace distance, and the entropy surplus.

Important parameters / regimes. Security parameter $\lambda$, output/key lengths ($n, r$), and the stretch factor.

Assumptions / limitations. The proof builds upon prior results regarding non-uniform equivalence and relies on the random-start idea holding for quantum side information.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the equivalence relationships between EFI, statistically verifiable one-way state generators (svOWSG), quantum commitments, and 1PRS.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the equivalence ($ ext{EFI} \Leftrightarrow ext{1PRS}$) using advanced entropy analysis, culminating in a theorem that provides the uniform meta-complexity characterization.

Abstract

A series of recent works has established EFI pairs as the "minimal" assumption for quantum cryptography. Recently, Cavalar et al. [CCC+26] showed that EFI pairs are equivalent to non-uniform expanding single-copy pseudorandom state generators (1PRS), and obtained a non-uniform meta-complexity characterization of EFI pairs. We remove the non-uniformity in their construction and prove that EFI pairs are equivalent to expanding 1PRS. As a corollary, we obtain the first uniform meta-complexity characterization of EFI pairs. Together with the equivalence of statistically verifiable one-way state generators and commitments proved by Batra and Jain [BJ24], this gives a quantum analogue of the classical equivalence between one-way functions, commitments, and pseudorandom generators.

Summary. This paper presents a novel construction for synthesizing arbitrary $n$-qubit unitaries using Clifford+T circuits, achieving a T-gate complexity of $\widetilde{O}(N^{8/7}L^{3/7}+NL)$. The authors improve upon previous bounds and rigorously prove that the $N^{8/7}$ scaling is optimal within the sequential block-SELECT model, advancing the theoretical understanding of quantum circuit resource requirements.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the fundamental resource cost of quantum computation, providing state-of-the-art bounds on gate synthesis complexity, which is crucial for designing practical quantum algorithms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Determining the minimum number of T gates required to implement an arbitrary n-qubit unitary circuit efficiently.

Main result. The authors achieve a T-gate count of $\widetilde{O}(N^{8/7}L^{3/7}+NL)$ and prove that the exponent $8/7$ is optimal in the worst case for the sequential block-SELECT model.

Method. The approach simulates the target unitary via its Hermitian logarithm, flattening the generator using random similarity transformations, and combining the resulting block-sparse evolutions using the qDRIFT technique.

Model / system. The model is quantum computation using Clifford+T circuits to realize arbitrary $n$-qubit unitaries $U \in U(2n)$. The resource measure is the T-gate count, as T-gates are non-Clifford and costly.

Key observables. T-gate count, clean ancilla count, and the approximation error $\epsilon$.

Important parameters / regimes. N (total qubits, $2^n$), L ($\log_2(N/\epsilon)$), and the exponent $8/7$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the use of clean ancillas, and the lower bound proof is restricted to the sequential block-SELECT model.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates matrix partitioning for $N=8$. Figure 2 compares schematic table access for dense vs. separate piece encodings. Figure 3 and 4 show the assignment and structure of blocks for Hamiltonian simulation.

Paper structure. The paper develops the construction by first establishing the simulation framework (using qDRIFT and block encoding) and then deriving the optimal complexity bounds by analyzing the required resources in the sequential block-SELECT model.

Abstract

We construct Clifford+$T$ circuits implementing arbitrary $n$-qubit unitaries with worst-case $T$-count $\widetilde{O}(N^{8/7})$ at constant operator-norm error, where $N=2^n$, improving the previous exponent $5/4$. The construction uses $\widetilde{O}(N^{6/7})$ clean ancillas. The main idea is to reduce the data addressed by each query to the block-encoding circuit for a sparse piece while controlling the additional simulation work needed to combine the pieces. We simulate a Hermitian logarithm of the target by flattening its traceless part with a two-layer random similarity transformation, randomly partitioning the transformed generator into block-sparse Hermitian pieces, and combining their evolutions using qDRIFT. For a target operator-norm error $ε\in(0,1/2)$, let $L=\log_2(N/ε)$, the qubit count plus the requested bits of accuracy. By successively correcting the approximation error, we obtain a circuit using $\widetilde{O}(N^{8/7}L^{3/7}+NL)$ $T$ gates and $\widetilde{O}(N)$ clean ancillas whenever $L\le N$. We also prove that, at constant precision, the exponent $8/7$ is optimal within a sequential block-SELECT model.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper determines the minimal number of alternating Hamiltonian switches required to synthesize any arbitrary unitary evolution in a $d$-dimensional quantum system.

Main result. For generic Hamiltonians, the required number of switches scales linearly with the dimension $d$ in expectation, and specific bounds (like $9d$) are derived for certain structured cases.

Method. The authors relate the problem to finding the minimal length representation of unitaries as products of elements diagonal in two different eigenbases, utilizing techniques from group theory and graph theory.

Assumptions / limitations. The main results often assume the spectra of the Hamiltonians are sufficiently generic (eigenvalues linearly independent over $\mathbb{Q}$), although bounds are extended to non-generic cases.

Abstract

Any pair of sufficiently generic Hamiltonians on $\mathbb{C}^d$ generates the full unitary group, i.e., any $W\in U(d)$ can be synthesized to arbitrary precision by alternating between the two evolutions for suitably chosen durations. For any given pair of Hamiltonians, the minimal number of switches required to cover the entire group is at least $d$ and unbounded in general. Despite this, we show that it scales linearly in $d$ in expectation. Assuming that the two spectra are sufficiently generic, we derive constructive upper bounds on the minimal switching number as a function of the change-of-basis matrix between the two eigenbases. In particular, if this matrix has a column whose entries all have equal moduli, as for a complex Hadamard matrix, then $9d$ switches suffice to reach every unitary. To obtain these results, we construct a short exact representation of any unitary as a product of unitaries that are alternately diagonal in two given bases. All bounds are extended to non-generic spectra and bounded evolution times.
