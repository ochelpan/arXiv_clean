Lossless Reductions and Hybrid Sampling for Phylogeny Using the Maximum Compatibility Criterion

Authors: Orson Ye, James Bonfield, Joshua Cudby, Richard Durbin, Sergii Strelchuk
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18029v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper enhances phylogenetic reconstruction by tackling the difficult problem of enumerating all maximum compatible character sets. It combines graph reduction techniques with quantum sampling methods and benchmarks these against advanced classical solvers. The goal is to efficiently solve the underlying maximum weight clique problem derived from sequence compatibility data.

Why it may be interesting. While the core problem is combinatorial biology, the explicit formulation of the MWC problem as a QUBO/MWIS problem and the application of QAOA instances make it relevant to quantum algorithm development for hard optimization problems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the computationally hard problem of enumerating all maximum weight cliques (MWC) derived from the Maximum Compatibility Criterion used in phylogenetic reconstruction.

Main result. The authors propose a hybrid approach combining graph reduction techniques with quantum sampling and advanced classical solvers to tackle the enumeration bottleneck, showing improvements over existing methods.

Method. The methodology involves formulating the problem as a maximum weight independent set enumeration, applying lossless graph reductions, and testing bespoke Quantum Approximate Optimisation Algorithm (QAOA) instances alongside classical solvers like AClique and SCCWalk4L.

Model / system. The model is phylogenetic reconstruction based on Multiple Sequence Alignments (MSAs), where compatibility data forms a weighted graph whose MWC corresponds to the optimal set of characters retained.

Key observables. The primary observables are the size and weight of the maximum compatible character set (MWC), and the computational time required to enumerate the entire set of optimal solutions.

Important parameters / regimes. Graph size (number of vertices/characters), weights assigned to characters, and the number of samples/iterations used in quantum algorithms.

Assumptions / limitations. The work assumes that graph reduction and quantum sampling can be effectively combined to maintain exact reconstruction, although the quantum results are not claimed as a definitive quantum advantage.

Figures summary. Figures benchmark various classical solvers (FastWCLQ2, SCCWalk4L, AClique) showing the trade-off between computational time and the quality (size) of the found maximum clique for graphs of increasing size.

Paper structure. The paper builds upon existing work (Cherry's algorithm), first detailing the formulation as an MWC enumeration problem, then introducing graph reduction techniques, and finally exploring hybrid solvers combining these reductions with quantum sampling and benchmarking against state-of-the-art classical solvers.

Abstract

Maximum compatibility provides a criterion for phylogenetic reconstruction in which the objective is to retain a maximum set of mutually compatible characters. For binary characters, pairwise compatibility can be represented by a weighted graph whose vertices correspond to characters and whose edges connect compatible pairs. A leading algorithm by Cherry [BMC Bioinformatics, 18(1):127, 2017] uses this representation to identify maximum compatible character sets as maximum weight cliques. We formulate the problem as maximum weight independent set enumeration on the complement graph and use deterministic postprocessing to map sampled bit strings to maximal feasible solutions. To explore the prospects of quantum computational advantage, we further construct bespoke correction-in-the-loop Quantum Approximate Optimisation Algorithm instances and find that traditional Lagrange multiplier choices may not be optimal for the enumeration problem on noisy devices. We also introduce lossless graph reductions with reconstruction rules that preserve the complete set of optimal solutions. Our results indicate that graph reduction and sampling can be effectively combined to address the enumeration bottleneck in maximum compatibility phylogeny, while retaining exact reconstruction when the residual optimum set is completely resolved. We enhance Cherry's approach to maximum compatibility by introducing graph reduction techniques and quantum sampling. Our classical benchmarks show a clear difference between finding one optimum and recovering the optimum set in practice. We demonstrate the utility of our methods on graphs constructed from SARS-CoV-2 sequences.

Methods for Automated Equivalence Checking of Quantum Error-Correction Codes

Summary. This paper addresses the challenge of automatically checking if two quantum error-correcting codes are equivalent, accounting for multiple sources of ambiguity. The authors develop and benchmark a hybrid computational framework that combines several advanced techniques to solve this problem efficiently. The work provides a crucial tool for the field by offering scalable, systematic methods for code comparison.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on QEC, the rigorous development of automated algebraic and computational tools for determining structural equivalence is highly relevant to characterizing complex quantum states and codes used in quantum simulation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops automated methods to determine if two descriptions of Quantum Error-Correction (QEC) codes are equivalent under various transformations.

Main result. No single algorithmic paradigm works universally, necessitating a hybrid checker combining group-action search, isomorphism methods, and constraint solving for optimal performance.

Method. The authors formalize equivalence notions (like permutation and local-Clifford) and combine three distinct algorithmic strategies into a staged Refute-Refine-Decide architecture.

Model / system. The focus is on Quantum Error-Correction Codes (QECCs), specifically stabilizer codes and CSS codes, which are defined by their generator matrices and associated Pauli group structures.

Key observables. Mean runtimes for equivalence checking, the fraction of distinguished qubit pairs ($ar{q}$), and the number of SAT solver decisions.

Important parameters / regimes. Physical qubit number ($n$), number of stabilizers ($r$), and the specific equivalence notion (permutation vs. local-Clifford).

Assumptions / limitations. The work assumes that the core challenge lies in automating the comparison across different representations and physical freedoms inherent in code descriptions.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the ambiguity of code representations (e.g., Steane code realizations) and compare the runtime and memory scaling of various encoding techniques (Full-Group vs. Matroid-Circuit) for equivalence checking.

Paper structure. The paper systematically defines the problem by separating physical freedom from representation freedom, develops multiple algorithmic tools (group action, isomorphism, constraint solving), and empirically evaluates their combined performance.

Abstract

Quantum error-correction code and circuit construction is an active field of research, motivating automated methods for identifying equivalent designs. We develop and evaluate automated strategies for deciding whether two descriptions of quantum error-correction codes are equivalent under specified transformations. We formalize permutation equivalence for stabilizer and CSS codes and local-Clifford equivalence for stabilizer codes within a unified framework that separates physical from representation freedom. We develop three decision strategies (group-action search, isomorphism-based methods, and constraint solving), together with invariants and signatures for preprocessing. The empirical evaluation shows that no paradigm dominates across equivalence notions, code families, and parameter ranges. We therefore combine their complementary strengths in input-dependent hybrid checkers following a staged Refute-Refine-Decide architecture. Mean hybrid runtimes remain below 10 s for most evaluated structured-code cases with $n \leq 37$; for inequivalent code instances with $n=144$, they are about 310 s for permutation and 16 s for local-Clifford equivalence.

Summary. This paper rigorously determines the conditions under which quantum uniformity measures act as true norms. It proves that these measures are only norms for $k=2, 3,$ and $4$, while demonstrating that the triangle inequality fails for all $k \ge 5$. This result provides a complete mathematical answer to an open question concerning the structure of quantum correlations.

Why it may be interesting. The failure of fundamental mathematical properties like the triangle inequality for higher-order quantum measures suggests deep structural limitations in how these measures capture quantum correlations, which is crucial for understanding resource theories in quantum information.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the mathematical properties, specifically the triangle inequality, of quantum uniformity measures ($\lVert \cdot Vert_{Qk}$).

Main result. The quantum uniformity measure $\lVert \cdot Vert_{Qk}$ is proven to be a norm only for $k=2, 3, 4$; for all $k \ge 5$, the triangle inequality fails.

Method. The proof for $k=4$ uses the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality on a defined quantum pairing, while the failure for $k \ge 5$ is shown by constructing contradictions using linear systems over finite fields.

Model / system. The context involves linear operators $T$ acting on the tensor product Hilbert space $H^{\otimes n}$, where $H = \mathbb{C}^p$ ($p$ prime). The measures are related to the Clifford hierarchy, relevant for fault-tolerant quantum computation.

Key observables. Quantum uniformity measures ($\lVert \cdot Vert_{Qk}$), quantum uniformity pairing ($\langle \cdot, \cdot angle_{Q4}$), and Weyl moments ($m_r(\psi)$).

Important parameters / regimes. The integer $k$, which dictates the specific uniformity measure being analyzed; the local dimension $p$ (a prime).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on relating the quantum norm to norms derived from associated linear systems, particularly the failure of $L(k)$ to be a norming system for $k \ge 5$.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the necessary mathematical tools, proving $\lVert \cdot Vert_{Q4}$ is a norm using Cauchy-Schwarz. It then proves the failure of the triangle inequality for $k \ge 5$ by constructing counterexamples using finite field linear algebra, providing a complete answer to an open problem.

Abstract

We prove that for $k=4$ the quantum uniformity measures are norms, whereas for every $k\geq5$ the triangle inequality fails. This gives a complete answer to an open question of Bu, Gu, and Jaffe. The $k=4$ case is proved using the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality for the quantum uniformity pairing $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{Q^4}$ introduced here, together with the triangle inequality in a suitable finite-dimensional Bochner $L^8$ space. For $k\geq5$, we construct systems of linear equations over a finite field and derive a contradiction from the structure theory of norming linear systems.

On the coincidence of discrete and continuous definitions of Entanglement of Formation

Authors: Haobo Ma, Maksim E. Shirokov, Wenlin Zhang
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18816v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper proves that the discrete and continuous definitions of the Entanglement of Formation coincide for infinite-dimensional bipartite quantum systems. The authors achieve this by developing a powerful discretization theorem that allows any continuous ensemble to be approximated arbitrarily closely by a countable ensemble. This result unifies the mathematical treatment of entanglement measures in complex quantum settings.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides rigorous mathematical grounding for entanglement measures in infinite dimensions, which is crucial for developing robust quantum information protocols that rely on these quantities.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to prove the mathematical coincidence between two definitions of the Entanglement of Formation (EoF) for infinite-dimensional bipartite quantum systems: the discrete version (based on countable decompositions) and the continuous version (based on Borel probability measures).

Main result. The core result is the equality $E_d^F( ho) = E_c^F( ho)$ for all bipartite states $ ho$, establishing that the continuous definition does not yield a lower bound below that achievable by countable ensembles.

Method. The proof employs an 'exact discretization theorem' by mapping the continuous ensemble to a Borel measure on Hilbert-Schmidt operators, which is then recursively partitioned and approximated by a countable ensemble.

Model / system. The system involves bipartite quantum states $ ho$ on separable Hilbert spaces $H_A \otimes H_B$. The analysis is formalized using the representation of pure states via Hilbert-Schmidt operators $A \in \mathcal{S}_2(H_B, H_A)$.

Key observables. Entanglement of Formation ($E^F$), Entanglement Entropy ($S(\cdot)$), and the associated cost functional $J(A)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The proof involves taking a limit $\eta o 0$ and relies on contraction factors $\alpha = \eta/4$ and $\eta/2$ during the recursive decomposition.

Assumptions / limitations. The proof assumes the existence of a Borel section for lifting the measure $\mu$ to a probability measure $\mu_0$, and it requires the cost functional $J$ to be convex and lower semicontinuous.

Paper structure. The paper develops the proof by first establishing the necessary discretization theorem (Theorem 1), then formalizing the continuous ensemble as a Borel measure on amplitudes, and finally applying a recursive decomposition process (Lemma 5) to show that the total cost converges to the discrete definition's value.

Abstract

It is proved that every Borel ensemble of pure states on a separable bipartite Hilbert space with finite average entanglement admits a countable ensemble with the same barycenter and arbitrarily small additional average entanglement. Consequently, the discrete and continuous versions of the Entanglement of Formation coincide on the whole space of states of infinite-dimensional bipartite quantum system (with infinite values allowed). The main idea of the proof (realized with the AI-assistance) is the identification of a Borel ensemble of pure bipartite states with a Borel probability measure on the unit sphere of the space of Hilbert--Schmidt operators followed by dividing the support of this measure into appropriate cells and replacing each cell by its conditional barycenter. The resulting countable ensemble is obtained by a recursive construction.

Organisation and aggregation of calcium carbonate ions in water via molecular simulation

Authors: Elizaveta Sidler, Rita S Dias, Raffaela Cabriolu
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18128v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work uses advanced molecular simulations (OPES and infinityRETIS) to study how calcium carbonate crystallizes in water. The research distinguishes between simple ionic aggregation and the formation of local structural order (the first coordination sphere). It finds that the crystallization pathway is complex, depending on the solution's supersaturation, and involves two distinct mechanisms for forming the initial ordered units.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical crystallization, the use of advanced sampling techniques like RETIS and the detailed mapping of free-energy barriers to distinguish between local order and bulk aggregation provides a sophisticated framework applicable to understanding phase transitions in complex, multi-component systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the complex relationship between the initial formation of the first coordination sphere of calcium carbonate (CaCO3) and the overall process of ionic aggregation during crystallization in aqueous solutions.

Main result. The kinetics of forming the first coordination sphere are highly dependent on the supersaturation level, revealing two distinct formation pathways (near minimum size vs. within large amorphous aggregates).

Method. The research employs advanced molecular simulation techniques, specifically On-the-fly probability-enhanced sampling (OPES) for free-energy landscapes and asynchronous/infinite-swap replica exchange transition interface sampling (infinityRETIS) for reaction kinetics.

Model / system. The system modeled is aqueous solutions of calcium carbonate ions (CaCO3), using a full-atomistic interaction potential combined with the SPC/Fw water model.

Key observables. Free-energy landscapes, reaction rate constants, average radius of gyration (Rg), and structural shape parameters (K1, K2) characterizing ionic clusters.

Important parameters / regimes. Two levels of supersaturation (0.01 and 0.02) and varying ion concentrations are key parameters used to probe different aggregation regimes.

Assumptions / limitations. The study assumes that crystallisation can be analyzed by differentiating between the local structural ordering (coordination sphere formation) and the bulk aggregation process.

Figures summary. Figures summarize CFE/FES surfaces mapping structural parameters and coordination numbers for various ion pairs, and probability density plots showing cluster size changes along reactive paths.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the system by first establishing the simulation methods (OPES, infinityRETIS) to study the formation of the first coordination sphere. It then compares kinetic behavior across different supersaturation levels, linking kinetic differences to concentration-dependent aggregation, and finally details the two coexisting formation pathways.

Abstract

Crystallisation of calcium carbonate (CaCO$_3$) in aqueous solutions is essential for carbon dioxide sequestration and the sustainable production of calcium carbonate materials, which are widely used in industry and increasingly explored for novel applications. While crystallisation is often analysed within the framework of classical nucleation theory, CaCO$_3$ and other ionic crystals often form through amorphous precursor phases, motivating the need for alternative descriptions. In this work, we investigate the relationship between the formation of the first CaCO$_3$ coordination sphere, a fundamental unit of local ordering, and ionic aggregation using two complementary simulation approaches: on-the-fly probability-enhanced sampling (OPES) and asynchronous and infinite-swap replica exchange transition interface sampling ($\infty$RETIS). OPES was used to explore free-energy landscapes, whereas $\infty$RETIS was used to determine reaction kinetics. We studied systems at two levels of supersaturation, between which the formation of the first coordination sphere exhibited distinct kinetic behaviour. These differences were found to be linked to concentration-dependent ionic aggregation. At lower concentration, aggregation remains limited, whereas higher concentration promotes extensive spinodal-like aggregation. For both concentrations, the first coordination sphere forms within the pre-formed clusters through one of two distinct pathways: either within a cluster close to the minimum size required for its formation, or within larger amorphous aggregates whose size and structure depend on concentration. The coexistence of these pathways at both levels of supersaturation reveals the multifaceted nature of early-stage CaCO$_3$ crystallisation and highlights the importance of differentiating ionic aggregation and organisation.

PADO-Pauli: A GPU-Accelerated Implementation of Pauli Propagation

Authors: Hyunwoo Kim, Youngseok Lee
Type: both · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.17755v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces PADO-Pauli, a highly optimized, GPU-accelerated toolkit for performing Pauli propagation—the classical evaluation of quantum circuit expectation values. By compiling circuits once and reusing the structure for batched evaluations with automatic differentiation, it drastically speeds up the training of variational quantum algorithms. This makes complex quantum simulations practical for modern quantum machine learning research.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a crucial, high-performance computational backend for implementing and training quantum machine learning models, which is vital for any theoretical physicist working on quantum algorithms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the need for an efficient classical method to evaluate expectation values of parameterized quantum circuits, which are central to training variational quantum algorithms (VQAs).

Main result. PADO-Pauli is presented as a GPU-accelerated engine that significantly outperforms existing tools, achieving up to 10x speedup and lower memory usage compared to competitors.

Method. It uses Pauli propagation in the Heisenberg picture, compiling the circuit and observables into sparse matrices that are reused for multiple evaluations, including native gradient calculation via PyTorch's automatic differentiation.

Model / system. The system involves parameterized quantum circuits whose observables are represented as sums of Pauli strings. The method approximates the expectation value by evolving the observable through the circuit gates.

Key observables. Expectation values of Pauli strings, Pauli weight, and coefficient size.

Important parameters / regimes. Number of qubits (up to 28 checked), rotation angles ($ heta$), Pauli weight truncation limit ($w_{ ext{max}}$), and coefficient size threshold ($\epsilon$).

Assumptions / limitations. The method relies on truncation (by Pauli weight and coefficient size) to keep the computation tractable, and the total error is bounded by the sum of discarded coefficient masses.

Figures summary. Figure 1 provides an overview of the PADO-Pauli workflow, illustrating single compilation, step-wise truncation, and subsequent multiple evaluations with gradient tracking.

Paper structure. The paper details the theoretical basis of Pauli propagation, introduces the PADO-Pauli GPU engine for compilation and evaluation, discusses various truncation and noise modeling techniques, and concludes with performance comparisons against state-of-the-art tools.

Abstract

Variational quantum algorithms utilize parameterized circuits many times during training, at new parameter values, over batches of data, and with gradients. Pauli propagation approximates the expectation values of such circuits classically by evolving the observable in the Heisenberg picture as a truncated sum of Pauli strings. We present PADO-Pauli, a GPU engine for Pauli propagation that compiles a circuit and its observables once into sparse matrices in which each rotation angle contributes only a cosine and a sine factor, so every later evaluation reuses the compiled program. It evaluates batches of parameters and data inputs embedded as rotation angles. By default its gradients come from a vector-Jacobian product registered with PyTorch's automatic differentiation, so they flow through the angles to any classical layer that produces them. PADO-Pauli also provides truncation by Pauli weight and coefficient size, and noise channels. Zero-filtering removes terms that do not contribute to an expectation value in the all-zero reference state and makes the batched forward pass about 1.5 times faster. Compared to cuPauliProp on an NVIDIA A100, PADO-Pauli evaluates up to 10 times faster on 4 to 12 qubits and uses up to 3.1 times less memory on 16 qubits. We check its accuracy against exact statevector simulation up to 28 qubits. These features make PADO-Pauli a practical toolkit for training variational quantum algorithms, including quantum machine learning models.

QGrAT: Quantum Grade-Aware Transformer for Jet Tagging

Authors: Pallavi Bhardwaj, Michael Spannowsky
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.17351v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces QGrAT, a novel quantum transformer designed for jet tagging in high-energy physics by embedding the Lorentz symmetry of spacetime into its attention mechanism. It utilizes the structure of the Clifford algebra to define invariant couplings between constituent qubits. The resulting model shows strong performance, demonstrating that explicitly modeling three-particle interactions is key to improving background rejection.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on particle physics, the core methodology—encoding physical symmetries (Lorentz invariance) into the structure of quantum gates and attention mechanisms—is highly relevant for developing physically constrained quantum machine learning models.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of designing compact quantum jet taggers capable of identifying highly boosted heavy particles by incorporating fundamental physical symmetries.

Main result. The proposed QGrAT architecture achieved competitive performance (AUC 0.944) on top tagging benchmarks, with the explicit inclusion of three-particle correlations significantly boosting background rejection.

Method. The method introduces QGrAT, a quantum transformer that encodes geometric features derived from particle four-momenta using the structure of the spacetime Clifford algebra $ ext{C}\ell(1,3)$.

Model / system. The model is a quantum transformer operating on constituent qubits, where attention couplings are determined by Lorentz-invariant inner products derived from the grades of the Clifford algebra. The system is tested using state-vector simulation on Top Tagging benchmarks.

Key observables. Area Under the Curve (AUC), background rejection ($1/\epsilon_B$), and signal efficiency ($\epsilon_S$).

Important parameters / regimes. Ten constituents per jet; the use of grades 0-3 and an auxiliary three-particle qubit.

Assumptions / limitations. The component-wise input encoding makes the complete classifier frame dependent, even though the attention couplings are Lorentz invariant.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the geometric interpretation of the five grades in $ ext{C}\ell(1,3)$ and maps them to the QGrAT features used in the quantum circuit.

Paper structure. The paper builds mathematically by first introducing the Clifford algebra, then proving the Lorentz invariance of the grade-specific inner products, detailing the quantum circuit construction, and finally presenting performance evaluations and analysis.

Abstract

Lorentz symmetry and the three-prong structure of hadronic top decays provide physical guidance for designing compact quantum jet taggers. We introduce QGrAT (Quantum Grade-Aware Transformer), a quantum transformer that incorporates these structures into its interactions between constituent qubits. Geometric features constructed from the constituent four-momenta are organised by the grades of the spacetime Clifford algebra $C\ell(1,3)$, and their grade-specific inner products determine the pairwise attention couplings. For fixed constituents and co-transforming reference vectors, these couplings are Lorentz invariant, although the component-wise input encoding makes the complete classifier frame dependent. An auxiliary qubit introduces an explicit three-particle interaction through products of pairwise Minkowski inner products, while a trainable in-circuit readout converts the resulting quantum state into a classification score. We evaluate the architecture in state-vector simulation on the Top Tagging benchmarks. With ten constituents per jet, the configuration using grades $0\text{-}3$ and the three-particle ancilla requires twelve qubits and $144$ trainable parameters, reaching AUC $0.944 \pm 0.002$ and background rejection $1/\varepsilon_B = 35.7 \pm 2.4\ (96.8 \pm 7.7)$ at signal efficiency $\varepsilon_S = 0.50\ (0.30)$. This performance is competitive with the L-GATr-Slim baseline at matched constituent and parameter budgets. Adding the three-particle ancilla raises background rejection at $50\%$ signal efficiency from $19.9$ to $35.7$, whereas extending the representation to the tested grade-$4$ feature gives no consistent improvement. The study establishes a framework for incorporating spacetime geometry into quantum attention and identifies explicit three-particle correlations as the principal source of improved background rejection among the architectural variations tested.

Summary. This work introduces an entropic descent method to solve the nonconvex optimization problem of maximizing secrecy capacity in wiretap channels. By leveraging relative-entropy identities, the authors prove quantitative descent and convergence for both classical and quantum settings. This provides a powerful, general framework for analyzing information-theoretic limits in secure quantum communication.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical tools developed for convergence in nonconvex optimization (entropic descent, KL arguments) are highly relevant for analyzing complex quantum information tasks where capacity calculations are non-convex.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops and analyzes an entropic descent method to optimize the secrecy capacity of a wiretap channel, which is formulated as a nonconvex optimization problem.

Main result. The method establishes quantitative descent and convergence guarantees for the objective function value for arbitrary finite classical channels, and extends these techniques to noncommuting quantum outputs.

Method. It employs an entropic descent method, utilizing an exact relative-entropy identity to derive a channel-independent majorization bound and an explicit mirror-descent iteration.

Model / system. The model is a wiretap channel, analyzed in both classical and quantum settings. Optimization variables define a randomized encoder, jointly optimizing the input distribution and stochastic preprocessing.

Abstract

We develop and analyze an entropic descent method for a nonconvex optimization problem on probability simplices arising in classical and quantum secure communication. The optimization variables describe a randomized encoder, allowing its input distribution and stochastic preprocessing to be optimized jointly. An exact relative-entropy identity yields a channel-independent majorization bound and an explicit mirror-descent iteration with a fixed unit step size. We establish quantitative descent, convergence of objective values, and stationarity of accumulation points on their support faces for arbitrary finite classical channels, without assuming degradedness or less noisiness. For strictly positive transition matrices, a square-root parametrization and a Kurdyka--Łojasiewicz argument establish finite length of the transformed trajectory and convergence of the entire sequence, including limits on the simplex boundary. For less-noisy channels, a restricted convex formulation admits global convergence with an explicit \(O(1/t)\) objective-error bound. We extend the relative-entropy construction to noncommuting quantum outputs and derive monotone alternating updates for covariant encoders, including correlated preprocessing over a fixed block. Numerical experiments compare the method with analytic benchmarks and constrained optimization, reveal multiple attracting values, and demonstrate strict improvements from stochastic preprocessing.

Abstract

We uncover an intrinsic gyrotropic spin current (IGSC) driven solely by an oscillating magnetic field, arising from the spin version of Zeeman quantum geometry. Using the density matrix formalism, we derive the IGSC in the low-frequency clean limit, in terms of the Zeeman spin quantum metric and the Zeeman spin Berry curvature. The effect is studied in a minimal Rashba-Dresselhaus model that also supports intrinsic gyrotropic charge currents from the Zeeman Berry curvature. The spin and charge currents exhibit a $π/2$ phase shift and are found to be perpendicular to each other for certain parameter ranges. This not only enables spin current detection but also opens pure spin current channels for energy-efficient spintronics. We further observe that these currents switch directions between longitudinal and transverse relative to the applied field, depending on the relative strength of Rashba and Dresselhaus interactions.

Abstract

Conventional theoretical descriptions of moiré superlattices, epitomized by the Bistritzer--MacDonald continuum model, are fundamentally constrained by low-energy Dirac cone approximations and first-shell interlayer coupling truncations. While successful for small-angle twisted bilayer graphene near charge neutrality, these frameworks fail to provide universal analytical solutions for strained lattices, high-energy band behaviors, or arbitrary commensurate geometries. Here, we establish a unified, approximation-free analytical formalism for generalized moiré systems based on three pillars: (i) a rigorous two-dimensional-to-one-dimensional supercell cyclic mapping, (ii) exact block-circulant diagonalization of intralayer Hamiltonians, and (iii) quasi-degenerate Löwdin perturbation theory centered on arbitrary energy degeneracy points across the full spectrum. Unlike traditional schemes anchored to the Dirac point, our approach enables systematic perturbative expansions around any band crossing, unifying the characterization of low-energy flat bands with high-energy degeneracies. We demonstrate that this framework perfectly recovers the Bistritzer--MacDonald model in the low-energy limit while extending its reach to heterostrained systems and high-energy regimes. By incorporating high-order interlayer coupling shells, we reveal an intrinsic scale self-similarity in moiré lattices and predict the emergence of higher-order magic angles and tunable flat bands driven by secondary degeneracies. This work eliminates the long-standing lack of universal analytical tools for diverse moiré platforms, providing a full-spectrum theoretical foundation for band engineering and the exploration of exotic correlated and topological states in van der Waals heterostructures.
