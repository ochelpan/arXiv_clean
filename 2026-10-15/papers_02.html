Summary. This theoretical work proposes using a superconducting diode as a physical realization of a Feynman ratchet to build a nanoscale heat engine. By exploiting the diode's asymmetry and coupling it to a temperature gradient, the device generates work from thermal fluctuations. The analysis confirms its function as a heat engine and demonstrates its ability to operate in reverse as a Peltier cooler.

Why it may be interesting. The work bridges concepts from non-equilibrium statistical mechanics (Feynman ratchets) with solid-state quantum phenomena (superconducting diodes), providing a concrete, tunable model for energy conversion driven by thermal gradients.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to realize a nanoscale heat engine by converting thermal energy into mechanical work using a superconducting diode acting as a Feynman ratchet.

Main result. The setup successfully functions as a heat engine generating work solely from a temperature gradient, and it can also operate in reverse as a Peltier cooler.

Method. The dynamics are analyzed using Langevin equations to model the superconducting phases coupled to two thermal reservoirs at different temperatures.

Model / system. The physical system is a superconducting circuit incorporating a superconducting diode (modeled by Josephson junctions) coupled to two reservoirs at $T_1$ and $T_2$. The dynamics are governed by coupled Langevin equations.

Key observables. Output power ($\dot{W}$), efficiency ($\eta$), open-circuit voltage ($V_1$), and the Seebeck coefficient ($S$).

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature gradient ($\Delta T = T_2 - T_1$), coupling strength ($\kappa$), and external current ($I_{ ext{ext}}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes a small effective inductance ($V_1 \approx V_2$) and that noise sources follow Gaussian distributions.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the circuit setup, the dependence of heat engine efficiency ($\eta$) on the hot reservoir temperature ($T_2$), and the reversal of heat flow when operating as a cooler.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the physical model and the Feynman ratchet concept, develops the theoretical framework using Langevin equations, analyzes the steady-state operation for heat engine work extraction, and finally examines the reverse operation as a Peltier cooler.

Abstract

Feynman's ratchet illustrates how work can be extracted from thermal fluctuations in the presence of two heat baths at different temperatures. While some experimental realizations of the ratchet effect have been studied, most lack miniaturization, which is important for some applications. Here, we propose a physical implementation of Feynman's ratchet based on Josephson junctions exhibiting the superconducting diode effect. This setup functions as a heat engine that generates work solely from a temperature gradient via thermal fluctuations. We analyze its output power and efficiency, and, through the Seebeck coefficient, identify the conditions under which the device operates in reverse, functioning instead as a Peltier cooler.

Summary. This theoretical work demonstrates how tailoring the spatial coupling between emitters and a waveguide can engineer atomic mirrors to achieve broadband reflection. By implementing a Gaussian coupling profile, the paper shows that boundary-induced spectral limitations are overcome, leading to a much wider and flatter high-reflectivity window. This establishes spatial coupling engineering as a practical tool for advanced quantum photonic device design.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete, controllable mechanism—spatial coupling engineering—to overcome fundamental limitations in designing high-performance photonic structures like atomic mirrors, which is highly relevant to quantum optics and open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of broadening the reflection band of atomic mirrors, which is typically limited by boundary effects in finite arrays.

Main result. By engineering the emitter-waveguide coupling profile (e.g., using Gaussian transverse displacements), the reflection bandwidth can be significantly broadened, suppressing unwanted Fabry-Perot oscillations.

Method. The study combines exact scattering calculations with spectral factorization and a semiclassical WKB analysis applied to an effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonian.

Model / system. The system is a finite waveguide Quantum Electrodynamics (QED) array of two-level emitters coupled to a 1D waveguide. The physics is modeled using an effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonian in the single-excitation subspace.

Key observables. Reflection bandwidth, transmission zeros, collective poles, and the finite-size scaling of the bandwidth.

Important parameters / regimes. The coupling profile width ($\sigma$), the number of emitters ($N$), and the interatomic phase ($\varphi_0$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the rotating-wave approximation, linearizes photon dispersion, and neglects propagation delay across the array.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the reflection spectrum $|r|^2$ versus detuning ($\Delta$) for different coupling strengths, showing the transition from oscillatory behavior to a broad plateau.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining the physical system and Hamiltonian, to applying advanced scattering formalisms, demonstrating the effect of coupling engineering on the spectrum, and finally deriving the finite-size scaling law for the bandwidth.

Abstract

We theoretically investigate single-photon coherent transport through a finite waveguide quantum electrodynamics (QED) array with spatially modulated emitter-waveguide couplings. By combining exact scattering calculations with spectral factorization and a semiclassical WKB analysis, we identify how the coupling profile controls the reflection bandwidth and its finite-size dependence. A Gaussian transverse displacement produces an exponential-of-Gaussian decay-rate profile that broadens the high-reflectivity window and suppresses the boundary-induced Fabry-Perot oscillations compared with a uniform array. The transmission zeros remain exactly degenerate at the atomic resonance for arbitrary positive coupling profiles at non-Bragg spacings. In contrast, the collective poles reorganize under the spatial modulation, redistributing their radiative widths and preventing individual subradiant resonances from terminating the reflection window. We further derive an algebraic 1/N scaling law for the contraction of the bandwidth toward its bulk value. The bulk bandwidth is determined by the largest local decay rate, whereas the finite-size correction increases as the profile becomes sharper, leading to an optimal profile width. These results establish spatial coupling engineering as a practical tool for designing broadband atomic mirrors in atom-nanofiber and circuit-QED platforms.

Abstract

Quantum computers are approaching the early fault-tolerant regime. Summary. This paper provides a detailed mathematical analysis of the long-time behavior of dynamical correlation functions using finite-temperature Fredholm determinants. By employing the Effective Form Factor method, the authors determine the asymptotic profile and fix the necessary finite normalization constant. The results confirm theoretical predictions from generalized hydrodynamics for systems like hard rods.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical techniques used to analyze the long-time dynamics of correlation functions are highly relevant for understanding non-equilibrium dynamics in quantum many-body systems, which is a core area of modern AMO and condensed matter theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper studies the long-time, large-distance asymptotics of finite-temperature dynamical Fredholm determinants for correlation functions.

Main result. The analysis successfully determines the transition profile using parabolic-cylinder functions and fixes the finite normalization constant, showing consistency with generalized hydrodynamics predictions.

Method. The authors employ the Effective Form Factor approach, splitting the Fredholm determinant into factors, and solving associated Riccati equations derived from Wronskian analysis.

Model / system. The study concerns dynamical correlation functions in quantum many-body systems, modeled via generalized sine-kernel Fredholm determinants, with connections drawn to the hard-rod gas and the Gaussian $\beta$ ensemble.

Key observables. Dynamical Fredholm determinant $D_\nu(x, t)$, effective phase shift, and the transition profile.

Important parameters / regimes. Finite temperature, long-time/large-distance limits, and the jump parameter $\Delta$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the outer asymptotics are sufficient for the leading behavior, and it relies on solving sewing conditions around a quadratic saddle point.

Figures summary. Not specified.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by establishing the asymptotic form of the determinant, determining the transition profile via solving sewing conditions, and finally fixing the finite normalization constant by matching results from the high-temperature Gaussian ensemble analysis.

Abstract

We study the long-time, large-distance asymptotics of finite-temperature dynamical Fredholm determinants using effective form factors. The method expresses these asymptotics as a functional of an effective phase shift. We determine its two branches on either side of a quadratic stationary point and the transition profile joining them. The profile is expressed through parabolic-cylinder functions. Its connection to the high-temperature Gaussian $β$ ensemble fixes the finite normalization, in agreement with an independent Riemann--Hilbert analysis. We also show that the leading exponential asymptotics suffice to recover the Euler-scale prediction of generalized hydrodynamics for the dynamical structure factor of hard rods.

Abstract

Recent efforts to detect non-Markovian memory have become increasingly important, motivated by the broad relevance of memory effects in quantum information processing, metrology, and thermodynamic engines. Here, we show that interferometric methods, particularly the phase shift and visibility, provide an experimentally accessible framework for probing environmental effects and witnessing non-Markovian dynamics. Specifically, suitably constructed witness operators enable the detection of non-Markovianity through measurable phase shifts in the interference pattern. Furthermore, we prove that the interferometric visibility is monotonically non-increasing under incoherent CP-divisible (Markovian) dynamics. Consequently, any revival of the optimized visibility constitutes a sufficient signature of information backflow and hence non-Markovianity. The effectiveness of the proposed phase-based and visibility approaches is illustrated for representative examples of non-Markovian dynamics. Our results offer a simple and experimentally feasible framework for probing memory effects, with potential applications in quantum sensing and noisy intermediate-scale quantum devices.

Abstract

Quantum mechanics and general relativity are remarkably successful, yet their joint predictions remain beyond direct experimental reach. Photonic interferometers operating in a gravitational field offer a promising route to test this regime: gravitational redshift differentiates photon propagation between vertically separated arms, leading to a predicted phase shift and reduction in interference visibility. These effects are minute on laboratory scales, motivating existing proposals to consider large interferometer areas to accumulate them geometrically. Indeed, such schemes typically require kilometre-scale optical paths to produce appreciable interferometric signals. Here, we propose a strategy that replaces geometric accumulation with spectral enhancement by coupling the two interferometer arms to optical scatterers based on ultranarrow atomic transitions. These transitions serve as local spectral references: gravitational redshift changes the photon's detuning from each resonance, producing large differences in reflection amplitude and hence substantial visibility loss and interferometric phase shift already for centimetre-scale height differences. We quantify the experimental requirements and assess the feasibility of implementation with current or near-term technology. Our proposal thus opens a route to tabletop optical tests of the interplay between quantum mechanics and general relativity.

Abstract

The valence-bond-glass state of the 4d^1 double perovskite Ba2YMoO6 combines Mo^5+ singlets with residual paramagnetic "orphan" spins whose coupling is hard to reach with bulk probes. Together the two channels probe the coupling and dynamics of valence-bond-glass orphan spins.

Summary. This theoretical study explores the resilience of non-Markovian quantum dynamics when the coupling to the environment is subject to unresolved disorder. The authors demonstrate that while disorder can make memory effects unresolvable, the underlying structural non-Markovianity can be preserved, particularly when considering physical bath state fluctuations or transverse coupling terms.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the crucial gap between theoretical structural properties (CP divisibility) and experimental observability (memory signatures) in open quantum systems, which is a major focus in quantum information and AMO physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates whether a quantum system's dynamics can remain structurally non-Markovian (CP indivisible) even when the memory effects become experimentally unresolvable due to unresolved quasi-static disorder in the system-bath coupling.

Main result. Transverse exchange interactions and physical fluctuations in the bath state are shown to preserve the structural non-Markovianity (CP indivisibility) against disorder, even when memory signatures are suppressed.

Method. The analysis uses process tomography to reconstruct disorder-averaged dynamical maps and employs CP divisibility as the primary criterion to distinguish between Markovian and non-Markovian dynamics.

Model / system. The system is an open quantum system, specifically a spin environment (bath) coupled to a system spin. The coupling is modeled as a quasi-static random variable drawn from a Gaussian distribution, and the analysis compares pure dephasing Ising interactions with anisotropic Heisenberg interactions.

Key observables. CP divisibility, return probability ($P_R$), $\ell_1$-norm of coherence, and memory revivals.

Important parameters / regimes. Disorder strength ($\epsilon$), bath polarization, and the presence/absence of transverse exchange coupling.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis distinguishes between the disorder-averaged map and the true realization-resolved dynamics, and notes that the disappearance of memory signatures does not imply CP divisibility.

Figures summary. Not specified.

Paper structure. The paper develops its argument by analyzing the pure dephasing case (fixed vs. fluctuating bath states) and then extends the findings to anisotropic Heisenberg interactions, using analytical extensions and exact diagonalization to confirm the robustness of non-Markovianity.

Abstract

We investigate how unresolved quasistatic disorder in system bath coupling affects non-Markovian dynamics in spin environments. The coupling is fixed within each experimental shot but varies between shots, so repeated shot tomography reconstructs a disorder averaged dynamical map. For a single spin bath with pure dephasing Ising interaction, disorder can drive a transition to CP-divisible dynamics for fixed bath preparations, with a critical strength set by bath polarization along the interaction axis. However, when the bath state fluctuates physically between shots, the averaged dynamics remain CP indivisible at any finite disorder strength. We extend these results analytically to inhomogeneously coupled pure dephasing baths of any number \(N\) of non-interacting spins. At any finite disorder strength, one unpolarized bath spin suffices to retain CP-indivisible dynamics, whereas a fully polarized bath yields Markovian (CP-divisible) dynamics. Dynamics with physically fluctuating bath states likewise remain non-Markovian (CP indivisible) at finite disorder for arbitrary \(N\) in the pure dephasing case. For anisotropic Heisenberg interactions, any nonzero transverse exchange eliminates the disorder driven transition to CP divisibility in a single spin bath. Exact diagonalization of anisotropic models with inhomogeneous couplings up to \(N=12\) reveals persistent trace distance revivals for fixed bath preparations, sampled Haar random preparations, and physically fluctuating bath states, showing that this effect of transverse exchange extends beyond a single bath spin. Increasing disorder still suppresses experimentally accessible revivals and can make memory effects unresolvable at finite measurement resolution even as the dynamics remain CP indivisible. Thus, transverse exchange and physical bath state fluctuations preserve dynamical indivisibility against unresolved coupling disorder.

Abstract

Abstract

Interacting fermions owe both their non-trivial correlations and their computational power to their departure from Gaussianity. Yet, quantifying that departure geometrically faces two challenges: the Gaussian manifold is non-convex, and perturbative treatments are confined to weak interactions. Here, we introduce a geometric measure of fermionic non-Gaussianity, the fidelity distance of a target state to the manifold of fermionic Gaussian states. We construct an iteration formalism to numerically access it, applicable also at strong coupling. Further, we prove a sharpened operator Jensen inequality, of interest in its own right. We use it to derive a witness that involves only two- and four-point fermionic correlators. Numerical benchmarks on the one-dimensional Fermi--Hubbard model show that the quality of the witness improves with system size and, for strong coupling, is tighter than antiflatness. Our results open a scalable route to charting non-Gaussianity of strongly-interacting fermionic quantum simulators.
