Summary. The paper proposes using permanent electric dipoles within a qubit coupled to a cavity to suppress Purcell decay. By exploiting the dipole's ability to create channel-selective field displacements, it achieves significant protection against radiative loss while maintaining necessary readout signals. This offers a pathway for building high-fidelity quantum devices by engineering dissipation itself.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides an advanced theoretical tool for engineering dissipation channels in superconducting or solid-state qubits, moving beyond simple filtering to utilize intrinsic material properties (dipoles) for quantum control.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge addressed is Purcell decay, a loss mechanism in cavity Quantum Electrodynamics (QED) that limits fast qubit readout.

Main result. Permanent electric dipoles can provide an internal, channel-selective form of Purcell protection, significantly increasing the qubit lifetime and reducing readout error rates.

Method. The analysis uses open-system quantum dynamics by calculating dressed decay rates derived from a Lindblad master equation in the system's eigenbasis.

Model / system. The model involves a qubit coupled to a cavity resonator, incorporating an additional interaction term mediated by a permanent electric dipole moment. The Hamiltonian includes transverse ($g_x$) and longitudinal ($g_p$) coupling terms.

Key observables. Normalized lifetime ($\kappa T_1$), single-shot readout error ($\epsilon_{tot}$), and the decay rates ($\Gamma_i$) for different coupling regimes.

Important parameters / regimes. The dimensionless longitudinal coupling $\eta = g_p / \omega_c$, which controls the degree of dipole-induced field displacement.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis compares results from a simplified displaced Rotating-Wave Approximation (RWA) against a more rigorous full-transverse model, noting that the RWA zero is not physically exact.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the competition between transverse and longitudinal couplings, showing how the decay rate suppression shifts from an analytical RWA zero ($\eta=\sqrt{2}$) to a larger operating point ($\eta_{FT}=2.60$) in the full model.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem of Purcell decay, develops a Hamiltonian incorporating dipole coupling, calculates open-system decay rates using dressed states, and benchmarks performance improvements by comparing RWA predictions against full-transverse calculations.

Abstract

Reading out a qubit often requires coupling it to a resonator, but that same resonator can also give the qubit an extra path to decay. Here, we study a way to reduce this loss using a built-in permanent electric dipole. The dipole shifts the cavity field in different directions for the qubit ground and excited states. This shift makes the relevant wave functions overlap less, which weakens the transverse qubit--cavity exchange that causes Purcell decay. In a simplified displaced rotating-wave model, this exchange vanishes at $η=\sqrt{2}$. In the full transverse model, this exact zero is lifted, but strong suppression remains at a larger dipole-induced displacement. Using dressed open-system decay rates, we find an operating point where the cavity-mediated decay is strongly reduced while the longitudinal readout signal remains finite. For the benchmark studied here, at fixed pointer separation, the normalized lifetime increases from $κT_1=11.1$ to $47.3$, and the estimated single-shot readout error drops from $0.21$ to $0.07$. These results show that permanent electric dipoles can provide an internal, channel-selective form of Purcell protection.

Abstract

Abstract

Our results provide a way for realizing dynamical fluctuations and long-range correlations in strongly interacting driven-dissipative open quantum systems.

Summary. Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to determine the universal mechanism behind Andreev reflection (a charge-conjugating boundary condition) when it appears in seemingly unrelated quantum impurity problems, such as those related to the two-channel Kondo effect.

Main result. Andreev reflection is shown to emerge naturally from a purely lattice duality defect—specifically, it corresponds to a one-site translation of Majorana operators within the folded transverse-field Ising chain model.

Method. The authors use theoretical mapping techniques, transforming the Ising Hamiltonian into a Majorana representation and analyzing scattering dynamics by 'folding' the system at an impurity site.

Model / system. The primary model is based on the transverse-field Ising chain, which is analyzed via its Majorana fermion formulation. Key observables. Scattering dynamics of wavepackets, charge fluctuation ($\delta n(x)$), comparison between free vs. EK boundary conditions, and conservation laws for axial $U(1)_A$ symmetry.

Important parameters / regimes. The system is analyzed in the continuum limit and at the interface between a Kitaev-chain SPT phase and gapless chains; the two-channel Kondo problem serves as a key physical realization.

Abstract

Andreev reflection converts an incoming fermion into an outgoing hole and is usually tied to a superconducting interface. We show that an analogous charge-conjugating boundary condition emerges from a purely lattice duality defect. Starting from a Majorana representation of the transverse-field Ising chain, we construct a folded lattice model in which a boundary Majorana impurity implements a one-site translation of a staggered Majorana chain. In the continuum, this translation becomes a chiral fermion-parity defect: it flips the sign of the only left-moving Majorana mode while leaving the right-moving mode unchanged. When the two Majorana modes are recombined into a complex fermion in the folded geometry, this sign flip becomes the Andreev-like boundary condition. Our lattice formulation gives a microscopic interpretation of the Emery--Kivelson boundary of the two-channel Kondo problem and of Maldacena--Ludwig monopole scattering, while identifying the boundary as the interface between a Kitaev-chain SPT phase and a gapless chain. The same Majorana translation defect also provides a lattice realization of an axial $U(1)_A$-symmetric charge-flip boundary.

Abstract

Abstract

We present an efficient protocol for learning an unknown $k$-local Lindblad generator on $n$ qubits using only product-state preparations, short-time evolution, and single-qubit Pauli measurements, without prior knowledge of the interaction structure. To our knowledge, these are the first efficient learning guarantees for general $k$-local dissipative quantum dynamics under such limited experimental control.
