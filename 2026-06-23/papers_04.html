Summary. This work models human mobility on a 2D lattice by assuming that trip destinations are biased based on the ratio of their distance from home relative to the starting point's distance. By simulating this process, the authors demonstrate that even simple rules can reproduce complex statistical features like power-law decay in movement distributions. Why it may be interesting. Although applied to social physics, the underlying dynamics involve non-equilibrium processes, anomalous diffusion ($\langle r^2 angle \sim t^ u$), and scaling laws derived from optimizing movement costs—concepts relevant to energy minimization in complex quantum systems or understanding transport in disordered media.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the fundamental dynamical rules governing human mobility patterns by exploiting empirical correlations between trip endpoints and the starting point (home). The goal is to model how movement decisions are influenced by spatial constraints relative to a fixed origin.

Main result. A simple movement dynamic, where transition probabilities depend on the ratio of endpoint distances to the home, can qualitatively reproduce key empirical statistics of human mobility, such as power-law distributions for traveled distances and visitation frequencies. The model also incorporates temporal effects like queue waiting times.

Method. The authors develop and simulate a discrete-time movement process on a 2D lattice using master equations to track site occupancy probabilities over time steps.

Model / system. The system is an agent moving on a square lattice, where the transition probability $W_t(a o b)$ is governed by an exponential function of the ratio of distances from the origin: $\exp\left\{-J (r_{b,O}/r_{a,O}) ight\}$. The model can be extended to include waiting times in queues.

Important parameters / regimes. The control parameter $J$ dictates the strength of the 'distance from home' bias; waiting time parameters like $\gamma$ modulate temporal effects.

Assumptions / limitations. The core assumption is that movement probability depends on the ratio of endpoint distances to the origin. The model assumes users perform sequential trips satisfying demands in a structured manner.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate how efficiency metrics ($\eta_r, \eta_v$) and exploration probabilities vary with $J$, showing optimal regimes; other figures compare Cumulative Distribution Functions (CDFs) for travel distance, waiting time, and visits under different queueing assumptions.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the motivation from empirical observations regarding endpoint correlations. It then develops a mathematical model based on these ratios, derives the master equations for site occupancy and distance distribution, and finally analyzes the results by varying parameters like $J$ (and $\gamma$) to match known statistical behaviors.

Abstract

Discovering the fundamental dynamical rules that generate the main statistical features of human mobility is essential for understanding the mechanisms underlying such processes. A prominent example is the exploration and preferential return model and its generalizations, which successfully reproduce several empirical findings. Here, we exploit another observation: the endpoint distances of a trip from the trajectory's starting point are strongly correlated. We consider a movement process in which each user performs a sequence of trips to satisfy a set of demands, given a spatial distribution of suppliers on a two-dimensional lattice. In each trip, destinations are chosen with a probability that depends on the ratio of the initial and final distances from the user's origin (home). We show that even a single agent with uniformly distributed demands and suppliers qualitatively reproduces key empirical statistics, such as the power-law distribution of traveled distances. The results are also robust to introducing interactions between agents via queues and incorporating more realistic demand and supplier distributions.

Summary. This paper introduces an Irreversible Quantum-Enhanced Markov-Chain Monte Carlo (IQEMC) algorithm to improve sampling efficiency for physical models like the Ising spin glass. By intentionally breaking detailed balance using energy-dependent proposals, IQEMC achieves faster convergence than standard QEMC methods. This work establishes irreversibility as a powerful and physically justifiable tool for accelerating quantum simulations.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a concrete, physically grounded mechanism—breaking detailed balance via energy dependence—to enhance sampling efficiency in complex systems, which is relevant for simulating non-equilibrium dynamics or highly frustrated quantum states.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The research investigates whether introducing irreversibility into Quantum-Enhanced Markov-Chain Monte Carlo (QEMC) sampling can accelerate convergence rates compared to standard methods.

Main result. An irreversible quantum-enhanced MCMC (IQEMC) method was developed that successfully accelerates sampling by exploiting energy dependence in the proposal mechanism, establishing irreversibility as a physical enhancement route for QEMC.

Method. The authors modify the transition kernel by introducing state-dependent proposals guided by Landau-Zener transitions, ensuring global balance while violating detailed balance to accelerate mixing.

Model / system. The study uses the Ising model (e.g., fully connected random Ising model) whose equilibrium distribution is governed by a classical Hamiltonian $H$. The sampling process involves quantum evolution approximated via Trotterization.

Key observables. Spectral gap ($\delta$), Autocorrelation Function (ACF), Integrated Autocorrelation Time ($ au_{ ext{int}}$), and average magnetization $\langle m angle$.

Important parameters / regimes. System size ($n$), temperature ($T$), driving strength ($C$), and the state-dependent sweep rate $k(s_i)$.

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes that the global balance condition can be maintained while violating detailed balance, and convergence is characterized by the spectral gap derived from the Markov generator.

Figures summary. Figures compare IQEMC's superior performance over QEMC and annealing baselines on spin-glass benchmarks; one figure illustrates the energy-adaptive nature of the proposal distribution modulation.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by establishing the theoretical motivation (classical irreversibility), developing the IQEMC Hamiltonian, analyzing its convergence using spectral gap/autocorrelation time diagnostics, and benchmarking it against existing QEMC and classical annealing methods on spin-glass models.

Abstract

Detailed balance underlies conventional Markov-chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithms. Yet in classical systems, breaking detailed balance generates irreversible probability currents and can accelerate sampling. Whether irreversibility can similarly enhance quantum MCMC remains an intriguing question. Here we show that irreversibility provides a new route to improving the recent quantum-enhanced MCMC (QEMC), which combines quantum proposals with classical acceptance. By introducing state-dependent proposals that break detailed balance while preserving the target stationary distribution, we develop an irreversible quantum-enhanced Monte Carlo (IQEMC). Guided by Landau-Zener transitions, IQEMC promotes large energy descents from high-energy states while maintaining stable transitions near low-energy states. On spin-glass benchmarks, IQEMC outperforms QEMC without increasing computational complexity and, unlike the annealing baseline, exhibits a spectral gap that increases with system size and annealing speed. These results establish irreversibility as a physically grounded mechanism for enhancing quantum MCMC.

Model / system. The physical system is grain boundaries in polycrystalline materials subjected to constant strain-rate shear loading. The dynamics are modeled by an overdamped Langevin equation for discrete disconnections, which is coarse-grained into the fKPZ equation.

Abstract

The kinetic roughening of driven grain boundaries (GBs) is fundamentally mediated by the collective behavior of disconnections, whose long-range $1/r$ elastic interactions distinguish them from conventional growing surfaces. Through large-scale molecular dynamics simulations and a nonlocal fractional Kardar-Parisi-Zhang (fKPZ) theory, we demonstrate that driven GBs undergo a sharp transition of morphology at the yield point, from the quenched Edwards-Wilkinson universality class ($H \approx 0.33$) to an anomalous fKPZ regime ($H \approx 0.8$), while the 1/r nonlocal elasticity manifests as the fractional relaxation of GB morphology. The results show that the avalanche noise arrests the gradient catastrophe induced by the KPZ nonlinearity. Governed by the nonlocal elastic kernels, the resulting morphology breaks the Gaussian self-affinity and parallels the universality class of dynamic fracture.

Summary. This paper develops advanced theoretical protocols to probabilistically store and retrieve unknown unitary superchannels, which model complex quantum processes with intervention slots. By comparing partial teleportation against a novel 'staircase backstitch' method, the authors demonstrate that the latter achieves asymptotically perfect retrieval probability for retrospective interventions. Why it may be interesting. This work pushes the boundaries of quantum memory by tackling higher-order temporal structures (superchannels) and introducing 'retrospective' control. The comparison between asymptotic protocols is highly relevant for understanding fundamental limits on quantum information storage.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The research addresses the challenging task of Probabilistic Storage-and-Retrieval (pSAR) for unknown quantum superchannels, extending previous work to higher-order structures involving 'definite-causal unitary superchannels' that require 'retrospective' intervention.

Main result. Two protocols are proposed: partial teleportation and staircase backstitch. The staircase backstitch protocol is shown to achieve unit success probability asymptotically as the number of storage queries increases, surpassing earlier methods in the large N limit.

Method. The authors employ advanced quantum information theory tools, including formalizing pSAR using Choi operators, solving constraints via Semidefinite Programming (SDP), and comparing different circuit constructions like partial teleportation versus deterministic backstitching.

Model / system. The core model is the quantum superchannel ($ ext{eC}$), which represents a sequence of unitary channels with open slots allowing arbitrary interventions. The focus is on unitary superchannels ($ ext{eU}$) and their probabilistic storage/retrieval capability.

Key observables. Maximum success probability ($p_{\max}$), the ability to achieve 'retrospective' intervention, and the comparison between different pSAR protocols (e.g., $p_{ ext{tele}}^N$ vs. asymptotic limit).

Important parameters / regimes. Number of storage queries ($N$), number of slots ($K$), and the dimensions of associated Hilbert spaces $(d_0, \dots, d_{2K+1})$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis focuses on definite causal structures (not indefinite ones), and some results rely on SDP formulations which provide upper bounds for multi-slot cases.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the structure of $K$-slot quantum superchannels, the process of obtaining a staircase from a superchannel, and detailed circuit diagrams for both partial teleportation and staircase backstitch protocols.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first formalizing pSAR for unitary superchannels (Section 2), proposing and comparing two distinct protocols in Section 3, deriving structural properties using mathematical tools like the comb formalism, and finally analyzing optimal probabilities via SDPs and symmetry arguments in later sections.

Abstract

Storing an unknown quantum computation in a quantum state and retrieving it at a desired later time is a challenging task, hindered by the no-programming theorem of quantum computations. In the previous studies on the task of probabilistic storage-and-retrieval (pSAR) of quantum channels, the maximum probability of exactly retrieving a single unknown unitary channel from a quantum state in which the unknown unitary has been encoded via multiple calls to the unknown unitary channel is derived. In this work, we consider a higher-order version of pSAR, the probabilistic storage-and-retrieval of definite-causal unitary superchannels, which are physically modeled by sequences of unitary channels with open slots where arbitrary channels can be inserted between the unitary channels for intervention. This task requires activating the ``retrospective'' intervention functionality on the superchannel, beyond its normal intervention functionality. We propose two protocols: partial teleportation, which is optimal for a small number of storage queries, and staircase backstitch, which achieves unit success probability asymptotically as the number of queries increases. We also derive a universal inversion protocol for unitary superchannels.

Summary. This theoretical paper investigates the emergence of classical Boolean facts from complex quantum dynamics using algebraic methods. It shows that if a system undergoes repeated, state-preserving coarse-graining, the resulting macroscopic structure will possess classical (Boolean) properties only if the limiting algebra is asymptotically abelian. This provides a mathematical constraint on how 'classicality' can arise naturally in fundamental quantum theories.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous mathematical criterion—asymptotic commutativity under coarse-graining—for when quantum correlations naturally collapse into classical probabilistic structures, which is highly relevant for understanding decoherence and measurement in open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The central question is whether classical Boolean facts can emerge naturally from local, completely positive quantum operations (coarse-graining) without being imposed as fundamental axioms.

Main result. If the coarse-graining process leads to an infrared range that is asymptotically abelian, then the resulting algebra of 'exact' macroscopic events forms a complete Boolean algebra.

Method. The analysis uses operator algebras, defining an 'influence algebra' based on quantum causal processes and examining the limit (Cesaro mean) of repeated coarse-graining channels.

Model / system. The framework is built upon Relational Quantum Causal Processes (RQCP), involving local von Neumann algebras subjected to state-preserving normal unital completely positive coarse-graining channels ($\Gamma$).

Key observables. Exact events are identified as projections within the center of the influence algebra, $ ext{Proj}(\mathcal{Z}(I_{C;B}^Y))$, and the key diagnostic is the asymptotic abelianness of the infrared range.

Important parameters / regimes. The convergence rate ($\gamma$) in exponential suppression estimates, and the limit $N o \infty$ for the Cesaro averages.

Assumptions / limitations. The result relies on assuming state-preserving normal unital completely positive coarse-graining channels and requires the infrared range to be asymptotically abelian.

Paper structure. The paper first defines the algebraic machinery for identifying events via influence algebras. It then analyzes the dynamics using Markovian coarse-graining, culminating in theorems that link asymptotic abelianness of the resulting algebra to the structure being a complete Boolean algebra.

Abstract

We formulate an operator-algebraic mechanism by which exact Boolean records can arise from local completely positive quantum operations without being imposed as microscopic structure. The kinematic input is an algebraic process functional assigning probabilities to local normal completely positive operations in a finite operational context. From the predual response of a target algebra to source interventions, relative to a background strategy class, we define an influence algebra; exact events are then, by definition, the projections in its center. The dynamical question is whether nontrivial centers can be generated by coarse-graining rather than inserted through split-record laboratories. We address this question using state-preserving normal unital completely positive coarse-graining channels. If the Cesaro means of such a channel converge to a Choi-Effros infrared range and the range is asymptotically abelian in the GNS seminorm, then the represented infrared algebra is a commutative von Neumann algebra. Its projection lattice is therefore a complete Boolean algebra. We also give a finite-sector block-primitive criterion, motivated by locality and scrambling, which implies this asymptotic abelianness with exponential suppression of off-sector coherences and intra-sector fluctuations. The result is a conservative mathematical statement: classical facts are not identified with arbitrary projections of a Type-III local algebra, but with central projections selected by an asymptotically abelian completely positive infrared limit.

Phys. 20, 610-614 (2024)]. However, further investigation of the intrinsic properties of this system has been hindered by the lack of large, high-quality single crystals. Abstract

Disordered stealthy hyperuniform materials combine liquid-like statistical isotropy with crystal-like homogeneity, suppressed density fluctuations at large length scales, bounded holes, and an isotropic structure factor that vanishes for a finite range of wavevectors. This combination yields unusual physical properties, including optical transparency, effective delocalization, ultrafast spreadability, optimal conductivity, and complete isotropic photonic bandgaps. Gyromorphs, point patterns whose structure factor includes rings of Bragg-like peaks arranged with discrete $G$-fold rotational symmetry, were recently introduced as counterexamples: disordered media that can somehow achieve the same physical properties, in some cases with higher performance, without stealthiness or hyperuniformity. In this paper, we resolve the puzzle of how gyromorphs fit consistently with the stealthy hyperuniform studies. We first show that gyromorphs are actually hyperuniform and, in the large-$G$ limit where they become nearly isotropic, belong to the weakest form of hyperuniformity, known as Class III. Thus, gyromorphs should have comparatively degraded physical properties compared to stealthy hyperuniform media, which belong to the strongest form of hyperuniformity, known as Class I. We verify this expectation using the rigorous spectral Green's matrix method for the calculation of the density of states (DOS) and Purcell factors in large arrays of electric dipoles. We find that gyromorphs display size-dependent pseudogaps richly populated by localized states rather than smooth band gaps like those found for highly stealthy hyperuniform materials or in deterministic structures such as Vogel spiral and triangular lattices. Furthermore, we predict similar disorder-induced degradation relative to stealthy hyperuniformity with regard to transparency, spreadability and diffusion properties.
