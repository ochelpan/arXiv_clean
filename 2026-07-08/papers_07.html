Canonical quantization of neurons

Authors: Alexander He, Nana Liu, Mark M. Wilde
Type: both · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.05000v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work establishes canonical quantization as a principled method to build quantum analogues of artificial neurons. By replacing classical energy functions with Hamiltonians and applying activation functions via matrix calculus, they create measurable 'activation observables'. The resulting quantized neural networks show superior expressive power over classical models when trained on quantum data.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a concrete framework for translating concepts from classical computation (ML) into the language of quantum operators and measurements, which is highly relevant for developing quantum machine learning algorithms that go beyond simple state encoding.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper applies canonical quantization, a physics procedure, to construct quantum models for machine learning primitives like neurons.

Main result. Quantized neurons exhibit enhanced expressive capabilities compared to their classical counterparts on representative learning tasks.

Method. Hybrid quantum-classical (HQC) algorithms are developed to train and evaluate these quantized neurons by estimating the expectation value of an activation observable $\varphi(H( heta))$ on input quantum states.

Model / system. The model quantizes a classical neuron defined by an energy function and an activation function. The classical energy function is mapped to a quantum Hamiltonian $H_C( heta)$, and the activation function becomes a matrix functional applied to this Hamiltonian, yielding an 'activation observable' $\varphi(H)$.

Key observables. The primary observable is the expectation value of the activation observable, $ ext{Tr}[\varphi(H( heta)) ho]$, used in minimizing the mean squared error loss function.

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature $T$ (used in hyperbolic tangent activation), and Hamiltonian parameters $ heta$ defining the quantum circuit/Hamiltonian.

Assumptions / limitations. The initial focus is on qubits with input variables restricted to $\{-1, 1\}$, requiring basic primitives like Hadamard test and Hamiltonian simulation for implementation.

Figures summary. Figure 3 compares training performance (Squared Loss) between noncommuting ($ ext{HTFIM}$) and commuting ($ ext{HIM}$) Hamiltonian models, showing the former outperforms the latter.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces quantizing a neuron via canonical quantization. It then develops HQC algorithms for learning observables from quantum data by minimizing a loss function based on measuring the activation observable. Finally, it compares different types of Hamiltonians used in these models.

Abstract

Canonical quantization provides a systematic procedure for constructing quantum models from classical Hamiltonians. Here, we apply this principle to a fundamental computational primitive of machine learning: the neuron. Specifically, by viewing a neuron as a composition of an energy function and an activation function, we quantize this model by replacing the energy function with a quantum Hamiltonian and applying the activation function to it through matrix functional calculus. This results in an activation observable that can be measured on an input quantum state. We investigate the use of these quantized neurons for function approximation, where the objective is to learn an unknown observable from labeled quantum data. For this purpose, we develop hybrid quantum-classical algorithms for training and evaluation, including procedures for measuring the activation observable and estimating gradients of the squared loss error. Our algorithms for gradient estimation rely on basic primitives like classical random sampling, the Hadamard test, and Hamiltonian simulation, and those for measuring an activation observable rely on quantum algorithms known as the power of one qumode and Schroedingerization. Numerical experiments demonstrate that our quantized neurons exhibit enhanced expressive capabilities relative to corresponding classical neurons on representative learning tasks. Our work establishes canonical quantization as a principled framework for constructing quantum machine learning primitives and provides a foundation for developing neural architectures tailored to quantum data.

Charge-Sector Construction of the Type-IIB Axion--Dilaton Wormhole Partition Function

Authors: Soo-Jong Rey

Summary. This paper constructs the partition function for a Type-IIB axion--dilaton wormhole using a charge-sector expansion. It analyzes this function by deriving constraints on its coefficients, showing how imposing physical requirements—such as positivity or symmetry—imposes deep mathematical structure on the resulting theta series.

Why it may be interesting. While highly theoretical, the rigorous development of coefficient constraints based on physical assumptions (like positivity leading to moment functions) mirrors techniques used in quantum field theory and statistical mechanics for ensuring physical consistency.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is constructing the Type-IIB axion--dilaton wormhole partition function, Z_wh(theta; b), by systematically analyzing its dependence on charge-sector data.

Main result. The paper establishes a hierarchy of mathematical constraints—including discrete symmetry covariance, moment positivity, and Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities—that the coefficients of this theta expansion must satisfy depending on the physical assumptions made.

Method. The method involves deriving the partition function through a multi-step process: calculating an initial coefficient matrix from semiclassical saddle points in each charge sector, followed by a reduction operation using specific data (b) to yield scalar coefficients W_nu[b].

Model / system. The model is Type-IIB string theory compactification involving wormhole geometries. The analysis focuses on the resulting partition function expressed as a Fourier series in the variable theta, conjugate to an integer axion charge.

Key observables. The wormhole partition function Z_wh(theta; b), the reduced scalar coefficients W_nu[b], and the absolute bound Z_abs[b].

Important parameters / regimes. The compact Fourier variable theta, the integer charge index nu, and the reduction data vector b.

Assumptions / limitations. Assumptions range from assuming positive reduced coefficients (leading to moment positivity) to assuming the unreduced coefficient matrix forms a positive quadratic form. The final microscopic calculation of the coefficients remains an external task.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates geometric objects related to the wormhole throat and two-end operators. Figure 2 summarizes the mathematical flow: Saddle Calculation -> Coefficient Matrix C_ij^nu -> Reduction R_b -> Scalar Coefficients W_nu[b] -> Partition Function Z_wh(theta; b).

Paper structure. The paper follows a logical progression from defining the physical object (wormhole partition function) to deriving its mathematical structure via charge-sector expansion, and finally analyzing this structure by imposing increasingly stringent physical constraints (symmetry, positivity, etc.) on the resulting coefficients.

Abstract

I construct the Type-IIB axion--dilaton wormhole partition function from charge-sector data. In a chosen axion charge, equivalently form-field flux sector, the long-distance saddle calculation supplies a two-end operator term with coefficient matrix \(C^{ij}_ν\). The labels \(i,j\) label end-insertion operators; the labels \(A,B\) label parent universes. Reduction data \(b\) convert this matrix into scalar coefficients \(W_ν[b]\). The wormhole partition function in the theta variable is \(Z_{\rm wh}(θ;b)=\sum_νW_ν[b]\e^{iνθ}\). I analyze properties and constraints this coefficients satisfy: discrete-symmetry covariance, phase, absolute bounds, moment positivity, Cauchy--Schwarz inequalities for the unreduced coefficient matrix, complex-\(θ\) domains, charge-lattice tails, and the dilute Bessel/Skellam limit. The \(θ\)-dependence of the wormhole partition function is the Fourier transform of the charge-sector scalar coefficients.

Data-Driven Prediction of NaCl-Type Entropy-Stabilized Oxide Compositions from First-Principles and Supervised Learning

Authors: Sebastien Junier, Celine Barreteau, David Berardan, Yann-Andrev Kerneur, Jean-Claude Crivello
Type: theory · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.04502v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: methods for driven-dissipative 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work presents a computational framework that merges Density Functional Theory, Special Quasirandom Structures, and supervised Machine Learning to screen for stable Entropy-Stabilized Oxides. By training ML models on DFT data from thousands of potential compositions, the authors efficiently predict phase stability and guide experimentalists toward novel materials candidates.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on materials science, the core methodology—combining high-fidelity quantum simulations (DFT) with machine learning to predict complex many-body properties in disordered systems—is highly relevant to modeling strongly correlated or disordered quantum materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is identifying stable Entropy-Stabilized Oxide (ESO) compositions within vast, complex multicomponent spaces by accurately assessing their thermodynamic stability against competing phases.

Main result. The developed data-driven framework successfully screens thousands of potential quinary ESOs, providing a computationally efficient route to guide experimental synthesis efforts.

Method. A high-throughput workflow combines first-principles DFT calculations (using SQS supercells) with supervised machine learning models (like MLP and Random Forest) trained to predict stability metrics.

Model / system. The system under study is the $ ext{NaCl}$-type structure of quinary Entropy-Stabilized Oxides, $(\{M_i\})O$, involving up to 16 different cation species. Stability is assessed by calculating the formation enthalpy relative to the convex hull.

Key observables. Formation enthalpy deviation from the convex hull ($\Delta_{ ext{hull}}H$) and the stabilization temperature ($T_{ ext{stab}}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The test error for the optimized ML model was reported as $\approx 4 ext{ kJ}\cdot ext{mol}^{-1}$. The calculations cover up to quinary mixtures.

Assumptions / limitations. Absolute stabilization temperatures are affected by systematic thermodynamic approximations, and magnetic ordering in the database was not fully optimized due to computational cost.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the performance of ML models (prediction vs. actual $\Delta_{ ext{hull}}H$) and show convergence tests for SQS supercell sizes and k-point meshes.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the physical problem, details the combined DFT/SQS/ML methodology, presents results on screening thousands of compositions, and concludes by establishing an efficient data-driven search protocol for ESOs.

Abstract

Entropy-stabilized oxides (ESOs) open access to vast multicomponent compositional spaces, but identifying promising candidates remains challenging because of the large number of possible mixtures and the need to assess their stability against competing phases. In this work, we develop a high-throughput computational framework to screen equimolar quinary ESOs in the NaCl structure type by combining density functional theory (DFT), special quasirandom structures (SQS), convex-hull thermodynamics, and supervised machine learning. A consistent reference database of binary and ternary ordered oxides, including disordered phases such as all binary cation combinations in the NaCl-type oxide, is first constructed using GGA and meta-GGA calculations. Quinary disordered phases are then described by SQS supercells and used to train machine-learning models that predict the distance to the convex hull and the corresponding stabilization temperature over the full set of 4368 possible equimolar quinary compositions generated from 16 cation species. Among the tested models, an optimized multilayer perceptron provides the best predictive performance, with a test error of about 4 kJ/mol, while requiring explicit DFT calculations for only about 10% of the quinary systems. Comparison with experimental synthesis tests and computed decomposition paths further shows that the approach captures the main stability trends and the dominant competing phases, although absolute stabilization temperatures remain affected by systematic thermodynamic approximations. These results establish an efficient route for the data-driven exploration of multicomponent oxides and provide practical guidance for the experimental search for new ESOs.

Emergent cosmology and gravity from quantum time?

Authors: Ovidiu Cristinel Stoica
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.05020v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: non-equilibrium dynamics 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper explores whether General Relativity can emerge purely from quantum mechanics by treating time as an intrinsic observable derived from a stationary wavefunction's amplitude. By enforcing uniformity on this probability density, the authors successfully derive the FLRW cosmological model without needing to input gravitational dynamics initially. This suggests that spacetime curvature itself might be an emergent feature of fundamental quantum time structures.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on cosmology, the core technique—deriving macroscopic dynamics (like curvature) from the structure of quantum wavefunctions using emergent time parameters—is highly relevant to open quantum systems theory where time evolution is often non-trivial or observer-dependent.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The central problem is determining if General Relativity (or modifications thereof) can emerge from fundamental quantum principles by interpreting time as an intrinsic observable rather than a background parameter.

Main result. By defining an intrinsic time $\tau$ based on the stationary wavefunction's amplitude, the dynamics naturally reproduce the Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) cosmological model without initially presupposing gravity.

Method. The methodology involves interpreting the squared amplitude of a stationary quantum state as a probability density for intrinsic time and deriving an effective Schrödinger equation with respect to this emergent time coordinate.

Model / system. The analysis uses quantum cosmology, starting from stationary solutions in four-dimensional Euclidean space. The system is modeled using a radial eigenvalue equation derived from the full Schrödinger equation, which is then reduced to a 1D form for mathematical tractability.

Key observables. Curvature emerging in the time direction ($\tau$), the FLRW metric components ($g_{00}, g_{ij}$), and the probability density $P(\tau) \propto |\Psi(\tau)|^2$.

Important parameters / regimes. The intrinsic time coordinate $\tau$, the effective potential $V_{\text{eff}}(r)$, and cosmological parameters like $\Omega_{m0}, \Omega_{r0}, \Omega_{\Lambda 0}$.

Assumptions / limitations. Key assumptions include interpreting the squared amplitude as a probability density for intrinsic time, assuming uniformization of this probability density ($\frac{dP}{d\tau} = \text{const}$), and analyzing spherically symmetric solutions.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the mathematical framework using stationary states in Euclidean space, then deriving an effective time evolution equation via intrinsic observables, and finally demonstrating how this leads to a standard FLRW metric consistent with observed cosmology.

Abstract

Macroscopic observables allow the recovery of intrinsic dynamics from stationary quantum states. I show that, by interpreting the squared amplitude as the probability density for each definite value of intrinsic time, a curvature emerges in the time direction. For example, from the perspective of intrinsic quantum time, the Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker cosmological model emerges from spherically symmetric stationary solutions in four-dimensional Euclidean space, without presupposing gravity. If there is no unique direction of time, curvature emerges in all spacetime dimensions, without presupposing gravity, from the variable amplitude of the stationary wavefunction alone. This opens a new possibility that general relativity or some modification of it emerges from intrinsic time observables.

Holographic heat engines for Schwarzschild black holes

Authors: Ripunjay Dwivedi, Manus R. Visser
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.03749v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: non-equilibrium dynamics 1/5

Summary. This paper develops a framework to analyze reversible heat engines powered by black holes confined within a cavity using quasi-local gravity. By mapping the black hole's thermodynamics onto standard P-V and T-S diagrams, the authors derive efficiencies for various cycles. The key finding is that the regenerated Stirling efficiency approaches the Carnot limit at high temperatures, providing insights into the fundamental thermodynamic constraints imposed by gravitational systems.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on general relativity, the rigorous application of classical thermodynamic cycles (Carnot, Stirling) to a gravitational system provides a powerful analogy for understanding energy conversion limits in complex physical systems, which has parallels in open quantum systems thermodynamics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study constructs reversible black hole heat engines operating within a finite cavity using quasi-local gravitational thermodynamics.

Main result. Exact efficiencies for standard heat engines (Carnot, Otto, etc.) are derived, showing that the regenerated Stirling efficiency approaches the Carnot bound in the high-temperature limit for the large branch.

Method. The analysis employs the quasi-local formalism to define thermodynamic potentials (E, S, P, V) on a boundary enclosing a Schwarzschild black hole. Standard thermodynamic cycles are then mapped onto these gravitational variables to calculate efficiencies.

Model / system. The system models a four-dimensional Schwarzschild black hole enclosed in a spherical cavity ($r_B$). The working substance is dual to the thermal system on this cavity boundary, allowing for the application of classical thermodynamics concepts (P-V, T-S diagrams).

Key observables. Engine efficiencies ($\eta$), heat transfers ($\delta Q$, $\delta W$), and thermodynamic potentials derived from quasi-local gravity.

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis is restricted to the large black hole branch for stability; high-temperature limits ($T_h o \infty$) are crucial for comparing with Carnot bounds.

Assumptions / limitations. The construction assumes asymptotically flat spacetime and relies on a quasi-local description of gravity, requiring specific boundary conditions (e.g., 'flat-space background subtraction').

Figures summary. Figures illustrate constant-property paths (P-V and T-S) for both the large and small black hole branches, detailing how different strokes traverse the thermodynamic space.

Paper structure. The paper systematically derives fundamental thermodynamic relations using quasi-local gravity, then applies these relations to calculate efficiencies for various standard heat engine cycles. The focus culminates in a detailed comparison of the regenerated Stirling cycle efficiency against the Carnot limit in the high-temperature regime.

Abstract

We construct reversible black hole heat engines in asymptotically flat spacetime using quasi-local gravitational thermodynamics. We enclose a Schwarzschild black hole in a finite spherical cavity and identify the working substance with the dual thermal system on the cavity boundary. The surface pressure and boundary area define a thermodynamic pressure-volume pair in the dual system, while all coupling constants of the gravitational theory remain fixed. We derive exact efficiencies for the Carnot, Otto, Diesel, Brayton, and Stirling engines and compare them numerically. These efficiencies probe the quasi-local equations of state of the black hole. The regenerated Stirling efficiency remains remarkably close to the Carnot bound and approaches it in the high-temperature limit.

It distinguishes between methodological needs when improving the exchange-correlation functional versus approximating kinetic energy functionals in orbital-free approaches. Abstract

We overview similarities and, importantly, differences in computational bottlenecks and accuracy requirements that can be addressed with machine learning (ML) and artificial intelligence (AI) techniques in electronic and nuclear DFT. From these follow different promising methodological and algorithmic choices depending on whether one machine learns the exchange correlation (XC) functional, the kinetic energy functional (KEF), the density or the basis functions. In particular, while the popular deep neural networks remain a potent choice in the context of KS DFT, we highlight their disadvantages when building KEFs and highlight conceptual advantages - yet to be fully realized - of symbolic regression for both electronic and nuclear DFT. We point out promising approaches that can be carried from the more extensively investigated ML-enhanced electronic DFT to nuclear DFT.

Noise-Aware Synthesis of Quantum LDPC Encoder Circuits via Two-Sided Hamming Descent

Authors: Aditya Sodhani, Keshab K. Parhi
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.04462v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: QC/QI experiment 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces a novel, multi-stage compiler pipeline to synthesize highly efficient quantum circuits for preparing LDPC encoded states. By combining 'two-sided Hamming descent' with hardware-aware scheduling and noise modeling, the authors achieve substantial reductions in gate count and circuit depth compared to existing methods. This work is critical for realizing fault-tolerant quantum computation on near-term devices.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the practical implementation gap in quantum computation by providing a systematic, hardware-aware compilation technique to reduce circuit overhead and noise susceptibility for error correction codes.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core problem is efficiently synthesizing low-cost, noise-mitigating quantum circuits required to prepare encoded states for Quantum Low-Density Parity-Check (LDPC) codes.

Main result. The proposed pipeline significantly reduces CNOT gate counts by an aggregate of 53.8% and reduces two-qubit depth by up to 71% compared to standard constructions, leading to higher fidelity state preparation.

Method. It employs a novel optimization pipeline featuring 'two-sided Hamming descent' for circuit resynthesis, followed by commutation re-layering, hardware routing, and noise-aware live-range scheduling.

Model / system. The system models fault-tolerant quantum computation using CSS LDPC codes (e.g., Bivariate Bicycle). The task is to find a minimal CNOT circuit implementing the encoder's linear-reversible matrix.

Key observables. CNOT count, circuit depth (ASAP vs. commutation-aware), routed two-qubit count, and preparation error under hardware noise.

Important parameters / regimes. The optimization balances gate count reduction against depth minimization using a penalty parameter $\mu$, and incorporates the idle-to-gate error ratio ($\kappa$) for noise selection.

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes knowledge of the target hardware's connectivity graph, which is used during routing and scheduling stages.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the six-stage noise-aware encoder pipeline, demonstrating how redundant CNOT structures can be systematically reduced via two-sided matrix operations.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem of circuit redundancy in LDPC encoders, details the novel multi-stage optimization pipeline (including two-sided Hamming descent), presents quantitative results showing significant improvements over baselines, and concludes by positioning the method as a crucial compiler tool.

Abstract

Quantum low-density parity-check (LDPC) codes are a promising route to fault-tolerant quantum computation, but their use requires efficient preparation of encoded states. Standard encoder constructions generate circuits through fixed algebraic procedures, yet the resulting circuit can contain substantial redundancy. We formulate LDPC encoder preparation as a circuit-resynthesis problem: given the linear-reversible matrix implemented by the encoder's CNOT block, we seek a lower-cost equivalent circuit that can be routed efficiently on the target hardware and which mitigates noise. We propose a novel optimization approach referred as two-sided Hamming descent and a noise-aware optimization pipeline for this task. Across several families of Calderbank-Shor-Steane (CSS) LDPC encoders, including Bivariate Bicycle, hypergraph-product, and entanglement-assisted codes, the proposed pipeline produces substantially smaller and shallower encoder circuits than the standard constructions and the synthesis baselines considered, cutting gate counts by 53.8% in aggregate across the benchmark and by up to 68% on the Bivariate Bicycle family. The gains remain visible after routing, where the two-qubit depth is reduced by up to 71% and translate into higher-fidelity state preparation under circuit-level noise. On the Bivariate Bicycle family, live-range scheduling further reduces routed preparation failure by up to 13.7% without adding two-qubit gates to the selected circuit. These results indicate that encoder-matrix resynthesis, combined with hardware-calibrated selection and scheduling, is an effective compiler-level tool for preparing quantum LDPC code states.

Security of Quantum Conference Key Agreement with Two-Way Classical Communication

Summary. This paper analyzes the security of Quantum Conference Key Agreement using GHZ states, focusing on enhancing its resilience against noise. By incorporating multiple two-way classical communication steps (B-steps and P-steps), the authors derive that the tolerable error threshold can be significantly increased. This demonstrates a major improvement in the practical feasibility of QCKA for future quantum networks.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses practical limitations in quantum networking protocols by providing concrete methods (iterative B-steps) to enhance fault tolerance beyond current theoretical limits.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is improving the tolerable error threshold for Quantum Conference Key Agreement (QCKA) protocols, which are crucial for future quantum networks.

Main result. Iterative application of B-steps can significantly boost the tolerable error threshold beyond 20%, substantially enhancing the robustness of practical GHZ-based QCKA protocols.

Method. The authors derive the asymptotic secure key rate by analytically analyzing the optimization problem associated with multiple B-steps and P-steps applied to the QCKA protocol.

Model / system. The system models Quantum Conference Key Agreement (QCKA) among three users using tripartite Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger (GHZ) states, restricted to two complementary measurement bases (Z and X).

Key observables. Secure key rate ($R$) and the tolerable error threshold.

Abstract

Quantum conference key agreement (QCKA) enables multiple users to establish a common secret key with information-theoretic security and is regarded as a key primitive for secure communication in future quantum networks. However, practical implementations of QCKA typically suffer from higher noise levels than conventional bipartite quantum key distribution (QKD), making the improvement of the tolerable error threshold an important challenge. Gottesman and Lo proposed two preprocessing procedures for QKD with two-way classical communication, known as the B-step and the P-step, which enhance the tolerable error threshold. In this paper, we analyze the asymptotic security of QCKA with tripartite GHZ states and two measurement bases using two-way classical communication, including multiple B-steps and P-steps. We derive the corresponding secure key rate analytically and demonstrate that iterative B-steps can increase the tolerable error threshold beyond 20%, significantly improving upon the approximately 11% threshold achievable without two-way classical communication and the approximately 15% threshold obtained with only a single B-step. Our results show that two-way classical communication can substantially enhance the robustness of practical QCKA protocols.

The results provide a relativistic quantum framework for controlling accelerated vortex particle beams using electrostatic fields.

Abstract

We study the emergence of generic quantum chaos from an arithmetic billiard in the hyperbolic plane. Starting from an arithmetic triangle, we introduce small area-preserving geometric perturbations and compute long consecutive sequences of eigenvalues and eigenstates. The spectral statistics show a perturbation-dependent crossover from Poisson-like arithmetic behaviour to the Gaussian orthogonal ensemble (GOE), with the crossover scale moving to higher wavenumbers as the perturbation decreases. To characterize the nearest-neighbour spacing distribution, we compare the data with Brody and generalized-gamma fits. The generalized-gamma form is used only as a two-parameter diagnostic, allowing the small- and intermediate-spacing structure to vary independently from the large-spacing tail, rather than as a universal crossover law. For the weakest perturbation, we also compare the data with an effective block-GOE model, which diagnoses residual block-like structure deep into the computed spectral range. We complement the spectral analysis with Poincaré-Husimi representations of the eigenstates and quantify phase-space localization using an entropy-based measure. Its distribution is well described by Beta distributions whose width decays as a power law in the wavenumber. Before the spectral crossover, the localization statistics retain memory of the near-arithmetic regime. After the GOE regime is reached, the localization-fluctuation widths collapse onto a common decay law, sigma(Beta) ~ k^{-eta}, with eta approximately 0.37--0.38. These results provide a combined spectral and eigenfunction-level picture of the arithmetic-to-GOE crossover in hyperbolic quantum billiards.

Abstract

Quantum compilers play a key role in transforming quantum circuits into lower-cost implementations with improved execution fidelity. This process is commonly guided by circuit-level metrics, such as gate counts and circuit depth. Although compiler pass tuning has been widely studied in classical compilation, directly transferring these techniques to quantum compilers is challenging, because quantum programs are expressed as circuits and exhibit optimization behaviors that are shaped by quantum-specific structures. Prior quantum compiler tuning approaches have begun to use circuit features to guide pass selection, but they remain limited in two aspects: they search only a small portion of the optimization-pass space, and they mainly rely on static features that do not explicitly reflect how a circuit reacts to compiler optimizations. We present QuTuner, a feature-guided quantum compiler pass tuning framework that generalizes across compilers and tuning objectives. QuTuner first builds a large optimization dataset. It then characterizes each circuit from two complementary views: static circuit features that describe circuit structure, and optimization-aware pass embeddings that summarize the circuit's responses to individual optimization passes. Using these representations, QuTuner trains two offline models to retrieve and rank candidate pass sequences for unseen circuits, followed by lightweight refinement. We evaluate QuTuner on Qiskit and PyTKET using two benchmark suites. On Qiskit, QuTuner improves the evaluation-metric reduction by up to 84.85% over the strongest baseline while reducing tuning time by 73.59%. On PyTKET, it improves metric reduction by up to 18.68% with a 64.49% reduction in tuning time. These results show that QuTuner provides an effective approach to adaptive pass tuning for quantum compilers.
