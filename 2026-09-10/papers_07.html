Observation of Magnetic-Anisotropy Crossover and High-Temperature Skyrmions in the Dirac Magnet Fe3Ge with a Distorted Kagome Lattice

Summary. This work reports the discovery of robust, high-temperature skyrmions in the Dirac magnet $ ext{Fe}_3 ext{Ge}$. By analyzing magnetic anisotropy crossovers and topological transport measurements, the authors demonstrate that $ ext{Fe}_3 ext{Ge}$ maintains stable skyrmion phases up to its Curie temperature ($\sim 650 ext{ K}$). This positions the material as a leading candidate for advanced spintronic applications.

Why it may be interesting. The combination of high Curie temperature, topological electronic structure, and stable magnetic textures makes this material highly relevant for developing next-generation spintronic devices, bridging fundamental condensed matter physics with applied quantum technology.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the interplay between magnetic anisotropy, electronic band topology, and the stability of skyrmions in the Dirac magnet $ ext{Fe}_3 ext{Ge}$.

Main result. The authors report observing robust skyrmions in $ ext{Fe}_3 ext{Ge}$ that persist over a wide temperature range (375 K to 650 K), establishing it as a promising platform for high-temperature spintronics.

Method. The research combines experimental techniques like Lorentz TEM and magneto-transport measurements with theoretical calculations using methods like FPLAPW.

Model / system. The physical system is the $ ext{Fe}_3 ext{Ge}$ single crystal, which possesses a distorted Kagome lattice and exhibits Dirac magnetic properties. The analysis focuses on the magnetic phase transitions and the resulting topological electronic structure.

Key observables. High Curie temperature ($\sim 650 ext{ K}$), large intrinsic Anomalous Hall Conductivity ($\sim 380 \ \Omega^{-1} ext{cm}^{-1}$), Spin Reorientation Transition ($\sim 375 ext{ K}$), and stable mesoscopic skyrmions.

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature range (375 K to 650 K), magnetic anisotropy constants ($K_{ ext{eff}}$), and the material's intrinsic electronic topology.

Assumptions / limitations. The electronic structure is linked to nontrivial band topology, and the magnetic behavior is governed by the crossover between easy-plane and easy-axis anisotropy.

Paper structure. The paper follows a standard structure: structural characterization, measurement of transport and magnetic properties across temperature, identification of the magnetic anisotropy crossover, and finally, visualization and confirmation of high-temperature skyrmion stability using LTEM.

Abstract

Topological materials that simultaneously host robust high-temperature skyrmions and nontrivial electronic band structures have attracted tremendous interest owing to their distinctive advantages for both fundamental research and prospective technological applications. Here, we report the observation of robust skyrmions in the Dirac kagome magnet Fe3Ge, which exhibits a high Curie temperature of ~ 650 K. At room temperature, Fe3Ge shows a large intrinsic anomalous Hall conductivity of ~ 380 Ω-1cm-1, originating from its nontrivial electronic band topology. Systematic magnetization measurements reveal a spin reorientation transition at ~ 375 K, indicating a crossover from easy-plane to easy-axis magnetic anisotropy. Below the spin reorientation temperature, a large topological Hall effect is observed, arising from microscopic noncoplanar spin structures. Lorentz transmission electron microscopy shows that mesoscopic skyrmions are stabilized in the easy-axis magnetic anisotropy regime and persist over an exceptionally wide temperature window of 375-650 K, far exceeding that of most previously reported skyrmion-hosting materials. These results establish Fe3Ge as a promising platform for exploring diverse topological properties, with strong potential for advancing future high-temperature spintronic applications, ranging from next-generation information storage to logic computing devices.

On the Limits of Quantum Multiparty Simultaneous Communication

Authors: Pedro Montealegre, Ivan Rapaport, Jorge Valenzuela
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10289v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

Summary. This work rigorously establishes a multipartite exponential separation between classical public randomness and unentangled quantum communication in the SMP model. By generalizing the Index Coordination problem, the authors prove that quantum communication lacks an asymptotic advantage over private randomness in this setting. This demonstrates that quantum superposition cannot efficiently simulate the coordination power of public randomness across multiple parties.

Why it may be interesting. The results provide fundamental, quantitative limits on how quantum superposition can simulate classical coordination mechanisms, which has implications for quantum cryptography and communication complexity.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper establishes a multiparty exponential separation between classical shared randomness (public coins) and unentangled quantum communication in the Simultaneous Message Passing (SMP) model.

Main result. It shows that quantum communication without shared entanglement cannot efficiently simulate the coordination provided by public randomness, yielding strong lower bounds scaling as $\Omega(n^{1-1/k})$ in the unambiguous regime.

Method. The authors use generalizations of state identification theorems, leveraging the factorization of orthogonal complements of subspaces in tensor product settings, combined with information-theoretic inequalities like AM-GM.

Model / system. The analysis is conducted within the Simultaneous Message Passing (SMP) model, generalizing the Index Coordination problem ($ ext{IC}_{k,n}$) to $k$ spatially separated players communicating to a central referee.

Key observables. Message length (in bits or qubits), success probability, and the scaling of lower bounds with the number of players ($k$) and input size ($n$).

Important parameters / regimes. The separation holds for arbitrary $k=k(n)\ge2$, and the complexity matches $\Theta(n)$ when $k$ grows sufficiently fast with $n$.

Assumptions / limitations. The quantum communication is restricted to unentangled messages (no shared entanglement) and the analysis focuses on the unambiguous and bounded-error regimes.

Figures summary. Not specified.

Paper structure. The paper builds upon the two-player Index Coordination separation, generalizes it to $k$ parties ($ ext{IC}_{k,n}$), and derives lower bounds by analyzing the factorization of the orthogonal complement of the 'wrong' subspace in the quantum state space.

Abstract

The Simultaneous Message Passing (SMP) model provides a fundamental framework for comparing classical and quantum communication. For two players, Gavinsky et al. (STOC 2006) established a separation underlying the incomparability of shared randomness and quantum communication: \textsc{Index Coordination} needs $O(\log n)$ public-coin bits but $Ω(n^{1/3})$ bounded-error qubits. In this work, we establish a multiparty exponential separation through $\operatorname{IC}_{k,n}$, a natural $k$-party generalization of \textsc{Index Coordination}. Public-coin protocols solve it unambiguously with maximum message length $O(\log n)$ bits. In contrast, quantum SMP protocols without shared entanglement or public coins require maximum message length $Ω(n^{1-1/k})$ qubits in the unambiguous regime and $Ω(n^{(k-1)/(k+1)})$ qubits in the bounded-error regime. A classical private-coin protocol matches the unambiguous bound, so quantum communication provides no asymptotic advantage over private randomness in this regime. For fixed error parameters, all constants are independent of $k$, establishing the exponential separation for every integer-valued function $k=k(n)\ge2$, without restricting its growth. Both quantum lower bounds become $Ω(n)$ when $k\ge c\log n$ for any fixed $c>0$, matching the full-input protocol and yielding tight linear complexity in both regimes. Our results demonstrate that quantum superposition cannot efficiently simulate the coordination afforded by public randomness, extending this separation to arbitrary $k$. To bound success probabilities for multiparty product states, we prove an exact factorization theorem for unambiguous quantum state identification, which may be of independent mathematical interest.

One Relative Phase Orders Operational Thresholds of Noisy Bell Pairs

Summary. This paper develops a general framework to determine the operational thresholds of quantum abilities in noisy Bell pairs. It proves that these thresholds are governed by the X-part of the noise and exhibit a nondecreasing dependence on the relative phase between noise and coherence. This provides powerful tools for certifying quantum capabilities even when closed-form criteria are unknown.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical framework developed for characterizing thresholds based on phase dependence and partial tomography is highly general and applicable to understanding noise resilience in quantum communication protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper determines the operational thresholds at which quantum communication abilities (like entanglement or steerability) are lost in noisy Bell pairs.

Main result. It establishes a monotonicity law showing that the ability threshold is nondecreasing with the relative phase between the noise and Bell coherences, and that the X-part of the noise provides a tight upper bound on this threshold.

Method. The analysis uses bounding techniques based on the X-part of the noise and establishes monotonicity laws by analyzing the dependence of the threshold on the relative phase $\theta$.

Model / system. The system models noisy Bell pairs described by a mixture $\rho_\lambda(\sigma) = \lambda \Phi_+ + (1 - \lambda) \sigma$, where $\sigma$ is the noise state characterized by its X-part $\sigma_X$.

Key observables. Operational thresholds ($\lambda_K$), the relative phase $\theta$, and the X-part of the noise ($\sigma_X$).

Important parameters / regimes. Bell weight $\lambda$ ($0 \le \lambda \le 1$) and the relative phase $\theta$ ($0 \le \theta \le \pi$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the operational ability's failing states form a convex set closed under local unitaries, and it relies on the X-part approximation for bounding.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the nondecreasing nature of operational thresholds (e.g., entanglement, CHSH-nonlocal) as a function of the relative phase $\theta$, showing certified intervals for these thresholds.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the X-part upper bound and the general monotonicity law for any suitable ability. It then focuses on the phase dependence, proving that the threshold increases monotonically with $\theta$, and finally applies these techniques to specific abilities like steerability.

Abstract

Unlike the two-qubit entangled and CHSH-nonlocal sets, the steerable and Bell-nonlocal ones have no known general closed-form criterion. For any such operational ability whose failing states form a convex set closed under local unitaries, and for a Bell mixture with arbitrary noise, the $X$ part of the noise gives an upper bound on the definitive threshold, the Bell weight above which the mixture attains the ability. That bound is the tightest that the seven of the fifteen Pauli expectation values fixing the $X$ part can support. The same theorem shows that a state has the ability whenever its $X$ part does, so partial tomography of a state can certify its ability. When noise is of $X$ form, the mixture carries a relative phase between the noise and Bell coherences that cannot be gauged away by local unitaries, and it controls the threshold through interference. We prove a monotonicity law: the threshold is nondecreasing in this phase, whether or not a closed-form criterion for the ability exists.

Private communication via zero-private-capacity quantum channels

Summary. This paper tackles the superactivation problem in quantum information by showing that two quantum channels, each individually incapable of supporting private communication, can jointly achieve a positive secure rate. The authors demonstrate this using a specific combination of a four-level and an erasure channel. This result proves that secure communication capacity is not solely determined by the private capacity of the individual channels.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a deep theoretical result showing that local capacity measures fail to capture global security properties, which is a fundamental concept in quantum communication theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper resolves the open problem of whether two quantum channels, each having zero individual private capacity, can jointly enable private communication (superactivation).

Main result. The joint use of a four-level channel and a qubit erasure channel, both with zero private capacity, achieves a positive private rate, demonstrating that channel private capacity alone is insufficient to determine secure communication capability.

Method. The analysis uses information-theoretic bounds, specifically Holevo information and entropy calculations, to establish a positive information difference between the received signal and the environmental leakage.

Model / system. The model involves two independent, memoryless quantum channels: a four-level channel and a qubit erasure channel. The analysis is highly mathematical, involving quantum information theory concepts like private capacity and joint measurements.

Key observables. Private capacity, information gain $I(U:Y)$, and environmental leakage $\chi(U:E'E)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The erasure probability $p$ (specifically $1/2 \le p < 1$), and the achievable rate, which is shown to be positive.

Assumptions / limitations. The result relies on a fixed joint measurement and classical coding across uses, and the formalization is built upon established quantum information theorems.

Figures summary. Figure 1 summarizes the logical dependencies of the formalized argument for $1/2 \leq p < 1$, detailing proof ingredients and conclusions.

Paper structure. The paper develops the problem by defining the private capacity, constructing a specific physical example (the two channels), deriving the positive rate through information bounds, and formalizing the entire argument in Lean 4.

Abstract

Private communication over a noisy quantum channel requires reliable transmission to the receiver and secrecy from the environment. Whether two channels with zero private capacity can jointly enable private communication is a longstanding open problem in quantum information theory. Here we resolve this problem by exhibiting a four-level channel and a qubit erasure channel with half erasure probability, each with zero private capacity, whose joint use achieves more than 0.0001903 private bits per product use. The encoding gives the receiver a linear information gain with at most quadratic environmental leakage, enabling privacy through a fixed joint measurement and classical coding. This superactivation, impossible for independent classical memoryless wiretap channels, shows that a channel's private capacity alone does not determine its value for secure communication. The initial activation example was identified through interactions with large language models, and the result has been formalized in Lean 4.

Quantifying translational and bond-orientational order metrics in hyperuniform and nonhyperuniform many-particle systems

Authors: Anirban Mukherjee, Salvatore Torquato
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10359v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

Summary. This work introduces two metrics, $ au_T$ and $ au_O$, to quantify translational and bond-orientational order in many-particle systems. By applying these metrics to hard-sphere packings and hyperuniform states, the authors demonstrate that structural order is highly dependent on the system's preparation history. The resulting $( au_T, au_O)$ pair offers a versatile tool for characterizing phase transitions and structural pathways in condensed matter physics.

Why it may be interesting. The development of robust, quantifiable metrics like $( au_T, au_O)$ provides a powerful, generalized coordinate system for mapping complex phase diagrams, which is highly relevant for understanding structural transitions in soft matter and amorphous materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the fundamental challenge of quantifying the degree of structural order (both translational and bond-orientational) in complex many-particle systems.

Main result. The pair of metrics $( au_T, au_O)$ successfully places both forms of order on a common statistical footing, revealing preparation-dependent structural differences between equilibrium and non-equilibrium states.

Method. The authors introduce and compute two metrics: $ au_T$ (based on the total correlation function $h(r)$) and $ au_O$ (its bond-orientational analogue), both defined via squared $L^2$ norms in real or Fourier space.

Model / system. The study analyzes sphere-packing models (hard spheres/disks) in 2D and 3D, comparing equilibrium states, non-equilibrium Random Sequential Addition (RSA) packings, and disordered stealthy hyperuniform (SHU) ground states.

Key observables. Translational order metric ($ au_T$), Bond-orientational order metric ($ au_O$), and the ratio $ au_O/ au_T$.

Important parameters / regimes. Packing fraction ($\phi$), stealthiness parameter ($\chi$), and system dimensionality (2D vs 3D).

Assumptions / limitations. The metrics are constructed from two-point correlation functions, and the analysis often requires careful handling of system size cutoffs ($L$) and wavevector cutoffs ($K_c$).

Figures summary. Figures compare correlation functions and structure factors ($S(k)$ vs $SO(k)$) across various phases (fluid, crystal, SHU) and demonstrate the system-size dependence of the metrics.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the metrics, applies them to nonhyperuniform systems (hard spheres/disks) to show preparation dependence, examines the SHU phase, and concludes by establishing the positive correlation between $ au_T$ and $ au_O$ across all studied regimes.

Abstract

Quantifying the degree of order/disorder in many-particle systems remains an outstanding problem in physics, materials science, and mathematics. To this end, we consider the translational order metric $τ_T$, defined as the squared $L^2$ norm of the total correlation function $h(\mathbf{r})$, and introduce its bond-orientational analogue $τ_O$, defined from the weighted total correlation function $h_{\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{r})$ using local orientational weights (S. Torquato et al., Phys. Rev. X 16, 011042 (2026)). The pair $(τ_T,τ_O)$ places both forms of order on a common two-point statistical footing. We compute both metrics for two nonhyperuniform sphere-packing models in 2D and 3D as functions of packing fraction $φ$: 1) equilibrium hard particles and 2) nonequilibrium random sequential addition (RSA) packings. For the nonhyperuniform systems, bond-orientational order remains subdominant along the equilibrium-fluid and RSA configurations; however, its magnitude relative to the translational metric increases near the upper end of the equilibrium-fluid branches, much more strongly in 2D than in 3D, and the two metrics become comparable along the sampled crystal branches. At common packing fractions, equilibrium fluids and RSA packings trace distinct $(τ_T,τ_O)$ trajectories, revealing preparation-dependent differences in structural order. As a representative hyperuniform family, we study 2D disordered stealthy hyperuniform (SHU) ground states for $0<χ<1/2$, where $χ$ is the stealthiness parameter. Within the disordered SHU phase, bond-orientational order remains subdominant, but its magnitude relative to the translational metric increases toward the disorder-to-order threshold. In all three models, $τ_T$ and $τ_O$ are positively correlated beyond the Poisson-reference regime: $τ_O$ increases monotonically with $τ_T$ across the sampled state points.

Summary. This work investigates the large linear magnetoresistance observed in the helimagnetic pnictide MnP. By combining detailed magnetotransport experiments with first-principles calculations, the authors propose that the effect arises from a small, semi-Dirac-like Fermi pocket that becomes accessible under high magnetic fields. This provides a quantum-mechanical framework for understanding magnetotransport in complex magnetic topological materials.

Why it may be interesting. The strong coupling between complex magnetism (noncollinear spins) and electronic transport (MR) is a hallmark of strongly correlated electron systems, providing a concrete platform to test theories of quantum transport in magnetic materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the origin of large linear positive magnetoresistance (LPMR) in complex magnetic materials, specifically addressing the role of spin-dependent scattering in noncollinear spin systems.

Main result. The observed LPMR is microscopically explained by a small Fermi pocket formed by a semi-Dirac-like band crossing near the Y point, which allows the system to access the extreme quantum-limit regime.

Method. The study combines comprehensive experimental magnetotransport measurements (Hall, MR) with first-principles electronic structure calculations.

Model / system. The physical system is Manganese phosphide (MnP), a helimagnetic binary pnictide exhibiting multiple field-induced magnetic transitions (SCR, FAN, FM2).

Paper structure. Abstract

Large linear positive magnetoresistance (LPMR) in topological and magnetic materials remains a subject of intense debate, particularly in noncollinear spin systems where spin-dependent scattering complicates charge transport. Manganese phosphide (MnP), a helimagnetic binary pnictide with multiple field-induced magnetic transitions, provides a useful platform to investigate the interplay between complex magnetism and electronic topology. Here, we present a comprehensive experimental and theoretical investigation of phase-dependent magnetotransport in high-quality MnP single crystals. Hall measurements reveal an anomalous Hall effect dominated by skew scattering at high temperatures and a finite topological Hall effect in the noncollinear fan (FAN) and low-temperature screw (SCR) phases. At low temperatures, we observe a large, non-saturating LPMR reaching nearly 800 percent at 4 K and 15 T, with a pronounced linear field dependence in the field-polarized ferromagnetic (FM2) state. First-principles calculations reveal a strongly anisotropic semi-Dirac-like band at the Y point that progressively approaches the Fermi level from the SCR to FAN and FM2 states. Our analysis indicates that the resulting small Fermi pocket can access the extreme quantum-limit regime at experimentally accessible fields, providing a microscopic framework for the observed LPMR within Abrikosov's quantum magnetoresistance theory.

Abstract

Summary. This paper presents a quantum algorithm to solve wave propagation problems by bypassing the limitations of standard quantum linear system solvers. Instead of inverting the full operator, the authors directly prepare quantum states supported on the resonant manifold defined by the wave equation's dispersion relation. This novel geometric approach removes exponential overhead, making the computation feasible regardless of the physical domain size.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete quantum algorithmic pathway for solving fundamental PDEs in physics (like wave equations) that are notoriously difficult for standard quantum linear solvers due to spectral singularities.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of solving wave propagation partial differential equations (PDEs) using quantum algorithms, specifically overcoming the exponential inefficiency encountered when dealing with singular operators.

Main result. By developing a method of geometric quantum state preparation directly on the resonant manifold, the authors eliminate the exponential overhead associated with post-selection, leading to a success probability independent of the computational domain size.

Method. The approach reformulates the problem from inverting the full discretized operator to preparing a quantum state whose support is restricted to the known resonant manifold, utilizing quantum comparators and arithmetic circuits.

Model / system. The model is wave propagation, exemplified by the Helmholtz equation, which is discretized into a linear system. The core difficulty arises from the singularity of the inverse operator on the dispersion surface $k^2 = \omega^2/c^2$.

Key observables. Success probability ($P_{ ext{succ}}$) for state preparation, which is shown to scale favorably with the number of sources and independently of domain size.

Important parameters / regimes. The condition number $\kappa(A)$ of the discretized operator, the resonant manifold itself, and the source location encoding via Fourier phases.

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes the hyperbolic PDE solutions possess singular support on lower-dimensional dispersion manifolds, allowing for geometric state preparation.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the quantum circuits for preparing states corresponding to geometric shapes, such as the Midpoint (Bresenham) circle algorithm and a finite-width resonant ring in momentum space.

Paper structure. The paper first contrasts standard Quantum Linear System Solvers (QLSS) with the wave equation's singular structure. It then introduces the novel geometric state preparation technique, detailing the quantum circuits required to construct states supported only on the resonant manifold, culminating in the derivation of the size-independent success probability.

Abstract

We present a quantum algorithm for solving partial differential equations through a linear-system formulation, focusing on wave propagation problems described by the discretized Helmholtz equation in frequency domain. Although quantum linear-system solvers offer exponential compression of the system degrees of freedom, their runtime complexity is generally governed by the condition number of the discretized operator. Exploiting the analytic structure of the differential operator can provide an alternative to explicit matrix inversion, as illustrated for the screened Poisson equation through an explicit quantum circuit. For the Helmholtz equation, however, the inverse operator becomes singular on the dispersion surface $k^2=ω^2/c^2$, rendering direct Fourier-space state-preparation methods exponentially inefficient. We address this challenge by directly preparing quantum states supported on the resonant manifold and encoding source locations through Fourier phases. The resulting algorithm eliminates the exponentially large overhead associated with post-selection on the resonant manifold, yielding a success probability that depends linearly on the number of sources and is independent of the computational domain size. More generally, our approach applies to hyperbolic differential equations whose Fourier-space solutions possess singular support on dispersion manifolds, recasting their solution as a problem of geometric quantum state preparation.

The Geometry of Transport in Quantum Walks and Parrondo's Paradox

Authors: Jose Alfredo de Leon, Mariana Pérez-Muralles, Jan Neuser, Carlos Pineda
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09269v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

Summary. This work applies the concept of Parrondo's paradox to quantum walks, establishing a geometric criterion based on the 'transport vector' to predict when combining simple quantum operations yields unexpectedly directional motion. It shows that this paradox is possible through coin composition but not through simple alternation, providing a deep structural insight into quantum transport design.

Why it may be interesting. The geometric interpretation of quantum transport and the connection to classical paradoxes like Parrondo's are highly relevant to understanding non-equilibrium dynamics and directional transport in quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates Parrondo's paradox—where combining losing strategies yields a winning one—within the framework of discrete-time quantum walks.

Main result. The paradox is characterized by a geometric condition: it occurs if and only if the transport vector of the combined strategy falls outside the cone spanned by the individual transport vectors. This can happen via coin composition but not via simple alternation.

Method. The analysis uses the transport vector, derived from Fourier space analysis, to determine the asymptotic velocity of the quantum walker. The paradox is analyzed by comparing the combined transport vector against the cone generated by individual vectors.

Model / system. The model is a one-dimensional discrete-time quantum walk (DTQW) on a lattice, evolving via a unitary step operator $U=SC$. The dynamics are analyzed using the Bloch sphere representation for the coin state.

Key observables. Asymptotic velocity ($v$), Transport Vector ($\vec{T}$), and the probability of paradoxical outcomes ($P(L \circ L = W)$ vs $P(L)^2$).

Important parameters / regimes. The initial coin state Bloch vector ($\vec{r}_0$), the structure of the coin operators ($C_1, C_2$), and the combination protocol (composition vs. alternation).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis focuses on the long-time limit (asymptotic behavior) and assumes the initial state is separable. The general criterion relies on the assumption that no coin operator is antidiagonal.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the Bloch sphere division into winning/losing hemispheres based on the sign of $v = \vec{T} \cdot \vec{r}_0$. They also map out outcome regions for different combination protocols, showing where the paradox occurs.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the transport vector and the paradox criterion. It then compares the paradox's emergence under coin composition versus step alternation, deriving explicit probabilities for the paradoxical set. Finally, it relates the asymptotic velocity to the coin's steady-state Bloch vector.

Abstract

We study Parrondo's paradox -- the phenomenon where combining losing strategies yields a winning one -- in a minimal discrete-time quantum walk. We introduce the transport vector, encoded in the coin's steady state, whose inner product with the initial coin state gives the walker's asymptotic velocity. More generally, the paradox emerges exactly when the transport vector of the combined strategy falls outside the cone spanned by the individual ones -- a geometric criterion valid for any combination of strategies. It explains, for instance, why composing two coin operators within a single step can produce the paradox while simple alternation between them cannot, since alternation keeps the combined vector confined to the cone. The paradoxical set has nonzero measure, and we compute its probability explicitly in representative cases. This casts the paradox as one instance of designing reachable transport in quantum walks.

Abstract

Altermagnetism is a recently established class of magnetic order that combines fully compensated moments with momentum-dependent spin splitting, yet identifying its spectroscopic fingerprints remains an open challenge. In this work, we investigate finite-momentum two-magnon excitations in a two-dimensional $d$-wave altermagnet by calculating the two-magnon light scattering intensity in different polarization channels. Using linear spin-wave theory and further incorporating the leading $1/S$ quantum corrections, we demonstrate that the characteristic magnon splitting of altermagnets shifts the upper edge of the two-magnon continuum and the corresponding spectral features to higher energy at $\boldsymbol{X}=(π,0)$ relative to the conventional antiferromagnet, by an amount linear in the exchange anisotropy $|δJ_2|$, while leaving the response at the Brillouin-zone center unchanged. Although magnon-magnon interactions strongly redistribute spectral weight toward lower energies, the energy scale associated with the high-energy momentum-selective reconstruction remains robust. The interactions additionally generate a pronounced low-energy two-peak structure that has no counterpart within linear spin-wave theory. The characteristic energy scales underlying both features are intrinsic to the two-magnon sector rather than to a specific scattering operator. These findings highlight the potential of finite-momentum two-magnon spectroscopy for identifying altermagnetic order in insulating magnets and motivate momentum-resolved resonant inelastic x-ray scattering studies of candidate altermagnetic materials.

Abstract

The entangling power of a unitary operator acting on a bipartite Hilbert space measures the entanglement it generates from product states, averaged over the inputs. A finite-dimensional unitary has a spectral decomposition $U=\sum_{a=1}^{n}e^{iθ_a}P_a$, where $e^{iθ_a}$ are the eigenvalues, $P_a$ the corresponding eigen-projectors, and $n$ is the number of distinct eigenvalues. After removing an overall phase, the entangling power is a function on the $(n-1)$-torus of relative eigenphases at fixed spectral projectors. We prove that this function is stationary at all $2^{n-1}$ points on the torus where every relative phase is $0$ or $π$, which we define as \textit{corners}. Up to an overall phase, $U$ at each corner is a generalized reflection $R=\mathbb{I}-2Q$ satisfying $R^2=\mathbb{I}$, where $Q$ is the sum of spectral projectors whose relative phase is $π$. At the corner the entangling power is expressed in terms of seven local-unitary invariants of $Q$. A unitary gate $U$ can be realized as a corner of some projector family if and only if $U^2\propto\mathbb{I}$, a condition satisfied by many Clifford and non-Clifford gates. We illustrate the theorem with two-qubit gates, $SU(N)$ channel decompositions, and two-site spin chains, obtaining examples of minima, maxima, and saddle points. In addition, a corner that is a saddle point on the full phase torus can appear as a local maximum or minimum along different time-evolution trajectories.

Abstract

We formulate a quasiclassical theory of the Edelstein effect in superconductors that incorporates both intraband and interband contributions. To describe the interband contribution, which is absent from the conventional leading-order quasiclassical formulation, we derive augmented Eilenberger equations in the presence of antisymmetric spin-orbit coupling. The intraband contribution is evaluated using multiband Eilenberger equations. We apply these formulations to supercurrent-induced surface spin magnetization in $s$-wave Rashba superconductors and investigate its dependence on temperature, distance from the surface, spin-orbit coupling strength, and supercurrent. The intraband contribution originates from a supercurrent-induced asymmetry of quasiparticles with opposite momenta and spin polarizations, whereas the interband contribution arises from the anomalous-velocity term generated by the momentum derivative of the Rashba spin-orbit potential. Thus, although the intraband contribution dominates the linear Edelstein effect, the nonlinear Edelstein effect can serve as a useful probe of the interband contribution originating from quantum geometry.

Abstract

We present a theoretical study focused on the influence of Coulomb interactions on thermoelectric properties of molecular junctions assuming that electron transport is strongly affected by thermalized phonon modes in the molecular ambient. Abstract

The geometrical and electronic properties of moiré materials are highly sensitive to the twist and strain in the samples due to the moiré magnifying effect. Accurate identification of twist and strain in moiré materials is therefore essential. In this work, we establish a general framework to extract the twist and strain configurations from moiré images with either atomic or moiré scale resolution. With only moiré-wavelength information, we show that there is a continuous family of possible twist and strain configurations, each one accounting for different orientations of the moiré pattern. To estimate the most likely twist-strain configuration, we discuss additional constraints and methods involving the minimum elastic energy and the electronic spectra. The minimum elastic energy, in particular, reflects that shear configurations become much more favorable as the strain increases. As an example of the developed methodology, we discuss the formation and identification of strained triangular moiré patterns. Our framework provides a comprehensive approach to identify the twist and strain configurations in systems with moiré-scale resolution.

Abstract

Numerical simulations of superconducting magnetic bearings (SMBs) are complicated by the relative motion between high temperature superconductors and the magnetic guideway, which commonly requires repeated updates of the geometry and mesh. We present a fixed-mesh based approach for modeling the motion induced effects in which a coordinate transformation is applied only to the air region between the moving regions. The transformation can be included in a time-dependent anisotropic material tensor, leaving the mesh during the simulation and the rest of the formulation unchanged. To provide numerical examples, the approach is implemented in 2D using the finite element method with the $j$-$a$ formulation and is combined with homogenization and circuit coupling for double-crossed loops of coated conductors. The open-source model implementations are made available. The proposed approach is verified and validated against existing modeling approaches and experimental results from the literature for SMBs. Two simulation examples are presented, and in both of them the levitation force predicted by the fixed-mesh model agrees with the experimental results, yielding $R^2$ values above 0.99. For a verification problem, it is shown that the fixed-mesh model converges towards the remeshed reference solution under mesh refinement as expected. For an SMB undergoing vertical and horizontal translations, the homogenized fixed-mesh model reproduces the lateral-force response of a fully resolved moving-geometry reference model. Additionally, the homogenized fixed-mesh model is computationally more efficient than the reference model, indicating a significant reduction in the number of degrees of freedom and the computation time. The results demonstrate accurate and computationally efficient simulation of translational motion with the fixed-mesh based approach.
