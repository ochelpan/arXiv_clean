Cascade-induced high-performance nonreciprocal quantum batteries

Authors: Niaz Ali Khan, Dahai He
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09690v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: methods for driven-dissipative 3/5 · correlated / nonlocal dissipation 1/5 · driven-dissipative phase transition 1/5

Summary. This paper advances the theory of nonreciprocal quantum batteries by replacing an inconsistent theoretical framework with a mathematically rigorous cascaded open quantum systems formalism. The authors demonstrate a significant improvement in energy storage efficiency, achieving a sixteen-fold advantage over prior benchmarks. This establishes a reliable theoretical foundation for high-performance quantum energy storage devices.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses open quantum systems dynamics, energy harvesting, and the rigorous mathematical treatment of dissipation, which are core topics in AMO physics and quantum optics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To develop a mathematically rigorous framework for nonreciprocal quantum batteries that surpasses previously reported energy accumulation benchmarks.

Main result. The authors achieved a regime-independent sixteen-fold steady-state nonreciprocal energy advantage over reciprocal systems, establishing the cascaded formalism as the correct physical foundation.

Method. The study employs the rigorously established cascaded open quantum systems formalism, using the Lindblad master equation to model energy transfer dynamics.

Model / system. The model consists of a quantum battery and a charger, both modeled as single-mode harmonic oscillators coupled to a shared reservoir, governed by a Lindblad master equation.

Key observables. Energy Storage Capacity ($E_b(t)$), Useful Work (Ergotropy, $W_b(t)$), and Battery-to-Charger Energy Efficiency ($\eta$).

Important parameters / regimes. Damping rates ($\kappa_a, \kappa_b$), coupling strengths, and the ratio of these rates ($\kappa_a/\kappa_b$).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that the cascaded formalism provides the correct physical description, correcting inconsistencies found in the Metelmann–Clerk formalism.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the QB model, showing energy efficiency vs. time under various damping conditions, comparing nonreciprocal versus reciprocal energy storage.

Paper structure. The paper critiques prior work using the Metelmann–Clerk formalism, introduces the cascaded open quantum systems approach, derives time-dependent solutions for energy and efficiency, and compares the results against the established benchmarks.

Abstract

Nonreciprocal quantum batteries harness reservoir engineering for controlled energy transfer, heralding a paradigm shift for quantum energy storage. A prior study utilizing the Metelmann-Clerk formalism reported a fourfold enhancement in nonreciprocal energy accumulation over reciprocal counterparts. This fourfold enhancement, however, appears to stem from a Lindblad master equation that exhibits fundamental inconsistencies, which may systematically understate the actual nonreciprocal advantage. Our approach is rooted in the rigorously established cascaded open quantum systems formalism, which ensures both mathematical consistency and physical fidelity. Remarkably, we surpass the previously reported fourfold benchmark, attaining a regime-independent sixteenfold steady-state nonreciprocal energy advantage over reciprocal systems. We further uncover a dissipation-dependent battery-to-charger efficiency, fourfold under symmetric damping, surpassing this benchmark when the battery is less dissipative, and lower otherwise. This work establishes the cascaded formalism as a mathematically rigorous and experimentally viable foundation for high-performance quantum energy storage.

Convex-Gaussianity of fermionic Gibbs states in perturbation theory

Authors: Kaifeng Bu, Yuanjie Ren
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09608v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 3/5 · Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work rigorously determines the parameter regimes, particularly the inverse temperature $eta$, for which the Gibbs state of interacting fermions remains convex-Gaussian. It achieves this by developing distinct perturbative methods for both weak and strong coupling limits of the Fermi-Hubbard model. The findings provide deep structural insights into the quantum phases of correlated electron systems.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous characterization of when quantum states retain simple mathematical structures (like being convex combinations of Gaussian states) is crucial for understanding entanglement structure and computational complexity in strongly correlated quantum materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the conditions under which the Gibbs state of weakly perturbed interacting fermionic systems remains convex-Gaussian.

Main result. It establishes precise, distinct bounds on the inverse temperature ($eta$) for convex-Gaussianity in both the weak-coupling (perturbing around free fermions) and strong-coupling (perturbing around atomic limits) regimes of the Fermi-Hubbard model.

Method. The analysis employs perturbation theory, utilizing Majorana operators and establishing membership in the Convex-Gaussian Cone (FGC) through structured expansions and inductive proofs.

Model / system. The primary model is the Fermi-Hubbard model, $H = tH_{ ext{hop}} + U V_{ ext{int}}$, studied on a general graph. The analysis is generalized to sparse Hamiltonians $H=H_0+V$ where $H_0$ is quadratic.

Key observables. Convex-Gaussianity of the Gibbs state $ ho_eta$, and the expectation values of dressed single-string factors.

Important parameters / regimes. Inverse temperature ($eta$), hopping strength ($|t|$), interaction strength ($|U|$), and the coupling ratio $\epsilon = |U|/(4|t|)$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is restricted to specific perturbative regimes (weak or strong coupling) and relies on the Hamiltonian being sparse or having specific structural properties.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the distinct weak-coupling and strong-coupling boundaries for the 2D Fermi-Hubbard model, showing the parameter regimes where convex-Gaussianity is maintained.

Paper structure. The paper develops results sequentially: first for general sparse Hamiltonians, then applying these results to derive specific bounds for the weak-coupling regime, and finally developing a separate framework for the strong-coupling regime.

Abstract

We study the structure of Gibbs states in weakly perturbed interacting fermionic systems. First, for a sparse Hamiltonian $H=H_0+V$ with a quadratic term $H_0$ and a non-quadratic perturbation $V$ of scale $ε$, we show that the Gibbs state $ρ_β$ decomposes into a convex combination of Gaussian states whenever the inverse temperature satisfies $β\le O(\log(1/ε))$. Moreover, we prove that this bound is asymptotically tight by establishing that $β\le Θ(\log(1/ε))$ is necessary for certain sparse Hamiltonians. This general framework applies directly to the weak-coupling (small-$\vert{}U\vert{}$) regime of the Fermi--Hubbard model with hopping $t$ and on-site interaction $U$ on any graph of maximum degree $D$. Complementarily, in the strong-coupling (small-$\vert{}t\vert{}$) regime, we show that the Gibbs state remains convex-Gaussian up to $β\le O\big(\vert{}U\vert{}^{-1}\log(\vert{}U\vert{}/(D\vert{}t\vert{}))\big)$, revealing a mechanism for convex-Gaussianity distinct from the weak-coupling setting.

Multiparameter quantum bounds for entanglement-assisted aperture synthesis

Authors: Kalaga Madhav, Pieter Kok, Nic Scott
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10111v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: interference shaping light 3/5 · quantum measurements 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work tackles the quantum limits for imaging with large interferometer arrays by treating the problem as a collective multiparameter estimation task. It shows that optimal measurement requires a global, collective receiver, which significantly beats standard pairwise measurements. The results provide actionable design guidelines, including a threshold determining when distributed entanglement surpasses direct optical links.

Why it may be interesting. It provides concrete quantum limits and design targets for quantum sensing in large-scale optical arrays, directly relevant to quantum optics and quantum metrology.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper formulates and solves the multiparameter quantum estimation problem for aperture synthesis using an M-station optical array.

Main result. A collective receiver measurement significantly outperforms pairwise receivers, and the optimal entanglement allocation follows a square-root law favoring low-visibility baselines.

Method. The analysis uses the Quantum Fisher Information (QFI) matrix, comparing the Holevo bound to the achievable variance using collective measurement strategies.

Model / system. The system is an M-station optical interferometry setup analyzing the coherence matrix G to estimate the brightness distribution I(s) across multiple baselines.

Key observables. Quantum Fisher Information (QFI) matrix, mean Uhlmann curvature, weighted parameter variance, and the incompatibility factor R.

Important parameters / regimes. The break-even baseline (L*), the entanglement resource factor f(r) = r/(1+r), and the number of stations M.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the weak thermal light assumption and neglects several effects like transmission loss and finite detector efficiency.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the incompatibility R for point vs. extended sources, compare weighted parameter variance for different receivers (collective vs. pairwise), and show the crossover defining the entanglement advantage threshold L*.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from formulating the multiparameter QFI problem, deriving the reduction lemma, comparing measurement strategies (collective vs. pairwise), determining the resource threshold, and finally solving the optimal entanglement allocation as a convex program.

Abstract

Entanglement-assisted optical interferometry promises diffraction-limited imaging over baselines where light cannot be physically combined, but existing theory treats only a single visibility on one two-station baseline. This work formulates the multiparameter estimation problem for an M-station array imaging an extended scene. A reduction lemma shows that the multimode quantum Fisher information (QFI) equals the mean photon number times the QFI of a single delocalized photon, collapsing the problem to a finite-dimensional one. The local photon-number superselection rule (SSR) erases all phase information from the bare state, while shared entanglement restores a fraction f(r)=r/(1+r) of the QFI on a baseline supplied with r pairs -- a factor shown to be achievable, since the reduced two-mode state of any pair is exactly the two-station weak-thermal state. The QFI matrix and its mean Uhlmann curvature reveal numerically that a point source is measurement-compatible, whereas for every extended-source model examined it is incompatible, the Holevo bound exceeding the symmetric-logarithmic-derivative (SLD) bound by up to ~74%. An explicit collective receiver -- a global mode-sorting (quantum Fourier transform) measurement -- then attains a weighted variance within ~7% of the SLD bound and roughly an order of magnitude below an explicit pairwise receiver, and a noisy-resource advantage threshold gives the break-even baseline (~20 km for near-term parameters) beyond which repeater-distributed entanglement beats direct transmission. Finally, the optimal allocation of a finite entanglement budget is a convex program with a closed-form proportional (square-root-law) solution favoring low-visibility baselines. These results recast quantum aperture synthesis as collective multiparameter estimation and give concrete design targets, illustrated on the CHARA array.

Summary. This work provides a comprehensive numerical and analytical study of the Abelian sandpile model on multiple lattices, aiming to characterize its universal correlations. By employing advanced sampling techniques and comparing results against Logarithmic CFT predictions, the authors confirm consistent scaling behavior across different geometries, including a first systematic study on the kagome lattice.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical statistical mechanics, the study of critical phenomena, scaling laws, and the connection to CFT provides deep insights into non-equilibrium dynamics and critical exponents, which are foundational concepts in quantum many-body physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the universal correlations and statistical properties of the Abelian sandpile model (ASM) on various 2D lattices.

Main result. The numerical and analytical results confirm that the ASM exhibits universal scaling behavior consistent with predictions from Logarithmic Conformal Field Theory (LCFT) across square, honeycomb, and kagome lattices.

Method. The authors combine advanced numerical sampling techniques (Wilson's algorithm with Majumdar-Dhar bijection) with analytical derivations using lattice Green functions to study bulk correlation functions.

Model / system. The system is the Abelian sandpile model (ASM) defined on planar lattices (square, honeycomb, kagome). The dynamics are governed by local toppling rules, and the focus is on the resulting recurrent, stable configurations.

Key observables. Bulk correlation functions (two-point), height probabilities (e.g., height-1 probability), and universal scaling exponents ($\Delta$).

Important parameters / regimes. The lattice structure (coordination number $z$) and the correlation length $\xi o \infty$ (criticality).

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining the ASM and its recurrence properties, establishing the mapping between recurrent configurations and spanning trees, presenting numerical studies on multiple lattices, and finally deriving analytical results for specific lattice types like the kagome lattice via Green functions.

Abstract

We numerically study the bulk correlation functions in the two-dimensional Abelian sandpile model, aiming both to compare with the predictions of logarithmic conformal field theory and to extend the analysis to lattices where analytical methods are difficult to apply. Wilson's algorithm efficiently generates large-scale uniform spanning trees in parallel, which can be mapped to independent recurrent configurations via the Majumdar--Dhar burning bijection, eliminating sample autocorrelations and yielding fast convergence. On the square (single-sublattice) and honeycomb (two-sublattice) lattices our results agree well with the analytical predictions. For the kagome lattice we provide the first systematic numerical study of the bulk correlation functions, and cross-check the bulk height-1 probability against a closed-form analytical expression that we also derive here via the lattice Green function.

Abstract

We studied the 2-Temperature induced phase separation (2-TIPS) in a system of soft repulsive sphe rocylinders (SRS), confined on the surface of a sphere. For the free anchoring, increasing either the packing fraction or the rod aspect ratio suppresses phase separation between the active and passive subsystems, and the system corresponds to a dense-dilute phase co-existence. For the tangential anchoring, the system phase separates from its initial isotropic state into a locally ordered dense region and a disordered dilute region. Our work provides useful insights into phase separation in binary mixtures under spherical confinement.

Optimal Intermediate Hamiltonians for Non-Equilibrium Free Energy Calculations: A Numerical Study of Markov Models

Authors: David Beyer, Helmut Grubmüller
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10519v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: methods for driven-dissipative 3/5 · quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops a method to find optimal time-dependent Hamiltonians that significantly improve the convergence of free energy estimates derived from non-equilibrium simulations. By minimizing the Mean Squared Error (MSE) of the Jarzynski estimator for Markov models, the authors show that these optimal Hamiltonians can drastically outperform simple linear or logarithmic interpolations. This offers a powerful new tool for calculating free energy barriers in complex physical systems.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides concrete, computationally derived heuristics for improving non-equilibrium free energy calculations, which is crucial for simulating processes in molecular dynamics and condensed matter systems where rare events dominate.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the poor convergence of free energy estimates derived from non-equilibrium, finite-time switching simulations using the Jarzynski relation.

Main result. Optimal intermediate Hamiltonians, determined by minimizing the Mean Squared Error (MSE), can reduce the MSE by over an order of magnitude compared to simple interpolations.

Method. The authors use gradient-based minimization techniques applied to an exact tilted-master-equation representation of the MSE for discrete-time Markov models.

Model / system. The study uses discrete-time Markov models defined on a line of $m$ states, whose evolution is governed by a time-dependent Hamiltonian $H(x, au)$ within a master equation framework.

Key observables. Mean Squared Error (MSE) of the Jarzynski estimator, mean work ($\langle W angle$), and mean position ($\langle x angle$).

Important parameters / regimes. The complexity is varied by changing the number of states ($m$), the depth ($\delta$) of the potential well, and the number of intermediate steps ($N$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is restricted to discrete-time Markov models, and the optimization relies on the large-sample limit ($n \gg 1$) for the MSE calculation.

Figures summary. Figures systematically compare the calculated mean position ($\langle x angle$) and the MSE for various intermediate Hamiltonians (optimal vs. linear/logarithmic) across different model parameters ($\delta, m, N$).

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the problem using the Jarzynski relation for Markov models. It then develops the mathematical framework to minimize the MSE using tilted-master-equations. Finally, it applies this method to complex model systems like the shifted potential well, comparing optimal results against standard interpolation schemes.

Abstract

The Jarzynski relation enables the estimation of equilibrium free energy differences from non-equilibrium, finite-time switching simulations. These estimates usually converge poorly because rare trajectories dominate the exponential work average. Here, we numerically determined and explored the sequence of intermediate Hamiltonians connecting initial and final states that minimize the mean squared error (MSE) of the Jarzynski estimator and thereby enhance convergence. For discrete-time Markov models, an exact tilted-master-equation representation of the MSE in the large-sample limit, combined with automatic differentiation, enables efficient gradient-based minimization over all intermediate energies. We applied our approach to three model systems of increasing complexity: a two-state model, a double-well potential, and a shifted potential well. In all three systems, the optimal intermediate Hamiltonians jump at the initial and final times. Extensive Monte Carlo simulations show that optimal intermediates can reduce the MSE by more than an order of magnitude compared with linear and logarithmic interpolation, most strongly for large changes in the energy landscape. Remarkably, they need not dissipate less work than intermediates yielding larger errors. Our results suggest heuristics for more efficient non-equilibrium free energy calculations of realistic molecular systems: optimal intermediate Hamiltonians jump at the initial and final times; for barrier-crossing problems, the barrier should be lowered rapidly and raised again later; and minimizing dissipation does not guarantee faster convergence.

Summary. This paper rigorously demonstrates that the self-testing of maximally entangled qubit strategies is not robust when restricted only to full-rank Projective Measurements (PVMs). Abstract

We construct a nonlocal game that self-tests a maximally entangled qubit strategy among pure full-Schmidt-rank projective strategies, but admits an inequivalent optimum using one nonprojective measurement. This resolves a conjecture of Baptista et al. on imposing full rank and projectivity simultaneously. The construction combines CHSH with an auxiliary game $G$ that forces deterministic answers under these assumptions and admits a trine POVM optimum without them. We classify the optimal correlations of $G$ as a line segment parametrized by the tracial states of $\mathbb{C}\oplus M_2(\mathbb{C})$. Projectivity on the state support removes the matrix summand, whereas projective dilations with nonzero nonabort probability have a nontracial local state whose zero left ideal is not two-sided. Finally, a family in local dimension six shows that the full-rank PVM self-test is not robust.

Abstract

Its violation then witnesses Bell nonlocality or measurement contextuality.

Abstract

Thermodynamics is useful because it lets us predict and control complex systems from a few accessible quantities when microscopic reconstruction is impractical. We ask whether this operational reduction can be less costly in a finite quantum system than in a matched classical one. We compare a few-boson Bose--Hubbard chain with its number-conserving classical-field counterpart under the same driving and the same coarse spatial record. The quantum system relaxes more in the observed particle distribution, yet the retained mean energy resolves more of the microscopic structure hidden by that record, leaving less information unusable and smaller entropy generation. We then close the process into a heat-engine cycle whose controls use only the measured mean energy and spatial record, not full state tomography; at matched thermal resources, the smaller entropy generation gives more work and higher efficiency. The advantage fades as the particle number grows toward the classical-field regime.
