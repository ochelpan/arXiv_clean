Signature of Short-Range Order in Static Response of the Three-Dimensional Electron Gas

Authors: Muhammed H. Güneş, Yubo Yang, Vitaly Gorelov, Miguel A. Morales, Matteo Gatti, Lucia Reining, Shiwei Zhang
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10006v1

Summary. This work uses Diffusion Monte Carlo simulations to calculate the static response functions of the 3D electron gas in highly correlated regimes. The authors identify a distinct structural feature in the local field factor G(q) that serves as a fingerprint of short-range order. This finding provides a crucial, benchmark result for improving theoretical descriptions of electronic structure in strongly interacting quantum matter.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous calculation of correlation functions like G(q) in the strongly coupled regime is crucial for developing accurate theoretical models for materials where electron-electron interactions dominate, which is relevant for understanding correlated phases in quantum materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to accurately determine the wave-vector-dependent static local field factor G(q) and the static density-density response function for the 3D electron gas, especially in the strongly coupled regime where benchmarks are lacking.

Main result. The calculations reveal a pronounced structure in G(q) at intermediate wave vectors, identified as a fingerprint of short-range order, which is necessary to correctly reproduce the static structure factor S(q).

Method. Diffusion Monte Carlo (DMC) simulations are employed to calculate the static density-density response function, from which G(q) is derived using the Dyson equation.

Model / system. The system modeled is the three-dimensional electron gas (HEG), a fundamental model in condensed matter physics used as a starting point for ab initio calculations.

Key observables. Static local field factor G(q), static density-density response function $\chi(q)$, and the static structure factor S(q).

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis focuses on the strongly coupled regime (low density, high correlation, $r_s > 10$) and intermediate wave vectors ($q/k_F \sim 1-4$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis utilizes the Single-Mode Approximation (SMA) for calculating S(q) and develops a parametrization for G(q) valid across the entire liquid phase.

Figures summary. Figures show G(q) for various $r_s$ comparing DMC data to fits, and also illustrate the evolution of $\chi(q)$, $S(q)$, and the dynamic structure factor $S(q, \omega)$ across different correlation regimes.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the computational challenge, presents the DMC results for $\chi(q)$ and $G(q)$, identifies the structural feature as the fingerprint of short-range order, and concludes by developing a comprehensive parametrization for G(q) that also predicts low-energy resonant modes.

Abstract

The three-dimensional electron gas is a fundamental model in condensed matter physics and quantum chemistry, and the exchange-correlation energy derived from it serves as the starting point of \textit{ab initio} computations of materials. However, the wave-vector-dependent response, and hence the static local field factor $G(q)$, has remained without accurate ground-state benchmark in the strongly coupled regime for three decades. Using diffusion Monte Carlo, we calculate $G(q)$ and the static density-density response function across the liquid phase and find a pronounced structure in $G(q)$ at intermediate wave vectors, already visible at metallic densities and growing with increasing interaction strength. We identify it as a fingerprint of short-range order by showing that it is required to reproduce the static structure factor. Our parametrization, valid in the entire liquid phase, predicts a low-energy resonant mode inside the particle-hole continuum.

Summary. This paper develops a combinatorial framework by studying how the single-particle spectrum of non-interacting quantum systems can be factorized. It uses symmetric function theory to derive explicit, related formulas for the canonical Bose and Fermi partition functions. This provides a systematic mathematical tool for understanding quantum statistics based on the underlying spectral structure.

Why it may be interesting. The deep connection between spectral factorization, combinatorial identities (symmetric functions), and the fundamental difference between Bose and Fermi statistics provides a powerful, unified mathematical tool for analyzing quantum many-body systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper studies the factorization properties of the single-particle spectrum of non-interacting quantum systems and how these factorizations determine the resulting many-particle Bose and Fermi statistics.

Main result. It establishes a consolidated combinatorial framework showing that product spectra lead to specific, related forms for the canonical Bose and Fermi partition functions, linking them via symmetric function identities and descent set complementation.

Method. The authors use spectral factorization, mapping tensor factorizations of the single-particle kernel to multiplicative factorizations of a formal alphabet, and employing advanced symmetric function theory (Schur functions, $\lambda$-rings).

Model / system. The analysis is general for non-interacting systems, initially focusing on the imaginary-time propagator ($e^{-eta H_1}$) but generalizing to arbitrary trace-class one-particle kernels $K$. Specific examples include harmonic traps and separable energy spectra.

Key observables. Canonical Bose and Fermi partition functions ($Z_{n,B}, Z_{n,F}$), the single-particle partition function $Z_1(eta)$, and the spectral alphabet $X$.

Important parameters / regimes. The dimensionality $d$ of the system (especially for harmonic traps), and the temperature parameter $eta$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the ability to factorize the spectral alphabet $X$ into simpler components ($\hat{X}, \hat{Y}$), and the mathematical framework is built upon identities derived from the underlying spectral structure.

Figures summary. The notes mention specific identities (Cauchy identities, dual Cauchy identities) and relationships involving major indices ($ ext{maj}(D)$) for harmonic traps, distinguishing between bosonic and fermionic sums.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining the spectral factorization framework, establishing the relationship between Bose and Fermi statistics via descent sets, and then applying these concepts to specific physical models like the harmonic trap and general separable systems.

Abstract

We study factorizations of the single-particle spectrum of non-interacting quantum systems and their consequences for many-particle statistics. Representing the single-particle partition function by a spectral alphabet, tensor factorizations become multiplicative factorizations of the alphabet, which can be lifted to canonical Bose and Fermi partition functions using standard symmetric-function and $λ$-ring identities. We show how product spectra arise from different factorizations, how antisymmetrization can be assigned across an odd number of factors, and which tensor factorizations are compatible with a fixed spectrum. The paper therefore studies spectral factorization and provides a consolidated combinatorial framework for canonical Bose and Fermi partition functions.

Abstract

Primitive quantum speedups are interface-relative: they depend on the input access used to run the primitive and on the output contract used to consume its state or samples. This paper introduces a transcript-level admissibility relation \(A_M\preceq_{\mathrm{int}}A_Q\), defined relative to the declared implementation package of the quantum interface. It identifies which adaptive classical access transcripts that same package licenses, with all setup, transcript-generation, and precision overheads charged. The main application is an operational audit for normalized-Betti estimation in clique-complex TDA, separating three declared-interface regimes. Reversible indexed simplex interfaces certify matched classical simplex sampling and local Laplacian row access by evaluating their reversible routines on single computational branches. Membership-based preparations induce a rejection route of overhead \(\binom{n}{k+1}/|S_k|\). Abstract spectral or block-encoding interfaces require an accompanying implementation package, transcript reduction, or shared representation. Under the indexed certificate and interface closure, the end-to-end cost is fixed by the imported estimator's spectral dependence on the gap \(γ\); the concretely realized bounded-treewidth family already admits exact \(\mathrm{poly}(n)\) classical Betti computation by rank over \(\mathbb{Q}\). A low-rank separation supports the role of access and output contracts.

A Sum-of-Squares Hierarchy with Quadratic Convergence for Quantum Channel Coding

Summary. This work develops a novel Hermitian Sum-of-Squares (SOS) hierarchy to provide upper bounds on the optimal success probability for quantum channel coding. By employing advanced techniques involving positive polynomial kernels, the authors prove that this hierarchy exhibits quadratic convergence, significantly improving upon existing error estimates. This provides a powerful, computationally tractable tool for analyzing fundamental limits in quantum communication theory.

Why it may be interesting. The development of polynomial-time, rapidly converging mathematical tools (SOS hierarchies) to solve fundamental, hard problems in quantum information theory is highly relevant for theoretical quantum physicists.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the NP-hard problem of computing the optimal success probability for transmitting classical messages through a single use of a quantum channel.

Main result. The authors construct a Hermitian Sum-of-Squares (SOS) hierarchy that achieves quadratic convergence for bounding this success probability.

Method. The approach combines state-discrimination duality with positive polynomial kernels on products of spheres to construct feasible polynomial dual certificates for the SOS hierarchy.

Model / system. The system involves a quantum channel $\Phi: L(A) o L(B)$ acting on finite-dimensional complex Hilbert spaces, where the goal is to maximize the average success probability $P_{ ext{succ}}(\Phi, k)$.

Key observables. Optimal success probability $P_{ ext{succ}}(\Phi, k)$ and the trace-norm contraction coefficient $\eta_{ ext{tr}}(\Phi)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The level $L$ of the hierarchy, and the dimension parameters $d_A$ and $d_B$ of the input and output spaces.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the structure of the sphere ideal and the use of positive squared-polynomial kernels to establish the convergence rate.

Figures summary. Figure 1 summarizes the construction, contrasting the SOS hierarchy with the feasible certificates used for convergence analysis.

Paper structure. The paper builds from defining the NP-hard problem and existing SDP bounds, to introducing the novel Hermitian SOS hierarchy, and finally proving the quadratic convergence rate using advanced polynomial and kernel techniques.

Abstract

Computing the optimal success probability for transmitting classical messages through a single use of a quantum channel is NP-hard, even for two messages. An existing semidefinite programming hierarchy based on symmetric extensions provides convergent upper bounds with an a priori error estimate that decays as the inverse square root of the extension level. In this work, we construct a Hermitian sum-of-squares hierarchy for an arbitrary number of messages and prove quadratic convergence in its level. The error bound is proportional to the advantage over random guessing. Our approach combines state-discrimination duality with positive polynomial kernels on products of spheres to construct feasible polynomial dual certificates. For binary messages, the resulting bounds give a multiplicative approximation from above of the trace-norm contraction coefficient.

Summary. This paper introduces a novel, hybrid decoding framework for Quantum Low-Density Parity-Check (QLDPC) codes by combining Belief Propagation (BP) with the A*-based Tesseract decoder. A reliability-based gating mechanism filters the output of the fast BP stage to guide the Tesseract search on a reduced problem. This significantly accelerates decoding complexity (up to 15x reduction) while preserving the high performance of the full Tesseract decoder.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the computational bottleneck in quantum error correction, which is crucial for scaling up quantum computation. The hybrid approach suggests a general methodology for combining fast, approximate methods with rigorous, but slow, exact solvers.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core problem is developing an efficient decoding algorithm for Quantum Low-Density Parity-Check (QLDPC) codes to enable reliable, large-scale quantum computation.

Main result. The proposed hybrid BP-Tesseract decoder significantly reduces the computational complexity of decoding by at least 5x, while successfully maintaining the logical error rate of the standalone Tesseract decoder.

Method. A two-stage decoding framework is proposed: Belief Propagation (BP) acts as a fast preprocessing step to generate initial estimates, and a reliability-based gating mechanism filters uncertain results before passing the residual problem to the A*-based Tesseract decoder.

Model / system. The work focuses on QLDPC codes, which are quantum error correction codes often constructed using the CSS framework. The decoding process is modeled using the Tanner graph structure inherent to these codes.

Key observables. Decoding complexity (measured by expanded nodes in the Tesseract search graph) and the Logical Error Rate (LER).

Important parameters / regimes. Physical error rate ($p$), the gating threshold ($ au$), and the fixed prior probability ($p_{ ext{gated}}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that the high speed of BP decoding can effectively preprocess the syndrome, allowing the computationally intensive Tesseract search to operate on a much smaller, refined problem.

Figures summary. Figures compare the mean number of node expansions and the LER for stand-alone Tesseract, BP-Tesseract, and the proposed gated BP-Tesseract decoder across various physical error rates.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the limitations of stand-alone Tesseract (high overhead) and BP (convergence issues). It then introduces the hybrid BP-Tesseract framework, detailing the reliability gating mechanism using metrics like $p_{ ext{flip}}$ and $\Delta_{ ext{mean}}$, and finally presents numerical results demonstrating the performance gains.

Abstract

Quantum low-density parity-check (QLDPC) codes represent a promising approach for error correction in quantum computing. The recently proposed Tesseract decoder uses the A* search algorithm that guarantees finding the most likely error pattern. However, practical implementations of Tesseract often involve an extremely large graph, and the inherently sequential nature of the search results in high computational overhead and long runtime. To improve decoding efficiency, we propose a two-stage decoding framework. First, a belief propagation (BP) decoder efficiently processes the syndrome. This step generates hard decisions (a binary error vector) and soft information (per-qubit confidence levels). In non-convergent BP cases, a gating mechanism examines the BP decoder's output to identify and filter out qubits with oscillating confidence values. In these cases, the refined output serves as input to Tesseract, which then attempts to identify and correct any residual errors. This hybrid approach leverages the high speed of BP decoding and delegates the more challenging decoding instances to Tesseract. Numerical results demonstrate a substantial reduction in overall decoding complexity while maintaining the logical error rate (LER) of the stand-alone Tesseract. Across all tested physical error rates, the proposed method achieves at least 5x reduction in the number of expanded nodes in the Tesseract, with a peak reduction of nearly 15x at a physical error rate of p = 0.05 for the [[126, 12, d < 11]] T1 code, and approximately 8.8x at p = 0.05 for the [[72, 12, 6]] bicycle bivariate (BB) code.

Abstract

We present and analyze an iterative refinement (IR) framework for improving the precision dependence of algorithms that combine a quantum linear system algorithm (QLSA) with quantum state tomography. Existing QLSAs achieve polylogarithmic dependence on the inverse error tolerance to prepare a quantum state encoding the solution, but extracting a classical description of such a state via tomography typically introduces a polynomial dependence on the target precision. To retain polylogarithmic dependence in the inverse error tolerance throughout the entire process, we develop an IR scheme that solves a sequence of related linear systems to progressively refine the solution while requiring only fixed-precision quantum subroutines, finally obtaining a classical description of a high-precision solution. We analyze the proposed framework under three input models: quantum-read/classical-write RAM (QRAM), linear combinations of unitaries (LCU), and sparse-access oracles. We evaluate the proposed scheme through numerical experiments on both quantum simulators and real quantum hardware. The results demonstrate that iterative refinement efficiently improves the precision of the solution and exhibits robustness to hardware noise.

Abstract

Macroscopic transport tensors are generally constrained by the average crystallographic and magnetic symmetries of a material. In the kagome antiferromagnetic Weyl semimetals Mn$_{3+δ}X$ ($X=$~Sn or Ge), previous studies showed that the anomalous Hall conductivity $σ_{yx}$ is forbidden by the average \hexsg{} structure and coplanar inverse-triangular magnetic order. Our results establish correlated disorder as an active symmetry-breaking degree of freedom that enables topological transport inaccessible from the Bragg-average structure alone.

Summary. This theoretical paper develops structural criteria to determine when quantum optimization algorithms, specifically the Arimoto-Blahut (AB) method, achieve global optimality. It establishes that optimality depends on a precise relationship between the AB update direction and the objective gradient relative to the constraint space. The findings provide powerful, computable certificates for assessing the reliability of quantum optimization routines.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides rigorous, computable criteria for assessing convergence in quantum optimization algorithms, which is crucial for designing reliable quantum information processing protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper establishes necessary and sufficient conditions for the global optimality of fixed points generated by generalized Arimoto-Blahut (AB) algorithms when optimizing quantum objectives.

Main result. Global optimality is guaranteed if and only if the AB update direction and the objective gradient differ by an element of the constraint normal space. Furthermore, an a posteriori certificate based on feasible directional derivatives can detect suboptimal fixed points.

Method. The authors compare the AB iterative update rule with the Mirror Descent (MD) update rule, deriving algebraic conditions for their equivalence and developing finite-difference certificates for optimality.

Model / system. The analysis focuses on optimizing objective functions $f( ho) = ext{Tr} \ ho \Omega( ho)$ over quantum states $ ho$ constrained to a manifold $M$ in a finite-dimensional Hilbert space $H$. The context involves quantum channels and relative entropy minimization.

Key observables. The objective function $f( ho)$, the AB update direction $\Omega( ho)$, the gradient $ abla f( ho)$, and the diagnostic quantity $ ext{Tr}[( ho - b ho_H)\Omega( ho)]$.

Important parameters / regimes. The structure of the linear constraints defining the manifold $M$, and the specific parameters of the quantum channels analyzed (e.g., dephasing vs. depolarizing).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the objective function is convex and differentiable, and it notes that monotonic objective decrease and numerical stabilization are insufficient guarantees for global optimality.

Figures summary. Not specified in the notes, but the analysis relies on deriving explicit gradient formulas (e.g., Equation (229)) and proving strict positivity of diagnostic quantities away from the fixed point.

It then develops a posteriori optimality certificates using feasible directional derivatives, culminating in proofs of uniqueness for the global minimizer in specific channel models.

Abstract

Generalized Arimoto--Blahut (AB) algorithms are widely used in information theory and quantum optimization, but monotonic objective decrease and numerical stabilization do not guarantee global optimality. We establish necessary and sufficient conditions for the global optimality of full-rank AB fixed points for convex differentiable objectives under linear constraints. An AB fixed point is globally optimal if and only if the AB update direction and the objective gradient differ by an element of the constraint normal space at that point. The same compatibility condition characterizes agreement between individual AB and mirror-descent (MD) updates, whereas pathwise equivalence requires it along the entire common trajectory. Thus, an AB algorithm may follow a trajectory different from MD and still reach the global optimum. We also develop a posteriori optimality certificates based on feasible directional derivatives, including finite-difference upper bounds on the objective gap that require only objective evaluations. For channel relative entropy between dephasing and depolarizing channels, we analytically identify the global minimizer as the unique full-rank AB fixed point, although pathwise equivalence fails. Numerical experiments illustrate convergence of AB and MD along different trajectories and validate the certificates. By contrast, an amplitude-damping example shows that a monotone AB iteration can stabilize at a suboptimal fixed point, whose nonoptimality is detected by the finite-difference certificate. These results provide structural and computable criteria for assessing global optimality in quantum AB algorithms.

Abstract

Abstract

The Holevo barycenter of a quantum channel is the unique output state obtained as the average output of any ensemble achieving the Holevo capacity. Given two quantum channels, the multiplicativity problem asks whether this barycenter tensorizes under parallel composition, namely whether the barycenter of the product channel coincides with the tensor product of the individual barycenters. This question is closely related to the additivity problem for the Holevo capacity: additivity implies tensorization of the Holevo barycenter, while tensorization alone is not sufficient for additivity. Although a construction is known that demonstrates the existence of channels with non-additive Holevo capacity, their corresponding Holevo barycenters still tensorize, leaving open whether multiplicativity might ultimately hold universally. Here, we answer this question in the negative by exhibiting channels for which the Holevo barycenter is not multiplicative under the tensor product of the channel with itself. Moreover, we show that the entropy of the Holevo barycenter is neither universally subadditive nor universally superadditive under tensor product.

Abstract

We prove the sharp Hausdorff--Young inequality on the quantum Euclidean space. Meanwhile, our result implies the sharp Hausdorff-Young constants for the Weyl transform, as well as that for the Heisenberg group. The key ingredient is a novel operator-valued flow related to the Gabor transform. Our method is mainly based on operator calculus and hence generally applicable to similar problems. Finally, we give the definition of noncommutative convolution and include the partial results for the sharp Young's inequality.
