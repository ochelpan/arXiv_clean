Observation of Flat Bands in Type-II Weyl Semimetal TaRhTe$_{4}$

Authors: Harry Rankin, Tyler J. Slade, Benjamin Schrunk, Yevhen Kushnirenko, Andrew Eaton, K. U. R. R. S. Rathnayaka, Maxwell Doyle, Lin-Lin Wang, Paul C. Canfield, Adam Kaminski

Summary. This work reports experimental evidence of unexpected flat energy bands in the Weyl semimetal TaRhTe4 using ARPES. The findings highlight a significant discrepancy between theory and experiment, suggesting that topological materials can host emergent electronic states not captured by current computational models. This combination of topology and flat-band physics makes it an interesting platform for condensed matter research.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on topology/band structure, the observation of emergent, unpredicted electronic features like flat bands provides a platform for investigating strong correlation effects or novel many-body physics that standard DFT might miss.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to observe flat electronic bands near the Fermi level in a noncentrosymmetric bulk Weyl semimetal, as such features are rare and unexpected based on initial theoretical calculations.

Main result. ARPES measurements reveal the presence of distinct, momentum-independent flat bands localized at specific energies below the Fermi level, particularly evident on one crystal surface termination (Termination A).

Method. The research combines Angle-Resolved Photoemission Spectroscopy (ARPES) experiments with Density Functional Theory (DFT) calculations to probe the electronic structure.

Model / system. The physical system is bulk TaRhTe4, classified as a noncentrosymmetric van-der Waals type-II Weyl semimetal. The analysis compares experimental measurements on different crystal terminations against theoretical predictions.

Key observables. Flat bands near the chemical potential, Fermi surfaces (FSs), and the location/existence of Weyl points (WPs).

Important parameters / regimes. Low temperatures (5–5.5 K) for ARPES; energy scales around 4 meV below E_F.

Assumptions / limitations. The flat bands observed were not predicted by initial DFT calculations, suggesting physics beyond the standard theory or surface effects are at play.

Figures summary. Figures compare calculated and measured FSs for two terminations (A and B); Figure 4 specifically highlights the momentum-independent flat bands on Termination A via EDCs/cuts.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the material's topological nature using DFT, presents ARPES data comparing different surface terminations, identifies general agreement with theory, and then focuses heavily on the discrepancy—the unexpected appearance of flat bands in experiment versus calculation.

Abstract

Flat bands have been theoretically predicted for decades but have only recently been realized in quantum materials such as magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene, kagome and Lieb lattices, and rare-earth metal compounds. To date, only twisted layered materials have enabled tuning of flat-band energies near the electronic chemical potential, thereby influencing transport and thermodynamic properties. Here, we report the presence of flat bands near the chemical potential in bulk TaRhTe$_{4}$, a noncentrosymmetric van-der Waals type-II Weyl semimetal. Flat bands are rarely observed in Weyl semimetals, particularly in nonmagnetic bulk systems, and the observed flat bands were not predicted by density functional theory calculations. TaRhTe$_{4}$ therefore provides a platform in which nontrivial topology coexists with flat bands near the Fermi level, as evidenced by our angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy measurements.

Summary. This work characterizes the weak ferromagnetism in NiS2 nanocrystals by measuring their magnetization across temperature ranges relative to antiferromagnetic transitions. The key finding is that the strong magnetic hysteresis below the lower transition is attributed to uncompensated surface moments, which successfully explains the observed field-cooling effects while simultaneously constraining theoretical models of bulk magnetism.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on magnetism, the study's rigorous comparison of bulk vs. nanoscale magnetic responses, coupled with detailed characterization of phase transitions via heat capacity, provides a model for how dimensionality and interfaces fundamentally alter many-body quantum ground states.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the origin of weak ferromagnetism observed in NiS2, specifically determining whether this magnetism arises from homogeneous canting throughout the bulk or is localized at structural features like domain walls.

Main result. The pronounced hysteretic ferromagnetic response below the lower antiferromagnetic transition temperature is best explained by uncompensated surface moments generated where the low-temperature magnetic order terminates at the nanocrystal surfaces.

Method. Magnetization (M-H) and specific heat measurements were performed on synthesized NiS2 nanocrystals, comparing results above and below the critical transition temperatures.

Model / system. The physical system is structurally well-ordered NiS2 nanocrystals ($\sim 27$ nm). The analysis contrasts two theoretical models for weak ferromagnetism: homogeneous canting (volume property) versus localization at domain walls (interface property).

Key observables. Hysteretic ferromagnetic response, large coercivity, exchange bias, and vertical loop shift observed below $T_{N2}$; nearly linear M-H response above $T_{N2}$.

Important parameters / regimes. Nanocrystal diameter ($\sim 27$ nm); Antiferromagnetic transition temperatures ($T_{N1} \simeq 39$ K, $T_{N2} \simeq 30$ K).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the observed magnetic features are highly sensitive to nanoscale confinement effects, allowing differentiation between bulk and surface contributions.

Figures summary. Figures show temperature dependence of magnetization (ZFC/FC) confirming two transitions; hysteresis loops demonstrate strong magnetic signatures only below $T_{N2}$, which are analyzed for exchange bias and coercivity.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the bulk properties, synthesizes nanocrystals, measures magnetic responses across different temperatures, and then uses the temperature-dependent contrast between hysteretic (low T) and linear (high T) behavior to rule out competing theoretical models in favor of surface moment generation.

Abstract

Structurally well-ordered NiS$_2$ nanocrystals with an average diameter of $27.0 \pm 6.5$ nm retain the bulk-like two-step antiferromagnetic transitions, as shown by magnetization and heat-capacity measurements. Below the lower transition, the nanocrystals exhibit a hysteretic ferromagnetic response with large coercivity, exchange bias, and a vertical loop shift after field cooling, whereas the $M$-$H$ response just above the transition is nearly linear. These features are best explained by uncompensated surface moments generated where the low-temperature antiferromagnetic order terminates at the nanocrystal surface. The absence of a clear additional bulk-like weak-ferromagnetic component constrains homogeneous-canting models and indirectly favors a domain-wall scenario for the weak ferromagnetism of bulk NiS$_2$.

Deconfined criticality between an antiferromagnetic insulator and a nodal d-wave superconductor: a quantum Monte Carlo study

Authors: Chuang Chen, Subir Sachdev, Zi Yang Meng
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.00762v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5 · analog quantum simulation 1/5

Summary. The authors use Quantum Monte Carlo simulations on a fractionalized lattice model to study the transition between an antiferromagnetic insulator and a d-wave superconductor. They find evidence for a deconfined quantum critical point where both orders vanish continuously, characterized by changes in the electronic dispersion from gapless Dirac cones to gapped states.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides advanced numerical evidence for deconfined quantum criticality in models relevant to high-Tc superconductivity, utilizing sophisticated gauge field theories within a QMC framework that is highly valuable for understanding emergent quantum phases.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the quantum phase transition (QPT) between an antiferromagnetic insulator (Néel state) and a nodal d-wave superconductor in strongly correlated electron systems.

Main result. Evidence is found for a second-order deconfined quantum phase transition (DQCP) where both Néel and d-wave superconducting orders vanish continuously. The electronic dispersion changes from gapless Dirac to gapped as the system moves into the AFM state.

Method. Quantum Monte Carlo (QMC) simulations are employed using a fractionalized representation of electrons into fermionic spinons and bosonic chargons coupled to an SU(2) lattice gauge field.

Model / system. The model is defined on the square lattice at half-filling, utilizing an SU(2) lattice gauge theory framework. The electron degrees of freedom are decomposed into spinon and chargon components whose dynamics govern the competition between magnetic and superconducting orders.

Key observables. Spin structure factor ($\chi_S$), pairing structure factor ($\chi_{el, d}$), electronic spectral function ($A(\mathbf{k}, \omega)$), and correlation ratios for AFM, dSC, and VBS order.

Important parameters / regimes. The transition is mapped by varying coupling constants like $\kappa$ and $\kappa_ au$, which tune the relative strengths of magnetic and superconducting interactions.

Assumptions / limitations. The method relies on successfully fractionalizing electrons into spinons and chargons to avoid the sign problem in QMC simulations. The analysis also assumes that specific gauge-invariant composite operators correctly capture physical observables.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate phase diagrams showing three distinct regimes (dSC, AFM, deconfined) separated by transition lines, alongside spectral plots demonstrating gapless Dirac dispersion in the dSC phase and gapped behavior in the AFM phase across the critical point.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the Hamiltonian decomposition into spinon and chargon parts. It then applies QMC simulations to calculate various correlation functions and spectra across different parameter regimes, culminating in a detailed comparison of spectral features near the proposed DQCP.

Abstract

We present a quantum Monte Carlo study of the transition between the insulating Néel state and the nodal $d$-wave superconductor on the square lattice at half-filling. We access a regime of frustrated magnetic order without a sign problem using a parton representation of the electron in terms of fermionic spinons and bosonic chargons. Both partons move in a background $π$-flux (so the electron experiences no net flux) and are coupled to a quantum fluctuating SU(2) lattice gauge field. In contrast to earlier studies directly on the electronic degrees of freedom, we find evidence for a second-order deconfined quantum phase transition at which both the Néel and $d$-wave superconductivity orders vanish continuously. We compute correlators of the spinon-chargon composite with the same quantum numbers as the electron: we find a gapless Dirac dispersion inside the $d$-wave superconductor, turning into a gapped dispersion in the antiferromagnet.

Non-signaling assistance in prepare-and-measure scenarios with classical communication

Authors: José Nogueira, Carlos Vieira, Lucas E. A. Porto, Lucas Pollyceno, Rafael Rabelo, Otfried Gühne

Summary. This paper rigorously compares the power of adaptive versus non-adaptive Non-Signaling (NS) assistance in Prepare-and-Measure quantum communication models. It establishes that while NS assistance can simulate full quantum communication, adaptivity only yields a strict advantage over non-adaptivity under specific structural conditions related to input discrimination tasks.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides fundamental theoretical bounds on what can be achieved using correlations weaker than entanglement but stronger than classical communication, offering insights into resource theories beyond standard quantum channels.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the operational advantage of using adaptive versus non-adaptive Non-Signaling (NS) assistance in Prepare-and-Measure (PM) quantum communication scenarios.

Main result. Adaptive NS assistance provides a strict advantage over non-adaptive assistance only in specific PM scenarios characterized by input-discrimination inequalities. Furthermore, non-adaptive NS assistance is shown to be powerful enough to simulate quantum communication for the same message dimension.

Method. The authors characterize achievable probability distributions using linear programming techniques and structural reductions, comparing sets defined by adaptive vs. non-adaptive correlation models.

Model / system. The model is a Prepare-and-Measure (PM) scenario $(n_X; n_Y, n_B)_{nA}$, where Alice prepares a message $a$ based on input $x$, and Bob measures given input $y$ and the message $a$. The resource analyzed is Non-Signaling (NS) correlation assistance.

Key observables. The achievable probability distributions $q(b|x y)$ characterizing the communication behavior, and derived constraints like $\max_{x \in [nX]} q(b|x, y) \le nA$.

Important parameters / regimes. Message dimension ($n_A$), input dimensions ($n_X, n_Y$), and output dimension ($n_B$). The comparison hinges on the relationship between $n_A$ and the system sizes.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is restricted to classical communication channels assisted by NS correlations. Key results rely on reducing general scenarios to single-input subscenarios ($n_Y=1$).

Figures summary. Figures illustrate schematics for non-adaptive and adaptive NS assistance, contrasting the mathematical definitions of the achievable sets.

Paper structure. The paper systematically defines the PM model, characterizes behaviors under classical randomness, then analyzes the constraints imposed by non-adaptive NS assistance (showing it simulates quantum communication). Finally, it rigorously compares this to adaptive NS assistance, identifying the precise conditions for a strict advantage.

Abstract

Extracting the full power of non-local correlations in prepare-and-measure (PM) scenarios requires precise control over the timing and structure of the receiver's measurements. Indeed, recent developments in entanglement-assisted classical communication scenarios have shown that adaptive strategies-where the receiver uses the transmitted message to guide their measurement choice-can outperform standard non-adaptive protocols. Moving beyond quantum theory, however, the ultimate limits of such advantages remain largely unexplored. In this work, we thoroughly study adaptive and non-adaptive non-signaling (NS) assistance in PM scenarios with classical communication. We provide simple characterizations of the sets of behaviors that can be realized using both non-adaptive and adaptive NS assistance in arbitrary PM scenarios. As a consequence, we show that non-adaptive NS assistance is already strong enough to reproduce quantum communication with the same message dimension: the transmission of a qudit can be simulated by a classical dit assisted non-adaptively by NS correlations. We then compare adaptive and non-adaptive NS assistance. We prove that any adaptive NS advantage can be traced back to scenarios in which the receiver has no measurement choice, ruling out the genuinely multi-setting advantages found in entanglement-assisted quantum protocols. Finally, we identify all PM scenarios where adaptive NS strategies provide a strict advantage over non-adaptive ones.

Summary. The paper addresses the critical challenge of deploying Quantum Illumination through turbulent air by developing a sophisticated, data-driven framework. Abstract

Quantum illumination offers a significant theoretical advantage for target detection in high background noise environments. However, its practical deployment in free-space channels is hindered by atmospheric turbulence. Stochastic fluctuations in atmospheric turbulence inevitably degrade the quantum signature, rendering the real-time evaluation of quantum advantage under such dynamic conditions a critical yet unresolved challenge. To circumvent the reliance on costly direct turbulence measurements, we propose a physics-driven framework that integrates Kolmogorov-Arnold networks directly bridge macroscopic meteorological observations with microscopic quantum channel dynamics. Trained on 105,120 samples from 12 climatically diverse sites and validated on 26,280 unseen samples from three extreme boundary conditions (arid continental, tropical maritime, high-altitude plateau), our approach establishes a physically consistent mapping from standard meteorological variables to the temporal evolution of the quantum advantage. This end-to-end system dynamically quantifies the degradation of quantum advantage across diverse turbulence conditions. Our results provide a rigorous theoretical and data-driven pathway for environmental adaptation, facilitating the transition of quantum radar networks from proof-of-principle demonstrations to all-weather operational systems.

Summary. This theoretical paper develops a rigorous framework to calculate the 'quantum complexity resource' from mixed Gaussian states of light. By formulating this as a convex optimization problem, the authors derive that while simple cases yield closed-form solutions, the general two-mode case requires finding the root of a high-degree polynomial, proving that no simple analytical solution exists.

Why it may be interesting. It provides deep mathematical insight into the limitations of finding closed-form solutions for fundamental physical quantities, connecting quantum resource theory to advanced algebraic geometry (Galois theory).

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper tackles the problem of quantifying a 'quantum complexity resource' inherent in mixed Gaussian states of multimode light.

Main result. For the two-mode case, the optimal quantum resource cannot be expressed in a closed form using elementary functions; instead, it is determined as the least real root of a degree-12 polynomial.

Method. The problem is formulated and solved via convex optimization (SDP), utilizing dual maximization techniques and analyzing necessary conditions derived from KKT multipliers.

Model / system. The system models multimode light using continuous variables, characterized by the quadrature covariance matrix V. The analysis focuses specifically on the two-mode case where modes are genuinely coupled.

Key observables. Quantum Advantage Resource ($N_q(V)$), $ ext{Tr}(V_{\star q})$, and the degree-12 polynomial $P(x; V)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The number of nonclassical directions ($\kappa$), which dictates whether a closed-form solution exists.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on Slater's condition (V must be strictly mixed) and proceeds by analyzing structured regimes ($\kappa=0, 1, 2$) before tackling the generic coupled case.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining the optimization problem for the quantum resource to solving it in specific low-dimensional sectors (e.g., $\kappa=1$), culminating in the derivation of a general polynomial equation whose root yields the solution for generic coupled states.

Abstract

We study the problem of extracting a quantum complexity resource from a mixed Gaussian state of the multimode light. We present the first complete, certificate-checked solution to this problem in a genuinely coupled sector. We carry this out for the two-mode case, the smallest case in which modes are genuinely coupled. Even in this case the solution is highly nontrivial, and we rigorously prove that it cannot be given in a closed form.

Summary. This theoretical paper analyzes Type-IIB axion-dilaton wormholes, distinguishing between stable BPS instantons and unstable non-BPS wormhole deformations. The core mathematical achievement is proving that the fluctuation spectrum around the BPS endpoint factorizes into a simple quadratic form of an operator $Q^\dagger Q$. This provides strong structural constraints on quantum gravity solutions.

Why it may be interesting. While highly specialized in quantum gravity, the rigorous mathematical techniques used—such as deriving physical operators by eliminating constrained degrees of freedom (Schur complement) and proving spectral properties via operator factorization—are analogous to methods used in effective field theories or open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper revisits Type-IIB axion--dilaton Euclidean saddles, comparing the BPS instanton ($E=0$) to non-BPS wormholes ($E>0$). It aims to provide a rigorous understanding of the spectrum and stability of these gravitational solutions.

Main result. The physical Hessian for the $E=0$ BPS instanton factorizes as $\mathcal{H} u = Q^\dagger u Q_ u$, establishing an endpoint theorem for the BPS sector. The analysis separates the wormhole throat structure from its long-distance multipole operator term.

Method. The methodology involves variational analysis of the Euclidean action, deriving radial equations, and applying constraint solving (Hamiltonian/gauge fixing) to reduce the quadratic fluctuation action to a physical Hessian operator.

Model / system. The system is Type IIB string theory compactified on geometries supporting axion-dilaton fields. The analysis covers both asymptotically flat 4D and AdS$_5$ backgrounds, treating wormholes as deformations of BPS instantons.

Key observables. The spectrum of the physical Hessian ($\mathcal{H}_ u$), which determines stability; the energy parameter $E$; and the charge sector invariants ($q_2$).

Important parameters / regimes. Energy parameter $E$ (determining BPS vs. non-BPS); neck radius $a_c$; and background curvature scales (e.g., AdS length scale).

Assumptions / limitations. The Hessian factorization theorem is strictly proven only for the physical domain corresponding to the $E=0$ BPS instanton, not necessarily for the full $E>0$ wormhole stability.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by establishing the general Euclidean action and radial equations. It then focuses on deriving the physical Hessian for the BPS endpoint ($E=0$) via constraint imposition, demonstrating its factorization. Finally, it compares this result to non-BPS wormholes and discusses dimensional reductions (4D vs AdS$_5$).

Abstract

I revisit Type-IIB axion--dilaton Euclidean saddles in a specified axion charge sector. In that sector, the solution with $E=0$ is the BPS instanton, while $E>0$ gives non-BPS wormholes with a smooth throat. The two cases solve the same radial equations but define different fluctuation problems. For the $E=0$ instanton, the Hamiltonian constraint, gauge quotient, charge-sector boundary condition, and removal of collective zero modes reduce the quadratic action to a physical Hessian. This Hessian factorizes, $ {\cal H}_ν={\mathcal Q}_ν^\dagger{\mathcal Q}_ν$. I interpret this as an endpoint theorem, beyond a stability theorem for the full $E>0$ wormhole. This puts Type IIB wormhole spectra on firmer grounds. I also separate the connected two-ended wormhole throat from its long-distance two-end multipole operator term. Once the coefficient matrix $C^{ij}$ is derived, the different-component and same-component placements of the two end insertions are terms in the same quadratic expression. Removing either term requires a genuine projection or cancellation.

Summary. This paper tackles the critical challenge of circuit scheduling on multi-core quantum processors by proposing a dependency-aware greedy algorithm. This method allows for fine-grained parallelism, significantly improving execution speed compared to simpler layer-based approaches. The results demonstrate that intelligent scheduling can drastically reduce makespan and better utilize hardware resources in these complex quantum systems.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical scheduling algorithms for quantum hardware, the analysis of communication bottlenecks (teleportation latency) and resource management in a distributed quantum setting is highly relevant to understanding physical constraints in large-scale quantum computation architectures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core problem is developing effective circuit scheduling algorithms for multi-core quantum systems to minimize the overall execution time (makespan).

Main result. Dependency-aware greedy scheduling achieves substantial performance gains, showing an average 40% reduction in makespan compared to baseline layered scheduling on real benchmarks.

Method. The authors propose a dependency-aware greedy scheduling strategy that schedules each gate at the earliest possible time based on resource and data dependencies, contrasting it with rigid layer-by-layer execution.

Model / system. The system modeled is a multi-core quantum computing architecture utilizing an n x n 2D mesh topology. Inter-core operations rely on quantum teleportation over a Network-on-Chip (NoC).

Key observables. Makespan ($ ext{T}{ ext{total}}$), Core Utilization, and Communication Overlap Ratio ($ ext{R}$).}

Important parameters / regimes. The communication delay is proportional to the number of hops ($H imes \lambda_{ ext{hop}}$), which is a critical bottleneck.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption addressed is that fine-grained parallelism can be exploited by overlapping computation and communication latency, overcoming limitations imposed by strict layered scheduling.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the multi-core architecture (NoC/Quantum Channel), compare makespans between layered vs. greedy scheduling using case studies, and show performance metrics across synthetic and real quantum circuits (QAOA, QFT).

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the physical model and the limitations of baseline layered scheduling. It then details the proposed dependency-aware greedy algorithm, followed by extensive evaluation on both synthetic random circuits and established real benchmarks to validate performance gains.

Abstract

Multi-core quantum computing architectures have emerged as a promising solution to the qubit scalability limitations of monolithic NISQ devices. Quantum algorithms are expressed as quantum circuits composed of single- and two-qubit gates. However, circuit scheduling in multi-core quantum systems remains largely unexplored. Reducing overall execution time (makespan), increasing core utilization, and hiding communication latency behind computation depends on effective scheduling. In this paper, we first introduce a layered scheduling approach as a baseline where quantum gates within the same layer are executed in parallel, while layers themselves are executed sequentially. We then propose a greedy scheduling strategy which schedules each gate as soon as all its dependencies and required resources are available. This allows fine-grained parallelism across cores. Our evaluation shows that on real benchmarks, greedy scheduling achieves an average 40% reduction in makespan and improvement in core utilization. The results suggest that the use of intelligent circuit scheduling to exploit parallelism can greatly enhance the speed of circuit execution in multi-core quantum architectures.

Summary. This work unifies the study of scattering and bound states for the 1D Dirac equation by mapping it onto a solvable Schrödinger-like problem using symmetry constraints. By applying Supersymmetric Quantum Mechanics to potentials like the Pöschl–Teller, the authors derive closed-form expressions for transmission probabilities. This framework allows them to systematically locate both discrete bound states and continuous resonances via pole analysis in the complex momentum plane.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous application of SUSYQM and its connection to spectral properties (bound vs. resonance) provides powerful, exact analytical tools for studying relativistic quantum systems, which is highly relevant for understanding particle confinement or wave propagation in structured media.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops a unified theoretical framework to analyze both scattering phenomena (continuum spectrum) and bound states/resonances (discrete spectrum) for the one-dimensional Dirac equation.

Main result. It successfully maps the relativistic problem onto an effective Schrödinger-like equation, allowing the use of supersymmetric quantum mechanics techniques to derive closed-form expressions for transmission probabilities and characterize resonance poles in the complex momentum plane.

Method. The core methods involve symmetry reduction (spin/pseudospin), utilizing Supersymmetric Quantum Mechanics (SUSYQM) with shape invariance, and employing analytic continuation to extract bound states from scattering amplitudes.

Model / system. The system is a fermion described by the one-dimensional Dirac equation subjected to combined scalar and vector potentials. The analysis focuses on specific solvable models like the hyperbolic Pöschl–Teller potential.

Key observables. Transmission probability $T(k)$, reflection amplitude, bound-state energies (poles of $T(k)$), and resonance frequencies/quasi-normal modes (QNMs) found as poles in the complex momentum plane.

Important parameters / regimes. The sign of the potential parameter $V_0$ determines whether the system behaves like an attractive well (bound states) or a repulsive barrier (resonances).

Assumptions / limitations. Key assumptions include the existence of spin-symmetry ($\Delta=0$) or pseudospin-symmetry ($\Sigma=0$) to reduce the coupled Dirac system, and assuming short-range interactions that vanish at infinity.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the energy dependence of scattering quantities, showing how $T(k)$ approaches unity in certain limits, and depict the pole structure in the complex momentum plane for resonance analysis.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by establishing the unified mapping from the Dirac equation to a Schrödinger-like problem. It then applies SUSYQM techniques using shape invariance to solve the Pöschl–Teller potential case, deriving scattering amplitudes, and finally analyzing the pole structure in the complex plane to distinguish between bound states and resonances.

Abstract

We develop a unified treatment of scattering and discrete spectra for the one-dimensional Dirac equation with scalar and vector interactions. Under the spin-symmetry condition, the coupled first-order Dirac system maps exactly onto an effective Sturm--Liouville (Schrö-din-ger-like) problem for a single spinor component. This mapping provides a convenient framework for analyzing transmission, reflection, and analytic continuation. As an explicit application, we consider effective interactions of hyperbolic Pöschl--Teller type and exploit supersymmetric quantum mechanics and shape invariance to obtain a closed-form expression for the transmission probability. The bound-state spectrum is then recovered from the poles of the analytically continued transmission amplitude, reproducing known results and offering a unified description of scattering and bound states. For the barrier configuration, we briefly comment on the resulting pole pattern in the complex momentum plane and its connection with resonance and quasi-normal-mode behavior. Moreover, we use the chiral transformation to relate the spin- and pseudospin-symmetry sectors and translate results between them without repeating the full derivation.

Summary. The authors experimentally study charge and spin dynamics in a double quantum dot subjected to nonequilibrium phonon irradiation. They observe that the system exhibits resonant tunneling at triplet resonance, and crucially, they demonstrate strong nonreciprocal electron transport cycles. This work establishes an accessible method for investigating directed current flow driven by energy gradients in nanoscale solid-state devices.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a clean experimental platform for studying non-equilibrium quantum transport and rectification effects driven by coupling to an external bath (phonons), which is highly relevant to open quantum systems theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates real-time charge and spin dynamics in excited states of a double quantum dot (DQD) driven by nonequilibrium phonons to understand emergent functionalities beyond equilibrium.

Main result. The research demonstrates that phonon irradiation induces resonant inter-dot tunneling at triplet resonance, leading to strong unidirectional electron transport cycles with high rectification ($\eta \approx 1$) when coupling is weak.

Method. Time-resolved charge sensing and waiting-time statistics are used to measure transition rates between different spin/charge states in the DQD under controlled phonon excitation.

Model / system. The physical system is a semiconductor Double Quantum Dot (DQD) fabricated on an AlGaAs/GaAs/AlGaAs heterostructure, which acts as the quantum device. Phonons are generated by applying a DC bias to a nearby source QD, creating a nonequilibrium phonon density gradient.

Key observables. Charge state transition time traces, inter-dot tunneling rates ($\Gamma_{ij}$), waiting-time histograms (single/double exponential), and the unidirectional transfer asymmetry ($\eta$).

Important parameters / regimes. Phonon bias voltage ($V_{PS}$), detuning voltage ($V_R$), and the relative strength of inter-dot coupling versus dot-reservoir rates.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on rate-equation models for DQD spin states, and some fitting procedures assume specific functional forms for waiting-time distributions.

Figures summary. Figures show charge stability diagrams revealing triplet resonance lines (Fig. 1c), time traces of transitions at both singlet and triplet resonances (Figs. 2a/b), histograms confirming double exponential behavior at ES, and finally, current measurements demonstrating unidirectional cycles with high rectification ($\eta$) (Fig. 4).

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the basic physics by observing resonant tunneling at GS vs. ES; it then analyzes detailed rate dynamics using waiting-time statistics to distinguish spin-conserving versus spin-flip processes; finally, it focuses on the nonreciprocal transport regime induced by phonon gradients.

Abstract

Driven nonequilibrium systems can host emergent functionalities beyond equilibrium, but real-time access to excited-state dynamics remains limited. Here we report real-time measurements of phonon-driven charge and spin dynamics in excited states of a double quantum dot. Under phonon irradiation, resonant inter-dot tunneling emerges at triplet resonance. Time-resolved charge sensing reveals that the resonant inter-dot tunneling is strongly modified by spin blockade. For weaker inter-dot coupling, the nonequilibrium phonon environment generates a unidirectional transport cycle along the phonon density gradient.
