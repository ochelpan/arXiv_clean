Summary. This work analyzes slow heat conduction in hard disk gases by coupling hydrodynamic theory with molecular dynamics simulations. The authors successfully extend the theoretical framework beyond the ideal gas limit to account for finite density effects using an appropriate equation of state. The strong agreement between theory and simulation validates the use of isobaric hydrodynamics for strongly inhomogeneous, low-Mach number cooling flows.

Why it may be interesting. Although focused on classical fluid dynamics, the rigorous comparison between continuum hydrodynamics (derived from statistical principles) and microscopic particle simulations provides a powerful benchmark for developing non-equilibrium transport theories applicable to quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates slow, heat-driven non-equilibrium flow in a gas of hard disks confined to a channel under nearly isobaric conditions.

Main result. The hydrodynamic theory, extended to finite densities using a hard-disk equation of state, shows excellent agreement with event-driven molecular dynamics simulations across both dilute and dense regimes.

Method. The authors solve coupled one-dimensional partial differential equations derived from the low-Mach number isobaric heat-conduction formalism and compare these solutions against particle-level Molecular Dynamics (MD) simulations.

Model / system. The system is a gas of elastically colliding hard disks confined to a long channel, modeled using hydrodynamic equations that incorporate non-ideal equation of state corrections for finite densities.

Key observables. Temperature T(x, t), Density $\rho(x, t)$, Velocity $v(x, t)$, and Pressure $p(x, t)$.

Important parameters / regimes. Low Mach number regime; initial conditions featuring large temperature/density contrasts but nearly equal pressures ($p \approx ext{const}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The flow is assumed to be low-Mach number (neglecting acoustic dynamics), and the theory extends from an ideal gas limit to finite densities using a specific equation of state.

Figures summary. Figures show self-similar scaling collapse of density, velocity, and temperature profiles when comparing MD data to hydrodynamic solutions; pressure plots confirm the system remains nearly isobaric over time.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the ideal gas limit theory, then extends it by incorporating a non-ideal equation of state for finite densities. The resulting 1D equations are solved numerically and validated against event-driven molecular dynamics simulations.

Abstract

We study a slow heat-driven flow in a gas of elastically colliding hard disks confined to a long channel. The initial state consists of two regions with large temperature and density contrasts but nearly equal pressures, leading to a low-Mach-number, nearly isobaric evolution. In the dilute limit, the corresponding isobaric hydrodynamic theory reduces to a previously known ideal-gas description. We extend this theory to finite densities by incorporating a non-ideal equation of state of a hard-disk fluid, and solve the resulting one-dimensional equations numerically. Finite-density effects produce appreciable deviations from the ideal-gas prediction. We then test the theory directly against event-driven molecular dynamics simulations of hard disks and find very good agreement in both the dilute and finite-density regimes. The results provide, to our knowledge, the first particle-level test of isobaric gas dynamics of a strongly inhomogeneous cooling flow.

Summary. The authors resolve a conflict between experimental measurements and Bohmian mechanics concerning particle speeds in classically forbidden regions. They show that naive estimates based on tunneling time or simple dwell times are incorrect because they neglect entanglement effects between spatial dimensions. By correctly applying the Büttiker dwell time formula, they demonstrate quantitative agreement with Bohmian predictions.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses fundamental conceptual issues regarding quantum measurement, non-equilibrium dynamics, and the interpretation of wave function properties (like speed) in regimes where classical intuition breaks down, which is central to AMO physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper resolves discrepancies between particle speeds inferred from experiments (Sharoglazova et al.) and predictions from Bohmian mechanics when a quantum particle tunnels through a classically forbidden region.

Main result. The discrepancy arises because naive speed estimates fail to account for entanglement between longitudinal and transverse degrees of freedom in the evanescent regime. A corrected application of the Büttiker dwell time formula yields results that perfectly agree with Bohmian predictions.

Method. Theoretical analysis involving detailed calculations of Bohmian trajectories, average speeds, and dwell times using a two-dimensional waveguide model derived from the Schrödinger equation.

Model / system. A non-relativistic quantum particle modeled in coupled waveguides realizing motion in a 2D potential landscape with a barrier/potential step. The dynamics are governed by the time-dependent Schrödinger equation.

Key observables. Bohmian velocity, average speed ($ar{v}_B$), tunneling time ($T_{tun}$), and dwell time ($ au_d$).

Important parameters / regimes. Evanescent regime (exponential decay), coupling constants ($\mu, ar{\mu}$), energy splitting ($J_0$), and wave packet width ($\sigma_k$).

Assumptions / limitations. The core assumption challenged is that transverse tunneling time is independent of the longitudinal degree of freedom's entanglement effects in the evanescent regime.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate Bohmian trajectories (parabolic paths) and compare density distributions, showing differences between propagative and evanescent regimes, particularly regarding wave function factorization.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first identifying the conflict with experimental results, then developing theoretical tools (Bohmian mechanics, dwell time corrections), and finally resolving the puzzle by analyzing how entanglement invalidates simple tunneling time assumptions in the forbidden region.

Abstract

Recently, Sharoglazova et al. [Nature 643, 67 (2025)] proposed a procedure for determining the speed of a quantum particle in the classically forbidden region of a potential step, and implemented it in a beautiful experiment. The inferred speeds disagree significantly with the Bohmian velocities, which the authors presented as an experimental challenge to Bohmian mechanics. This is puzzling because the speeds are inferred from particle populations in coupled waveguides for which Bohmian mechanics and standard quantum mechanics make identical predictions. We resolve the puzzle by a detailed theoretical analysis of the experimental setup. We show that the speed inference rests on an assumption that fails in the relevant (evanescent) regime according to both Bohmian mechanics and standard quantum mechanics -- namely that the inter-waveguide tunneling time is set by the transverse coupling and is not affected by entanglement with the longitudinal degree of freedom. We also consider a second speed estimate suggested by Sharoglazova et al., which is based on the Büttiker dwell time formula for particles in the forbidden region. We show that the authors applied the formula incorrectly, and that a correct application yields exact agreement with the predictions of Bohmian mechanics. Our analysis includes explicit calculations of Bohmian trajectories, dwell times, and longitudinal speeds in the two-dimensional waveguide model of the experiment.

Leggett-Garg inequality in the massive scalar vacuum: No violation under spacelike-separated measurements

Authors: Yang Xiang
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.00314v1

Summary. This theoretical paper investigates whether the vacuum state of a massive scalar field violates the Leggett-Garg inequality (LGI) when measurements are strictly spacelike separated. By employing a protocol that rigorously satisfies noninvasive measurability, the authors calculate the LGI parameter $K_3$ and find it remains below the classical bound of 1. This result highlights a fundamental distinction between spatial entanglement violations (Bell tests) and temporal macrorealism in relativistic quantum fields.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous methodological benchmark by successfully adapting Bell/LGI tests to QFT while satisfying causality constraints. The contrast between violating spatial Bell inequalities and preserving temporal macrorealism is highly relevant for understanding quantum foundations in relativistic settings.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to test for macrorealism using the Leggett-Garg inequality (LGI) in a relativistic quantum field theory setting, specifically by ensuring noninvasive measurability (NIM) through spacelike separation.

Main result. Under strict spacelike separation conditions, the vacuum state of a free massive scalar field does not violate the LGI ($K_3 < 1$), establishing that macrorealism is preserved for Gaussian fields in this context.

Method. The authors use a protocol involving three independent ensembles measured at spacelike-separated events to calculate the LGI parameter $K_3$ from two-time correlation functions derived using the Heisenberg picture.

Model / system. The system modeled is a free massive scalar field ($\phi$) in 1+1 dimensions. The test utilizes dichotomic observables defined by the sign of smeared field values, $ ext{sign}(\phi(f))$.

Key observables. The primary diagnostic is the LGI parameter $K_3 = 2C( au, L) - C(2 au, 2L)$, derived from two-time correlation functions $C( au, L)$.

Important parameters / regimes. Mass ($m$), time separation ($ au$), and spatial separation ($L$). The condition $L > au + 2 au_0$ ensures spacelike separation.

Assumptions / limitations. The protocol strictly satisfies the Noninvasive Measurability (NIM) condition by ensuring all measurement events are spacelike separated. Calculations rely on the Gaussian nature of the vacuum state for free fields.

Figures summary. Figure 1 plots $K_3( au, L)$ versus $ au$, showing that $K_3 < 1$ and demonstrating exponential decay for large time separations ($ au \ge 2$).

Paper structure. The paper establishes the theoretical framework by defining the LGI test in QFT with NIM. It then calculates the necessary two-time correlation functions using techniques appropriate for spacelike separation, culminating in a numerical demonstration that $K_3$ remains below the classical bound of 1.

Abstract

We overcome the long-standing noninvasive measurability (NIM) challenge in Leggett-Garg tests by exploiting the causal structure of quantum field theory (QFT). Our protocol uses three independent ensembles of the vacuum state, each measured by a different pair of observers at spacelike-separated events, yielding the three two-time correlators. By placing these events at positions $(0,0)$, $(τ,L)$, and $(2τ,2L)$ with $L>τ+2τ_0$, we rigorously ensure that no measurement can influence another. We investigate the vacuum state of a free massive scalar field in 1+1 dimensions, employing the dichotomic observable $Q(f)=\operatorname{sign}(φ(f))$ where $φ(f)$ is the smeared field. In the Heisenberg picture, the time evolution is absorbed into a translation of the time-window function, allowing us to derive the two-time correlation function $C(τ,L)$ and the Leggett-Garg parameter $K_3=2C(τ,L)-C(2τ,2L)$. For non-overlapping time windows, we find that the correlation function decays exponentially with $τ$ for a massive field. For overlapping windows, our numerical computation for a rectangular time window yields $K_3<1$ across the entire mass range, firmly establishing that the vacuum does not violate the LGI. Thus, under strict noninvasive conditions, the vacuum shows no violation of macrorealism, in stark contrast to its well-known violation of spatial Bell inequalities. Our spacelike-separated protocol provides the first LGI test in QFT with rigorously satisfied NIM, setting a methodological benchmark for future studies and highlighting the fundamental distinction between spacelike entanglement and temporal macrorealism in relativistic quantum fields.

Summary. The authors propose and derive exact interlayer triplet-pairing eigenstates for the multi-layer extended Hubbard model. These unique states only exist in bilayer and trilayer systems due to specific constraints on the Hamiltonian's algebra. The study confirms these states via time evolution simulations, highlighting a coexistence of singlet and triplet pairing mechanisms.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous analytical framework for finding exotic, long-range ordered states in strongly correlated lattice models, which is highly relevant for understanding high-$T_c$ superconductivity mechanisms beyond simple mean-field treatments.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The work investigates the existence and properties of exact interlayer triplet-pairing eigenstates within the multi-layer extended Hubbard model, extending prior knowledge on $\eta$-pairing states.

Main result. Exact condensate-pair eigenstates exhibiting off-diagonal long-range order are found for bilayer and trilayer systems when interlayer interactions are present. These states demonstrate a unique pairing mechanism distinct from the standard $\eta$-pairing symmetry.

Method. The analysis relies on constructing these exact eigenstates by analyzing commutation relations involving the Hamiltonian and specialized triplet-pairing operators, utilizing a Restricted Spectrum Generating Algebra (RSGA).

Model / system. The system is the multi-layer extended Hubbard model defined on a 2D square lattice with parameters including intralayer hopping ($t$), interlayer hopping ($t_\perp$), on-site repulsion ($U$), and interlayer interaction ($V$). The Hamiltonian incorporates both singlet ($\eta$) and triplet ($\zeta$) pairing channels.

Key observables. Off-diagonal long-range order (ODLRO), fidelity $F(t)$ under time evolution, and correlation functions for singlet ($C_S$) and triplet ($C_T$) pairs.

Important parameters / regimes. The existence of exact states is critically dependent on the number of layers ($\lambda=2$ or $\lambda=3$) and the presence of interlayer interactions ($V \neq 0$).

Assumptions / limitations. The derivation requires restricting Hamiltonian parameters to satisfy the conditions necessary for applying the Restricted Spectrum Generating Algebra (RSGA).

Figures summary. Figure 2 illustrates that the fidelity $F(t)$ remains unity for $\lambda=2, 3$ when $V \neq 0$, while decaying for $\lambda=4$. Figure 3 shows correlation functions decaying when interlayer interactions are present.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the model and operators, derives the conditions for exact eigenstates using RSGA, proves their existence only for bilayer/trilayer systems, and finally uses quench dynamics simulations (fidelity analysis) to confirm these theoretical findings.

Abstract

$η$-pairing symmetry generalizes the pairing mechanisms in superconductivity but is broken in the presence of interlayer interactions. In this work, we extend this approach to triplet pairs. We propose interlayer triplet-pairing operators for the multi-layer extended Hubbard model. We find that a set of exact condensate-pair eigenstates can be constructed, which exhibit off-diagonal long-range order. In contrast to the $η$-pairing mechanism, this originates from restricted spectrum generating algebra and is only available for bilayer and trilayer systems in the presence of interlayer Hubbard interactions. Nevertheless, the system also retains the original on-site $η$-pairing symmetry in the absence of interlayer interactions. Consequently, both singlet and triplet pairs coexist in the eigenstates of the multi-layer Hubbard model. We employ quench dynamics to demonstrate the results through numerical simulations. Our findings open avenues for the study of exact condensate-pair states in strongly correlated systems.

Abstract

We find that the landscape of nonlocality paradoxes is far richer than previously understood, violating regularity conditions underlying all prior constructions.

Summary. Main result. Achieving optimal performance requires enforcing cyclic equivariance across all four architectural interfaces—encoding, dynamics, measurement, and readout—a concept termed 'observable-orbit completion'.

Model / system. The study uses QRC applied to cyclic time-series forecasting tasks (e.g., weather data on a latitude circle) implemented via quantum circuits, tested on spin-ring models and real-world datasets like WeatherBench 2.

Abstract

Symmetry is a powerful inductive bias, but in quantum reservoir computing (QRC) it cannot be imposed only by making the reservoir symmetric. QRC maps inputs through fixed quantum dynamics into nonlinear expectation-value features and trains only a classical readout, so the relevant symmetry must be visible in the measured feature map. We study cyclic forecasting tasks, such as sensors around a turbine or weather stations along a latitude circle, where the same local pattern should be forecast by the same rule wherever it appears on the ring. Thus, rotating the input by one site should rotate, not change, the predicted field. We show that a symmetric Hamiltonian is not enough: even large Pauli measurement sets can fail if their channels do not match the data symmetry, since optimization cannot recover channels that were never measured. We address this through observable-orbit completion, which measures symmetry-related observable channels and aligns encoding, dynamics, measurement, and readout. The strongest gains arise from aligning all four interfaces together, with matched spin-ring, real-weather, and IBM hardware checks supporting the same measured-span mechanism.

Abstract

These results show that rigorous validation of complete noise architectures is indispensable for accurately predicting operational thresholds in future quantum technologies.

Abstract

Abstract

Current research on the disordered topological quantum phases primarily focuses on the uncorrelated and short-range correlated disorder regime. These topological Anderson insulators are typically self-averaging. However, topological quantum systems with long-range correlated disorder have received limited attention due to the absence of tractable analytical methods. In fact, the long-range correlated disorder introduces more complex effects on topological quantum states. Here, we demonstrate that the long-range correlated disorder could induce anomalously statistical feature where the topological Anderson states become non-self-averaging, and the phase diagram is strongly dependent on individual disorder configurations. We term this statistical phase the non-self-averaging topological Anderson insulator. The non-self-averaging property is identified by the non-vanishing finite values of the relative variance of the Lyapunov exponent in the thermodynamic limit, alongside the non-Gaussian distributions of the Lyapunov exponent. Consequently, the topological properties of a single disordered sample deviate from the ensemble average, causing a breakdown of the central limit theorem. The non-self-averaging topological Anderson insulator provides insights into the interplay between correlated disorder and topology.
