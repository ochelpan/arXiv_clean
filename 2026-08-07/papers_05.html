Resource Estimation for Fault-Tolerant Quantum Programs

Authors: Bonan Su, Yuan Feng, Li Zhou, Mingsheng Ying
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.04573v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: methods for driven-dissipative 1/5

Summary. This paper addresses the critical challenge of accurately estimating resources for fault-tolerant quantum algorithms. The authors propose a novel programming language and a cross-layer framework that allows programmers to explicitly manage error correction while enabling joint analysis of resource trade-offs. This results in significantly more accurate and detailed resource estimates compared to existing black-box methods.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on quantum computation theory, the systematic approach to resource accounting across multiple abstraction layers is highly relevant for developing robust simulation tools or understanding hardware overheads in any complex physical system modeled by layered control structures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Resource estimation for fault-tolerant quantum computation is difficult because existing tools either force programmers to handle low-level hardware details or abstract away error correction schemes, leading to inaccurate resource utilization estimates.

Main result. The proposed framework enables substantial resource savings and provides detailed, fine-grained, and accurate resource estimates by jointly analyzing the logical program and its associated error-correction schemes.

Method. The authors introduce a specialized quantum programming language and a cross-layer resource-estimation framework that allows programmers to define QEC schemes explicitly while enabling joint analysis across different abstraction layers.

Model / system. The work focuses on Fault-Tolerant Quantum Computation (FTQC), where logical qubits are encoded using physical qubits via Quantum Error-Correction Schemes ($\langle ext{QEC} angle$). The framework models the compilation and execution flow from high-level logic down to hardware configuration.

Key observables. Space cost (# total physical qubits), Time cost (total runtime / syndrome-extraction cycles), and error rates (e.g., activate-operation vs. idle memory error).

Important parameters / regimes. Code distances ($d_{data}, d_{anc}$), the underlying physical error rate ($p_{phy} = 3 imes 10^{-3}$), and mean decoding latency.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis requires a joint, cross-layer analysis of the program and QEC specifications, which is more complex than independent estimation methods. It also assumes that decoding latency can be utilized by inserting intermediate statements.

Figures summary. Figure 2 illustrates the overall architecture of the resource estimation framework across multiple layers (Logical $ ightarrow$ QEC Specification $ ightarrow$ Hardware). Other figures show code examples for logical gates like $T$-gates.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem limitations, presents the novel programming language and cross-layer framework, details the joint analysis methodology, evaluates it on practical large-scale algorithms, and concludes with resource savings demonstrations.

Abstract

Fault-tolerant quantum computation enables the deployment of practical quantum algorithms but incurs substantial overhead from error correction, making resource estimation a central concern. Beyond case-by-case analyses, existing quantum programming languages either require programmers to manipulate low-level hardware details, rendering fault-tolerant implementations cumbersome, or abstract away the underlying error-correction schemes, reducing the effectiveness of resource utilization and estimation. To address these limitations while preserving programmability, we present a quantum programming language that enables efficient resource utilization, together with a resource-estimation framework for comprehensive resource analysis. Our framework features programmer-visible abstractions of error-correction schemes and cross-layer program-hardware analysis, allowing systematic exploration of resource trade-offs. We evaluate our approach on detailed fault-tolerant implementations of practical large-scale quantum algorithms, including components typically treated as black boxes in existing frameworks. The results demonstrate that our framework enables substantial resource savings while delivering detailed, fine-grained, and accurate resource estimates for fault-tolerant quantum programs.

Zero-Clustering Geometry in Realistic Fractional Quantum Hall Wave Functions

Authors: Xin Wan, Ziang Wang, Zi-Xiang Hu, Zhao Liu
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.04395v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work develops the zero displacement ratio ($\zeta$) as a quantitative diagnostic tool to map out phase transitions in Fractional Quantum Hall systems by analyzing the geometry of wave function zeros. By comparing results from Laughlin models against realistic Coulomb interactions, the authors show that the zero structure collapses or shifts predictably when topological order is lost. The findings strongly support Composite Fermion descriptions over simpler crystal approximations for describing these correlated electron liquids.

Why it may be interesting. The use of geometric properties (zero clustering) to diagnose topological order is a powerful, non-local probe that complements standard energy gap or correlation function measurements. This methodology could be adapted to study other exotic quantum phases where local observables are insufficient.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to quantify how deviations from ideal model wave functions affect the topological properties of Fractional Quantum Hall (FQH) systems, particularly during phase transitions.

Main result. The zero displacement ratio ($\zeta$) serves as a diagnostic tool: its statistical behavior reveals the transition from Laughlin liquid order to charge-ordered or crystalline phases. Furthermore, Composite Fermion Fluid descriptions provide an improved description of $ u=1/5$ states compared to simple crystal embeddings.

Method. The analysis relies on calculating and analyzing the geometric distribution (density maps) of zeros in the ground state wave functions using a zero displacement ratio ($\zeta$) derived from nearest neighbor zeros. Overlap amplitudes are also used to compare different theoretical approximations.

Model / system. The system is strongly interacting electrons confined to the Lowest Landau Level, modeled at fractional fillings like $ u=1/3$ and $ u=1/5$. The Hamiltonian is varied by mixing terms between simple pseudopotentials ($V_1$) and full Coulomb interactions ($H_C$).

Key observables. Zero displacement ratio ($\zeta$), density distribution $P(\phi)$ of the zero argument, Kurtosis ($K$) of this distribution, Overlap Amplitude ($O$), and Pair Correlation Function ($g(r, R)$).

Important parameters / regimes. Filling fractions $ u=1/3$ and $ u=1/5$; mixing parameters ($\lambda$ or $\eta$); the ratio defining $\zeta$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis often relies on studying specific small-N systems ($N=3$) to extract general scaling laws, and approximations are made when constructing Composite Fermion states.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the evolution of zero patterns (density maps) as interaction strength changes, showing shifts in $\zeta$ peaks. Other figures track order parameters like $P(\phi)$ and overlap amplitudes ($O$) versus tuning parameters ($\lambda$ or $\eta$).

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the geometry of zeros for different FQH states ($ u=1/3$, $ u=1/5$), introducing the zero displacement ratio as a diagnostic. It then compares results from idealized Laughlin models against realistic Coulomb interactions, culminating in the development and testing of Composite Fermion Fluid descriptions.

Abstract

The clustering pattern of zeros in the ground state of a fractional quantum Hall system is a defining feature of its topological properties. We analyze the geometrical fluctuations of the zeros around individual electrons and propose to use the displacement ratio of the zeros to visualize and measure the distance of a realistic state to a model wave function. The distribution of the zero displacement ratio behaves like an order parameter in the transition from a Laughlin phase to a topologically trivial one. The statistical comparison between quantum Hall states belonging to different Jain sequences leads to a composite fermion fluid description of the $ν= 1/5$ ground state with long-range Coulomb interaction that agrees almost perfectly for as few as $3$-$5$ electrons, overcoming the long-standing difficulties of accommodating the competing liquid and crystal orders at short distances.

Dynamical Lie Algebras Cannot Describe Shallow QAOA: Cragged Terrains, Barren Plateaus, and Empirical Hardness Models

Authors: Harrison Copp, Charlton Li, Anžej Margeta-Cacace, Amy Qiao
Type: both · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.04252v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work investigates the loss landscape of QAOA for MIS, finding that established theory based on Dynamical Lie Algebras fails in shallow circuits. Instead of barren plateaus, the landscapes exhibit 'cragged terrains' with polynomially increasing variance. The findings emphasize that empirical, data-driven models are necessary to characterize VQA hardness accurately.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a critical, empirically grounded challenge to highly abstract theoretical frameworks (like DLA) used to predict VQA performance, suggesting that practical limitations (shallow depth) invalidate deep mathematical assumptions.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To determine if theoretical predictions based on Dynamical Lie Algebras (DLA) accurately describe the loss landscape scaling of shallow Variational Quantum Algorithms (VQAs), specifically for QAOA applied to MIS.

Main result. The DLA theory fails in the shallow-circuit regime, as empirical studies show that 'cragged terrains' (polynomial variance increase) are common, contradicting the predicted exponential vanishing variances associated with 2-design assumptions.

Method. A combination of large-scale numerical simulations across thousands of graph instances and training Empirical Hardness Models (EHMs) to predict instance-wise landscape metrics.

Model / system. The study focuses on VQAs, particularly QAOA for the Maximum Independent Set (MIS) problem, using parametrized quantum circuits acting on an N-qubit Hilbert space. The theoretical framework involves analyzing the circuit ensemble's adherence to a 2-design property derived from DLA theory.

Key observables. Loss function variance ($ ext{Var}{ heta}[\partial\mu C( heta)]$), Barren Plateau (BP) vs. Cragged Terrain scaling, and model prediction accuracy ($R^2$).

Important parameters / regimes. Circuit depth (shallow regime), system size ($N$), graph structure (random vs. vertex-transitive).

Assumptions / limitations. The DLA theory assumes the circuit ensemble forms an approximate 2-design, which is shown to be insufficient for shallow circuits; EHMs are trained on finite subsets of data.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate GCNN predictions for gradient variance across different scaling regimes (BP vs. cragged terrains) using metrics like von Neumann entropy and edge density.

Paper structure. The paper contrasts the theoretical DLA prediction (based on 2-design theory) with empirical results from QAOA-MIS, demonstrating where the theory breaks down in shallow circuits. It concludes by validating the utility of data-driven EHMs over asymptotic unitary design theories for this regime.

Abstract

The dynamical Lie algebraic (DLA) theory of variational quantum algorithms (VQAs) predicts commonplace exponentially vanishing loss and gradient variances for sufficiently deep parametrized circuits. In this work, we show that these predictions fail dramatically in the shallow-circuit (and particularly constant-depth) regime for the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) applied to the maximum independent set (MIS) problem. In a large-scale numerical study across $\sim$23,000 problem instances, we find that barren plateaus are rare, while landscapes whose variances polynomially increase with system size---which we term "cragged terrains"---are common across graph families. This aggregate polynomial growth persists both for generic, low-symmetry random graphs and for highly symmetric vertex-transitive graphs, indicating that DLA-based variance predictions do not describe landscape scaling in this regime. As a stopgap alternative to the theory, we train empirical hardness models to predict instance-wise hardness metrics for QAOA-MIS. While these models generalize poorly, they nonetheless recover the correct landscape scaling class (barren plateau vs. cragged terrain) with high fidelity. Taken together, our results identify shallow QAOA for MIS as a prototypical setting in which asymptotic, unitary-design-centric predictions may be fundamentally insufficient to describe shallow variational quantum algorithms more broadly, emphasizing the need for more empirically-informed models of VQA loss landscapes.

Efficient Depth--Ancilla Tradeoffs for Hamming Weight Computation and Symmetric Boolean Functions

Authors: Wei Zi, Pei Yuan, Junhong Nie, Shengyu Zhang
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.04627v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper analyzes the resource trade-offs (depth vs. ancillas) for computing fundamental functions like Hamming weight in quantum circuits. By comparing standard and dynamic models under different connectivity constraints, they show how constant depth can be achieved using a polynomial number of ancillary qubits. This work is vital for understanding the practical limits of implementing complex quantum algorithms.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on abstract quantum circuits, achieving constant depth with polynomial ancillary overhead is crucial for designing scalable quantum algorithms that can run efficiently on near-term hardware architectures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the optimal trade-offs between circuit depth and the number of ancillary qubits required to compute Hamming weight and general symmetric Boolean functions.

Main result. They establish that in dynamic quantum circuits, constant depth can be achieved for these computations, although this requires an ancillary qubit count scaling as $O(n^{1+\epsilon} ext{polylog } n)$.

Method. The authors employ circuit construction techniques by reducing the problem to weighted counting and applying analysis across different connectivity models (all-to-all vs. 2D) and circuit types (standard vs. dynamic).

Model / system. The study is purely theoretical, analyzing quantum circuits designed for specific computational tasks on abstract qubit architectures constrained by connectivity (all-to-all or 2D nearest-neighbor grid).

Key observables. Circuit Depth ($D$) and Ancilla Count ($ ext{Ancilla}$), relative to the input size $n$.

Important parameters / regimes. Connectivity model (All-to-all, 2D); Circuit Model (Standard vs. Dynamic); Input Size ($n$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis distinguishes between output register space and ancillary workspace; dynamic models rely on measurement-based fan-out and cat-state recovery.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the high-level construction: a reduction to weighted counting, the dynamic weighted-counting primitive, and its implementation within a two-dimensional patch geometry.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the depth-ancilla tradeoffs for Hamming weight computation first, then generalizes these results to arbitrary symmetric Boolean functions across four settings (standard/dynamic $ imes$ all-to-all/2D).

Abstract

Hamming weight computation maps an $n$-bit input to the number of ones it contains. It is a basic subroutine in quantum computing, and the core building block for symmetric Boolean functions, whose value depends only on the Hamming weight of the input. Moreover, symmetric Boolean functions are among the most common primitives in quantum computing. Efficient circuits for both problems are therefore important for the efficiency of many quantum algorithms. We study the depth-ancilla tradeoffs of Hamming weight computation under two qubit connectivity models, all-to-all and two-dimensional nearest-neighbor square grid (2D), in both the standard and dynamic circuit models. In the standard all-to-all model, we obtain depth $O(\log n)$ with a sublinear number of ancillas. In the standard 2D model, we give a circuit of depth $O(\sqrt n)$ with $O(\log^2 n)$ ancillas, and a matching lower bound showing that $Θ(\sqrt n)$ is optimal. In both dynamic models, we obtain constant-depth circuits with $O(n^{1+\varepsilon}\operatorname{polylog}\,n)$ ancillary qubits for every fixed $\varepsilon>0$. All constructions give a smooth depth-ancilla tradeoff, and they also extend to arbitrary symmetric Boolean functions.

Impact of molecular orbital localization on quantum computational resources for Hamiltonian simulation: A benchmark study of hydrogen chain systems

Authors: Kenji Sugisaki, Yuhei Tachi, Masayoshi Terabe, Hiroyuki Tezuka
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.04481v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This study benchmarks Hamiltonian simulation resources for hydrogen chains using different molecular orbital bases and truncation methods. It finds that employing localized orbitals (LMOs) with locality-based truncation yields a dramatically better scaling of quantum gate requirements—polylogarithmic versus polynomial—compared to canonical molecular orbitals (CMOs). This provides critical guidance for designing efficient quantum algorithms for large-scale quantum chemistry.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses resource estimation in quantum chemistry simulations, providing concrete guidelines on how basis set choice (localization) can fundamentally change the complexity scaling from polynomial to polylogarithmic, which is crucial for feasibility studies.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how the choice of molecular orbital basis set (CMO vs. LMO) and Hamiltonian truncation strategy impacts the required quantum resources for simulating electronic structure via Trotter decomposition.

Main result. The analysis shows an exponential advantage for using localized orbitals (LMOs) combined with locality-based Hamiltonian truncation, leading to a polylogarithmic growth in gate count compared to the polynomial growth seen with canonical molecular orbitals (CMOs).

Method. The authors compare quantum resource estimation (quantum gate counts) derived from simulating hydrogen chains using first-order Trotter decomposition across different basis sets and truncation methods.

Model / system. The model is one-dimensional hydrogen chain systems ($ ext{H}_n$), analyzed by constructing a qubit Hamiltonian via the Jordan–Wigner transformation. The core comparison involves two orbital bases: Hartree–Fock canonical molecular orbitals (CMOs) and Pipek–Mezey localized molecular orbitals (LMOs).

Key observables. Number of quantum gates ($\# ext{Clifford}(1q)$, $\# ext{CNOT}$, $\# ext{Rz}$), ground-state energy, and fidelity ($F$).

Important parameters / regimes. System size (up to $ ext{H}{100}$), truncation thresholds ($ ext{c}$ or $k_{\max}$), and the bond dimension ($D$) for related DMRG convergence checks.}

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on first-order Trotter decomposition, and comparisons are made between truncated and full Hamiltonians to estimate resource scaling.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the procedure of orbital localization (for $ ext{H}{10}$) and show the distribution of rotational angles ($ ext{Rz}( heta)$ gates) for larger chains ($ ext{H}$), comparing original vs. truncated Hamiltonians.

Paper structure. The paper systematically compares computational costs by first analyzing short chains (ground-state energy/fidelity) to determine optimal truncation thresholds, and then extrapolating these findings to estimate the scaling of quantum gate resources for very long chains ($ ext{H}_{100}$).

Abstract

We investigate how molecular orbitals used as the basis of wave function expansion and how operator coefficient-based and locality-based Hamiltonian truncation affects the computational cost of Trotter decomposition-based Hamiltonian simulation in one-dimensional hydrogen chain systems. The analysis is performed using both Hartree--Fock canonical molecular orbitals (CMOs) and Pipek--Mezey-based localized molecular orbitals (LMOs). For short hydrogen chains, we evaluate the ground-state energy and fidelity and find that, in the CMO-based wave function expansion, introducing a threshold on Hamiltonian coefficients is effective in reducing the gate cost while maintaining computational accuracy. In contrast, in the LMO-based wave function expansion, operator locality-based Hamiltonian truncation is found to be more effective. By fitting the relationship between the truncation threshold and the ground-state energies and fidelities with empirical formulas, we estimate the threshold values required to achieve high fidelity ($F \ge 0.99$) in the ground-state wave function. Using the estimated thresholds, we then perform quantum gate resource estimation for longer hydrogen chains up to H$_{100}$. The results suggest an exponential advantage of the LMO-based wave function expansion with Hamiltonian truncation: the number of quantum gates required for Hamiltonian simulation grows polynomially when the CMO-based wave function expansion with operator coefficient-based Hamiltonian truncation is adopted, whereas it grows polylogarithmically when the LMO-based wave function expansion is combined with operator locality-based Hamiltonian truncation. These results provide useful guidelines for choosing orbital representations and Hamiltonian truncation strategies in large-scale quantum chemical simulations.

RKKY interaction as a probe of spin splitting and odd-parity nature in Floquet collinear magnets

Authors: Hou-Jian Duan, Yong-Jia Wu, Xiaoliang Xiao, Ming-Xun Deng, Mou Yang, Rui-Qiang Wang
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.04969v1

Summary. The paper demonstrates that the Ruderman-Kittel-Kasuya-Yosida (RKKY) interaction can act as a universal probe for characterizing exotic, odd-parity magnets generated in antiferromagnets using Floquet engineering. By analyzing distinct magnetic signatures within the RKKY coupling—such as specific sign changes and symmetry constraints on the DM term—the authors provide a clear theoretical pathway to experimentally verify these complex quantum states.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, symmetry-based theoretical diagnostic (RKKY) that links abstract concepts like odd-parity spin polarization and Floquet engineering to measurable magnetic responses accessible via techniques like STM spectroscopy.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is developing a method to detect both spin splitting and confirm the odd-parity nature of exotic magnets predicted to emerge in collinear antiferromagnets via Floquet engineering.

Main result. The RKKY interaction serves as a unified magnetic probe, yielding distinct signatures like magnetism reversals in Heisenberg/Ising terms and specific symmetries in the DM term that allow clear discrimination of these novel odd-parity magnets.

Method. The study utilizes theoretical calculations based on the Ruderman-Kittel-Kasuya-Yosida (RKKY) interaction, which is derived using Green's functions applied to effective Floquet Hamiltonians.

Model / system. The model focuses on collinear $f$-wave magnets realized in 2D collinear antiferromagnets on a honeycomb lattice, compared against baseline systems like graphene and standard AFMs. The physics is governed by an effective low-energy Floquet Hamiltonian.

Key observables. Distinct magnetic signals from the RKKY interaction: magnetism reversal in Heisenberg/Ising terms, sign alternation of the DM term, and confirmation of odd-parity symmetry ($\mathcal{J}^{ ext{DM}}(\mathbf{R}) = -\mathcal{J}^{ ext{DM}}(C_2^q\mathbf{R})$).

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis is performed in the high-frequency off-resonant regime of Floquet theory, and the results are shown to be general for $p$-wave magnets ($q=1$).

Assumptions / limitations. The approach assumes that the RKKY interaction can effectively probe the underlying spin polarization features generated by the Floquet driving.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates band dispersions and Fermi surfaces for various magnetic systems (graphene, AFM, f-wave magnet). Figure 2 compares the calculated RKKY components ($J^H, J^I, J^{DM}$) versus impurity distance $R$ for different material types.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the theoretical framework using Floquet engineering to generate odd-parity magnets. It then introduces and applies the RKKY interaction as a diagnostic tool, systematically comparing its calculated components across various magnetic models to isolate the unique signatures of the target f-wave magnet.

Abstract

Odd-parity magnets were recently proposed to emerge in collinear antiferromagnets (AFMs) via Floquet engineering, with valley-dependent spin splitting underlying the odd-parity spin polarization. This proposal brings about two key challenges: detecting the spin splitting to verify the generation mechanism of these magnets, and identifying such polarization to confirm the odd-parity nature. Here, we show that the Ruderman-Kittel-Kasuya-Yosida (RKKY) interaction provides a unified magnetic probe for both tasks. Taking collinear $f$-wave magnets as a representative example, we find that the RKKY interaction yields distinct magnetic signals of the spin splitting---including a magnetism reversal in the Heisenberg/Ising terms and a sign alternation of the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya (DM) term---that enable clear discrimination of collinear $f$-wave magnets from other related AFMs. Moreover, the DM term exhibits an $f$-wave shape with odd-parity symmetry, satisfying $J^{αβ}_{DM}(\mathbf{R}) = -J^{αβ}_{DM}(C_{2q}\mathbf{R})$ ($q=3$), which directly reflects the odd-parity spin polarization $S_z(\mathbf{k}) = -S_z(C_{2q}\mathbf{k})$ in momentum space. This behavior persists in $p$-wave magnets ($q=1$), demonstrating the generality of our approach. Our work establishes the RKKY interaction as a versatile probe for detecting band features of collinear odd-parity magnets, with predictions accessible to existing experimental techniques such as spin-polarized scanning tunneling spectroscopy.

Summary. The paper introduces two robust theoretical methods to calculate the Chern number of Bloch bands by analyzing phase singularities directly within the eigenstates, rather than integrating the Berry curvature. These methods are successfully applied to a photonic lattice experiment, confirming that the resulting topological invariant is quantized and independent of gauge choice.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides an experimentally accessible route to topological invariants in condensed matter systems using only measurements of wavefunction phases/zeros, which is highly relevant for quantum optics and photonics research.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper proposes novel methods to directly calculate topological invariants, specifically Chern numbers, of Bloch bands by analyzing phase singularities (vorticities) in the eigenstates, bypassing traditional Berry curvature integration.

Main result. Two distinct methods—the 0-section method and the tautological bundle method—were successfully applied to demonstrate that the calculated Chern number is robustly quantized and independent of gauge choice, even when using experimental data from photonic lattices.

Method. The core methodology involves identifying zeros (singularities) in continuous sections of complex line bundles formed by Bloch eigenvectors. This leads to calculating topological indices or winding numbers at these zero points across the Brillouin zone torus.

Model / system. The theory is applied to two-dimensional insulators, demonstrated using a photonic lattice experiment simulating a quantum walk model governed by Floquet operators. The analysis uses concepts from Bloch band theory and complex geometry.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate reconstructed eigenvectors ($ ilde{\alpha}^+, ilde{eta}^+$) in both topologically trivial ($C=0$) and topological ($C^+=-2$) regimes. Abstract

We propose two methods to extract the Chern numbers of the Bloch bands of a two dimensional insulator, and apply them to a photonic lattice experiment. These two methods require the knowledge of the complex-valued components of the Bloch eigenvectors, or their ratios. Unlike other tomography methods relying on the approximate reconstruction of the Berry curvature and its integration over the Brillouin zone, our methods boil down to the observation of phase vorticities in the eigensates structure. Those measurements, robust to experimental noise, yield exactly quantized values. One of the two methods exploits the non-normalization of the eigenmodes, making it particularly relevant for classical wave systems.

Summary. This paper analyzes wave damping in weakly viscous deep-water waves by extending the Dysthe theory using multiple-scales analysis. The authors compare the results from this rigorous fluid dynamics framework against an independent phase-lag model for mean-flow interaction. Why it may be interesting. Although focused on fluid dynamics, the mathematical techniques—such as multi-scale analysis, deriving higher-order nonlinear evolution equations, and modeling delayed feedback via relaxation kernels—are highly relevant to open quantum systems where memory effects or non-Markovian damping are crucial.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The authors investigate extending weakly viscous Dysthe wave theory by determining if the standard weak-viscosity framework can systematically generate a complex multiplier associated with nonlinear mean-flow damping.

Main result. They show that while higher-order analysis reveals specific direct viscous contributions at sixth order, an independent finite-adjustment-time model predicts a phase-lag correction entering at fifth order, suggesting the systematic origin of this effect is not guaranteed by the standard weak-viscosity reduction.

Method. The work employs multiple-scales perturbation analysis on the Navier-Stokes equations, extending the Dias-Dyachenko-Zakharov (DDZ) framework, and independently utilizes a finite-adjustment-time model for mean-flow response.

Model / system. The system is two-dimensional free-surface gravity waves in an incompressible, weakly viscous fluid of infinite depth. The analysis builds upon the DDZ weak-viscosity reduction of the full Navier-Stokes equations.

Key observables. Nonlocal mean-flow interaction terms ($\mathcal{A}c_M$), the complex multiplier $(1 + ieta)$, and the effective frequency-dependent damping coefficient $eta_{ ext{eff}}(\Omega) = \Omega au$.

Important parameters / regimes. Weak-viscosity scaling ($ar{ u} = \epsilon^2 u$) and the small parameter $\epsilon$ (wave steepness). The adjustment time $ au$ is a key parameter in the phase-lag model.

Abstract

We extend the Carter-Govan multiple-scales analysis of weakly viscous, narrowband deep-water wave packets beyond Dysthe order within the potential-flow reduction of Dias, Dyachenko, and Zakharov (DDZ). We seek to determine whether this framework generates the complex multiplier $(1 + iβ)$ used phenomenologically to modify the nonlocal Dysthe mean-flow interaction. Although order counting places a direct viscous carrier-mean interaction at sixth order, it does not exclude an indirect fifth-order contribution arising from viscosity dependent lower-order harmonics and nonlinear interactions. We therefore derive the first correction to the induced mean flow and the complete fifth-order first-harmonic solvability condition. The resulting nonlocal terms are derivative--dependent and contain no explicit viscosity, excluding the proposed indirect mechanism within the DDZ framework. Its low-frequency expansion produces the multiplier $ 1 + iβ_{\mathrm{eff}}(Ω)$, with $β_{\mathrm{eff}}(Ω) =Ωτ.$ When $Ωτ= \mathcal O(ε)$, the resulting phase-lag correction enters at fifth order, one order beyond the leading Dysthe mean-flow interaction.

We found that several new materials simultaneously exhibit high-T$_c$ and ductility including HfPd$_2$Al, TiRuSb, and ZrNi$_2$Ga with predicted isotropic T$_c$= 6.80K, 12.88K, and 8.23K, respectively. This work offers a quantitative mapping of mechanical performance across a wide range of superconductors and provides a reference to identify new mechanically promising superconductors.

Abstract

The demand for novel self-powering and sustainable electronics requires major efforts in identifying new advanced materials for nano-applications. MXene have gathered attention due to their electronic and mechanical properties, however, their nanomechanical compliance against a metal-like interface is still not clearly identified. In this work, we employ atomic force microscopy to characterize the nanomechanical properties of a self-assembled thin flake of titanium carbide MXene (Ti3C2Tx) against a gold probe. The investigation returns an interfacial shear stress of 399 MPa, and the observation of nano-wear localized in the center of the MXene flake. Nevertheless, MXene flakes retained their crystallinity in the tribofilms as confirmed by transmission electron microscopy and electron energy loss spectroscopy. The outcomes of this work set the basis for the use of Ti3C2Tx in novel nano harvesting systems involving metal interfaces (e.g., tribovoltaic nanogenerators) with large scope in nanoelectronics, wearable sensing, electric vehicles, and robotics.
