Summary. This paper develops advanced concentration inequalities to provide rigorous, nonasymptotic bounds on the uncertainty when estimating thermodynamic quantities from limited experimental data. By analyzing generalized currents, the authors quantify the required observation time and sample size needed to distinguish between equilibrium and non-equilibrium states. This offers a powerful theoretical framework for modern stochastic thermodynamics and data-driven physics.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides rigorous, quantitative tools for analyzing non-equilibrium steady states, which is crucial for understanding energy dissipation and fluctuation theorems in complex physical systems, bridging statistical mechanics with data science.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper derives nonasymptotic concentration inequalities to rigorously control the uncertainty associated with estimating thermodynamic quantities from finite-time or small-sample stochastic trajectories.

Main result. It establishes refined inverse thermodynamic uncertainty relations (iTUR) and provides explicit confidence intervals, quantifying how observation time and sample size must scale to achieve a desired accuracy, especially highlighting differences between equilibrium and non-equilibrium dynamics.

Method. The methodology relies on deriving concentration bounds (e.g., Cramér-Chernoff approach) for generalized currents and their sample means, extending techniques from stochastic processes theory to finite-data inference.

Model / system. The analysis is performed on geometrically ergodic, time-homogeneous stochastic processes governed by overdamped Langevin equations on a continuous state space. The focus is on the dynamics' ability to relax to a stationary measure.

Important parameters / regimes. Relaxation time, spectral gap ($\lambda_{ ext{gap}}$), dissipation rate, and the ratio $\Sigma_U/\lambda_{ ext{gap}}$ which quantifies the deviation from equilibrium.

Assumptions / limitations. The dynamics are assumed to be geometrically ergodic, and the derivations rely on bounding moment-generating functions and utilizing properties of the underlying Markovian process.

Paper structure. Abstract

We derive nonasymptotic upper bounds on the probability that a generalized current of a geometrically ergodic diffusion observed for any amount of time, or its sample mean over any arbitrary sample size, deviates from the stationary mean by more than any given amount. The concentration-of-measure behavior of generalized currents is universally governed by the relaxation time of the underlying dynamics, the locally observed dissipation rate, and the intrinsic local fluctuations of the observable. We uncover stark qualitative and quantitative differences in fluctuations in and out of thermodynamic equilibrium. We further obtain refined inverse thermodynamic uncertainty relations, bounding the variance of generalized currents from above. We construct nonasymptotic confidence intervals for controlling uncertainty in thermodynamic inference from small data, i.e., from short trajectories and small samples, and provide the first quantitative answer to when a trajectory is sufficiently long and a sample is sufficiently large. As an illustration, we apply our results to currents observed on a two-dimensional Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process in and out of equilibrium and show how they can be used to rigorously detect broken detailed balance.

By optimizing for gate count, depth, and atom movement, the authors demonstrate significant resource savings for algorithms like the QFT. This work is crucial for realizing fault-tolerant quantum computation on scalable physical platforms.

Model / system. The work focuses on neutral-atom quantum processors utilizing Rydberg-mediated gates and atom shuttling capabilities. The model uses Parity Twine Networks (PTNs) to encode arbitrary interaction graphs.

Abstract

We present an efficient implementation of the Parity Architecture for neutral-atom quantum processors. We adapt Parity Twine Networks (PTNs) to different atom layouts, native entangling gates, and atom-shuttling capabilities. This provides a general framework for hardware-aware optimization of gate count, circuit depth, and atom transport for quantum circuits encoding arbitrary interaction graphs in a common basis. Specifically, we develop PTN constructions based on different native entangling-gate realizations, namely CZ, CZSWAP, and iSWAP, providing flexibility to accommodate different hardware capabilities on both static and mobile neutral-atom platforms. Using the quantum Fourier transform (QFT) as a representative example, we demonstrate substantial reductions in two-qubit gate count, atom transport, and circuit depth. These resource savings translate into an estimated circuit fidelity three orders of magnitude higher than competing compilation strategies for a 30-qubit QFT. We further extend the construction to the recently introduced optimistic QFT and discuss the broader applicability of PTNs to other quantum algorithms on neutral-atom platforms.

Summary. This paper theoretically analyzes quantum-enhanced phase estimation in an SU(1,1) interferometer using photon-added even and odd coherent states. The authors demonstrate that adding photons significantly boosts phase sensitivity, pushing performance beyond the Standard Quantum Limit. Crucially, they show that this photon addition mitigates the initial dependence of the measurement on whether the state was initially even or odd.

Why it may be interesting. This work is highly relevant to quantum optics and open quantum systems, as it explores using structured, nonclassical light states (photon-added states) to surpass fundamental measurement limits in an established quantum optical setup (SU(1,1) interferometer).

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how using photon-added even and odd coherent states enhances phase estimation precision in an SU(1,1) interferometer compared to standard coherent states.

Main result. Photon addition significantly enhances phase sensitivity, pushing the achievable precision beyond the Standard Quantum Limit towards the Heisenberg Limit, while also diminishing the initial dependence on the state's even or odd parity.

Method. Phase sensitivity is analyzed using intensity detection and the quantum Cramér-Rao bound, quantifying performance via the quantum Fisher information.

Model / system. The physical model is an SU(1,1) interferometer, which is realized using two Optical Parametric Amplifiers (OPAs) acting on input states, specifically photon-added even/odd coherent states and a squeezed vacuum state.

Key observables. Phase sensitivity ($\Delta\phi$), Quantum Fisher Information (QFI), and the Quantum Cramér-Rao Bound ($\Delta\phi_{QCRB}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Photon-addition number ($m$), OPA gain ($g$), and squeezing parameter ($r$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the use of specific nonclassical input states (PAECS/PAOCS) and models the interferometer using the SU(1,1) transformation.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the phase sensitivity ($\Delta\phi$) vs. OPA gain ($g$) for varying $m$, comparing results to the QCRB, SQL, and HL. Other figures show the convergence of the performance difference ($\delta$) and total photon number ($N_T$) as $m$ increases.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the metrological enhancement by comparing the performance of PAECS and PAOCS against standard coherent states, first using intensity detection, and then determining the ultimate limit via QFI and QCRB, concluding with the convergence analysis.

Abstract

We investigate phase estimation in an SU(1,1) interferometer employing $m$-photon-added even and odd coherent states as nonclassical input resources. The phase sensitivity is evaluated through intensity detection and the error propagation method, while the ultimate precision limit is determined from the quantum Cramér-Rao bound with the quantum Fisher information serving as the relevant metrological quantity. Our results demonstrate that photon addition significantly enhances the phase sensitivity, increases the quantum Fisher information, and reduces the quantum Cramér-Rao bound, leading to a clear improvement over the corresponding even and odd coherent states. Furthermore, the achievable sensitivity exceeds the standard quantum limit and gradually approaches the Heisenberg scaling with increasing photon-addition number. We also find that the $m$-photon-added even coherent states exhibit a modest advantage over their odd counterparts. As $m$ increases, however, this distinction becomes progressively weaker, suggesting that photon addition diminishes the role of the initial parity of the coherent state in determining the interferometric performance.

Summary. This paper proposes a novel, topologically protected method for charging quantum batteries over long distances using a superconducting qubit chain modeled by the SSH Hamiltonian. By dynamically controlling the couplings with optimized pulses, the authors ensure efficient, directional energy transfer that is robust against noise and imperfections. This establishes a theoretical framework for enhancing quantum energy storage devices.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses quantum energy storage and transfer, which is highly relevant to quantum computing hardware development, particularly in superconducting qubit architectures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of developing a robust, long-distance, and topologically protected charging protocol for quantum batteries.

Main result. They demonstrate that pulse-controlled topological charging can achieve complete energy transfer free of backflow, and this protocol remains robust against system imperfections and noise.

Method. The study uses theoretical modeling based on the SSH model and analyzes the time evolution of the system's zero-energy eigenstate using time-dependent Hamiltonian techniques.

Model / system. The system is modeled as a one-dimensional SSH-type qubit chain where the ends act as the charger and battery. The dynamics are controlled by time-dependent pulse-mediated couplings, allowing the system to adiabatically switch between edge states.

Key observables. Charger energy ($E_C(t)$), battery energy ($E_B(t)$), extractable work (Ergotropy), and the adiabatic ratio ($C$).

Important parameters / regimes. The control pulse shape (cosine vs. tangent) and the disorder strength ($W$) are key parameters used to test robustness and acceleration.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the topological framework using the SSH model to implementing the charging protocol via pulse control, and finally analyzing the performance and robustness against noise using optimized pulse shapes.

Abstract

Quantum batteries have emerged as a promising new generation of energy-storage devices for powering quantum technologies. Long-distance charging is particularly attractive because it minimizes interference between the charger and the battery, thereby attracting considerable interest. Here, we propose a topologically protected long-distance charging protocol for quantum batteries based on a pulse-controlled superconducting qubit chain. By dynamically modulating the pulse-mediated couplings, we realize topologically protected energy transfer from the charger to the battery. We show that the charging process is free of energy backflow and remains robust against imperfections in pulse control. Moreover, the energy stored in the battery at the target time is fully extractable, and the protocol remains effective for relatively large system sizes. To further accelerate charging, we optimize the pulse shape and elucidate the underlying physical mechanism. Our pulse-controlled topological quantum battery protocol provides a versatile framework for implementing long-distance topological charging and establishes a theoretical foundation for designing optimal-control strategies to enhance quantum battery performance.

Quantum Hamiltonian Evolution for Coherent Quantum Learning

Summary. This paper proposes Coherent Quantum Learning (CQL), a novel quantum training framework where the optimization process is replaced by coherent Hamiltonian evolution. By encoding the loss function into the system's Hamiltonian, the system naturally evolves toward parameters that minimize the loss through quantum interference. This offers a purely quantum alternative to classical gradient-based optimization in quantum machine learning.

Why it may be interesting. The core idea of encoding optimization into Hamiltonian dynamics is highly relevant to AMO physics, suggesting that physical evolution itself can perform complex computational tasks like optimization, potentially linking quantum simulation to machine learning.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the need for a quantum mechanical training framework for quantum machine learning, moving beyond classical optimization loops.

Main result. It introduces Coherent Quantum Learning (CQL), a framework where parameter evolution is governed by a Hamiltonian encoding the loss function, achieving optimal parameter estimation via quantum interference.

Method. The training is realized by evolving the system unitarily under a time-dependent Hamiltonian that combines kinetic terms and a loss-encoding Hamiltonian. This process replaces classical gradient estimation with coherent interference.

Model / system. The model uses a parameterized quantum circuit whose parameters are treated as quantum degrees of freedom. The loss function $L(x, heta)$ is encoded into a diagonal Hamiltonian $\hat{H}_L$ acting on the parameter register.

Key observables. The probability amplitude concentrating near low-loss configurations, and the final state distribution peaking at optimal parameters.

Important parameters / regimes. The evolution time $ au$, the structure of the loss Hamiltonian $\hat{H}_L$, and the batch size for sequential updates.

Assumptions / limitations. The framework assumes the ability to construct block encodings for the loss Hamiltonian and relies on sequential evolution for batched training.

Figures summary. Figure 1 conceptually contrasts traditional hybrid training with CQL's loss-guided evolution. Figure 6 numerically compares the loss evolution of CQL against traditional gradient descent for a 1D binary classification task.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the CQL concept, detailing the Hamiltonian construction using block encodings for various loss types (polynomial, smooth). It then details the sequential, batched training algorithm and validates the approach with numerical simulations on classification and phase estimation tasks.

Abstract

We introduce Coherent Quantum Learning (CQL), a training framework for quantum learning models in which the model parameters are quantum degrees of freedom evolved under a Hamiltonian that encodes the loss function. Current quantum machine learning retains classical optimization: parameters are updated by a classical outer loop using gradient estimates from measurements, and quantum coherence has no role in the training dynamics, just as in any classical treatment of the same problem. In the quantum case, a parameter register initialized in superposition evolves unitarily, and probability amplitude concentrates near low-loss configurations through interference, without gradient computation or classical feedback. We give an explicit construction using block encodings and Hamiltonian simulation, applicable to arbitrary parameterized circuits. Numerical experiments on binary classification and interferometric phase estimation confirm that the evolved distribution peaks at the optimal parameters, matching gradient-based performance. The construction is compatible in principle with fault-tolerant implementations and extends to batched training via sequential Hamiltonian evolution.

We outline how such an experiment could be adapted to probe properties of quantum fields beyond the Standard Model of particle physics.

Summary. This work introduces a novel diagnostic tool based on the entropy of the quantum coherence distribution to characterize quantum phases. By applying it to the interacting Aubry-André model, the authors demonstrate that this entropy remains robustly sensitive to quantum phase transitions, even when significant thermal fluctuations are present, outperforming simpler moment-based diagnostics.

Why it may be interesting. The focus on using coherence entropy to distinguish quantum phases from thermal noise is highly relevant for AMO physics, as many experimental platforms (like cold atoms) are inherently studied at finite, non-zero temperatures.

Main problem. The paper aims to develop a diagnostic framework to probe both single-particle and many-body quantum phases at finite temperatures, where thermal fluctuations typically obscure quantum critical signatures.

Main result. The entropy of the quantum coherence distribution ($H_c$) proves to be a significantly more robust indicator than individual moments, retaining clear signatures of underlying quantum phases across increasing thermal fluctuations.

Model / system. The analysis is first applied to the non-interacting Aubry-André (AA) model to study localization transitions, and then extended to the interacting AA model containing spinless fermions with nearest-neighbor interactions.

Key observables. Entropy of the coherence distribution ($H_c$), first and higher-order moments of the coherence distribution, and the relative entropy of coherence ($C[\hat{ ho}]$).

Abstract

We introduce a diagnostic framework based on the full probability distribution of quantum coherence to probe single-particle and many-body phases at finite temperature. We first apply this approach to the localization transition in the non-interacting Aubry-André model and then extend the analysis to its interacting counterpart, whose phase diagram is substantially richer. We show that the moments of the coherence distribution provide clear signatures of quantum phase transitions and capture the emergence of many-body phases. More importantly, we find that the entropy of the coherence distribution emerges as an even more powerful indicator, retaining clear signatures of the underlying quantum phases while remaining remarkably robust against increasing thermal fluctuations.

Tunable topological enhancement of covariant quantum Fisher information via non-Bloch skin effect in non-Hermitian SSH lattices

Authors: Qi-Cheng Wu, Yan-Hui Zhou, Tong Liu, Dong-Xu Chen, Chui-Ping Yang

Summary. This paper investigates how the non-Hermitian skin effect in SSH lattices can topologically enhance the quantum precision limit, quantified by the CQFI. By comparing open boundary conditions (OBC) to periodic boundary conditions (PBC), the authors show a large, tunable enhancement factor $E(N)$. This establishes a novel, experimentally accessible spatial-domain strategy for quantum metrology.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete, physically realizable mechanism—the NHSE—to boost quantum sensing precision beyond standard bounds, linking topological properties directly to metrological enhancement in open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To extend the covariant quantum Fisher information (CQFI) formalism to multi-mode non-Hermitian systems to find a tunable topological enhancement mechanism for quantum metrology.

Main result. The CQFI exhibits a tunable topological enhancement factor $E(N)$ due to the non-Hermitian skin effect (NHSE), which can surpass 30 for modest system sizes and significantly reduce estimation uncertainty.

Method. The authors calculate the total CQFI by summing contributions over the Generalized Brillouin Zone (GBZ) manifold, utilizing partial-fraction decomposition to find the asymptotic scaling behavior.

Model / system. The study uses non-Hermitian Su-Schrieffer-Heeger (SSH) lattices, which are analyzed using a Hamiltonian framework that incorporates non-Hermitian parameters ($\gamma_1, \gamma_2$) leading to the NHSE.

Key observables. Covariant Quantum Fisher Information (CQFI), Enhancement Factor $E(N) = F_{ ext{OBC}}^{ ext{total}} / F_{ ext{PBC}}^{ ext{total}}$, and the joint Cramér-Rao bound.

Important parameters / regimes. Non-Bloch decay rate ($\kappa$), system size ($N$), and non-Hermitian asymmetry parameters ($\gamma_1, \gamma_2$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on extending CQFI from single-mode to multi-mode systems and assumes the applicability of non-Bloch band theory for open boundary conditions.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the total CQFI scaling (OBC vs. PBC), the enhancement factor $E(N)$ vs. $N$ (showing large enhancement), and the non-monotonic dependence of $E$ on the non-Hermitian parameters $\gamma_1$.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the CQFI formalism for non-Hermitian systems, then derives the OBC total CQFI by summing over the GBZ, contrasting this with the PBC sum. The core development involves analyzing the resulting enhancement factor $E(N)$ and demonstrating its tunability and topological origin.

Abstract

The covariant quantum Fisher information (CQFI) has recently been established as the ultimate precision benchmark for pseudo-Hermitian sensors [Phys. Rev. Lett. 136, 080802 (2026)], yet existing analyses are limited to single-mode systems. Here we extend the CQFI formalism to multi-mode non-Hermitian Su-Schrieffer-Heeger lattices and reveal tunable topological enhancement enabled by the non-Hermitian skin effect (NHSE). Under open boundary conditions, the NHSE deforms the conventional Brillouin zone into a generalized Brillouin zone of radius r = exp(\k{appa}), where \k{appa} denotes the non-Bloch decay rate. While the total CQFI scales linearly with system size N, its prefactor depends critically on \k{appa}, yielding an enhancement factor E(N) = F_OBC / F_PBC that exceeds 30 for N ~ 30, substantially outperforming periodic-boundary sensors. The enhancement is robust against moderate local disorder and supports multi-parameter estimation, with the joint Cramér-Rao bound reduced by up to 15 orders of magnitude. These findings establish a spatial-domain mechanism for quantum metrology that complements time-domain strategies and is experimentally accessible using topoelectrical circuits, photonic lattices, and superconducting circuits with current technology.

Summary. This work details how structural distortions in moiré superlattices of nonmagnetic materials can induce altermagnetism. Abstract

We propose a mechanism for nonmagnetic materials to develop altermagnetic order by moiré interference with nonmagnetic substrate, which is driven by structural relaxation and spontaneous twirls in moiré domain walls of lattice-mismatched moiré square lattices. When doped with one electron per moiré domain, a correlated insulating gap is opened by electron interaction. Depending on the location of the moiré potential minima, the moiré bands can show d-wave or g-wave altermagnetic splitting. The former can be enhanced by a finite twist angle; the latter is sensitive to strains that drive a transition to d-wave.

Abstract

Quantum thermalization explains how an isolated many-body system naturally evolves towards a thermal state, rendering information about the initial conditions inaccessible to local measurements. This is precisely the mechanism utilized in quantum error correction, where information is protected by design through a nonlocal encoding. In this work, we leverage this connection to port the rigorous framework of (approximate) quantum error correction to the study of quantum thermalization. Treating typical late-time states as codewords, we characterize the error-correcting properties of generic thermalizing dynamics. We numerically uncover a universal relationship between the encoding rate, distance, and thermal entropy density of the emergent code. At infinite temperature, this universal curve saturates the quantum Singleton bound, achieving the same optimal limit as Haar-random codes. At finite temperature, we introduce a code family based on the Scrooge ensemble, the natural thermal analogue of the Haar ensemble, and prove it saturates the entropic quantum Singleton bound, establishing this family as optimal within entropic constraints. Our extracted universal curve independently saturates this same bound, revealing that finite-temperature thermalization is itself optimal. Finally, we show how conserved quantities limit the error-correcting behavior of thermalization: codewords with differing energies, or other conserved charges, leak only classical information, and correctability persists until the difference reaches the scale of thermal fluctuations. Our results reveal a universal optimal coding structure in thermalizing dynamics, while introducing new optimal codes that achieve fundamental limits of approximate quantum error correction.

Abstract

Re-visiting the problem of the coupled electronic--nuclear motion, we prove that the so called `mean-field' Ehrenfest picture is an exact theory, rather than an approximation, in the classical limit for nuclei. By establishing its full equivalence to the much more involved Exact Factorization approach to the problem (Abedi {\it et al.}, Europhys.
