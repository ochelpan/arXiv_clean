Strongly anisotropic non-Kramers electron spin as a quantum coherence probe of angular fluctuations

Authors: Achuthan Manoj Kumar, Remy Dassonneville, Guillaume Gerbaud, Nolwenn Le Breton, Athanassios K. Boudalis, Patrice Bertet, Philippe Goldner, Sylvain Bertaina
Type: experiment · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03989v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: correlated / nonlocal dissipation 1/5 · quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. The researchers used electron paramagnetic resonance on $ ext{Tb}^{3+}$ ions to study quantum coherence, finding that conventional models failed to explain the measured coherence time ($T_2$). They successfully incorporated dynamical angular fluctuations of the crystal axis into their model, demonstrating that ambient vibrations are a dominant, quantifiable decoherence mechanism for anisotropic solid-state spins.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a direct, measurable link between solid-state quantum coherence properties and macroscopic environmental noise (vibrations), offering a novel, highly sensitive quantum sensor platform.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To utilize the spin transitions of strongly anisotropic rare-earth ions as a highly sensitive quantum coherence probe for measuring ambient angular fluctuations.

Main result. The dominant decoherence pathway is found to be due to dynamical angular fluctuations of the crystal axis, which can be quantified and modeled by extending standard spin-bath theories.

Method. Pulsed electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) measurements are used to map the Hahn-echo coherence time ($T_2$) as a function of temperature and magnetic field, comparing results against detailed theoretical models.

Model / system. The system involves $ ext{Tb}^{3+}$ ions in a $ ext{CaWO}_4$ crystal, which possesses strong magnetic anisotropy ($g_{\perp}=0$). The analysis models the spin Hamiltonian and incorporates contributions from spin-lattice relaxation, impurity diffusion, and crucially, angular jitter.

Key observables. Hahn-echo coherence time ($T_2$), $ ext{EPR}$ linewidth ($\Gamma_{ ext{EFS}}$), and the temperature/field dependence of $T_2$.

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature range (2 to 10 K), resonant field ($10^3$ to $10^4$ G), and the angular noise spectral density ($\sim 36 ext{ n}\circ/\sqrt{ ext{Hz}}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The initial model failed because it neglected the dynamical angular fluctuations of the crystal axis, which was confirmed by controlled mechanical experiments.

Figures summary. Figures show $ ext{EPR}$ spectra illustrating field-dependent transitions, linewidth measurements ($\Gamma_{ ext{EFS}}$) showing angular divergence, and $T_2$ measurements demonstrating deviations from simple spin-bath models.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the physical sensitivity of the spin system to orientation, presents initial modeling failures, and culminates in a successful two-parameter extension that incorporates dynamical angular fluctuations to reproduce all experimental data.

Abstract

Strongly anisotropic non-Kramers rare-earth ions combine giant longitudinal g-factors with a vanishing transverse component imposed by time-reversal symmetry, a combination that makes their spin transitions exquisitely sensitive to the orientation of the applied magnetic field. We show that this sensitivity carries a dual identity: it is simultaneously an overlooked decoherence channel and the basis for a spin-coherence-based angular probe. Using pulsed electron paramagnetic resonance at X-band, we report the first measurements of the quantum coherence of Tb$^{3+}$ in a native-doped CaWO$_4$ crystal (15 ppb) and map the Hahn-echo coherence time $T_2$ as a function of temperature (2 to 10 K) and resonant field ($10^3$ to $10^4$ G). A parameter-free model combining spin-lattice relaxation, instantaneous diffusion and spectral diffusion from all independently quantified impurities overestimates $T_2$ by an order of magnitude at low temperature and wrongly predicts the field dependence of $T_2$, inconsistent with the observed monotonic decrease of $T_2$ with $B_r$. A two-parameter extension, including dynamical angular fluctuations of the crystal axis, reproduces the full dataset across multiple setups and laboratories. Two controlled experiments nominally identical except for different mechanical configuration of the setup establish the mechanical origin of the dominant contribution. The two-parameter extension corresponds to an angular amplitude noise spectral density of overall order 36 n°/$\sqrt{Hz}$ from global external vibrations (ranging from 10 to 66 n°/$\sqrt{Hz}$ depending on the exact setup mechanical configuration) estimated at $\sim$ 2.5 kHz plus a temperature-dependent contribution assumed to come from local phonon-driven angular jitter. It identifies and highlights a decoherence pathway of practical relevance to any anisotropic solid-state spin system.

Wavefunctions for Anyon Superconductors

Summary. This work develops a comprehensive wavefunction framework to describe anyon superconductivity, moving beyond conventional BCS theory. By constructing hierarchy wavefunctions derived from topological orders, the authors successfully diagnose superconducting order and topological properties for systems like the $\nu=2/3$ state. This establishes a unified theoretical link between anyon condensation, superconducting pairing, and topological field theory.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous wavefunction approach provides a powerful, concrete tool to analyze topological superconductivity, bridging abstract CFT concepts with measurable physical diagnostics like ODLRO.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to develop a systematic wavefunction description for anyon superconductors, as a general framework is currently lacking despite existing field-theoretic descriptions.

Main result. A unified wavefunction-level framework is established, successfully linking anyon condensation, superconducting order, and topological field theory for various fractional quantum Hall states.

Method. The authors develop a 'hierarchy-wavefunction construction' using the plasma analogy and topological field theories to analyze superconducting states derived from parent topological orders.

Model / system. The study focuses on anyon superconductors, where superconductivity arises from mobile anyon condensation rather than conventional Cooper pairing. Specific models include the $\nu=2/3$ state, Laughlin states ($\nu=1/3$), and the Pfaffian state.

Key observables. Off-Diagonal Long-Range Order (ODLRO), condensate charge, chiral central charge, and residual topological order.

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis examines the long-distance limit of the anyon-Hilbert-space formulation, which applies to ideal Chern bands and moiré bands.

Assumptions / limitations. The formalism relies on the correspondence between quantum Hall hierarchy states and anyon superconductivity, and the plasma analogy requires the exponent matrix to be positive semidefinite.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first defining the general hierarchy wavefunction construction, then applying it to specific fractional quantum Hall states (semions, $\nu=2/3$, $\nu=1/3$), and finally using the reduced density matrix and ODLRO operator to confirm the superconducting nature across all cases.

Abstract

Anyon superconductivity arises from the condensation of mobile anyons rather than from a conventional Cooper instability, yet a systematic wavefunction description remains lacking. We develop a hierarchy-wavefunction construction for superconducting states derived from parent topological orders and identify their off-diagonal long-range order, condensate charge, chiral central charge, and residual topological order through the plasma analogy and topological field theories. We construct Abelian and non-Abelian examples descending from the semion state, a $ν=2/3$ hierarchy state, the $ν=1/3$ Laughlin state, $ν=1$ integer quantum Hall state, and Pfaffian state. Remarkably, for the semion case, the superconducting many-semion wavefunction is equivalent to a state of fermionized anyons filling two effective Landau levels, recovering Laughlin's original construction of semion superconductor. Finally, we show that hierarchy wavefunctions emerge naturally in the dilute, long-distance limit of the anyon-Hilbert-space formulation, which applies to ideal Chern bands and moiré bands of twisted bilayer MoTe$_2$. Our results establish a unified wavefunction-level framework linking anyon condensation, superconducting order, and topological field theory.

Algebraic Operator Decomposition: A Partitioned Architecture for Noise-Resilient Quantum Computing

Authors: Wladimir Silva
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04076v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces Algebraic Operator Decomposition (AOD), a method to break down complex quantum operations into many smaller, shallow, and independent sub-circuits. By processing these fragments in parallel and summing the results classically, it proactively mitigates noise accumulation by keeping the circuit depth below hardware coherence limits. This shifts the computational bottleneck from deep quantum coherence to manageable classical sampling overhead.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a novel, mathematically rigorous framework for structuring quantum computations that directly addresses the depth limitations inherent in NISQ devices, which is highly relevant to open quantum systems modeling.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is mitigating noise and decoherence in Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) computing by avoiding error correction entirely.

Main result. The proposed Algebraic Operator Decomposition (AOD) successfully reduces the required quantum circuit depth by fracturing global operators into shallow, independent sub-channels.

Method. AOD decomposes a global linear operator into a sum of local operators, which are then processed in parallel using a MapReduce model based on a commutative monoid.

Model / system. The framework is built upon operators belonging to a Vector Space over Additive Abelian Groups, applicable to complex quantum operations like inner products, convolutions, and differential operators.

Key observables. The reconstructed global observable ($\langle T angle$), the required shot budget ($S_{ ext{total}}$), and the error bound ($\Delta T$).

Important parameters / regimes. Noise parameters ($\epsilon_{1Q}, \epsilon_{2Q}$), hardware-dependent coherence threshold ($D_{ ext{max}}$), and the number of partitions ($K$).

Assumptions / limitations. The approach assumes independent local noise channels and trades reduced quantum depth for increased classical reconstruction and sampling overhead.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the quantum circuits for specific scenarios, such as the Hadamard Test for inner products and the evolution across discrete operational slices for differential operators.

Paper structure. The paper introduces AOD, details its application to various global operations (e.g., dot products, convolutions), derives theoretical bounds on error containment and sampling cost, and concludes with optimization strategies.

Abstract

We present an operator-decomposition architecture that mathematically maps a global operator into independently executable local operators, reducing the maximum quantum circuit depth at the cost of classical reconstruction and sampling overhead. By framing complex Quantum Circuits around an operator in a vector space that can be algebraically pre-decomposed, AOD complements quantum error correction and error-mitigation approaches by performing algebraic decomposition before quantum execution. Our approach leans in the computer science definition of a Monoid: a design pattern and mathematical concept consisting of a data type, a combining function that is associative, and a safe identity (neutral) element that does not change other values when combined. Simulation wise we define a MapReduce programming model where the addition (+) is the reducer, thus leveraging a naturally stable commutative monoid which carries zero "negative-probability tax" or phase conflicts. Furthermore, we define a Vector Space of Linear Operators over Additive Abelian Groups that benefit from this paradigm, including: Inner Products, Series expansions, Traces and Convolutions. Finally, we present the mathematical foundations and simulation results for this paradigm.

Cutting corners: exciting and magical bounds from the cusp bootstrap

Authors: Ryan A. Lanzetta, Ian Moult, Yifan Wang
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04041v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical paper derives universal, rigorous lower bounds on the anomalous dimensions characterizing sharp corners (cusps) of line defects in a CFT. By applying bootstrap consistency checks across various defect geometries, the authors establish fundamental constraints on the underlying quantum field theory structure. This work provides powerful mathematical tools for constraining physical theories based on symmetry and consistency.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on CFT, the techniques involving universal bounds derived from consistency conditions (bootstrap) are highly relevant to understanding critical points and strongly correlated systems where such constraints often appear.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to establish general, universal bounds on the cusp anomalous dimensions ($\Gamma$) associated with line defects in a Conformal Field Theory (CFT). These bounds are derived from fundamental principles like unitarity and the consistency of defect geometries.

Main result. The authors prove that singlet cusps are the lightest possible cusps and derive an optimal, universal lower bound for the dimension of a right-angle bare cusp using bootstrap constraints.

Method. The analysis employs advanced techniques including the Operator State Correspondence (OSC), the Cusp Operator Expansion (COE), and applying bootstrap constraints derived from varying defect geometries (e.g., rectangular or wedge shapes).

Model / system. The study is set in the context of conformal line defects within a Conformal Field Theory (CFT). The analysis uses concepts from radial and flat quantization to model the dynamics of these defects.

Key observables. Cusp anomalous dimensions ($\Gamma$), the universal defect Casimir energy ($\epsilon_{aar{a}}$), and the bare cusp dimension ($\Gamma_{aar{a}}^0( heta)$).

Important parameters / regimes. The cusp angle ($ heta$), the separation distance ($L$), and the geometry of the defect configuration (e.g., right angle, rectangular).

Assumptions / limitations. The theory is assumed to be local, unitary, and possess a complete defect Hilbert space. The derived bounds are claimed to be valid in any scale-invariant theory.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates cusped defect changing operators; Figure 2 shows defect configurations on a sphere punctured at right angles; Figure 3 illustrates the wedge-shaped defect used in the proof of one of the key inequalities.

Paper structure. The paper builds by first establishing foundational results regarding singlet cusps and then deriving increasingly general bounds by applying consistency checks (bootstrap constraints) to increasingly complex defect geometries (e.g., rectangular, cuboid).

Abstract

An illuminating probe of the dynamics of a line defect is the global geometry of its worldline. For a conformal line defect in a conformal field theory, sharp corners in the worldline, i.e. cusps, host dynamical degrees of freedom characterized in part by a spectrum of scaling dimensions, called cusp anomalous dimensions. We present various general bounds on cusp anomalous dimensions following from unitarity and cutting-and-gluing consistency of different defect geometries. We first establish, for cusps involving conjugate defects, that level crossings upon varying the cusp angle are forbidden between the lightest singlet cusp and any non-singlet cusp, proving that singlet cusps are the lightest. Then, we study line defects arranged in a rectangular geometry, which are subject to bootstrap constraints reminiscent of the spinless modular bootstrap. We find an analytic ``magic" functional that produces an optimal and universal lower bound on the dimension of a right angle bare cusp in terms of the universal defect Casimir energy between the corresponding defect and its conjugate in flat space.

Discretization-Aware Fine-Tuning for Quantum Machine Learning with Chemical Foundation Models

Authors: Shunji Matsuura, Sonika Johri
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03220v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

Summary. This work addresses the information bottleneck in QML by introducing Discretization-Aware Fine-Tuning (DAFT). DAFT modifies the representation learning process to minimize data collisions during quantization, which is crucial for near-term quantum devices. The results prove that achieving quantum advantage requires explicitly aligning continuous chemical embeddings with the discrete constraints of quantum encoding.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on ML, the core concept of information compression and the necessity of preserving continuous information structure when mapping to discrete quantum states is highly relevant to understanding quantum channel capacity and encoding schemes in quantum information theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge in Quantum Machine Learning (QML) is the information bottleneck where high-dimensional classical data must be compressed into limited quantum registers, leading to cross-class collisions.

Main result. The proposed Discretization-Aware Fine-Tuning (DAFT) method significantly reduces these collisions, enabling the quantum model to surpass a matched classical baseline at higher qubit counts.

Method. DAFT adapts a pre-trained chemical foundation model using a differentiable soft collision loss to ensure that the resulting quantized representations remain highly informative.

Model / system. The system uses near-term quantum devices processing molecular representations derived from chemical foundation models (like ChemBERTa-77M) via bit-string encoding.

Key observables. Collision count, quantum classification accuracy, and the performance gap compared to a matched classical baseline.

Important parameters / regimes. Qubit count (tested up to 10 qubits), and the degree of information compression imposed by the bit-string encoding.

Assumptions / limitations. The comparison isolates the effect of representation by ensuring both quantum and classical models receive identical discretized bit-string inputs.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the pipeline comparing baseline vs. DAFT, and tables summarize the reversal of performance advantage from classical-wins to quantum-wins as qubit count increases.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem of data compression in QML, details the DAFT methodology (including soft collision loss), evaluates it on a molecular property prediction benchmark, and concludes by demonstrating the conditions necessary for quantum advantage.

Abstract

A key challenge in practical quantum machine learning (QML), particularly for discriminative tasks such as classification, is the limited capacity of near-term quantum devices to encode high-dimensional classical data into small quantum registers. In optimized basis-encoded (bit-bit) settings, this constraint leads to cross-class collisions, where samples with different labels are mapped to the same discrete bit-string and thus become indistinguishable to any downstream model. In this work, we investigate how data representation affects QML performance under such severe information bottlenecks. We introduce discretization-aware fine-tuning (DAFT), a method that adapts a pre-trained chemical foundation model to produce representations that remain informative after quantization. DAFT reduces collision probability through a differentiable soft collision loss. We evaluate both quantum and classical models under a controlled setting in which they receive identical discretized bit-string inputs, isolating the effect of representation from model architecture. On the blood-brain barrier penetration (BBBP) molecular property prediction benchmark using ChemBERTa-77M, DAFT reduces collision counts by several orders of magnitude and improves quantum classification accuracy by more than 12 percentage points compared to a frozen backbone. Importantly, without DAFT, classical models outperform QML under the same input constraints. With DAFT, however, this comparison reverses at higher qubit counts. At 10 qubits, the quantum model surpasses a matched classical baseline trained on identical bit-strings (0.883 vs. 0.855, $p = 0.026$). These results show that, in information-constrained regimes, achieving a quantum advantage critically depends on aligning continuous representations with discrete quantum encodings.

By tracking changes in the g-factor and linewidth, the authors distinguish the physics in paramagnetic, antiferromagnetic, and quantum-critical regimes. Abstract

In the heavy-fermion metal YbRh2Si2, quantum criticality at a suppressed antiferromagnetic order is governed by the interplay of local magnetic moments and itinerant conduction electrons. We demonstrate how this can be investigated by a new experimental approach that enables the observation of electron spin resonance (ESR) across a broad range of frequencies and fields at very low temperatures. This allowed us to cover a large part of the phase diagram from the paramagnetic Fermi-liquid phase to the phase with antiferromagnetic order and including the quantum-critical regime. Both the ESR g-factor and the linewidth present distinct behaviors in these three regimes, providing further insight into the physics across a quantum critical point. Notably, when cooling down at a field directly towards the quantum critical point, both g-factor and linewidth continuously decrease. Furthermore, we observe a very good matching of the g-factor behavior upon field-tuning and temperature-tuning towards the quantum-critical point. We analyze and discuss the results in the context of present theories on ESR in strongly correlated electron systems.

Abstract

The recent discovery of excitons coupled to the magnetic order, and the consequent strong magneto-optic responses, in some van der Waals magnetic semiconductors has triggered intense activity at the interface of magnetism and semiconductor optics. Here, we present an analytically tractable minimal model that describes magnetic order, electrons, holes, and excitons within a unified framework, thereby capturing a wide range of phenomena. It treats the magnetic order and itinerant carriers to be comprised by distinct electronic orbitals that are mutually coupled via orbital-dependent onsite exchange, similar to the treatment of metallic magnets using an s-d model. Investigating CrSBr bilayer as a case study, we benchmark our model and its predictions against recent experimental and ab-initio results finding good agreement as well as new insights enabled by the model's simplicity. Examining the optical selection rules, we find the conservation of a quantum number formed from a combination of spin and layer pseudospin to be a useful guiding principle, even in noncollinear magnetic configurations. Our analysis finds a series of bright and dark excitonic states in such layered A-type antiferromagnets. The presented framework should be valuable in achieving intuitive understanding of recently discovered excitonic phenomena and guiding the discovery of other excitonic states in layered magnetic semiconductors.

Abstract

Simulating quantum error correction (QEC) circuits including non-Clifford gates at scale is important to accelerate progress toward fault-tolerant quantum computing. Here we demonstrate that matrix product state (MPS) techniques can handle many QEC circuits exactly and without restriction on gate types. Crucially, we find that MPS efficiency depends sensitively on implementation choices, and we introduce a series of targeted optimizations that reduce bond dimensions and simulation time by several orders of magnitude compared to naive approaches. We illustrate this with examples including: (a) a rotated surface code quantum memory up to distance 11, (b) logical Bell-state preparation up to distance 9, (c) a 15-to-1 magic-state distillation circuit including hundreds of QEC rounds that we optimize to be simulated with only 11 logical qubits (187 physical qubits) and a maximal bond dimension of 64 in under 40 seconds, and (d) a narrow, deep random circuit that scales linearly with the number of T gates. These results demonstrate the importance of circuit-level optimizations and position MPS as a valuable complement to near-Clifford simulators for QEC circuits.

Parameterised graph theory for tensor networks: entanglement rerouting, structural simplification, and agnostic tomography

It proves that graph parameters like cutwidth bound the required bond dimensions for efficient representations (MPS/TTN). Why it may be interesting. This work provides rigorous, graph-theoretic tools to quantify the computational cost associated with simulating or characterizing quantum states, which is crucial for understanding the limits of quantum simulation algorithms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to characterize the structural graph parameters that determine the computational tractability of representing quantum states as Tensor Networks (TNSs) and to bound the complexity of learning these states.

Main result. It establishes that graph parameters like cutwidth and tree-cutwidth bound the bond dimension overhead for MPS/TTN representations, and derives explicit graph-dependent complexity bounds for state tomography.

Abstract

Parameterised graph theory studies how the complexity of graph-theoretic problems depends on structural parameters of the input graph. This perspective has proved useful in analysing tensor-network simulation (Markov and Shi, 2008). Its implications for tensor-network representations and tomography are less well understood. In particular, which graph parameters determine whether a tensor-network state (TNS) admits a tractable matrix product state (MPS) or tree tensor network (TTN) representation, and which control the complexity of learning the state? We address these questions using parameterised graph theory. First, we show that cutwidth and tree-cutwidth bound the bond dimension overhead required to represent a TNS as an MPS or TTN. In the TTN case, tree-cutwidth also bounds the local dimension of the grouped subsystems. Abstract

Classically equivalent formulations of an optimization problem need not lead to equivalent quantum algorithms. For MaxCut, fixing the value of a single vertex removes a simple global symmetry without changing the underlying optimization problem, yet it can substantially alter the quantum dynamics generated by the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA). These dynamics are captured by the circuit's dynamical Lie algebra (DLA), whose direct construction can become exponentially expensive. Here we show that, for symmetry-reduced QAOA on trees, substantial information about the reduced DLA can instead be obtained efficiently from the graph alone. We introduce a polynomial classical algorithm that recursively distinguishes vertices using shortest path structure and degree parity. When all vertices are resolved individually, the method determines the complete reduced DLA and, under the corresponding graph conditions, certifies maximal expressibility of the reduced QAOA ansatz. Even when full resolution is not achieved, the algorithm identifies embedded subalgebras, provides rigorous lower bounds on DLA dimension, and certifies controllable subsystems. Our theoretical and experimental results show how classical graph structure can be used to diagnose and potentially guide the quantum dynamics of variational algorithms before running them on quantum hardware.

Abstract

We present a permutation-symmetric supporting relation between the pairwise concurrences and the three-tangle in three-qubit system, whose extrema are attained only by the symmetric $W$ and GHZ local-unitary classes. Furthermore, by introducing the concept of a four-dimensional compatibility body, we show that our proposed global relation and the previous CKW relation can be uniformly viewed as different supporting directions of the same achievable set. Subsequently, we provide a necessary-and-sufficient semi-algebraic characterization of the complete four-dimensional compatibility of a three-qubit pure state. Based on this, we can conversely describe the compatibility between entangled components: given the remaining entangled components, the missing two-body entangled component can only take values within a precise parameter-free interval. We demonstrate that the above construction also applies to arbitrary concurrence-generated entanglement measures.
