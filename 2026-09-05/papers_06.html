Hidden Magnetic Octupolar Order driven by Spin-Orbit coupling in KFeF$_3$

Authors: C. A. Crawford, C. I. Hiley, N. C. Bristowe, C. Stock, J. Gainza, C. Ritter, M. R. Lees, R. I. Walton, M. S. Senn
Type: both · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03684v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work demonstrates that the material KFeF3 undergoes a low-temperature phase transition driven by a hidden magnetic octupole order, rather than conventional structural distortion. The key mechanism involves spin-orbit coupling stabilizing this higher-rank magnetic order, which is responsible for observed weak ferromagnetism. The study establishes KFeF3 as a model system for understanding multipolar magnetism driven by competing electronic energy scales.

Why it may be interesting. The concept of hidden, multipolar order driven by SOC is highly relevant to understanding exotic magnetic states in strongly correlated materials, which often exhibit complex, non-collinear magnetic ground states.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates phase transitions driven by 'hidden order'—magnetic or structural instabilities lacking conventional symmetry-breaking signatures.

Main result. The low-temperature transition in KFeF3 is driven by a hidden magnetic octupole order parameter, which is stabilized by spin-orbit coupling (SOC) and accounts for weak ferromagnetism.

Method. The research combines high-resolution powder diffraction, magnetometry, symmetry-based Landau analysis, and first-principles DFT calculations.

Model / system. The physical system is the 3d perovskite, KFeF3, which exhibits multiple magnetic and structural transitions between cubic, rhombohedral, and monoclinic phases.

Key observables. Weak ferromagnetism (wFM), symmetry-breaking strain, magnetic octupole order parameter, and the evolution of the effective magnetic moment ($\mu_{ ext{eff}}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The competition between spin-orbit coupling (SOC) and orbital degeneracy, which dictates the emergence of higher-rank magnetic multipolar degrees of freedom.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on symmetry-based Landau theory and DFT calculations to model the coupling between different magnetic multipolar orders.

Figures summary. Figures show magnetic susceptibility (M(T), M(H)), variable temperature neutron diffraction data confirming structural transitions, and theoretical diagrams illustrating the coupling of the octupole order parameter to strain and weak ferromagnetism.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from characterizing conventional transitions (90 K) to identifying the hidden instability (40 K). It uses symmetry analysis (Landau theory) to pinpoint the octupole order, and DFT calculations confirm the role of SOC in suppressing competing instabilities and stabilizing the multipolar state.

Abstract

Phase transitions are typically driven by symmetry-breaking structural distortions that lift electronic degeneracies, yet in some cases, these transitions may be driven by a hidden order without conventional structural signatures. Here, we demonstrate that the 3$d$ perovskite, KFeF$_3$, hosts such a hidden instability. Using a combination of high-resolution powder diffraction, magnetometry, symmetry-based analysis and first principles calculations, we reveal that while the 90 K cubic to rhombohedral transition arises from conventional magnetostriction accompanying antiferromagnetic order, a second transition at 40 K lowers the symmetry to monoclinic without any detectable Jahn-Teller distortion or translational symmetry-breaking. Symmetry-based Landau analysis supports a hidden zone centered magnetic octupole order parameter, whose improper coupling accounts for the weak ferromagnetism and pronounced symmetry-breaking strain. Density functional calculations show that spin-orbit coupling suppresses the competing Jahn-Teller instability, and thereby favors higher rank magnetic multipolar degrees of freedom. These findings establish KFeF$_3$ as a model system in which the competing energy scales between spin-orbit coupling and orbital degeneracy result in the emergence of hidden, multipolar order. We show that the control of such magnetic, multipolar order could, in principle, provide a route to induce altermagnetism.

Limits of Stochastic Semigroups and Block-Triangular Majorisation

Authors: Fabio Deelan Cunden, Jakub Czartowski, Giovanni Gramegna, Marilena Ligabò
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04057v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical paper analyzes the limiting behavior of stochastic semigroups derived from Gibbs distributions as temperature approaches zero. It proves that the limit of the semigroups does not generally commute with taking the semigroup of the limit distribution. The resulting structure is characterized by block-upper-triangular matrices, defining a novel mathematical concept called Block-Triangular majorisation.

Why it may be interesting. The non-commutativity result is crucial for understanding how thermodynamic limits affect the dynamics of open quantum systems, suggesting that the path taken to the limit matters significantly.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the mathematical structure of the limit of stochastic semigroups associated with probability vectors $\gamma(eta)$ as the inverse temperature $eta$ approaches infinity.

Main result. The limiting semigroup is generally not the semigroup preserving the limiting distribution, but rather a subsemigroup characterized by block-upper-triangular stochastic matrices, leading to a new concept called Block-Triangular majorisation.

Method. The authors develop a general framework involving multiscale decomposition and analyzing the Kuratowski limit of the semigroups, utilizing majorization theory and combinatorial counting.

Model / system. The model involves stochastic matrices whose invariant distributions are Gibbs vectors $\gamma(eta) \propto e^{-eta E_i}$, arising in the context of statistical mechanics at varying temperatures.

Key observables. Stochastic matrices, semigroups, Block-Triangular majorisation, and Rényi $\alpha$-entropies.

Important parameters / regimes. The inverse temperature $eta$, particularly its limit $eta o \infty$ (zero-temperature limit).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis often relies on the exponential separation of scales in the Gibbs case and the existence of a limiting temperature $eta_0$ for certain inequalities.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the non-commutativity of limit operations using different asymptotic trajectories and provide flowcharts detailing the extreme points of the stochastic semigroups for small systems ($n=3, 4$).

Paper structure. The paper establishes a general framework for limiting semigroups, specializes this to the Gibbs case using multiscale profiles, characterizes the resulting block-upper-triangular structure, and proves the equivalence between this structure and the Block-Triangular majorisation preorder.

Abstract

We investigate limits of semigroups of stochastic matrices defined by their invariant distribution. Given probability vectors $γ(β)$ depending on a parameter $β$, we introduce a notion of convergence as $β\to\infty$ for the corresponding semigroups of $γ(β)$-preserving stochastic matrices and investigate the structure of the resulting limit. In general, the limiting semigroup differs from the semigroup preserving the limiting distribution, showing that these two operations do not commute. We develop a general framework for such limiting semigroups and study in detail the case in which the invariant distributions are Gibbs vectors at the inverse temperature $β$. We show that the limiting semigroup consists of block-upper-triangular stochastic matrices subject to additional substochasticity constraints. We characterise and enumerate their extremal elements and determine the preorder on probability vectors induced by the action of the semigroup. The resulting notion of Block-Triangular majorisation interpolates between ordinary majorisation and upper triangular (aka unordered) majorisation. We show that it is completely characterised by a finite family of monotones and analyse the corresponding behaviour of Rényi $α$-entropies as $β\to\infty$.

Metricity of separable quantum optimal transport with the Hilbert-Schmidt cost

Authors: Tomasz Miller
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03769v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper rigorously proves that the separable quantum optimal transport cost, derived using the Hilbert-Schmidt cost, satisfies the triangle inequality, thereby establishing it as a true metric. The authors achieve this by employing sophisticated tools from convex analysis, such as convex-roof duality, to overcome known mathematical obstructions in the field. This result provides a fundamental geometric structure for quantifying distances between quantum states.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical proof of metricity for quantum transport distances is crucial for developing robust geometric tools in quantum information theory, providing a quantitative measure of distinguishability between quantum states.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to prove that the separable quantum optimal transport cost, $d_{sep}( ho, \sigma)$, associated with the Hilbert-Schmidt cost, defines a genuine metric on the space of density matrices.

Main result. The core result is the establishment of the triangle inequality for $d_{sep}( ho, \sigma)$, confirming it is a true distance metric. Furthermore, it shows that the separable SWAP fidelity yields a metric on the state space.

Method. The proof relies heavily on advanced convex analysis techniques, specifically replacing a failing 'gluing argument' with convex-roof duality and utilizing a dimension-independent interpolation result for Hermitian operators.

Model / system. The system involves quantum states represented by density matrices ($ ho, \sigma$) in a finite-dimensional Hilbert space ($\mathcal{H}^n$). The analysis focuses on the quantum optimal transport framework restricted to separable quantum couplings.

Key observables. The separable quantum optimal transport cost, $d_{sep}( ho, \sigma)$, and the associated separable SWAP fidelity, $F_{S,sep}( ho, \sigma)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The cost function is derived from the Hilbert-Schmidt distance between pure states, $c(x, z) = rac{1}{2}(1 - |\langle x, z angle|^2)$.

Assumptions / limitations. The proof requires establishing a key interpolating Hermitian operator (Lemma 2.3) to circumvent the 'quantum marginal obstruction' that invalidates standard gluing arguments.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes $d_{sep}$ as a semidistance, then derives the dual formulation for the transport cost using convex analysis (convex-roof duality), and finally proves the main theorem by establishing the triangle inequality.

Abstract

We prove that the square root of the separable quantum optimal transport cost associated with the orthogonal projection onto the antisymmetric subspace defines a genuine distance between density matrices. Equivalently, this establishes the triangle inequality for the order-two Beatty-França quantum optimal transport construction induced by the Hilbert-Schmidt distance between pure states. The result also proves metricity of the corresponding distance derived from separable SWAP fidelity. The proof replaces the unavailable gluing argument by convex-roof duality and a dimension-independent interpolation result for Hermitian operators.

Multi-Boundary Many-Body Quantum Teleportation

Authors: Tal Schwartzman, Antonio F. Rotundo, Raz Monsonego, Shira Chapman
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03053v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work develops a multi-body quantum teleportation protocol using scrambling, motivated by holographic wormhole geometries. By extending the standard two-party setup to three parties, the authors demonstrate that the protocol can probe spatial information spreading, revealing features like holographic causal shadows. The results offer a new diagnostic tool for understanding information transfer in complex quantum systems.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a concrete, testable quantum information protocol whose diagnostics (like spatial resolution) are directly linked to deep concepts in quantum gravity and holography, making it highly relevant for theoretical quantum information scientists.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper generalizes quantum teleportation to a multi-body setting using quantum scrambling, drawing motivation from holographic models of traversable wormholes.

Main result. The three-party setup reveals spatial or subsystem resolution of information spreading, and the fidelity and required coupling exhibit dependencies on distance reminiscent of holographic causal shadows.

Method. The analysis involves studying the protocol's success probability using both analytical techniques and numerical simulations employing random quantum circuits.

Model / system. The system is a three-party quantum teleportation protocol involving three qubit systems (A, B, C), initialized with EPR pairs analogous to a three-boundary holographic state.

Key observables. Teleportation fidelity, minimum required coupling, and the dependence of these on distance from the injection site.

Important parameters / regimes. The dimensionality of dynamics (one-dimensional vs. all-to-all) and the number of parties (three).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis uses a single-qubit message and often relies on the infinite-temperature limit for holographic comparisons.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the two-sided protocol, the pair of pants geometry for the three-party state, and the causal structure including a causal shadow.

Paper structure. The paper builds from the standard two-party protocol to a three-party generalization, analyzing dynamics under 1D and all-to-all coupling, and comparing results to predictions from multi-boundary wormhole holography.

Abstract

Unlike standard quantum teleportation, many-body teleportation uses scrambling to transmit quantum information. In this protocol, initially localized information spreads over many degrees of freedom and is later refocused at the receiver by a simple coupling between the systems, followed by further many-body evolution. The protocol was developed from models of traversable wormholes in holography and has become a useful probe of scrambling. In particular, it can distinguish genuine scrambling from decoherence or noise when out-of-time-order correlators fail, and can reveal signatures of different scrambling mechanisms, including the distinctive behavior expected in holographic systems. Holography predicts that related protocols can transmit information between selected boundaries of multi-boundary wormhole geometries. Motivated by this setting, we study single-qubit many-body teleportation among three systems of qubits. The initial state consists of EPR pairs distributed among them, providing a simple analogue of an infinite-temperature three-boundary holographic state. We analyze the protocol with one-dimensional and all-to-all dynamics, both analytically and numerically using random circuits. We find that the third system suppresses teleportation once the spreading message reaches the region of qubits that are entangled with it. In one dimension, both the minimum coupling required for successful teleportation and the fidelity depend on the distance from the injection site to this region, a feature reminiscent of holographic causal shadows. For all-to-all dynamics, successful teleportation is instead restricted to early times and to sufficiently few qubits entangled with the third system. A third system therefore provides spatial, or subsystem, resolution of information spreading that is absent from the two-sided protocol. Our results offer a step toward many-body teleportation networks.

Non-local Magic: closed-form solution and equivalence with magic of purification

Authors: Michele Viscardi, Lorenzo Leone, Alioscia Hamma
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04119v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work develops a resource theory framework to quantify 'non-local magic' in bipartite quantum states. The authors derive an exact, closed-form solution for this magic measure using the state's Schmidt spectrum. Crucially, they establish that this non-local magic is equivalent to the minimum magic achievable through the purification process, offering both an efficient calculation and a deep physical interpretation.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a rigorous, analytically tractable measure (non-local magic) for quantifying non-local quantum correlations, linking it to established resource theories like purification.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to provide an efficient analytical characterization and resource-theoretic interpretation of 'non-local magic,' a measure quantifying the non-stabilizerness of bipartite quantum states.

Main result. They prove that the non-local magic, specifically for the log-stabilizer fidelity, coincides with the minimum magic of purification along the unitary orbit of the reduced density operator.

Method. The authors derive an exact closed-form solution for the log-stabilizer fidelity based on the Schmidt spectrum and introduce the 'magic of purification' as a resource-theoretic extension.

Model / system. The study focuses on bipartite quantum states ($ ho_{AB}$ or $|\psi angle_{AB}$), analyzing their non-local magic relative to stabilizer states ($ ext{STAB}_1, ext{STAB}_0$) and the concept of state purification.

Key observables. Non-local magic, log-stabilizer fidelity ($D_{ ext{NL stab}}$), magic of purification ($D_{ ext{pur stab}}$), and the Schmidt spectrum.

Important parameters / regimes. The Schmidt coefficients ($ heta_i$) of the bipartite state are crucial as they determine the closed-form solution.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is tractable by focusing on the log-stabilizer fidelity, as the general evaluation of non-local magic requires difficult optimization over local unitaries.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by defining non-local magic, deriving the closed-form solution for the log-stabilizer fidelity, introducing the magic of purification, and finally proving the equivalence between the two measures via the unitary orbit of the reduced density operator.

Abstract

Non-local magic quantifies the non-stabilizerness of a bipartite quantum state that cannot be removed by local unitary transformations. Despite its natural definition, its evaluation generally requires a difficult optimization over local unitaries. Here, we show that for the log-stabilizer fidelity this optimization admits an exact closed-form analytic solution depending only on the Schmidt spectrum. We then introduce the magic of purification, defined as the minimum pure-state magic over all purifications of a mixed state, and show that it naturally induces a resource theory whose free states are normalized stabilizer-code projectors. For the log-stabilizer fidelity, the magic of purification admits a distance-based formulation in terms of the Uhlmann fidelity. Remarkably, we prove that non-local magic coincides with the minimum magic of purification along the unitary orbit of the reduced density operator. Our results provide both an efficient analytical characterization and a mixed-state resource-theoretic interpretation of non-local magic.

On the geometry and typicality of quantum magic

Authors: Zhenhuan Liu, Z-Wen Liu
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03944v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical paper analyzes the geometric structure of the stabilizer polytope in high-dimensional quantum systems. It derives quantitative bounds on the polytope's size and proves a sharp phase transition for when random quantum states are likely to be 'magic.' The results show that the polytope has extremely high combinatorial complexity, suggesting that its exact description requires an exponentially large number of constraints.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous geometric characterization of the boundary between 'magic' and 'non-magic' states provides fundamental insights into the structure of quantum correlations, which is crucial for understanding quantum error correction and resource theories.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper characterizes the geometry and typicality of 'quantum magic' states—states that are convex mixtures of pure stabilizer states—in high-dimensional quantum systems.

Main result. It establishes sharp bounds on the geometric complexity (e.g., doubly exponential number of facets) of the stabilizer polytope and determines a sharp phase transition scale for when random induced states are likely to be magic.

Method. The analysis relies heavily on advanced geometric techniques from convex geometry, including bounding the Hilbert-Schmidt inradius and volume radius of the polytope, and applying Gaussian approximation theorems to random states.

Model / system. The system is an $n$-qubit system with dimension $d=2^n$. The focus is on the stabilizer polytope ($ ext{STAB}_n$), which contains all mixed stabilizer states, and random states obtained by tracing out a subsystem from a Haar-random pure state.

Key observables. Purity ($ ext{Tr}( ho^2)$), Hilbert-Schmidt inradius ($ ext{rin}(K_n)$), Volume radius ($ ext{vrad}(K_n)$), and the transition dimension $k_\star$.

Important parameters / regimes. The system dimension $d=2^n$, the environment dimension $k$, and the critical purity threshold $a^* \approx 0.458327$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis uses the assumption of Haar-random pure states and relies on asymptotic convex geometry tools like the Bourgain-Milman inequality.

Figures summary. No figures were explicitly summarized, but the notes detail the derivation of bounds for the inradius, volume radius, and the transition scale $k^*(d)$ using various inequalities.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by first establishing purity bounds related to the inradius (Theorem 1), then bounding the volume radius (Theorem 2), followed by determining the sharp phase transition scale for magic typicality (Theorem 3), and finally quantifying the combinatorial complexity via the facet count (Theorem 4).

Abstract

We prove that, for an $n$-qubit system of dimension $d=2^n$, every state satisfying $\operatorname{Tr}(ρ^2)\le 1/(d-a_\ast)$, with $a_\ast=0.458327\cdots$, lies inside the stabilizer polytope and is therefore magic-free. Combining this result with general geometric properties of high-dimensional polytopes, we establish quantitative estimates for the Hilbert--Schmidt inradius and volume radius of the stabilizer polytope, and use them to characterize the typicality of magic in random induced states obtained by tracing out a $k$-dimensional subsystem from a $d\times k$-dimensional Haar-random pure state. We prove a sharp phase transition in the probability of such states having magic, whose transition dimension $k_\star$ is bounded between $Ω(d^2/\log^2d)$ and $\mathcal{O}(d^2)$. We further prove that the number of facets of the stabilizer polytope lies between $\exp[Ω(d^2/\log^2 d)]$ and $\exp[\mathcal{O}(d^2\log^2 d)]$, substantially improving upon the previous quasipolynomial lower bound and implying that any exact description of the magic-free region requires a doubly exponential number of linear inequalities in the number of qubits. Overall, our results show that the stabilizer polytope exhibits near-maximal geometric complexity allowed for a high-dimensional polytope with a certain number of vertices.

Projected Riemannian Gradient Descent for the Bures-Wasserstein Barycenter: Dimension-Independent Linear Convergence at Unit Step Size

Authors: A. Afham
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03762v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops a Projected Riemannian Gradient Descent algorithm to compute the Bures-Wasserstein barycenter of quantum states represented by positive definite matrices. By introducing a novel, non-expansive projection based on eigenvalue clipping, the authors achieve dimension-independent linear convergence at the computationally desirable unit step size. This provides a significant algorithmic improvement over existing methods in quantum information geometry.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a highly efficient, theoretically robust numerical tool for averaging quantum states or covariance matrices, which is fundamental in quantum estimation and resource theories.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the computational challenge of finding the Bures-Wasserstein (BW) barycenter of an ensemble of positive definite matrices and solving related invariant matrix projection problems.

Main result. It proposes a Projected Riemannian Gradient Descent (RGD) algorithm that achieves dimension-independent linear convergence at unit step size, resolving a known theoretical dichotomy in the field.

Method. The core method is a Projected RGD algorithm that incorporates a novel, exact, non-expansive projection based on eigenvalue clipping to constrain iterates within a spectral interval.

Model / system. The analysis is conducted on the manifold of positive definite matrices (PD(d)), using the Bures-Wasserstein distance, which is relevant for quantum information tasks like Bayesian quantum tomography.

Key observables. Convergence rate, which is shown to be linear with a rate of $(1 - \kappa^{-3/2})$, and the condition number $\kappa = eta/\alpha$.

Important parameters / regimes. The spectral bounds $\alpha$ and $eta$ define the constrained set $[\alpha I, eta I]$, and the step size is fixed at unity ($\eta=1$).

Assumptions / limitations. The key assumption enabling the result is the novel Projection Lemma, which proves that eigenvalue clipping is the closed-form, non-expansive BW-metric projection onto the constrained set.

Figures summary. Figure 1(a) compares the convergence speed of unit-step vs. small-step RGD, showing the proposed method achieves convergence in $\approx 16$ iterations versus $\approx 3300$ iterations for small steps.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the scientific problem (BW barycenter/invariant projection), then introduces the Projected RGD method, proves the crucial Projection Lemma, and finally analyzes the convergence rate and its generalization to related submanifolds.

Abstract

The computation of the Bures-Wasserstein (BW) barycenter of an ensemble of positive definite matrices arises throughout machine learning, optimal transport, and quantum information. Riemannian gradient descent (RGD) at unit step size -- the fixed-point iteration used in practice -- converges rapidly, yet existing analyses present a dichotomy: unit-step guarantees carry worst-case exponential dependence on the dimension, while dimension-independent guarantees require small step sizes that forfeit the empirical speed. We resolve this dichotomy, not by improving the guarantees for unit-step RGD, but by proposing a Projected RGD algorithm that achieves dimension-independent linear convergence at unit step size. The achieved rate, $(1 - κ^{-3/2})$, where $κ$ is the condition number of the ensemble, also polynomially improves on the best small-step guarantee ($κ^{3/2}$ versus $κ^{5/2}$ iteration complexity). The crux is a novel Projection Lemma: clipping the eigenvalues of a positive matrix to an interval $[α, β]$ is the closed-form, non-expansive (1-Lipschitz) BW-metric projection onto the set $\{S : αI \leq S \leq βI\}$ -- a statement which, unlike its known one-sided counterpart, does not follow from convexity. The projection is moreover free: it reuses an eigendecomposition the next iteration must perform in any case, so the projected and unprojected iterations cost the same per step. The same analysis covers the invariant matrix projection problem of Brahmachari et al. (2025), whose fixed-point algorithm we identify as unit-step RGD on a totally geodesic submanifold, thereby extending the dimension-independent guarantee to that setting verbatim.

Q-Edge: Symmetry-Reduced Quantum Simulation of Structured Extreme Dependence

Authors: Hongrui Zhang, Paolo Recchia, Ying Chen
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03706v1

Summary. This paper introduces Q-Edge, a novel quantum framework designed to overcome the exponential complexity barrier in simulating high-dimensional structured extreme dependence. It achieves this by mathematically exploiting inherent symmetries to map the vast dependence space onto a much smaller, compact orbit space. This results in a quantum simulation that requires significantly fewer qubits while maintaining full expressive power for complex, rare events.

Why it may be interesting. The technique of transferring symmetry into the data representation rather than constraining the quantum circuit is a powerful general principle for designing resource-efficient quantum algorithms for complex physical systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is the combinatorial complexity of simulating high-dimensional multivariate extremes, which limits current quantum simulation methods.

Main result. The authors introduce Q-Edge, a framework that uses symmetry to map the exponentially large dependence space onto a compact, symmetry-reduced orbit space, drastically lowering the required quantum resources.

Method. The approach involves exploiting the mathematical principle that symmetry allows for a lossless orbit-space representation, which is then used to guide an unconstrained quantum generative model.

Model / system. The work focuses on simulating structured extreme dependence (ESD) in high-dimensional random variables, treating the dependence structure via its angular distribution on a simplex.

Key observables. The number of reachable symmetry-constrained orbits ($ ext{OG}( ext{QN})$) and the reduction in required qubits (e.g., 21 qubits down to 8 for a 30D problem).

Important parameters / regimes. The dimensionality ($D$), the resolution ($N$), and the specific symmetry group structure (e.g., related to Dirichlet distributions).

Assumptions / limitations. The core assumption is that the structured dependence admits a lossless orbit-space representation that can be utilized by quantum generative models.

Figures summary. Figure 1 conceptually illustrates the flow: structured extremes $ ightarrow$ orbit-space reduction $ ightarrow$ reduced representation $ ightarrow$ quantum model $ ightarrow$ reconstruction.

Paper structure. The paper builds from identifying the complexity bottleneck in high-dimensional simulation, deriving the mathematical framework for orbit-space reduction using symmetry principles (Burnside's Lemma), and finally proposing the Q-Edge quantum implementation.

Abstract

High-dimensional simulation of multivariate extremes is fundamentally limited by the combinatorial complexity of dependence, often more than by the scarcity of extreme observations. We show that symmetry admits a lossless orbit-space representation that preserves structured extreme dependence while replacing an exponentially large dependence space with a compact set of symmetry classes. Based on this principle, we develop Q-Edge (Quantum Extreme Dependence Engine), a symmetry-reduced quantum framework that operates directly in orbit space, enabling scalable simulation and digital twins of structured extreme systems. By transferring symmetry into the data representation rather than the quantum circuit, Q-Edge allows unconstrained quantum generative models to exploit dramatically reduced state spaces. For a 30-dimensional problem, approximately 1.6 million angular states collapse to 256 orbit states, reducing the required quantum representation from about 21 qubits to 8. Our results establish a general computational principle for scalable quantum simulation of structured extreme dependence.

Abstract

In this paper, we introduce a notion of abstract quantum data type within the framework of universal algebra. This notion provides an algebraic foundation for describing data abstraction in quantum programming. We further establish conditions under which quantisation preserves structural relationships and constructions of classical data types, including embeddings, isomorphisms, and products.

Quantum communication and Bell nonlocality require infinite classical communication to simulate

Summary. This work determines the classical communication cost needed to simulate quantum communication and Bell nonlocality for arbitrary dimensions. It finds that while qubits and qutrits can be simulated with finite classical bits (though the cost for qutrits is high), any system with dimension four or higher requires an infinite amount of classical communication for exact simulation.

Why it may be interesting. The finding that quantum correlations require infinite classical resources above a certain dimension is a fundamental statement about the non-classical nature of quantum information, relevant to understanding the resource theory of quantum communication.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the fundamental limits on simulating quantum communication and Bell nonlocality using only finite classical communication.

Main result. It establishes that for quantum dimensions $d_Q \geq 4$, no finite classical communication can simulate the quantum correlations, revealing a qualitative transition at $d_Q=4$.

Method. The authors compare quantum probability distributions (derived from quantum measurements) against classical probability distributions achievable through finite classical encoding and shared randomness.

Model / system. The study models quantum communication using states $ ho$ of dimension $d_Q$ and local measurements $M$, and analyzes the simulation cost $C_{cl}(d_Q)$ required to match quantum statistics classically.

Key observables. The required classical communication dimension $d_C$, and the comparison between quantum and classical probability averages $p_Q$ vs $p_C$.

Important parameters / regimes. Quantum dimension $d_Q$ (e.g., 2 for qubits, 3 for qutrits, $\geq 4$ for ququarts), and the required classical bits $d_C$.

Abstract

Exact CNOT synthesis asks for a minimum-size CNOT circuit implementing an invertible linear transformation. Although several related synthesis models have been shown to be computationally hard, their hardness proofs rely on additional structure such as restricted qubit connectivity, encoded inputs, or unrestricted intermediate variables. The complexity of the most basic setting---identity input, a fixed number of labelled qubits, no ancillas, and all-to-all CNOT connectivity---had remained unresolved. In this work, we prove that the decision version of this vanilla exact CNOT synthesis problem is NP-complete, and consequently that its optimization version is NP-hard. Our proof gives a polynomial-time reduction from the Hamiltonian-path problem on grid graphs in two steps. First, we isometrically embed the grid graph into a hypercube via a unary encoding map. We then encode this hypercube Hamiltonian path problem into vanilla exact CNOT synthesis. The main challenge is that CNOT synthesis specifies only the final parity matrix and cannot directly enforce the intermediate vertex visits required by a Hamiltonian path. To overcome this difficulty, we introduce extra recorder qubits that encode the required intermediate vertex visits into the final transformation, forcing any CNOT circuit implementation to realize the intended path structure. Beyond CNOT synthesis, our result directly implies hardness for several related problems, including the shortest word problem over $\mathrm{GL}(n,2)$, distance computation on Cayley graphs over $\mathrm{GL}(n,2)$, minimization of sequential XOR programs, and exact synthesis of phase polynomial circuits.
