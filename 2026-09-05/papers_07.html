Variable-Cliff Nielsen Geometry and an Exponent -4/3 Lower Bound for the Infinite-Cliff Diameter

Authors: Honghuai Fang
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03323v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper analyzes the geometric properties of the unitary group PU(D) using specialized 'cliff metrics' to study quantum circuit complexity. It derives a novel lower bound for the infinite-cliff diameter, showing it scales with an exponent of $4/3$. This result is significant because it disproves an existing conjecture (Brown's exponent-one conjecture) and provides a concrete lower bound for quantum circuit depth.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a significant theoretical lower bound on the complexity of quantum circuits, directly impacting the understanding of quantum computational limits by improving upon previous conjectures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to establish lower bounds for the diameter of the unitary group PU(D) under various metrics, specifically comparing the 'infinite-cliff' distance to other bounds.

Main result. It proves that the infinite-cliff diameter has a lower bound scaling of order $D^{4/3}M^{2/3}$, which disproves Brown's exponent-one conjecture and yields a corresponding lower bound for fixed-error quantum circuits.

Method. The analysis employs advanced geometric techniques, including small-ball probability estimates on the Haar measure, Jacobi field comparisons, and Gaussian transfer techniques.

Model / system. The system is the unitary group PU(D), representing quantum evolution, analyzed using right-invariant cliff metrics ($d_Q$) and the infinite-cliff distance ($d_\infty$). The complexity is related to $n$ qubits, where $D=2n$.

Key observables. The normalized Haar measure of small balls ($\mu_D(B_{Q_D}([I],x\sqrt{Q_D}))$) and the diameter of the space ($ ext{diam}( ext{PU}(D), d_\infty)$).

Important parameters / regimes. The penalty parameter $Q$, the dimension $D=2n$, and the subspace dimension $M=3n+9inom{n}{2}$. The analysis is restricted to the window $M \ll Q \ll D^{8/3}M^{-4/3}$.

Assumptions / limitations. The results hold outside an $e^{-\Omega(D^2)}$ exceptional set, and the derivation relies on specific asymptotic conditions on the parameters $Q$ and $M$.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing small-ball estimates for the finite-cliff metric, then transferring these estimates to the infinite-cliff distance using comparison techniques, culminating in the derivation of the lower bound for the diameter that contradicts a known conjecture.

Abstract

Let $D=2^n$ and $M=3n+9\binom n2$. We study the right-invariant one-step-cliff metric $d_Q$ on $\operatorname{PU}(D)$, with unit penalty on Pauli weights one and two and penalty $Q$ on all higher weights. If $M/Q_D\to0$ and $MQ_D^{3/4}/D^2\to0$, then, for every fixed $00$ yields a Haar-typical lower bound of order $D^{4/3}M^{-2/3}$ for the corresponding infinite-cliff Carnot--Carathéodory distance, outside an $e^{-Ω(D^2)}$ exceptional set. The infinite-cliff diameter therefore has exponential lower rate at least $4/3$, disproving Brown's exponent-one conjecture. The same estimate gives a fixed-error no-ancilla two-qubit circuit lower bound of the same order.

Highlighted author(s): Nigel R. Cooper
Authors: Daniel Spasic-Mlacak, Qi Camm Huang, Wen Wei Ho, Nigel R. Cooper
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03833v1

Summary. This work develops the concept of the Projected Ensemble (PE) to probe topological order in FQH states beyond standard entanglement spectra. By analyzing states conditioned on measurement outcomes, the authors reveal a hidden, measurement-dependent hierarchy in the entanglement structure. This provides a sharp, new diagnostic tool confirming topological order even in realistic Coulomb-interacting systems.

Why it may be interesting. The concept of 'unravelling' entanglement via measurement outcomes provides a powerful, non-equilibrium probe into the topological order of strongly correlated electron systems, offering diagnostics beyond standard ground-state entanglement measures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To uncover a finer universal structure within the entanglement spectra of fractional quantum Hall (FQH) states, going beyond what the standard entanglement spectrum can detect.

Main result. The projected ensemble (PE) exhibits a hidden hierarchy, leading to a measurement-resolved Li-Haldane conjecture where conditional subspaces are governed by CFT counting.

Method. The study analyzes the Projected Ensemble (PE), defined by conditioning quantum states on measurement outcomes of a subsystem's complement, which unravels the standard Reduced Density Matrix (RDM).

Model / system. The focus is on Fractional Quantum Hall (FQH) states, specifically the non-Abelian Moore-Read state and the $ u=5/2$ Coulomb ground state, analyzed on a sphere bipartitioned into two subsystems.

Key observables. Entanglement spectra, Projected Ensemble (PE), conditional reduced density matrices (CRDM), and the rank of the conditional subspaces.

Important parameters / regimes. Filling factor ($ u$), measurement outcomes ($n_B$), and the topological sector (e.g., $\Delta_{LA}=2$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the assumption that the relevant information for the rank is encoded in a subset of measured degrees of freedom, and that the structure persists under coherent deformations (rank rigidity).

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the PE structure, showing rank collapse upon measurement conditioning, and compare the geometric organization of conditional subspaces between the idealized Moore-Read model and the realistic Coulomb ground state.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the limitations of the standard entanglement spectrum to introducing the PE, formulating the measurement-resolved conjecture, demonstrating rank collapse, and finally showing that this structure persists for realistic Coulomb interactions.

Abstract

The entanglement spectra of fractional quantum Hall states contain universal fingerprints of their underlying topological order, as posited by the Li-Haldane conjecture. In this work, we uncover a finer universal structure within the entanglement spectra unravelled by projective measurements. Concretely, we study the projected ensemble of fractional quantum Hall states, defined as the collection of quantum states on a subsystem conditioned on measurement outcomes of its complement. We find that this ensemble exhibits a hidden hierarchy inside the Li-Haldane edge manifold: by conditioning on measurement outcomes, the entanglement spectrum's support is split into measurement-dependent sectors whose ranks we demonstrate are fixed by conformal field theory counting, an observation we dub the measurement-resolved Li-Haldane conjecture. For the non-Abelian Moore-Read state, this hierarchy is particularly rich: each parity-resolved edge manifold contains internal subspaces whose dimensions reproduce the conformal field theory counting of the opposite-parity sector. This structure persists even in realistic Coulomb-interacting ground states, establishing the projected ensemble as a sharp new probe of topological order beyond what the entanglement spectrum alone can detect.

Summary. This paper reports on the coherent coupling of excitons from $ ext{WS}_2$ and quasi-2D perovskites mediated by a cavity photon mode across a micrometer gap. This work establishes a platform for engineering novel light-matter functionalities in 2D materials.

Main problem. The research aims to demonstrate and characterize the coherent coupling between excitons from two distinct, spatially separated 2D materials ($ ext{WS}_2$ and quasi-2D perovskites) mediated by a single cavity photon mode.

Main result. The study successfully demonstrated the formation of three polariton branches, confirming coherent, photon-mediated interaction between the two excitonic systems over a micrometer separation distance.

Method. The work combines experimental measurements (white-light reflectivity and photoluminescence) with theoretical modeling using coupled harmonic oscillator and Wannier-Hopfield frameworks.

Model / system. The physical system is a hybrid heterostructure comprising a $ ext{WS}_2$ monolayer and a quasi-2D halide perovskite ($ ext{HaP}$) placed within an open optical microcavity. The system dynamics are modeled using coupled oscillator Hamiltonians.

Key observables. Polariton energy branches, coupling strengths ($g$), Hopfield coefficients, white-light reflectivity spectra, and photoluminescence (PL) intensity.

Important parameters / regimes. Separation distance ($\sim 1.5 \ \mu ext{m}$), coupling strengths ($g_{ ext{WS}_2} \approx 16-20 ext{ meV}$, $g_{ ext{HaP}} \approx 36 ext{ meV}$), and cavity detuning.

Abstract

The coherent coupling of cavity-confined photons and excitonic matter resonances leads to the formation of cavity polaritons, hybrid light-matter quasi-particles. If multiple exciton resonances couple to the same photonic mode, the resulting polariton constitutes a coherent interaction between matter resonances that can be spatially separated without any direct electronic coupling. In this work, we demonstrate the formation of such a coherent coupling at room temperature using an open optical cavity containing two distinct van der Waals materials - monolayer WS2 and layered quasi-2D halide perovskites (HaPs) - separated by $1.5 μ\rm m$. The system forms three polariton branches, with the middle branch possessing nearly equal fractions of both excitons and the photonic mode. White-light reflectivity and luminescence measurements are in good agreement with simulations using a coupled harmonic oscillator and a microscopic Wannier-Hopfield framework. Our results lay the foundation to combine highly complementary degrees of freedom in 2D materials in an in-situ tunable fashion to enable new polaritonic functionalities.

Real-space Floquet topology written by the orbital angular momentum of light

Authors: Mohammad Shafiei, Milorad V. Milošević

Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 3/5 · driven-dissipative phase transition 3/5 · interference shaping light 3/5 · Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 2/5 · methods for driven-dissipative 2/5 · non-equilibrium universality 2/5

Summary. This paper demonstrates that using structured light with Orbital Angular Momentum (OAM) allows for the direct, real-space writing of topological features in quantum materials like topological insulators. The topology forms a controllable annulus whose existence and geometry are independently tuned by the light's OAM number and helicity. This establishes OAM as a powerful control parameter for programmable topological landscapes.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete, optically controllable mechanism for engineering non-equilibrium topology in condensed matter systems, which is highly relevant for understanding light-matter interactions in topological materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To demonstrate that structured light carrying Orbital Angular Momentum (OAM) can write topological properties directly into real space within a material, moving beyond global control mechanisms.

Main result. The study shows that OAM enables the formation of a topological annulus in ultrathin topological insulators, with the topology being controllable by the light's helicity and OAM number.

Method. The analysis uses the leading Floquet–Magnus term to derive an effective Hamiltonian for a Dirac system driven by a circularly polarized Laguerre–Gaussian beam.

Model / system. The system is modeled using quasi-two-dimensional Dirac systems realized in ultrathin Bi2Se3 films, driven by a time- and space-dependent optical field characterized by OAM ($\ell$) and circular polarization ($\hat{\epsilon}$).

Key observables. Topological annulus (defined by two concentric chiral ring modes), radial Floquet mass sign changes, and vortex-core zero modes.

Important parameters / regimes. Orbital Angular Momentum quantum number ($\ell$), light helicity ($\hat{\epsilon}$), and the ratio of peak optical amplitude to critical amplitude ($A_{ ext{peak}}/A_c$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the off-resonant regime approximation ($\omega \gg \Delta_0$) and the use of the leading Floquet–Magnus term.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the formation and expansion of the topological annulus for different OAM values ($\ell$), show the dependence of the inner and outer ring radii on the driving field strength, and summarize the helicity-selective crossover frequencies.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the theoretical framework using the Floquet-Magnus expansion, analyzes the role of helicity in driving mass inversion versus gap opening, and then demonstrates how OAM independently controls the geometry of the resulting topological annulus.

Abstract

Floquet engineering usually treats light as a uniform control field that changes the topology of an entire driven material. Here we show that structured light carrying orbital angular momentum (OAM) enables a different regime, in which topology is written directly in real space. For ultrathin topological insulator films, circularly polarized Laguerre--Gaussian beams generate a radial Floquet mass whose sign changes define a topological annulus bounded by two concentric chiral ring modes. The transition is helicity selective: below a thickness-dependent critical frequency, left-circularly polarized light drives mass inversion, whereas right-circularly polarized light increases the gap and leaves the film trivial. Independently, the OAM quantum number shifts and reshapes the annulus without changing the frequency, intensity, or helicity. In the decoupled-surfaces limit, the same mechanism produces a purely Floquet-induced topological mass and a vortex-core zero mode. These results identify photon OAM as a control parameter for nonequilibrium topology and provide a route to programmable topological landscapes in quantum materials.

Summary. This paper introduces a powerful 'edge chain' method to calculate the topologically protected edge and corner states in 2D topological insulators. By treating the boundary layer independently, the authors demonstrate that these states can be found using advanced mathematical tools applied to the analytically continued Bloch Hamiltonian. This provides a systematic approach for analyzing boundary physics in complex quantum materials.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical framework developed for finding boundary states in non-Hermitian, quasi-periodic systems is highly relevant to understanding topological phases in open quantum systems or engineered photonic/acoustic lattices.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to determine the adiabatically protected edge and corner states in semi-infinite, two-dimensional topological insulators.

Main result. A novel 'edge chain' method is proposed, which successfully computes these boundary states by analyzing only the first layer of lattice sites, and the protection of these states is confirmed using Shemesh's theorem.

Method. The method involves analytically continuing the Bloch Hamiltonian to the complex plane, treating the boundary as an independent 'edge chain,' and applying conditions derived from the bulk structure.

Model / system. The study focuses on semi-infinite 2D topological insulators, using extensions of the Su-Schrieffer-Heeger (SSH) model, the Haldane model, and the Breathing Kagome Lattice as explicit examples.

Key observables. Edge and corner state wave functions, energy eigenvalues, and the dimension of the kernel space defining the boundary states.

Important parameters / regimes. Chiral symmetry, the complex wave number $z$, and the hopping parameters ($t_1, t_2, t_3$) defining the lattice structure.

Assumptions / limitations. The core assumption is that edge states can be found by considering only the first layer ('edge chain') independently of the bulk, and that the connecting sites can be set to zero amplitude.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the 2D SSH model, labeling the 'edge chain' sites, and show visualizations of the model with alternative unit cells used to find different types of boundary states.

Paper structure. The paper builds from establishing the edge chain ansatz, using Shemesh's theorem to prove protection, and then applying this framework to specific models (SSH3, Haldane, Kagome) using advanced techniques like para-Hermiticity and analyzing exceptional points.

Abstract

We show that it is possible to compute the adiabatically protected edge and corner states of semi-infinite, two-dimensional, topological insulators by considering the first layer of lattice sites, what we call the "edge chain," independently from the bulk. We start by accepting this claim as an ansatz, then, using the Shemesh theorem, we show that the edge states one finds using our procedure are adiabatically protected, provided we restrict ourselves to adiabatic evolutions that do not break chiral symmetry. We show explicit examples of our method in a 2D extension of the SSH model, the SSH3 model, the Haldane model and the Breathing Kagome Lattice.

Summary. This work develops a non-perturbative theory to calculate magnetic exchange couplings in half-filled bipartite magnetic lattices. By utilizing the Magnetic Force Theorem and circumventing issues related to zero-energy modes, the authors provide a universal analytical tool. Main problem. To establish a non-perturbative generalization of magnetic exchange rules in half-filled bipartite networks, overcoming the limitations of traditional perturbative frameworks.

Main result. The derived non-perturbative formula for magnetic coupling is universal, valid for any bipartite graph, and successfully bypasses the problematic zero-energy mode (ZEM) calculations.

Abstract

We break from perturbative frameworks to establish a non-perturbative generalization of magnetic exchange rules in half-filled bipartite networks. While historical models remain strictly tied to the debatable perturbation approach in the dilute limit and require the absence of zero-energy modes, our universal mechanism governs exchange interactions across arbitrary bipartite graphs. By bypassing traditional expansion bottlenecks, this framework provides an analytical predictive tool for magnetism in complex lattices, offering key design principles to engineer stable, long-range order in next-generation 2D spintronic devices

Summary. This work develops a weak-coupling mean-field theory to map out the complex phase diagram of doped and biased Bernal bilayer graphene. By treating the system's interactions and external fields rigorously, the authors predict the existence of multiple fractional metallic states. The results provide quantitative agreement with recent experiments, advancing the understanding of correlated electronic behavior in 2D materials.

Why it may be interesting. The study of correlated phases in graphene systems, especially those exhibiting fractional metallic states, touches upon strongly correlated electron physics, which shares theoretical tools with many-body physics relevant to AMO systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper theoretically investigates the many-body electronic phases, particularly fractional metallic states, that emerge in doped and electrically biased Bernal-stacked (AB) bilayer graphene.

Main result. The study predicts a rich phase diagram hosting several fractional-metal states, reproducing key experimental features, including the energy scale for the loss of fractional metallic order.

Method. A variational mean-field theory is employed, utilizing a parameter-free Random-Phase Approximation for the short-range electron-electron interaction.

Model / system. The physical system is AB bilayer graphene subjected to doping and an external bias field. The model incorporates kinetic energy, a screened short-range Coulomb repulsion, and a long-range capacitive potential dictated by the displacement field D.

Key observables. Single-particle gap, nature of electronic phases (e.g., quarter-metal, half-metal, fractional metal), and the free energy.

Important parameters / regimes. Doping level, applied bias voltage, and the experimentally controlled displacement field D.

Abstract

The paper presents a theoretical study of many-body electronic phases in doped and electrically biased Bernal-stacked (AB) bilayer graphene. We develop a variational mean-field theory with no fitted parameters. For the electron-electron interaction, we employ a parameter-free random-phase approximation to model the short-range screened Coulomb repulsion. The remaining long-range Coulomb potential energy is dictated by the geometry of the sample, behaving as a parallel-plate capacitor. We formulate the theory directly in terms of the experimentally controlled displacement field $\frak D$ rather than the interaction-renormalized interlayer potential difference. It makes our theory better suited for direct comparison with experiment. The resulting phase diagram is quite rich. It hosts several fractional-metal states connected by first- and second-order transitions. At higher bias and doping, three distinct fractional metallic states emerge. We classify these phases by the number of doped sectors and the symmetry of the order parameters. Our results - obtained without any parameter fitting - reproduce key qualitative and quantitative features of recent experiments. This includes the energy scale associated with the loss of fractional metallic order. At lower bias and doping, the model stabilizes a broad spectrum of fractional-metal phases with more exotic symmetry-breaking patterns. This suggests that further experimental exploration of the latter regime is warranted.

Summary. This theoretical study proposes a method to create and control a hierarchy of topological superconducting phases (FOTSC and SOTSC) in a heterostructure combining a QSHI and a p-wave magnet. Abstract

A theoretical framework is proposed to engineer both first and second-order topological superconducting phases in a two-dimensional (2D) heterostructure, consisting of a quantum spin Hall insulator (QSHI) and an unconventional $p$-wave magnet in presence of proximity-induced $s$-wave superconducting pairing. Our analysis establishes that the transitions between the trivial and topological superconducting (TSC) phases can be regulated though the parameters of $p$-wave magnet. Presence of chiral symmetry leads to the characterization of both types of TSC phases by the respective invariants, one-dimensional winding number and quadrupolar winding number. These results are supplemented by an analytical effective low-energy edge theory that yields a deeper insight into the emergence of the different topological phases of the system. Bulk pairing analysis reveals the competition between the effective $(p_x+p_y)$ and $(p_x+ip_y)$ type pairings that are governed by the intrinsic spin-orbit coupling inherited to the QSHI and spin-split bands of the $p$-wave magnet, respectively.

Hydrodynamics of two-dimensional electrons due to scattering by disorder

Summary. This work develops a comprehensive theory for magnetotransport in 2D electron systems by combining memory effects from localized disorder scattering with hydrodynamic viscosity. The model successfully explains the observed giant negative magnetoresistance in high-quality quantum wells, distinguishing between narrow and wide sample regimes based on the relative dominance of these two physical mechanisms.

Why it may be interesting. The detailed modeling of non-Markovian transport and the explicit separation of memory and hydrodynamic contributions provide a rich framework for understanding transport in disordered quantum systems, which is relevant to open quantum systems dynamics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the puzzle of observing hydrodynamic-like transport signatures, such as giant negative magnetoresistance, in 2D electron systems even when electron-electron scattering is too weak to cause them conventionally.

Main result. The developed theory successfully explains the observed giant negative magnetoresistance by modeling the mixed transport regime involving both memory effects from localized defects and unconventional viscosity from bulk/edge scattering.

Method. The authors developed a theoretical model using phenomenological balance transport equations for two types of electrons (circling and traveling) and solved them in the stationary regime.

Model / system. The system is two-dimensional electron gas in high-quality nanostructures, such as GaAs quantum wells, subjected to weak disorder. The model incorporates memory effects and hydrodynamic viscosity arising from scattering mechanisms.

Key observables. Magnetoresistance ($ ho_{xx}(B)$), exhibiting characteristic sharp peaks (narrow samples) or blunt maxima (wide samples) at zero magnetic field.

Important parameters / regimes. Sample width ($W$), magnetic field ($B$), temperature ($T$), and characteristic scattering/transition lengths ($l_{tc}, l_G$).

Assumptions / limitations. The model assumes the existence of two electron populations (c- and t-electrons) and uses approximations like the gradual channel approximation and stationary flow conditions.

Figures summary. Figures compare the model's predictions for $ ho_{xx}$ vs. $B$ (showing characteristic shapes) against experimental measurements from GaAs quantum wells, demonstrating agreement in both narrow and wide sample limits.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theory by formulating balance equations for electron densities and flows, deriving analytical expressions for $ ho_{xx}$ in different sample width limits, and finally comparing these theoretical predictions across various regimes (low/high B, narrow/wide W) against experimental data.

Abstract

The hydrodynamic regime of electron transport, induced by fast inter-electron collisions, was discovered in high-quality nanostructures in recent ten years. However, signs of hydrodynamic transport, primarily, the giant negative magnetoresistance, were observed even at very low temperatures, when electron-electron scattering is too weak to affect the transport. To address this puzzle, here we develop a theory of mixed, hydrodynamic and non-Markovian, magnetotransport of two-dimensional electrons at zero temperature in samples with weak but still important disorder. Namely, we account for both the memory effects at electron scattering by localized defects in magnetic field and an unconventional viscosity effect due to electron scattering by defects in bulk and by rough sample edges. Solution of the model yields a strong negative magnetoresistance, which exhibits at zero magnetic field a sharp maximum in narrower samples or a blunt maximum in wider samples. Abstract

Zigzag-edged graphene nanoribbons (ZGNRs) host large magnetic moments at two zigzag edges due to spontaneous magnetism, whose ground state is antiferromagnetically coupled spin-order edge state (AF state). In this paper, the spontaneous magnetism and thermoelectricity of ZGNRs with periodic substituted doping of boron and nitrogen atoms along the axial direction are investigated by first principle calculation. The doping induces an internal electric field, which modulates the magnetic moments at the two zigzag edges and the band structure with spin splitting. For varying doping configuration, the ground state could be AF state, ferromagnetically coupled spin-order edge state (FM state), or degenerated pair of AF and FM states. External transversal static electric field can further modulate the magnetic moments and the band structure. By designing the doping configuration and the external field, pure thermal spin current with the absence of thermal charge current can be generated at room temperature, which can be switched on and off by flipping the magnetic configuration between AF and FM states. Thus, the doped ZGNRs can be applied as prototype devices for spin-caloritronic.

Abstract

A copper-based metal-organic framework, Cu(Im)2, was synthesized using a sol-gel process and subsequently melt-quenched into glass upon heating above 240 °C. In this paper, we present a multimodal, multiscale interrogation of the MOF nanocrystals and the resulting glasses. Structural characterization using X-ray diffraction, atomic force microscopy, and electron microscopy was performed to understand the morphology and size of the synthesized nanocrystals and glasses. Thermogravimetric analysis and differential scanning calorimetry were employed to understand the melting process of the crystals to form the glass. Synchrotron pair distribution function analysis was performed to verify that the framework structure is maintained upon melting, and nearfield infrared nanospectroscopy provided insight into the local chemical structure of the materials. The mechanical characterization via nanoindentation revealed that the resulting glasses exhibit an appreciably high elastic modulus (~10 GPa) and the highest fracture toughness (K_1c ~ 0.5 MPa m^1/2) yet reported for MOF glasses. This development is central to the emerging field of MOF-based materials processing and shaping, while upholding mechanical robustness and resistance to cracking.
