Summary. This work develops a rigorous theoretical framework using boundary value problems to analyze nonlinear microwave propagation in superconducting HKI transmission lines. By retaining full spatial field dependence, the authors predict strong directionality (diode effect) and complex spectral features like bistability and anti-resonance shifts purely from structural asymmetry. This provides a versatile tool for designing next-generation nonlinear superconducting microwave components.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on superconducting circuits, the methodology—developing a robust BVP approach for strongly nonlinear wave equations with spatial dependence—is highly relevant to modeling non-linear dynamics in open quantum systems or photonic structures where boundary conditions are critical.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To develop a theoretical framework for nonlinear wave propagation in High-Kinetic-Inductance (HKI) transmission lines that remains accurate even when spatial field variations are strong, overcoming the limitations of the slowly varying envelope approximation.

Main result. The authors demonstrated power-dependent resonance shifts, bistable solutions, and significant direction-dependent transport (diode effect) in superconducting microwave resonators without external magnetic biasing fields.

Method. The problem is formulated as a boundary value problem (BVP), allowing for the retention of full spatial field dependence, which is solved using advanced numerical techniques like the shooting method.

Model / system. The system involves nonlinear HKI superconducting microwave resonators modeled by incorporating a nonlinear inductance per unit length into the telegrapher's equations. The analysis extends to multi-segment and stub geometries.

Key observables. Power-dependent resonance shifts, bistable transmission solutions, direction-dependent transport contrast (up to 93%), and Duffing-like spectral distortions in anti-resonances.

Important parameters / regimes. The nonlinearity is governed by the kinetic inductance term; key regimes include moderate power ($P \ll Z I_*^2$) and structural asymmetry between impedance segments ($Z_1 eq Z_2$).

Assumptions / limitations. The initial analysis uses a harmonic balance ansatz, assuming higher harmonics are negligible, although the full BVP approach mitigates this limitation.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate schematic layouts of nonlinear resonators and stub geometries; spectral plots compare $S_{21}$ vs $S_{12}$ to show directionality, and power response curves demonstrate bistability.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the necessity of a full spatial boundary value problem over envelope approximations. It then applies this framework to analyze two-port scattering (showing diode effects) and extends it to multi-segment and stub geometries to explore complex nonlinear interference patterns.

Abstract

High-kinetic-inductance (HKI) transmission lines provide a promising platform for compact nonlinear superconducting microwave devices. Existing theoretical descriptions are typically based on the slowly varying envelope approximation, which becomes inadequate for strongly nonlinear resonant structures with pronounced spatial field variations. Here, we develop a theoretical framework for single-frequency nonlinear wave propagation in HKI transmission lines by formulating the problem as a boundary value problem that retains the full spatial dependence of the electromagnetic fields. Applying the method to resonant transmission-line geometries, we demonstrate power-dependent resonance shifts, bistable transmission solutions, and strongly direction-dependent transport arising from asymmetric impedance barriers, yielding transmission contrasts of up to $93\%$ without magnetic bias fields. The framework is further extended to a three-port stub geometry, where nonlinear interference produces shifted anti-resonances and Duffing-like spectral distortions. Our approach provides a versatile tool for the analysis and design of strongly nonlinear superconducting microwave devices.

Why it may be interesting. Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates nonlinear Meissner states, vortex sheets, and laminar structures that emerge in extreme Type-II superconductors.

Main result. It derives exact solutions showing that the superconducting state transitions through soliton-like vortex sheets into periodic laminar mixed states as the applied field increases.

Method. The analysis uses a nonlinear velocity theory derived from Ginzburg-Landau theory, focusing on solving the resulting one-dimensional transverse free energy functional.

Model / system. The system is extreme Type-II superconductors ($\kappa o \infty$), modeled using a nonlinear velocity theory where the free energy depends only on the superfluid velocity $v$. The governing equation simplifies to Ampère's law in terms of $v$ and the magnetic induction $b$.

Key observables. Nonlinear Meissner states, vortex sheets (solitons), laminar structures (periodic solutions), and the thermodynamic critical field ($h_c$).

Important parameters / regimes. The penetration depth parameter $\kappa$, applied external magnetic field $h$, and characteristic fields like $h_{c1}$ and $h_c$.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the profiles ($v, b, ho$) associated with the soliton solution and its truncation defining Meissner states.

Paper structure. The paper develops the nonlinear theory by analyzing the free energy functional, deriving the governing differential equation, and then solving for specific structures: the Meissner state (half-soliton), the vortex sheet (static soliton), and periodic solutions representing laminar lattices.

Abstract

A recently derived nonlinear velocity theory of extreme type-II superconductors is shown to possess an exactly solvable one-dimensional sector. Its solutions include nonlinear Meissner states exhibiting universal tails, vortex sheets, and periodic laminar structures. The thermodynamic critical field emerges from a marginal Meissner profile equivalent to the normal-superconducting boundary and may be viewed as a half-soliton. The vortex sheet is a soliton characterized by a discontinuity of the velocity field, a continuous and localized magnetic field and vanishing superconducting density at its center, and may be interpreted as the coarse-grained limit of a dense row of Abrikosov vortices. Periodic solutions describe laminar states that may be interpreted as coarse-grained rectangular vortex lattices.

Abstract

Hybrid heterostructures combining two-dimensional semiconductors with ferroelectric materials offer a versatile route to actively control light-matter interactions at the nanoscale. Here, we report all-optical, light-induced domain-selective control of second-harmonic generation (SHG) in monolayer molybdenum disulfide (MoS$_2$) integrated with periodically poled lithium niobate (LiNbO$_3$). Spatially resolved SHG imaging reveals a pronounced modulation of the nonlinear optical response of monolayer MoS$_2$ governed by the ferroelectric domain pattern of the underlying substrate. A strong SHG contrast is observed between domains of opposite polarization, with a marked dependence on both the excitation wavelength and the incident optical power. The comparison between ferroelectric domains that either enable or do not exhibit light-driven photodoping in the MoS$_2$ monolayer provides a direct assessment of the role of carrier density in the nonlinear optical response. We find that ferroelectric-polarization-controlled photodoping at the MoS$_2$/LiNbO$_3$ interface enhances the effective second-order susceptibility, $χ^2$, producing an increase in SHG intensity of up to ~70% under resonant excitation conditions. Ab initio calculations corroborate that charge doping modifies the electronic band structure of MoS$_2$ and strongly affects $χ^2$ in the resonant regime, providing microscopic support for the experimentally observed modulation. The results highlight the combination of light intensity and ferroelectricity as a powerful knob for band-structure modulation in 2D materials and reconfigurable nonlinear optical responses, opening pathways toward programmable frequency conversion, smart light modulators, and advanced nonlinear photonic functionalities in integrated hybrid platforms.

Abstract

Motivated by the experimental observation of electronic Poiseuille flow in graphene [J.A. Sulpizio et al. Nature 576, 75 (2019)] we analyze the linear stability of plane-Poiseuille flow of electrons in a two-dimensional material using a modified Orr-Sommerfeld equation. We predict the streamwise wavenumber and frequency at which the instability occurs and discuss the difficulties of an experiment aimed at probing this phenomenon.

Abstract

Abstract

Advancing the understanding of electron-electron interactions in one-dimensional systems remains one of the central challenges in low-dimensional physics, especially for Coulomb-coupled Tomonaga-Luttinger liquids. Utilizing vertically coupled GaAs-AlGaAs quantum wires, we fully characterize the one-dimensional parameters through magnetic depopulation. Coulomb drag exhibits a systematic evolution with magnetic field, reflecting the successive depopulation of 1D subbands and the suppression of disorder effects. Summary. This theoretical paper demonstrates that exciton properties are highly sensitive to the quantum geometry of Bloch states, especially near topological phase transitions. By analyzing a Lieb-lattice model, the authors show that the momentum dependence of electron and hole wavefunctions suppresses Coulomb interactions, which in turn enlarges the real-space exciton radius. This effect provides an experimentally accessible signature—an enhanced diamagnetic response—that relies on quantum geometry rather than just semiclassical parameters.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete mechanism linking topological band structure features (like Berry curvature) directly to measurable optical properties (exciton size), offering an advanced route for material design beyond simple effective mass theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how quantum geometry, specifically near a topological band-gap closing, fundamentally alters exciton properties like their size.

Main result. Suppression of the electron-hole Coulomb matrix elements due to momentum-dependent Bloch states leads to a narrowing in momentum space and a measurable enlargement of the exciton radius in real space.

Method. The authors solve the band-projected Wannier equation using a Lieb-lattice model, comparing results obtained with full quantum geometry effects versus simplified unity-overlap approximations.

Model / system. The system is modeled using a spin–orbit-coupled Lieb-lattice Hamiltonian tuned toward a topological phase transition. Excitons are treated as fundamental excitations whose properties depend on the Bloch states of the underlying electron and hole bands.

Key observables. Exciton radius ($\xi$), weak-field diamagnetic response, projected Coulomb matrix elements ($|\Lambda_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}'}|$), and the Hilbert–Schmidt quantum distance.

Important parameters / regimes. Topological phase transition point (band-gap closing), spin-orbit coupling strength ($\lambda_{SOC}$), and momentum separation ($q$).

Paper structure. The paper systematically develops the theoretical framework by defining the projected relative-position operator using covariant derivatives. It then applies this to a Lieb lattice, showing how topological transitions modify the quantum metric and subsequently alter exciton size and diamagnetic response compared to semiclassical limits.

Abstract

Exciton engineering traditionally focuses on modifying semiclassical material properties, such as the effective mass and dielectric screening, while largely overlooking the quantum geometry of the underlying electron and hole Bloch states. This approximation is adequate for many materials but breaks down near a topological band-gap closing, where the quantum metric around the band extrema becomes strongly enhanced. In this regime, Bloch states at different momenta become less similar, reducing the projected electron--hole Coulomb matrix elements and consequently weakening exciton binding. We demonstrate this mechanism in a spin--orbit-coupled Lieb-lattice model tuned toward a topological phase transition. The suppressed Coulomb matrix elements narrow the exciton wavefunction in momentum space, leading to an enlarged exciton radius in real space. This increase in exciton size produces an experimentally accessible enhancement of the weak-field diamagnetic response. Our results show that quantum geometry can fundamentally reshape exciton properties near a topological phase transition, revealing a previously underexplored route for engineering excitonic states.

Abstract

These results identify the quantum metric as the key geometric quantity controlling orbital Hall transport in degenerate multiband systems.

Abstract
