Displacement-field-driven reconstruction of low energy transport in few-layer PtSe2

Summary. This work demonstrates that applying an external perpendicular electric field can fundamentally reconstruct the electronic band structure in few-layer PtSe2, particularly when the material is near a semiconductor-to-semimetal transition. By measuring transport properties, the authors show that this field causes gap closure and modifies valence-band conductivity. This establishes finite-density transport as a sensitive tool for probing field-driven electronic structure engineering beyond simple band-gap control.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on solid-state physics, the concept of external fields inducing fundamental changes in electronic band structure and transport properties is relevant to understanding engineered quantum materials and open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how a perpendicular displacement field modifies the low-energy electronic transport in few-layer semiconductors, focusing on the interplay between the field and interlayer hybridization.

Main result. The displacement field strongly reconstructs the low-energy band structure, causing gap suppression near charge neutrality and modifying valence-band conductivity in a thickness-dependent manner.

Method. The research combines electrical transport measurements on dual-gated devices with theoretical modeling using Wannier tight-binding calculations.

Model / system. The system is few-layer PtSe2, a layered semiconductor whose electronic structure is sensitive to thickness. The theoretical model uses a Wannier Hamiltonian incorporating an out-of-plane electric field term.

Key observables. Conductance (G), minimum current, normalized current vs. displacement field (D), and the evolution of the band gap.

Important parameters / regimes. Layer thickness (3L vs 6L), temperature (50 K to 300 K), and the magnitude of the perpendicular electric field (D).

Assumptions / limitations. The theoretical calculations utilize a fixed-relaxation-time approximation and neglect contact resistance or field-dependent disorder scattering.

Figures summary. Figures show device structures, temperature-dependent transfer/output characteristics, current maps, and theoretical conductance plots confirming field-induced band overlap and reconstruction.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the physical setup using device measurements, presents the core findings regarding field-induced transport changes in 6L PtSe2, and supports these observations with detailed Wannier model calculations.

Abstract

In layered semiconductors, a perpendicular displacement field generates an interlayer potential difference that competes with interlayer hybridization, modifying both the band gap and the finite-density electronic states that carry current. Resolving this interplay requires a material lying close to the semiconductor-to-semimetal transition, where moderate electric fields can strongly reshape the low-energy electronic structure. Here, we investigate displacement-field-driven transport in dual-gated semiconducting PtSe2, whose pronounced thickness-dependent electronic structure provides access to this low-band-gap regime. Unlike thinner layers, the displacement-field response is strong in six-layer PtSe2, which lies at the verge of the semiconductor-to-semimetal crossover with only a small residual transport gap. Even weak displacement fields rapidly suppress this residual gap near charge neutrality, driving the system toward a band-overlap regime. At the same time, the conductivity decreases in the heavily hole-doped regime, demonstrating that the displacement field modifies not only the gap but also the conducting valence-band states. Fixed-relaxation-time Wannier transport calculations reproduce both responses, showing that they originate from field-induced band overlap together with reconstruction of the valence-band dispersion. These results establish finite-density transport as a sensitive probe of displacement-field-driven electronic structure reconstruction and extend electrical control beyond conventional band-gap engineering.

Summary. This theoretical work analyzes how edge states in fractional topological insulators (FTIs) behave when they partially localize. By applying K-matrix formalism, the authors derive unique conductance signatures for three distinct localization channels. This provides a powerful experimental diagnostic tool, allowing researchers to identify the specific physical state of the FTI edge based on transport measurements.

Why it may be interesting. Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the transport properties of edges in fractional topological insulators (FTIs) when partial localization occurs.

Main result. The combination of measured edge conductance and the minimal quasiparticle charge provides a unique fingerprint to distinguish between three distinct partially localized edge phases.

Method. The authors use Wen's K matrix formalism to derive the reduced edge theory for different localization channels, calculating the resulting scattering matrices and conductances.

Model / system. The system is an FTI realized in van der Waals moiré materials, characterized by filling factors $\nu_{\text{FTI}} = 2(1\pm 1/n)$. The edge theory involves analyzing the coupling between integer and fractional bosonic fields.

Key observables. Edge conductances ($G$), minimal quasiparticle charge ($Q_m$), and the four-terminal conductance matrix ($G$).

Important parameters / regimes. Filling factor $\nu_{\text{FTI}}$, integer $n \geq 2$, and the specific localization channels (e.g., backscattering, two-electron tunneling, superconductivity).

Assumptions / limitations. Time-Reversal Symmetry (TRS) is preserved, and the analysis relies on the assumption of separate contactability for integer and fractional mode pairs.

Figures summary. The notes detail the derivation of scattering matrices ($S$) and conductance ($G$) for the three channels, showing distinct results for the four-terminal conductance matrices.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the general edge theory using K-matrices, to analyzing the conditions for partial localization, and finally calculating the specific conductances and fingerprints for the three identified channels.

Abstract

Recent experiments in van der Waals moiré materials have reported realizations of two-dimensional fractional topological insulators (FTIs). We study transport properties of FTI edges at filling factors $ν_{\text{FTI}} = 2(1\pm 1/n)$ with integer $n \geq 2$ (even or odd), each of which supports four counter-propagating edge modes. The edge modes can undergo partial localization, leaving two of the four edge modes conducting. We identify three distinct partial-localization channels and, for each channel, determine the reduced theory describing the remaining conducting modes, from which we obtain the minimal quasiparticle charge. We further explore transport properties of each partially localized phase and in particular determine the corresponding edge conductances. We demonstrate that measurements of the conductance together with the minimal quasiparticle charge provide a fingerprint that uniquely distinguishes the three partially localized phases.

Summary. This work investigates the mechanism of ferroelectric switching in $ ext{Zn}_{1-x} ext{Mg}_x ext{O}$ thin films, finding that the process is governed by nucleation rather than the lateral movement of domain walls. Using advanced microscopy and modeling, the authors demonstrate that domain walls exhibit negligible mobility, establishing a new, nucleation-dominated switching mechanism for wurtzite ferroelectrics.

Why it may be interesting. While the core physics is ferroelectrics, the rigorous, quantitative measurement of domain wall dynamics using advanced microscopy techniques (SO-PFM) and the comparison to theoretical models of phase transitions make it relevant to understanding non-equilibrium dynamics in condensed matter.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study compares the mechanisms of polarization reversal (switching) in wurtzite ferroelectrics ($ ext{Zn}_{1-x} ext{Mg}_x ext{O}$) versus classical perovskite ferroelectrics.

Main result. Polarization reversal in $ ext{Zn}_{1-x} ext{Mg}_x ext{O}$ is found to be dominated by the nucleation of vertically extended columnar filaments, as lateral domain wall mobility is negligible ($<10 ext{nm}$).

Method. The research employs advanced local characterization techniques, including scanning oscillator microscopy and point pulse-imaging, complemented by detailed simulations.

Model / system. The system is $ ext{Zn}_{1-x} ext{Mg}_x ext{O}$ thin films, which are wurtzite ferroelectrics. The analysis contrasts their switching behavior with that of perovskites, modeling the switching process as nucleation-controlled rather than growth-mediated.

Key observables. Local switching events, domain wall responses, lateral domain wall displacement, and the comparison of switching kinetics between wurtzite and perovskite systems.

Important parameters / regimes. Lateral domain wall mobility (shown to be sub 10 nm), coercive field, and grain size (mean size $\sim 10 ext{ nm}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The simulations often neglect elastic and depolarization effects, and the analysis of wall position relies on subtracting electrostatic contributions from the raw measurement signal.

Figures summary. Figures demonstrate that when lateral wall motion is disabled in simulations, the results match experimental observations of multiple unswitched grains, supporting the nucleation-dominated model.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the fundamental difference in switching mechanisms between perovskites (growth-dominated) and wurtzites. It then uses SO-PFM and pulse-probe methods to experimentally prove the lack of lateral wall movement in $ ext{Zn}_{1-x} ext{Mg}_x ext{O}$, culminating in simulations that confirm nucleation-limited switching.

Abstract

Polarization reversal in ferroelectrics arises from the coupled processes of domain nucleation and subsequent growth, yet the governing mechanisms differ fundamentally between classical perovskite oxides and emerging wurtzite ferroelectrics. While switching in perovskites is typically governed by mobile domain walls whose field-driven propagation dominates macroscopic kinetics, here we show that polarization reversal in wurtzite Zn1-xMgxO proceeds through a qualitatively different pathway. Using scanning oscillator microscopy, in combination with point pulse-imaging methods, we directly map local switching events and domain wall responses, revealing that domain walls in Zn1-xMgxO exhibit negligible lateral mobility (sub 10nm) and that polarization reversal proceeds predominantly through the nucleation of vertically extended columnar filaments with a lateral size on the order of the grains. This nucleation-controlled switching contrasts sharply with the growth-mediated dynamics characteristic of perovskite ferroelectrics and explains the abrupt, spatially localized switching behavior observed in wurtzite systems. These results establish nucleation-dominated filamentary reversal as a defining switching mechanism in Zn1-xMgxO and point towards the need for further studies to understand correlation lengths and nucleation processes across a range of grain sizes.

Summary. This work applies fidelity susceptibility to study the topological phase transition in the Su-Schrieffer-Heeger model augmented with a further neighbour hopping term. The divergence of $\chi_f$ at critical points confirms the phase transition, and the scaling analysis shows the fundamental physics remains consistent with the standard SSH model. This provides a robust diagnostic tool for characterizing quantum critical points in 1D topological systems.

Why it may be interesting. The use of fidelity susceptibility as a diagnostic tool for topological phase transitions is a powerful, non-local measure applicable to many quantum systems, making it relevant for understanding critical points in condensed matter models.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the topological phase transition in the Su-Schrieffer-Heeger (SSH) model when augmented with a further neighbour hopping term.

Main result. The fidelity susceptibility ($\chi_f$) is found to diverge at the phase transition points, and the scaling exponent obtained numerically agrees with that of the standard SSH model.

Method. The analysis uses the fidelity susceptibility, derived from the overlap matrix of single-particle eigenstates, to detect quantum critical points.

Model / system. The system is the SSH model, a 1D tight-binding model, extended by adding a further neighbour hopping term. The Hamiltonian is analyzed in both momentum space and for finite chains with open boundary conditions.

Key observables. Fidelity susceptibility ($\chi_f$), which is sensitive to parameter variations and diverges at phase transitions.

Important parameters / regimes. The control parameter is $\lambda = t/t'$, and the study explores the effect of staggered potentials ($\mu$) and the strength of the further neighbour hopping term ($t_0$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the non-interacting fermionic nature of the system, allowing the use of Slater determinants for calculating fidelity.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the energy spectrum under different perturbations (staggered potential, FN hopping) and show the variation of $\chi_f(\lambda)$ near the transition points, demonstrating divergence.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the use of fidelity susceptibility to locate phase transitions in the standard SSH model. It then extends the analysis to include a further neighbour hopping term, deriving analytic and numerical expressions for $\chi_f$ to confirm the transition points and analyze scaling behavior.

Abstract

In this study, topological phase transition in Su-Schrieffer-Heeger (SSH) model with a further neighbour hopping term has been studied in terms of fidelity susceptibility. Topological phase transition point in the standard SSH model has been identified before by noting the divergence of fidelity susceptibility. The same approach has been employed here where fidelity susceptibility is found to diverge at the phase transition points. Additionally, effect of staggered potential on the fidelity susceptibility has been explored. Analytic expression for fidelity susceptibility has been obtained along with its numerical estimation on finite chains by exact diagonalization. Fidelity susceptibility exhibits sharp peaks at the phase transition points in both approaches. Scaling exponent of this divergence has been obtained numerically which is found to agree to that of the standard SSH model without further neighbour terms.

Summary. This paper introduces iDART, a novel technique for Contact-Resonance AFM that overcomes limitations from excitation artifacts. By combining advanced interferometry with two-frequency tracking, the method achieves high sensitivity. Crucially, it demonstrates that the contact resonance frequency can be measured robustly using only thermal (Brownian) motion, enabling high-resolution imaging without perturbing the sample with large electrical biases.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical mechanics, the ability to extract a stable, low-noise signal (frequency) from a system where the primary measurement signal (amplitude/phase) vanishes due to external driving mimics the challenge of measuring weak quantum signals in the presence of strong classical noise or decoherence.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Conventional Contact-Resonance AFM methods are limited by excitation artifacts or the need for averaging incompatible with routine imaging.

Main result. The proposed iDART technique successfully enables resonance-enhanced, drive-free nanomechanical imaging by showing that contact resonance frequency contrast persists even when electrical driving signals vanish.

Method. The authors introduce Interferometric Dual-AC Resonance Tracking (iDART), combining quadrature-phase differential interferometry with two-frequency resonance tracking.

Model / system. The experiment uses a cantilever tip interacting with a sample surface to measure local mechanical properties via contact resonance. The measurement system employs interferometry and active electrical/photothermal excitation.

Key observables. Contact stiffness, dissipation, electromechanical response, amplitude contrast, phase contrast, and contact-resonance frequency.

Important parameters / regimes. AC bias amplitudes (ranging from 400 mV down to 0 mV), photothermal modulation powers, and separation between drive tones ($\Delta f$).

Assumptions / limitations. The frequency measurement is robust and determined by local dynamic boundary conditions, independent of the coherent excitation source.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the iDART setup using two drive tones and a reference tone. Key comparisons show that frequency maps remain stable at zero bias, while amplitude/phase maps vanish, confirming the robustness of the frequency measurement.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the limitations of existing CR-AFM techniques, details the iDART methodology combining interferometry and dual-AC tracking, presents experimental results comparing driven vs. thermally driven measurements, and concludes by establishing the technique's practical utility.

Abstract

Contact-resonance atomic force microscopy (CR-AFM) provides nanoscale maps of contact stiffness, dissipation, and electromechanical response, but conventional piezoacoustic excitation can obscure the cantilever resonance with actuator and sample-holder modes. Pure Brownian excitation avoids this transfer-function background, yet its small amplitude generally requires averaging that is incompatible with routine imaging. We introduce interferometric dual-AC resonance tracking (iDART), which combines quadrature-phase differential interferometry with two-frequency resonance tracking. By positioning the interferometric spot near the displacement maximum of the first contact-resonance mode, the detector noise floor is reduced below the off-resonance thermal displacement of the cantilever. Brownian spectra identify the contact mode and define the tracking frequencies, while active electrical excitation provides the signal-to-noise ratio required for pixel-resolved imaging. Simultaneous low-frequency and resonant measurements show that both electrical and photothermal drive increases contrast in the amplitude and phase channels without appreciably shifting the contact resonance. These results establish a practical route to resonance-enhanced, drive free nanomechanical imaging at conventional AFM scan rates.

Abstract

Germanium hole spin qubits are a leading platform for semiconductor quantum computation due to their strong spin-orbit coupling, all-electrical operability, and absence of valley degeneracy. A central obstacle is charge noise, which couples to the qubit through the same spin-orbit interaction that enables fast electrical control. In this work, we propose a new operational mode of a three-hole quantum dot in a planar Ge/SiGe heterostructure, modeled within a four-band Luttinger-Kohn--Bir-Pikus framework: a spin-$p$-orbital (SpO) qubit encoded in the $p$-shell of the topmost hole. We characterize charge noise sweet spots in the parameter space of electrostatic confinement and magnetic field, and estimate that relaxation rates of the SpO qubit are comparable to those of spin qubits hosted in single holes. We then design and optimize an all-electrical Landau-Zener state-transfer protocol that induces logical qubit state transitions without microwave driving, and we show that the quadrupole-quadrupole Coulomb interaction between neighboring dots enables fast two-qubit entangling gates operated by adiabatic shuttling.

Summary. This paper theoretically investigates the electronic properties of topological insulators patterned with antidots. Abstract

We investigate the electronic properties of a two-dimensional topological insulator patterned with an array of circular holes. The band structures of the topological insulator superlattices are calculated using the Bernevig-Hughes-Zhang model, discretized with the finite element method. The band topology is studied using the Fukui-Hatsugai-Suzuki method, exploiting the fact that topological charge is conserved when a band gap closes and reopens. We find that when the holes are close to each other and the edge states overlap significantly, the material loses its topological character and becomes a trivial insulator. The results show that patterning topological insulators changes their properties, and this can be achieved with a feature size compatible with current lithographic techniques. This shows the potential to incorporate patterned topological insulators in future electronics.

Summary. This theoretical study analyzes the superconducting pairing mechanism in $\mathrm{FeTe}$ using a five-orbital tight-binding model and the FLEX approximation. It reveals a strong competition between extended-$s$-wave and $d_{x^2-y^2}$-wave pairing symmetries. The favored symmetry switches depending on the doping level, providing concrete gap structures for comparison with spectroscopic measurements.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on iron pnictides, the methodology of using fluctuation-exchange theory to map out competing pairing symmetries based on Fermi surface nesting is a general technique for understanding unconventional superconductivity in correlated electron systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the competing superconducting pairing symmetries (extended-$s$-wave vs. $d_{x^2-y^2}$-wave) in stoichiometric and doped $\mathrm{FeTe}$.

Main result. The dominant pairing symmetry is highly dependent on doping: extended-$s$-wave is favored near stoichiometry, while $d_{x^2-y^2}$-wave becomes leading upon electron doping.

Method. The pairing instabilities are determined by solving the linearized Eliashberg equation within the fluctuation-exchange (FLEX) approximation.

Model / system. The study uses an experimentally constrained five-orbital tight-binding model for the tetragonal 11-type chalcogenide $\mathrm{FeTe}$. Electronic correlations are treated via a local Hubbard-Kanamori interaction Hamiltonian.

Key observables. Pairing tendency ($\lambda$), gap function ($\Delta(\mathbf{k})$), and spin/charge susceptibilities ($\hat{\chi}_{\mathrm{C/S}}(\mathbf{q})$).

Important parameters / regimes. Doping level ($n$), interaction strength ($U$), and momentum transfer vectors $\mathbf{q}$ near $(\pi, 0)$, $(0, \pi)$, and $(\pi, \pi)$.

Assumptions / limitations. The model neglects interlayer hoppings along the z-direction, and the pairing is analyzed within the spin-singlet channel approximation.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the Fermi surface topology, spin susceptibility peaks related to nesting vectors, and the calculated gap functions for different doping regimes.

Abstract

The recent observation of superconductivity in stoichiometric $\mathrm{FeTe}$ raises the question of how pairing develops in this tetragonal 11-type chalcogenide once interstitial Fe is removed. We construct an experimentally constrained five-orbital tight-binding model from first-principles calculations and treat electronic correlations and pairing within the fluctuation-exchange approximation. The linearized Eliashberg equation yields competing extended-$s$- and $d_{x^2-y^2}$-wave spin-singlet pairing instabilities. Near stoichiometric filling, spin fluctuations near $(π,0)$ and $(0,π)$ in the unfolded one-Fe Brillouin zone connect the $Γ/M$ hole pockets with the $X/Y$ electron pockets and favor an extended-$s$ gap that changes sign between the hole and electron sheets. Upon electron doping, depletion of the hole pockets shifts the dominant scattering toward the $X$--$Y$ channel near $(π,π)$, making the $d_{x^2-y^2}$-wave state the leading instability, with nodal lines that avoid most of the Fermi surface. The relative strengths of the two pairing channels vary with filling and interaction strength as the dominant spin-fluctuation channel changes. Under matched interaction strength, temperature, and filling, the extended-$s$ eigenvalue is larger in $\mathrm{FeTe}$ than in $\mathrm{FeSe}$ throughout the range considered, while the $d_{x^2-y^2}$-wave eigenvalue is also generally larger, particularly under electron doping. These results give concrete gap structures against which spectroscopic measurements of stoichiometric $\mathrm{FeTe}$ can be compared.

Summary. This paper introduces a novel method to measure 'odd viscosity' in time-reversal-broken fluids by leveraging the mathematical structure of Parity-Time ($\mathscr{PT}$) symmetry. Abstract

Odd viscosity, the nondissipative part of the viscous response of a time-reversal-broken fluid, is notoriously difficult to measure precisely because it does no work. Here we show that parity-time ($\mathscr{PT}$) symmetry, familiar from non-Hermitian optics, converts this elusiveness into a measurement principle. The odd Navier-Stokes equations, that include the nonlinear inertial terms, are $\mathscr{PT}$-symmetric, follow from a Lagrangian, and linearize to a Schrödinger equation in which the odd viscosity plays the role of Planck's constant; potential vorticity obeys a generalized Ertel conservation law. A probe trapped in an odd liquid realizes a pair of oscillators coupled by odd friction, and supplying balanced loss and gain drives a twofold $\mathscr{PT}$ transition whose exceptional point and Rabi sidebands locate the odd viscosity with square-root-enhanced sensitivity. Upon quantization the spectrum is of Fock-Darwin form, and the dissipative pair exhibits a Liouvillian exceptional point separating linear from exponential heating. These results furnish mechanical, stochastic, and spectroscopic protocols for measuring odd transport coefficients in classical and quantum fluids.

Abstract

Abstract

Conventional simulation of current-driven magnetization relies on fine-step integration of the spin-transfer-torque Landau--Lifshitz--Gilbert equation, creating a computational bottleneck in parameter sweeps and control searches.
