Quantifying Multipartite Entanglement Based on unified entropy

Authors: Yan Hong, Tongtong Xu, Limin Gao
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.22684v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

Summary. This paper introduces a novel approach to quantifying multipartite entanglement using the unified $(q, s)$-entropy framework. It develops specific measures for $k$-nonseparability, proving they possess key desirable properties like monotonicity under LOCC. The work provides tighter analytical bounds for bipartite entanglement and establishes order relations between the proposed measures.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical framework connecting generalized entropy measures to quantifiable entanglement monotones is highly relevant for understanding resource theories in quantum information.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to develop and quantify measures for multipartite entanglement in quantum states.

Main result. It proposes and analyzes two new measures, $A_k^{q,s}( ho)$ and $G_k^{q,s}( ho)$, which are shown to satisfy desirable properties like faithfulness and local unitary invariance, and establishes their strong monotonicity under LOCC.

Method. The methodology centers on utilizing the unified $(q, s)$-entropy framework to construct entanglement measures for both bipartite and multipartite systems, extending results to mixed states via the convex roof construction.

Model / system. The study concerns general quantum states ($ ho$) in $N$-partite systems, applicable to both bipartite ($\mathcal{H}_A \otimes \mathcal{H}_B$) and multipartite settings. The analysis is general and does not specify a particular physical Hamiltonian or platform.

Key observables. Bipartite entanglement measure $E_{A|B}^{q,s}( ho)$, and multipartite $k$-nonseparability measures $A_k^{q,s}( ho)$ and $G_k^{q,s}( ho)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The parameters $q$ and $s$ defining the unified entropy, and the number of parties $k$.

Assumptions / limitations. The derivation of strong monotonicity relies on the properties (P1)-(P3) of the unified $(q, s)$-entropy and the use of the convex-roof extension for mixed states.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the unified $(q, s)$-entropy, then develops the bipartite entanglement measure $E_{A|B}^{q,s}( ho)$ with associated lower bounds, and finally generalizes these concepts to propose and analyze the multipartite $k$-nonseparability measures $A_k^{q,s}( ho)$ and $G_k^{q,s}( ho)$.

Abstract

In this paper, we investigate the characterization of multipartite entanglement based on the unified $(q,s)$-entropy framework. For bipartite quantum states, we propose an entanglement measure $E_{q,s}^{A|B}(ρ)$ based on unified entropy and derive several analytical lower bounds for it using local orthonormal observables. We demonstrate with concrete examples that our lower bounds provide tighter estimates of quantum entanglement than several existing analytical bounds. For multipartite quantum states, based on unified entropy, we propose two measures $A^k_{q,s}(ρ)$ and $G^k_{q,s}(ρ)$ for quantifying $k$-nonseparability, and prove that these measures satisfy several desirable properties, such as faithfulness, local unitary invariance, and convexity. Moreover, we rigorously prove that when the parameters $q$ and $s$ are in certain ranges, $A^k_{q,s}(ρ)$ and $G^k_{q,s}(ρ)$ also satisfy monotonicity and strong monotonicity. Furthermore, we establish the order relations of the measures $A^k_{q,s}(ρ)$ and $G^k_{q,s}(ρ)$ with respect to the parameters $q$ and $s$ for fixed quantum states. In addition, for fixed $q$ and $s$, we also give their order relations for any two pure states.

Quantifying Nonstabilizerness of Codeword-Stabilized Codes

Authors: Yuan Liu, Ke-Mi Xu
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.22017v1

Summary. This paper develops a quantitative theory to measure the 'nonstabilizerness' of quantum error-correcting codes, which quantifies their ability to support non-Clifford quantum gates. It achieves this by mapping the quantum resource to a classical combinatorial problem involving the collision structure of the code's codewords. The main result shows that the most 'magical' codes are those whose structure resembles Sidon sets, turning the search for quantum resources into a classical counting problem.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, purely mathematical tool (additive combinatorics) to quantify a fundamental resource ('magic') required for building fault-tolerant quantum computers, bridging quantum information theory and number theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops a quantitative theory for the 'nonstabilizerness' (or 'magic') carried by Codeword-Stabilized (CWS) codes, which is necessary because stabilizer codes cannot provide transversal non-Clifford gates for fault-tolerant quantum computation.

Main result. The nonstabilizerness is shown to be determined by classical additive combinatorics, specifically related to how codewords collide under translation. The maximum magic is achieved by Sidon sets, and the search for magic-rich codes is reduced to classical counting problems.

Method. The authors establish a dictionary relating the quantum nonstabilizerness (measured by the second-order stabilizer Rényi entropy, $M_2$) to the classical parallelogram energy $E^{(1)}(C)$ of the underlying binary code $C$.

Model / system. The system analyzed is the CWS code, defined by a graph $G$ and a classical binary code $C \subseteq \mathbb{F}_2^n$. The code state is a uniform superposition over the codewords.

Key observables. Nonstabilizerness ($M_2$), Parallelogram energy ($E^{(1)}(C)$), and the structure of the classical code $C$ (e.g., Sidon sets).

Important parameters / regimes. The number of logical qubits ($k$), the size of the code ($m=|C|$), and the structure of the graph $G$ (e.g., edge-chromatic number $\chi'(G)$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the development of a quantitative link between the quantum observable $M_2$ and the classical combinatorial energy $E^{(1)}(C)$.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the construction of the CWS code. Other figures/tables mentioned relate to comparing $M_2$ values for different code types (e.g., Sidon sets vs. affine codes).

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the problem of non-Clifford gates and the need for non-stabilizer codes. It then develops the key dictionary relating $M_2$ to classical energy. Subsequent sections derive bounds, show structural invariances (like coset closure), and apply the results to specific gate requirements.

Abstract

Fault-tolerant quantum computation requires non-Clifford gates, which stabilizer codes cannot supply transversally. Non-stabilizer codes are the natural place to look for them, yet no quantitative theory of the nonstabilizerness (or magic) carried by such a code has existed. We develop one for codeword-stabilized (CWS) codes and show that the key quantity is classical: a code's nonstabilizerness is fixed by how its codewords collide under translation, a question that belongs to additive combinatorics. We show that the most magical codes are exactly the Sidon sets whenever a Sidon set of the required size exists, whose pairwise differences are all distinct. No code carries more than twice its number of logical qubits of nonstabilizerness however large it is physically. Furthermore, the same reduction gives structural and operational results. Nonstabilizerness is unchanged by coset closure, which yields non-stabilizer codes with arbitrarily many logical qubits and constant nonstabilizerness as the number of logical qubits grows. A diagonal transversal gate with $k$ logic qubits that is non-Clifford on $t$ coordinates forces the code's nonstabilizerness to be at most $2(k-t)$; thus the nonstabilizerness also bounds the non-Clifford gates needed to build the code and the cost of classically simulating it. Finally, entire families become exactly computable, and we obtain closed-form values for the Kerdock codes. Together these results turn the search for magic-rich codes and transversal non-Clifford gates into classical counting problems, which can be approached with standard tools from additive combinatorics.

Quantifying the dimensionality of multiparticle entanglement via partition rank

Summary. This paper introduces the 'partition rank' as a generalized measure to quantify the dimensionality of genuine multipartite entanglement in quantum states. By relating this concept to the tensor rank of quantum states, the authors provide a powerful mathematical tool that extends beyond the limitations of two-particle entanglement measures. This advances the theoretical understanding of entanglement resource quantification for complex quantum systems.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a rigorous, mathematically grounded tool (partition rank) to quantify entanglement complexity in multi-particle systems, which is crucial for understanding resource theories in quantum information.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of quantifying the dimensionality of entanglement in multipartite quantum systems, a problem where existing methods are limited beyond two particles.

Main result. The authors introduce the 'partition rank' as a novel measure to characterize genuine multipartite entanglement dimensionality, establishing that it provides a lower bound on existing measures like the GME-dimension.

Method. The core method involves defining the partition rank based on the minimum number of product terms required for state decomposition, which is mathematically formulated using tensor rank and slice rank concepts.

Model / system. The analysis focuses on general pure and mixed multiparticle quantum states, often exemplified by three-qutrit systems, and relates the concept to tensor decomposition theory.

Key observables. Partition rank ($r_P$), Overlap $\Omega_k^2(\psi)$, and the Partition Rank Vector (PRV).

Important parameters / regimes. The number of particles (e.g., three-qutrit systems) and the local dimension of the subsystems.

Assumptions / limitations. The approach generalizes tools from the geometric measure of entanglement and relies on tensor decomposition theory, though numerical stability is noted as a limitation.

Paper structure. The paper develops the concept by first introducing the partition rank, then providing constructive methods and mathematical formulations using tensor ranks, and finally applying these tools to calculate overlaps and compare the new measure against established entanglement quantifiers.

Abstract

The usefulness of entanglement as a resource in quantum technologies increases for larger systems, that is, if more particles or higher-dimensional quantum systems are considered. Yet, the interplay between dimensionality and multiparticle entanglement is not well understood. Only for two-particle systems an unambiguous and coherent notion of entanglement dimensionality, based on the Schmidt decomposition, is known. We introduce a concept to characterize the entanglement dimensionality of multiparticle states based on decompositions of pure states into superpositions of states without genuine multiparticle entanglement. We provide constructive methods to characterize the resulting partition rank for pure and mixed states. This allows the identification of novel maximally correlated states as well as a discrete classification of quantum states under stochastic local operations and classical communication. From a mathematical perspective, our approach can be formulated in terms of the slice rank and partition rank of tensors and our results allow to characterize these by connecting them to a generalized injective tensor norm.

Summary. This paper explores using quantum circuits as sidecar modules within a classical neural network for decoding neural activity. Method. The authors employed a systematic ablation study, comparing various quantum-classical coupling strategies (e.g., frozen vs. backbone-gradient-trained projections) on a 31-class classification task.

Model / system. The system is a hybrid architecture coupling a classical ResNet-50 backbone to a 4-qubit quantum sidecar circuit. Training involves optimizing parameters under controlled conditions using noiseless statevector simulation.

Key observables. Top-1 accuracy, Linear CKA similarity, Inter/Intra cosine centroid ratio (R), and the latent star-topology mixing weight (pi_star).

Abstract

We study parameterized quantum circuits (PQCs) integrated as residual sidecar modules within a ResNet-50 backbone for 31-class neural population decoding---imagined handwriting classification from multi-neuron spike rasters. Under strictly controlled conditions (fixed data splits, seeds, and optimizer), we compare four model variants: baseline, quantum sidecar with frozen input projection, quantum sidecar with backbone-gradient-trained projection, and a measurement-guided variant that aligns angle encodings with circuit measurement outcomes. The backbone-gradient variant improves accuracy in 3/4 seeds (+0.19% mean, 95% CI [-1.10%, +1.48%]) and consistently reduces Linear CKA similarity to baseline features ($Δ=-0.025$, 4/4 seeds), indicating genuine structural reorganization of representations. A nine-variant ablation identifies simple shallow architectures as the most effective and reproducible configuration. Measurement-guided training consistently improves representation geometry without reducing accuracy. All results use noiseless statevector simulation on 4 qubits, a regime chosen to reflect the practical constraints of current near-term superconducting hardware; no quantum computational advantage over classical methods is claimed.

Abstract

We investigated the quantum size effect (QSE) in bimetallic Pt$_{1-x}$Pd$_x$ and Pt$_{1-x}$Ni$_x$ nanoparticles, using $^{195}$Pt nuclear magnetic resonance measurements. The temperature and size dependencies of the anomaly in the nuclear spin-lattice relaxation rate divided by temperature $1/T_1T$ in the Pt$_{1-x}$Pd$_x$ nanoparticles suggest similar electron states between Pt and Pd atoms and are well understood by the QSE. The temperature and composition variations of $1/T_1T$ and Knight shift reveal a systematic increase in the density of states and reduction of the characteristic energy scale $T^*$ with increasing Ni content, consistent with the Kubo gap $δ_{\mathrm{Kubo}}$. In contrast to Pt$_{1-x}$Cu$_x$ nanoparticles where the QSE is suppressed, the Pt$_{1-x}$Ni$_x$ nanoparticles exhibit clear signatures of quantum energy discretization. This discrepancy highlights the essential role of $d$-electrons in the manifestation of the QSE. Furthermore, analysis of the modified Korringa parameter $K(α)$ suggests enhanced ferromagnetic correlations with increasing Ni concentration, approaching a ferromagnetic quantum critical regime. These results provide experimental evidence that $d$-electron density of states plays a crucial role in the manifestation of the QSE in the nanoparticles formed by the metallic $d$-electron atoms.

Abstract

The \emph{Minimum Matching Problem} consists of finding an independent edge set of minimum weight $M_{\star}(G)$ in a given edge-weighted graph $G$. When $G$ is bipartite, this reduces to the \emph{Assignment Problem}. We consider a variant of this problem defined by taking the union of optimal matchings across various slightly modified versions of the base graph: $H_{\mathcal{J}}(G)=\bigcup_{U \in \mathcal{J}} M_{\star}(G_{U})$. We establish two families of results: (1) In two distinct settings for the Assignment Problem, we prove that the resulting graphs $H_{\mathcal{J}}$, as well as certain associated graphs $\bar{H}_{\mathcal{J}}$, are spanning trees on the relevant base graphs $G$ and $\bar{G}$. (2) In these same settings, assuming the edge weights are given by the $p$-th power of Euclidean distances for point configurations in the plane, we show that for $p=1$ the tree $H_{\mathcal{J}}$ is non-crossing (i.e., its planar embedding has no crossing edges), whereas, remarkably, for $p=2$ the associated tree $\bar{H}_{\mathcal{J}}$ is non-crossing. Finally, we introduce novel conjectures in Statistical Mechanics, to be explored in future work: in the Random Euclidean Assignment Problem (where points are i.i.d.\ on a planar domain), we conjecture that for $p=2$ the trees $\bar{H}_{\mathcal{J}}$ are asymptotically distributed as Uniform Spanning Trees with free and wired boundary conditions in the two respective settings. In particular, suitable paths on the tree in the second setting, and on its planar dual in the first setting, are asymptotically distributed as $\text{SLE}_κ$ with $κ=2$.

Summary. This paper provides a theoretical guarantee for compressing deep neural networks by showing that the required width to maintain accuracy scales only polylogarithmically with the inverse error budget, independent of the original width. It achieves this using a novel derivative-matching technique applied layer-wise in reverse order. The work establishes a fundamental complexity bound for deep function approximators.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical MLPs, the rigorous mathematical techniques involving functional approximation bounds, complexity scaling with dimension, and the distinction between fixed vs. parameterized systems are highly relevant to understanding the complexity of quantum state representations in neural networks.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To prove a uniform compressibility theorem for deep multilayer perceptrons (MLPs), demonstrating that a wide teacher network can be approximated by a much narrower network of the same depth.

Main result. The required retained width for approximation is polylogarithmic in the inverse of the error budget, $O((\log(1/\epsilon))^{d_{in}})$, and is strikingly independent of the original network width.

Method. The core technique involves a novel derivative-matching procedure that converts finite-order input-derivative matching into a uniform sup-norm bound on a bounded domain.

Model / system. The model is a deep multilayer perceptron defined by sequential applications of analytic activation functions ($\sigma$) and weight matrices ($W^{(\ell)}$). The analysis focuses on approximating a fixed, trained teacher network function.

Key observables. Retained width ($d'_\ell$), Approximation error ($\epsilon$), Effective input dimension ($d_{in}$)

Important parameters / regimes. Error budget ($\epsilon$), Input dimension ($d_{in}$), Depth ($L$)

Assumptions / limitations. The activations must be analytic (holomorphic extension required), and the analysis is restricted to approximating a fixed teacher network, not general families of networks.

Figures summary. One table summarizes per-seed diagnostics after backward compression, showing MSE, radius checks, and the first task-preserving dimension $D_k$ for several seeds.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theory by first establishing per-layer bounds using derivative matching, then extends this to a full deep-network compression via backward layer-wise composition, culminating in the main theorem bounding the final width.

Abstract

It has long been known that well-trained neural networks can be compressed very strongly without affecting their performance, an important phenomenon that remains poorly understood. We prove a uniform compressibility theorem for deep multilayer perceptrons with analytic activations. For a deep, wide fixed teacher network, there exists a narrow (same depth) network that approximately represents the same function as the original. The reachable compressed width is strikingly independent of the original width, but is $O((\log(1/\varepsilon))^{d_{in}})$, where $\varepsilon$ is the error budget and $d_{in}$ is the effective input dimension. Our construction involves a novel derivative-matching technique which is aware of the low-dimensional input, and a layer-wise reweighting that preserves the input-output mapping.

Abstract

Based on a many-body formulation, we derive an electron-phonon coupling including excitonic effects that preserves thermodynamic detailed balance between electronic and phononic scattering processes. We start from the microscopic electron-nucleus Hamiltonian, expand around the Born-Oppenheimer equilibrium geometry, and construct an effective action for the electronic and phononic propagators. From the same effective action, we derive both electronic and phononic self-energies in terms of a nonlocal vertex $\mathcal G^{\textrm{s}}$ including excitonic effects, which generalizes the usual local interaction vertex $g^{\textrm{s}}$. When electrons and phonons are well-defined quasiparticles in the screened-exchange approximation and $\mathcal G^{\textrm{s}}$ is taken in its static limit, both self-energies reduce to Fermi-golden-rule expressions containing the same $\mathcal G^{\textrm{s}}$, thereby ensuring detailed balance. As an application, we compute electronic and phononic linewidths in graphene and illustrate this common-vertex construction. We analyze the competition between the reduced scattering phase space induced by the screened-exchange band structure and the enhancement of the electron-phonon vertex due to excitonic effects, finding that the vertex enhancement can compensate for and overcome the phase-space reduction in both electronic and phononic linewidths.

Abstract
