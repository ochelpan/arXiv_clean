Satisfying Quantum Codes: Physics-Informed and Hardware-Aware Code Design with SAT Solvers

Authors: Ben DalFavero, William M. Watkins, Margarite L. LaBorde, Vincent Russo, Ethan Egger, Gregory Quiroz, Ryan LaRose
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.23460v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces a powerful, general framework for designing quantum error correction codes by reformulating the design problem as a Boolean Satisfiability (SAT) problem. By using state-of-the-art SAT solvers, the authors can computationally discover codes that respect specific physical symmetries or are tailored to realistic hardware noise profiles. This advances the practical feasibility of building fault-tolerant quantum computers.

Why it may be interesting. This is highly relevant as it provides a computational, systematic methodology for designing robust quantum hardware components (QEC codes) by mapping a complex physical problem onto a well-studied computational problem (SAT).

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of designing quantum error correction (QEC) codes, which is currently done manually without considering specific physical or hardware constraints.

Main result. They developed a general framework that formulates code design as a Boolean Satisfiability (SAT) problem, successfully designing novel, hardware-aware codes with lower error rates than state-of-the-art surface codes.

Method. The core method involves translating the code design problem (including constraints from physics or noise models) into a SAT problem and leveraging state-of-the-art SAT solvers to find solutions.

Model / system. The work focuses on quantum stabilizer codes, which are used to protect quantum information. The framework is general enough to incorporate physics-inspired symmetries, such as those derived from the Fermi-Hubbard model, and detailed noise models.

Key observables. Logical error rate, satisfiability probability (PSAT), and the ability to encode up to 100 physical qubits.

Important parameters / regimes. Code parameters $[[n, k, d]]$, code-design density $m/(n-k)$, and the number of physical qubits ($n$).

Assumptions / limitations. The general problem is proven to be NP-complete, meaning an efficient algorithm for all cases is unlikely, and the approach relies on the practical effectiveness of modern SAT solvers.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the code design framework overview, the phase transition in satisfiability probability vs. error parameter, and comparisons showing reduced logical error rates for novel codes under biased noise.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the framework, proves the NP-completeness of the problem, details the SAT formulation for code discovery and extension, and validates the method by applying it to physics-inspired and hardware-aware noise models.

Abstract

Although quantum error correction is widely believed to be necessary for impactful applications of quantum computers, the design of quantum error correction codes is largely done by hand, without respect to problem or hardware constraints. In this work, we present a highly general and flexible framework for the computational design of both physics-inspired and hardware-aware quantum codes. To do so, we formulate code design as a Boolean satisfiability (SAT) problem and show how to incorporate all required error correction criteria. We prove that code design is NP-complete, ruling out any efficient algorithm for designing codes in general. Nonetheless, we show that state-of-the-art SAT solvers are able to effectively find solutions for many practical problems. Notably, we are able to design physics-inspired codes with up to 100 physical qubits in minutes, and we design new hardware-aware codes for biased noise which have a lower logical error rate than state-of-the-art surface codes.

Sharp State-Independent Uncertainty Relations for Multipartite systems

Authors: Yiling Wang, Naihuan Jing
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.22656v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops a general theoretical framework to find state-independent uncertainty relations for complex multipartite quantum systems governed by a symmetry algebra. By decomposing the total variance using representation theory, the authors show that the minimum unavoidable uncertainty is dictated entirely by the system's underlying symmetry structure. This provides a powerful, symmetry-based constraint on quantum fluctuations.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, general mathematical tool derived from representation theory to constrain quantum fluctuations in complex, interacting many-body systems, offering insights beyond simple pairwise correlations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of determining state-independent uncertainty relations for multipartite quantum systems whose symmetry representation is reducible.

Main result. It establishes a general framework showing that the minimum total uncertainty is determined solely by the intrinsic symmetry structure, specifically by the lowest accessible irreducible symmetry sector.

Method. The authors employ representation theory, utilizing the isotypic decomposition of the system's Hilbert space to decompose the total variance into intrinsic sector fluctuations and intersector dispersion.

Model / system. The analysis is applied to multipartite quantum systems whose collective observables are generated by a compact semisimple Lie algebra (like $su(2)$ for spin systems). The system's state space is treated as a reducible tensor-product representation.

Key observables. Total generator variance ($\Delta^2_ ho(g)$), intrinsic sector uncertainty ($c(\lambda) = 2\langle\lambda, \delta angle$), and the sector weights ($p_\lambda$).

Important parameters / regimes. The symmetry algebra $\mathfrak g$, the set of highest weights $\Lambda(\mathcal{H})$, and the Weyl vector $\delta$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the existence of a compact semisimple Lie algebra and the validity of the isotypic decomposition theorem.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the symmetry-resolved structure of uncertainty, showing the exact separation into intrinsic fluctuations and intersector dispersion, and demonstrating how the lowest accessible sector sets the sharp uncertainty floor.

Paper structure. The paper builds from the general problem of state-independent uncertainty to deriving an exact variance decomposition based on symmetry sectors. It then derives the sharp lower bound by minimizing over the accessible sectors, confirming results for spin-1/2 and spin-1 systems.

Abstract

Uncertainty relations constrain the fluctuations of incompatible observables, but most familiar bounds depend on the quantum state. State-independent uncertainty relations instead ask how much fluctuation remains unavoidable for every quantum state. For observables generated by a continuous symmetry, sharp state-independent bounds are known when the symmetry representation is irreducible. Multipartite collective systems, however, generally appear as reducible tensor-product representations of a symmetry algebra, which raises the question of how to determine their total uncertainty. We resolve this problem for multipartite quantum systems with a compact semisimple symmetry algebra $\mathfrak g$. Exploiting the symmetry structure, we formulate a general framework for state-independent uncertainty based on representation theory. The total variance admits an exact decomposition into intrinsic fluctuations within irreducible sectors and a nonnegative dispersion between sectors. This yields the sharp state-independent bound for the total variance $Δ_ρ^2(\mathfrak g)$ on the multipartite Hilbert space $\mathcal H$ \[ \min_ρΔ_ρ^2(\mathfrak g) = \min_{λ\inΛ(\mathcal H)} 2\langleλ,δ\rangle, \] where $ρ$ is any density operator on $\mathcal H$, $Λ(\mathcal H)$ is the set of highest weights $λ$ labeling those sectors, and $δ$ is the Weyl vector. This demonstrates that the ultimate uncertainty is completely controlled by its intrinsic symmetry structure. As a special example, this result confirms our previous conjecture that the total uncertainty floor of collective spin-$1/2$ systems depends only on the parity of the particle number. We further illustrate the framework for multipartite spin-$1$ systems, demonstrating that the same symmetry-sector mechanism persists beyond spin-$1/2$.

Abstract

A first-principles description of noncollinear magnets is challenging, and the practical behavior of genuinely noncollinear exchange-correlation approximations is largely untested. Spin-current density-functional theory (SCDFT) admits noncollinear approximations constrained by local U(1)$\times$SU(2) gauge invariance, an exact condition unavailable in the more restrictive framework of spin-DFT (SDFT). Using the Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP), we compare two recently developed noncollinear SCDFT functionals, NCMSCAN and LFNCBR89-NCCS, against locally collinear SDFT extensions of four common functionals (PZ, PW92, PBE, and SCAN) for several noncollinear $d$-electron systems. Yet the two SCDFT functionals differ in equilibrium volumes, band gaps, and on-site magnetic moments. Overall, this survey illustrates the usefulness of SCDFT in capturing noncollinear physics with the tested approximations.

An Exactness Barrier for ZX-Calculus Optimization of Synthesized Clifford+T Circuits

Authors: Chon-Fai Kam, Anuradha Mahasinghe, Kaushika De Silva, Frédéric Cadet, Jingbo Wang
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.22801v1

Summary. This paper provides a unified theoretical framework to quantify the minimum number of T-gates required in a Clifford+T circuit, establishing an 'exactness barrier' using ZX-calculus. It resolves contradictions in circuit compilation by showing that both approximation-based and number-theoretic synthesis methods are fundamentally limited by this computable algebraic bound. This work is crucial for understanding the true compressibility of quantum circuits.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on quantum computation, the rigorous mathematical machinery developed—especially the use of algebraic invariants (the barrier) to constrain physical resources (T-gates)—provides powerful theoretical tools applicable to understanding resource limitations in complex quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the discrepancy between how different methods synthesize quantum circuits and how much those circuits can be optimized using ZX-calculus rewriting.

Main result. It establishes a single, computable 'exactness barrier' (a lower bound on T-count) that governs the optimization of Clifford+T circuits, showing that both Solovay-Kitaev and number-theoretic synthesis approaches are constrained by this bound.

Method. The authors use advanced algebraic techniques involving ZX-calculus rewriting, exact arithmetic in Z[sqrt(2)], and rigorous proofs to derive this lower bound.

Model / system. The focus is on Clifford+T quantum circuits, which are represented and manipulated using ZX diagrams. The optimization goal is minimizing the T-gate count.

Key observables. T-count, the 'exactness barrier' (a lower bound on T-count), and the compression ratio achieved by ZX rewriting.

Important parameters / regimes. Recursion depth (e.g., depth five), the denominator exponent of the synthesized ring element, and the number of qubits ($n \ge 2$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the optimization process preserves the implemented element exactly, and some results rely on calibrated ergodicity hypotheses.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the three tiers of ZX-calculus rewriting rules (fusion, complementation, teleportation) and show the passage from a circuit representation to a graph-like form and finally to a generalized flow.

Paper structure. The paper builds the argument by first analyzing local simplification (Tier 1, free-product reduction), then establishing the general exactness barrier (Theorem 3), and finally proving the saturation of this barrier for specific synthesis methods (Linkage Theorem for single-qubit words).

Abstract

Gate synthesis and circuit optimization are usually studied separately, and evidence on their interaction is contradictory: ZX-calculus rewriting removes a stable fraction of Solovay-Kitaev circuits, yet almost nothing from number-theoretically synthesized circuits. We show both behaviours follow from a single bound. For any optimizer that preserves the implemented element exactly--including all sound ZX rewriting with extraction--the achievable T-count is bounded below by the denominator exponent of the synthesized ring element. This exactness barrier is computable per instance and separates exact post-processing from approximation-aware resynthesis by a certified factor reaching 101x at recursion depth five. The two behaviours are then the barrier operating at different distances from the floor. For Solovay-Kitaev circuits we prove that the local ZX simplification layer (spider fusion and identity removal) computes exactly the free-product normal form of Z_2 * Z_8, giving exact per-instance compression and, under a calibrated ergodicity hypothesis, a depth-independent limit law confirmed on two independently constructed nets. For number-theoretically synthesized circuits the floor is already saturated: on single-qubit words automated ZX simplification attains it exactly, via a closed-form formula for minimal T-count in terms of phase linkage through the Z-axis normalizer. At two qubits and beyond the same valuation yields unconditional rigidity certificates, which on the quantum-Shannon-decomposition plus gridsynth pipeline certify 99.4-99.9% of the synthesized T-count as incompressible, with rigidity strengthening as accuracy tightens. This explains, and predicts the size of, the near-null optimization recently reported for that pipeline.

Abstract

Metallic environments are generally expected to suppress excitons through strong dielectric screening, yet their influence can differ in composite systems where metallic and insulating regions coexist. Here, we investigate excitonic states in PdxCu1-xCrO2 thin films across a percolation-driven metal-insulator transition. Optical spectroscopy and many-body GW calculations show that CuCrO2 hosts strongly bound excitons with a binding energy of about 489 meV. With increasing Pd substitution, the system approaches an insulator-to-metal transition near x = 0.5, consistent with the site-percolation threshold of a triangular lattice. Although this model provides a possible interpretation of the observed excitonic evolution, an alternative scenario in which metallic and excitonic responses originate from electronically distinct states and evolve independently cannot be excluded. These results reveal unusual metallic-excitonic coexistence near a percolation-driven metal-insulator transition and suggest nanoscale metallic proximity as a possible route for modifying excitonic interactions.

Asymptotic entanglement in circle stabilizer states and states forbidding arbitrary vertex-minors

Summary. This theoretical work establishes a deep link between the structural properties of quantum states (defined by forbidden vertex-minors) and their entanglement content. It proves that if a stabilizer state cannot contain a specific substructure, its entanglement is severely limited, growing only logarithmically with system size. The results are highly technical, relying on advanced concepts from matroid theory and graph theory, and are conditional on a major structural conjecture.

Why it may be interesting. The connection between forbidden substructures (vertex-minors) and the asymptotic scaling of entanglement is a fundamental structural result that constrains the complexity of quantum states achievable in physical devices.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the asymptotic entanglement properties of stabilizer states when those states are constrained by forbidding a specific substructure, known as a vertex-minor.

Main result. Assuming a structural conjecture, the entanglement (measured by distance and locally accessible information) of such constrained states grows only logarithmically with system size, contrasting with unconstrained states.

Method. The analysis connects entanglement measures to rank functions of multimatroids and uses graph theory (specifically 4-regular multigraphs) to establish structural bounds.

Model / system. The system under study is the set of stabilizer states, particularly 'circle stabilizer states,' which are associated with 4-regular multigraphs. The constraints are defined via the concept of a vertex-minor.

Key observables. Distance ($d$) and Locally Accessible Information ($\alpha_{ ext{loc}}$).

Important parameters / regimes. System size ($n$), the fixed forbidden vertex-minor ($\vert \phi angle$), and the rank of graph perturbations ($p$).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary results are conditional on Geelen’s weak structural conjecture regarding vertex-minors.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the contrast between entanglement in arbitrary vs. constrained stabilizer states, and show graph detachments related to rank function evaluations in 4-regular multigraphs.

Paper structure. The paper builds from general bounds on entanglement measures (Prop I.3), connects these to multimatroid rank functions, establishes specific asymptotic bounds for circle stabilizer states (Theorems E.4, E.5), and culminates by lifting these results to general proper vertex-minor-closed families via graph perturbation theory (Theorem F.10).

Abstract

Stabilizer states play a central role in quantum information theory, and understanding their entanglement has motivated a large body of work. A well-studied question in particular is when a stabilizer state $|ψ\rangle$ can be transformed into another stabilizer state $|φ\rangle$ using only single-qubit Clifford operations and Pauli measurements. If this is possible, we say that $|φ\rangle$ is a vertex-minor of $|ψ\rangle$. Assuming Geelen's weak structural conjecture on vertex-minors, we establish the following general statement. For any fixed stabilizer state $|φ\rangle$, the entanglement in stabilizer states $|ψ\rangle$ that do not contain $|φ\rangle$ as a vertex-minor is asymptotically constrained. More concretely, we show that the distance of any sufficiently rank-connected $|ψ\rangle$ not containing $|φ\rangle$ as a vertex-minor grows as $O(\log n)$, and prove similar results for the so-called locally accessible information, a quantity that captures the amount of information that can be learned through single-qubit Pauli measurements. Our results rely on (i) connecting the above two entanglement measures to rank functions of multimatroids, (ii) connecting the rank functions of circle stabilizer states to rank functions on $4$-regular multigraphs, which asymptotically constrains the entanglement of circle stabilizer states, and (iii) using Geelen's weak structural conjecture on vertex-minors to `lift' the previous result to sufficiently connected states in proper vertex-minor-closed families of stabilizer states. Our results establish a connection between asymptotic stabilizer entanglement and forbidden vertex-minors, with direct implications for the entanglement that can be generated in quantum devices.

Abstract

This paper addresses the structural breakdown of the Edgeworth expansion in the finite-blocklength (FBL) regime, where conventional asymptotic approximations yield unphysical negative probabilities in the deep-tail region. We propose a $q$-deformed framework that resolves this inconsistency by replacing additive polynomial perturbations with a geometric deformation of the information density space. Motivated by the linearization of nonlinear dynamics, we prove that dynamically scaling the $q$-logarithmic parameter exactly absorbs the third-order skewness while preserving global nonnegativity. We establish a universal asymptotic matching, demonstrating that the framework encapsulates higher-order asymptotic scales. Numerical results confirm that the proposed method matches the state-of-the-art precision of the Cornish-Fisher bound without the risk of negative probabilities. The framework offers a robust and computationally stable foundation for evaluating operational limits in ultra-reliable communications such as 6G and URLLC.

Abstract

In particle physics the virtual N$^3$LO contribution to gluon-fusion Higgs production in the Standard Model requires four-loop multi-scale QCD amplitudes that in dimensional regularization are dominated by singular integral reduction and difficult master-integral evaluations. In this note we show that a nonlocal UV completion makes each fixed-order four-loop Feynman diagram fully convergent in four dimensions while keeping the analytic structure needed for Lehmann-Symanzik-Zimmermann (LSZ) reduction formula. For gluon-fusion $gg\!\to\!H$ we derive a closed form Schwinger-parameter representation where all loop-momentum integrals are performed analytically and thus leaving finite parameter integrals viable for direct numerical evaluation without needing the usual UV subtractions. This method heps to isolate the collider-hard contribution as a convergent four-loop form factor. The strong coupling then is defined by a finite normalization condition in the entire-function regularization scheme and it is related to the $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ coupling by finite matching where after this matching, the regulator-dependent corrections decouple in the local limit $E_M\!\to\!\infty$.

Summary. This paper introduces qiskit-qudits, an extension allowing quantum circuits to operate on qudits of dimension $d$ up to 16. It achieves this by encoding qudits into qubits and employing a sophisticated gate synthesis strategy that balances efficiency (for power-of-two dimensions) with completeness (for arbitrary dimensions). This work is vital for advancing quantum simulation tools beyond the qubit paradigm.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on software implementation, the rigorous analysis of resource scaling (e.g., the massive overhead of the dense fallback vs. optimized cascades) provides crucial theoretical insights into the feasibility and resource requirements of simulating higher-dimensional quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of extending the Qiskit quantum computing framework to simulate circuits involving qudits (d-level systems) beyond standard qubits.

Main result. It successfully implements a qudit-aware circuit model that supports exact simulation for dimensions $2 \le d \le 16$ by combining transpiler-friendly qubit decompositions with dense unitary synthesis.

Method. The core method involves encoding qudits into multiple qubits and using a hybrid gate synthesis strategy: optimized qubit cascades when dimensions are powers of two, and exact dense unitary embedding otherwise.

Key observables. Resource costs (encoded qubits, CX count, depth) and the fidelity of gate decomposition against dense unitaries.

Important parameters / regimes. The qudit dimension $d$ (supported up to 16), the encoding width $m$, and the resulting total encoded Hilbert space dimension $D=2^N$.

Assumptions / limitations. The use of identity-padding to handle the mismatch between the logical qudit subspace and the physical encoded qubit space. The dense fallback path is computationally expensive and limited by memory.

Abstract

qiskit-qudits is a Qiskit extension that simulates d-level qudits by encoding each one into m = ceil(log_2 d) qubits. Qudit gates are exposed as ordinary Qiskit Gate and ControlledGate subclasses, and operations that are not unitary gates (measurement, reset, barrier, state preparation) as dedicated Instruction subclasses dispatched through a dedicated apply() hook; every gate carries both a dense encoded unitary (via NumPy's array protocol) and a qubit-level definition. Because d need not be a power of two, the encoded Hilbert space is generally larger than the logical one; the library resolves this by the identity-padding convention, in which every gate acts as the identity on the unphysical part of the encoded space. When every operand dimension is a power of two, gates decompose into a fixed, transpiler-recognisable cascade of standard qubit gates; otherwise the library falls back to exact dense unitary synthesis, so that dimensions 2 <= d <= 16 are supported exactly, not only powers of two. This paper describes the software's circuit model, gate hierarchy, decomposition strategy, and measurement/decoding machinery, states its limitations, and verifies the implementation numerically: the qudit QFT against the discrete Fourier transform for both power-of-two and non-power-of-two d, extending the check of the underlying theory paper, which could only be run for d = 2^m, and every gate's emitted decomposition against its dense unitary across the whole gate set.
