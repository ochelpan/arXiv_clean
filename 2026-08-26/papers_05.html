Summary. This paper generalizes the Bloch sphere concept to non-Hermitian quantum systems by utilizing the Spacetime Algebra. It shows that the state space evolves onto a future light cone, and the dynamics are classified into four geometric types corresponding to Lorentz transformations. This provides a deep geometric understanding of non-unitary evolution, linking it to concepts like exceptional points.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful geometric framework connecting non-Hermitian quantum dynamics to fundamental concepts from special relativity (Lorentz transformations), which is highly relevant for understanding open quantum systems and non-unitary evolution in quantum optics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to establish a geometric generalization of the Bloch sphere for two-level quantum systems when subjected to non-Hermitian Hamiltonians.

Main result. The state space expands from the standard unit 2-sphere to a future light cone, and the Hamiltonian dynamics are classified into four geometric regimes corresponding to Lorentz transformations.

Method. The analysis employs the Spacetime Algebra (STA) formulation, lifting the state density operator to the full algebra to analyze non-unitary time evolution.

Model / system. The system studied is a two-level quantum system (qubit) governed by a general non-Hermitian Hamiltonian, whose time evolution is non-unitary.

Key observables. The trajectory of the state vector (null vector) on the light cone, and the eigenvalues of the Hamiltonian in different regimes.

Important parameters / regimes. The relative parameters defining the Hamiltonian, such as $a^2$ vs $b^2$, which determine the geometric class of the dynamics.

Assumptions / limitations. The core assumption is that lifting the state representation to the full STA is necessary to correctly model non-Hermitian time evolution, which corresponds to Lorentz transformations.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the light cone geometry and the trajectories of the state vector under different types of non-Hermitian evolution, particularly highlighting the null rotation case.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the generalization of the Bloch sphere using STA, then analyzes the time evolution operator's action, classifying the dynamics into four geometric types (rotations, boosts, null rotations), and finally using this framework to study topological features like exceptional points.

Abstract

We establish a geometric generalization of the Bloch sphere for two-level quantum systems with non-Hermitian Hamiltonians using the Spacetime Algebra (STA) formulation. By lifting the state density operator from the even subalgebra to the full STA, we show that the state space expands from the unit 2-sphere to a future light cone. The non-unitary time evolution generated by a general non-Hermitian Hamiltonian corresponds to proper orthochronous Lorentz transformations on the null vectors. We classify the Hamiltonian dynamics into four distinct geometric classes: spatial rotations (corresponding to $\mathcal{PT}$-symmetric systems), pure boosts (anti-$\mathcal{PT}$-symmetric systems), null rotations (exceptional points), and general mixtures. We also use this formulation to study several results from PT-symmetric quantum mechanics, including the topological features of the exceptional points.

Spontaneous currents determine capillary rise in active matter

Summary. This work develops an active generalization of Jurin's law for capillary action in active fluids. It shows that the capillary rise is governed by a current-induced drag force, $f_d$, which is a non-equilibrium effect. Crucially, this active contribution makes the capillary rise dependent on the global geometry of the confining tube, not just its local diameter.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical active matter, the derivation of non-equilibrium force balances and the inclusion of current-induced non-local effects are highly relevant to understanding energy dissipation and boundary conditions in open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the capillary rise in active matter, which deviates significantly from the equilibrium Jurin's law due to the presence of self-propelled active particles.

Main result. The capillary rise is determined by the self-organized particle currents ($f_d$), leading to an active Jurin's law that depends on the global shape of the tube, not just its diameter.

Method. The authors derive the active force balance equation by integrating the momentum balance across the liquid-gas interface, leading to a generalized active Jurin's law.

Model / system. The system models active fluids composed of self-propelled particles confined within a tube, where particle dynamics are governed by Itô-Langevin equations.

Key observables. Capillary rise (H), active current drag ($f_d$), and the mechanical surface tension ($\gamma_{LG}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Tube shape (pointed vs. round tips), wall stiffness ($\epsilon_w$), and particle persistence length ($\ell_p$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on force balance equations and makes approximations regarding the bulk liquid/gas isotropy and the limits of system size.

Figures summary. Figures compare capillary rise dependence on global configuration (tip shape, number of tubes) and show how $H$ and $f_d$ vary with wall stiffness and contact angle.

Paper structure. The paper builds from the standard force balance equation to derive the active Jurin's law, analyzing the contributions from surface tension, gravity, and the current-induced drag term ($H_J$).

Abstract

The capillary rise of simple passive fluids in a tube is controlled by the force balance between surface tension and gravity --Jurin's law of capillary action. For fluids composed of active particles interacting via pairwise forces, mechanical surface tension is negative, but a capillary rise was nevertheless reported and remains unexplained. We establish the active form of Jurin's law from the microscopic dynamics. It includes a drag emerging from particle currents that we find responsible for capillary rise. These active currents, alongside negative surface tension, lead to complex and counterintuitive capillary action phenomena that are impossible in equilibrium. In particular the capillary rise of active fluids depends on the shape of the tube, not solely on the tube diameter.

Summary. This paper rigorously analyzes the anomalous diffusion of random walks on a 3D lattice where the axes are randomly oriented. It proves that in this critical dimension, the diffusion coefficient exhibits a specific superdiffusive divergence rate, confirming a long-standing conjecture. The methodology is highly technical, relying on advanced tools from functional analysis and stochastic processes applied to the resolvent operator.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical random walks, the rigorous mathematical techniques involving spectral analysis, operator bounds, and chaos expansions are highly relevant to analyzing transport and dynamics in complex quantum systems, such as disordered quantum Hamiltonians or open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper studies the superdiffusive behavior of random walks on a specific, randomly oriented lattice structure.

Main result. In the critical dimension $d=3$, the diffusion coefficient is shown to diverge in the Tauberian sense as $\sqrt{\log t}$ with a multiplicative correction involving $(\log\log t)^{\pm(2+\epsilon)}$.

Method. The analysis employs the resolvent method, utilizing a Rademacher chaos expansion to estimate the Laplace transform of the mean squared displacement.

Model / system. The model is a continuous-time random walk on the three-dimensional randomly oriented Manhattan lattice ($\mathbb{Z}^d$). The environment is random, assigning independent directions (forward/backward) to each axis-aligned line.

Key observables. Diffusion coefficient, Mean squared displacement $E[|X_t|^2]$, and the Laplace transform of the mean squared displacement $D(\lambda)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The critical dimension $d=3$, and the small parameter $\lambda$ used in the resolvent analysis.

Assumptions / limitations. The random environment is assumed to be stationary and doubly stochastic. The proof relies on bounding operators using recursive inequalities derived from the chaos expansion.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the problem context, relates the mean squared displacement to the resolvent of the generator, and then uses a recursive operator bounding technique (via chaos expansion) to derive the asymptotic bounds leading to the main theorem.

Abstract

We study the superdiffusive behavior of random walks on the randomly oriented Manhattan lattice, i.e., the $d$-dimensional integer lattice $\mathbb{Z}^d$ where each axis-aligned line is independently assigned a random direction (forward or backward) with equal probability. The walker takes nearest-neighbor steps, choosing an axis randomly and moving along the assigned direction of that axis's line, with equal probabilities for each axis. We show that, in the critical dimension $d=3$, the diffusion coefficient of the random walk diverges in the Tauberian sense as $\sqrt{\log t}$ with a multiplicative correction $(\log\log t)^{\pm(2+\varepsilon)}$ as time $t\to\infty$. This gives an answer to a conjecture by Ledger, Tóth and Valkó (2018).

Abstract

We find that, generically, such models must violate one of the properties of conventional Euclidean QFTs, such as reflection positivity or cluster decomposition. We present several complementary ways of understanding which features can and cannot be preserved at finite $N$, both in QM and in QFT.

Abstract

Efficient, deterministic, and high-fidelity preparation of large Fock states is essential for scaling bosonic quantum technologies and exploring quantum phenomena at large excitation energies. This framework provides a broadly applicable route to highly excited bosonic states on platforms supporting these elementary controls.

Abstract

Run-and-tumble particles (RTPs) are canonically modeled with uniform reorientation probabilities, an assumption that breaks down for many biological microswimmers. In this work, we investigate the dynamics of RTPs with arbitrary non-uniform tumble distributions. Summary. Main result. It derives a sharp quadratic bound, $T \langle H angle \geq C_\star \Delta^2 / \sigma_A^2$, which complements existing linear bounds based on energy variance.

Method. The derivation uses techniques involving H"older's inequality and the tangent-line lemma to relate the observable change to the mean energy above the ground state.

Abstract

The Mandelstam-Tamm and Margolus-Levitin quantum speed limits bound how fast a state evolves, using the energy variance and the mean energy above the ground state. Speed limits on observables -- the change of an expectation value -- have so far used the variance (the Mandelstam-Tamm / quantum-Fisher-information lineage); the mean energy has not been brought to bear on the change of in a fixed state. We close this branch. First, a no-go theorem: there is no state-independent linear mean-energy bound on the time to change an observable's expectation by Delta = | - |; the optimal state-independent exponent of Delta is exactly two. Second, the corresponding sharp quadratic bound, T ( - E_0) >= C_ Delta^2 / sigma_A^2, for every time-independent Hamiltonian H (ground energy E_0), every bounded observable A with spectral spread sigma_A = (lambda_max - lambda_min)/2, and every pure or mixed state, with the dimension-independent constant C_ = 1/(8 sin x_) = 0.172506267461..., where x_ is the smallest positive root of tan(x/2) = x. The bound is tight, approached but not attained by a near-ground two-level family. We then give the exact energy-time/swing trade-off curve of which C_* is the small-swing slope -- tight at every swing, the observable analog of the Giovannetti-Lloyd-Maccone curve for states -- show the constant survives for mixed states via joint convexity of the trace distance, sharpen it for bandwidth-limited generators and several observables at once, and show the quadratic law degrades to a linear one when the initial state is an eigenvector of the observable. It completes the (mean-energy x observable) corner of the speed-limit landscape and, being quadratic, is most constraining where the linear variance bounds are weakest. Its cleanest physical home is the autonomous quantum clock, where it gives a coherent mean-energy resolution floor.

Abstract

We investigate localization phenomena and exact mobility edges in a quasiperiodic Aharonov-Bohm chain, where the 1D canonical diagonal and off-diagonal Aubry-André-Harper models are laterally coupled to an auxiliary sublattice threaded by a synthetic magnetic flux. By analytically computing the Lyapunov exponent via Avila's global theory of one-frequency Schrödinger operators, we derive exact expressions for mobility edges. In the off-diagonal regime, the interference between quasiperiodic hopping and indirect tunneling through the auxiliary sublattice gives rise to exact anomalous mobility edges that separate critical and extended states. Critical states and anomalous mobility edges emerge even in the absence of incommensurately distributed zeros in the hopping modulation, a behavior distinct from that of conventional off-diagonal models. Our results establish a rigorous theoretical framework for engineering controllable mobility edges, with the synthetic flux providing a tunable experimental knob that paves the way for realizations in platforms such as superconducting quantum circuits and photonic waveguides.

The ability to tailor photon-number statistics is central to the generation and control of nonclassical states of light. Here, we investigate how Fock-state filtering of squeezed coherent states can be used to engineer their photon statistics. We consider the selective removal of the $m=0$ and $m=1$ Fock components and show that this process can enhance the sub-Poissonian character of the field, depending on the coherent amplitude. We then analyze the evolution of these engineered states in a thermal environment. Although all states eventually relax toward thermal statistics, the signatures of the filtering process may persist over a significant part of the evolution, while the purity of the filtered states is rapidly degraded by incoherent population redistribution. Our results illustrate both the potential and the limitations of photon-number engineering in realistic, dissipative settings.

Summary. This work proposes a Time-Dimensional Exchange Coupling (TEC) to enhance the efficiency of solving combinatorial optimization problems like MaxCut on Ising machines. By introducing a temporal interaction between successive spin states, TEC improves sampling exploration at low temperatures and stabilizes solutions at high temperatures. This provides a hardware-efficient alternative to complex replica methods, significantly speeding up convergence.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical optimization, the dynamics involve time-delayed feedback loops and enhanced sampling mechanisms, which are conceptually related to non-equilibrium dynamics and thermalization processes studied in AMO physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the inefficiency of sampling the configuration space for solving combinatorial optimization problems, such as MaxCut, using probabilistic Ising machines.

Main result. The proposed Time-Dimensional Exchange Coupling (TEC) significantly accelerates convergence speed for MaxCut problems, offering a scalable and hardware-efficient alternative to replica-based quantum Monte Carlo methods.

Method. The authors introduce a TEC term into the Ising Hamiltonian, which couples the current spin configuration to the previous one in time, modifying the stochastic dynamics.

Model / system. The system is based on the Ising model implemented on a p-bit network, simulating the optimization process. The total energy includes both spatial interactions and the novel temporal exchange coupling term, E_total = E_spatial - sum(J_v * sigma_i(t) * sigma_i(t-1)).

Key observables. Convergence speed, solution accuracy (for MaxCut), update velocity, and the shift in optimal TEC coupling strength (J_v) with temperature.

Important parameters / regimes. The coupling strength J_v, temperature (T), and the size of the problem (up to 2000 vertices).

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes the time interval between successive spin configurations is much longer than the spin relaxation time, and the hardware implementation is assumed to be feasible despite minor SPICE simulation inaccuracies.

Figures summary. Figures compare TEC to conventional methods, showing accelerated annealing dynamics for MaxCut; they also illustrate how the optimal TEC coupling strength shifts systematically with temperature.

Paper structure. The paper first identifies the limitations of existing sampling methods, then introduces the TEC Hamiltonian and dynamics, validates its effect on exploration/stability at different temperatures, and finally benchmarks its performance on large-scale MaxCut problems using both simulation and hardware modeling.

Abstract

Conventional probabilistic Ising machines often suffer from inefficient exploration of configuration space, while replica-based quantum Monte Carlo methods reduce sampling bottlenecks at the cost of large hardware overhead. Here we propose a time-dimensional exchange coupling (TEC) that replaces spatial replica coupling with a temporal exchange interaction between successive spin configurations of a single p-bit network. This TEC improves sampling efficiency without duplicating replica hardware. At low temperatures, antiferromagnetic TEC expands sampling range; at high temperatures, ferromagnetic TEC stabilizes the optimal state. For MaxCut problems with up to 2000 vertices, TEC significantly accelerates convergence speed. SPICE simulations confirm hardware feasibility. TEC offers a scalable, hardware-efficient strategy to enhance combinatorial optimization on existing Ising machines.
