Energy Internet Routing using Quantum Optimization Algorithms

Authors: Alireza Alamgir Tehrani, Mehrdad Boroushaki, Abbas Rajabi
Type: both · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.28401v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This research tackles the NP-hard energy routing problem in the Energy Internet by formulating it as a QUBO/Ising model. The authors compare solving this model using classical algorithms (like Dijkstra) against various quantum approaches (QAOA and Quantum Annealing). While the quantum methods validate the formulation, classical solvers demonstrated significantly faster runtime performance on the tested networks.

Why it may be interesting. The work demonstrates a direct application of quantum optimization techniques (QUBO/Ising) to a complex, real-world engineering problem, providing a benchmark for quantum computation feasibility in infrastructure management.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the NP-hard problem of finding the energy-efficient path selection (energy routing) within the Energy Internet (EI).

Main result. While quantum methods successfully formulated the problem, classical algorithms like Dijkstra's showed superior runtime performance for the tested 9-node and 30-node networks.

Method. The problem is mapped onto a Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) and Ising Hamiltonian, which are then solved using comparative quantum and classical optimization techniques.

Model / system. The system modeled is the Energy Internet (EI), a network integrating renewable energy sources and loads. The core mathematical tool is the QUBO/Ising formulation derived from minimizing total energy losses (router and conduction losses).

Key observables. Optimal routing path, total energy losses (W), and computational time (s).

Important parameters / regimes. Network size (9-node and 30-node), transmission power (1 kW), and quantum circuit depth (p=1 for QAOA).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes treating power transmission as a constant parameter to linearize the non-linear loss function for QUBO formulation; quantum simulations are run on classical hardware.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the EI components and the 9-node network graph structure; Tables compare the performance metrics (time, loss) across Dijkstra, QAOA, and quantum-inspired annealing for both 9-node and 30-node cases.

Paper structure. The paper first defines the EI routing problem and its NP-hard nature. It then develops the QUBO/Ising formulation by incorporating loss functions and constraints. Finally, it compares the performance of classical solvers (Dijkstra) against quantum approaches (QAOA, Quantum Annealing) on increasing network sizes.

Abstract

The Energy Internet (EI) is a new concept aimed at enhancing the integration of renewable energy sources with the energy grid. Energy-efficient path selection in EI is NP-hard. This research presents an innovative Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) and Ising Hamiltonian formulation for energy routing. The validation and scalability of the proposed formulation were evaluated by applying quantum-inspired annealing and quantum gate optimization to two case studies, a 9-node and a 30-node EI network. A comparative analysis was presented between classical optimization using the Dijkstra algorithm, optimization-based methods, and QAOA using the Qiskit Sampler Primitive, NumpyEigenSolver, and quantum-inspired annealing using the Ocean exact Solver, D-Wave Tabu Sampler, and D-Wave Simulated Annealing. Simulation results based on the proposed formulation agree with the exact solution, while the runtime of classical approaches is less than that of quantum approaches. However, the Simulated Annealing sampler offers the shortest runtime among all quantum methods.

Exact quantification of nonlocal magic

Authors: Piotr Sierant
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.28563v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical work introduces and solves the problem of quantifying 'nonlocal magic,' a measure of quantum correlation independent of local basis choices. The authors show this quantity is directly computable from the state's entanglement spectrum, providing a robust diagnostic tool. This advances the understanding of quantum resources by distinguishing genuine multipartite correlations from local redundancies.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a powerful, computable diagnostic tool ($D_{ ext{NL}}^{\min}$) for characterizing quantum correlations in many-body states, which is crucial for understanding resource theories beyond simple entanglement measures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to rigorously quantify 'nonlocal magic,' which is the component of quantum nonstabilizerness residing purely in the correlations between two subsystems, independent of local basis choices.

Main result. The nonlocal magic is quantified by the distance of the state's entanglement spectrum from the nearest Bell-pair sector, providing a universal and operationally meaningful measure of quantum correlation.

Method. The authors solve an intractable optimization problem by relating the nonlocal magic to the entanglement spectrum structure, leading to closed-form formulas based on Schmidt coefficients.

Model / system. The analysis focuses on pure multiqubit states $|\Psi angle$ subjected to bipartitions $A \cup B$, utilizing concepts from stabilizer states and entanglement theory.

Key observables. Nonlocal Min-Relative Entropy of Nonstabilizerness ($ ext{NMRE}$ or $D_{ ext{NL}}^{\min}$), Nonlocal Stabilizer Fidelity ($F_{NL}$), and the entanglement spectrum.

Important parameters / regimes. The entanglement spectrum ($\lambda$), the number of qubits ($ u$), and the ratio of system sizes ($d/D$) in scaling limits.

Assumptions / limitations. The core results rely on solving the optimization problem by analyzing the structure of the entanglement spectrum, and later sections assume convergence properties for large random states.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the concept of local vs. correlation magic, showing the nonlocal magic as a distance measure on the entanglement spectrum relative to Bell pairs.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining the intractable nonlocal magic problem to deriving a closed-form solution based on the entanglement spectrum. It then establishes equivalences with other measures and analyzes scaling behavior under tensor products and random state limits.

Abstract

Magic, or nonstabilizerness, is the resource that lifts Clifford circuits to universal quantum computation and has become a standard diagnostic of many-body states. For a state shared between two parties, however, a basic question has remained open: how much of the magic resides in the correlations between the parties rather than in their local bases? Isolating this nonlocal magic requires minimizing over all local bases, an optimization that has so far resisted exact solution. Here we solve it for the stabilizer fidelity: the nonlocal magic of every pure multiqubit state is the distance of its entanglement spectrum from the closest spectrum of Bell pairs. The same quantity governs an apparently unrelated task: a family of states universally embezzles entanglement under local operations and classical communication if and only if its nonlocal magic diverges. The deciding property is not the amount of entanglement but the way the entanglement spectrum spreads its weight across factor-of-two windows of rank, so that critical chains and random-singlet states, with identical logarithmic entanglement scaling, carry unbounded and vanishing nonlocal magic, respectively. Nonlocal magic thereby becomes an operationally meaningful property of quantum correlations, directly accessible to tensor-network simulations and, through entanglement spectroscopy, to experiments.

Abstract

We study the Fermi surface topology and quasiparticle properties in the heavy fermion compound YbNi4P2 at high magnetic fields using quantum oscillation measurements. We observe a large decrease of the quasiparticle mass with increasing field and demonstrate good qualitative agreement with the single-ion Kondo model. At the putative Lifshitz transition at 17 T, we observe a sudden change of quantum oscillation frequencies suggesting an abrupt change of the electronic structure and/or quasiparticle characteristics. Our results demonstrate the ability to tune the electronic structure and provide input for theoretical models of YbNi4P2 and correlated electron systems in high magnetic fields.

Fractional Hall Conductance of Laughlin States from a Topological Index

Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27767v1

Summary. This paper rigorously proves the fractional quantization of the Hall conductance for Laughlin states by defining and calculating a many-body topological index. Using an infinite Matrix Product State formalism on a thin cylinder, the authors show that this index yields $\sigma_H = 1/q$, establishing the quantization as a fundamental topological property of the FQH phase.

Why it may be interesting. The use of topological invariants derived from algebraic structures (like the index on the CAR algebra) to prove fundamental quantization results in strongly correlated electron systems provides a powerful, non-perturbative theoretical tool applicable to other quantum many-body problems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to rigorously establish the fractional quantization of the Hall conductance ($\sigma_H$) for Laughlin states by deriving it directly from a topological index, bypassing reliance on spectral gap assumptions.

Main result. The authors prove that the Hall conductance for Laughlin states is quantized to $\sigma_H = 1/q$, where $q$ is an integer, demonstrating that this value is a robust topological invariant.

Method. The core method involves recasting the Laughlin-pump argument into a topological invariant defined as an index ($ ext{ind}(\omega_1, \omega_2)$) for a pair of pure states, which is then calculated using an infinite Matrix Product State (iMPS) representation.

Model / system. The physical system is the Fractional Quantum Hall (FQH) effect, modeled by Laughlin states ($\omega_q$). The analysis is performed on a thin cylinder geometry using the CAR algebra of observables.

Key observables. Hall conductance ($\sigma_H$), the topological index ($ ext{ind}(\omega_1, \omega_2)$), and the filling fraction ($1/q$).

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis is most rigorous in the thin-cylinder limit (large $\gamma$) and involves the flux quantum insertion parameter $k$.

Assumptions / limitations. The derivation relies on the validity of the iMPS representation for sufficiently large cylinder radii ($\gamma$) and the definition of the index's stability under deformations.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the conformal equivalence between a cylinder and an annulus. Figure 2 shows the iMPS representation, detailing how elementary 'squeezing' operations map the root partition related to flux insertion.

Paper structure. The argument progresses by first defining the physical problem (flux insertion $ ightarrow \sigma_H$), then abstracting it into a topological index, proving the index's mathematical robustness (automorphism invariance, stability), and finally applying this index to the specific Laughlin state to derive the quantization result.

Abstract

We provide a rigorous Laughlin-pump argument that establishes the fractional quantization of the Hall conductance directly from the Laughlin state as a topological index. Our proof uses a recently defined index of a pair of pure states together with an infinite matrix product state analysis that is rigorous on a sufficiently thin cylinder. By virtue of the connection to an index, our result implies, in particular, that Laughlin states are representatives of topologically stable fractional quantum Hall phases.

The findings provide insights into how crystal structure dictates magnetic ordering and electronic behavior in these boride compounds.

The investigation into localized magnetic moments and the strong correlation effects in the $4f$ electrons of Cerium, combined with the analysis of crystal field splitting in the $ ext{Cr}$ $3d$ orbitals, touches upon correlated electron physics relevant to heavy fermion systems.

Abstract

CeCrB$_4$ belongs to the family of MTB$_4$ ternary metal borides, some of whose magnetic phases have recently been identified as quantum spin dimers. Employing density functional theory within the GGA+$U$ framework, this work investigates the key ground-state properties of this system, including its ferromagnetic ground state, the mixed-valence nature of cerium, and the charge transfer from the cationic cerium--chromium layers to the anionic boron layers. In particular, we focus on elucidating the bonding mechanism within the structurally embedded Cr-Cr dimers. Specifically, we evaluate a previously proposed interpretation that the localized Cr $3d_{z^2}$ states are molecular-like bonding and antibonding states. The calculated dependence of the $3d_{z^2}$ electronic structure on the intra-dimer distance correlates with the level-splitting characteristics known for classical homonuclear diatomic molecules.

Learning to Decode Concatenated Quantum Codes with Hierarchical Message Passing

Authors: Jiahui Wu, Chao Zhang, Zipeng Wu, Shilin Huang
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.28571v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work presents a novel neural message-passing decoder for decoding complex concatenated quantum error-correcting codes. By propagating soft beliefs across code levels, the decoder achieves substantial improvements in error thresholds compared to existing state-of-the-art methods. This framework offers a general tool for advancing fault-tolerant quantum computation design.

Why it may be interesting. The use of neural networks to model complex, structured error correction processes provides a powerful, trainable tool for analyzing fault tolerance limits in quantum computation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of efficiently and accurately decoding general concatenated quantum error-correcting codes.

Main result. The proposed neural message-passing decoder significantly outperforms state-of-the-art hard-decision decoders, notably nearly doubling the depolarizing pseudo-threshold for the [[15, 7, 3]] Hamming code.

Method. It introduces a neural message-passing framework where soft beliefs propagate bidirectionally across code concatenation levels, using lightweight networks only for message aggregation.

Model / system. The model focuses on concatenated quantum stabilizer codes, tested on specific examples like the [[15, 7, 3]] Hamming code and many-hypercube codes. The framework is designed to be generic for exploring various code constructions.

Key observables. Pseudo-threshold ($p^*$) under bit-flip and depolarizing noise, and logical-CNOT failure rates.

Important parameters / regimes. The number of message-passing iterations (fixed for bounded latency), and the specific noise models (bit-flip, depolarizing).

Assumptions / limitations. The core message-passing rules are kept fixed, departing from fully end-to-end neural decoders, and the initial state can be learned to capture circuit-level error distributions.

Figures summary. Figures compare block failure rates for the Hamming code and many-hypercube code under different noise types, demonstrating the decoder's superior performance compared to baseline decoders.

Paper structure. The paper details the neural message-passing architecture, presents results comparing its performance against hard-decision decoders on various code families, and discusses fine-tuning on circuit-level error data.

Abstract

We introduce a neural message-passing framework for decoding general concatenated stabilizer codes. Soft beliefs propagate bidirectionally across concatenation levels, and lightweight neural networks learn only to aggregate incoming messages. For the concatenated $[[15,7,3]]$ quantum Hamming code, the resulting decoder achieves substantially higher thresholds than the state-of-the-art bidirectional hard-decision decoder under both bit-flip and depolarizing noise. In particular, the depolarizing pseudo-threshold nearly doubles, from $6.5\%$ to $12.3\%$. For many-hypercube codes, a decoder fine-tuned on circuit-level errors in Knill's teleportation-based error correction can achieve lower logical-CNOT failure rates than their dedicated decoder, using a fixed number of message-passing iterations instead of extensive combinatorial search. Our framework provides a generic decoding tool for exploring the design space of concatenated codes, including non-CSS constructions, toward low-overhead fault tolerance.

Next-to-Leading-Order Electroweak Corrections to Quantum Observables in Lepton-Lepton Collisions

Summary. This paper calculates how electroweak radiative corrections affect quantum measures like entanglement and magic in processes such as $\ell^+\ell^- o au^- au^+$ and $ZZ$. By treating the final state as a multi-qudit system, the authors show that these corrections coherently alter the quantum structure of the final state. The results establish these quantum observables as powerful, new tools for precision measurements at future lepton colliders.

Why it may be interesting. The application of quantum information diagnostics (entanglement, magic) to high-energy particle physics processes provides a novel, precision-sensitive probe of fundamental interactions, bridging quantum information theory with particle phenomenology.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how virtual next-to-leading-order (NLO) electroweak corrections modify the quantum structure of observables like entanglement and magic in lepton-lepton collisions.

Main result. Radiative corrections coherently modify the quantum structure of the final state, causing shifts in regions of maximal entanglement and altering the generated magic relative to leading order.

Method. The authors compute entanglement entropy and stabilizer second Rényi entropy by treating outgoing particles as multi-qudit systems and performing one-loop calculations.

Model / system. The physical system is lepton-lepton collisions ($\ell^+\ell^- o au^- au^+, ZZ$), analyzed within Quantum Field Theory. The outgoing particles are modeled as multi-qudit systems (qubits for fermions, qutrits for massive bosons).

Key observables. Entanglement entropy, stabilizer second Rényi entropy, and 'magic' (non-stabilizerness).

Important parameters / regimes. Next-to-leading-order (NLO) electroweak corrections, future lepton collider energies, and particle masses.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis focuses on the production-side quantum state before decay reconstruction, and the initial state is assumed to have definite helicities.

Paper structure. The paper systematically calculates quantum observables by incorporating one-loop electroweak corrections, detailing the theoretical framework for handling divergences and projecting the state onto the outgoing two-particle Hilbert space.

Abstract

We study how virtual next-to-leading-order electroweak corrections affect the quantum observables of entanglement and magic in lepton--lepton collisions. Treating the outgoing particles as multi-qudit systems, $\textit{i.e.}$, qubits for spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ fermions and qutrits for massive vector bosons, we compute entanglement entropy and the stabilizer second Rényi entropy for the processes $\ell^+\ell^- \to τ^-τ^+,\, ZZ$ at one-loop accuracy. We show that radiative corrections coherently modify the quantum structure of the final state, shifting regions of maximal entanglement and altering the generated magic relative to leading order. Our results demonstrate that these quantum observables provide novel, precision-sensitive probes of electroweak dynamics at future lepton colliders and open a path toward their use in searches for new physics.

Orbital-Selective Coexistence of Interlayer Spin-Singlet Formation and SDW Order with Anomalous Spin Reconfiguration in Bilayer Nickelate La$_{3}$Ni$_{2}$O$_{7}$ Revealed by $^{17}$O-NMR

Authors: H. Lee, M. Yashima, M. Kakoi, T. Ino, Y. Arai, K. Kitagawa, H. Sakurai, Y. Takano, K. Kuroki, H. Mukuda

Summary. This work uses site-selective $^{17} ext{O-NMR}$ to probe the spin structure of the bilayer nickelate La3Ni2O7. It reveals a complex interplay between an in-plane Spin Density Wave (SDW) order and the formation of an interlayer spin-singlet state. Important parameters / regimes. Transition temperatures $T_{ ext{SDW}} (= 150 ext{ K})$ and $T_{ ext{A}} (\sim 115 ext{ K})$; the relative contributions of $d_{x^2-y^2}$ and $d_{3z^2-r^2}$ orbitals to magnetism.

Abstract

The spin structure of the spin density wave (SDW) order in the bilayer nickelate La$_3$Ni$_2$O$_7$ has been investigated using site-selective $^{17}$O-NMR measurements on the inner apical O(1), outer apical O(2), and planar O(3,4) sites. Below $T_{\rm SDW}$ (= 150 K), the peak of all planar O(3,4) sites significantly broadens due to the emergence of a finite internal magnetic field, whereas O(2) sites remain with no (or a negligibly small) internal field. These results are consistent with commensurate SDW order with a single spin-spinless (or large-tiny spin) stripe. As for the O(1) sites that bridge the NiO$_2$ planes, the internal field is nearly canceled below $T_{\rm SDW}$, indicating an antiparallel spin configuration between adjacent planes. However, below $T_\text{A}$ ($\sim$ 115 K), the spectrum of the O(1) site disappears even though the in-plane SDW order remains robust, implying that the antiparallel spin configuration through the Ni--O(1)--Ni bond is not particularly stable below $T_{\rm A}$, despite the expected strong interlayer spin coupling between the NiO$_2$ planes. Above all, we emphasize that the local spin susceptibility is extremely small at the O(2) site that has a strong covalency with the $d_{3z^2-r^2}$ orbital, indicating a well-developed interlayer spin-singlet formation in the Ni-$d_{3z^2-r^2}$ orbitals bridging the NiO$_2$ planes. These findings shed new light on the interlayer spin-singlet formation and the anomalous spin reconfiguration through the $\text{Ni--O(1)--Ni}$ bonding orbitals connecting the NiO$_2$ planes, which characterize the orbital-selective nature of the bilayer nickelate La$_3$Ni$_2$O$_7$.

Plateau-Constrained Selection of Commuting Phase-Term Orderings Under a Fixed Maintained-Parity Compiler Contract

Authors: Owen Friedewald, Ali Shiri Sichani, Chi-Ren Shyu

Summary. This paper introduces a novel two-stage compiler optimization technique for ordering commuting phase terms in quantum circuits. It leverages the fact that multiple orderings can achieve the same minimum energy cost (a 'plateau'). By sampling and selecting among these equal-cost options, the authors demonstrate measurable improvements in circuit depth and gate count compared to prior state-of-the-art methods.

Why it may be interesting. This work tackles a highly practical, low-level optimization problem in quantum compilation—resource reduction—by mathematically characterizing and exploiting the inherent symmetries (degeneracy) in the problem space.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of selecting optimal orderings for commuting phase terms in quantum circuits, specifically exploiting the degeneracy (plateau) of multiple equal-cost solutions.

Main result. A two-stage search strategy that samples equal-cost tours significantly reduces circuit depth (e.g., 12.83% reduction) compared to prior methods, although this benefit is shown to be dependent on the specific quantum compiler routing used.

Method. The methodology employs a classical two-stage permutation search under a fixed compiler contract: Stage 1 finds the primary support optimum, and Stage 2 samples and selects among all equal-cost tours using a 'frozen routed score.'

Model / system. The work models quantum computation using synthetic 16-qubit assignment-Ising instances derived from a QUBO model, focusing on the ordering of commuting phase terms in the Hamiltonian simulation.

Key observables. Circuit depth (e.g., opposite-SABRE-seed depth), number of routed controlled-NOT (CNOT) gates, and the multiplicity of minimum-support-cost orderings.

Important parameters / regimes. The size of the system (e.g., 16 qubits, 36 or 48 terms) and the specific compiler contract constraints (fixed placement, maintained-parity).

Assumptions / limitations. The depth benefit is explicitly noted to be 'router-conditioned' and not a guaranteed hardware benefit, and the synthetic workload does not represent end-to-end electronic-design-automation behavior.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the two-stage ordering process, detailing how Stage 1 identifies the minimum support cost, and Stage 2 selects among multiple tours achieving that cost based on depth metrics.

Paper structure. The paper is structured around defining a fixed compiler contract, executing the two-stage search (Stage 1 for primary cost, Stage 2 for plateau selection), and then rigorously comparing the resulting circuit metrics against various benchmarks and sensitivity analyses.

Abstract

Ordering objectives for commuting phase terms can have many equal optima, yet prior methods do not characterize or exploit those ties. We use a classical two-stage permutation search under fixed placement and maintained-parity quantum lowering: Stage 1 certifies the primary support optimum, and Stage 2 samples equal-cost tours and selects by a frozen routed score. On synthetic 16-qubit assignment-Ising instances, exact counting through 20 terms establishes instance-dependent multiplicity; when the support lower bound is attained, the reversal-reduced width equals the number of undirected Hamiltonian paths of the support line graph. A revised engineering analysis found 9.14% fewer routed controlled-NOT gates than unoptimized order, while the registered comparison found 11.10% fewer than prior stochastic search. Among 24 sampled minimum-support-cost orders at 36 terms, direct-depth selection reduced opposite-SABRE-seed depth by 12.83% in all 20 aggregates, whereas a matched 24-restart control changed depth by only -0.41% (unresolved). Candidate rankings persisted across SABRE routing seeds, explaining why selection survived routing re-randomization. The depth benefit transferred to a second generator and to 48 terms, but reversed under BasicSwap. On a prospective IBM Heron panel, raw generator error shifted by -0.0025 (-0.59%); fixed-panel shot uncertainty excluded zero, but term-seed inference remained unresolved. Equal-primary-cost tours are a useful router-conditioned compiler freedom, not a guaranteed hardware benefit.

Abstract

Localized interlayer excitons in semiconducting transition-metal dichalcogenide heterobilayers are quantum emitters with a static electric dipole moment, making them excellent nanoscale charge sensors to probe correlated quantum phases in a proximal layer. These emitters are electrically tunable and inherit spin-valley selection rules, yet their deterministic spatial control remains challenging due to subwavelength confinement. Here, we present a platform that combines cryogenic optical spectroscopy with scanning probe microscopy to investigate trapped interlayer excitons in WSe$_2$/MoSe$_2$ bilayers. By exploiting AFM-based local Stark shift, we achieve super-resolution localization of emitters separated by only a few tens of nanometers and demonstrate deterministic control of individual charge states, including trion formation, opening a path towards coherent inter-dot coupling. Time-resolved measurements reveal tip-induced modification of the electromagnetic vacuum around individual emitters, thus controlling their radiative emission. Our multi-point charge sensing platform with optical readout is particularly well-suited to study fractionalization and anyon dynamics in semiconducting FCIs.

Abstract

We studied acoustoelectric (AE) currents in materials that undergo charge density wave (CDW) transitions, induced by a surface acoustic wave (SAW) on a piezoelectric substrate. The polarity and magnitude of the AE current in NbSe$_3$ and 2H-TaSe$_2$ were modulated due to their CDW transitions. We also found that the sign of the AE current depends on the SAW propagation direction with respect to the crystalline axis of the substrate.A phenomenological model assuming strain-modified conductivity can qualitatively account for the significant modulation of the AE current associated with the CDW transition, as well as the SAW propagation orientation dependence. The present results offer a powerful probe for exploring SAW-electron interactions in van der Waals materials, thereby highlighting their potential for advancing the emerging field of straintronics.
