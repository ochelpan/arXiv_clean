Generalized LIMDDs: Succinctness and Canonicity for Decision Diagrams Modulo a Group

Authors: Arend-Jan Quist, Alexis de Colnet, Thomas Reps, Alfons Laarman
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.35271v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper generalizes decision diagrams by quotienting the state space by a group action derived from quantum gates (like phase rotations and bit flips). It proves that this generalization yields exponentially more succinct representations for quantum states, specifically characterizing hypergraph states. The methodology combines group theory with advanced algebraic minimization techniques to establish unique, efficiently computable canonical forms.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, mathematically rigorous framework for compressing the description of complex quantum states (like those arising in quantum computation) using algebraic structures derived from quantum gates.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper studies the structure, succinctness, and canonicity of decision diagrams when the standard equality condition is replaced by a quotienting by a group action (Generalized LIMDDs).

Main result. The proposed family of groups allows for unique normal forms computable in polynomial time, and the diagrams precisely characterize hypergraph states.

Method. The authors use algebraic methods involving group theory (quotienting by group orbits) and linear algebra (Gaussian elimination over Z2 and Zq) to establish canonical forms and determine the succinctness order.

Model / system. The model involves representing quantum states (specifically hypergraph states) using L-labeled decision diagrams whose edge labels are drawn from a group $\langle G angle$ generated by phase rotations ($R_q$) and controlled by qubits ($C_{\le k}R_q$).

Key observables. Succinctness (diagram size), canonicity (existence of unique normal form), and the structure of the stabilizer subgroup $ ext{Stab}(v)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The control arity ($k$) and the root order ($q$) of the generating group $\langle G angle$ are key parameters determining the exponential hierarchy of succinctness.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the structure of the group $\langle G angle$ and relies on establishing canonical representations for nodes satisfying specific reduction rules (R1-R5).

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates an example of a $\langle R_3, X angle$-LIMDD and its 0-edge normal form. Figure 2 provides a 'Succinctness map' showing the exponential separations between different LIMDD families based on $q$, $k$, and $X$.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theory by first defining the generalized diagrams, then characterizing the group structure $\langle G angle$, establishing the minimization procedure for the normal form using linear algebra, and finally proving the exponential succinctness gains and the characterization of hypergraph states.

Abstract

A reduced ordered decision diagram is the minimal automaton of a function on words of fixed length: its nodes are the residuals, and reduction is the Myhill--Nerode quotient. We study what happens when that quotient is coarsened by a group. Fix a group $G$ acting on residuals, merge two nodes when their residuals lie in one $G$-orbit, and record the group element on the edge. For functions $\{0,1\}^n\to\mathbb{C}$ and $G$ the Pauli group this is the Local Invertible Map Decision Diagram. We take $G$ from the two-parameter family generated by $C^{\leq k}R_q$, the phase rotation of order $q$ with up to $k$ control qubits, with and without the bit flip $X$. We show that this gives exponential succinctness improvements compared to Pauli-LIMDD, and we determine the succinctness order of the family completely. The separating objects are hypergraph states, which can always be efficiently represented by some member of the family. We settle the tractability of five queries and eight transformations, which is invariant across the family, and shows the same behavior as Pauli-LIMDD. We give a five-rule reduction system whose normal forms are unique for every member of the family, and we show that this canonical form is computable in polynomial time in the size of the LIMDD. We show that, when coarsening beyond the (anti-)diagonal groups, the calculation of a minimal sized normal form turns out to be non-local and it might to rebuild the whole diagram.

Hardness of Approximating Quantum Code Distance Beyond $\sqrt{N}$

Authors: Upendra Kapshikar
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.33956v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper rigorously analyzes the computational hardness of approximating the minimum distance of quantum stabilizer codes. It closes a prior theoretical gap by proving that achieving a linear approximation gap requires solving problems at the level of $ ext{NP}$-hardness. The results establish deep connections between quantum error correction and foundational complexity theory.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides fundamental computational limits on the efficiency of quantum error correction, which is crucial for understanding the feasibility of large-scale quantum computation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the computational complexity of approximating the minimum distance of a stabilizer quantum code (QMDP).

Main result. It establishes strong hardness results, showing that approximating the distance to within a linear additive gap ($\alpha N$) is $ ext{NP}$-hard unless $ ext{NP} \subseteq ext{coRP}$, and provides fine-grained lower bounds based on complexity assumptions like SETH.

Method. The authors use complexity-theoretic reductions, mapping hard classical problems (like $k$-SAT) to the quantum distance problem via Code from Graph (CWS) constructions.

Model / system. The focus is on quantum stabilizer codes, which are defined by an abelian subgroup of the Pauli group on $N$ qubits. The distance is the minimum weight of an error not in the stabilizer group.

Key observables. The quantum minimum distance, qdist(Q), is the primary observable of interest.

Important parameters / regimes. Block length ($N$), number of logical qubits ($\kappa$), and the approximation gap ($\alpha N$).

Assumptions / limitations. The hardness results rely on strong complexity assumptions, including $ ext{NP} \subseteq ext{coRP}$, randomized SETH, and non-uniform Gap-ETH.

Paper structure. The paper builds upon prior work by showing how to close the gap in previous $\sqrt{N}$ bounds, establishing hardness for linear gaps, and then proceeding to fine-grained analyses using various complexity assumptions.

Abstract

We study the computational complexity of approximating the minimum distance of a stabilizer code. Classically, the minimum distance problem is NP-hard to approximate even to within an additive gap linear in the block length. For quantum codes, however, the reductions of Kapshikar and Kundu and of Grigorescu, Jha and Samperton reach only an additive $O(\sqrt{N})$ gap in the block length $N$. We close this gap and, in a separate direction, initiate the fine-grained study of the problem. We show that no randomized algorithm approximates the distance to within an additive $αN$, for a constant $α> 0$, unless $\mathsf{NP} \subseteq \mathsf{coRP}$. In the fine-grained setting, for every $\varepsilon > 0$, no randomized $2^{(1-\varepsilon)κ}\,\mathrm{poly}(N)$-time algorithm computes the distance of a code with $κ$ logical qubits unless SETH falls; and no randomized $2^{o(N)}$-time algorithm approximates the distance to within a linear additive gap, on instances where block length, number of logical qubits, and gap are simultaneously linear, unless non-uniform Gap-ETH falls.

Heuristic lower bounds on real Grothendieck constants of finite and infinite order

Authors: Erika Bene, Tamás Vértesi
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.35399v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical paper derives improved heuristic lower bounds for the real Grothendieck constant, a quantity relevant to quantum information theory. The authors use a cubic kernel analysis to conjecture a new lower bound, significantly improving existing mathematical limits. The work also constructs specific matrices using high-dimensional lattices, linking abstract functional analysis to quantum correlation testing.

Why it may be interesting. The connection between bounding fundamental mathematical constants like $K_G$ and the structure of quantum correlations (Bell inequalities) provides deep theoretical insights into the limits of quantum information processing.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to establish heuristic lower bounds on the real Grothendieck constants, $K_G(d)$ and its infinite limit $K_G$. This constant is relevant to bounding the quantum-to-classical ratio in correlation Bell expressions.

Main result. The authors conjecture a new lower bound $K_G \geq 9\pi/16 \simeq 1.767146$ using a cubic kernel, which improves upon the previously known bound of $6\pi/11$.

Method. The primary method involves analyzing the stability of 'hemispherical binary strategies' under cubic boundary deformations of rotationally invariant kernels, supplemented by large-scale optimization on finite dimensions using lattice constructions.

Model / system. The mathematical framework involves optimizing ratios of quadratic forms defined by real matrices derived from unit vectors on the sphere, connecting functional analysis to quantum correlation bounds.

Key observables. The real Grothendieck constant $K_G(d)$ and its infinite limit $K_G$.

Important parameters / regimes. The cubic kernel parameter $c$, the critical parameter $c^*(d) = (d+1)(d+3) / (3(d+5))$, and the dimension $d$ (up to 24).

Assumptions / limitations. The main conjecture is that hemispherical strategies are globally optimal at the critical threshold $c^*(d)$, and that the finite-setting lower bounds are exact.

Paper structure. The paper develops the problem by defining the Grothendieck constant using kernels, analyzes the continuous case using cubic deformations to find a conjectured bound, and then extends the results to finite dimensions using lattice-based matrix constructions.

Abstract

We propose candidate lower bounds on the real Grothendieck constants $K_{\mathrm G}(d)$ and their infinite-dimensional limit $K_{\mathrm G}$ using rotationally invariant cubic kernels. For the continuous construction, we identify an explicit threshold above which hemispherical binary strategies are unstable under cubic boundary deformations. Supported by numerical evidence, we conjecture that hemispherical strategies are globally optimal at this threshold. This conjecture would imply $K_{\mathrm G}\geq9π/16\simeq1.767146$, improving the rigorous lower bound $6π/11\simeq1.713596$. We also construct finite symmetric coefficient matrices from unit vectors in dimensions up to $24$. Large-scale see-saw optimization supports the continuous predictions and suggests improved lower bounds on $K_{\mathrm G}(d)$ in the studied dimensions from $4$ to $24$, conditional on the conjectured binary optima being exact. Selected matrices also yield finite-setting candidate dimension witnesses when interpreted as bipartite correlation Bell functionals. If the corresponding binary upper bounds hold, these witnesses would certify a local Hilbert-space dimension of at least five for each party.

How fast can a parent estimate the value of their children? A quantum algorithm for stochastic games

Authors: Marien Raat, Merlin Incerti-Medici, James R. Wootton, Evert van Nieuwenburg, Daniel Bultrini
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.35511v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper presents a quantum algorithm to efficiently estimate the value of stochastic games modeled by expectiminimax trees. By combining quantum techniques like multilevel Monte Carlo estimation and coherent binary search, it achieves significant query complexity speedups over classical methods. This work is important for understanding the limits of quantum computation in solving complex, multi-stage decision-making problems.

Why it may be interesting. The techniques developed for bounding query complexity in nested, alternating structures are highly relevant for analyzing quantum algorithms applied to complex decision processes or quantum simulation of structured interactions.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops a quantum algorithm to estimate the value of stochastic 2-player games, which are modeled by expectiminimax trees.

Main result. The quantum algorithm achieves a query complexity bound that shows quadratic speedups compared to classical methods in both the branching factor and the required accuracy.

Method. It employs a combination of a derandomised multilevel Monte Carlo estimator for chance layers and a coherent binary search for adversarial layers, utilizing quantum amplitude amplification.

Model / system. The model is the stochastic 2-player game represented by an expectiminimax tree, which alternates between adversarial (max/min) and chance (mean) layers.

Key observables. The estimated value of the game at the root, with a target Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) of $\epsilon$.

Important parameters / regimes. Depth ($D$), branching factor ($ ext{deg}$), interval length ($N$), and the error tolerance ($\epsilon$).

Assumptions / limitations. The value function must be Lipschitz continuous, and the value at chance vertices must be linear in their children's values for the speedup to hold.

Figures summary. Not specified.

Paper structure. The paper builds upon existing quantum algorithms for simpler tree structures (AND-OR, minimax) and extends them recursively to handle the nested structure of chance nodes within the expectiminimax framework, culminating in complexity bounds derived via induction.

Abstract

We give a quantum algorithm for stochastic $2$-player games. Their game trees are the expectiminimax trees of classical game search: $m$ adversarial layers alternate with $m$ chance layers, every node has $deg$ children, and the leaf values lie in an interval of length $N$. The algorithm estimates the value of the game to root mean square error $ε$ with \[ O\!\left( K^{D}\, deg^{\frac{D}{4}} \, \frac{N}ε \, D^{4D} \log\left( \frac{deg N}ε \right)^{4D} \right), \qquad D = 2m \] queries to the leaf values, where $K$ is an absolute constant. If we treat the depth $D$ as a constant as is sometimes done in the literature, the bound is $\widetilde O(deg^{m/2}ε^{-1})$ against the classical $\widetilde O(deg^{m}ε^{-2})$. So the quadratic speedup in the branching factor known for adversarial trees and the quadratic speedup in the accuracy known for a single expectation both survive when we nest one inside the other. We use a derandomised multilevel Monte Carlo estimator for chance layers and a coherent binary search for adversarial layers. The composition is done by introducing a conversion between root-mean-square and uniform error guarantees, and a composition to nest it with quantum mean estimation. We state the algorithm for stochastic games, but it is valid for the following conditions: the value of a parent is a Lipschitz function of the values of its children and the value at a chance vertex is linear in its children, however the speedup is preserved only if the deterministic step has a quantum subroutine with sublinear query complexity.

By forcing the model to utilize the spatial information from the electron density during energy prediction training, the authors demonstrate a significant improvement in accuracy and data efficiency. This provides a powerful, physically grounded method for developing next-generation machine learning potentials for molecular simulations.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on condensed matter/chemistry, the methodology—using physical constraints (density) to regularize a representation learned for a primary task (energy)—is highly relevant to developing robust ML potentials for complex quantum systems.

Main problem. The paper aims to improve the accuracy and data efficiency of predicting molecular energetics by jointly training a machine learning model on both the molecular energy and the electron density.

Main result. Jointly supervising the model with electron density supervision significantly improves energy prediction accuracy, achieving substantial reductions in Mean Absolute Error compared to energy-only training.

Method. A multi-task learning framework using a Graph Neural Network (GNN) is employed, where the shared molecular representation is trained to predict both the global energy and the spatially resolved electron density.

Model / system. The system studied is molecules, represented by atomic structures and coordinates. The model uses a GNN architecture that learns a shared, equivariant molecular representation from which task-specific heads predict the energy and density.

Important parameters / regimes. Loss weights (alpha, beta), GNN channel widths, and the fraction of density data used for supervision.

Abstract

We present a graph neural network-based learning framework trained to jointly predict electron densities and molecular energies. The model is trained to predict electron densities calculated with a Generalized Gradient Approximation (GGA) functional while simultaneously learning to predict molecular energies obtained with hybrid functional calculations at the B3LYP level of theory. Our results indicate that the rich spatial information in electron density distribution can be used to improve and accelerate the learning of accurate energies. By sharing an equivariant molecular representation across density and energy prediction heads, the model learns complementary molecular quantities within a unified framework. This provides a new promising route for practical use cases of many recent charge density learning frameworks for atomic scale simulations.

Interactions and dynamics of metastable triplet-state helium dimer with Ne and Ar

Authors: Dibyendu Sardar, François Lique
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.34713v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

Summary. This theoretical study calculates the interaction potentials and collision dynamics for the metastable helium dimer interacting with Neon and Argon. By performing detailed quantum scattering calculations, the authors reveal that the $ ext{He}_2+ ext{Ar}$ system exhibits a richer resonance structure than $ ext{He}_2+ ext{Ne}$. The results establish crucial theoretical benchmarks for interpreting future experimental measurements in the field of cold molecular physics.

Why it may be interesting. This work is highly relevant as it uses advanced quantum scattering theory to model interactions in the ultracold regime, a cornerstone of modern AMO physics. The detailed comparison between $ ext{Ne}$ and $ ext{Ar}$ provides insights into how atomic properties (like polarizability or reduced mass) dictate quantum scattering resonances.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the intermolecular interactions and collision dynamics of the metastable triplet-state helium dimer ($ ext{He}_2(a\ ^3\Sigma^+_u)$) with noble gas atoms ($ ext{Ne}$ and $ ext{Ar}$) in ultracold and cold energy regimes.

Main result. The $ ext{He}_2+ ext{Ar}$ system exhibits a richer resonance structure compared to $ ext{He}_2+ ext{Ne}$, highlighting the sensitivity of low-energy scattering to the interaction potential and reduced mass. The calculated properties provide theoretical benchmarks for future experiments.

Method. The authors employed high-level ab initio methods, including spin-restricted coupled cluster ($ ext{RCCSD(T)}$) and multireference configuration interaction ($ ext{MRCISD}$), to construct potential energy surfaces ($ ext{PES}$). Full close-coupling calculations were then performed to analyze scattering dynamics.

Model / system. The system involves the $ ext{He}_2(a\ ^3\Sigma^+_u)$ dimer interacting with $ ext{Ne}$ and $ ext{Ar}$. The $ ext{PES}$ for $ ext{He}_2+ ext{Ne}$ was constructed by averaging over the ground vibrational state, while the $ ext{He}_2+ ext{Ar}$ system was analyzed using more complex correlated methods.

Key observables. Scattering cross sections for excitation and quenching transitions, resonance structure, and the interaction potential depth ($\approx 4.612 ext{ cm}^{-1}$ for $ ext{He}_2+ ext{Ne}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Ultracold and cold collision energies, fine-structure levels of $ ext{He}_2(a\ ^3\Sigma^+_u)$, and the reduced mass of the colliding pair.

Assumptions / limitations. The $ ext{He}_2+ ext{Ne}$ $ ext{PES}$ is vibrationally averaged over the ground vibrational state ($v=0$), and the $ ext{He}_2+ ext{Ar}$ $ ext{PES}$ treatment involves specific approximations regarding the molecular Hamiltonian.

Figures summary. The notes mention figures comparing scattering cross sections using 2D vs. 3D-averaged $ ext{PES}$ for $ ext{He}_2+ ext{Ne}$, and showing cross sections vs. collision energy for $ ext{He}_2+ ext{Ar}$.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the theoretical framework by calculating the $ ext{PES}$ for $ ext{He}_2+ ext{Ne}$ and $ ext{He}_2+ ext{Ar}$ using advanced quantum chemistry methods. It then proceeds to perform full close-coupling calculations to analyze low-energy scattering dynamics, comparing the results obtained from different dimensionality treatments (2D vs. 3D-averaged $ ext{PES}$) and comparing the $ ext{Ne}$ and $ ext{Ar}$ interactions.

Abstract

Helium dimer in its metastable triplet electronic state, He$_2(a~^3Σ_u^+)$, is a promising candidate for direct laser cooling and precision measurements. Interactions and collisions involving He$_2(a~^3Σ_u^+)$ remain unexplored in ultracold and cold energy domains. Here, we present the first theoretical investigation of intermolecular interactions and collision dynamics of He$_2(a~^3Σ_u^+)$+Ne van der Waals complex. For the ground electronic state, $X~^3\mathrm{A}'$, we employ a spin-restricted coupled cluster method with single, double, and noniterative triple excitations. The resulting interaction potential exhibits an extremely shallow well with a depth of 4.612 cm$^{-1}$. We construct a three-dimensional vibrationally averaged potential energy surface. Using this surface, we perform full close-coupling calculations including fine-structure levels of He$_2(a~^3Σ_u^+)$ to investigate collisional excitation and quenching induced by Ne. We analyze the low-energy scattering dynamics and model resonant features associated with quasi-bound states supported by interaction potential. Propensity rule for fine-structure conserving transitions is established. We additionally consider He$_2$+Ar collisions and compare their energy dependence and resonance structure with those of He$_2$+Ne. The He$_2$+Ar system exhibits a richer resonance structure, highlighting the sensitivity of low-energy scattering to interaction potential and reduced mass. Calculated PES and scattering properties provide a reliable benchmark for description of He$_2$+Ne van der Waals complex and may be useful for interpretation of future experimental spectra and collision measurements.

Low-Weight Canonical Logical Bases from Pair-Partition Codes

Authors: Koki Okada, Kenta Kasai
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.35601v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper presents a sophisticated algebraic method to construct complete canonical logical bases for qubit CSS quantum error-correcting codes derived from Pair-Partition codes. It utilizes quaternary coefficients and polynomial algebra to ensure the logical representatives span the entire logical space. The result provides explicit construction procedures and parameters for several high-performance quantum codes.

Why it may be interesting. While highly abstract, the rigorous algebraic construction of error-correcting codes is foundational to quantum computation theory, providing concrete tools for designing fault-tolerant quantum hardware.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper focuses on constructing complete canonical logical bases for qubit CSS quantum error-correcting codes.

Main result. A polynomial method using quaternary coefficients successfully constructs these canonical logical bases, yielding specific examples with high minimum distances.

Method. The construction involves a multi-stage process: building initial binary checks, assigning quaternary coefficients ($\mathbb{F}_4$) to satisfy commutation relations, constructing logical matrices over $\mathbb{F}_4$, and finally expanding these into binary matrices.

Model / system. The model is the qubit CSS code, defined by binary check matrices $H_X$ and $H_Z$. The construction is applied to Pair-Partition (PP) codes, utilizing circulant permutation matrices (CPMs) and algebraic techniques over polynomial rings.

Key observables. Code parameters $[[n, k, d]]$, check ranks, and the binary weights of the canonical logical representatives (e.g., 25, 27).

Important parameters / regimes. The construction is stated for general block dimensions ($J, L, P$), and examples include parameters like $[[320, 80, 14]]$ and $[[2048, 512, 24]]$.

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes the existence of invertible submatrices on disjoint column sets for the check systems, and it relies on the invertibility of a pairing polynomial $g(z)$.

Figures summary. The notes reference tables summarizing the parameters of seven constructed codes, detailing the resulting $[[n, k, d]]$ values and basis weights.

Paper structure. The paper follows a structured algebraic development: defining the problem (canonical bases), detailing the four stages of construction (initial checks, $\mathbb{F}_4$ assignment, $\mathbb{F}_4$ logical matrices, binary expansion), and concluding with specific examples and parameter reporting.

Abstract

We construct complete canonical logical bases for qubit CSS codes by assigning quaternary coefficients to binary circulant permutation matrix pair-partition (CPM-PP) checks, constructing and normalizing logical representatives, and expanding the result into binary matrices. When the two check systems have invertible submatrices on disjoint column sets, cofactor representatives are canonically paired by the inverse of a single pairing polynomial. The resulting pairs span the entire logical space. When the pairing polynomial is a cyclic-shift monomial with coefficient one, normalization preserves the binary weights of the representatives. We state the construction for general block dimensions and CPM size, work through a corresponding example, and report the parameters, check ranks, and basis weights of seven binary codes. Representative examples have parameters $[[320,80,14]]$, $[[448,112,18]]$, and $[[2048,512,24]]$. All three have maximum check weight 10 on both the X and Z sides. Their canonical logical representatives have binary weights 25, 27, and 27, respectively, on both the X and Z sides.

Minimal output entropy for the channels that add or remove a box of a Young diagram, and a Pauli principle for every permutation symmetry

Authors: Robin Reuvers
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.33498v1

Summary. This theoretical paper studies quantum channels associated with adding or removing a box from a Young diagram, connecting representation theory to quantum information theory. It proves that coherent states minimize the output entropy for these channels and derives a generalized Pauli principle bounding the spectrum of reduced density matrices for systems with permutation symmetry.

Why it may be interesting. The connection between representation theory (Young diagrams) and fundamental quantum information concepts like entropy minimization and generalized uncertainty principles makes this highly relevant for theoretical quantum physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper determines the minimal output entropy for quantum channels that add or remove a single box from a Young diagram, and establishes a generalized Pauli principle for systems exhibiting permutation symmetry.

Main result. Coherent states are proven to minimize the output entropy for the relevant quantum channels, and a sharp bound (generalized Pauli principle) is found for the spectrum of the reduced density matrix.

Method. The analysis uses representation theory of U(d), covariant quantum channels derived from isometric intertwiners, and majorization inequalities analogous to Lieb's conjecture.

Model / system. The model involves quantum channels connecting irreducible representations of U(d) whose Young diagrams differ by one box. The application considers N particles with a given permutation symmetry.

Key observables. Minimal output entropy, the spectrum of the reduced density matrix ($\gamma$), and the optimal output computed using hook lengths.

Important parameters / regimes. The size and structure of the Young diagrams ($\lambda$), the number of particles ($N$), and the dimension $d$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the existence of covariant quantum channels and the applicability of majorization arguments derived from coherent state properties.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the spectrum of the output channel $\Psi^-( ho_0)$ for removing a box, and Figure 2 illustrates the duality between removing a box from $\lambda^+$ and adding a box to its complement.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes four types of quantum channels ($\Phi^\pm, \Psi^\pm$) connecting representations differing by one box, proving the majorization property and identifying coherent states as minimizers. It then applies these results to derive the generalized Pauli principle for reduced density matrices.

Abstract

Two irreducible representations of $U(d)$ whose Young diagrams differ by a single box are connected by four covariant quantum channels: the box can be removed or added, and one keeps either the new diagram or the box ($\mathbb{C}^d$ or its dual). We prove that for each of these channels the output of a coherent state majorizes every other output, so that coherent states minimize the output entropy, and we compute the optimal output explicitly in terms of hook lengths. For the two channels that keep the box we also determine all minimizers; these need not be coherent, even when they are pure. As an application we consider $N$ particles with a given permutation symmetry and find sharp bounds on the spectrum of the reduced density matrix of a single particle, as well as the exact set of spectra that mixed states can reach (for fermions these are the Pauli principle and Coleman's theorem).

Native implementation of the machine-learned Skala exchange-correlation functional in CP2K: Unified one-centre reconstruction for molecular and condensed-phase calculations

Authors: Johann Pototschnig, Franz Pöschel, Jürg Hutter, Thomas D. Kühne
Type: theory · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.34055v1

Summary. This work details the native implementation of the machine-learned Skala exchange-correlation functional in the CP2K code for solid-state physics. The authors introduce a joint one-centre reconstruction technique to ensure accuracy across different basis sets and calculation types. The results demonstrate high accuracy for lattice energies and band gaps, advancing the capability of DFT codes to incorporate complex, data-driven XC functionals.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on solid-state DFT, the rigorous development of a machine-learned functional and its integration into a complex, multi-basis-set framework (GPW/GAPW) showcases advanced computational physics techniques relevant to developing next-generation quantum simulation tools.

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of natively implementing the machine-learned Skala exchange-correlation (XC) functional within the CP2K code for both molecular and condensed-phase electronic structure calculations.

Main result. The implementation successfully achieves low mean absolute errors (MAE) for lattice energies (e.g., 1.16 kJ mol-1 for ice phases) and band gaps (0.43 eV) compared to high-level benchmarks like DMC, while also identifying systematic structural overbinding issues.

Model / system. The calculations are performed on various molecular crystals and the thirteen crystalline ice phases (DMC-ICE13). The underlying theory is Density Functional Theory (DFT) utilizing the machine-learned Skala XC functional.

Key observables. Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for lattice energies (e.g., 1.16 kJ mol-1), MAE for band gaps (0.43 eV), and the resulting equilibrium lattice constants.

Abstract

We implement the machine-learned Skala exchange-correlation (XC) functional natively in CP2K using its Gaussian and plane-wave (GPW) and Gaussian and augmented-plane-wave (GAPW) methods. A joint one-centre reconstruction of density, density gradients, and kinetic-energy density before functional evaluation preserves mixed gradient terms and nonlocal couplings between smooth and atom-local contributions in all-electron and pseudopotential calculations. An XC-specific GAPW representation resolves rapidly varying local contributions on atom-centred grids, reducing plane-wave requirements for pseudopotential calculations. The implementation includes Brillouin-zone sampling and point-group symmetry reduction. With sufficiently flexible orbital bases, it retains the molecular benchmark accuracy of our earlier GauXC formulation. Combining all these techniques, we have obtained an $\mathcal{O}(N^4)$ algorithm for the low-level method; a non-iterative $N_{\rm frag} \mathcal{O}(N^4)$ algorithm for the screened interaction; and an iterative $\mathcal{O}(N_{\rm frag}^6)$ algorithm for the fragment solver, where $N_{\rm frag}$ is the number of fragment orbitals and $N$ is the total number of orbitals. The resulting low-scaling implementation of the MPCC method is applied to trans polyacetylenes, that is, C$_{2n}$H$_{2n+2}$ molecules for chain lengths $n=1-8$, and the timing and accuracy of the method are compared to the original unfactorized implementation. Moreover, the approximations are further validated by applying the method to the potential energy surface of the \ce{O3} molecule. Finally, we apply the method to the selected molecules in the S22 dataset for which the performance of the MP2 method is known to be poor. The results demonstrate that the DF-based implementation of the MPCC method provides a significant reduction in computational cost while maintaining accuracy comparable to that of the original implementation.

Abstract

Quantum linear-system algorithms access the coefficient matrix through a block-encoding oracle $U_A$ and the right-hand side through a state-preparation oracle $U_b$. The optimal query complexities of these two oracles are known separately: $O(κ\log(1/\varepsilon))$ queries to $U_A$ and $O(κ/s)$ queries to $U_b$, where $s=α\|A^{-1}b\|$. Whether a single algorithm can achieve both bounds simultaneously has remained open. We resolve this question affirmatively. Given a constant-factor estimate of $s$, we give a quantum linear-system algorithm that prepares the normalized solution to error $\varepsilon$ using simultaneously $ O\left(κ\log\frac1\varepsilon\right)$ queries to $U_A$ and $O\left(κ/{s}\right)$ queries to $U_b$. The algorithm separates state preparation from precision refinement, so only the matrix-query count depends on the target accuracy. We also prove a matching same-instance worst-case lower bound: for every quantum linear-system algorithm and every solution-norm scale, there exists a single Hermitian instance on which that algorithm requires both $Ω(κ\log(1/\varepsilon))$ matrix queries and $Ω(κ/s)$ state-preparation queries. Thus the two oracle complexities can be optimized simultaneously, and the resulting bounds are jointly optimal.

Abstract

We prove the existence of succinct arguments for QMA, assuming only the existence of collapsing hash functions. This is the first scheme that relies only on unstructured ``Minicrypt'' assumptions, which are not known to imply public-key encryption. Our main technical contribution is a quantum-succinct \emph{claw-state generation} protocol that allows us to bootstrap a small number of quantum correlations into an arbitrarily large number of claw-state correlations, using classical communication only. This improves upon the work of [Zhang, STOC 2021], having better round complexity, a proof in the standard model, and being overall much simpler. This yields a quantum-succinct blind delegation of quantum computation protocol from one-way functions, which we plug into the communication-compression compiler of [Bartusek, Liu, and Malavolta, EUROCRYPT 2026] to obtain succinct arguments for QMA.
