Nash Equilibrium from Quantum-Field Entanglement

Authors: Hao Xu
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.33188v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5 · quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical work explores the intersection of quantum field theory and game theory by modeling a game where payoffs are determined by vacuum entanglement harvested from a quantum field. The authors show that the specific physical regime of the field correlations dictates the number and nature of the stable strategic equilibria. This suggests that quantum correlations can fundamentally alter the predictable outcomes of interactions between quantum observers.

Why it may be interesting. It connects fundamental concepts from quantum field theory (vacuum entanglement, causal structure) directly to the mathematical framework of game theory (Nash equilibria), suggesting entanglement can dictate strategic outcomes.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates whether entanglement harvested from a quantum field can serve as a strategic resource capable of altering the stable equilibrium outcomes in a quantum-input game.

Main result. The game structure allows for $1 + 2n_+$ trembling-hand perfect equilibria, where the number of equilibria is determined by the signs and ordering of rewards derived from different field-correlation regimes.

Method. The authors formulate a quantum-input game using Unruh-DeWitt detectors probing vacuum entanglement, analyzing the resulting payoffs using game theory concepts like trembling-hand perfect equilibria.

Model / system. The model involves two spacelike-separated Unruh-DeWitt detectors interacting with a free massless scalar field in (3+1)D Minkowski spacetime. The game's payoffs are derived from the harvested vacuum entanglement.

Key observables. Mean reward $g(s)$, entanglement margin $\sqrt{\mathcal{H}^2+\mathcal{Q}^2}-\mathcal{P}$, and the number of perfect equilibria $NTHP$.

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis depends on field correlation contributions ($\mathcal{H}, \mathcal{Q}$), the local excitation noise ($\mathcal{P}$), and timing parameters ($\ell, T, \Delta$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is performed to leading perturbative order in the coupling constant $\lambda$, and the game structure is fixed by the choice of an entanglement witness.

Figures summary. Tables summarize the count of positive-reward active equilibria ($n_+$) for five distinct physical settings, mapping specific field-correlation regimes to unique equilibrium counts.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by defining the physical setup (detectors/field), deriving the entanglement-based payoff function, applying game theory to count the equilibria, and finally mapping specific field-correlation regimes (spacelike, lobes, etc.) to distinct stable strategic structures.

Abstract

Entanglement harvesting transfers quantum field correlations to localized probes, yet whether these correlations can alter stable strategic behavior has remained unclear. We formulate a quantum-input game in which two Unruh-DeWitt detectors harvest vacuum entanglement from a free massless scalar field in $(3+1)$-dimensional Minkowski spacetime. A referee converts this state into payoffs using independent question qubits and phase-calibrated local Bell measurements. To leading perturbative order, the mean reward is proportional to the signed entanglement margin $\sqrt{\mathcal{H}^2+\mathcal{Q}^2}-\mathcal{P}$, where $\mathcal{H}$ is the Hadamard correlation contribution, $\mathcal{Q}$ is the Pauli-Jordan causal contribution, and $\mathcal{P}$ is the local excitation noise. Away from payoff degeneracies, allowing each player to exit or to choose an early or late interaction yields $1+2n_+$ trembling-hand perfect equilibria, where $n_+$ counts the positive-reward timing equilibria. For one fixed protocol, five analytically certified settings realize the sequence $7\to3\to1\to7\to1$. They represent the spacelike region, the first commutator lobe, central carrier cancellation, the second lobe, and the post-wavefront Huygens regime. Thus, within this fixed quantum-input verification protocol, distinct field-correlation regimes generate different stable strategic structures.

Optimal Query Complexity for Ground-State Preparation

Authors: Boyang Chen, Minbo Gao, Xinzhao Wang, Shuo Zhou
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.35668v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5 · quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper rigorously determines the optimal query complexity for preparing a quantum state near the ground state of a Hamiltonian. By combining spectral filtering with amplitude amplification, the authors provide improved bounds for both expected and worst-case query counts. These results are significant for understanding the resource requirements of quantum simulation algorithms.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides rigorous, optimized resource estimates for a fundamental task in quantum simulation—finding the ground state—which is crucial for designing practical quantum algorithms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper determines the optimal query complexity required to prepare a quantum state close to the ground state of a Hamiltonian, given knowledge of an energy threshold in the spectral gap.

Main result. They derive both expected and worst-case query complexity bounds for both the Hamiltonian oracle ($U_H$) and the state-preparation oracle ($U_I$), matching established lower bounds in both regimes.

Method. The approach combines spectral filtering (using QSVT) with amplitude amplification in a two-stage process to suppress excited states and boost the ground state overlap to the desired fidelity.

Model / system. The system involves a Hamiltonian $H$ with a unique ground state $|\psi_0 angle$, accessed via an $\alpha$-block-encoding $U_H$. A state-preparation oracle $U_I$ is assumed to provide an initial state $|\phi angle$ with a guaranteed overlap $\gamma$ with $|\psi_0 angle$.

Key observables. Trace-distance error ($\epsilon$), expected query count ($E[q_H], E[q_I]$), and worst-case query count ($Q_H, Q_I$).

Important parameters / regimes. Energy gap parameter ($\Delta$), block encoding parameter ($\alpha$), initial overlap ($\gamma$), and target error ($\epsilon$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on knowing an energy threshold $\mu$ that separates the ground state energy from all excited state energies by at least $\Delta/2$.

Figures summary. The notes mention a Table 1 comparing query bounds across different algorithms (Lin–Tong, expected, worst-case).

Paper structure. The paper establishes the problem, details the two-stage algorithm combining spectral filtering and amplitude amplification, presents the derived expected and worst-case query complexity theorems, and finally proves matching lower bounds.

Abstract

We determine the optimal query complexity of ground-state preparation to trace-distance error $\varepsilon$ when an energy threshold in the spectral gap is known. Let $U_H$ be an $α$-block-encoding of a Hamiltonian with unique ground state $|ψ_0\rangle$, and suppose $|\langleψ_0|U_I|0\rangle|\geγ$ for a state-preparation oracle $U_I$. The threshold lies at least $Δ/2$ above the ground-state energy and at least $Δ/2$ below every excited-state energy. We give two algorithms that prepare a state within trace distance $\varepsilon$ of the ground state. One uses $O((α/Δ)(γ^{-1}+\log(1/\varepsilon)))$ calls to $U_H$ in expectation; the other uses $O((α/(γΔ))\log(1/\varepsilon))$ calls to $U_H$ in the worst case. We prove a lower bound matching the expected query count; the corresponding worst-case lower bound follows from Somma and de Wolf [SdW26]. The respective bounds on calls to $U_I$ are $O(1/γ)$ in expectation and $O(γ^{-1}\log(1/\varepsilon))$ in the worst case. On $(N+1)$-dimensional systems, these $U_I$ bounds are also optimal when the expected or worst-case count of $U_H$ calls, respectively, is $o((α/Δ)\sqrt N)$. Both algorithms use a constant-accuracy spectral filter to construct a purifier, which we then sequentially compose during amplitude amplification to prepare a state with constant overlap with the ground state. The expected-query algorithm repeats the preparation followed by one high-accuracy spectral filter until success. The worst-case algorithm uses filters of increasing accuracy and limits the total number of queries.

Summary. This work tackles the challenge of connecting distinct non-invertible SPT phases using quantum circuits. The authors develop and construct 'symmetry-permuting entanglers' realized as Matrix Product Unitaries. These circuits successfully demonstrate the realization of TQFT dualities by inducing specific anyon permutations on the system's order parameters.

Why it may be interesting. The connection between abstract topological field theory dualities (like FAD) and concrete, finite-depth quantum circuit realizations is highly relevant for understanding the computational power and physical constraints of topological quantum memory.

Main problem. The paper addresses how to characterize and connect different Symmetry-Protected Topological (SPT) phases, particularly when the underlying symmetry is non-invertible.

Main result. The authors construct explicit symmetry-permuting entanglers, realized as Matrix Product Unitary circuits, that successfully connect distinct $ ext{Rep}(D_8)$ SPT phases, realizing dualities predicted by TQFT.

Model / system. The focus is on 1+1D SPT phases characterized by a fusion category $C$, specifically examining the $ ext{Rep}(D_8)$ symmetry group. The physics is analyzed using the framework of Topological Quantum Field Theory (TQFT).

Key observables. String order parameters, anyon permutations (generating the $S_3$ group), and the action of the entangler on these parameters.

Important parameters / regimes. The bond dimension of the Matrix Product Unitary (MPU) circuit, and the specific structure of the non-invertible symmetry group $D_8$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the existence of a microscopic realization for 'active dualities' via a finite-depth local circuit, and initially assumes the MPU tensor has bond dimension two.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the theoretical need for symmetry-permuting entanglers for non-invertible symmetries. It then develops the mathematical machinery by deriving the constraints on the MPU tensor, constructing the explicit circuit for the $ ext{Rep}(D_8)$ case, and finally verifying the resulting anyon transformations.

Abstract

1+1D symmetry-protected topological (SPT) phases with invertible symmetry can be characterized by the symmetric entanglers: globally symmetric finite-depth local unitary circuits that generate representative ground states from a product state. For non-invertible symmetries, however, distinct SPT phases, such as the three $\mathrm{Rep}(D_8)$ SPT phases, may not be connected by any symmetric entangler. We show that the three $\mathrm{Rep}(D_8)$ SPT phases can nevertheless be connected by \emph{symmetry-permuting} finite-depth local unitary circuits, realizing dualities predicted by topological quantum field theory (TQFT). We construct an explicit matrix product unitary connecting two of the $\mathrm{Rep}(D_8)$ SPT phases, and show that its action on string order parameters reproduces the anyon permutation predicted by the TQFT duality.

An exponential strong converse for private communication over degradable quantum channels

Authors: Mark M. Wilde
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.32515v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical paper proves an exponential strong converse for the private capacity of degradable quantum channels. It achieves this by developing sophisticated mathematical tools, including an overlap bound for privacy tests and a single-filter reduction, which are combined with established techniques like signed averaging. The result provides a fundamental limit on how much secret information can be reliably transmitted over such quantum channels.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on quantum communication theory, the rigorous mathematical techniques involving entropy bounds, channel capacity, and error analysis are fundamental to understanding the limits of quantum information processing.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to establish an exponential strong converse for the unassisted private capacity of all finite-dimensional degradable quantum channels.

Main result. It proves that at any rate strictly above the channel's maximum coherent information, the optimized fidelity decays exponentially with the number of channel uses.

Method. The proof combines an overlap bound for privacy tests with a single-filter reduction technique, utilizing the signed averaging construction to establish the finite-block converse.

Model / system. The study concerns quantum communication over degradable quantum channels, focusing on the secure transmission of secret keys.

Key observables. Private capacity, infidelity (error criterion), coherent information, and the overlap between receiver placements.

Important parameters / regimes. Key size (M), channel uses (n), and the rate relative to the coherent information threshold.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on using a joint fidelity criterion and establishing bounds on the dimension of common quantum systems via Gaussian filters.

Paper structure. The structure involves developing key ingredients—the overlap bound and the single-filter reduction—and then combining them with the signed averaging construction to derive the final exponential strong converse bound.

Abstract

We prove an exponential strong converse for the unassisted private capacity of every finite-dimensional degradable quantum channel. We use a single error criterion: the infidelity between the actual message-estimate-environment state and an ideal state consisting of uniformly distributed, perfectly correlated messages that are independent of the environment. At every rate strictly above the channel's maximum coherent information, the optimized fidelity to this ideal state decays exponentially in the number of channel uses. The same bound holds when the ideal environment state is fixed to the actual environment marginal. The proof applies to arbitrary mixed-state encoders and arbitrary collective decoders. Its main operator ingredient is an overlap bound for privacy tests: two receiver placements with a common subsystem of dimension $d_C$ have overlap at most $d_C/M$, where $M$ is the key size, independently of both shield dimensions. We combine this bound with the signed averaging construction presented in arXiv:2608.01308 and a single-filter reduction to symmetric states. A Gaussian filter replaces the common receiver subsystem by a system of controlled dimension from which the original correlations can be approximately recovered using quantum side information. The filter preserves exact exchange symmetry, and a uniform smooth-entropy estimate bounds its output dimension for arbitrary correlated channel inputs. We also obtain an exponential strong converse at zero for antidegradable channels under the same joint criterion and, consequently, for the private capacity of the quantum erasure channel throughout its parameter range.

Bounding Retraining Equivalence and the Deletion Floor in Materials Machine Unlearning

Authors: Can Polat, Mustafa Kurban, Erchin Serpedin, Hasan Kurban
Type: both · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.35635v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces the 'deletion floor' as a robust metric to quantify the expected residual error when performing machine unlearning on materials data. It develops theoretical tools to bound this error based on retraining equivalence and provides empirical evidence showing that structural redundancy significantly stabilizes predictions after data deletion. This shifts the focus of unlearning evaluation from simple post-deletion accuracy to a comprehensive view of retained model utility.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on ML, the rigorous mathematical framework for bounding information retention and residual error after data removal shares conceptual parallels with understanding system stability or information leakage in complex quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Standard metrics for machine unlearning in materials science are ambiguous because related retained data can maintain accurate predictions even after a record is deleted.

Main result. The authors propose the 'deletion floor' as the definitive metric, showing that unlearning evaluations must report reference loss, prediction change, and retained utility together.

Method. The work develops theoretical bounds using retraining equivalence and employs exact ridge regression identities to calculate the deletion floor and residual fit.

Model / system. The study applies to machine learning models trained on materials datasets, mapping crystal structures and compositions to target properties like formation energy.

Key observables. Deletion floor ($\Phi$), Prediction Change, Retained Utility, Loss Gap ($g_i$), and retraining loss.

Important parameters / regimes. The size of the retained relative set, the regularization parameter ($\lambda$), and the equivalence parameters ($\epsilon, \delta$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on strong convexity assumptions for theoretical bounds and assumes that the deletion floor resolves the ambiguity of post-deletion accuracy.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate that related materials can keep a removed record predictable, defining the deletion floor as the expected retraining loss, and show the drop in retraining loss when retained relatives remain.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from identifying the ambiguity in unlearning metrics to defining the deletion floor, deriving theoretical bounds using retraining equivalence, and validating these concepts using both synthetic and real-world materials datasets.

Abstract

In materials machine learning, closely related retained structures can sustain accurate property predictions even after removing a specific record, rendering post-deletion prediction error an ambiguous metric for machine unlearning. To resolve this ambiguity, we define the deletion floor as the expected target loss under a specified retraining procedure at the deleted request. Standard indistinguishability constraints yield a sharp interval bounding an update's target loss around this baseline reference. Theoretically, a conditional neighbor bound links a low deletion floor directly to retained fit, prediction regularity, and local label agreement, while an exact ridge identity isolates residual fit from the prediction change induced by record deletion. Empirically, controlled redundancy sweeps show an $\approx 8\times$ drop in median normalized retraining loss when one retained relative remains after deletion. Across two distinct fitting regimes in a paired Materials Project study, the lower-floor regime also exhibits a larger prediction change on more than 50% of the shared requests. Systematic comparisons against approximate updates and the original model decouple deliberate target suppression from preserved overall model utility. Consequently, request-level unlearning evaluations should report reference loss, prediction change, and retained utility together, interpreting post-deletion accuracy against what retraining itself leaves behind.

Summary. This theoretical paper proves that the modular data $(S, T, W)$ is not a complete set of invariants for classifying Modular Tensor Categories. The authors construct a family of inequivalent categories and use the partition function on a closed 3-manifold to distinguish them, showing that higher-order topological invariants are necessary for a full classification.

Why it may be interesting. While highly abstract, the techniques of using topological invariants (like 3-manifold partition functions) to distinguish between mathematically equivalent but physically distinct quantum field theories (MTCs) are relevant to understanding the robustness of quantum information encoded in topological states.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the open question of whether the modular data $(S, T)$ together with the Whitehead-link matrix $W$ uniquely determine a Modular Tensor Category (MTC) up to ribbon equivalence.

Main result. The authors prove negatively that $(S, T, W)$ are insufficient to determine an MTC, constructing a family of braided-inequivalent categories with identical invariants.

Method. They use link invariants (Reshetikhin-Turaev type) and, crucially, the uncolored partition function on a closed oriented three-manifold to distinguish between categories that share the same low-order invariants.

Model / system. The model involves Dijkgraaf-Witten modular categories associated with the group $(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^3$, whose structure is analyzed using 3-cocycles and cohomology classes.

Key observables. The uncolored partition function $Z_u(M_p)$ on a 3-manifold $M_p$ derived from a specific link surgery.

Important parameters / regimes. Prime $p \ge 5$, the structure of the group $(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^3$, and the cocycle parameters $u$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes specific normalizations for link invariants and relies on the existence of a single vacuum-preserving bijection to equate the low-order invariants.

Figures summary. The notes do not describe specific figures, but the structure implies comparisons between invariants derived from different link components (1-component vs 2-component) and the final 3-component manifold invariant.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first showing agreement of link invariants for 1- and 2-component links (Section 4), then establishing algebraic constraints on equivalence (Section 5), and finally using the 3-manifold partition function (Section 7) to provide the definitive obstruction.

Abstract

It remains an open question whether the modular data $(S,T)$ together with the Whitehead-link matrix $W$ determine a modular tensor category up to ribbon equivalence. We answer negatively by constructing two braided-inequivalent Dijkgraaf--Witten modular categories for $(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^3$ for prime $p\ge5$ with identical $(S,T,W)$ and, more generally, identical Reshetikhin--Turaev invariants of all framed oriented links with at most two components under a single bijection of simple objects. The construction varies an alternating $3$-cocycle while fixing a quadratic cohomology class, using restriction to subgroups of rank at most two to match link invariants and the quadratic and alternating classes together to obstruct braided equivalence. A complete classification of modular tensor categories up to ribbon equivalence therefore requires invariants beyond those of colored framed links with at most two components. We also analytically evaluate an uncolored partition function on a closed oriented three-manifold that separates all $(p-1)/2$ equivalence classes in the family.

Summary. This theoretical work analyzes how thermal equilibrium states (KMS states) of infinite fermionic lattices change when the system Hamiltonian is perturbed. Using advanced tools like Quantum Belief Propagation and Lieb-Robinson bounds, the authors prove that strong initial locality properties lead to specific, quantifiable decay rates in the perturbed state. This advances the understanding of stability and locality in quantum many-body physics.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous connection between correlation decay, local perturbations, and local indistinguishability provides powerful tools for understanding how local physics dictates global thermal properties in interacting quantum many-body systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the stability of locality properties of thermal (KMS) states in infinite fermionic lattice systems when the Hamiltonian is subjected to perturbations.

Main result. It shows that under certain decay conditions, exponential decay of correlations and local indistinguishability for the initial state imply stretched-exponential decay for the perturbed state.

Method. The authors employ Quantum Belief Propagation (QBP) formulated on the $C^*$-algebra, combined with Lieb-Robinson bounds, to establish rigorous mathematical implications between various notions of locality.

Model / system. The system is an infinite fermionic lattice described by a $C^*$-algebra, with the study focusing on the $( au, eta)$-KMS state and its response to extensive perturbations $V$.

Important parameters / regimes. The decay rates (exponential vs. stretched-exponential), the interaction norm of the perturbation, and the temperature parameter $eta$.

Paper structure. The paper builds mathematically by first establishing the $C^*$-dynamical system framework, then applying QBP to characterize perturbed KMS states, and finally using Lieb-Robinson bounds to derive the decay estimates for locality.

Abstract

We study how thermal equilibrium states of infinite fermionic lattices respond to perturbations of the Hamiltonian. Our main result concerns extensive perturbations of a chosen KMS state. We show that exponential decay of correlations and exponential local indistinguishability under the extensive perturbation yield stretched-exponential decay of correlations for the perturbed state. Neither the initial nor the perturbed state is required to be unique, allowing stability to be studied in the presence of phase coexistence. Our approach uses Quantum Belief Propagation (QBP), which we establish directly on the underlying $C^*$-algebra. Combining QBP with Lieb--Robinson bounds, we show implications between decay of correlations, the principle that local perturbations perturb locally, and local indistinguishability, closing the cycle for perturbations of the tracial state under the stated decay, support and uniformity assumptions. For extensive perturbations under uniform clustering conditions, we also obtain a differentiable path of local equilibrium expectations and bound their change linearly in the interaction norm of the perturbation.

Localization in tight-binding models with power-law distributed couplings

Authors: Maximilian Weigmann, Luca Schaefer, Barbara Drossel

Summary. This work investigates how disorder, characterized by power-law couplings, affects electron localization in 1D and 2D tight-binding models. By comparing the Laplacian and adjacency matrices, the authors show that while the adjacency matrix always yields localized modes, the Laplacian reveals system-spanning modes due to conservation laws. The critical finding is a dimension-dependent transition point in 2D that dictates when transport shifts from extended to localized behavior.

Why it may be interesting. The study provides quantitative, dimension-dependent criteria ($s > 0.75$ in 2D) for the transition between delocalized and localized transport regimes in disordered lattices, which has implications for understanding energy/heat transport in disordered materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the localization properties of one-dimensional and two-dimensional tight-binding models subjected to power-law distributed couplings.

Main result. The Laplacian matrix always yields system-spanning modes due to conservation laws, while the localization behavior in 2D depends critically on the coupling exponent $s$, transitioning from system-spanning to localized modes when $s > 0.75$.

Method. The analysis employs numerical diagonalization of the adjacency and Laplacian matrices, calculating diagnostics like the Participation Ratio ($P_i$) and analyzing the resulting probability distribution functions (PDFs) and density of states (DOS).

Model / system. The models are 1D and 2D tight-binding systems featuring power-law distributed couplings $f(t) \sim t^{-s}$. Two primary Hamiltonians are analyzed: the Random-Coupling Model (RCM) using the weighted adjacency matrix, and the Diffusion Model (DM) using the discretized Laplacian matrix.

Key observables. Participation Ratio ($P_i$), Probability Distribution of $P$ ($ ext{PDF}(P)$), Density of States ($ ext{DOS}(E)$), and the relation between $P$ and $E$.

Important parameters / regimes. The power-law exponent $s$ (controlling disorder strength), system dimension (1D vs. 2D), and system size $N$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis distinguishes between RCM (bond disorder) and DM (site/bond disorder correlated via $\sum_m t_{nm} = 0$), and it notes that finite-size effects are a consideration.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the $ ext{PDF}(P)$, $ ext{DOS}(E)$, and $P$ vs $E$ relationships for 1D and 2D systems, showing how these distributions change as the disorder exponent $s$ is varied.

Paper structure. The paper systematically compares the localization diagnostics (Participation Ratio, DOS) between the Laplacian (DM) and the Adjacency matrix (RCM) in 1D and 2D. It derives specific power-law behaviors for the PDF of $P$ in 1D and identifies critical thresholds for mode localization in 2D.

Abstract

We study the localization properties of 1D and 2D tight-binding models with power-law distributed couplings by comparing the spectrum and the localization properties of the eigenmodes of the Laplacian and the adjacency matrix, using numerical diagonalization of these matrices for different system sizes and connectivities. These two matrices are relevant for different types of dynamical processes. While all eigenmodes of the adjacency matrix are localized for sufficiently large system sizes, the Laplacian matrix always leads to a small proportion of system-spanning modes due to a conservation law, and therefore to power-law tails in the probability distribution of the participation ratio and its relation to the eigenvalues. In one dimension, the exponent of these power laws change continuously with the exponent that characterizes the distribution of couplings. In two dimensions, the modes with the largest relaxation times change from system-spanning to localized when the exponent of the distribution of couplings becomes larger than 0.75. We provide phenomenological explanations for all these findings.

Abstract

We develop a rational-function approximation for the structural properties of multicomponent fluids with narrow square-well and square-shoulder interactions, together with weakly nonadditive hard-sphere mixtures. The theory is obtained by reformulating the analytical Percus--Yevick solution for additive sticky hard-sphere mixtures so as to account for finite interaction widths, leading to a unified semianalytical framework for these classes of fluids. The approximation satisfies the exact low-density limit and preserves the continuity of the cavity functions; a simple local correction restores the continuity of their first derivatives. Comparison with Monte Carlo simulations for binary mixtures shows that the theory accurately describes both the radial distribution functions and their Fourier transforms for narrow wells and shoulders and for weak nonadditivity. The proposed formulation extends previous sticky-hard-sphere-based approaches while retaining their analytical simplicity.

Abstract

Entanglement and nonlocal non-stabilizerness are distinct quantum resources encoded in the Schmidt spectrum of a bipartite pure state. For qubit systems, we establish a universal quadratic constraint on their simultaneous allocation, governed by the minimum number of logical qubits required on each side to encode the occupied Schmidt support. Additional spectral constraints reveal a strong asymmetry in their allocation with the Entanglement that can exhaust the logical capacity, whereas the maximal nonlocal non-stabilizerness grows only logarithmically with it. For a tensor-network cut crossed by a single separating virtual edge, the logical capacity is determined by the occupied bond dimension. We further derive a one-sided certificate for the resources of the untruncated state from a retained Schmidt spectrum and its discarded weight. For small discarded weight, the resulting corrections are linear in that weight. These results connect a universal resource budget to resource-aware control of tensor-network truncations.
