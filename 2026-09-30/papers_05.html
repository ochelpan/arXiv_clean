Enhanced sliding in the coexisting Charge Density Wave phase of strained TbTe3

Authors: Antoine Gallo Frantz, Aleksander Sinchenko, Luc Ortega, Abdellali Hadj Azzem, Jose Emilio Lorenzo, Pierre Monceau, David Le Bolloch, Vincent Jacques
Type: experiment · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.34569v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Frenkel-Kontorova 3/5 · driven-dissipative phase transition 1/5 · non-equilibrium universality 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work investigates how mechanical tensile strain affects the sliding dynamics of Charge Density Waves (CDWs) in $ ext{TbTe}_3$. By measuring electrical transport properties across different CDW phases—pristine, strain-induced, and coexisting—the authors confirm that the strain-induced phase also exhibits incommensurate sliding behavior. This research opens new avenues for understanding CDW physics in quasi-2D materials under external mechanical tuning.

Why it may be interesting. The study provides experimental evidence of how mechanical strain can tune and reveal the fundamental dynamics of CDW order in quasi-2D systems, which is a key area of research in correlated electron physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the sliding dynamics of Charge Density Wave (CDW) phases in strained $ ext{TbTe}_3$, comparing the pristine $c$-oriented $ ext{CDW}_c$ with the strain-induced $a$-oriented $ ext{CDW}_a$. It also examines the behavior in the coexistence phase.

Main result. The strain-induced $ ext{CDW}_a$ phase exhibits sliding characteristics similar to $ ext{CDW}_c$, confirming its incommensurate nature. Furthermore, sliding occurs in both CDW phases with reduced threshold currents in the coexistence phase.

Method. The research uses four-probe resistivity measurements to obtain current-voltage ($V(I)$) characteristics, which are analyzed to determine the threshold voltage ($V_{th}$) associated with CDW sliding, while simultaneously applying uniaxial tensile stresses.

Model / system. The physical system is $ ext{TbTe}_3$, a rare-earth tritelluride exhibiting CDW phases. The material is quasi-2D and its CDW order can be tuned by applying uniaxial tensile stress along the $a$ and $c$ axes, leading to distinct $ ext{CDW}_c$ and $ ext{CDW}_a$ phases.

Key observables. Current-voltage characteristics, differential resistances, threshold current/voltage ($V_{th}$), and lattice parameters ($a, c$) measured via XRD.

Important parameters / regimes. Applied uniaxial tensile stress (along $a$ and $c$ axes), temperature ($T$), and the $a/c$ lattice ratio.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the observed electrical non-linearities are due to the collective sliding motion of the incommensurate CDW order parameter.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the crystal structure and phase diagram, sample geometry, the evolution of lattice parameters vs. applied force, and the temperature dependence of the threshold voltage for both CDW phases.

Paper structure. The paper systematically probes the sliding dynamics by measuring electrical transport properties across the pure $ ext{CDW}_c$, pure $ ext{CDW}_a$, and the coexisting phase, correlating these electrical measurements with structural changes induced by strain.

Abstract

In the last years, the application of mechanical tensile stress has been shown to trigger unconventional phases in quantum materials. Recently, an orientational transition of the charge density wave (CDW) of TbTe3 was reported under the application of moderate uniaxial tensile stress, associated to the in-plane a/c lattice anisotropy. This leads to the disappearance of the pristine c-oriented CDW (CDWc) and to the appearance of the orthogonal a-oriented one (CDWa). A coexistence phase with a superposition of CDWa and CDWc appears when a=c. Here, we probe the sliding dynamics of both CDWc and CDWa as a function of applied tensile stress, i.e. in the pure CDWc, pure CDWa and coexistence phases, by measuring current-voltage characteristics and associated differential resistances. If the pristine CDWc has been known to display non-linear dynamics and thus sliding for several years, we show here that the strain-induced CDWa phase also displays the same characteristics, proving its incommensurate nature. Moreover, we show that sliding also takes place both for CDWc and CDWa, with reduced threshold currents, in the coexistence phase, which questions the microstructure of these coexisting orthogonal CDWs in real space. Finally, the linearity of the threshold fields is kept for all deformation states, with finite threshold fields at Tc, which is discussed in terms of commensurability and pinning potentials. This work opens new questions for the theoretical description of sliding CDWs in quasi-2D systems.

How many photons can a molecule scatter before its coherence is lost?

Authors: M. R. Tarbutt
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.33475v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 3/5 · correlated / nonlocal dissipation 1/5 · quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical paper calculates the decoherence rate for molecular qubits due to photon scattering from optical traps. It shows that while molecular decoherence is generally high, the rate can be significantly suppressed by choosing specific trapping laser wavelengths where the molecular polarizabilities are minimized. This guides the experimental search for robust quantum platforms.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses a critical decoherence mechanism in molecular quantum simulation platforms, providing quantitative methods to engineer 'magic' trapping wavelengths that extend coherence times.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper determines the decoherence rate of molecular qubits held in optical traps due to photon scattering from the trapping lasers.

Main result. Decoherence can be suppressed at specific 'magic wavelengths' where the molecular polarizabilities ($\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$) are simultaneously small, allowing for long coherence times.

Method. The decoherence rate is calculated by summing contributions from Raman and Rayleigh scattering, expressed in terms of the vector and tensor parts of the molecular polarizability.

Model / system. The system involves ultracold diatomic molecules (e.g., CaF) used as qubits, trapped in conservative optical traps. The analysis distinguishes between 1$\Sigma$ and 2$\Sigma$ molecular states.

Key observables. Decoherence rate ($\Gamma_{sc}$), coherence time, and the scattering rates ($R_{ ext{Raman}}$, $R_{ ext{Rayleigh}}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Molecular polarizabilities ($\alpha_1, \alpha_2$), trapping laser wavelength ($\lambda$), and the detuning from resonances.

Assumptions / limitations. The calculation often assumes the light frequency is far detuned from resonances, and approximations are made regarding the overlap integrals for vibrational transitions.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the dependence of Rayleigh and Raman scattering rates on wavelength for CaF, highlighting specific wavelength regions where these rates are minimized.

Paper structure. The paper develops the formalism for calculating scattering rates using the Kramers-Heisenberg formula, derives the total decoherence rate ($\Gamma_{sc} = \Gamma_{ ext{Raman}} + \Gamma_{ ext{Rayleigh}}$), and then applies this to specific molecular species to identify optimal trapping wavelengths.

Abstract

Molecular qubits held in conservative optical traps may lose coherence when they scatter photons from the trapping lasers. The rate for this process has two parts, one equal to the mean Raman scattering rate of the two qubit states, and the other proportional to the square of the difference in their Rayleigh scattering amplitudes. This paper calculates the decoherence rate, expressing results in terms of the vector and tensor parts of the molecular polarizability, $α_1$ and $α_2$. While the decoherence rate is generally much larger for molecules than for atoms, it is suppressed at wavelengths where $α_1$ and $α_2$ are both small. The wavelengths where this occurs are identified for CaF molecules.

Nuclear scattering phase shifts with factorized geometric-time RODEO on a quantum processor

Authors: Myeong-Hwan Mun, Jubin Park, Myung-Ki Cheoun, Eunja Ha
Type: both · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.34298v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: QC/QI experiment 3/5 · quantum measurements 2/5

Summary. This research demonstrates a method to reconstruct neutron-proton scattering phase shifts by measuring trapped energy levels on a quantum processor. The authors introduce and validate a novel, resource-efficient quantum circuit implementation (FG-R6) that significantly reduces circuit depth and gate count compared to prior methods. This work establishes a rigorous benchmark showing that quantum algorithm performance must be evaluated based on the fidelity of the final physical observable, not just local energy measurements.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly connects the abstract performance metrics of quantum algorithms (circuit depth, gate count) to a concrete, physically measurable observable (nuclear scattering phase shifts), providing a crucial benchmark for quantum simulation in nuclear physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of reconstructing fundamental nuclear scattering phase shifts from trapped spectra using quantum processors.

Main result. A factorized geometric-time RODEO circuit (FG-R6) significantly outperforms a standard ten-cycle direct RODEO circuit (direct R10) in terms of quantum circuit resources and accuracy for phase shift reconstruction.

Method. The authors compare two quantum circuit implementations (FG-R6 vs. direct R10) for the RODEO algorithm, utilizing circuit compression and geometric time evolution to measure energy levels and extrapolate scattering parameters.

Model / system. The physical system is the elastic s-wave neutron-proton (np) interaction, modeled using a schematic square-well potential. Measurements are performed on trapped spectra using a quantum processor (IBM Aachen).

Key observables. Elastic s-wave neutron-proton phase shifts ($\delta_0$), median compiled depth, two-qubit-gate count, and maximum central phase-shift deviation.

Important parameters / regimes. Energy grid ranging from 0.1 to 30.0 MeV; circuit cycle counts (6 vs. 10); and various spectral tolerances.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on extrapolating trap-dependent energy levels to free space using the classical Modified Effective Range Expansion (MERE).

Figures summary. Figures compare the cycle structure of the two quantum circuits and show the reduction in phase-shift deviation achieved by FG-R6 compared to direct R10 across the energy grid.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem of phase shift reconstruction, details the two competing quantum circuit methods (R10 vs. FG-R6), presents quantitative comparisons of circuit resources and achieved accuracy, and concludes by emphasizing the importance of assessing circuit performance via the full scattering reconstruction.

Abstract

We reconstruct elastic $s$-wave neutron-proton ($np$) phase shifts from trapped spectra measured on IBM Aachen using a compressed RODEO circuit. For a schematic square-well interaction represented by classically constructed four-dimensional effective Hamiltonians, four positive-energy levels at five trap strengths supply twenty inputs to classical modified effective range expansion (MERE) extrapolation to free space. The six-cycle factorized geometric-time RODEO implementation (FG-R6) combines ancilla reuse, exact query-phase separation, post-compilation binding, and six numerically optimized geometric evolution times. Numerical cycle-count tests support this choice within the adopted local spectral tolerances. At the same three-qubit width and equal shot budgets, FG-R6 reduces median compiled depth and two-qubit-gate count by 33.1% and 37.2% relative to ten-cycle dynamic direct RODEO (direct R10), increases the median fitted amplitude, and lowers median finite-shot energy uncertainty from 0.610 to 0.534 keV. For this dataset's primary fit on the 0.1-30.0 MeV grid, the maximum central phase-shift deviation from exact-energy MERE decreases from $0.842^\circ$ to $0.163^\circ$, despite a larger root-mean-square (RMS) trapped-energy deviation. The full-grid gain arises mainly in the low-energy extrapolation region; direct R10 has slightly smaller residuals relative to the same reference on the 10.0-30.0 MeV common energy-interpolation subset. The FG-R6 central curve differs from the analytical square-well solution by at most $0.156^\circ$. Fit-form sensitivity remains appreciable and is assessed separately from finite-shot uncertainty. This reduced-space benchmark connects RODEO circuit compression to nuclear continuum observables and shows why circuit performance must be assessed through the scattering reconstruction and its energy range, not spectral RMS errors alone.

Quantum Fisher Information as the Speed Limit for Multipartite Entanglement

Authors: Zain H. Saleem, Da-Wei Luo, Anjala M. Babu, Ting Yu, Stephen K. Gray, Anil Shaji
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.31853v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 3/5 · quantum measurements 2/5

Summary. This work establishes that the Quantum Fisher Information (QFI) sets a universal upper bound on the speed of multipartite entanglement generation. By analyzing models like the TFIM, the authors show that while QFI can be strongly enhanced at quantum critical points, the rate of entanglement change may remain low. This reveals a decoupling between maximal parameter sensitivity and rapid entanglement growth.

Why it may be interesting. It connects fundamental concepts from quantum estimation theory (QFI) directly to the dynamics of multipartite entanglement in many-body systems, providing a quantitative measure of how fast quantum correlations can evolve.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper derives a universal upper bound on the speed at which multipartite entanglement can be generated during parameter-dependent quantum evolution.

Main result. The rate of change of generalized concurrence is bounded by the square root of the Quantum Fisher Information ($\left| rac{dC_N}{dg} ight| \leq \sqrt{F_Q}$), demonstrating QFI as a fundamental speed limit for entanglement generation.

Method. The primary proof utilizes the Symmetric Logarithmic Derivative (SLD) formalism from quantum estimation theory, complemented by Schmidt-spectrum analysis for bipartite cases.

Model / system. The results are illustrated using interacting many-body spin models, specifically the Transverse-Field Ising Model (TFIM), and general pure multipartite states undergoing differentiable evolution.

Key observables. Generalized Concurrence ($C_N$), Quantum Fisher Information ($F_Q$), and the rate of change of concurrence ($ rac{dC_N}{dg}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The critical point of the TFIM ($g=h$) is investigated, where $F_Q$ is enhanced while entanglement change is suppressed.

Assumptions / limitations. The general bound is not saturated away from the product-state boundary, although a tighter bound exists for bipartitions with Schmidt rank exactly two.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the dynamics of concurrence, its rate of change, and $\sqrt{F_Q}$ for the isotropic N-qubit Pauli interaction, showing $\sqrt{F_Q}$ acts as an envelope for the entanglement rate.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the general bound using SLD theory, then analyzes saturation conditions using Schmidt decomposition, and finally applies these concepts to the TFIM to show that quantum criticality does not necessitate rapid entanglement changes.

Abstract

We derive a universal upper bound on the speed of multipartite entanglement generation. For arbitrary differentiable pure multipartite states undergoing parameter-dependent evolution, we prove that the rate of change of generalized concurrence is bounded by the square root of the quantum Fisher information (QFI). The proof follows directly from the symmetric logarithmic derivative formalism, revealing the bound as a consequence of quantum estimation theory, while a complementary Schmidt-spectrum analysis identifies the conditions for saturation and yields a tighter bound for rank-two bipartitions. We illustrate these results in interacting many-body spin models and investigate their behavior across the quantum phase transition of the transverse-field Ising model. At the critical point, we find that the QFI can be strongly enhanced while the generalized concurrence is nearly stationary and its sensitivity to the coupling is suppressed. This demonstrates that the enhanced parameter sensitivity associated with quantum criticality need not arise from rapid changes in the magnitude of multipartite entanglement.

Accurate and bounded approximation of quantum correlation functions

Authors: Alexander van Lomwel, Florian Mintert

Summary. This paper develops a rigorous mathematical framework to bound the error when approximating quantum correlation functions using finite-window methods. By deriving a tight error bound, the authors provide a powerful tool to validate and improve numerical simulations of complex, non-integrable quantum many-body systems in 1D and 2D geometries.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a crucial theoretical tool for analyzing non-equilibrium quantum dynamics in complex systems where exact simulation is impossible, offering quantifiable error control for numerical techniques like MPS/MPO.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The exact simulation of non-integrable quantum systems is computationally intractable, and existing approximate methods lack rigorous guarantees on their error.

Main result. The authors derive a rigorous, computable error bound for approximating quantum correlation functions using finite-window methods, showing this bound is tighter than simpler estimates.

Method. The core method involves the window approximation, which decomposes the full system into a finite window and its environment, and then deriving error bounds for correlation functions using techniques involving operator deviations and the Hilbert-Schmidt norm.

Model / system. The analysis is applied to non-integrable quantum spin systems, specifically using the mixed-field Ising and isotropic Heisenberg models on 1D and 2D lattices.

Key observables. Quantum correlation functions, particularly the infinite-temperature autocorrelation function $C_j(t) = 2^{-n} ext{tr}(A_j(t)B)$.

Important parameters / regimes. System size (1D, 2D), time $t$, window radius $R$, and simulation parameters like bond dimension ($\chi$) and time step ($dt$).

Assumptions / limitations. The derivation relies on setting up and solving equations of motion for observable deviations ($\Delta A, \Delta B$) and assumes specific properties like $ ext{tr}([V, A_W]B_W) = 0$ under standard couplings.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the window approximation concept (Fig. 1), compare the true error against derived bounds ($B_O(t)$ vs $B_C(t)$) for 1D systems (Fig. 2), show the accuracy dependence on window size $R$ (Fig. 3), and compare MPO vs. window approximations in 2D (Fig. 5).

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the necessity of approximation methods, derives the rigorous error bound $B_C(t)$ for the window approximation, applies this bound to 1D and 2D spin models, and finally compares the resulting error bounds against established MPO simulation results.

Abstract

The exact classical simulation of non-integrable quantum systems rapidly becomes intractable beyond small system sizes, yet its role is central to predicting many-body dynamics. Approximate methods alleviate this cost but typically lack rigorous guarantees on their deviation from the exact result. For the infinite-temperature correlation function, we derive a rigorous error bound for approximating the full dynamics within a computable finite-sized region. Applied to autocorrelations, this finite-region approach is especially accurate across one-dimensional systems and beyond, and can further provide rigorous error bounds for other approximate methods, demonstrated here for matrix-product-operator simulations.

Generating Vector-Vortex $γ$ Photons by Nonlinear Compton Scattering

Authors: Yong-Zheng Ren, Mamutjan Ababekri, Jun-Lin Zhou, Feng Wan, Qian Zhao, Zhong-Peng Li, Kun Xue, Ya-Qing Huang, Zhao-Hui Chen, Zhong-Feng Xu, Jian-Xing Li

Summary. This paper presents a theoretical method to generate highly structured vector-vortex gamma photons via nonlinear Compton scattering using elliptically polarized lasers. By controlling the laser's ellipticity, the authors demonstrate the ability to achieve maximal spin-OAM entanglement in the resulting high-energy photons. This opens a new avenue for utilizing structured light concepts in nuclear-scale photonics and high-energy quantum information.

Why it may be interesting. This work bridges high-energy quantum electrodynamics (QED) with structured light concepts, providing a pathway to engineer quantum states of light at MeV energies, which is highly relevant for advanced quantum optics and high-energy physics applications.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge addressed is the generation of vector-vortex gamma photons, which are structured light states exhibiting a nonseparable coupling between polarization and orbital angular momentum (OAM), particularly in the high-energy gamma-ray regime.

Main result. The authors propose a novel mechanism using nonlinear Compton scattering (NCS) with elliptically polarized lasers, demonstrating that the mode-pair concurrence can reach unity for MeV gamma photons, realizing maximally nonseparable vector-vortex states.

Method. The analysis involves calculating the leading-order S-matrix element for NCS, projecting the emitted photon state onto a Bessel photon mode, and quantifying the spin-OAM entanglement using mode-pair concurrence.

Model / system. The physical system is the nonlinear Compton scattering process where an ultrarelativistic electron beam interacts head-on with an elliptically polarized laser pulse. The theoretical framework utilizes the Furry picture and Volkov states.

Key observables. Mode-pair concurrence (measure of spin-OAM entanglement), energy–angle resolved probability density ($d^2W_{VV}$), and HOPS coordinates.

Important parameters / regimes. Laser ellipticity ($\epsilon$), laser amplitude ($a_0$), and the resulting gamma-ray energy (MeV).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis focuses on the non-spin-flip channel ($\lambda' = \lambda$), which dominates in the moderate-field regime ($a_0 \sim 1$), and assumes an ultrarelativistic NCS geometry.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate how laser ellipticity controls the photon state on the Higher-Order Poincaré Sphere (HOPS), showing transitions from scalar-vortex to vector-vortex states, and display energy-angle probability distributions for selected vector-vortex modes.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theoretical framework by calculating the scattering amplitude, analyzing the resulting photon state in a Bessel basis, and then demonstrating the control mechanism by tuning laser ellipticity ($\epsilon$) to maximize the spin-OAM concurrence.

Abstract

Vector-vortex photons, characterized by a nonseparable coupling between polarization and orbital angular momentum (OAM), offer opportunities for optical manipulation, quantum communication, nuclear photonics, etc. However, their generation in the $γ$-ray regime remains challenging. Here, we put forward a novel method to generate vector-vortex $γ$ photons via nonlinear Compton scattering in elliptically polarized laser pulses. We reveal that tailoring laser ellipticity directs the multiphoton absorption to coherently populate OAM modes with opposite winding numbers, $\pm \ell$, tied to orthogonal circular polarizations, producing nonseparable spin-OAM photon states. For a linearly polarized laser of moderate intensity (dimensionless amplitude $a_0 \sim 1$), the mode-pair concurrence--a 0-to-1 measure of spin-OAM entanglement--reaches unity for MeV $γ$ photons, realizing maximally nonseparable radial- or azimuthal-type vector-vortex states. The laser amplitude further controls the accessible OAM spectrum. Our method offers a route to MeV vector-vortex photons, opening a new avenue for nuclear-scale structured photonics and high-energy quantum information.

Summary. This paper investigates using hybrid Quantum Machine Learning models, combining classical feature compression with quantum classifiers (QSVM/VQC), for cybersecurity tasks like intrusion and spam detection. The authors benchmark these models on simulation and validate them on real quantum hardware, showing that carefully engineered quantum components can provide competitive, budget-aware performance even on small, noisy devices.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on cybersecurity, the work provides concrete, resource-aware benchmarks for applying quantum kernels (QSVM) and variational circuits (VQC) on near-term hardware, which is relevant for understanding noise-mitigated quantum computation.

Main problem. The paper addresses the gap in understanding how quantum components improve end-to-end threat detection in resource-constrained cybersecurity pipelines.

Method. A hybrid architecture is employed, using a classical MLP for feature compression followed by quantum heads (QSVM or VQC) for classification, benchmarked against classical counterparts.

Abstract

Under tight feature and compute budgets, classical threat detection pipelines often degrade on near-decision-boundary events. Small quantum processors are now available, but existing work inadequately shows whether quantum components improve end-to-end threat detection under the above resource constrained conditions. This paper tries to address this gap with a hybrid architecture that uses a compact multilayer perceptron layer to compress the information in data and then routes the processed features to a few qubit quantum heads implemented in quantum support vector machine (QSVM) and variational quantum circuit (VQC) models. On a simulation platform, we benchmark these hybrid models against classical models with comparable parameter budgets on two representative cybersecurity tasks, network intrusion detection on NSL-KDD dataset and spam filtering on Ling-Spam dataset. To validate their precision on real quantum hardware, we deploy the best 4-qubit QSVM model on an IBM Quantum device with noise-aware execution, evaluated on a smaller sub-dataset. In the results, shallow quantum heads consistently match, and on difficult near-boundary cases modestly reduce missed attacks and false alarms compared to classical models using the same features. Hardware validation results track the simulation behavior closely enough that the remaining gap is dominated by device noise rather than model design. Furthermore, we conduct adversarial attacks to test the robustness of one QSVM model. Taken together, the study shows that even on small, noisy devices, carefully engineered quantum components may function as competitive, budget-aware components in practical cyber threat detection applications.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper revisits a conjecture stating that the effective noise channel on entangled qubits must contain a joint fully depolarizing component, potentially obstructing quantum fault tolerance.

Abstract

In this paper I revisit my 2006 conjecture on correlated errors in entangled physical qubits, originally proposed as a potential obstruction to quantum fault tolerance. The conjecture asserts that, in any physical implementation of a quantum computer, the effective noise channel acting on entangled physical qubits contains a joint fully depolarizing component, with a rate comparable to that of two-qubit gate errors. The conjecture remains open, but recent advances in experimental quantum computing bring it within reach of empirical testing on current devices. I also discuss two related directions in my critical study of quantum computation: the role of noise sensitivity and computational complexity in noisy intermediate-scale quantum systems, and the statistical analysis of experimental claims of quantum advantage. Finally, since this paper is written for a volume honoring Yuri Gurevich, I include some reflections on the ways in which my scientific and personal trajectory became intertwined with Yuri's.

Abstract

Abstract

Can a system with more stored energy cool faster than one with less? An overdamped resistor--inductor--capacitor (RLC) circuit offers a classical, experimentally accessible spectral Mpemba analogue: both states lose energy monotonically, yet the initially higher-energy state can cross below the lower-energy one. The reason is that initial energy alone does not determine the relaxation path; initial charge and current set how strongly the slow and fast decay modes are excited. We develop this mechanism from deterministic to stochastic and open-system descriptions. For the classical circuit, we derive the modal preparation conditions and crossing criterion, explain geometrically why equal-energy states can relax differently, and confirm the effect experimentally by reconstructing charge, current, and energy from oscilloscope traces. The inversion survives when the circuit is driven by externally injected white noise. An equilibrium Ornstein--Uhlenbeck treatment separates information carried by the ensemble mean from that stored in its covariance: centered Gibbs preparations preserve mean-energy ordering, while displaced or anisotropic Gaussian states can encode distinct slow and fast sectors. A Fokker--Planck generator then bridges to Markovian quantum dynamics. For a thermally damped quantum harmonic oscillator, the mean bare-energy excess decays with a single exponential factor, forbidding energy-order inversion for finite mean occupation. A below-threshold parametric oscillator restores slow and fast quadrature sectors, allowing crossings controlled by displacement and covariance. The Liouvillian formulation shows that observed relaxation depends jointly on the generator spectrum, the modes populated by preparation, and the modes visible to the observable.
