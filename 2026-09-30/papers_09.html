AG-CoT: Verified Algorithmic Traces for LLM Program Synthesis on Clifford Circuits

Authors: Lu Wei, Yufeng Wang, Chenfeng Cao, Lu Pang, Haibin Ling
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.33192v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper tackles the unreliability of LLMs generating quantum circuits by focusing on Clifford circuits used in quantum error correction. The authors introduce an AG-CoT supervision technique, guided by an exact verifier, which forces the model to learn the underlying algorithmic structure. This results in substantial, statistically significant improvements in the ability of LLMs to synthesize circuits that correctly prepare the target quantum state.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the reliability of using large language models for generating quantum code, providing rigorous benchmarks for verifiable quantum circuit synthesis.

Detailed structure

Main problem. LLM program synthesis often generates executable quantum circuits that fail to compute the intended scientific state, especially when dealing with complex quantum error correction circuits.

Main result. Supervising LLMs with Aaronson-Gottesman chain-of-thought (AG-CoT) traces and using verifier-filtered continuation training significantly boosts state-equivalence accuracy compared to circuit-only baselines.

Method. The authors use an exact verifier to check OpenQASM circuits against a target state defined by signed stabilizer generators, supervising the LLM on the verifiable algorithmic trace (AG-CoT) steps.

Model / system. The system involves synthesizing Clifford circuits, which prepare stabilizer states crucial for quantum error correction. The target state is compactly represented by signed Pauli generators forming a tableau.

Key observables. State equivalence (matching the prepared state to the target state), Clifford validity, and syntax validity are the key observables, with the gap between validity and equivalence being a major diagnostic.

Important parameters / regimes. The performance is measured across different model sizes (3B, 7B, 32B) and depends on the complexity of the target state and the search budget (e.g., Pass@N).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the fact that Clifford state preparation admits an exact, finite stabilizer representation, allowing for rigorous verification.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the pipeline involving AG-CoT supervision and verifier checks, while tables summarize quantitative performance gains across various training and testing regimes.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem, details the structured representation (stabilizer tableaux), presents the AG-CoT and verifier-filtered training methodology, and concludes with extensive quantitative comparisons showing the necessity of exact verification.

Abstract

Scientific code generation can produce executable programs that fail to compute the intended scientific object. We study this problem in language-model synthesis of Clifford circuits, which prepare the stabilizer states used in quantum error correction and admit exact classical verification. In our target-conditioned framework, each target is given as compact signed stabilizer generators, and an exact verifier checks the generated OpenQASM circuits. We supervise models with Aaronson-Gottesman chain-of-thought (AG-CoT) traces checked by the verifier, and continue training on model generations that the verifier accepts. Across two independently trained model families (3B and 7B), AG-CoT supervision multiplies greedy-decode state-equivalence accuracy by four to six times over circuit-only baselines, and verifier-filtered continuation training adds a further consistent gain atop both. A complementary 32B study shows that supervised models achieve near-perfect syntax and Clifford validity while the strongest direct model reaches 6.14% state equivalence per target, rising to over 10% under verifier-guided selection with multiple candidates. These results show that algorithmic trace supervision gives a large, statistically significant gain in both model families and that verifier-filtered continuation adds a further repeated gain. The persistent gap between Clifford validity and state equivalence confirms that exact verification is necessary: a circuit can be syntactically and physically valid yet prepare the wrong quantum state.

An Optimal Quantum Linear Systems Algorithm

Authors: Carlos Bravo-Prieto, Aram W. Harrow, Robin Kothari
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.35660v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper determines the optimal quantum query complexity for solving the Quantum Linear Systems Problem (QLSP) to be $\Theta(\kappa \sqrt{d} \log(1/\epsilon))$. The authors achieve this by developing a sophisticated block encoding technique and simultaneously proving the matching lower bound by embedding a known hard problem into the QLSP structure.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a fundamental, optimal complexity result for a cornerstone problem in quantum computation, directly impacting the theoretical limits of quantum simulation and quantum machine learning algorithms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the Quantum Linear Systems Problem (QLSP): finding an approximation to the solution vector $\vec{x}$ for the equation $A\vec{x}=\vec{b}$ given quantum query access to the sparse matrix $A$.

Main result. The authors establish the optimal query complexity for the QLSP to be $\Theta(\kappa \sqrt{d} \log(1/\epsilon))$, matching the best known lower bounds.

Method. The upper bound is achieved by reducing the QLSP to solving a larger linear system via a block encoding construction, which is implemented using $O(1)$ queries to the sparse-matrix oracle.

Model / system. The analysis is based on the abstract mathematical model of quantum query complexity, involving sparse matrices $A$ (with condition number $\kappa$ and sparsity $d$), and the ability to prepare quantum states proportional to $\vec{b}$.

Key observables. Query complexity, approximation error $\epsilon$, condition number $\kappa$, and matrix sparsity $d$.

Important parameters / regimes. The complexity scales with $\kappa \sqrt{d} \log(1/\epsilon)$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes access to standard sparse-matrix oracles and utilizes the model developed by Childs, Kothari, and Somma.

Paper structure. The paper first defines the QLSP and reviews prior bounds. It then presents the main result (Theorem 1) establishing the $\Theta(\kappa \sqrt{d} \log(1/\epsilon))$ complexity. The methodology involves constructing a block encoding for a larger system and deriving the lower bound by embedding a hard problem (related to $FR,D$) into the QLSP instance.

Abstract

In the quantum linear systems problem (QLSP), we are given query access to a $d$-sparse $N\times N$ matrix $A$ with condition number $κ$, and the ability to prepare a quantum state proportional to a vector $\vec b$. The goal is to output an $ε$-approximation to the quantum state proportional to the solution $\vec x$ of $A\vec{x}=\vec{b}$. Following a long line of work, the best previously known quantum algorithms for the QLSP had query complexities $O(κd\log(1/ε))$ and $κ\sqrt d(κd/ε)^{o(1)}$, while the best known lower bounds were $Ω(κ\log(1/ε))$ and $Ω(κ\sqrt d)$. We improve these bounds and show that the complexity of the QLSP is $Θ(κ\sqrt d\log(1/ε))$. We also resolve an open problem of Berry and Childs by showing that any $N\times N$ unitary can be implemented with bounded error using $O(\sqrt N)$ queries to its matrix entries.

Asymptotically Good Quantum Codes with Addressable Transversal T Gates

Summary. This paper constructs an explicit family of asymptotically good binary quantum error-correcting codes that support fully addressable transversal T gates. It achieves this by combining algebraic-geometry codes with specific binary embeddings to satisfy necessary divisibility conditions. This result is a significant theoretical advance for building fault-tolerant quantum computers.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete, constructive method to achieve a major goal in fault-tolerant quantum computation—implementing universal gates transversally—using established tools from algebraic coding theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of designing quantum error-correcting codes that are both asymptotically good in terms of error-correcting parameters and possess fully addressable transversal non-Clifford gates, specifically the T gate.

Main result. The authors construct an explicit family of binary CSS codes with constant rate and linear distance that successfully admit fully addressable transversal logical T gates.

Method. The construction method combines algebraic-geometry (AG) codes with a suitable binary embedding, leveraging generalized divisibility criteria derived from the code's algebraic structure.

Model / system. The model is based on binary CSS quantum codes, which encode logical qubits into physical qubits. The underlying mathematical machinery involves function fields, divisors, and Riemann-Roch spaces to construct the codes.

Key observables. The primary observables are the code parameters: block length (N), dimension (K), and minimum distance (D), alongside the existence of fully addressable transversal T gates.

Important parameters / regimes. Asymptotic rates ($K/N$, $D/N$), the field size ($\mathbb{F}_{1024}$), and the required divisibility conditions (e.g., weighted 8-divisibility).

Assumptions / limitations. The construction relies on the existence of specific properties within AG codes (like the 15-multiplication property) and the successful translation of classical algebraic properties into quantum gate requirements.

Figures summary. Not specified in the notes, but the development involves deriving specific code parameters and bounds based on the algebraic structure.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from the general problem statement to the mathematical tools (AG codes, function fields), develops the necessary coding theory machinery (divisibility criteria, puncturing-and-shortening), and culminates in the construction and proof of the fully addressable transversal T gates for asymptotically good codes.

Abstract

Designing quantum codes with both transversal non-Clifford gates and good error-correcting parameters is an important goal in fault-tolerant quantum computation. Here, we construct an explicit family of binary CSS codes with constant rate and linear distance that admit fully addressable transversal $T$ gates. Specifically, any prescribed tensor product of logical powers of $T$ is implemented by a tensor product of physical powers of $T$, without any subsequent correction. Our construction combines algebraic-geometry codes with suitable binary embedding to obtain generalized divisibility of binary codes. This divisibility then enables fully addressable transversal $T$ gates. Furthermore, we formulate the minimum binary embedding length problem as a minimum-weight problem over an affine space and numerically improve the constants in the asymptotic rate and relative-distance bounds.

Beyond NISQ Assumptions: One-time Memory in the Classically Accessible Random-Oracle Model

Authors: Boyang Chen, Tianren Liu, Luojian Wei
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.32603v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops a One-Time Memory (OTM) protocol secure in the Classically Accessible Random-Oracle Model (CAROM), addressing the limitations of NISQ-based security assumptions. By utilizing BB84 states and a structured oracle query mechanism, the authors achieve information-theoretic security with an exponentially small simulation advantage. This work advances the theoretical understanding of quantum cryptography for future fault-tolerant quantum computers.

Why it may be interesting. While the paper is purely theoretical, its focus on robust cryptographic primitives and moving beyond NISQ assumptions is foundational for understanding the long-term computational limits of quantum information processing.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To construct an efficient One-Time Memory (OTM) protocol secure against quantum adversaries beyond the restrictive assumptions of Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) computing.

Main result. The authors show that an efficient OTM scheme is possible under the Classically Accessible Random-Oracle Model (CAROM), achieving exponentially small simulation advantage for polynomial query bounds.

Method. The protocol adapts the CAROM framework, using BB84 states and replacing basis information with oracle tags to ensure security against quantum adversaries.

Model / system. The security proof is built within the abstract mathematical framework of the Classically Accessible Random-Oracle Model (CAROM), which restricts the adversary's interaction with the random oracle to classical queries.

Key observables. Simulation advantage (the probability of distinguishing the real protocol from the simulated one), communication complexity (qubits and classical bits).

Important parameters / regimes. Message length ($\lambda$), number of words ($n$), and word length ($\ell$), which are set as functions of $\lambda$.

Assumptions / limitations. The security relies on the CAROM model, which is assumed to be more robust than NISQ assumptions, and the difficulty of coherently evaluating the random oracle.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem, adapts the CAROM model, details the protocol construction using BB84 states and oracle tags, and culminates in a security proof that bounds the simulation advantage using techniques related to parallel repetition theorems.

Abstract

Quantum information enables many cryptographic primitives that are impossible in the classical world. A line of works has developed cryptographic protocol under the assumption that quantum adversaries are restricted to noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) computing power, enabling strong one-time functionalities. But the advent of early fault-tolerant quantum computers eras will allow deeper logical quantum circuits, calling into questions the applicability of these NISQ-based assumptions. In this work, we adapt the classically accessible random oracle model (CAROM) as in [BDF+11] and [AK22], in which adversaries are only allowed to classically query the random oracle. The restriction is well motivated for NISQ quantum adversaries and may remain plausible in the presence of early fault-tolerant quantum computers. Then, we show that an efficient simulation-secure one-time memory (OTM) is possible under CAROM. Our protocol uses only BB84 states and has quadratic communication: for a $λ$-bit message and integer-valued parameters $n=n(λ)$ and $\ell=\ell(λ)$, the construction uses $n\ell$ qubits and $(n+2)λ$ classical bits, and for any quantum adversary with at most $2^{\ell/2-1}-1$ classical queries to the random oracle, its simulation advantage is at most $(n+3)\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^n.$ Hence exponentially small simulation advantage in $n$.

Chiral Weyl-Kondo semimetallic state through enhanced correlation in CeGaGe

Authors: Arushi, Kevin Allen, Yuan Fang, Kuan-Sen Lin, Mounica Mahankali, Hari Bhandari, Karthik Rao, Alberto Ruiz Biestro, Christopher Lane, Jian-Xin Zhu, Sanu Mishra, Geoffroy Hautier, Qimiao Si, Emilia Morosan

Summary. This work reports evidence for a chiral Weyl-Kondo semimetallic state in CeGaGe, a material that uniquely combines crystal chirality, non-symmorphic symmetry, and strong electronic correlations. By analyzing anomalous Hall conductivity and structural transitions, the authors demonstrate that the AHC is intrinsically linked to Kondo hybridization in the low-temperature phase. This establishes CeGaGe as a key experimental platform for studying Kondo-driven topological semimetallics.

Why it may be interesting. The interplay between structural phase transitions, strong electronic correlations (Kondo effect), and topological band structures (Weyl points) is a frontier area in condensed matter physics, highly relevant to understanding emergent quantum phenomena.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To establish the first experimental realization of a chiral Weyl-Kondo semimetal (cWKSM) state, which requires the confluence of crystal chirality, non-symmorphic symmetry, and Kondo correlations.

Main result. The material CeGaGe exhibits evidence for a cWKSM state in its low-temperature phase, confirmed by anomalous Hall conductivity (AHC) behavior tracking Kondo coherence. This establishes CeGaGe as a rare platform realizing this complex correlated topological state.

Method. The study combines experimental measurements (magnetization, Hall resistivity, specific heat) with first-principles DFT calculations and theoretical toy models to analyze the electronic structure and topological properties.

Model / system. The physical system is CeGaGe, which undergoes a structural transition to a chiral, non-centrosymmetric $P4_3$ phase. The theoretical analysis employs a Periodic Anderson Lattice Model and a toy model to incorporate Kondo hybridization effects into the band structure.

Key observables. Anomalous Hall Conductivity (AHC), Kondo temperature ($T_K$), magnetic ordering temperature ($T_N$), and the Berry curvature ($\Omega_{ij}(\mathbf{k})$).

Important parameters / regimes. Kondo temperature ($T_K \approx 7-8 ext{ K}$), Néel temperature ($T_N = 4.7 ext{ K}$), and the structural transition temperature ($T_{struct} = 230 ext{ K}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The AHC is assumed to be an intrinsic, Berry-curvature-driven contribution from Kondo hybridization, and theoretical calculations rely on approximations like the saddle-point method.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the structural transition ($I4_1md o P4_3$), magnetic ordering, Kondo correlations via specific heat, and electronic band structures showing Berry curvature maps at different $k_z$ values.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first identifying the necessary ingredients (chirality, symmetry, correlations), presenting experimental evidence (structural/magnetic transitions, AHC measurements), and finally using theoretical models (toy models, Berry curvature calculations) to confirm the cWKSM state in the low-temperature phase.

Abstract

Strongly correlated chiral materials have been proposed to host a chiral Weyl-Kondo semimetal (cWKSM) state, in which Kondo hybridization pins chirality-induced Kramers-Weyl point crossings, together with additional symmetry-enforced band crossings, near the Fermi level. Realizing this state requires a material that combines crystal chirality, non-symmorphic symmetry, and Kondo correlations, and currently there are no known materials that combine all these requirements. Here we report evidence for a cWKSM state in CeGaGe, a member of the RXY (R = rare earth; X = Al, Si; Y = Ga, Ge) family. Magnetization measurements confirm long-range antiferromagnetic order below T$_N$ = 4.7 K with a complex, canted magnetic structure. Unique to CeGaGe among the known RXY systems is a structural transition from an achiral tetragonal I4$_{1}$md structure (at high temperatures) to the chiral tetragonal P4$_{3}$ structure (at low temperatures). The structural transition in CeGaGe allows us to highlight the role of chirality in stabilizing the Kramers cWKSM state. Hall resistivity measurements with $H || c$ reveal an anomalous Hall conductivity (AHC) that is constant below the Kondo temperature $T_K \approx 7$ K and drops sharply above it. This crossover tracks the onset of Kondo coherence rather than the magnetic ordering, which occurs at T$_N$ = 4.7 K. Together with first-principles calculations for the $P4_3$ structure, this identifies the AHC as an intrinsic, Berry-curvature-driven contribution generated by Kondo hybridization, rather than a consequence of extrinsic scattering or dynamic scalar spin chirality. These results establish CeGaGe as a rare experimental platform in which crystal chirality, strong electronic correlations, and non-trivial band topology coexist, providing the first material realization of a Kondo-driven cWKSM state.

Summary. This paper benchmarks the performance of GKP-concatenated quantum low-density parity-check (qLDPC) codes (BB and tricycle) for fault-tolerant quantum computation. By simulating error propagation through a detailed circuit model, the authors demonstrate that incorporating analog GKP information into the decoding process significantly improves error thresholds compared to traditional hard-decision methods. The results guide the selection of optimal outer codes for practical quantum hardware.

Main result. Analog-informed belief-propagation decoding consistently outperforms hard-decision decoding across all noise models, and the BB sequence yields a higher finite-size crossing estimate than the tricycle sequence in the full circuit model.

Method. The authors developed a unified simulation framework to benchmark GKP-concatenated codes across three noise models, comparing decoding strategies (hard vs. analog-informed BP-OSD).

Model / system. The system involves encoding logical qubits using Gottesman-Kitaev-Preskill (GKP) codes, which are then concatenated with outer qLDPC codes (BB and tricycle). The process simulates error propagation through syndrome extraction using bosonic interactions.

Key observables. Finite-size crossing estimates ($\sigma_{th}$), squeezing requirements (e.g., 10.46 dB for BB), and word logical error rates.

Important parameters / regimes. Noise strength ($\sigma$), code parameters (BB/tricycle structure), and the choice of decoder (hard vs. analog-informed).

Paper structure. Abstract

Scalable fault-tolerant quantum error correction based on Gottesman-Kitaev-Preskill (GKP) codes requires finite-rate outer codes that can exploit analog GKP information under realistic circuit noise. However, quantum low-density parity-check (qLDPC) outer codes have not been systematically compared as candidates for circuit-level GKP concatenation. Here, we benchmark BB and tricycle codes using a unified simulation framework that progresses from code-capacity noise to repeated noisy syndrome extraction and schedule-resolved displacement propagation. Across all three noise models, analog-informed belief-propagation with ordered-statistics decoding (BP-OSD) yields higher finite-size crossing estimates than hard-decision decoding. In the full circuit model, the analog-informed crossings are \(σ_{\mathrm{th}}\simeq0.212\) for the selected BB sequence and \(0.142\) for the tricycle sequence, corresponding to squeezing requirements of approximately \(10.46\,\mathrm{dB}\) and \(13.94\,\mathrm{dB}\), respectively. These results show that analog GKP information continues to improve decoding under circuit-level noise. Under the common simulation assumptions used here, the selected BB sequence also yields a higher finite-size crossing estimate than the selected tricycle sequence.

Connecting finite-size scaling and renormalization-group flows in Anderson localization on random graphs

Authors: Ignacio García-Mata, Weitao Chen, John Martin, Bertrand Georgeot, Gabriel Lemarié
Type: theory · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.31872v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper compares finite-size scaling and renormalization-group approaches to characterize the Anderson transition on random graphs. It finds that while different observables yield different scaling behaviors, the system's critical properties can be consistently described by a single-parameter scaling function derived from normalized eigenfunction moments. This clarifies the underlying scaling structure, suggesting that the apparent complexity of the RG flow might stem from the choice of observable rather than intrinsic multi-parameter physics.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous theoretical comparison between two advanced scaling theories (FSS and RG) applied to a fundamental quantum problem (Anderson localization), offering insights into the nature of critical exponents and scaling dimensionality in disordered quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the relationship between two complementary theoretical descriptions—finite-size scaling (FSS) of eigenfunction moments and renormalization-group (RG) flows of multifractal dimensions ($D_q$)—for the Anderson transition on random graphs.

Main result. The analysis shows that while the RG flow for $D_1$ might suggest a two-parameter scaling, the structure is highly dependent on the moment order $q$. A single-parameter scaling can be confirmed by constructing a $eta$ function for normalized moments that collapses across different disorder strengths.

Method. The authors employ both FSS techniques using eigenfunction moments and RG flow analysis using multifractal dimensions ($D_q$). They compare the scaling behavior derived from these two approaches.

Model / system. The system is Anderson localization on small-world random graphs (SWRGs). The Hamiltonian includes random site energies ($\epsilon_i$) and hopping terms, including nearest-neighbor and long-range random hoppings.

Key observables. Eigenfunction moments ($P_q$), generalized multifractal dimensions ($D_q$), and the running $eta$ functions ($eta_q$).

Important parameters / regimes. Disorder strength ($W$), graph size ($N$), and the moment order ($q$). The critical point is associated with $q=1/2$.

Assumptions / limitations. The central point is distinguishing between a true two-parameter scaling structure (suggested by comparing different observables) and an apparent two-dimensional flow from a single observable due to non-trivial size dependence.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the collapse of normalized moments ($F_q$) for different $q$ values, showing how different scaling regimes (volumetric vs. linear) govern the flow on either side of the transition.

Paper structure. The paper systematically compares the scaling predictions from FSS (using moment collapse) against those from RG (using $D_q$ flow). It develops the mathematical framework to reconcile the scale conversions between the two methods, concluding that the physical flow retains a single-parameter structure even if the initial observables suggest otherwise.

Abstract

We investigate the relation between two complementary descriptions of the Anderson transition on small-world random graphs: finite-size scaling of eigenfunction moments and renormalization-group (RG) flows of multifractal dimensions $D_q$. The RG flows recover the qualitative structure previously reported for the information dimension $D_1$, while their extension to other $D_q$ with smaller moment orders $q<1/2$ confirms the strongly multifractal nature of the localized phase characterized by a clear separation between multifractal and localized behaviors at strong disorder. On the other hand, we show that we can construct another beta function characterizing the flow of eigenfunction moments, whose collapse onto a single function across different disorder strengths provides a clear confirmation of single-parameter scaling property. These results indicate that the family of RG trajectories observed for the information dimension $D_1$ need not imply two-parameter scaling and Kosterlitz-Thouless like critical behavior. To characterize these properties, one should consider different observables, such as large $q>1/2$ and small $q<1/2$ eigenfunction moments, which are associated with distinct critical behaviors, in particular different critical exponents.

Cyclotomic Cosets: Hidden Subgroup and Quantum Sieving Algorithm for Prime-Power Moduli

Authors: Mathias Boucher, Pierre-Alain Fouque, Yixin Shen
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.34996v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

Summary. This paper introduces the Cyclotomic Coset Problem (CCP) as a structured generalization of known hard problems in post-quantum cryptography, like the Dihedral Coset Problem. The authors develop a novel quantum sieve algorithm that solves CCP efficiently over cyclotomic rings. This work extends quantum algorithmic techniques to a broader class of algebraic structures relevant to lattice-based cryptography.

Why it may be interesting. While the focus is on number theory and cryptography, the development of quantum sieving techniques over structured rings like cyclotomic extensions represents a sophisticated application of quantum algorithms to abstract algebraic structures, which has parallels in quantum simulation of lattice models.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the difficulty of applying Hidden Subgroup Problem (HSP) techniques to the Extrapolated Dihedral Coset Problem (EDCP) because EDCP lacks a direct coset structure.

Main result. The authors introduce the Cyclotomic Coset Problem (CCP) and develop a quantum sieve that solves CCP in time $2^{O_p(\log n\log q)}$ for fixed prime $p$ and modulus $q=p^t$.

Method. The core method involves constructing a quantum sieve that successively reduces phase states across the $\pi$-adic ideal chain within the cyclotomic ring $R_q = \mathbb{Z}_q[\zeta_p]$.

Model / system. The mathematical model is based on the cyclotomic ring $R_q = \mathbb{Z}_q[\zeta_p]$, which generalizes the structure used in the Dihedral Coset Problem (DCP). The quantum computation is performed on states defined over this ring structure.

Key observables. The primary observables are the quantum states associated with the CCP, and the success probability of the quantum sieve.

Important parameters / regimes. Key parameters include the prime $p$, the power $q=p^t$, the dimension $n$, and the error parameters $\alpha$.

Assumptions / limitations. The result does not yield a quasi-polynomial-time algorithm for standard LWE because the known reduction only produces a limited number of approximate CCP states.

Figures summary. Figure 1 provides an overview comparing the contributions between $ ext{LWE}{n,q,\alpha}$, $ ext{DCP}}$, $ ext{CyLWE{n,q,p,\alpha}$, and $ ext{CCP}$.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the algebraic framework over the cyclotomic ring $R_q$, then develops the quantum sieve algorithm for CCP, and finally shows how this solver can be used to achieve quasi-polynomial time algorithms for related problems like uniform EDCP and Gaussian S|LWE>. The structure builds from the algebraic generalization (CCP) to the algorithmic application.

Abstract

The Learning With Errors (LWE) problem is a fundamental assumption in post-quantum cryptography. Regev established a quantum reduction from LWE to the Dihedral Coset Problem (DCP). Later, Brakerski et al. introduced the Extrapolated Dihedral Coset Problem (EDCP), proving its equivalence to LWE. However, unlike DCP, EDCP no longer admits a coset structure. This limits the direct application of techniques for hidden subgroup problems. In this work, we introduce the Cyclotomic Coset Problem (CCP), a cyclotomic generalization of DCP that preserves an exact hidden-subgroup structure. Let $ζ_p$ be a primitive $p$-th root of unity, let $π=ζ_p-1$, and write $q=p^t$ and $L=t(p-1)$. We work over $R_q=\mathbb Z_q[ζ_p] \cong \mathbb Z[ζ_p]/(π^L)$, where the isomorphism follows from the total ramification identity $(p)=(π)^{p-1}$. We exploit the resulting $π$-adic ideal chain to construct a quantum sieve that successively reduces phase states modulo $π^{L},π^{L-1},\ldots,π$. For every fixed prime $p$ and modulus $q=p^t$, our algorithm solves the CCP in time and sample complexity $2^{O_p(\log n\log q)}$, using polynomial quantum space. The sieve also applies to uniform EDCP and Gaussian S|LWE>, yielding quasi-polynomial time algorithms for all the above problems when $q=\text{poly}(n)$. This extends the power-of-two EDCP sieve of Bai et al. (CRYPTO 2025) to a cyclotomic setting. However, we emphasize that our result does not, by itself, yield a quasi-polynomial-time algorithm for standard LWE, because the currently known reduction produces only a limited number of approximate CCP states.

Abstract

Diffusion models trained on a finite dataset first learn to generate novel, high-quality samples and only much later collapse onto their training set. We identify the mechanism behind this separation of timescales and the object that probes it. The training dynamics of the score function are governed---exactly, and at any width---by the Gram matrix of the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) evaluated on the noisy training data, so the timescales of generalization and of memorization must be encoded in its spectrum. We show that they are, and that the structure responsible has no analogue in standard kernel settings. The use of multiple noise realizations per sample ($m$ noised copies at a fixed noise level) in the score-matching loss is what restructures the Gram matrix spectrum into two distinct parts. The first, of large eigenvalues, carries the global features of the target distribution and is present already for $m=1$. The second, which the repeated noising creates, consists of the smallest eigenvalues and is supported on eigenvectors aligned with the sample-specific noise directions; it sets a memorization timescale parametrically larger in the training set size $n$. We establish this picture on two fronts. Analytically, we solve the spectrum in the lazy high-dimensional limit for both linear ($n \asymp d$) and polynomial ($n \asymp d^k$) sample complexities, and prove through a bias--variance decomposition that the first bulk minimizes the approximation error while the second drives the error associated with memorization. Empirically, we show the same two-bulk structure in Convolutional NTKs on CelebA and in finite-width U-Nets trained well beyond the lazy regime, and we make the link causal: truncating the Gram matrix at rank $r$ tunes the generalization--memorization transition, and an $L_2$ penalty targeting the second bulk suppresses memorization in feature-learning U-Nets.

Abstract

The output of a parameterized quantum circuit (PQC) can be expressed as a finite Fourier series whose accessible frequencies are fixed by the data-encoding gates. While the encoder determines which frequencies can appear, the corresponding Fourier coefficients depend on how the trainable and entangling gates are arranged. Although existing studies provide metrics for characterizing how gate structure affects Fourier coefficients, they do not translate these analyses into an explicit design criterion specifying how gates should be arranged to make a target coefficient reachable. In this paper, we provide such a criterion. Using the adjoint action of the encoding generator, we decompose operator space into two-dimensional invariant planes indexed by frequency and show that each Fourier coefficient is exactly a sum of bilinear projections of the effective state and observable onto the planes at that frequency. Because, for Pauli encodings, high-frequency planes are spanned by mixed multi-qubit Pauli strings, a target coefficient can contribute to the output only when local rotations and entangling layers are arranged so that both the effective state and observable acquire support on one of its planes. For Pauli readouts and commuting two-qubit entanglers, this yields a circuit design rule that specifies, for a given interaction graph, a placement of local rotations and entangling layers that makes a target Fourier coefficient reachable. Using a single encoding layer, we validate the proposed design through a placement ablation, regression tasks from PDEBench and physics-informed Maxwell field modeling, and further demonstrate its robustness to moderate simulated gate noise and damping.
