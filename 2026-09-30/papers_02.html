Feedback-based quantum optimization with low depth and measurement

Authors: Zi-Wen Huang, Jia-Cheng Fan, Xiao-Hui Ni, Su-Juan Qin, Xiao-Kai Hou, Wei Huang, Bing-Jie Xu, Fei Gao
Type: both · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.32433v1

Summary. This paper introduces BLS-FALQON, an improved quantum optimization algorithm designed to solve combinatorial problems with minimal quantum resources. By integrating a backtracking line search into the feedback mechanism, it significantly reduces measurement overhead compared to existing state-of-the-art methods like SO-FALQON. The method is validated through both extensive simulations and real-world experiments on superconducting quantum hardware.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses resource limitations (depth and measurement overhead) in variational quantum algorithms, which is crucial for scaling quantum computation to practical problems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Developing a quantum optimization algorithm that achieves low circuit depth and low measurement overhead for solving combinatorial optimization problems.

Main result. The proposed BLS-FALQON method reduces the total measurement count by 37.7% compared to SO-FALQON in simulations and reduces estimated hardware execution time by 43% on real quantum hardware.

Method. The authors propose BLS-FALQON, which adapts the feedback-based quantum optimization framework by incorporating a backtracking line search mechanism inspired by BLS theory.

Model / system. The work is based on the FALQON algorithm, which uses a time-dependent Hamiltonian $H(t) = H_p + eta(t)H_d$ to drive the expectation value of the problem Hamiltonian $\langle H_p angle$ to decrease monotonically. Simulations use the max-cut problem, and experiments are performed on the Tianyan-176 quantum computer.

Key observables. The primary observables are the expectation values $\langle H_p angle$, and the key metrics are circuit depth and total measurement count.

Important parameters / regimes. The time step $\Delta t$, the control coefficient $eta(t)$, and the backtracking factor $ au \in (-1, 0)$ are important parameters.

Assumptions / limitations. The core assumption for BLS-FALQON is that the backtracking mechanism guarantees a valid descent step ($\Delta E < 0$) by iteratively adjusting the control coefficient $eta^{(FO)}$ until the expectation value decreases.

Figures summary. Not specified in the notes, but the results compare the performance (measurement count, depth, time) of BLS-FALQON against SO-FALQON and FALQON across simulations and hardware tests.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem, details the FALQON framework, compares it to SO-FALQON, and then proposes and analyzes BLS-FALQON using theoretical proofs (Proposition 1) and validating it with numerical simulations and real quantum hardware experiments.

Abstract

Feedback-based ALgorithm for Quantum OptimizatioN (FALQON) is a hybrid quantum-classical algorithm for solving combinatorial optimization problems, which circumvents classical parameter optimization but requires a deep quantum circuit. To reduce circuit depth, Arai et al. proposed second-order FALQON (SO-FALQON), achieving the best depth reduction among existing approaches. However, SO-FALQON brings a 2.3 times per-step measurement overhead, as it needs to calculate an additional second-order control coefficient. In this paper, inspired by the Backtracking Line Search (BLS) theory, we propose another method called BLS-FALQON, which not only reduces circuit depth to a comparable extent, but also achieves fewer measurements than SO-FALQON. Numerical simulations on max-cut problem with 8 to 20 vertices demonstrate that BLS-FALQON reduces the total measurement count by 37.7% compared to SO-FALQON, while maintaining a comparable circuit depth. Furthermore, we conduct real quantum hardware experiments on the Tianyan-176 quantum computer, which uses the zuchongzhi2 superconducting quantum processor, confirming that BLS-FALQON remains effective under real quantum hardware conditions.

Summary. This work reports on achieving high-fidelity quantum gate operations using a single-hole spin qubit in a germanium quantum dot. By implementing geometric quantum computation, the researchers overcame fidelity limitations caused by off-resonance noise inherent in standard gate sequences. This demonstrates a promising, robust pathway toward scalable quantum computation in solid-state semiconductor platforms.

Why it may be interesting. The focus on geometric control is highly relevant to open quantum systems, as it provides a mechanism to engineer robustness against environmental noise (like detuning) by leveraging geometric phases, a concept applicable across many physical platforms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core challenge is achieving high-fidelity and robust qubit manipulations necessary for fault-tolerant quantum computation.

Main result. The authors successfully demonstrated that implementing geometric quantum computation allows for qubit gate fidelities exceeding 99.9%, significantly outperforming standard dynamical gate methods, especially under noise conditions.

Method. The research involves characterizing single-qubit gate control fidelities using gate set tomography (GST) and comparing these results against those obtained using geometric quantum computation (GQC) protocols.

Model / system. The platform utilized is a single-hole spin qubit housed within a germanium quantum dot, controlled via Electric Dipole Spin Resonance (EDSR) mediated by intrinsic spin-orbit interaction (SOI).

Key observables. Control fidelities for I, X/2, and Y/2 gates; Rabi frequency ($\omega_{Rabi}$); dephasing time ($T_2^*$).

Important parameters / regimes. Gate fidelities exceeding 99.9%; microwave frequency detuning ($\pm 2.5$ MHz); Rabi frequency range up to 19 MHz.

Assumptions / limitations. Standard gate sequences are severely limited by off-resonance noise, which GQC is proposed to overcome.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the device structure (SEM, cross-section), qubit characterization (Rabi/CPMG decay), and critically, compare the fidelity of dynamical gates versus geometric gates under varying Rabi frequencies and detunings.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the physical system and the need for high fidelity. It then characterizes the qubit using standard methods (GST) to identify noise limitations. The core contribution is the introduction and demonstration of GQC, showing its superior, noise-resilient performance compared to the standard methods.

Abstract

Achieving high-fidelity and robust qubit manipulations is a crucial requirement for realizing faulttolerant quantum computation. Here, we demonstrate a single-hole spin qubit in a germanium quantum dot and characterize its control fidelity using gate set tomography. The maximum control fidelities reach 97.48%, 99.81%, 99.88% for the I, X/2 and Y /2 gate, respectively. These results reveal that off-resonance noise during consecutive I gates in gate set tomography sequences severely limits qubit performance. Therefore, we introduce geometric quantum computation to realize noiseresilient qubit manipulation. The geometric gate control fidelities remain above 99% across a wide range of Rabi frequencies. The maximum fidelity surpasses 99.9%. Furthermore, the fidelities of geometric X/2 and Y /2 (I) gates exceed 99% even when detuning the microwave frequency by +-2.5 MHz (+-1.2 MHz), highlighting the noise-resilient feature. These results demonstrate that geometric quantum computation is a potential method for achieving high-fidelity qubit manipulation reproducibly in semiconductor quantum computation.

Summary. This paper reports on optimizing hole spin qubits in germanium quantum dots by tuning the in-plane magnetic field orientation. The authors found that minimizing the qubit's susceptibility to charge noise significantly enhances coherence times and gate fidelity. This work advances the feasibility of using hole spin qubits for scalable quantum computation.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides concrete experimental evidence for mitigating charge noise effects in solid-state spin qubits by engineering the magnetic field orientation, which is crucial for scaling quantum processors.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is to improve the coherence time and control fidelity of hole spin qubits, which are susceptible to charge noise due to their physical mechanisms.

Main result. By optimizing the orientation of the in-plane magnetic field, the authors achieved a five-fold enhancement in Hahn-echo coherence time and demonstrated a maximum gate fidelity of 99.82%.

Method. The study involves measuring the anisotropic spin-electric susceptibility to charge noise while varying the magnetic field orientation and performing gate set tomography.

Model / system. The research uses hole spin qubits realized in planar germanium quantum dots (DQD). The system's properties are highly dependent on the orientation of an applied in-plane magnetic field and gate voltages.

Key observables. Longitudinal spin-electric susceptibility (LSES), Hahn-echo coherence time (T_Echo^2), and maximum gate fidelity.

Important parameters / regimes. In-plane magnetic field orientation ($ heta_{xy}$), charge noise sensitivity, and gate fidelity (e.g., 99.82%).

Assumptions / limitations. The coherence time limitation is directly correlated with the minimization of the spin-electric susceptibility, suggesting charge noise is the dominant decoherence source.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the anisotropy of effective g-factors and the angular dependence of LSES, T_Echo^2, and the resulting quality factors (Q*) for the two qubits (Q1 and Q2).

Paper structure. The paper systematically investigates the angular dependence of qubit parameters, linking the minimization of charge noise susceptibility (LSES) to the maximization of coherence time and gate fidelity.

Abstract

Hole spin qubits based on semiconductor quantum dots are promising for building future large-scale quantum computers owing to their all-electrical manipulation. However, abundant physical mechanisms of valence band holes lead to anisotropic qubit properties. There is an opportunity to prolong the coherence time and achieve high-fidelity qubit manipulations. Here, we report a single-hole spin qubit in a planar germanium quantum dot and investigate its anisotropic susceptibility to charge noise under an in-plane magnetic field. By correlating the longitudinal spin-electric susceptibility with qubit coherence and control performance, we identify an optimal operating point where the sensitivity to charge noise is minimized. We find that optimizing the magnetic-field orientation reduces the spin-electric susceptibility, resulting in a five-fold enhancement of the Hahn-echo coherence time and a nearly tenfold suppression of control infidelity. At the optimal operating point, gate set tomography demonstrates the maximum gate fidelity of 99.82 %. Our finding enhances the prospects of hole spin qubits for scalable quantum information processing.

Induced Coherence in Quantum and Classical Interferometry: Origin and Control

Authors: Balakrishnan Viswanathan, Ravi Kamal Pandey, Devendra Kumar Mishra, Sibasish Ghosh, Prasanta K. Panigrahi

Summary. This paper theoretically analyzes induced coherence in quantum interferometers, showing that the degree of interference, predictability, and entanglement are linked by a universal triality relation. The key finding is that coherence is fundamentally induced by achieving path indistinguishability between photon sources. This provides a quantitative understanding of the trade-off between these quantum properties.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a detailed theoretical framework connecting fundamental quantum concepts (coherence, entanglement, indistinguishability) to a measurable physical phenomenon (interferometry), which is highly relevant to quantum optics and open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the origin and control of coherence and entanglement in interferometry, specifically demonstrating the persistence of a universal 'triality relation' involving coherence, predictability, and entanglement.

Main result. The authors prove that the triality relation ($V^2 + P^2 + C^2 = 1$) holds in the quantum induced coherence interferometer, showing that path indistinguishability is the key mechanism inducing coherence and entanglement.

Method. The analysis uses quantum state formalism, deriving key observables like visibility (V), predictability (P), and concurrence (C) based on the overlap of idler photon paths.

Model / system. The system is a quantum induced coherence interferometer utilizing two nonlinear crystals ($ ext{NL}_1, ext{NL}_2$) pumped by a weak laser to generate correlated photon pairs via SPDC.

Key observables. Visibility (V), Predictability (P), and I-concurrence ($C_I$).

Important parameters / regimes. Idler mode overlap/Transmission coefficient ($|T|$), background field mean photon number ($ar{n}$), and the mixedness degree ($\eta$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is performed in the low-gain regime, assuming two-photon emission dominates, and later extends to mixed states.

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically illustrates the interferometer setup, showing the pumping of two crystals and the crucial alignment of the idler paths.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the quantum model and the triality relation in the low-gain regime. It then explores the role of idler alignment ($|T|$) and later extends the analysis to include background fields ($ar{n}$) and mixed states ($\eta$).

Abstract

The wave-particle duality that manifests in the Young's double-slit interferometry satisfying the well known Englert-Greenberger-Yasin inequality has recently been shown to obey a universal triality relation involving coherence, predictability and rather surprisingly entanglement. In the present work, we explicitly demonstrate the persistence of the triality relation in the quantum induced coherence interferometer and the classical configuration recovers the well known duality inequality. Furthermore, we explore the origin of coherence and entanglement, in the induced coherence interferometer, and means to control them. We show that path indistinguishability (or intrinsic indistinguishability of the sources) in our setup plays a complementary role in influencing both interference and entanglement. We bring out manifestly the nature of coherence in both classical and quantum versions of induced coherence interferometers.

Effective atom-dimer bands of three-body lattice systems at strong coupling: exact critical mass ratios and a statistics-quasimomentum duality

Authors: Mikhail V. Dolgopolov

Summary. This theoretical paper investigates the effective energy bands for three-body quantum systems on a lattice under strong contact interactions. By employing Feshbach-Schur reduction, the authors derive explicit Hamiltonians and determine the critical mass ratios governing the existence of bound states across the entire Brillouin zone. The results establish exact mathematical dualities between particle statistics and momentum shifts.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides rigorous, exact analytical results for strongly interacting, mass-imbalanced quantum systems, which are highly relevant for modeling quantum simulation experiments using optical lattices.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper analyzes the effective energy bands for three-body systems (bosonic and fermionic) interacting via contact potentials on a lattice in the strong coupling limit.

Main result. It derives explicit effective Hamiltonians and determines the complete atom-dimer zone and the critical mass ratio $\pi/(\pi-2)$ at zero quasimomentum, establishing exact dualities between statistics and quasimomentum shifts.

Method. The methodology involves Feshbach-Schur reduction onto the degenerate eigenvalue subspace, followed by solving a threshold problem formulated as a $4 imes 4$ Hermitian matrix criterion.

Model / system. The system consists of three particles (bosons or fermions) on a square lattice ($\mathbb{Z}^2$) with contact interactions of infinite strength ($\mu o \infty$). The analysis focuses on the 'atom-dimer sector' of the three-body Hamiltonian.

Key observables. Effective Hamiltonians ($h_B, h_F$), critical mass ratio $\gamma_c(\mathbf{K})$, and the onset laws for bound states.

Important parameters / regimes. Strong coupling limit ($\mu o \infty$), quasimomentum $\mathbf{K}$ across the entire Brillouin zone, and the statistics (3B, 2+1F, 2+1B).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the Feshbach-Schur reduction technique and asymptotic expansions for lattice sums in the small parameter $\delta$.

Figures summary. The notes mention tables summarizing the status of the problem and providing numerical convergence checks for lattice constants and binding energies.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by first establishing the general framework using Feshbach-Schur reduction, then deriving the explicit effective Hamiltonians for different statistics, followed by determining the zones and critical ratios via matrix criteria, and concluding with proofs of dualities and onset laws.

Abstract

We study three particles on the square lattice with contact interactions of infinite strength, for all total quasimomenta, for three statistics: three identical bosons, two identical fermions plus a third particle, and two identical bosons plus a third particle. Previous work treated only the trimer and zero quasimomentum. Here the atom-dimer sector is analyzed on the whole Brillouin zone. A Feshbach-Schur reduction onto the infinitely degenerate eigenvalue 1 of the pair-contact operator gives explicit effective Hamiltonians with proved first-order error. The threshold problem reduces to a 4x4 Hermitian matrix criterion built from the two-point resistances of the square lattice, and hence to a quartic polynomial. This yields the complete atom-dimer zone and the critical mass ratio on the Brillouin zone, with bound between 1 and pi/(pi-2), and closed forms at all symmetry points. We prove: the zones in closed form; the critical mass ratio with exact value pi/(pi-2) at zero quasimomentum, reproducing a numerical value; at most two atom-dimer states for every quasimomentum and mass ratio; the onset laws, an essential singularity on the zone boundary and a linear-over-logarithm onset in the pure p-wave; two exact dualities showing that a change of statistics is equivalent to a shift of quasimomentum by pi; and the global range of the second fermionic threshold. The onset laws admit a renormalization-group reading: the inverse lattice Green function is a marginal s-wave coupling, binding energies arise by dimensional transmutation with lattice scale 16, and the zone boundary is of marginal (BCS/Kondo) type rather than of Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless type. All constants are verified independently by lattice quadrature, computer algebra, exact diagonalisation, and infinite-lattice solvers.

Summary. This research tackles the difficulty of performing reliable quantum gates in NV ensembles due to short coherence times and pulse imperfections. By implementing a ZXZXZ gate decomposition, the authors developed and tested a composite pulse sequence. They demonstrated that this composite pulse significantly mitigates error accumulation compared to standard pulses, providing a practical route for robust quantum control in solid-state quantum memories.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides concrete, experimental protocols for achieving robust quantum control in solid-state qubits, directly relevant to building quantum processors based on NV centers.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of implementing reliable multi-pulse quantum gate sequences in Nitrogen-Vacancy (NV) ensembles, which suffer from short coherence times and hardware imperfections.

Main result. A composite pulse sequence was shown to suppress the accumulation of coherent control errors by over 70% compared to standard pulses over longer gate sequences.

Method. The authors synthesized universal single-qubit gates using a ZXZXZ decomposition, comparing standard pulse sequences against specially designed composite pulses.

Model / system. The system is an ensemble of NV centers in diamond, controlled using microwave pulses. The physics is modeled using the ground-state Hamiltonian, focusing on single-qubit rotations parameterized by pulse sequences.

Key observables. Mean Squared Error (MSE) for gate fidelity and the decay parameter ($\eta$) from Randomized Benchmarking (RB).

Important parameters / regimes. Short coherence time ($T_2^* \sim 1 \mu ext{s}$), high-power operation regime where Rabi frequency approaches hyperfine splitting.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that gate performance is limited by the accumulation of coherent control errors during repeated pulse applications, rather than just single-pulse fidelity.

Figures summary. Figures compare MSE for standard vs. composite pulses across multiple gate applications, and show RB decay parameters ($\eta$) for both implementations.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the need for robust gate synthesis, details the ZXZXZ decomposition using calibrated $\pi/2$ primitives, compares standard vs. composite pulse performance via MSE, and analyzes RB results to quantify error suppression.

Abstract

Ensemble nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers are widely used for quantum sensing and magnetometry, where performance depends on the accurate implementation of microwave pulse sequences. In these systems, inhomogeneous broadening and short coherence times limit reliable multi-pulse control, while hardware imperfections and off-resonant excitation distort implemented rotations, making direct calibration of arbitrary gates tedious. To address this, we implement a control framework with a ZXZXZ gate decomposition which uses fixed-angle, phase-parameterized pulses, enabling universal single-qubit control from a single calibrated $ π/2$ primitive ($X_{π/2}$) along with virtual Z operations. We compare a standard pulse with a composite pulse designed for high-power operation in a regime where the Rabi frequency is comparable to the hyperfine splitting, leading to non-uniform evolution across hyperfine levels. Using pre-determined combinations of $π/2$ pulse sequences, we observe that standard pulses rapidly deviate from ideal behavior under repeated application, while the composite pulse suppresses this error accumulation by more than 70$\%$ for longer sequences despite its longer duration. In contrast, randomized benchmarking yields similar decay factors for both implementations, highlighting its limited sensitivity to coherent control errors. These results establish a practical route to robust control in short-coherence ensemble systems and emphasize the importance of pulse-level characterization.

Learning sparse quantum states from single-qubit measurements

Authors: Su-un Lee, Liang Jiang, Kunal Sharma

Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: 🔥 quantum measurements 4/5

Summary. This work presents efficient algorithms to reconstruct quantum states that are sparse in a product basis, even if they possess long-range entanglement. Crucially, the learning process is shown to require only single-qubit measurements, making it compatible with current quantum hardware limitations. The complexity analysis shows polynomial scaling for constant sparsity and quasipolynomial scaling for structured polynomial sparsity.

Why it may be interesting. The result is highly relevant as it provides a resource-efficient method for quantum state characterization compatible with near-term quantum hardware, which often limits measurements to single-qubit operations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of learning a quantum state that is sparse in a product basis, especially when that state exhibits long-range entanglement.

Main result. It demonstrates that such sparse states can be efficiently learned using only single-qubit measurements, avoiding the need for entangling gates.

Method. The approach relies on selected-entry tomography, which estimates individual matrix elements of the density matrix using only single-qubit measurements.

Model / system. The system involves $n$-qubit quantum states ($ ho$) whose density matrices are assumed to be $s$-sparse in an unknown product basis $B$.

Key observables. The sparsity $s$ (number of non-zero entries in the density matrix) and the ability to estimate individual matrix elements $[ ho]_B(x, y)$ via single-qubit measurements.

Important parameters / regimes. Sparsity $s$ (constant vs. polynomial in $n$), the error tolerance $\epsilon$, and the number of qubits $n$.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that the state $ ho$ is $s$-sparse in some product basis $B$. The complexity results depend on whether $B$ is known or structured.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates two types of adaptivity in single-qubit measurements. Figure 2 provides an overview of Algorithm 1, detailing the use of Pauli moment tensors.

Paper structure. The paper develops algorithms (Algorithm 1 and Algorithm 2) to learn sparse states. It establishes complexity bounds based on the sparsity $s$ and the knowledge of the product basis $B$, culminating in theorems detailing sample and computational complexity.

Abstract

We study the problem of learning a sparse quantum state, an $n$-qubit quantum state whose density matrix has at most $s$ nonzero matrix entries in an unknown product basis. While such states admit compact classical descriptions, they can carry long-range entanglement that prevents reconstruction from local reduced density matrices alone. Therefore, previous learning approaches addressed such long-range-entangled states using many entangling gates to extract the necessary information. In this work, we show that sparse states can nevertheless be efficiently learned using only single-qubit measurements. Specifically, when the sparsity $s$ is constant, our algorithm can learn sparse states from single-qubit measurements with polynomial sample complexity and classical computational complexity. When $s$ grows polynomially with $n$, sparse states can still be learned from single-qubit measurements with polynomial sample complexity, although efficient classical computation is not guaranteed in general. In this regime, however, the classical computational complexity becomes quasipolynomial when the state is sparse in an unknown basis that is a product of a known fixed finite set of single-qubit bases (e.g., eigenbases of Pauli operators). These results establish efficient learning of sparse states with long-range entanglement without entangling gates, and the single-qubit measurement requirements make our algorithms compatible with current quantum devices.

Mobile-impurity dynamics in a non-Hermitian Stark-localised bath

Summary. This paper analyzes how a mobile impurity interacts with a bath in a non-Hermitian Stark-localised environment. It finds that the non-Hermitian asymmetry alters the bath's memory retention and shifts the conditions under which the impurity itself becomes localized. The results highlight a counterintuitive localization mechanism driven by density fluctuations in the intermediate potential regime.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the interplay between non-Hermiticity, external potentials (Stark), and impurity dynamics, which is highly relevant for understanding open quantum systems and engineered quantum matter.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the dynamics of a mobile impurity coupled to a bath subjected to a linear Stark potential and asymmetric hopping in one dimension.

Main result. The impurity localizes counterintuitively in an intermediate Stark potential regime where the bath's initial-state memory is suppressed, and the non-Hermitian skin effect shifts this localization window toward stronger tilt.

Method. The analysis uses time evolution simulations, exact diagonalization for small systems, and theoretical derivations involving non-Hermitian spectral decomposition and similarity transformations.

Model / system. The system consists of a bath of hardcore bosons (species 'a') on an open chain coupled to a single mobile impurity boson (species 'b'). The Hamiltonian includes hopping, a linear Stark potential, and on-site interaction terms.

Key observables. Bath imbalance ($\langle I_{avg} angle$), impurity mean-square displacement ($\langle M_{avg} angle$), and entanglement entropy ($S(t)$).

Important parameters / regimes. Interaction strength ($U_{ab}$), Stark potential tilt ($\Delta_a$), and non-Hermitian hopping asymmetry ($g$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes open boundary conditions (OBC) and models the bath using hardcore bosons mapped to non-interacting fermions.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the average late-time bath imbalance ($\langle I_{avg} angle$) versus interaction/tilt strengths for both Hermitian and non-Hermitian cases, and show the impurity mean-square displacement ($\langle M_{avg} angle$).

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the model and key diagnostics, then analyzes how the non-Hermitian asymmetry affects bath memory via resonances, and finally determines the conditions for impurity localization, showing the shift due to the skin effect.

Abstract

We investigate the dynamics of a clean mobile impurity coupled to a bath subjected to a linear Stark potential as well as asymmetric hopping in one dimension. The impurity mediates resonant tunnelling in the bath whenever the interaction strength matches the Stark potential and leads to loss in the memory of the initial state of the bath particles. The non-Hermitian asymmetry does not shift these resonances but reshapes them directionally, and it destroys the bath's Stark-induced memory through the non-Hermitian skin effect. On the other hand, we find that the impurity is localised only within an intermediate range of Stark potential. At very weak tilt strengths the bath offers no potential to trap the impurity, while at strong tilt the bath freezes into a nearly periodic charge density wave that pushes the impurity towards partial localisation. Counterintuitively, the impurity localises in the intermediate regime where the bath's initial-state memory is strongly suppressed, yet a spatially inhomogeneous density profile persists. The interaction amplifies these density fluctuations, dressing the impurity with an effective disordered potential that traps it. Since the skin effect degrades this intermediate density distribution, the impurity localisation window shifts toward stronger tilt in the non-Hermitian regime.

Abstract

We establish a fully static, dissipation-only primitive for Gaussian state transfer in which collective coupling to a shared environment fixes a single transfer time without coherent transport or time-dependent control. The dynamics close in a bright--dark decomposition, yielding an analytic transfer time $t^{*} = π/ g_{a}$ and a real interference factor that eliminates dynamical phase accumulation, so that all phase sensitivity arises solely from the Gaussian fidelity metric. This defines a fixed-time Gaussian communication channel in which dissipation mediates information transfer without active control. Ensemble analysis shows that channel performance remains robust to amplitude-level asymmetries, which act as uniform-rate dressing, whereas phase-level system detuning disrupts the fixed-time mechanism via dynamical phase winding and produces revival behavior. Abstract

Electrolytes under an external magnetic field exhibit a Hall conductivity, much like metals and semiconductors. However, existing theories fail to quantitatively account for their measured Hall conductivities. To make progress toward such a theory, we develop a stochastic density functional framework for electrolytes in a magnetic field. This framework, which we dub odd SDFT, extends the celebrated stochastic density functional theory (SDFT) to incorporate transverse mobilities and chiral time-correlated noise. Using this framework, we calculate the Hall conductivity in the absence of hydrodynamic solvent effects through three approaches: (i) direct response, (ii) Green-Kubo relation, and (iii) a hybrid fluctuation-dissipation relation. We further show that odd SDFT applies more broadly to fluids with broken time-reversal and mirror symmetry, including odd-diffusive fluids and chiral active matter. Our results generalize the relaxation correction of Debye-Hückel-Onsager theory to magnetized electrolytes and provide a foundation for incorporating additional effects mediated by the solvent that could quantitatively reconcile theory and experiments.

Abstract

We study a quantum battery consisting of $N$ qubits interacting uniformly with each other. Starting from its ground state, the battery is charged by introducing periodic kicks in the transverse direction along with adding disorder in the interaction direction. Although the battery Hamiltonian along with the initial state preserves the permutation symmetry, the dynamics in presence of disorder during the charging phase takes the system out of the permutation symmetric subspace, resulting to a larger amount of stored energy as compared to that without disorder. In the limit of large disorder, this interplay brings in a possibility of a chaotic charging phase when stored energy can be exactly calculated using random matrix theory. More importantly, we also find that the energy fluctuations decrease and become negligible in the limit of large disorder, which is also when the energy stored is maximum, a condition highly desirable for the optimum functioning of the battery. We complete the battery cycle by considering the storage phase in the presence of dephasing noise. We find that for larger disorder strengths, the ergotropy remains close to the stored energy, indicating nearly complete energy extractability with an efficiency approaching unity.
