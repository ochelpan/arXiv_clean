Rare but stable: hidden states in the 1D swarmalator model

Authors: Rommel Tchinda Djeudjo, Kevin P. O'Keeffe, Timoteo Carletti
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.33929v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 2/5 · driven-dissipative phase transition 2/5 · methods for driven-dissipative 2/5 · non-equilibrium universality 2/5 · Frenkel-Kontorova 1/5 · correlated / nonlocal dissipation 1/5

Summary. This paper reveals that the 1D swarmalator model hosts numerous stable, 'hidden' equilibrium states beyond the well-known macroscopic phases. The authors prove these states are locally robust, characterized by an algebraic decay in their stability core radius. This work provides a detailed mathematical contrast to other coupled oscillator models, showing that the hidden states possess compact local basins rather than large, complex global basins.

Why it may be interesting. The study provides a detailed mathematical characterization of pattern formation and stability in coupled nonlinear oscillators, offering insights into how local stability can persist even when global attractors are dominant, which is relevant for understanding complex collective behavior in many-body quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To demonstrate that the 1D swarmalator model possesses a rich set of stable, 'hidden' equilibria beyond the three commonly recognized macroscopic states (synchrony, phase wave, and asynchrony).

Main result. These hidden states, including q-twisted and m-clustered families, are locally robust with survival radii decaying algebraically (1/q or 1/m), contrasting sharply with the global basin structures found in related models like the Kuramoto ring.

Method. The analysis combines linear stability analysis using the Jacobian matrix, closed-form eigenvalue calculations, and local robustness measurements via Gaussian perturbations (kicks).

Model / system. The one-dimensional swarmalator model, which describes coupled oscillators with bidirectional spatial and internal phase coupling. The dynamics are analyzed by viewing the flow as a gradient descent on a potential U.

Key observables. Local survival radius ($\epsilon^*$), far-return probability ($P_{ret}(\epsilon)$), and the stability eigenvalues derived from the Jacobian matrix.

Important parameters / regimes. Coupling strengths ($J', K'$), the winding number ($q$), and the cluster size ($m$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis focuses on fixed points away from degenerate coupling axes and assumes the local robustness can be characterized by the algebraic decay of the core radius.

Figures summary. Figures compare the local survival radius scaling between the swarmalator (algebraic decay) and the Kuramoto ring (Gaussian basins), and compare the far-return probability, showing the swarmalator's basin ends at a compact core.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the model's Hamiltonian structure, performs linear stability analysis on structured equilibria (q-twists, m-clusters), and then rigorously compares the local stability core size against the global basin geometry, contrasting it with known results for nearest-neighbor systems.

Abstract

The one-dimensional swarmalator model is usually understood through three macroscopic states: synchrony, the phase wave, and asynchrony. We show that the identical model also contains a much larger set of hidden stable equilibria - families of q-twisted and m-clustered states - which random initial conditions almost never reach, because many are embedded in a degenerate neutral manifold of vanishing global basin. They are nonetheless locally robust: under Gaussian perturbations of per-coordinate amplitude ε their survival radii shrink only algebraically, as 1/q and 1/m, in sharp contrast to the Gaussian basins and winding ceiling of twisted states on a nearest-neighbour Kuramoto ring, both removed by mean-field coupling. A block-circulant Jacobian fixes the stability in closed form, and far-kick experiments indicate the basins are compact cores rather than tentacled sets. Several patterns reported in extended swarmalator models thus already exist, stable or neutrally stable, in the minimal one.

Summary. This work characterizes the electrothermal response of superconducting nanowires embedded within a commercial CMOS chip. The results establish a two-channel model governing the current's dependence on wire length.

While focused on solid-state electronics, the detailed thermal modeling, phase transition analysis (superconducting transition), and non-equilibrium heat transfer mechanisms (phonon cooling) are highly relevant to understanding energy dissipation in nanoscale quantum devices.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the electrothermal behavior of superconducting nanowires integrated into a commercial CMOS process, specifically determining how heat is dissipated to sustain a resistive hot spot.

Main result. The measured effective cooling coefficient ($\Sigma_K$) is significantly lower than ideal theoretical benchmarks, indicating that the buried CMOS environment severely limits heat removal. The retrapping current ($I_r$) is governed by a two-channel model balancing longitudinal conduction and interfacial phonon cooling.

Method. The study uses four-point I–V measurements across varying temperatures and geometries to compare measured retrapping currents ($I_r$) against theoretical thermal models.

Model / system. The system is a TiN superconducting nanowire buried in the front-end-of-line of a 22 nm FD-SOI CMOS process. Heat loss is modeled via longitudinal conduction to contacts and interfacial phonon cooling into the surrounding silicon/metal stack.

Key observables. Retrapping current ($I_r$), switching-to-retrapping current ratio ($I_{sw}/I_r$), and the effective cooling coefficient ($\Sigma_K$).

Important parameters / regimes. Bath temperatures ($T_b$) ranging from 0.15 K to 1.19 K; wire widths ($W$) of 0.5 and 1.0 $\mu$m; crossover length ($\ell^*$) of $\approx 3.5 \mu$m.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on fitting the temperature dependence of $I_r$ to an interfacial phonon radiation model ($n=4$) and employing a two-channel model to describe length dependence.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the hysteretic I-V curves, confirm the $T_b$ dependence of $I_r$ follows an $n=4$ law, and show the constant value of the extracted $\Sigma_K$ across different measurement points.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by characterizing the hysteretic I-V curves, fitting the temperature dependence of the retrapping current to determine the cooling mechanism, and finally analyzing the length dependence of $I_r$ using a two-channel model to extract the effective cooling coefficient $\Sigma_K$.

Abstract

Superconducting films native to a commercial CMOS process enable co-integration of superconducting devices (high-kinetic-inductance elements and nanowire detectors) with cryogenic CMOS on one monolithic die. Such films are buried in the front-end-of-line, beneath the back-end-of-line metal stack, an environment that might quench a self-heated hot spot. Current-voltage characteristics are reported for titanium nitride (TiN) nanowires in the polycrystalline-silicon resistor layer of a 22 nm FD-SOI process, and measured retrapping currents are compared with thermal models for longitudinal conduction to the contacts and interfacial phonon cooling into the stack. The wires are strongly hysteretic, with switching-to-retrapping current ratios I_sw/I_r of 7.4-9.8, sustaining a normal hot spot. For two wires of length ell = 50 micrometers and widths W = 0.50 and 1.00 micrometers, over bath temperatures T_b = 0.15-1.19 K, I_r follows the interfacial form (T_hs^n - T_b^n)^(1/2) with n = 4 (T_hs, the effective hot-spot temperature), and is strongly inconsistent with the (T_hs - T_b)^(1/2) constant-kappa longitudinal-conduction limit; free-exponent fits give n = approximately 4.8-5.0. An effective cooling coefficient Sigma_K = 7.9(3) W/(m^2 K^4) describes the data: 31(21) times below the acoustic- (diffuse-) mismatch value for a single clean interface, and approximately 12 times below the lowest previously reported comparable nanowire coefficient. The model-based film thermal conductivity is kappa = approximately 20 mW/(m K). Across lengths ell = 1-50 micrometers, I_r matches neither ell^(-1) of pure longitudinal conduction nor ell^0 of pure interfacial cooling; a two-channel model reproduces I_r(ell) with crossover length ell* = approximately 3.5 micrometers predicted, to an O(1) prefactor, from Sigma_K and kappa.

Summary. This paper demonstrates that directional population pumping in a two-level system can be achieved by coupling it to a quantum source, rather than a classical field. By treating the source quantum mechanically and using the Fluctuation-Regulated Quantum Master Equation, the authors show that the source's fluctuations generate an asymmetric dissipative channel. This mechanism allows the system to exceed the standard optical-Bloch saturation limit.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a detailed theoretical framework for engineering non-equilibrium quantum processes, specifically demonstrating how quantum noise (fluctuations) can be harnessed to violate classical saturation limits in quantum optics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses how to achieve directional population transfer (pumping) in a two-level system (TLS) when driven by a quantum source, overcoming the saturation limit imposed by classical driving fields.

Main result. Directional pumping emerges from the second moments of the quantum source, leading to a drive-induced dissipative contribution in the master equation that can push the steady-state population above the classical optical-Bloch limit.

Method. The dynamics are analyzed using the Fluctuation-Regulated Quantum Master Equation (FRQME) framework, treating the source as a quantum subsystem that is subsequently traced out.

Model / system. The system consists of a target TLS coupled to a quantum source, modeled as a single-excitation degree of freedom. The interaction is an exchange coupling, and the dynamics are governed by the FRQME.

Key observables. Steady-state excited-state population ($ ho_{ss}^{ee}$), pumping rate ($\kappa$), and target coherence ($ ho_{ss}^{eg}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Source correlation time ($ au_c$), detuning ($\Delta\omega$), and coupling strength ($g$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the FRQME and approximations such as retaining the secular second-order contribution; the source is treated as a stream of single-excitation ancillas.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the dependence of $ ho_{ss}^{ee}$ on source angles ($ heta, \phi$) and coupling strengths, and show the characteristic Lorentzian detuning dependence of the pumping rate.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theory by first establishing the limitations of classical driving, then deriving the effective dynamics using the FRQME based on source moments, and finally analyzing the resulting pumping rates and steady-state populations under various parameter regimes.

Abstract

A resonant classical field drives a two-level system coherently and, with ordinary relaxation, produces saturation rather than directional population transfer. Here we show that directionality emerges when the excitation source is retained as a quantum subsystem and subsequently traced out. We model the source as a single-excitation degree of freedom, or equivalently a stream of two-level ancillas, exchange-coupled to a target two-level system. The source first moment generates the usual coherent drive, while its second moments produce upward and downward dissipative channels. For a number-diagonal, fully excited source, the first moment vanishes and only the upward channel remains, yielding monotonic pumping of the target. The same structure follows from both an exact collision map and a fluctuation-regulated second-order quantum master equation. For exponentially decaying source correlations, the pumping rate shows a Lorentzian detuning dependence governed by the correlation time $τ_c$, accompanied by a dispersive frequency shift. In the presence of thermal relaxation, the steady-state excited-state population can exceed the resonantly driven optical-Bloch limit of one half. Within the fluctuation-regulated master equation, this directional pumping arises as a drive-induced dissipative contribution whose strength and spectral response are controlled by $τ_c$.

Abstract

This thesis studies how disorder and fluctuations shape large-scale growth phenomena in three related settings: fluctuating interfaces, random spring chains, and random multiplicative growth. The first part concerns interface growth and a phenomenon denoted anomalous scaling, where local and global exponents differ.To clarify its origin, we study two models in which this mismatch has different causes. The first is a heterogeneous elastic line, where we show that the apparent anomaly is a purely statistical effect. The second is the stochastic porous medium equation, a strongly nonlinear model in which the anomaly is genuine. The second part concerns Anderson localization, which appears here through the spectral properties of the heterogeneous elastic lines. We study chains with both random masses and random spring constants, with particular emphasis on the strong-disorder regime, where standard weak-disorder expansions break down. We develop a new combinatorial method to probe this regime. The last part concerns random multiplicative growth with redistribution. In such models, changes in wealth, population, or mass, for instance, are proportional to the amount already present, so that small differences are amplified over time. This naturally generates broad distributions and provides a simple mechanism for the emergence of inequality and concentration, with applications to population dynamics, wealth distribution, city growth, and ecology. We in particular study models in which each site has its own quenched growth rate, representing a persistent advantage or disadvantage, and is also subject to transient fluctuations. Redistribution competes with these mechanisms by homogenizing the system. This competition leads to rich phase diagrams.

Monotonicity of the Rényi channel capacity under non-signaling assisted channel simulation

Summary. This theoretical paper proves that non-signaling correlations cannot enhance the Rényi capacity of a classical channel at any order $\alpha$. By using a variational method based on the Legendre transform, the authors show that the capacity remains bounded by the original channel's capacity, establishing a fundamental limit on information processing assisted by non-causal correlations.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical information theory, the techniques involving capacity bounds, information geometry, and the comparison between physical constraints (causality) and idealized correlations (non-signaling) are highly relevant to understanding resource theories in quantum information.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates whether non-signaling correlations shared between sender and receiver can increase the Rényi capacity of a classical communication channel.

Main result. The authors prove that no non-signaling box can raise the Rényi capacity at any order $\alpha$, meaning non-signaling simulation cannot improve the channel's capacity bounds.

Method. They employ a variational approach based on the Legendre transform to linearize the channel dependence, allowing the analysis of the capacity as an extremum over affine functionals.

Model / system. The model involves classical communication channels and the concept of a 'non-signaling box,' which represents correlations beyond those allowed by causality. The analysis covers the Rényi capacity $R_\alpha$ for all orders $\alpha \in [0, \infty]$.

Key observables. Rényi capacity ($R_\alpha$), random-coding exponent, and strong-converse exponent.

Important parameters / regimes. The order $\alpha$ of the Rényi capacity, and the non-signaling constraints defining the simulation box.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the mathematical structure of the non-signaling box being linear while the capacity functional is nonlinear, which is resolved by the Legendre transform.

Paper structure. The paper systematically proves the monotonicity of $R_\alpha$ across different regimes of $\alpha$, starting with the Shannon capacity ($\alpha=1$) and extending to the limiting cases $\alpha=0$ and $\alpha=\infty$.

Abstract

We ask whether non-signaling correlations shared between the sender and receiver can increase the Rényi capacity of a classical channel. Non-signaling boxes form a strictly larger class than that of encoder and decoder pairs assisted by shared randomness, so the usual argument based on independent preprocessing and postprocessing is insufficient, and the constraints defining such boxes are linear in the box, while the Rényi capacity depends on the channel nonlinearly. We resolve this mismatch with a variational approach based on the Legendre transform, which recasts the capacity as an extremum over a family of affine functionals in which the channel appears linearly. Combining this method with separate arguments for the Shannon capacity and endpoint orders, we prove that no non-signaling box can raise the Rényi capacity at any order, from order zero through the Shannon capacity to the limiting order at infinity. Consequently, non-signaling simulation cannot increase the random-coding or sphere-packing exponent below capacity, or decrease the strong-converse exponent above capacity.

Summary. This paper introduces the log-Euclidean Rényi Conditional Mutual Information, a generalized measure designed to overcome theoretical hurdles in extending classical concepts to quantum settings. It proves that this new quantity maintains desirable properties like additivity and monotonicity, and it derives improved bounds for the standard QCMI. This advances the theoretical toolkit for quantifying conditional independence in quantum systems.

Why it may be interesting. The development of robust, generalized measures of conditional independence is crucial for understanding entanglement structure and correlations in complex quantum many-body systems, which is a core topic in AMO physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The central problem is finding a fully quantum Rényi generalization of the Conditional Mutual Information (QCMI) that retains desirable structural properties like monotonicity and additivity.

Main result. The authors introduce the log-Euclidean Rényi Conditional Mutual Information, $I_{ lat}^{\alpha}(A : B|C) ho$, which satisfies key properties and provides improved bounds on the standard QCMI.

Method. The work employs variational principles, log-Euclidean divergences, and Taylor expansions around the $\alpha=1$ limit to define and analyze the generalized information measures.

Model / system. The analysis is conducted within the framework of quantum information theory, focusing on tripartite quantum states $ ho_{ABC}$ and their associated conditional independence measures.

Key observables. Quantum Conditional Mutual Information (QCMI), Log-Euclidean Rényi Conditional Mutual Information ($I_{ lat}^{\alpha}$), and various forms of quantum relative entropy (e.g., Umegaki, Petz).

Important parameters / regimes. The Rényi parameter $\alpha$, which is varied to show convergence to the standard QCMI as $\alpha o 1$.

Assumptions / limitations. The definitions for the generalized quantities often require the states to be full-rank, necessitating the use of limiting definitions for non-full-rank cases.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by defining the generalized quantities, proving their structural properties (monotonicity, additivity), establishing bounds on QCMI using relative entropies, and finally deriving novel chain rules.

Abstract

Quantum conditional mutual information (QCMI) is a fundamental measure of conditional independence, with central applications throughout quantum information theory and many-body physics. Despite its importance, finding a fully quantum Rényi generalization that satisfies all desirable structural properties has remained an open question. In this work, we introduce a quantum Rényi conditional mutual information possessing these desirable properties: it is monotone under local quantum channels, additive under tensor products, monotone in the Rényi parameter, and converges to the QCMI in the limit $α\to 1$. We establish bounds on this quantity in terms of relative entropies to the Petz-recovered state, showing that it provides a distinct measure of approximate conditional independence. As a consequence, we derive recovery-based upper and lower bounds on the QCMI in terms of quantum generalizations of the Kullback-Leibler divergence, improving upon the best known results in certain regimes. Finally, we establish bounds in terms of relative entropies to the sets of Markov and separable states and derive Rényi chain rules, which appear to be novel even in the classical setting.

Abstract

Summary. This paper presents a novel Multilevel Plaquette-Space Sampler (PSS) designed to efficiently sample the complex distributions of lattice gauge theories. By reformulating the sampling problem using a coarse-to-fine factorization, it overcomes the limitations of traditional link-space samplers, particularly in the weak coupling regime. The method is validated by showing superior performance in calculating key physical observables like topological susceptibility.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on gauge theory, the methodology—using multilevel normalizing flows to solve highly constrained, high-dimensional sampling problems—is a powerful general technique applicable to other complex many-body systems where constraints are present.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core challenge is developing an efficient sampling method for the high-dimensional probability distribution of lattice gauge theories while exactly satisfying the geometric Bianchi constraints.

Main result. The proposed Multilevel Plaquette-Space Sampler (PSS) substantially outperforms link-space generative samplers, especially in the challenging weak coupling regime.

Method. The authors introduce a multilevel normalizing-flow construction that factors the global constraint problem into a sequence of local, coarse-to-fine solves, allowing exact sampling in plaquette space.

Model / system. The study focuses on Lattice Gauge Theories (e.g., U(1) and SU(2)) defined on a hypercubic lattice, governed by the Wilson action, which dictates the target Boltzmann distribution.

Key observables. Effective Sample Size (ESS), Topological Susceptibility ($\chi_{ ext{top}}$), Wilson Loops ($\langle W angle$), and the KL divergence ($ ext{KL}(q|p)$).

Important parameters / regimes. The gauge coupling ($eta$) and lattice size ($L$) are key parameters, with special interest in the weak coupling limit.

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes that the constraint completion maps ($R_i$) are fixed, bijective, and volume-preserving, and that coarse-to-fine satisfaction implies global satisfaction.

Paper structure. Abstract

Lattice gauge theories are an important class of physical models with high-dimensional structured distributions that underpin first-principles calculations in particle, nuclear, and condensed-matter physics. Recent advances in generative modeling have opened new avenues for sampling Boltzmann distributions in lattice gauge theories, offering the potential to alleviate limitations of traditional Monte Carlo methods, including critical slowing down and topological freezing. However, generative samplers for lattice gauge theories are typically constructed in link space, where correlations become increasingly long-ranged toward weak coupling, posing a challenge for learning. Plaquettes provide a more natural representation, as the action is local in these variables. However, exact Bianchi constraints restrict them to a lower-dimensional manifold, complicating direct generative modeling. We introduce a multilevel normalizing-flow construction that samples directly in plaquette space while satisfying these constraints exactly. The key idea is a coarse-to fine factorization that transforms a globally coupled constraint problem into a sequence of local solves: at each refinement, every determined plaquette depends on at most four newly generated variables, while the size of the constraint problem remains independent of the lattice size. We validate the construction for $U(1)$ in two and four dimensions and for $SU(2)$ in two dimensions. Our multilevel Plaquette-Space Sampler (PSS) substantially outperforms link-space baselines, with the advantage increasing toward weak coupling, where the gauge coupling becomes small. This regime is particularly challenging for generative sampling and, in asymptotically free gauge theories, is relevant to continuum studies.

Abstract

Preparation contextuality is often manifested operationally through a quantitative advantage over preparation-noncontextual models in information-processing tasks, yet quantum theory typically falls short of perfect success. Here we introduce a parity-oblivious Hidden Matching task that instead yields an all-vs-nothing manifestation of preparation contextuality. Alice encodes an $n$-bit string so that her message reveals no information about any input parity other than the two-bit parities. Bob, given a perfect matching on the input positions, must output an edge of the matching together with the parity of its two bits. A quantum protocol using a single $\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$-qubit message satisfies the parity-obliviousness constraint and succeeds with certainty. We prove that no preparation-noncontextual model achieves perfect success for any even $n\ge6$. The result holds for arbitrary ontic state spaces and assumes neither outcome determinism nor measurement noncontextuality. For $n=6$ and $n=8$, the optimal noncontextual success probabilities are $4/5$ and $3/4$, respectively, while asymptotically the optimal noncontextual success probability is $1/2+Θ(1/\sqrt n)$. Our asymptotic upper bound follows from a Fourier-analytic sum rule derived via hypercontractivity on the Boolean hypercube. The resulting separation is qualitative rather than merely quantitative: quantum theory achieves perfect success, whereas preparation-noncontextual models cannot. We aslo show that at $n=8$ the noncontextual bound remains $3/4$ even when Bob is restricted to just suitable $4$ of the $105$ possible perfect matchings, bringing the effect within experimental reach.

Abstract

Quantum processes can exhibit indefinite causal order, allowing coherent superpositions of distinct orderings of events. Whether such processes generate genuinely noncausal statistics, however, depends not only on their global causal structure but also on the local operations available to the parties. Here, we restrict communication to the classical outcomes of local operations, yielding a causal polytope with new causal inequalities that can be violated using measure-and-reprepare, entanglement-breaking instruments requiring no coherent transmission through the laboratories. Using a semidefinite-programming framework, we bound the achievable violations and reveal noncausal correlations that satisfy all conventional input-signalling inequalities, demonstrating that restricting the information available for communication can uncover forms of indefinite causal order inaccessible under less restrictive operational assumptions.
