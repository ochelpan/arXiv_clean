Finite-temperature phase transitions across spin symmetries in two-dimensional magnets

Authors: Chen Chen, Moritz A. Goerzen, Megha Arya, Lionel Calmels, Yongping Du, Dongzhe Li
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.32644v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work investigates phase transitions in 2D magnets by simulating the Heisenberg model across varying magnetic anisotropies. The authors find that while BKT theory predicts certain critical temperatures in the easy-plane regime, the actual behavior is dominated by the interplay between finite-size effects and the nucleation of vortex-antivortex pairs. This clarifies how experimental temperature scales depend on magnetic anisotropy and system size.

Why it may be interesting. The study provides a detailed, quantitative framework for understanding how finite-size effects and specific physical mechanisms (like vortex proliferation) modify universal critical behavior predicted by continuum theories (like BKT), which is crucial for interpreting experimental measurements in nanoscale magnets.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To achieve a unified understanding of finite-temperature phase transitions across different spin-symmetry classes in two-dimensional (2D) magnets.

Main result. The characteristic temperature in finite easy-plane Heisenberg magnets is governed by the interplay between finite-size spin correlations and vortex-antivortex pair proliferation, deviating from pure BKT theory predictions.

Method. Large-scale Monte Carlo (MC) simulations using the Metropolis algorithm to study the classical 2D Heisenberg model across varying magnetic anisotropy.

Model / system. The classical 2D Heisenberg model on a triangular lattice, described by a Hamiltonian interpolating between easy-plane (U(1)), isotropic (O(3)), and easy-axis (Z2) symmetries via the magnetocrystalline anisotropy parameter K.

Key observables. Normalized magnetization (M/M0), in-plane correlation length ($\xi/a$), spin stiffness ($ ho_s$), and characteristic temperatures ($T_c$) derived from heat capacity and magnetization.

Important parameters / regimes. The anisotropy parameter K, which controls the spin symmetry; finite system size effects; and the Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless (BKT) transition physics.

Assumptions / limitations. The Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction is neglected; the analysis focuses on the interplay between finite-size effects and vortex proliferation mechanisms.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the temperature dependence of magnetization across different anisotropy regimes (K), and compare characteristic temperatures derived from magnetization, heat capacity, and BKT fits in the easy-plane regime.

Paper structure. The paper systematically investigates the Hamiltonian across its symmetry limits (K=0, K<0, K>0), using MC simulations to calculate various thermodynamic observables and comparing the results against established theories like BKT physics.

Abstract

Recent advances in intrinsic two-dimensional (2D) magnets have created a need for a unified understanding of finite-temperature phase transitions across different spin-symmetry classes. Here, we investigate finite-temperature magnetism in the classical 2D Heisenberg model using large-scale Monte Carlo simulations, continuously spanning the easy-plane, isotropic, and easy-axis regimes. We find that characteristic temperatures are strongly affected by the interplay of finite-size effects and spin symmetry, induced by magnetocrystalline anisotropy, being most pronounced in the isotropic Heisenberg regime and substantially reduced toward the easy-plane and easy-axis limits. Examining Berezinskii--Kosterlitz--Thouless (BKT) physics in the easy-plane regime, we find that the characteristic temperatures independently estimated from the magnetization and heat capacity deviate significantly from those proposed by BKT theory, but match a previously reported anomaly in the XY-model. We report that characteristic temperatures instead are correlated with the proliferation of vortex-antivortex pairs, suggesting the gain in entropy by their accelerated nucleation as the origin of the anomaly. Establishing this connection both theoretically and numerically, we argue that the thermodynamic characteristic temperature in finite easy-plane Heisenberg magnets, including the deviation from BKT theory, is subject to the interplay between finite-size spin correlations and vortex proliferation. These results clarify how experimental and technologically relevant temperature scales evolve with magnetic anisotropy, spin symmetry, and system size in finite 2D magnets.

In-Memory AM Demodulation Using an All-Silicon Independent-Dual-Gate Gain-Cell Memory with $<10^{-22}$ A Leakage Determined by Single-Electron Counting

Authors: Katsuhiko Nishiguchi, Toshiaki Hayashi, Kensaku Chida, Takase Shimizu, Gento Yamahata, Seiya Kasai
Type: experiment · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.32122v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper presents an all-silicon, ultra-low-leakage memory device capable of in-memory signal processing. By achieving leakage currents below $10^{-22}$ A, the device enables the exploitation of transistor nonlinearity to perform Amplitude-Modulation (AM) demodulation directly within the memory array. This offers a highly energy-efficient, CMOS-compatible platform for advanced sensing and signal processing.

Why it may be interesting. While the physics is solid-state device physics, the concept of using fundamental electronic nonlinearities (subthreshold behavior) for signal processing in memory is relevant to energy-efficient quantum computing architectures that seek to integrate computation and storage.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is to develop ultra-low-leakage memories suitable for performing in-memory Amplitude-Modulation (AM) demodulation, thereby reducing the need for external analog circuitry.

Main result. The proposed all-silicon independent-dual-gate memory achieved leakage currents below $10^{-22}$ A, enabling long retention and allowing the exploitation of subthreshold nonlinearity for successful AM envelope detection.

Method. The authors measured leakage current using single-electron counting statistics and demonstrated AM demodulation by applying an AC signal and measuring the resulting time-averaged Signal-to-Noise (SN) voltage.

Model / system. The system is an all-silicon independent-dual-gate memory (iDGCM) fabricated on SOI, which uses an intrinsic Si channel controlled by two gates to suppress leakage paths.

Key observables. Leakage current ($< 10^{-22}$ A), retention time ($> 1000$ s), Signal-to-Noise (SN) voltage dependence on input amplitude ($S_{amp}$), and the output envelope of the AM signal.

Important parameters / regimes. Leakage current ($< 10^{-22}$ A), retention time ($> 1000$ s), and the frequency relationship required for demodulation ($f_{mod} \ll f_{cutoff} \ll f_{carrier} - f_{mod}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The leakage current measurement is an inference from single-electron counting statistics, and the analysis assumes the coupling between the Word Line and Upper Gate does not limit charge transfer in the relevant frequency range.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the device structure, leakage characterization via single-electron counting, and the core demonstration: AC-to-DC conversion showing the frequency dependence of the SN voltage and the successful recovery of the AM signal envelope.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the need for ultra-low-leakage memories. It then details the iDGCM structure and its superior leakage characteristics. The core of the work demonstrates the AM demodulation capability by exploiting the subthreshold nonlinearity, showing the frequency response and the final envelope detection.

Abstract

Ultra-low-leakage memories are attracting increasing attention for in-memory sensing and computing. However, achieving sufficiently long retention in silicon memories for analog signal processing remains challenging because of leakage through the access transistor. In this work, we demonstrate an all-silicon independent-dual-gate memory whose leakage current, inferred from single-electron counting statistics, is below $10^{-22}$ A, giving a measured retention time exceeding 1000 s. Owing to the extremely low leakage, the subthreshold nonlinearity of the access transistor can be exploited without disturbing the stored charge, enabling in-memory amplitude-modulation (AM) demodulation. The proposed memory provides a CMOS-compatible platform for ultra-low-power signal processing and in-memory sensing.

Observing Colossal and Tunable Near-field Thermal Radiation with A Highly Sensitive Annular Micro-thermocouple Junction

Authors: Ken Araki, Wei Han Won, Liping Wang
Type: experiment · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.34355v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work reports on the experimental observation of colossal and tunable near-field radiative heat transfer using a highly sensitive annular micro-thermocouple junction. By achieving measurements down to 10 nm gaps, the authors demonstrated heat transfer enhancements up to 2600 times the blackbody limit. The study confirms theoretical predictions and shows that the heat transfer can be actively tuned by inducing a phase transition in a VO2 thin film emitter.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical thermal transport, the extreme sensitivity and precise control over energy flux at the nanoscale bridge concepts relevant to quantum thermal conductance and energy transport in mesoscopic systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The research aims to observe and quantify colossal and tunable near-field radiative heat transfer, which significantly exceeds the far-field blackbody limit.

Main result. The authors experimentally demonstrated near-field heat transfer enhancement up to 2600 times over the blackbody limit at 10-nm gaps using a novel micro-thermocouple junction, and showed tunability using a VO2 phase transition.

Method. The study uses an annular micro-thermocouple junction attached to a glass fiber to measure heat transfer between a silica microsphere and an emitter, varying the gap distance down to 10 nm.

Model / system. The physical system involves a microsphere separated by a vacuum gap from an emitter (quartz, doped silicon, or VO2 thin film). The measurements are compared against theoretical models derived from fluctuational electrodynamics and the Derjaguin approximation.

Key observables. Near-field heat transfer coefficient (h_nfr), enhancement factor over blackbody limit, and the temperature dependence of the heat flux.

Important parameters / regimes. Gap distance (down to 10 nm), high Seebeck coefficient (25 uV/K), and the material phase transition temperature of VO2.

Assumptions / limitations. The temperature of the microsphere is assumed uniform due to the large annular junction area; the analysis relies on established theoretical models for radiative heat transfer.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the design and fabrication of the micro-thermocouple junction, show calibrated Seebeck coefficients and temperature profiles, and plot the measured heat transfer coefficient versus gap distance for different materials and phases.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the challenge of measuring near-field heat transfer, details the novel annular micro-thermocouple setup, presents measurements showing colossal enhancement for various materials (quartz, Si), and finally demonstrates tunable control using the VO2 phase transition, comparing all results to theoretical modeling.

Abstract

Near-field radiative heat transfer between two objects has been theoretically predicted and experimentally demonstrated to exceed far-field blackbody limit across nanoscale vacuum gap distance enabled by evanescent surface waves coupling, while significant enhancement by more than 100 times usually requires sub-50-nm vacuum gaps around room temperature. This is challenging for parallel-plate configuration with millimeter sample sizes due to intrinsic wafer bow and contaminant particles. Sphere-plate configuration with a microsphere attached to bimaterial cantilevers or micro-thermocouple tips has been used to experimentally demonstrate near-field radiative heat transfer down to 30-nm gaps, but it is much less developed because of the challenges in the sophisticated sensor fabrication, low sensitivity and weak signals. In this work, we overcome these challenges by an annular micro-thermocouple junction fabricated at the end of a glass fiber with straightforward thin-film deposition to achieve high measurement accuracy with large Seebeck coefficient 25 uV/K and thermal resistance 8.7e6 K/W. With a silica microsphere attached underneath the micro-thermocouple junction, we report experimental observation of colossal near-field radiation heat transfer over blackbody limit down to 10-nm gap up to 2600 times with quartz and 900 times with doped silicon. Upon phase transition of VO2 thin film emitter, tunable near-field heat transfer up to 430-fold enhancement is experimentally demonstrated at 15-nm gap with 64% reduction. Experimental data agrees well with rigorous modeling based on fluctuational electrodynamics and Derjaguin approximation, and underlying mechanism is understood by energy transmission calculations. The results will advance the experimental study and fundamental understanding of energy transport at nanoscale gaps.

Periodic Trends and a Physics-Based Multistage Search for High-$T_c$ Hydride Superconductors

Authors: Tianran Chen, Taner Yildirim
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.35582v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work presents a novel, computationally efficient, multi-stage workflow to screen for high-temperature superconducting hydrides. By introducing the spectral moment $A_1$ and correlating it with electronic descriptors ($\alphaeta ho$), the authors successfully prioritize thousands of candidate structures. This framework significantly accelerates the search for novel superconducting materials by guiding expensive first-principles calculations.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on solid-state superconductivity, the methodology of creating a multi-stage, physics-informed screening pipeline using low-cost electronic descriptors ($\alphaeta ho$) to guide expensive quantum calculations is highly relevant to developing predictive models in strongly correlated or complex quantum materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is developing an efficient, interpretable, and scalable workflow to prioritize candidate high-temperature superconducting hydrides, as full first-principles electron-phonon-coupling (EPC) calculations are computationally prohibitive.

Main result. The authors developed a three-stage search framework that successfully identified promising candidates, notably predicting a high $T_c$ for $ ext{Pm}ar{3}m ext{NaH}_6$ down to 50 GPa, demonstrating the utility of the screening descriptor.

Method. The workflow combines multiobjective structural screening (using USPEX), harmonic-phonon stability filtering, and coarse Density-Functional Perturbation Theory (DFPT) EPC calculations. A key diagnostic introduced is the spectral moment $A_1$.

Model / system. The study focuses on hydrogen-rich materials, specifically cubic binary hydride prototypes ($X_mH_n$) under high pressure (50 GPa to 200 GPa). The physics is governed by electron-phonon coupling mechanisms.

Key observables. Superconducting critical temperature ($T_c$), spectral moment ($A_1$), H-1s density-of-states fraction at the Fermi level ($\alpha$), projected Fermi-surface descriptor ($eta$), and atomic number density ($ ho$).

Important parameters / regimes. High pressures (50 GPa, 200 GPa); the descriptor relation $A_1 \approx h\alphaeta ho$.

Assumptions / limitations. The descriptor $A_1$ is used for prioritization and correlation, not as a universal $T_c$ predictor; the descriptor relation is calibrated for specific cubic hydride systems.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the convergence of various diagnostic measures ($T_c, A_1, \lambda, \omega\log$) versus computational cost, show correlations between $T_c$ and $A_1$, and map out Pareto fronts for structural optimization.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the computational bottleneck, introduces the diagnostic $A_1$ as a superior screening measure, derives the empirical descriptor relation $A_1 \approx h\alphaeta ho$, and applies this framework through a three-stage evolutionary search to identify and predict the properties of stable, high-$T_c$ hydride candidates.

Abstract

Hydrogen-rich materials under pressure are candidates for conventional phonon-mediated superconductivity, but first-principles electron--phonon-coupling (EPC) calculations are computationally demanding. We develop an interpretable multistage workflow for prioritizing binary hydride superconductors before full EPC calculations. Using controlled cubic $X_m$H$_n$ prototypes at 200~GPa, we introduce the spectral moment $A_1$ as an intermediate measure of electron--phonon perturbation strength. In its linewidth-based construction, $A_1$ remains finite as harmonic phonons soften, allowing related structures with imaginary harmonic modes to be screened; it does not establish superconductivity or physical realizability for unstable structures. For dynamically stable cubic systems, $A_1$ correlates strongly with the H-$1s$ density-of-states fraction at the Fermi level, $α$, a projected Fermi-surface descriptor, $β$, and atomic number density, $ρ$. These trends are summarized heuristically by $A_1\approx hαβρ$, where $h$ is framework- and pressure-dependent; the descriptor is used for ranking, not as a universal $T_c$ predictor. We incorporate $αβρ$ and enthalpic competitiveness into a three-stage evolutionary search comprising multiobjective structural screening, harmonic-phonon stability filtering, and coarse density-functional perturbation-theory (DFPT) EPC calculations. Application to more than 100,000 binary hydride structures at 50 and 200~GPa identifies candidates and prioritizes NaH$_6$ structural families. Higher-accuracy calculations show that $Pm\bar{3}m$ NaH$_6$ is dynamically stable down to 50~GPa and has a harmonic Allen--Dynes estimate of $T_c\approx230$~K. The framework combines electronic descriptors with progressively more expensive stability and EPC calculations for scalable hydride exploration.

Phonon angular momentum transfer torque

Authors: Verena Brehm, Daniel A. Bustamante Lopez, Shu Zhang, Dominik Juraschek
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.32575v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops a theoretical framework to describe how angular momentum stored in lattice vibrations (phonons) can exert a torque on the magnetization of a magnetic material. By quantifying the Phonon Angular Momentum Transfer Torque (PAMTT), the authors predict measurable effects, including shifts in ferromagnetic resonance frequencies and the ability to drive magnetization reversal on fast timescales.

Why it may be interesting. This work bridges lattice dynamics (phonons) with magnetism, providing a mechanism (PAMTT) to control magnetic switching and resonance frequencies using thermal gradients, which is relevant to understanding energy dissipation and control in spintronics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper introduces and quantifies the transfer of angular momentum from lattice vibrations (phonons) to magnetic order via the Phonon Angular Momentum Transfer Torque (PAMTT). It explores thermal routes for generating this phonon angular momentum.

Main result. PAMTT can produce measurable ferromagnetic-resonance frequency shifts and possesses the potential to drive magnetization reversal on nanosecond timescales in magnetic materials.

Method. The study uses theoretical formulation by adapting the Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert-Slonczewski equation to incorporate the torque term derived from phonon angular momentum accumulation.

Model / system. The system involves a magnetic layer whose dynamics are modeled by a macrospin description governed by the LLG equation. The coupling mechanism is the PAMTT, generated by phonon sources like the Phonon Edelstein effect or temperature gradients.

Key observables. Ferromagnetic-resonance (FMR) frequency shifts ($\Delta\omega$), magnetization switching time, and the magnitude of the torque ($ au_L$).

Important parameters / regimes. The strength of the PAMTT ($ au_L$), the anisotropy constant ($d_z$), and the coupling coefficients ($eta_P, eta_R$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis simplifies the dynamics by assuming $\alpha=0$ and $eta_R=0$ for the core LLG derivation, and it uses order-of-magnitude estimates for phonon accumulation.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the geometries for PAMTT, including the Phonon Edelstein effect. Figure 2 (mentioned) shows the dependence of magnetization switching times on the strength of the PAMTT.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the concept of PAMTT and the thermal generation mechanisms (Edelstein, temperature gradient). It then develops the theoretical framework by modifying the LLG equation and presents order-of-magnitude estimates. Finally, it uses numerical simulations to predict measurable effects like FMR shifts and switching dynamics.

Abstract

Angular momentum in solids is carried by (quasi)particles such as magnons, plasmons, and phonons, and coupling between these reservoirs enables diverse hybrid phenomena. Here, we introduce the transfer of angular momentum from lattice vibrations to magnetic order through the phonon angular momentum transfer torque (PAMTT), generated by a polarized phonon bath. We identify thermal routes for generating phonon angular momentum: a phonon Edelstein accumulation in noncentrosymmetric crystals and temperature-gradient-induced PAM currents with longitudinal Seebeck-like and transverse Hall components. Using realistic order-of-magnitude estimates, we find that PAMTT could produce measurable ferromagnetic-resonance frequency shifts and, for favorable interfacial coupling, drive magnetization reversal on nanosecond timescales.

Physics-based logarithmic description of electrostatic field enhancement in hemisphere-on-cylindrical-post structures for high-field applications

Authors: Thiago A. de Assis, Fernando F. Dall-Agnol, Richard G. Forbes
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.32974v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper presents a new, highly accurate analytical approximation for the electrostatic field enhancement factor in the hemisphere-on-cylindrical-post (HCP) structure. By deriving a compact formula with a logarithmic structure, the authors significantly improve upon previous power-law approximations. This advancement is crucial for engineering applications requiring precise knowledge of high-field regions, such as lightning protection and particle accelerator design.

Why it may be interesting. While the physics is classical electrostatics, the rigorous mathematical development of a highly accurate, non-trivial analytical approximation (logarithmic form) for a complex physical system's limiting behavior is mathematically interesting.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of accurately modeling the electrostatic (ES) field enhancement factor (FEF) at the apex of sharp conducting structures.

Main result. The authors developed a new, compact analytical approximation for the FEF that has a logarithmic structure, achieving a maximum error of only 0.15% when compared to finite-element simulations.

Method. The methodology involves deriving a new analytical formula for the apex sharpness coefficient, contrasting with older power-law approximations, and validating it against high-precision numerical simulations.

Model / system. The canonical geometry studied is the hemisphere-on-cylindrical-post (HCP) model, which is relevant for applications like lightning protection and electrical breakdown in vacuum.

Key observables. Apex field enhancement factor ($\gamma_a$), defined as the ratio of the local ES field at the apex to the background ES field.

Important parameters / regimes. Apex sharpness ratio ($\sigma_a = h/r_a$), which characterizes the geometry of the post.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis focuses on the negative-field situation, and the derived formula is validated for the range $1 \le \sigma_a \le 1000$.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the axisymmetric finite-element simulation domain for the HCP model. Figure 2 compares the calculated coefficient $c_a(\sigma_a)$ against FEM results and the new analytical fit. Figure 3 compares the new formula against older, less accurate power-type fitting formulas.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the physical problem and the HCP model, develops the new logarithmic analytical approximation for the field enhancement factor, validates this formula against detailed finite-element simulations, and compares its accuracy against historical approximations.

Abstract

Electrostatic (ES) field enhancement at sharp conducting structures plays a central role in lightning protection, corona discharge, electrical breakdown in vacuum (for example in particle accelerators), and more generally in technological applications of field electron emitters. A canonical geometry for studying this ES effect is the hemisphere-on-cylindrical-post (HCP) model, in the regime where the structure stands on a planar conductor of large lateral extent, and a large gap exists between the structure and the counter-electrode. A parameter of major interest is the apex field enhancement factor (AFEF) (apex-ES-field/background-ES-field). For this (and other) structures, a formula for the AFEF can be written in the form AFEF = ASC x ASR, where the apex sharpness ratio (ASR) is given by the ratio (post-height/apex-radius-of-curvature), and the apex sharpness coefficient (ASC) depends on the post shape. For the HCP model, no exact analytical formulas for the ASC or the AFEF are currently known. (Quite possibly none exist.) This paper develops a compact analytical approximation for the ASC and hence for the AFEF. This compact formula has a logarithmic-style structure, rather than the power-style structure used in previous approximations. When compared with precise finite-element analyses of the HCP model, over the computationally accessible range 1 to 1000 for the ASR, this logarithmic-style formula has a maximum error-magnitude of 0.15 percent, which is significantly better than older approximations.

Plasmon modes in three-dimensional nodal-ring semimetals: tilt and gap effects

Authors: Ipsita Mandal
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.35373v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical work analyzes plasmon modes in 3D nodal-ring semimetals, examining the impact of band tilting and an intrinsic mass-gap. Using the Random-Phase Approximation, the authors derive the plasmon dispersion and Drude weights for both gapless and gapped scenarios. The results provide a systematic comparison, yielding closed-form expressions particularly for the complex partially-gapped regimes.

Why it may be interesting. The detailed analysis of collective excitations (plasmons) in topological/semimetallic materials provides insights into charge dynamics that can be relevant for understanding plasmonics in engineered quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the plasmon modes in three-dimensional nodal-ring semimetals, focusing on how the introduction of band tilting and a mass-gap affects the electronic response.

Main result. The authors derive closed-form results for the density of states and Drude weights in partially-gapped regimes, systematically comparing the behavior between gapless and gapped nodal-ring systems.

Method. Calculations are performed using the Random-Phase Approximation (RPA) on the non-interacting density-density response function, analyzing the low-doping limit.

Model / system. The system is a 3D nodal-ring semimetal, modeled using a low-energy effective Hamiltonian that can be analyzed in terms of a gapless (PTNR) or gapped (GNR) structure.

Key observables. Plasmon dispersion ($\omega_p$), non-interacting density-density response function ($\Pi$), and the Drude weight tensor ($D_{ij}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Tilt parameters ($\eta_x, \eta_z$), mass-gap ($\Delta$), and chemical potential ($\mu$), analyzed across gapless, gapped, and partially-gapped regimes.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is restricted to the low-doping regime where the linearized Hamiltonian is valid, and it uses the Random-Phase Approximation.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the plasmon response by first establishing the low-energy Hamiltonian, then calculating the response function and Drude weights for the untilted case, followed by detailed investigations of the in-plane and axial tilt effects, culminating in closed-form results for the partially-gapped regime.

Abstract

We study plasmon modes in three-dimensional nodal-ring semimetals, considering both the gapless $\mathcal{PT}$-symmetric nodal ring (PTNR) and its gapped counterpart (GNR). Working in the low-doping regime where the linearised Hamiltonian is valid, we compute the non-interacting density-density response function, the Drude weight, and the plasmon dispersion within the random-phase approximation. We treat the untilted configuration as well as in-plane tilt along $k_x$ and axial tilt along $k_z$. For the in-plane tilted GNR, a strong-enough tilt or a small enough chemical potential pushes part of the ring into a partially-gapped regime. Only part of the Fermi surface survives there. We obtain closed-form results for this regime in two limits: a small tilt and a small gap. The small-gap limit produces a fractional-power correction to the Drude weight and the plasmon frequency. For the axially-tilted GNR, the partially-gapped window has a double-valued local Fermi surface for which we obtain the density-of-states and the Drude weights in closed forms. We analyse how the tilt and the mass-gap affect the plasmon dispersion and the anisotropy between axial and in-plane directions. Our results provide a systematic comparison between the gapless and gapped cases and between different tilt directions.
