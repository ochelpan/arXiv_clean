Local Many Worlds in Spacetime: Deutsch-Hayden Descriptors and Local Wavefunctions

Authors: Mordecai Waegell
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.33453v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper proposes a 'Local Many Worlds' formalism where quantum evolution is strictly local in spacetime, avoiding non-separable universal wavefunctions. It introduces Deutsch-Hayden (DH) descriptors as a compact mathematical tool to represent the history of local interactions. This hybrid picture aims to provide a fully Lorentz-covariant quantum theory suitable for quantum gravity research.

Why it may be interesting. It offers a novel, manifestly local, and Lorentz-covariant framework for quantum mechanics that attempts to resolve foundational conflicts between quantum theory and spacetime structure.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To develop a local, Lorentz covariant quantum theory of spacetime that avoids the issues of non-separable universal wavefunctions found in standard interpretations like Everett's.

Main result. The authors propose a hybrid Schrödinger-Heisenberg picture using Deutsch-Hayden (DH) descriptors, which allows for a compact, local description of quantum evolution consistent with Lorentz covariance.

Method. The paper compares the cumbersome Local Schrödinger (LS) picture, which tracks all local unitaries in the past light cone, against the DH descriptor formalism, which encodes the necessary history in a compact form.

Model / system. The model is 'Local Many Worlds in Spacetime,' treating quantum evolution as a sequence of local unitary operations acting on local wavefunctions at each spacetime event.

Key observables. Local wavefunctions, Deutsch-Hayden (DH) descriptors, and entanglement correlations (tested via Bell experiments).

Important parameters / regimes. Lorentz covariance, local unitary operators ($U_j$), and the structure of the past light cone.

Assumptions / limitations. The theory assumes no superdeterminism or retrocausality, and the DH descriptors are assumed to contain all necessary local information.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the descriptor updates for Bell tests under different measurement settings, showing how local wavefunctions are constructed sequentially in spacetime.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the LS picture's difficulties, then introduces DH descriptors as a compact alternative, and finally demonstrates that the descriptors can reconstruct the local wavefunctions, achieving a hybrid picture.

Abstract

Bell's theorem establishes that a local and spatially separable physical theory with no superdeterminism or retrocausality must be a theory of many local worlds. The local many worlds formalism of quantum mechanics is a transparent separable physical theory in spacetime with a Lorentz covariant causal structure. Unlike the Everett many worlds interpretation, there is no nonseparable universal wavefunction in configuration space, but like Everett, there is only local unitary evolution which conserves probability (fluid) current, and no wavefunction collapse. Each event in spacetime has its own local wavefunction which is built up using only the local wavefunctions and local unitary evolution operators within its past light cone, and which is entirely disconnected from spacelike separated local wavefunctions. We call this the local Schrödinger picture. Local many worlds can also be described in the Heisenberg picture by ascribing Deutsch-Hayden (DH) descriptors to each individual system, each of which evolves under local unitary operations. Both the local wavefunctions and DH descriptors contain records of past interactions, and while the local wavefunctions provide a clear and intuitive physical narrative, the descriptors contain all of the same information in a much more compact form. Unfortunately, the compact form is also somewhat cryptic, which has made the local Heisenberg picture relatively inaccessible. The purpose of this article is to show that the local wavefunctions for any $N$-qubit system can always be constructed directly from the DH descriptors, allowing us to combine the benefits of both pictures.

Placement-driven reversal of preferred spin alignment in monolayer hBN vacancy pairs

Authors: Daniel Hashemi
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.33714v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical study uses DFT to map out how the spin alignment preference between two interacting vacancies in monolayer hBN is dictated by their precise atomic placement. The results show that small structural changes can reverse the preferred spin coupling, indicating a complex, placement-sensitive electronic interaction regime. This work establishes a key physical principle for engineering coupled spin qubits in 2D materials.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a microscopic, first-principles foundation for understanding coupled spin centers in atomically thin materials, which is crucial for designing next-generation quantum devices based on solid-state defects.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how the electronic interactions between two closely placed, negatively charged boron vacancies in monolayer hBN reverse the preferred collinear spin alignment based on their atomic-scale placement.

Main result. The spin alignment preference is highly placement-sensitive, with the sign of the energy splitting reversing when the vacancies are shifted by a single lattice vector, demonstrating a departure from simple bilinear Heisenberg models.

Method. Density-Functional Theory (DFT) calculations are used to compute the energy difference ($\Delta E$) between parallel and antiparallel spin alignments for various vacancy configurations.

Model / system. The system is a monolayer of hexagonal boron nitride (hBN) containing two negatively charged boron vacancies ($ ext{V}^-$). Calculations are performed using DFT at a total charge $q=-2$ across seven different atomic placements.

Key observables. Energy splitting ($\Delta E$) between parallel (P) and antiparallel (AP) states, spin-density contrast, and the dependence of $\Delta E$ on vacancy separation and relative placement.

Important parameters / regimes. The closest pair (4.3 Å) favors parallel alignment by $27.6 ext{ meV}$, while a 7.5 Å pair favors antiparallel alignment by $50.7 ext{ meV}$. The splitting is significantly larger than the isolated-center zero-field splitting.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on collinear Kohn–Sham solutions, which are not guaranteed to represent true quantum multiplets, and the comparison is sensitive to the choice of DFT functional (PBE vs. HSE06).

Figures summary. Figures illustrate atomic placement effects, $\Delta E$ vs. separation, and spin-density isosurfaces for specific vacancy configurations, while tables summarize the calculated energy splittings for multiple placements.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the placement dependence of spin alignment using DFT, comparing results across different separations and structural motifs (e.g., chain vs. contact modes) to establish a placement-sensitive regime for interacting defects.

Abstract

The negatively charged boron vacancy is an optically addressable spin centre in hexagonal boron nitride (hBN), but bringing two vacancies together introduces electronic interactions absent from the isolated-defect picture. Here we use density-functional theory at total charge q = -2 to show that atomic-scale placement reverses the preferred collinear spin alignment in monolayer hBN. Among seven configurations, the closest pair, separated by 4.3 angstrom, favours parallel alignment by 27.6 meV within the semilocal approximation. Moving one vacancy by a single lattice vector reverses the preference, and a 7.5 angstrom pair favours antiparallel alignment by 50.7 meV. Numerical controls change these two reference splittings by less than 1 meV; hybrid-functional calculations retain their signs while substantially changing their magnitudes. Local distortions connect the antiparallel preference at 7.5 angstrom to changes in vacancy-state hybridization along an intervening atomic chain. The closest pair instead exhibits a contact-localized spin-density contrast and constrained angular energies that depart from the bilinear Heisenberg form. These results identify a placement-sensitive regime of interacting defects and provide a microscopic foundation for investigating coupled spin centres in atomically thin materials.

Scaling Theory for Learning Low-Energy Quantum Subspaces

Authors: Guijing Duan, Chen Mo, Di Luo
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.33777v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work introduces a scaling theory to predict the accuracy of approximating a low-energy quantum subspace by sampling nearby Hamiltonians. It proves that the error scales predictably with the sampling distance and system size, providing a quantitative measure of learnability. This has significant implications for designing efficient numerical methods for studying quantum phase diagrams.

Why it may be interesting. The quantitative scaling laws derived provide a rigorous, data-driven framework for understanding how much information about a complex quantum ground state can be extracted from limited sampling, which is crucial for developing efficient quantum simulation protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops a scaling theory to efficiently learn the low-energy quantum subspace of a Hamiltonian family when solving for many parameters is too costly.

Main result. It establishes that the energy-density error scales with the sampling distance $d$ according to a power law, $\epsilon \propto d^{2(q+1)}$, and identifies the scaling variable $nd^2$ for local gapped systems.

Method. The approach involves constructing a variational subspace spanned by wavefunctions sampled at nearby parameter points and analyzing the resulting energy error bounds based on canceling response orders.

Model / system. The theory is tested on the spin-1/2 Transverse-Field Ising Chain, which serves as a model for studying quantum phase transitions and low-energy spectral flow.

Key observables. Energy-density error ($\epsilon_a$), effective distance exponent ($eta$), and the scaling variable $nd^2$.

Important parameters / regimes. Sampling distance ($d$), number of sampled points ($K$), cancellation order ($q$), and system size ($n$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the existence of an external gap ($\Delta_{ ext{high}} > 0$) around the low-energy subspace, although it analyzes behavior near gap closing.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the energy-density error scaling confirming the predicted power-law exponents, show data collapse onto the $nd^2$ scaling curve, and track the effective exponent $eta$ near critical points.

Paper structure. The paper moves from establishing the general error bound based on response order cancellation to deriving the specific scaling law for gapped local systems, and finally validates this theory using numerical simulations on the Ising chain, including analysis near criticality.

Abstract

Phase diagrams and energy surfaces require low-energy states across a family of Hamiltonians, but solving each parameter point independently is prohibitively expensive. An important question is not how a single labeled eigenstate behaves under parameter variation, but how much information about the low-energy sector is contained in a small set of reference wavefunctions. We show that the physically relevant object is the isolated low-energy subspace itself: it remains well defined through degeneracies and level crossings, and it can be learned efficiently from nearby sampled states. Working in this subspace, we prove that a sampling pattern cancelling the first $q$ nonconstant response orders yields an energy-density error bounded by $d^{2(q+1)}$ for every retained level, where $d$ is the parameter-space sampling distance. For a gapped local ground state on $n$ sites, locality sharpens this to $d^2(nd^2)^q$, identifying $nd^2$ as the natural scaling variable governing the effectiveness of higher-order information from sampled wavefunctions. In a local analytic regime this gives a sampling cost $K=\mathcal{O}([\log(1/\varepsilon)]^D)$ at fixed parameter dimension $D$. Numerical results on Heisenberg and transverse-field Ising chains confirm the distance scaling law and the $nd^2$ collapse. Beyond characterizing learnability, the scaling behavior further provides a finite-size probe of phase transitions near criticality.

Speed-Fisher Information: Chaos and Irreversibility in Classical and Quantum Dynamics

Authors: Nachiket Karve, Nathan Rose, David Campbell, Anatoli Polkovnikov

Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: non-equilibrium universality 2/5

Summary. This paper introduces the 'speed-Fisher information' as a unified diagnostic tool to quantify chaos in both classical and quantum systems subjected to slow driving. It shows that chaotic behavior leads to a divergence in this information, while also predicting a unique quantum scaling regime at very short timescales. This provides a powerful, information-geometric approach to studying the transition between order and chaos.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a quantitative, information-theoretic measure (speed-Fisher information) to distinguish between regular and chaotic dynamics in both quantum and classical settings, linking chaos directly to thermodynamic irreversibility.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to establish a unified framework to study classical and quantum chaos by analyzing how a system's stationary states respond to slow, time-dependent driving.

Main result. Chaotic dynamics are characterized by a divergence in the 'speed-Fisher information' with protocol time, which is controlled by the perturbation's low-frequency spectral weight. A unique quantum scaling regime is also identified at very short timescales.

Method. The core diagnostic is the 'speed-Fisher information,' which measures the susceptibility of the final state to the average driving speed ($ar{v}$). This is analyzed using first-order perturbation theory applied to the fidelity of the state.

Model / system. The framework applies to general classical and quantum Hamiltonian systems, extending to non-Hamiltonian classical flows. The analysis involves subjecting the system to slow, cyclic driving protocols parameterized by the inverse duration $\mu=1/t$.

Key observables. Speed-Fisher information ($I_{ar{v}}$), Fidelity ($F$), and the low-frequency spectral weight of the system's correlation functions.

Important parameters / regimes. Protocol time ($t$), inverse duration ($\mu=1/t$), average speed ($ar{v}$), and the Planckian timescale ($\hbar/k_B T$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on first-order perturbation theory, which is valid under the condition that the maximum perturbation strength ($\lambda_{\max}$) is small compared to unity.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates slow, cyclic driving, contrasting regular systems (where the final state approaches the initial state) with chaotic systems (where thermodynamic friction causes dissipated work).

Paper structure. The paper introduces the speed-Fisher information as the diagnostic tool, derives its dependence on the driving speed and time scale, establishes the distinct scaling laws for regular versus chaotic dynamics, and finally examines the crossover between the unique quantum scaling and the classical scaling.

Abstract

We propose a unified perspective on classical and quantum chaos based on the sensitivity of a system's stationary states to slow driving. We probe this sensitivity via the system's susceptibility to the average protocol speed, which we call the ``speed-Fisher information," and relate it to irreversible entropy production in the system. We show that chaotic dynamics manifests as a divergence of the speed-Fisher information with the protocol time, and that this response is controlled by the perturbation's low-frequency spectral weight. This approach to chaos applies to both classical and quantum Hamiltonian systems, and naturally extends to non-Hamiltonian classical flows. Additionally, we identify a quantum regime at times shorter than the Planckian timescale, in which the speed-Fisher information exhibits a uniquely quantum scaling. We illustrate this framework with simple classical and quantum examples

Trimers, pairs and localisation on disordered random graphs

Authors: Alexei Vazquez
Type: theory · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.32768v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical work examines the stability of emergent multi-particle bound states (hyperedges) formed by pairwise interactions on disordered random graphs. It finds that these higher-order structures are surprisingly fragile, localizing at much weaker disorder strengths than their individual components. This highlights that the emergence of complex quantum correlations is highly sensitive to network disorder.

Why it may be interesting. The study provides quantitative predictions for how higher-order correlations (like three-body interactions) behave under disorder, which is crucial for understanding complex quantum materials where interactions are not purely pairwise.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates whether a higher-order group interaction (like a trimer) can emerge from simple pairwise dynamics on a graph and whether this emergent structure remains stable against disorder (Anderson localization).

Main result. Emergent hyperedges localize at a disorder strength significantly lower than that required for their constituent single-particle or pair components to localize, indicating the composite structure is more fragile to heterogeneity.

Method. The analysis uses the Bethe lattice and cavity method to calculate binding thresholds and localization transitions for $k$-body states, comparing results across different graph structures and disorder strengths.

Model / system. The model involves $k$ distinguishable fermions hopping on a graph (Bethe lattice, random graphs) with on-site attractions ($U$) and hopping ($t$), subjected to site disorder ($\epsilon_i$).

Key observables. Localization thresholds ($\Delta$ or $W_c$) for single particles, pairs, and trimers; the existence and stability of the 'Borromean' hyperedge.

Important parameters / regimes. Hopping amplitude ($t$), attraction strength ($U$), disorder strength ($\Delta$ or $W$), and the number of particles ($k=1, 2, 3$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis often relies on extrapolating results from finite truncations (Bethe lattice) to the thermodynamic limit, and sometimes treats the composite structure as rigid.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the comparison of critical disorder thresholds ($W_c$) for trimers versus pairs/single particles, and phase diagrams showing the transition between liquid, glass, and localized regimes.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining the general problem of emergent $k$-body states to calculating binding thresholds on the Bethe lattice, and finally analyzing the localization transition on disordered random graphs by comparing the critical disorder for different composite orders.

Abstract

Higher-order networks take group interactions as given. I ask what happens when a group interaction is made by pairwise dynamics on a graph, and follow it through the transition that decides whether it matters at large scale, Anderson localisation. An emergent hyperedge is a $k$-body bound state of a Hamiltonian whose terms live on the vertices and edges of the graph, Borromean when none of its $(k-1)$-body faces is bound, detected on a finite graph by a $k$-fold coincidence that survives the thermodynamic limit while the $(k-1)$-fold ones vanish. Sparse random graphs produce one from the simplest pairwise dynamics: three distinguishable particles with on-site attraction $U$ and hopping $t$ bind into a trimer before any pair binds. The thresholds are obtained on the Bethe lattice, in closed form for the pair, in both sectors that an expander graph has and a lattice lacks, the one a condensate sees and the one a Fermi liquid sees. On a disordered graph the emergent hyperedges nucleate on rare deep sites and localise before their parts: the compact trimer, with a narrow band and a threefold sensitivity to site energies, undergoes the Anderson transition at a disorder of $1.6t$, against $17t$ for a single particle and $4t$ for a pair, and the Fermi liquid of trimers is a glass above a line between $1.5t$ and $1.7t$, where a liquid of single particles at the same density needs $15t$ to $16t$. The transition is the single-particle cavity transition with the composite's hopping and effective disorder. A group interaction made by pairwise dynamics is an order of magnitude more fragile to heterogeneity than the network that makes it.

Zoology of chiral superconductors in Chern bands

Authors: André Grossi Fonseca, Aidan Reddy, Ahmed Abouelkomsan, Liang Fu, Marin Soljačić
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.31839v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 2/5

Summary. This paper develops and applies a sophisticated optimization technique to map the phase diagram of chiral superconductors in Chern bands, simulating systems relevant to moiré materials. By analyzing the pair-binding energy and employing rigorous screening, the authors uncover a rich variety of superconducting states, including novel $p\pm ip$ and $f\pm if$ phases. The work establishes a general computational strategy for exploring complex interacting quantum matter.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, generalizable computational framework (target-phase optimization) for mapping out complex, interacting quantum phases in topological materials, which is highly relevant for understanding emergent phenomena in condensed matter systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To systematically search for and characterize chiral superconducting phases in systems of interacting electrons confined within a topological Chern band.

Main result. The study identifies a diverse landscape of competing chiral superconducting instabilities, finding novel $p\pm ip$ and $f\pm if$ superconductors at specific fractional fillings like $ u=2/3$ and $ u=1/2$. Furthermore, it establishes a direct transition between chiral superconductors and composite Fermi liquids.

Method. The authors adapt the target-phase optimization method, using a differentiable loss function based on the pair-binding energy, combined with gradient-based optimization and rigorous screening procedures.

Model / system. The research uses a minimal model of interacting spinless electrons in a Chern band, motivated by moiré materials like twisted bilayer MoTe2. The system is modeled using exact diagonalization on periodically modulated Landau levels with screened Coulomb interactions.

Key observables. Pair-binding energy, superconducting loss function ($L < 0$), Off-Diagonal Long-Range Order (ODLRO) confirmed via the two-body reduced density matrix, and quantum geometric quantities like the Fubini–Study metric and Berry curvature.

Important parameters / regimes. Filling factors $ u=2/3$ and $ u=1/2$; modulation parameters ($\delta B_0, V_0, \phi, U_0, d$); and the comparison between ideal and non-ideal quantum geometry regimes.

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes the relevant single-particle dispersion has a single minimum for the even-odd effect analysis, and the final confirmation of superconductivity requires satisfying multiple criteria beyond just minimizing the loss function.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the target-phase loss function and the workflow for searching for superconductivity. Supplemental figures project the existence of chiral superconductors in the multi-dimensional parameter space for fillings $ u=2/3$ and $ u=1/2$, and show geometric characterizations.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem by referencing recent observations in moiré systems. It details the methodology—adapting target-phase optimization and defining the loss function—before presenting results across different filling fractions, culminating in a characterization of the complex phase diagram.

Abstract

Evidence for chiral superconductivity has recently been observed in several van der Waals systems including rhombohedral multilayer graphene and twisted bilayer MoTe$_2$. In the latter, superconductivity emerges at carrier densities near a fractional Chern insulator. This raises the question of what kinds of superconductors may emerge in a system of electrons in a topological band with strong repulsive interactions. Here, we adapt the target-phase optimization method to search for chiral superconductors in a minimal model of interacting electrons in a Chern band. We construct a differentiable loss function for superconductors from the sign oscillation of the pair-binding energy and combine gradient-based optimization with a rigorous screening procedure to identify and characterize superconductors. Applying this framework within exact diagonalization to spinless electrons in periodically modulated Landau levels with screened Coulomb interactions, we uncover a broad family of chiral superconducting phases. At filling $ν=2/3$, we recover the previously identified $f-\mathrm{i} f$ hole superconductors and find additional $p\pm \mathrm{i} p$ and $f+\mathrm{i} f$ superconductors of both electrons and holes, occurring near and far from the limit of ideal quantum geometry. At $ν=1/2$, we identify $p- \mathrm{i} p$ electron and $f-\mathrm{i} f$ hole superconductors and find, for the first time, a direct transition between chiral superconductors and composite Fermi liquids. Our results reveal that chiral superconductivity in Chern bands comprises a diverse landscape of competing pairing instabilities and establish target-phase optimization as a general strategy for searching for quantum phases in complex interacting systems.

Inverse-Designed Nanobeam Cavities for High-Cooperativity Spin-Photon Interfaces in Silicon

Summary. This paper presents an inverse-design methodology to engineer silicon nanobeam cavities optimized for coupling spin defects (T and Al1 centers) to photons. By simultaneously optimizing for Purcell enhancement and directional coupling, the authors achieve high cooperativities necessary for quantum information transfer. This provides a scalable, fabrication-compatible route for integrated quantum photonic devices.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the engineering challenge of coupling solid-state quantum emitters to photonic circuits, which is central to building scalable quantum networks and quantum repeaters.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core challenge is creating efficient spin-photon interfaces to transfer quantum information from stationary spin memories to flying photonic qubits for integrated quantum networks.

Main result. The authors successfully designed two complementary cavity geometries—symmetric and asymmetric—that achieve high cooperativities ($\approx 280-5000$) for silicon spin defects like the T and Al1 centers.

Method. They employed a unified adjoint-optimization framework within 3D FDTD simulations to simultaneously optimize Purcell enhancement, resonance alignment, and directional photon extraction.

Model / system. The system involves telecom-band spin defects (T and Al1 centers) embedded in silicon, coupled to engineered silicon nanobeam cavities. The design process optimizes the cavity geometry to maximize the coupling rate ($g$) relative to decoherence rates.

Key observables. Cooperativity ($C$), Purcell Factor ($F_P$), and the fraction of emitted power coupled into the on-chip waveguide ($eta_R$).

Important parameters / regimes. High cooperativity ($C \gg 1$), fabrication-compatible lattice parameters, and the ability to maintain performance under realistic absorption-limited quality factors ($Q \approx 10^5$).

Assumptions / limitations. The optimization is restricted to modifying lattice parameters of a conventional nanobeam structure, ensuring fabrication compatibility.

Figures summary. The figures illustrate the adjoint-optimization pipeline, comparing the operating principles of symmetric (for maximal Purcell enhancement) and asymmetric (for directional coupling) cavity designs.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the physical need for high-cooperativity interfaces, details the adjoint-optimization methodology for cavity design, presents the results for two complementary cavity types, and confirms the robustness of these designs against fabrication imperfections.

Abstract

Telecom-band spin defects in silicon offer a compelling platform for integrated quantum networks by combining scalable silicon photonics with spin-based quantum memories and multiqubit registers. The T center and recently demonstrated Al1 center are particularly promising, but their limited coherent zero-phonon emission requires strong cavity enhancement for efficient spin-photon interfaces. Here, we inverse-design silicon nanobeam cavities for both centers using a unified adjoint-optimization framework targeting Purcell enhancement, resonance alignment, and photon extraction. For each emitter, symmetric cavities maximize Purcell enhancement, while asymmetric cavities provide directional coupling to an integrated waveguide. Three-dimensional finite-difference time-domain simulations yield loaded quality factors up to 3.25 x 10^6, Purcell factors up to 1.43 x 10^5, and cooperativities of approximately 280-5000, corresponding to cavity-coupled emission fractions exceeding 99.6%. The asymmetric cavities direct approximately 90% of emitted power into a single on-chip waveguide while maintaining cooperativity above 280. Under nanometer-scale fabrication perturbations, all four designs remain within the high-cooperativity regime, and high cooperativity persists when the cavity quality factor is constrained to a realistic absorption-limited value of 10^5. This unified, fabrication-compatible framework establishes a scalable cavity-design route for silicon telecom spin-photon interfaces and, to our knowledge, the first cavity designs for the Al1 center.

Summary. This paper models LLM jailbreaking as a physical process of escaping a safety barrier using a 'barrier model' derived from statistical physics. It predicts that the attack success rate deviates from simple power-law scaling, suggesting that the observed scaling in prior work is an artifact of limited data. The framework provides a universal, physics-based tool to map the entire attack surface based on fundamental safety parameters.

Why it may be interesting. The use of concepts like entropic barriers, thermal activation, and glassy dynamics to model information security vulnerabilities provides a novel, physics-grounded framework for understanding complex, non-equilibrium failure modes in AI systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper analyzes Best-of-N jailbreaking (BoN) attacks on LLMs, challenging the established power-law scaling of the Attack Success Rate (ASR) with the number of augmentations ($N$).

Main result. The ASR does not follow a clean power-law, and the previously observed exponential crossover is predicted to be a finite-size artifact of the adversarial dataset.

Method. The authors introduce a physical 'barrier model' inspired by glassy dynamics, treating jailbreaking as escaping a 'Safe Basin' through an 'Entropic Bottleneck' governed by thermally activated processes.

Model / system. The model uses a barrier analogy where safety is separated by a hyperplane, and the probability of unsafe completion is governed by an Arrhenius-like factor involving prompt-specific barriers ($\alpha_b$) and augmentation-specific saddles ($\eta_{b,a}$).

Key observables. Attack Success Rate (ASR) as a function of the two budgets ($N, M$), and the effective barrier parameters ($\mu_E, \sigma_E$).

Important parameters / regimes. The model is parameterized by four core numbers ($\mu_\alpha, \sigma_\alpha, \mu_eta, \sigma_eta$) describing the distributions of the entropic and energetic barriers.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the physical mechanism (Safe Basin $ o$ Entropic Bottleneck $ o$ Unsafe Basin) and show how the model collapses multiple distinct empirical datasets onto a single scaling function.

It then derives approximations for the large-$N$ and low-budget limits, fitting the model parameters to empirical data across various augmentation scales ($\sigma$).

Abstract

Best-of-$N$ jailbreaking (BoN) bypasses safeguards of aligned models by drawing $N$ independent augmentations of an unsafe prompt and sampling $M$ completions of each. Previous works have shown that the attack success rate (ASR) seems to follow a power-law in $N$, which we challenge. The exponent drifts with $N$, with an exponential crossover which is a finite-size artifact of the adversarial dataset. Little work has been done to explore the entire two-budget ($N, M$) attack surface as well as its dependence on the generation temperature $T$. We introduce a simple barrier model where each prompt has a baseline safety level and each augmentation a random thermally activated barrier. Then four numbers, each backed by an interpretable safety mechanism, determine the entire ($N, M$) attack surface. They extrapolate predictions from $N \leq 100$ to $N = 10^4$, collapse five distinct models on the same scaling function and predict ASR at different temperatures from the one they were fitted at.

Abstract

Carleman linearization represents nonlinear lattice Boltzmann collision dynamics in a form amenable to quantum block encoding, but low postselection probabilities in existing population-space encodings make coherent multi-step evolution exponentially unlikely to succeed. We present a quantum Carleman lattice Boltzmann scheme based on the multi-relaxation-time collision operator in a weighted Hermite moment basis. In this basis, the lifted collision operator separates into small coupled blocks whose norm exceeds unity because of the coupling between conserved and relaxing modes. This block structure enables a norm-attaining block encoding with a single-step success probability close to unity. Crucially, scaling the pair sector for a prescribed evolution horizon makes the combined encoding and sector-selection overhead grow polynomially rather than exponentially with the number of steps. For a hundred-step evolution, the resulting complete-run success probability exceeds that of population-space encodings by orders of magnitude. Over horizons of several tens of steps, the truncated dynamics reproduces the nonlinearly generated modes of a benchmark flow to within a few percent. We verify the combined preparation, evolution and readout protocol as a single unitary circuit and give a complete gate-level resource estimate for extracting a nonlinear observable of the benchmark flow from a coherent Carleman evolution. The block structure of conserved and relaxing modes holds equally on three-dimensional lattices and provides a constructive route to norm-attaining encodings for lattice Boltzmann models with quadratic equilibria.

Abstract

We study the problem of implementing multivariable matrix-valued functions on a quantum computer and propose two quantum algorithms for multivariable matrix eigenvalue transformations acting on tuples of pairwise commuting matrices. The first algorithm is based on multivariable contour integrals and applies to arbitrary holomorphic functions on $\mathbb{C}^n$. The second algorithm is based on high-dimensional Fourier transforms and designed for smooth functions of commuting Hermitian matrices. For both algorithms, we discuss and analyze the complexity of their quantum implementation leveraging quantum singular value transformation, compression gadgets, and linear combination of unitaries. We additionally study their hybrid quantum--classical variants that reduce the number of required ancilla qubits to logarithmic dependence on the number of variables, at the cost of increased query complexity. As an application, we show that multivariable matrix polynomials can be implemented with no additional explicit degree dependence, depending only on global properties of the polynomials.

Abstract

We study the Heisenberg dynamics of continuum fermions in $\IR^d$ interacting through a pair potential. For a large class of nonconstant pair potentials and under suitable assumptions on the dispersion relation and external potential, we prove that the dynamics fails to be pointwise-norm continuous even on the gauge-invariant CAR algebra. An automatic-continuity argument then shows that neither the CAR algebra nor its gauge-invariant subalgebra is invariant under the dynamics, with non-invariance occurring for almost all times.
