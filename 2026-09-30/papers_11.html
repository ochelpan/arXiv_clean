The Breakdown of Classical Minicrypt Equivalences in the Quantum-Computation Classical-Communication Model

Authors: Boyang Chen, Yiming Wang, Ziyi Xie
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.33098v1

Summary. This paper analyzes the limits of cryptographic security in a quantum setting where computation is quantum but communication is classical (QCCC). It proves that the classical equivalence of cryptographic tools breaks down by constructing oracle worlds where certain primitives, like one-way functions, cannot be simulated from others. The work develops advanced quantum information tools to establish these fundamental separations.

Why it may be interesting. The techniques developed, such as the concentration theorems and the generalization of classical inequalities (Efron-Stein) to quantum states, are highly relevant for analyzing information flow and security in quantum communication protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates whether the classical equivalence of fundamental cryptographic primitives (Minicrypt) holds when computation is restricted to the Quantum-Computation Classical-Communication (QCCC) model.

Main result. The classical Minicrypt landscape breaks down in the QCCC model, demonstrated by oracle separations showing that primitives like one-way functions and bit commitments do not have fully black-box counterparts.

Method. The authors construct specific oracle worlds and employ advanced quantum information techniques, including concentration theorems and the Hilbert-valued Efron–Stein inequality, to prove these separations.

Model / system. The core model is the QCCC setting, where quantum local computation is possible, but communication is restricted to classical messages. The analysis revolves around cryptographic primitives like One-Way Puzzles (EV-OWPuzz) and commitment schemes.

Key observables. The primary observables are the existence or non-existence of specific cryptographic primitives (e.g., OWFs, bit commitments) relative to the oracle structure.

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis depends on the complexity of the oracle access (unitary oracle model) and the parameters governing the security reduction, such as the error bounds $\epsilon$ and $\delta$.

Assumptions / limitations. The results are shown to hold in the unitary-oracle access model, and some constructions rely on the assumption that the puzzle distribution is $\epsilon$-close to uniform.

Figures summary. Figure 1 provides an intuitive visualization of known black-box reductions and oracle separations among QCCC primitives.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by first establishing the general problem in the QCCC model, then developing tools like the Hilbert-valued Efron–Stein inequality, and finally applying these tools to construct separations proving the breakdown of classical cryptographic equivalences.

Abstract

Classically, it is well-known that several fundamental cryptographic primitives, including one-way functions, pseudorandom number generators, commitments, and signatures, characterize the same cryptographic world, which is known as "Minicrypt". In this paper, we investigate to what extent this picture persists in the quantum-computation classical-communication (QCCC) setting, where parties may perform quantum local computation, but communicate through only classical messages. We demonstrate that the classical Minicrypt landscape breaks down in the QCCC model by oracle separations. We construct two oracle worlds where efficiently verifiable one-way puzzles (EV-OWPuzz) exist. By a known equivalence, QCCC one-time signatures also exist in both worlds. In the first, one-way functions do not exist, even if we allow quantum pseudodeterministic evaluation. In the second, QCCC bit commitments do not exist. Thus, the classical Minicrypt reductions from signatures to one-way functions and bit commitments have no fully black-box counterparts in the QCCC setting. Our proofs develop techniques for analyzing the random phase states, including a concentration theorem and an LOCC decoupling theorem, which may be of independent interest.

Summary. This paper proves the equivalence of two topological indices, the Ogata index and the twist index, for quantum spin chains with $\mathrm{U}(1) times\mathbb{Z}_2$ symmetry. Using advanced techniques from operator algebras and strong convergence estimates, the authors derive a computable formula for this index. This is significant because it provides a robust mathematical framework for characterizing topological order in strongly correlated quantum materials.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, mathematically rigorous tool to characterize topological order in 1D quantum magnets, extending index theory beyond simple symmetry constraints and offering concrete, computable methods for determining topological invariants.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to rigorously prove the mathematical equivalence between two distinct topological indices—Ogata's operator-algebraic index ($ ext{IndO}$) and the elementary twist index ($ ext{IndT}$)—for quantum spin chains.

Main result. The equivalence $ ext{IndO} = ext{IndT}$ is proven for $\mathrm{U}(1) times\mathbb{Z}_2$-symmetric integer-spin chains, providing a computable formula for the topological invariant.

Method. The proof combines techniques from operator algebras, utilizing a charge-fluctuation estimate to establish strong convergence of half-chain rotations, which is then linked to the Ogata index via an exact group commutator identity.

Model / system. The study focuses on quantum spin chains defined on the infinite lattice ($\mathbb{Z}$) with Hamiltonians possessing $\mathrm{U}(1) times\mathbb{Z}_2$ symmetry. The analysis is performed in the ground state ($\omega$) of such a system, assuming it is gapped and locally unique.

Key observables. Topological indices ($ ext{IndO}$, $ ext{IndT}$), local twist expectations ($\omega(T_N)$), and the gap lower bound ($\gamma$).

Important parameters / regimes. The symmetry group $\mathrm{U}(1) times\mathbb{Z}_2$, the integer spin value $S$, and the gap $\gamma > 0$.

Assumptions / limitations. The existence of a locally-unique gapped ground state ($\omega$) and the specific $\mathrm{U}(1) times\mathbb{Z}_2$ symmetry structure are crucial assumptions.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the context by defining both indices, then develops the proof by first using charge-fluctuation estimates to prove the strong convergence of half-chain rotations. Finally, an exact group commutator identity is used to equate the two definitions, leading to a computable formula and an application to the Heisenberg chain.

Abstract

Symmetry-protected topological (SPT) phases in quantum spin chains are distinguished by topological indices. Ogata's operator-algebraic indices apply to a broad range of symmetry classes without requiring continuous symmetry, whereas the elementary twist index of Tasaki requires $\mathrm{U}(1)$ symmetry. We prove their equivalence in the common setting of integer-spin chains with on-site $\mathrm{U}(1)\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_2$ symmetry and a symmetric locally-unique gapped ground state, without assuming translation invariance or a matrix product representation. A charge-fluctuation estimate yields a strong-limit construction of half-chain rotations with their phases fixed. An exact group commutator identity then identifies the Ogata index with the limit of local twist expectations and gives an explicit error bound. This provides a computable formula when a lower bound on the gap and the relevant ground-state expectations are available. As an application, a previous twist-index calculation determines the Ogata index to be $(-1)^S$ for the antiferromagnetic Heisenberg chain under explicit uniqueness and boundary-field gap assumptions. The author formulated the problem and proposed the basic strategy, while ChatGPT made substantial contributions to the development of the proof.

Abstract

In this paper, we prove that the Mean-Field Approximate Optimization Algorithm (MF-AOA), a quantum-inspired classical algorithm of the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA), is unable to give arbitrary near optimal solution for problems exhibiting the Overlap Gap Property (OGP). We show this by relating the MF-AOA to finite-memory Approximate Message Passing (AMP) algorithms, which are known to be limited in performance by the OGP. Thus, our paper argues that problems exhibiting the OGP are one such instance where the QAOA can outperform the MF-AOA and AMP algorithms in general. We supplement this by numerically simulating the performance of the QAOA on instances of Max-$4$-XORSAT that exhibit the OGP and find that the QAOA is able to surpass the barrier, while our finite-size scaling analysis favors a super-polynomial over a polynomial depth dependence. In addition, we observe a non-differentiable point in the performance of the QAOA near by the OGP barrier, where the optimal QAOA parameters change from an adiabatic to a non-adiabatic schedule.

The power of oracle access: Optimal sample and query complexity of the abelian state hidden subgroup problem

Summary. This work determines the optimal resource scaling for solving the abelian State Hidden Subgroup Problem (StateHSP). It proves that accessing the state-preparation unitary coherently (the query model) yields a quadratic speedup in complexity compared to using independent copies (the sample model). This demonstrates that the ability to coherently reverse the state preparation is key to achieving the best quantum algorithmic performance.

Why it may be interesting. The explicit comparison between sample and query complexity highlights the crucial role of coherent control over quantum resources, a fundamental concept in quantum algorithm design.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper characterizes the optimal sample and query complexity for solving the abelian State Hidden Subgroup Problem (StateHSP).

Main result. It establishes a quadratic separation in complexity: $O(\log(|G/H|)/\sqrt{\epsilon})$ queries are sufficient in the query model, compared to $O(\log(|G/H|)/\epsilon)$ copies in the sample model.

Method. The analysis compares two input access models (independent copies vs. unitary access) and uses techniques involving amplitude amplification and algebraic structure theorems (like Howell normal form) to derive both upper and lower bounds.

Model / system. The problem is defined on finite abelian groups $G$, where the goal is to find a hidden subgroup $H$ characterizing the symmetries of an unknown quantum state $|\varphi angle$.

Key observables. The success probability of identifying the hidden subgroup, and the required number of queries/samples ($\epsilon$ dependence).

Important parameters / regimes. The promise gap parameter $\epsilon$, and the size of the quotient group $|G/H|$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is restricted to abelian groups, and the query model assumes coherent access to the state-preparation unitary $U_\varphi$ and its inverse.

Figures summary. Not specified, but the notes mention Figure 6 illustrating the subspace identifier circuit for the binary case.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the StateHSP under different input access models (sample vs. query) to determine the optimal resource scaling, culminating in the demonstration that coherent access provides a quadratic speedup.

Abstract

In the quest to identify further quantum algorithms exhibiting superpolynomial speed-ups, a recurring theme is that the complexity of a problem is largely shaped by the input access model. Here, we study this phenomenon for the state hidden subgroup problem (StateHSP), a quantum generalization of the hidden subgroup problem in which the goal is to identify the symmetries of an unknown quantum state. For finite abelian groups, existing Fourier-sampling algorithms use $O(\log(|G|)/ε)$ copies of the state, but whether this scaling is optimal has remained open. We settle the complexity of the abelian StateHSP in both the previously studied sample model and a new query model, which is a stronger and operationally natural generalization that provides access to the state-preparation unitary and its inverse. In the query model, we give a time-efficient quantum algorithm using $O(\log(|G/H|)/\sqrtε)$ forward and inverse queries, and prove a matching $Ω(\log(|G/H|)/\sqrtε)$ lower bound which holds even in the stronger conjugate-query and controlled-query settings. By contrast, we show that in the sample model, $Θ(\log(|G/H|)/ε)$ copies are both sufficient and information-theoretically necessary, even if one allows for arbitrary collective measurements. Thus, the quadratic improvement in $ε$ genuinely arises from coherent access to the preparation circuit. As applications, we obtain faster algorithms for learning stabilizer groups, locating unentanglement, and identifying hidden translation symmetries.

Main result. A sufficient condition for the composition of PPT-squared Diagonal Orthogonal Covariant (DOC) channels to be EB is derived, involving an entrywise bound on the product of diagonal-sector matrices.

Method. The proof involves decomposing the composition into a toroidal core (related to coherence data) and a classical residual, leading to a concrete mathematical criterion.

Abstract

The positive-partial-transpose-squared (PPT-squared) conjecture asks whether the composition of two positive-partial-transpose (PPT) quantum channels must be entanglement breaking (EB). The problem remains open in general and, within the diagonal orthogonal covariant (DOC) class, the first unresolved deterministic dimension is four. A sufficient condition for PPT-squared composition is derived for DOC maps in arbitrary finite dimension. The proof isolates the quantum coherence data into two correlation matrices, mixes each with the identity until it lies in the convex hull of rank-one correlation matrices, and converts the resulting objects into an explicit triplewise completely positive (TCP) core. The remaining contribution is purely classical and is TCP whenever a simple entrywise mixing inequality is satisfied. For PPT DOC maps $Φ_{A,B,C}$ and $Φ_{D,E,F}$ on $M_d$, the entrywise bound $(AD)_{ij}\ge 2\lfloor\sqrt{d}\rfloor\sqrt{A_{ii}A_{jj}D_{ii}D_{jj}}$ for all $i,j$ guarantees that $Φ_{A,B,C}\circΦ_{D,E,F}$ is EB. In dimension four the universal coefficient becomes four. A continuous family of bistochastic PPT channels is then constructed; every member with $a\ne1$ is not EB, while every pairwise composition within the family is proved to be EB. A rank-sensitive refinement shows explicitly how low coherence rank can improve the universal coefficient. The resulting certificate is sufficient rather than necessary and is complementary to factor-width and semidefinite-hierarchy approaches.

Weighting Schedules Govern What and When Score-Based Generative Models Learn from Multimodal Data

Summary. This work provides a rigorous theoretical analysis of score-based generative models trained on multimodal data. It shows that the learning of different features—such as the relative weights between modes—is critically dependent on the training weighting schedule $w(t)$. Optimal learning of all features only occurs near a specific 'speciation time' where the signal-to-noise ratio is near unity.

Abstract

Score-based generative models generate new samples by integrating a time-dependent drift that carries Gaussian noise onto the target distribution. In practice this drift is modeled by a neural network, trained on a loss integrated over time $t$ with a weighting schedule $w(t)$. Along the backward dynamics, and for multi-modal distributions, trajectories commit to modes of the target within a narrow time window, the \textit{speciation time}. In this work, focusing on high-dimensional data, we decompose the integrated loss into its single-time contributions and analyze each at fixed signal-to-noise ratio $Λ(t)$: we show that $Λ(t)$ sets the rate at which each feature of a multimodal target - the mode directions and their relative weights - is acquired during training. Crucially, at high $Λ(t)$ all mode directions are acquired together, on a single timescale insensitive to their amplitudes, while the relative weights are not learned at all. Only near the speciation time, where $Λ(t)$ becomes of order one, do all features become learnable, each on its own timescale: the weights are acquired jointly with the directions, and the directions at rates set by their relative amplitudes. For models trained on time-integrated objectives, the learning dynamics is then governed by how much of the weighting effectively sits near the speciation time, which provides insights on $w(t)$ design choices. These results follow from an exact high-dimensional analysis of the training dynamics of unbalanced and hierarchical Gaussian mixtures. Numerical experiments on image and human genome haplotype generation recover the predicted hierarchy of learning timescales in more complex settings.

Abstract

Abstract

Even-layer topological antiferromagnetic thin films such as MnBi$_2$Te$_4$ exhibit an unconventional layer Hall effect (LHE) arising from a layer-locked Berry curvature. This effect manifests as a layer-polarized anomalous Hall effect that can be controlled by a vertical displacement field. However, achieving a quantized version of the LHE remains experimentally challenging. In this work, we propose that moiré engineering, using an electrostatic superlattice potential from control layers, can realize the quantum layer Hall effect (QLHE). Specifically, by placing moiré control layers in close proximity to the surfaces of a MnBi$_2$Te$_4$ thin film without breaking $PT$ symmetry, the resulting system exhibits a vanishing net Hall conductance but a quantized layer Hall conductance of $e^2/h$. A weak gate electric field then breaks $PT$ symmetry and drives the system into a layer-polarized quantum anomalous Hall (QAH) phase. Furthermore, when a single control layer is introduced on only one surface, the layer-polarized QAH emerges even in the absence of an external electric field. We identify the microscopic origin of the QLHE as an emergent layer-$U(1)$ gauge field generated by the scalar moiré potential, which in turn produces opposite periodic pseudomagnetic fields on the top and bottom layers. Our work provides a feasible pathway toward realizing the QLHE in antiferromagnetic thin films.

Abstract

To reliably extract such information, detailed knowledge about the atom-specific field evaporation processes is required.
