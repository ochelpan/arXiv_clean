Summary. This work introduces a scalable technique called 'quantum circuit knitting' to perform distributed quantum computations. By assuming weak coupling across circuit cuts, the classical resources required scale polynomially rather than exponentially. This allows for simulating large, complex quantum circuits by decomposing them into manageable, weakly interacting sub-problems.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on computation, the rigorous mathematical framework developed for bounding error propagation ($\epsilon_{ ext{total}}$) through weak coupling connections is highly relevant to open quantum systems where system-environment interactions are modeled perturbatively.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of performing Distributed Quantum Computing (DQC) by reducing the classical information cost associated with circuit knitting.

Main result. A method is presented that reduces the classical resource requirement from exponential to polynomial scaling by exploiting weak coupling approximations across circuit cuts.

Method. It uses a weak-coupling approximation, modeling the quantum process as a partition between two sub-circuits connected by a single weakly coupled qubit. This allows for the derivation of transformation matrices ($\Gamma$) relating measurement probabilities.

Model / system. The model involves general quantum circuits (e.g., those used in QAOA) that are conceptually cut along a central, weakly coupled qubit line. The analysis tracks probability evolution using symbolic string representations ($\alpha$-type and $\sigma$-type strings).

Key observables. Approximate full-circuit probability ($P_n^F$), total error ($\epsilon_{ ext{total}}$), and the condition number of the reconstruction matrix ($\kappa_\infty(\Gamma_n)$).

Important parameters / regimes. Coupling strength ($\lambda$) and approximation parameter ($\gamma$); the approximation level $n$ dictates the polynomial scaling of resources.

Assumptions / limitations. The core assumption is that the coupling between the two main parts of the process via the cut qubit is weak, allowing for Taylor-like expansions in $\lambda$.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate quantum circuits cut along a single qubit line showing input/output operations; another figure depicts a simulated 5-qubit register circuit with layers of gates.

Paper structure. The paper develops from the weak-coupling expansion leading to commutator relations, details the positional-symbolic correspondence (PSC) for maintaining consistency between string types, and culminates in error bounding formulas that analyze the total error as a combination of approximation, hardware noise, and statistical errors.

Abstract

We present a method for performing distributed quantum computing with controlled approximations. Exact distributed quantum computing requires exponential classical information to reconstruct the quantum process. However, we show how the classical cost is reduced to polynomial if the quantum procedure can be partitioned between a qubit that is weakly coupled the other qubits. We demonstrate our method for a layered circuit based on the circuits used for the quantum approximate optimization algorithm.

Summary. This paper introduces the first receiver-centric framework for covert communication over noisy bosonic channels. It exploits the fundamental difference in how an eavesdropper (Willie) and a legitimate receiver (Bob) detect signals—quadratic versus linear distinguishability—to derive optimal signaling strategies. The result provides explicit design laws linking channel parameters, security budgets, and required detection segment lengths for quantum optical systems.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete bridge between abstract information-theoretic security guarantees (covertness) and practical quantum optical engineering design rules (receiver optimization, signal processing).

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops a receiver-centric framework for covert quantum communication over thermal-loss bosonic channels, aiming to maximize detection probability while satisfying an adversary's covertness constraint.

Main result. It derives an explicit minimum detection-segment length law that balances the linear growth of Bob's detectability against Willie's quadratic detectability using a CUSUM detector.

Method. The authors use small-signal approximations to analyze distinguishability measures (KL divergence) and optimize energy allocation subject to covertness constraints, leading to concrete design rules for blockwise coding.

Model / system. The system is quantum communication over thermal-loss bosonic channels. Detection relies on a physically realizable Gaussian receiver followed by a single-pass Cumulative Sum (CUSUM) detector.

Key observables. Bob's post-change information growth (linear in signal energy, $D_B \sim E$), Willie's detectability via Quantum Relative Entropy (quadratic in signal energy, $D_W \sim E^2$), and the CUSUM crossing probability.

Important parameters / regimes. Thermal loss ($\eta$), background brightness ($ar{n}_B$), per-block covert budget ($\delta_{ ext{block}}$), and detection segment length ($L_{ ext{signal}}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies heavily on small-signal regime approximations for the distinguishability measures, and assumes a Gaussian receiver readout.

Figures summary. Figures validate the small-signal asymmetry (Willie quadratic vs. Bob linear) and confirm that the empirical same-block crossing probability scales correctly with a predicted characteristic length scale derived from system parameters.

Paper structure. The paper builds by first establishing the physical model, deriving the asymptotic distinguishability laws for both parties, optimizing energy allocation under the covertness constraint (uniform allocation), and finally deriving the explicit minimum detection segment length required for reliable same-block detection using CUSUM theory.

Abstract

We develop, to our knowledge, the first receiver-centric blockwise sequential-detection framework for covert communication over thermal-loss bosonic channels. In this architecture, each block serves as a binary super-symbol, and the key design problem is to determine the minimum detection-segment length that enables Bob to detect an active block before the block ends while remaining covert to Willie. For any fixed physically realizable general-dyne receiver, Bob's post-change information growth is linear in the small-signal regime, whereas Willie's detectability obeys a quadratic quantum relative entropy law. Exploiting this asymmetry, we show that under a per-block covertness budget the asymptotically optimal signaling strategy is uniform across the detection segment, and we derive an explicit minimum-length condition under which a single-pass cumulative sum (CUSUM) detector crosses threshold within the same block with exponentially high probability. The resulting design law yields a covert blockwise binary codebook over a finite transmission horizon and establishes a concrete link between bosonic covert communication, sequential detection, and blockwise signaling design. More broadly, these results provide design guidance for covert quantum communication systems with physically realizable receivers, and help bridge information-theoretic covertness guarantees with implementable receiver-aware optical communication design.

Abstract

Statistical simulations spanning compositions from Si to SiO$_2$ reproduce the full extent of this broadening, reaching 5 eV for SiO$_{1.0}$ , in quantitative agreement with 0.23 nm layer-resolved spectra reconstructed from Ar$^+$ sputtering data. This continuous distribution blurs distinct spectral fingerprints of local structural motifs, thereby challenging conventional chemical state assignment in oxide X-ray photoelectron spectra.

Summary. The research uses advanced Quantum Monte Carlo methods to study how tensile strain affects electron correlations in graphene. By comparing standard and Resonating Valence Bond (RVB) wave functions, the authors find that stretching the lattice enhances correlation effects up to a critical strain ($\delta_{cr}$). This demonstrates a tunable pathway to stabilize highly correlated electronic states in Dirac materials.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides direct, quantitative evidence that external mechanical tuning (strain) can be used as a simple knob to tune fundamental electronic interactions in Dirac materials, potentially stabilizing exotic correlated phases like those described by RVB theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To determine how tensile strain modifies electronic correlations in graphene, potentially driving a transition into a regime dominated by Resonating Valence Bond (RVB) physics.

Main result. Tensile expansion enhances RVB-like singlet correlations up to a critical strain ($\delta_{cr} \approx 15\% - 20\%$), where the energy gain of the RVB ansatz peaks, indicating a nonmonotonic evolution of correlation strength.

Method. Variational and Diffusion Quantum Monte Carlo (VMC/DMC) simulations are used to calculate ground state energies by comparing two wave function ansätze: a standard Jastrow-Slater determinant and an RVB-based Geminal Product ansatz.

Model / system. The system is graphene, modeled on its honeycomb lattice. The study investigates the effect of biaxial tensile strain ($\delta$) on the electronic correlation energy, treating electron interactions beyond simple mean-field approximations.

Key observables. Energy gain ($\Delta E = E_{ ext{RVB}} - E_{ ext{JSD}}$) as a function of strain $\delta$; band structure renormalization (pDOS); total ground state energy.

Important parameters / regimes. Tensile strain ($\delta$); Critical Strain ($\delta_{cr}$) in the $15\% < \delta_{cr} < 20\%$ range; System sizes ($N=16, 36, 64$).

Assumptions / limitations. The use of fixed-node approximation in DMC; The necessity of using an RVB ansatz (JAGP) to capture static correlation effects neglected by single-determinant forms.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the energy gain ($\Delta E$) vs. strain, showing nonmonotonic behavior with a peak/crossover point at $\delta_{cr}$. Tables present calculated total energies using both JSD and JAGP wave functions across various strains.

Paper structure. The paper systematically calculates ground state properties (energy) for graphene under increasing biaxial strain ($\delta$) by comparing the results from two increasingly sophisticated quantum many-body wave function ansätze (JSD vs. RVB/AGP), revealing a critical transition point.

Abstract

Electronic correlations in graphene are generally considered weak due to the large bandwidth of its $π$ electrons. Here we show that tensile expansion of the honeycomb lattice provides a direct route to enhancing correlation effects. Using variational and diffusion quantum Monte Carlo, we compare a conventional Jastrow-Slater determinant wave function with a resonating-valence-bond (RVB) Jastrow-antisymmetrized geminal product ansatz for a series of stretched graphene lattices. We find that the energy gain of the RVB state relative to the single-determinant description increases with bond expansion up to a critical strain $δ_{\mathrm{cr}}$, and decreases beyond it, revealing a nonmonotonic evolution of electronic correlations. The crossover is found to occur in the range $15\% < δ_{\mathrm{cr}} < 20\%$, in agreement with mechanical stability limits. This behavior indicates a transition from a weakly correlated Dirac semimetal to a regime with enhanced non-dynamic correlation and short-range singlet pairing. Our results provide direct many-body evidence that lattice expansion drives graphene into a regime where RVB-like correlations become energetically favorable, offering a simple route to tuning correlation effects in Dirac materials.

Summary. This paper rigorously proves that the replica overlap in the random field Edwards-Anderson spin glass model exhibits self-averaging in the infinite volume limit. Main result. The variance of the replica overlap is proven to vanish in the infinite-volume limit for almost all coupling constants, establishing self-averaging properties for this disordered system.

Method. The proof utilizes advanced techniques involving derivatives of the free energy density with respect to the random field strength and relies on correlation inequalities (like Tasaki's) to bound the variance terms.

Abstract

The self-averaging of the replica overlap is proven in the Edwards-Anderson (EA) model under random field almost everywhere in the coupling constant space in any dimension. The EA order parameter is represented in terms of the derivative of the free energy density with respect to the random field strength, regardless of boundary conditions. Tasaki's correlation inequality for finite-dimensional spin glass models shows that the expectation of the squared replica overlap is bounded by the squared EA order parameter. These simple evaluations enable us to prove that the variance of the replica overlap vanishes in the infinite-volume limit. The self-averaging of the replica bond overlap is proven also in the EA model with Gaussian exchange interaction without random field. Short-range spin glass models have been shown to behave differently from mean-field spin glass models with RSB phase.

Abstract

We construct exact, positive-energy, normalizable wave-packet solutions of the Dirac equation in the axisymmetric potential $V=-\,v_0/ρ$ -- to our knowledge, the first such solutions in any external potential. Abstract

We study the non-equilibrium dynamics of emergent dynamical axion quasiparticles (DAQ) coupled to a photon bath in equilibrium via a Chern-Simons term as a quantum open system. A quantum master equation (QME) is derived up to second order in this coupling implementing only a \emph{partial} Markov approximation, allowing time dependent rates in the Lindblad (QME). These are determined by the equilibrium correlation functions of the Chern-Simons density, and their time dependence allows us to explore transient dynamics in coherences and population: the formation of the quasiparticle on short time scales and its decay, and the build-up of population with an effective time dependent rate. Early time evolution features quantum \emph{anti} Zeno dynamics with enhanced quasiparticle decay and population growth. These phenomena describe transient violations of Fermi's Golden rule and of \emph{detailed balance}, and are distinct \emph{non-Markovian} effects directly related to the spectral density of the Chern-Simons correlators. We obtain the equation of motion of coherent (DAQ) condensates both with the (QME) and with quantum many body linear response establishing a direct bridge between both methods. As a corollary we obtain the expectation value of the Chern-Simons density \emph{induced} by a (DAQ) condensate in linear response, the topological susceptibility is shown to be proportional to the (DAQ) many body self-energy. We provide a Feynman diagram-based interpretation of approximations invoked in the (QME) and corrections from system-bath correlations in higher order.
