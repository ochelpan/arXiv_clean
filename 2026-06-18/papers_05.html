Summary. This paper theoretically models the control of magnetic domain walls in superconductor/ferromagnetic insulator bilayers using supercurrents. It shows that the superconducting state allows for low-power, measurable motion of these walls by generating an equilibrium superfluid momentum. This work is significant for developing energy-efficient memory elements for cryogenic quantum computing applications.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a detailed theoretical framework connecting superconducting phenomena (supercurrents, superfluid momentum) directly to spintronic memory elements (domain walls), offering insights into energy-efficient quantum/cryogenic device operation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The research aims to develop a fast, reliable magnetic memory technology for cryogenic computing by controlling magnetic domain walls using superconducting supercurrents.

Main result. Supercurrent-driven motion of domain walls is predicted, generating a measurable voltage and dissipating power orders of magnitude lower than in normal state setups. The mechanism relies on the generation of an equilibrium superfluid momentum ($q_0$).

Method. The analysis employs advanced theoretical techniques including quasiclassical Green's functions (Keldysh formalism), coupled action formulations for superconducting and magnetic systems, and solving coupled differential equations for domain wall dynamics.

Model / system. The system is a superconductor/ferromagnetic insulator bilayer. The physics involves coupling between supercurrent-induced spin accumulation (via ISGE) and the magnetization dynamics described by modified LLG equations.

Key observables. Domain wall velocity ($v_{dw}$), measurable voltage ($V \propto q_0 v_{dw}$), dissipated power ($P$), and temperature increase ($\Delta T$).

Important parameters / regimes. Supercurrent density ($j$), superconducting gap ($\Delta$), spin-orbit coupling strength, Gilbert damping parameter ($\alpha$), and the equilibrium superfluid momentum ($q_0$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis often assumes an adiabatic limit for motion (low speed) and that the FI is much thinner than the SC to ensure spin-charge coupling dominates over Oersted fields.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the physical setup, domain wall dynamics under varying currents relative to a critical current ($I_W$), snapshots of superfluid momentum during motion, and theoretical derivations relating voltage/power to velocity.

Paper structure. The paper builds from establishing the fundamental coupling mechanisms (ISGE, $q_0$ generation) to deriving the coupled equations of motion for domain wall coordinates. It then analyzes the resulting dynamics, power balance ($P=D$), and finally addresses thermal stability by calculating temperature increases due to dissipation.

Abstract

Establishing a versatile, fast and reliable magnetic memory technology is a giant bottleneck for cryogenic computing since present-day room-temperature solutions either cease to work or consume too much power. The long-term goal of superconducting spintronics has been to overcome this bottleneck by generating magnetic memories with equal-spin triplet supercurrent driven through them to control their magnetization direction. This path has been hampered by the short spin relaxation length and strong anisotropy in ferromagnets. Here we show how the supercurrent driven generation of spin accumulation in a superconductor/magnetic insulator bilayer, together with Gilbert damping of magnetization lead to a motion of magnetic domain walls. This manifests as a local voltage across the wall, which allows its position to be identified. Associated with this voltage and the current, there is Joule power which is dissipated via the Gilbert damping. The power required to maintain domain wall motion is orders of magnitude smaller than in the normal state, where most of the power is wasted in producing the current.

Terahertz radiation from $\text{Bi}_2\text{Sr}_2\text{CaCu}_2\text{O}_{8+δ}$ intrinsic Josephson junctions (IJJs) provides an ideal platform to study the synchronization of a macroscopic quantum system. Here, we present a spectral analysis of a frequency-modulated Josephson plasma emitter coupled with patch antennas. Crucially, double-peak spectra obtained at a constant bias voltage imply either a rapid temporal distribution of resonances or their simultaneous excitation, driven by the mutual electromagnetic coupling between the IJJ mesa and the antennas. Summary. This work demonstrates that the statistical nature of charge carriers fundamentally limits the maximum power achievable by quantum heat engines. By modeling magnon transport and incorporating Haldane's fractional exclusion statistics, the authors show that bosonic behavior significantly enhances this limit over fermionic predictions, establishing statistical exclusion as a tunable thermodynamic resource.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To investigate quantum statistical exclusion as a thermodynamic resource by comparing the maximum power extractable from heat engines operating with different carrier statistics (fermionic vs. bosonic).

Main result. Bosonic working media yield strictly enhanced universal maximum power compared to fermionic limits, and Haldane's fractional statistics parameterizes this enhancement continuously.

Method. The analysis uses the nonlinear Landauer-Büttiker framework applied to a quantum thermoelectric heat engine model.

Model / system. The system is modeled using magnon transport through a ferromagnetic spin chain, analyzed via an effective quadratic bosonic tight-binding Hamiltonian in the low-temperature limit.

Key observables. Maximum power ($P_{ ext{max}}$), efficiency ($\eta$), and the ratio of maximum power output vs. fermionic bound.

Important parameters / regimes. Haldane exclusion statistics parameter ($g$), temperature difference ($T_L - T_R$), and magnetic field strengths defining chemical potentials.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the nonlinear Landauer-Büttiker framework, assuming low temperatures for noninteracting magnon approximation.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the schematic of the spin chain setup, transmission profiles showing filtering thresholds, and plots comparing power output ratios versus the statistics parameter g.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the theoretical bounds using Landauer-Büttiker formalism, compares fermionic vs. bosonic limits, introduces Haldane interpolation, and applies this framework to a magnon transport model to quantify the enhancement.

Abstract

The maximum power extractable from a quantum thermoelectric heat engine operating with free fermion carriers is bounded by the universal Whitney limit, $P_{\text{fermion}}^{\max} \simeq 0.0321π^2 k_B^2(T_L-T_R)^2/h$. We demonstrate that this bound is not fundamental to quantum heat engines but is instead an artifact of fermionic statistics. Within the nonlinear Landauer-Büttiker framework, a bosonic working medium yields a strictly enhanced universal maximum power, $P_{\text{boson}}^{\max} = (\ln 2)^2\, k_B^2(T_L-T_R)^2/h$, exceeding the fermionic limit by a factor of $(\ln 2)^2/(0.0321π^2) \approx 1.52$. We propose magnon transport through a ferromagnetic spin chain as an experimentally viable bosonic realization. Incorporating Haldane fractional exclusion statistics with parameter $g$ provides a continuous interpolation between the bosonic ($g = 0$) and fermionic ($g = 1$) limits, revealing a monotonic enhancement of maximum power for $g < 1$ at reduced bias cost. These results establish quantum statistical exclusion as a previously unrecognized and independently tunable thermodynamic resource, opening performance regimes inaccessible to conventional carrier-engineering approaches.

Abstract

Antiferromagnetic skyrmions are swirling topological spin textures with rich dynamics and intriguing transport properties, yet their bidirectional dynamics remain largely unexplored. Here, we investigate the dynamics of antiferromagnetic skyrmions driven by current-induced spin-transfer and spin-orbit torques. Our results not only uncover a hidden mechanism for bidirectional skyrmion motion but also facilitate the development of antiferromagnet-based logic devices.

Abstract

In a magnetic field bilayer graphene hosts an octet of pseudo-zero-mode electron levels nearly degenerate in orbitals $n=(0,1)$, valleys and spins. They split in valleys by electrostatic gating. In gated bilayer graphene, in which the interlayer bias is set up to flip sign across a line, one has a line junction that traps a portion of pseudo-zero-mode electrons inside the insulating bulk band gap, giving rise to electron states localized along the junction, known as kink states. A close look is made into the spectra and electromagnetic response of such kink states. There are two species of valley current associated with them, a drift current driven by the bias gradient and a circulating current coming from cyclotron motion. It turns out that they both flow in essentially the same direction, with the circulating current exhibiting a magnetic character distinct from those of other higher levels. In equilibrium they spatially circulate within the kink states, creating a quasi-one-dimensional channel of orbital magnetization. The electric control of the orbital magnetization and valley currents via a network of gated junctions will find useful applications in valley electronics.
