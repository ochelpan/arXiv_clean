Summary. This paper extends the teleportation-based Quantum State Tomography protocol from qubits to qutrits (spin-1 systems). It demonstrates that by performing multiple quantum teleportation experiments using nine distinct input states, one can fully reconstruct the density matrix of a two-qutrit system. The method is shown to scale systematically to $n$-qutrits, providing a powerful theoretical tool for characterizing high-dimensional quantum states.

Why it may be interesting. This work is highly relevant as it develops a concrete, scalable protocol for characterizing multi-partite quantum states in higher-dimensional systems (qutrits), which are crucial testbeds for understanding entanglement beyond simple qubit models.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is extending the quantum state tomography protocol, originally designed for qubits (spin-1/2), to reconstruct arbitrary states of qutrits (spin-1 systems). This extends to higher dimensions ($n$-qutrits) as well.

Main result. The protocol successfully determines the density matrix $\rho_{12}$ (for two qutrits) by performing multiple quantum teleportation experiments using nine distinct known input states. The method scales to $n$-qutrits, requiring $9^{n-1}$ such experiments.

Method. The reconstruction relies on measuring the resulting single-qutrit state ($ ho_2$) at Bob's end after Alice performs a Bell measurement on her two qubits (the unknown system and an auxiliary qubit). A consistent set of linear equations is solved using these measurements to determine the density matrix elements.

Model / system. The physical system involves qutrits, which are spin-1 systems ($ ext{dim}=3$). The process models quantum state tomography on a two-qutrit system $\rho_{12}$ by leveraging the principles of quantum teleportation.

Key observables. The matrix elements ($m_{ij}$) of the unknown density matrix $\rho_{12}$, and the probabilities ($Q_j$) associated with various Bell measurement outcomes.

Important parameters / regimes. The number of required input states scales exponentially, $9^{n-1}$, for an $n$-qutrit system. The reconstruction requires at least nine distinct teleportation measurements for two qutrits.

Assumptions / limitations. It is assumed that Bob can experimentally reconstruct his single-qutrit density matrix ($ ho_2$) using standard QST methods, and the protocol assumes specific measurement outcomes (e.g., measuring $|B_1\rangle$ initially).

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically illustrates the general teleportation-based QST protocol for two qutrits. Figure 3 extends this concept to three qutrits, showing Alice preparing and projecting pairs of states onto Bell bases.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the extension from qubits to qutrits, detailing the required measurements (nine input states). It then provides explicit formulas relating the unknown density matrix elements ($\rho_{12}$) to Bob's measured single-qutrit states. Finally, it discusses the general scaling of this method to $n$-qutrits.

Abstract

We show that the teleportation-based quantum state tomography (QST) protocol, originally built to reconstruct qubits (spin-1/2 systems), can be extended to deal with qutrits (spin-1 systems) as well. Similarly to the original proposal, only two resources are needed to implement the spin-1 teleportation-based QST protocol: (1) Alice should be able to implement the analog of Bell measurements for spin-1 systems; and (2) she should be able to prepare a few different single qutrit states that will be teleported to Bob.

Abstract

Highly collimated laser beam with excellent spatial quality is essential for quantum sensing experiments, where even small residual beam divergence can accumulate over distance and introduce significant systematic errors. In this article, we present the design and detailed characterization of a high precision laser beam collimator developed for a cold-atom Gravimeter system, capable of producing an expanded laser beam with a diameter of 16 mm while achieving microradian level collimation accuracy through a five-degree-of-freedom (5-DOF) adjustment mechanism. Abstract

In agreement with the tight-binding calculations, we found a dispersive intensity around the X point in the $μ$-ARPES spectra, where the A-type antiferromagnetic (A-AFM) phase has no bands experimentally and theoretically, demonstrating that we have successfully captured the signatures of the CE-AFM band structure for the first time. This indicates that the CE-AFM domain size would be no larger than the beam footprint size of a $\sim\!20$~{\textmu}m scale.

Entanglement as a Structural Complexity Axis: A PAC-Bayesian View of Generalization in Quantum Policies and Value Functions

Summary. The paper introduces the Fisher effective dimension as the true measure of complexity governing generalization in quantum circuits used for machine learning tasks. By showing that entanglement inflates this dimension independently of parameter count, it establishes a clear entanglement-generalization trade-off. This reframes quantum policy design away from mere expressivity towards structural complexity control.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a theoretical framework connecting geometric properties (Fisher information/entanglement) directly to generalization bounds in quantum machine learning models. This is crucial for designing efficient and provably generalizable quantum algorithms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core problem is determining a rigorous measure of generalization complexity for quantum policies ($\pi_ heta$) and value functions used in Quantum Reinforcement Learning (QRL). The authors aim to move beyond simple parameter counting to understand the role of entanglement.

Main result. Generalization capability is governed by the effective dimension of the Fisher geometry, which quantifies structural complexity. Entanglement acts as an independent axis of complexity, causing circuits with high entanglement but fixed parameters to generalize worse than non-entangled counterparts.

Method. The authors derive and apply a PAC-Bayesian generalization bound based on the Fisher Information Matrix ($\hat{F}$). They analyze how varying entanglement connectivity inflates the effective dimension ($ ext{deff} = \log \det(I + \gamma \hat{F})$) while keeping parameter count constant.

Model / system. The model utilizes Parameterized Quantum Circuits (PQCs) trained for tasks like supervised classification and value estimation. The circuits are systematically varied by controlling the entangling connectivity pattern (e.g., none, linear chain, full coupling) across a fixed number of qubits and parameters.

Key observables. Generalization gap (test vs. train performance), Fisher effective dimension ($ ext{deff}$), entanglement connectivity, and correlation coefficients ($ ho$) between $ ext{deff}$ and the gap.

Important parameters / regimes. The generalization constant $\gamma$ used in the bound; fixed number of trainable rotations/parameters ($d$); sample size ($N$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is primarily focused on controlled decision models (single-observable readout) to isolate the entanglement effect. The results are claimed to be a 'ranking certificate' rather than a numerically tight bound.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate that increasing entangling connectivity monotonically increases the rank of the Fisher matrix and thus $ ext{deff}$, which correlates strongly with an increased generalization gap, even when parameter count is held fixed.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the need for a better complexity measure than raw parameters. It derives the PAC-Bayesian bound using the Fisher geometry, proves that entanglement inflates this effective dimension by enlarging the light-cone of the readout observable, and validates this mechanism across multiple quantum learning tasks.

Abstract

Parameterized quantum circuits (PQCs) are increasingly used as policies and value functions in quantum reinforcement learning, yet it remains unclear when and why quantum policies generalize. We give a PAC-Bayesian account in which generalization is governed not by the raw number of circuit parameters, but by the effective dimension of the Fisher geometry induced by the circuit. This quantity is inflated by entanglement, making entangling connectivity an independent axis of complexity.In controlled experiments that fix the number of trainable rotations and vary only entanglement, we find that circuits with larger Fisher effective dimension exhibit larger train-test gaps, while parameter count is a weak predictor. The resulting bound acts primarily as a ranking certificate: it correctly orders circuits with identical parameter count, which parameter-counting bounds cannot do. We validate this mechanism across supervised classification, quantum contextual bandits, and value-function generalization, where entangled circuits consistently generalize worse than non-entangled circuits of equal parameter count, with gaps shrinking as sample size increases.Our strongest evidence comes from low-variance decision models, including single-observable classifiers, value heads, and one-step policies. In end-to-end multi-step policy learning, entanglement effects remain statistically significant but high return variance leaves the full ordering only partially resolved. Partial-correlation analysis shows that Fisher effective dimension screens off entangling pattern, and controls for training accuracy, readout, and optimizer rule out major optimization confounders. The effect also persists on an IBM Heron quantum processor under real noise. Overall, our results reframe quantum policy design around an entanglement--generalization trade-off rather than expressivity alone.

Summary. This paper uses DMRG simulations on the critical $J_1$-$J_2$ Ising chain to characterize static vacancies as tunable conformal defects. By analyzing the algebraic decay of vacancy interactions, the transmission ratio, and boundary entropy, the authors demonstrate that these three observables vary smoothly with the coupling ratio $J_2/J_1$. Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how static nonmagnetic vacancies act as parametrized conformal defects within the critical $J_1$-$J_2$ transverse-field Ising chain.

Main result. The vacancy defect forms a continuous, one-parameter family of partially transmissive conformal defects controlled by the ratio $J_2/J_1$, evidenced by the smooth variation of three key observables ($\alpha_\infty$, $T_\infty$, $\log g_\infty$).

Method. Density-Matrix Renormalization Group (DMRG) calculations were performed on open chains, analyzing power-law decay exponents and boundary entropy.

Model / system. The system is the transverse-field Ising chain with first ($J_1$) and second ($J_2$) neighbor couplings, $H = -\sum_{i} J_1 \sigma^x_i \sigma^x_{i+1} - \sum_{i} J_2 \sigma^x_i \sigma^x_{i+2} - \sum_{i} b \sigma^z_i$, with vacancies acting as defects.

Key observables. Vacancy-vacancy binding energy decay exponent ($\alpha$), spin correlator transmission ratio ($T_\infty$), and Affleck-Ludwig boundary entropy ($\log g_\infty$).

Important parameters / regimes. The tuning parameter is the ratio $J_2/J_1$, which controls the defect's properties across the quantum critical line.

Paper structure. Abstract

We revisit the problem of two static nonmagnetic vacancies in the transverse-field Ising chain with first- and second-neighbor couplings $J_1$ and $J_2$, now on the critical line, using density-matrix renormalization-group (DMRG) calculations in open chains of up to $N=300$ sites. In contrast to the gapped regime studied previously, where the vacancy-vacancy interaction decays exponentially, along the entire quantum critical line the interaction becomes algebraic, $|Δ_b(r)|\sim r^{-α}$, with $α$ close to the universal Casimir value of unity and a weak but systematic dependence on the second-neighbor coupling, $α_\infty \simeq 1.070 + 0.091\, J_2/J_1)$ across $J_2/J_1\in[0.1,1.0]$. The transmission ratio of the spin correlator across a vacancy approaches a $J_2$-dependent plateau $T_\infty(J_2)$ that grows from $0.11$ to $0.33$ over the same range, and the Affleck-Ludwig boundary entropy is small and approximately constant, $\log g_\infty \approx -0.073$, well above the Ising fixed-BC value $-\ln\sqrt{2}$ and close to the free-boundary value. The three observables vary smoothly and monotonically with $J_2$, consistent with a one-parameter family of partially transmissive conformal defects controlled by $J_2$. Throughout, the critical line is located using the bulk spin-correlator exponent $η=1/4$, the order-parameter exponent of the Ising universality class, which provides a robust criterion in this open geometry.

Summary. This work establishes that the statistical concept of community detection, quantified by the Ramsey number, has a deep physical underpinning. Main problem. The paper determines when a network's community structure becomes an 'objective fact,' quantified by the Ramsey community number ($r_\kappa$), and establishes its thermodynamic stability.

Main result. The Ramsey community number is shown to be an exact renormalization-group crossing, and the optimal partition forms a persistent, thermodynamically ordered hierarchy of communities.

Method. The analysis combines Bayesian model comparison (evidence ratio) with Renormalization Group (RG) techniques applied to lattice models and spin Hamiltonians.

Model / system. The study uses the diamond hierarchical lattice structure and analyzes community ordering via a generalized Potts Hamiltonian, which is related to modularity maximization.

Abstract

The Ramsey community number $r_k$ is the smallest size at which a network is better described by communities than by none, under a Bayesian detection rule. On the diamond hierarchical lattice we show that $r_k$ is an exact renormalization-group crossing: the block-model sufficient statistics obey a linear map with eigenvalues $\{bs,b\}$, the degree-corrected evidence density flows to $\ln K$ at a community fixed point, and $r_k$ is the generation at which the running evidence clears the detection threshold. Degree correction advances detection by two generations. We derive $r_k(b,s;q)$ in closed form for the whole family. Finally, placing on the lattice the Reichardt--Bornholdt community Hamiltonian -- whose ground state is the partition itself -- we find an exact community-ordered phase: below the ferromagnetic critical temperature the two hubs lock into opposite communities for any resolution $γ>0$, a staggered order that persists as $n\to\infty$. Allowing each nested sub-community its own label, the optimal partition is a hierarchy of $q_{\rm opt}\sim\sqrt{n}$ communities, so the number of Potts states that best describes the network grows with the network. This hierarchy orders thermally level by level, through a cascade of first-order transitions whose temperatures fall as $1/\ln q$, so every stable level persists as $n\to\infty$: the emergent partition is detectable, optimal, and thermodynamically ordered.

Summary. The paper solves the challenge of simulating mixed-state geometric phases (Uhlmann phase) using only standard, Hermitian Hamiltonian evolution. By embedding the system into an enlarged four-level model, they prove that this complex phase can be exactly reproduced by the Wilczek-Zee connection in 1D parameter spaces. Main problem. The primary goal is to find a purely Hermitian, Hamiltonian-based method to simulate the mixed-state geometric phase (Uhlmann phase), which is otherwise complicated by non-Hermitian dynamics.

Method. The authors construct an enlarged, Hermitian parent Hamiltonian whose degenerate ground state subspace is used to calculate the WZ connection and compare it directly to the definition of the Uhlmann connection.

Model / system. The system involves qubit density matrices undergoing adiabatic cyclic evolution along a 1D loop. This dynamics is embedded into an auxiliary four-level Hermitian Hamiltonian $H(t) = \Delta (I - P(t))$.

Abstract

The Uhlmann phase extends geometric phases to mixed quantum states via a parallel-transport condition on purification amplitudes, yet its direct implementation under standard Hamiltonian dynamics is obstructed by the non-Hermitian nature of the purification. We establish that for any smooth one-dimensional closed loop of full-rank qubit density matrices, there exists a four-level Hermitian parent Hamiltonian whose doubly degenerate ground-state subspace carries a Wilczek--Zee connection exactly equal to the Uhlmann connection. Consequently, the Uhlmann holonomy is faithfully reproduced by adiabatic evolution in the enlarged system. We further prove that this auxiliary-field construction is obstructed in generic two-dimensional parameter spaces by a Frobenius integrability condition, which we derive explicitly. The one-dimensional Uhlmann phase is thus placed on the same footing as the non-Abelian Berry phase, offering a purely Hermitian, Hamiltonian-based route to simulating mixed-state geometric phases. Numerical integration of the adiabatic dynamics confirms the exact correspondence and validates the convergence to the Uhlmann holonomy in the large-gap limit.

Abstract

The temporal Clauser-Horne-Shimony-Holt (CHSH) inequality witnesses the nonclassicality of temporal correlations, but its violation is generally degraded by environmental noise. Here, we show that violation of the temporal CHSH inequality can be revived through coherent control of noisy quantum evolutions. We compare two physically distinct implementations: coherent control of noisy evolutions induced by interaction of the system with independent environments, and coherent control of two physically distinct, unitarily equivalent Stinespring dilations of the same noisy channel. Although these constructions generate identical deterministic system dynamics, they induce distinctly different post-selected evolutions. With a focus on the amplitude damping channel (ADC), we show that coherent control of equivalent dilations extend the range of temporal CHSH inequality violation well beyond both the incoherently controlled, or deterministic, scenario and what is achievable with independent environments. Under setting-independent post-selection of the coherent control implementation, the resulting violation further certifies that the channel is not strongly CHSH nonlocality-breaking.
