Summary. This work analyzes how two coupled particles (a dimer) transition across a barrier under external periodic driving and thermal noise. By introducing the Conditional Residence Time (CRT), the authors show that the time delay between the two monomers crossing the barrier is highly sensitive to the drive frequency. This establishes CRT as a key measure for understanding sequential, rate-limiting steps in coupled stochastic systems.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical stochastic dynamics, the concept of analyzing sequential transitions (initiation vs. completion) using a specialized time delay metric like CRT is highly relevant to understanding decoherence or multi-step quantum tunneling processes in open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the completion dynamics of an overdamped dimer moving in a bistable potential under thermal fluctuations and a weak periodic force.

Main result. The mean Conditional Residence Time (CRT) exhibits a non-monotonic dependence on the external drive frequency, demonstrating that transition initiation and completion are distinct sequential processes.

Method. The authors use the Conditional Residence Time (CRT) as a diagnostic tool to quantify the delay between successive barrier crossings of two coupled monomers.

Model / system. The system is an overdamped dimer modeled by coupled Langevin equations in a one-dimensional double well potential, incorporating harmonic and Lennard-Jones interactions, thermal noise, and a weak sinusoidal external force.

Key observables. Conditional Residence Time (CRT), Mean First Passage Times (MFPTs), Escape rates, Probability distributions of transition windows.

Important parameters / regimes. Coupling strength ($k$), external drive frequency ($\Omega$), Noise strength ($D$), and the competition between escape times and the driving period.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the process can be partitioned into distinct time windows (immediate, first cycle, later cycles) to analyze sequential dynamics.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate CRT distributions for varying coupling strengths and drive frequencies, showing how the mean CRT changes non-monotonically with $\Omega$, especially in the weak coupling regime.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the physical model and Langevin equations, defines the CRT diagnostic tool, analyzes the dynamics by partitioning the process into three time windows, and concludes by establishing CRT's utility for sequential barrier crossing analysis.

Abstract

We investigate the completion dynamics of an overdamped dimer moving in a bistable potential under thermal fluctuations and a weak periodic force. Both monomers start in one of the two wells separated by a barrier. The transition is initiated when the monomer closer to the barrier makes a jump across it. The completion dynamics refers to the next part of the dynamics where the second monomer has to wait for some time before it can follow up. We use the Conditional Residence Time (CRT) to study the delay between the successive barrier crossing of the two monomers. The CRT distributions highlight qualitatively different regimes formed by the competition between the escape times of the lagging monomer and the time period of the external drive. The effect is strongest in the weak coupling regime where the delayed completion is spread across multiple forcing cycles. By partitioning this process into three windows, i.e. the immediate, first cycle and later cycles, we show that the probability that the lagging monomer will make a transition in the said cycle is redistributed among these pathways as we change the frequency of the drive. This leads to a non-monotonic dependence of the mean CRT on the frequency of the drive. Our results demonstrate that transition initiation and completion in a coupled system are two separate processes and establish CRT as a useful measure to quantify the sequential barrier crossing dynamics in coupled stochastic systems.

Summary. This paper introduces doubly chiral active particles (dcABPs) that exhibit a unique form of topologically protected transport along boundaries. By rigorously contrasting this behavior with simpler models, the authors establish a new physical mechanism for directed motion in active matter. The work bridges theoretical derivation, simulation, and experimental realization using vibrobots.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical active matter, the concept of 'topological protection' in non-equilibrium systems is highly relevant to understanding robust edge states in quantum materials or engineered quantum circuits where dissipation and noise are present.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To determine the conditions under which robust, topologically protected boundary-induced transport can occur in active particles, contrasting this with edge currents observed in simpler chiral models.

Main result. Doubly chiral active Brownian Particles (dcABPs) exhibit robustly protected directed motion along boundaries, unlike simple cABPs or chiral rods which either lack protection or backscatter at corners.

Method. The work combines theoretical analysis of steady-state probability currents with detailed numerical simulations and the design/testing of a physical vibrobot realization.

Model / system. The primary model is the doubly chiral active Brownian particle (dcABP), characterized by two sources of chirality: an intrinsic angular velocity and a translation-rotation coupling arising from asymmetric friction. The analysis extends to simple cABPs and chiral rods in confined geometries (channels, arenas).

Key observables. Edge currents ($J_y(x)$ or $J_\phi(r)$), integrated net transport ($\Phi$), and the persistence of directed motion around corners.

Important parameters / regimes. The relative strengths of intrinsic rotation versus translation-rotation coupling, and the presence/absence of boundary curvature are critical parameters determining topological protection.

Assumptions / limitations. Initial analyses often assume overdamped dynamics for cABPs; later work rigorously derives transport using stochastic averaging techniques applicable to various noise regimes.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate schematics comparing current distributions and cumulative displacement for different particle types (cABP vs. dcABP) in confined geometries, demonstrating the failure of simple models to achieve true boundary-induced transport.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes that simple cABPs do not exhibit true boundary-induced transport ($\Phi$ arises from bulk orbits). It then contrasts this with chiral rods showing backscattering. Finally, it introduces and proves the topological protection mechanism for dcABPs using theory (Appendix A) and simulation/experimentation.

Abstract

Using theory, simulation, and experiment, we introduce a new class of active particle which we term doubly chiral active Brownian particles (dcABPs), which show robust topologically protected transport along boundaries without backscattering at corners. Their double chirality stems from the coexistence of an intrinsic angular velocity, which can cause rotation independently of translation, and a translation-rotation coupling inducing cross-alignment to the instantaneous velocity, which causes rotation only concomitantly with translation. A mechanically detailed model shows that the latter effect can arise from an asymmetric friction distribution in the direction perpendicular to the self-propulsion direction. We show that topologically protected modes emerge when the two sources of chirality have opposite sign and the intrinsic rotation is weaker than the translation-rotation coupling. In the deterministic limit, we characterize the emergence of these modes not only along straight boundaries, but also along curved boundaries and during interparticle interactions. We provide a proof-of-principle experimental realization by building a doubly chiral vibrobot. While setting the work into context, we moreover show that the topologically protected boundary-induced transport of dcABPs stands in contrast to the edge currents observed for simple chiral ABPs, which we demonstrate are not associated with boundary-induced transport, as well as to those observed for chiral active rods or self-aligning chiral ABPs, which we show to be associated with boundary-induced transport but to backscatter at corners, implying lack of topological protection.

Summary. This perspective paper rigorously re-derives the theory of activated rate processes, showing how continuous Brownian motion maps onto discrete chemical kinetics. It resolves ambiguities in defining reaction rates by introducing a concept of unidirectional flux based on trajectory history rather than simple spatial partitioning. This provides a unified and robust theoretical tool for studying barrier crossing phenomena.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides advanced theoretical tools for calculating rates in complex systems where dynamics are governed by free energy landscapes, which is foundational knowledge for understanding relaxation processes in many quantum or mesoscopic physical systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to develop a rigorous theoretical framework for mapping continuous, diffusive dynamics (like Brownian motion) onto discrete-state chemical kinetics describing activated barrier crossing.

Main result. It establishes that the rate constant can be calculated robustly by considering unidirectional fluxes between sub-ensembles defined by trajectory history, yielding results consistent with established theories like Kramers' theory and correlation function methods.

Method. The authors use concepts from non-equilibrium statistical mechanics, analyzing fluxes in phase space and comparing different mathematical formulations (e.g., conditional probabilities vs. relaxation modes) to ensure physical consistency.

Model / system. The model is a particle undergoing Brownian motion between two potential wells ('reactant' and 'product') separated by an activation barrier $U(x)$. The dynamics are governed by the Langevin or Smoluchowski equation.

Key observables. Reaction rate coefficients ($k_{A o B}$), equilibrium populations, and the unidirectional reactive flux ($J_{A o B}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The barrier height relative to thermal energy ($U(0) - U(a) \gg k_BT$) is a crucial parameter for simplifying assumptions.

Assumptions / limitations. Naive state separation based only on position fails; the analysis requires considering the full trajectory history and assuming high barriers allow flux independence from boundary placement.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates two methods of mapping continuous dynamics to discrete states (position cut vs. trajectory history). Figure 2 conceptually shows the cycle of ensemble members leading to a net unidirectional flux.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by reviewing Kramers' theory, identifying the mathematical shortcomings of simple state separation, and then rigorously constructing an internally consistent mapping using fluxes derived from both conditional probabilities and time-evolution operator analysis.

Abstract

More than 80 years ago Kramers published a paper calculating how fast a Brownian particle escapes from a potential well over an activation barrier. Since then Kramers' model has been widely adopted by nuclear physics, biophysics and chemical physics communities as a description of activated barrier crossing. From a chemical kinetics perspective, Kramers' theory provides a mapping from continuous dynamics to discrete-state chemical kinetics. Motivated by recent developments, this Perspective provides a rigorous way of performing such a mapping, explaining why and how Kramers' theory works from several points of view. Specifically, we consider transitions of a Brownian particle between two potential wells corresponding to the reactant'' and theproduct'' of a chemical reaction. A central unifying idea is to divide the equilibrium ensemble of possible states of the system into two sub-ensembles corresponding to the reactant and product states and then to consider fluxes between these sub-ensembles. Importantly, naive separation based on the location measured relative to the barrier top does not result in a mapping that is physically tenable, and instead the past of the trajectory should be considered. Thus constructed reactant and product ensembles provide an internally consistent description of the problem when also viewed from two different perspectives: one based on the definition of the rate as a conditional transition probability per unit time and the other based on the relaxation modes of the time-evolution operator governing the dynamics.

Abstract

Level statistics in non-integrable quantum many-body systems with time reversal symmetry are expected to follow the Gaussian Orthogonal Ensemble (GOE), a hallmark of quantum chaos. The resulting restructuring of the spectrum weakens level repulsion without restoring integrability. We show the robustness of this mechanism in extensions of the model which break chiral and inversion symmetry.

Abstract

We study the quantum decoherence of a bound state interacting with a reservoir of strongly interacting matter within the framework of open quantum systems. The bound state is modeled as a quantum harmonic oscillator whose parameters are tuned to reproduce the root-mean-square radius of $J/Ψ$ particle. The surrounding medium, representing the many degrees of freedom of strongly interacting matter, acts as an environment that induces dissipation and decoherence through system-reservoir coupling. By analyzing the time evolution of the reduced density matrix, we quantify the loss of quantum coherence and its dependence on medium properties. Abstract

Differential privacy provides a rigorous framework for quantifying privacy leakage in data analysis, while its quantum extensions have become increasingly relevant with the development of quantum computing and quantum machine learning. In this work, we introduce and study quantum probabilistic local differential privacy, a relaxation of quantum local differential privacy in which the privacy constraint is allowed to fail on a spectral violation event with low probability. These results provide a systematic foundation for studying probabilistic privacy guarantees in quantum information processing and their operational consequences for private quantum statistical inference.

Abstract
