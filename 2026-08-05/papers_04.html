Summary. This paper investigates whether local stability derived from Renormalization Group theory determines the final state reached by a propagating front described by the FKPP equation. Using RG analysis, the authors confirm that while all fronts with $v \geq 2$ are locally stable, this stability criterion is insufficient to predict which specific asymptotic state will materialize. They conclude that dynamic selection relies on external information carried by the leading edge of the solution.

Why it may be interesting. The distinction drawn between formal stability (RG) and dynamic selection is crucial in non-equilibrium statistical mechanics, impacting how one models pattern formation or phase transitions where multiple stable states might exist.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to determine if the stability of Renormalization-Group (RG) fixed points dictates which asymptotic state is selected during nonlinear wave propagation.

Main result. The analysis shows that for the FKPP equation, RG fixed-point stability and the actual asymptotically selected front state are distinct concepts. The selection depends on the leading-edge structure of the initial condition, not solely on local stability.

Method. A unified Renormalization-Group (RG) framework is applied to analyze traveling-wave solutions of nonlinear PDEs, supplemented by linearizing the dynamics at the solution's leading edge.

Model / system. The primary model is the Fisher–Kolmogorov–Petrovsky–Piskunov (FKPP) equation ($\partial_t V = V(1 -V) + \partial_{xx}V$), which serves as a canonical example for studying front propagation and asymptotic state selection.

Key observables. Traveling wave velocity ($v$), RG fixed points, local stability eigenvalues ($\omega$).

Important parameters / regimes. The velocity threshold $v \geq 2$ is highlighted in the stability analysis.

Assumptions / limitations. The leading-edge dynamics are analyzed under the approximation that $V^2 \ll V$ at the edge. The RG framework itself provides a description of existence and local stability.

Figures summary. Not specified by the notes.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the FKPP equation as the model system, then applies an RG framework to show that solutions form a continuous family of fixed points. It subsequently performs a detailed stability analysis confirming local stability for $v \geq 2$, culminating in the argument that this stability does not determine the final selected state.

Abstract

The Fisher--Kolmogorov--Petrovsky--Piskunov equation provides a paradigmatic example of front propagation and asymptotic-state selection. Using a unified renormalization-group (RG) framework, we show that its traveling-wave solutions form a continuous family of RG fixed points and that all fronts with $v \ge 2$ are RG-stable. Nevertheless, the asymptotically selected front is not determined by RG stability. The FKPP equation therefore provides an explicit example in which RG fixed-point stability and asymptotic-state selection are distinct concepts.

Abstract

We investigate the interplay of spin-orbit coupling, crystal field splittings, and electronic correlations in the $t_{2g}$ Hubbard-Kanamori model within the recently formulated ghost rotationally invariant slave-boson method (GRISB). Abstract

Abstract

Grokking is a striking phenomenon in neural network training, where a model can undergo a prolonged period of pure memorization before abrupt generalization. While previous works have attempted to interpret it through classical machine learning mechanisms like weight norm, recent research draws an analogy from statistical physics, framing grokking as a form of computational glass relaxation. This theory defines the initial memorization as a result of fast cooling' where the training loss is reduced so quickly that a glass state is formed, followed by aslow relaxation' towards final generalization. Although providing a unifying framework for representative grokking theories, this perspective has remained largely at the theoretical on macroscopic level without direct empirical validation on training dynamics. Here we introduce a three-component framework to directly characterize the training dynamics via parameter mobility (PM), and two representative measurements from glassy dynamics: replica correlation (RC) and fractal dimension (FD). We demonstrate that standard optimization presents clear signatures of glass dynamics and inherently traps the grokking network in a kinetic arrested memorization state with a collapsed mobility, strong history dependence, and channel-like motions. This quantitative agreement motivates us to introduce State-Aware Monte Carlo Parameter Swapping (SAM-Swap), an optimization plug-in that can accelerate generalization, inspired by swap Monte Carlo algorithm widely used in glass dynamics. Comparing SAM-Swap, weight decay, and Gaussian gradient noise, we find that accelerated generalization is consistently associated with random exploration in the parameter space, similar to diffusion in physics.

Abstract

A conservation-law-guided classification framework is developed for the execution of coarse-graining operations in quantum field theory. Coarse-graining produces a selection at the level of Feynman diagrams. Diagrams with nonzero momentum transfer are suppressed by oscillatory factors, zero-momentum-transfer ladder diagrams can accumulate through geometric series resummation to produce a spectral pole, and single-bubble topologies contribute only to the continuum. Conservation laws govern this classification. For operators protected by a Ward identity, the matrix element at zero momentum is guaranteed to be nonvanishing. For operators protected by BRST symmetry, the Slavnov-Taylor identities provide no mandatory suppression. The two cases differ in the strength of the algebraic guarantee. For unprotected operators the injection term vanishes and the spectral function remains continuous. The sign of the single-bubble contribution is determined by spin statistics. A positive sign leads to amplificative feedback in the ladder resummation, a negative sign leads to suppressive feedback. These two attributes, the nonvanishing of the injection term and the sign of the single-bubble contribution, are the defining criteria of the classification. As a direct application, a general classification of local operators is established within the emergence framework and verified on eight physical channels and three known solvable systems. The framework indicates which emergence paths are possible for each operator; whether the critical condition is reached is left for independent nonperturbative computation. The logical structure of this classification is parallel to the strategy used in deriving fluid equations from molecular kinetic theory in classical statistical physics.
