Complete Structural Determination of Mesostructural Dodecagonal Quasicrystalline Particles

Authors: Xueliang Zhang, Xi Wang, Nobuhisa Fujita, Osamu Terasaki, Lu Han
Type: experiment · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.02142v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Frenkel-Kontorova 1/5 · analog quantum simulation 1/5 · methods for driven-dissipative 1/5

Summary. This work presents the first complete 3D structural determination of self-assembled mesostructural dodecagonal quasicrystals using advanced electron tomography. By analyzing nodal sites, the authors revealed that the structure deviates from simple theoretical models, quantifying defects and fluctuations. This advances understanding of soft-matter quasiperiodic order by providing detailed atomic-scale structural data.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on materials science crystallography rather than fundamental quantum phenomena, the rigorous use of advanced tomography to resolve local structural details and quantify deviations from idealized periodic models (like TCP) shares methodological parallels with characterizing complex many-body ground states or defect structures in condensed matter theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is achieving a precise, local three-dimensional (3D) structural determination of constituent atoms or secondary building units within complex self-assembled soft matter quasicrystalline systems.

Main result. The study reports the first complete 3D structure of mesostructural dodecagonal quasicrystals, revealing that the actual arrangement deviates from ideal tetrahedral close packing and exhibits quantifiable defects and fluctuations.

Method. Advanced electron tomography was employed to acquire tilt-series data, followed by custom structural tracing workflows to extract 3D coordinates of nodal sites across the entire particle volume.

Model / system. The physical system is self-assembled mesostructural dodecagonal quasicrystalline particles formed from cationic surfactant and silica precursors. The analysis compares the experimental structure against idealized models, such as the Tetrahedrally Close-Packed (TCP) lattice.

Key observables. 3D nodal coordinates, coordination numbers (CN), phason strain parameters ($\lambda$, $d$), and comparison of simulated vs. experimental TEM images.

Important parameters / regimes. The periodicity along the stacking axis (e.g., 6.9 nm for QC-1) and the cutoff distance used for neighbor counting ($1.5 imes$ mean nearest-neighbor distance).

Assumptions / limitations. Initial reconstruction assumes a spherical geometry for each extracted node, although subsequent analysis incorporates positional fluctuations to refine this.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the entire process: 2D tiling patterns, 3D tomogram reconstructions showing 12-fold symmetry in FFT, and direct comparisons between simulated TEM images based on derived models (Ideal vs. Fluctuated) and experimental views.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from identifying the structural challenge to detailing the advanced electron tomography data acquisition and reconstruction process. It then analyzes the extracted 3D nodal positions by classifying node types, quantifying deviations from ideal lattices using displacement analysis, and finally proposing a simplified two-layer stacking model that best fits the observed disorder.

Abstract

Quasicrystals have revolutionized our understanding of order in solids by demonstrating exotic structural and physicochemical properties with diverse potential applications. Despite the development of various theoretical models and experimental techniques to describe quasicrystal structures, the precise determination of local three dimensional (3D) arrangements of constituent atoms, or of secondary building units such as clusters or micelles, remains elusive. This challenge is particularly acute in self assembled soft matter quasicrystalline systems, where the complex assembly of molecular groups introduces additional defects and structural modulations. Herein, we report the first complete structural determination of self-assembled mesostructural dodecagonal quasicrystalline particles. Employing advanced electron tomography, combined with dedicated structural tracing and processing workflows, the 3D coordinates of all nodal sites were extracted. This approach reveals that the actual structure deviates from the conventionally assumed tetrahedral close packing geometry, exhibiting diverse coordination environments and displacive fluctuations. We identified and quantified rotational intergrowths arising from node exchange, as well as various defects and disorder, with these features discernible only through 3D analysis. Additionally, we propose a simplified two-layer stacking of isomorphic hexagonal model to form dodecagonal quasicrystal. This work advances our understanding of soft-matter dodecagonal quasicrystals and paves the way for detailed structural elucidation of self-assembled systems.

Screening phonon-mediated superconductors from static orbital Hamiltonians

Summary. This paper introduces a novel, low-cost computational framework called SEPR to screen for phonon-mediated superconductors by estimating electron-phonon coupling directly from static orbital Hamiltonians. By bypassing expensive explicit phonon calculations, the authors screened over 36,000 compounds and identified 34 promising candidates with $T_c > 10$ K. The work reveals that high-$T_c$ superconductivity arises primarily through two distinct mechanisms: strong covalent $\sigma$-bonding or high density of states accumulation near the Fermi level.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on condensed matter superconductivity rather than quantum information, the development of an efficient, physically transparent theoretical framework (SEPR) to bypass computationally prohibitive steps in many-body calculations is methodologically relevant to complex quantum simulations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The high computational cost of first-principles calculations for electron-phonon coupling (EPC) severely limits large-scale screening of potential superconducting materials.

Main result. The authors developed a low-cost framework identifying 34 dynamically stable superconducting candidates with calculated Tc > 10 K, revealing two distinct microscopic routes to high-Tc superconductivity.

Method. They propose the Static Electron-Phonon Response (SEPR) scheme, which estimates EPC tendency directly from static orbital-based Hamiltonians, bypassing explicit phonon perturbation calculations during screening.

Model / system. The study focuses on phonon-mediated superconductors analyzed using an orbital-based tight-binding Hamiltonian. The framework uses approximations like the Dynamic Isotropic Approximation and Bonding Attractivity (BA) model to derive screening parameters $\Xi$ and $\chi$.

Key observables. Superconducting critical temperature ($T_c$), EPC scattering scale ($\lambda$, $V_{ep}$), and intrinsic deformation response ($\chi$).

Important parameters / regimes. The two key regimes identified are the metallized covalent $\sigma$-bond route (high $V_{ep}$) and the Fermi-level density-of-states accumulation route (high $N^*(\epsilon_F)$).

Assumptions / limitations. The SEPR framework is an estimation of electronic tendency, not a replacement for full DFPT calculation; it relies on several approximations to achieve computational efficiency.

Figures summary. Figure 1 compares the conventional costly DFPT workflow with the efficient SEPR workflow. Figure 5 maps the identified candidates in a $V_{ep} - N^*(\epsilon_F)$ plane, showing clustering into two distinct high-$T_c$ regimes.

Paper structure. The paper first identifies the computational bottleneck of EPC calculations. It then introduces and details the SEPR scheme using three approximations to calculate screening parameters ($\Xi$, $\chi$). Finally, it applies this method to a large database, identifying candidates and classifying them based on their underlying microscopic mechanism for high-$T_c$.

Abstract

The first-principles search for superconductors is severely limited by the high cost of electron-phonon coupling (EPC) calculations. Here we develop a low-cost, physically transparent framework that identifies strong-EPC materials directly from static orbital-based Hamiltonians without explicit phonon perturbation calculations. Verification using density functional perturbation theory (DFPT) for representative superconductors shows that the framework captures semi-quantitatively the EPC scale at substantially lower computational cost. Applied to more than 36,000 compounds in the MattKeyBond database, it identifies 34 dynamically stable superconducting candidates with calculated $T_c > 10$ K after DFPT verification. These candidates reveal two distinct routes to relatively high-$T_c$ superconductivity: a metallized covalent $σ$-bond route that is more favorable for achieving high-$T_c$ superconductors, and a Fermi-level density-of-states accumulation route that can enhance $T_c$ but usually to a more limited extent.

Abstract

In this work we provide compelling experimental validation of the Bianconi Perali Valletta (BPV) theory predicting a superconducting dome based on a quantum material design of Artificial High TC Superlattices (AHTS) made with a selected nanoscale heterostructure geometry. These AHTS are SNSN superlattices of quantum wells of period d, composed of first units, superconducting doped Mott insulator layers with Rashba spin orbit coupling (S) of thickness L, intercalated by second units, normal metal spacers (N). In these superlattices, grown by molecular beam epitaxy (MBE), the experimental superconducting dome is obtained by material quantum design changing the chemical potential via the quantum geometrical factor L/d which tunes the Fano-Feshbach shape resonance in the pair transfer between superconducting gaps in the BCS regime and different gaps in the BEC-BCS crossover. Here we present a systematic magneto-transport study of AHTS artificial superlattices across the full doping range of the superconducting dome, from the deeply underdoped to the overdoped regime, using pulsed magnetic fields up to 72 T. By varying the L/d ratio, we tune the effective hole concentration delta=0.45(1-L/d) and map the evolution of the resistive transitions, the upper critical magnetic field and the Ginzburg-Landau coherence length

Time reversal symmetry broken quantum spin hall effect in pseudospin-1 Dirac-Rashba system

Summary. This paper theoretically explores the complex interplay between time-reversal symmetry breaking, Rashba spin-orbit coupling, and magnetism in a pseudospin-1 fermionic system. It demonstrates that the QSH phase persists under broken TRS but transitions into distinct QAH phases. Crucially, it also shows how applying an in-plane magnetic field can induce tunable Second-Order Topological Insulator (SOTI) phases hosting localized corner states.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a detailed theoretical playground for understanding how multiple topological orders (QSH, QAH, SOTI) can coexist and transition within a single, tunable lattice structure using external magnetic fields and SOC.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how the Quantum Spin Hall (QSH) phase survives, transitions, or changes character when Time-Reversal Symmetry (TRS) is broken by a ferromagnetic exchange field and spin mixing occurs via Rashba SOC.

Main result. The system exhibits multiple topological phases, including QSH and distinct Quantum Anomalous Hall (QAH) phases. Furthermore, in-plane magnetization can induce Second-Order Topological Insulator (SOTI) phases with tunable corner states.

Method. Theoretical analysis using tight-binding models on the pseudospin-1 $\alpha$-T3 lattice, calculating topological invariants like the spin-Chern number and total Chern number via momentum-space Hamiltonian diagonalization.

Model / system. The system is a pseudospin-1 fermionic model on an $\alpha$-T3 lattice, incorporating Kane-Mele hopping, Rashba SOC ($\lambda_R$), and ferromagnetic exchange coupling ($\lambda_{ex}$). The Hamiltonian is analyzed in spin-up/spin-down sectors.

Key observables. Spin-Chern number ($C_\sigma$), total Chern number ($C$), bulk band gaps ($\Delta E$), and localized zero-energy corner states (SOTI).

Important parameters / regimes. Lattice parameter $\alpha$, Rashba coupling strength ($\lambda_R$), and exchange field strengths ($\lambda_{\perp ext{ex}}, \lambda_{\parallel ext{ex}}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on projecting the Hamiltonian onto occupied subspaces to define topological invariants when symmetries are broken.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate band structures (e.g., zigzag nanoribbons) showing gap closing/reopening at critical parameter values ($\alpha = \lambda_{ ext{ex}}$), and real-space spectra confirming tunable zero-energy corner modes in SOTI phases.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the Hamiltonian's response to broken TRS, first establishing QSH protection, then showing how Rashba SOC drives transitions into specific QAH states, and finally exploring the realization of SOTI phases via in-plane magnetization.

Abstract

The Quantum spin Hall (QSH) phase is conventionally understood to be protected by time-reversal symmetry (TRS). Here, we theoretically investigated the fate of the QSH phase in a pseudospin-1 fermionic $α-\mathcal{T}_3$ system in the presence of a TRS-breaking ferromagnetic exchange field and spin-nonconserving Rashba spin-orbit coupling. Despite broken TRS, the QSH phase survives over a finite parameter regime and is characterised by a non-zero projected spin-Chern number $C_σ(σ= \uparrow, \downarrow)$, protected by a spin-spectral gap. In the absence of Rashba coupling, the QSH phase remains robust up to an $α$-dependent critical exchange field. Rashba SOC qualitatively reshapes the phase diagram by driving transitions into two distinct quantum anomalous Hall (QAH) phases: a $C=2$ phase, irrespective of $α$-values, and a $C=1$ phase for $α\neq 0,1$, which is further identified as a valley-polarized QAH phase arising from a single valley. Rotating the magnetization to in-plane gaps out the first-order helical edge states and gives rise to second-order topological insulator (SOTI) phases that host localized corner states in suitable finite geometry. We further identify a topological phase transition between two different SOTI phases, mediated by nanoribbon edge states at an exchange field equal to $α$. These results establish spin-resolved topology in a higher pseudospin system as well as the $α-\mathcal{T}_3$ lattice as a versatile platform for engineering and controlling multiple topological phases through magnetic exchange and spin-orbit coupling.

Abstract

Dirac semimetal BaMg2Bi2 is reported to be a unique topological material that manifests surface superconductivity that coexistswith bulk band topology at ambient pressure. Here, we present a comprehensive investigation of high-pressure superconductingproperties in BaMg2Bi2 single crystal. Significantly, a pressure-driven double-dome superconducting behavior was revealed, withthe superconducting transition temperature Tc approaching the maximum values of 6.67 K at 4.5 GPa and 7.22 K at 10.4 GPafor the first and second superconducting domes, respectively. Summary. The paper introduces a novel, robust method using the 'backbone curve' derived from ringdown measurements to accurately characterize higher-order conservative nonlinearities in nanomechanical resonators. By analyzing the frequency dependence on amplitude, this technique overcomes limitations of conventional methods that suffer from frequency drift or are restricted in their nonlinearity scope. This advancement is crucial for utilizing these high-precision mechanical systems in advanced quantum sensing applications.

Why it may be interesting. Main problem. The primary goal is developing a robust method to accurately extract higher-order conservative nonlinearities from nanomechanical resonators, especially those that are symmetry-breaking.

Main result. The proposed backbone curve method successfully extracts nonlinearity coefficients ($\alpha_i$) with high accuracy, showing superior robustness against frequency fluctuations compared to conventional fitting techniques.

Method. The core technique involves using ringdown measurements to construct a 'backbone curve' that maps the instantaneous oscillation frequency versus amplitude, which is then used in conjunction with action-angle formalisms for coefficient extraction.

Model / system. The system studied is a high-Q nanomechanical string resonator (silicon nitride on fused silica). The dynamics are modeled by an anharmonic potential $U(q) = \sum M_i \gamma_i q^i$, and the frequency dependence $\omega(A)$ is derived from the action-angle formalism.

Key observables. Nonlinear eigenfrequency $\omega(A)$, nonlinear coefficients ($\alpha_i$ or $\gamma_i$), ringdown decay time constant ($ au$), and the backbone curve $\omega_{ ext{inst}}(A)$.

Important parameters / regimes. High-Q factor, operating temperature (293 K), drive power levels (e.g., $-35 ext{ dBm}$), and nonlinearity coefficients $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \dots$.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate ringdown measurements, phase space trajectories to accurately determine the start time ($t_0$), and plots comparing nonlinearity coefficients derived from different fitting approaches (e.g., response curve vs. backbone fit).

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the physical system and theoretical framework ($\omega(A)$), then detailing multiple measurement techniques (Duffing fits, ringdown analysis), and finally advocating for and validating the superior robustness of the backbone-based extraction method.

Abstract

Nanomechanical resonators are a powerful platform for studying nonlinear dynamics with high sensitivity and precision. We explore the nonlinear response of a high-Q nanomechanical string resonator in and beyond the Duffing regime and introduce a robust framework for accurately extracting its conservative nonlinearities. The method is based on the backbone curve obtained from ringdown measurements, making it inherently resilient to small frequency fluctuations while explicitly accounting for both symmetry-breaking and non-symmetry-breaking nonlinearities. To validate the approach, we perform complementary ringdown and frequency-response measurements on the nanostring resonator and benchmark the backbone-based extraction against established frequency-response techniques. The comparison confirms the accuracy of the proposed framework and demonstrates its advantages over conventional methods for nonlinear characterization.

Summary. This theoretical study analyzes the nonlinear optical response, specifically Second-Harmonic Generation (SHG), in twisted double bilayer graphene. This provides a general framework for controlling non-linear optics in moiré materials.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a detailed quantum optical analysis of tunable electronic structures (moiré bands), which is highly relevant to understanding non-linear responses in designer quantum materials that can be tuned via external fields like bias voltage.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the mechanism behind enhanced Second-Harmonic Generation (SHG) in twisted double bilayer graphene (TDBG). The focus is on identifying and quantifying the 'double-resonant' enhancement beyond standard moiré flat band effects.

Main result. The authors demonstrate that a pronounced double-resonant process, where optical transitions at both $\omega$ and $2\omega$ are simultaneously satisfied, leads to substantial amplification of the SHG response. They also find distinct symmetry signatures ($\pi$-phase shift) between AB–AB and AB–BA stacking configurations.

Method. The investigation uses a perturbative approach based on an effective continuum Hamiltonian derived for the moiré scale. The calculation involves deriving the nonlinear susceptibility tensor $\sigma_{\mu\alpha\alpha}$ using Green's function formalism.

Model / system. The physical system is Twisted Double Bilayer Graphene (TDBG), which exhibits a moiré superlattice at small twist angles ($ heta$). Important parameters / regimes. Twist angle ($ heta$), vertical bias voltage ($V_{AB}$), Fermi energy ($E_F$), and stacking configuration (AB–AB vs AB–BA).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on coarse-graining the system to the moiré scale at small $ heta$, and approximations are made in the perturbative expansion of the Hamiltonian.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate stacking configurations, Feynman diagrams for Hamiltonian construction, and color-density maps showing SHG spectra revealing single and double resonance features.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the SHG response by first establishing the effective continuum model for TDBG. It then details the calculation of the nonlinear susceptibility using perturbation theory, culminating in the identification and theoretical description of the double-resonant enhancement mechanism.

Abstract

We theoretically investigate second-harmonic generation (SHG) in twisted double bilayer graphene (TDBG) with AB--AB and AB--BA stacking configurations using a perturbative approach based on an effective continuum Hamiltonian. We present a systematic analysis of the SHG response as a function of twist angle, vertical bias voltage, Fermi energy, and stacking configuration. We find that the SHG signal is strongly enhanced at small twist angles due to the emergence of moiré flat bands and the associated increase in the joint density of states. Beyond this conventional enhancement mechanism, we demonstrate that the reduced bandwidth enables a pronounced double-resonant process, in which optical transitions at both $ω$ and $2ω$ are simultaneously satisfied over extended regions of the moiré Brillouin zone. This mechanism leads to a substantial amplification of the SHG response, analogous to the enhancement observed in systems with discrete energy levels, but realised here in a tuneable moiré band structure. Furthermore, we show that the AB--AB and AB--BA configurations exhibit a systematic $π$ phase shift in the SHG response at large bias voltages, reflecting their distinct symmetry and electronic structure. Our results identify double-resonance effects as a generic mechanism for enhancing SHG in moiré systems and provide a unified framework for understanding and controlling nonlinear optical responses in tuneable flat-band materials.
