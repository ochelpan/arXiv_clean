Summary. This paper revisits the thermal limits of superconducting quantum computers by developing an advanced heat estimation model. It demonstrates that previously overlooked heat sources, particularly from readout amplifiers and wiring resistance, create a dominant thermal bottleneck. The findings are critical for designing viable, large-scale cryogenic quantum hardware.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses hardware limitations in scaling quantum computation, which is crucial for any theoretical physicist working on open quantum systems or building complex quantum architectures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The readout amplification chain imposes a critical thermal scalability bottleneck that limits the size of superconducting quantum processors.

Main result. Amplifier-associated heat emerges as the dominant thermal bottleneck in modern large-scale cryogenic systems, requiring new modeling approaches to predict true qubit capacity.

Method. The authors developed an improved system-level thermal estimation model that fully accounts for active dissipation, passive conduction, and Joule heating across multiple cryogenic stages.

Model / system. The study models superconducting quantum processors housed in a dilution refrigerator (DR) with distinct temperature stages ($ ext{RT}$ down to $\sim 10 ext{ mK}$). The model calculates the total normalized heat load by summing contributions from cables, attenuators, and amplifiers.

Key observables. Total Normalized Heat Load ($ ext{NHL}_{ ext{THL},s}$) at each cryogenic stage; maximum supported physical qubit capacity.

Important parameters / regimes. Active dissipation, passive conduction coefficients, Joule heating resistance/currents, target scale (e.g., 10k qubits).

Assumptions / limitations. Previous models are criticized for omitting or underestimating the combined heat load from the readout amplifier chain, leading to overestimation of scalability.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the thermal bottleneck problem, details the improved unified modeling framework incorporating all major heat sources, analyzes cross-technology trade-offs, and concludes by evaluating pathways toward large single-fridge systems.

Abstract

The readout amplification chain imposes a critical thermal scalability bottleneck in large-scale superconducting quantum systems. This happens through three mechanisms: amplifier dissipation, passive conduction through bias wiring and Joule heating within that same wiring. These terms are absent or only partially represented in several prior system-level thermal-scalability models, leading to bottleneck misidentification and scalability overestimation. In this work, we improve upon previous system-level heat estimation models by fully accounting for the major heat sources in modern cryogenic quantum systems including the active dissipation, passive conduction, and Joule heating in the readout amplifier module. Our analysis demonstrates that amplifier-associated heat emerges as the dominant thermal bottleneck that fundamentally alters the thermal landscape of modern large-scale cryogenic systems. We explore various technology options and their tradeoffs to identify configurations that reduce this critical heat load and improve scalability. Finally, we evaluate forward-looking system configurations, including larger refrigeration platforms and optical approaches, and analyze forward-looking pathways toward single-fridge 10k-qubit cryogenic systems.

Summary. This work analyzes the entanglement structure of the 1D repulsive Hubbard model by examining the reduced density matrix after partitioning one spin species. The authors find that this leads to a modular Luttinger liquid whose correlations are governed by a single effective parameter, $\sqrt{K_c K_s}$. This demonstrates a universal principle: even when tracing out degrees of freedom, the entanglement structure retains critical algebraic correlations characteristic of 1D quantum liquids.

Why it may be interesting. The concept of an 'entanglement Hamiltonian' provides a powerful tool to characterize entanglement in interacting quantum systems. Finding that this modular structure is robust and yields a simple geometric mean for the correlation parameter offers deep insights into how entanglement encodes underlying symmetries, which is relevant for understanding exotic phases in correlated materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to determine the universal low-energy entanglement Hamiltonian associated with a spin partition in an interacting one-dimensional metal, specifically investigating its structure when tracing out one spin species from the Hubbard model.

Main result. Tracing out one spin species yields a modular Luttinger liquid whose effective correlation is governed by a single parameter equal to the geometric mean of the charge and spin Luttinger parameters. This reveals a universal modular structure in 1D fermionic systems.

Method. The authors combine bosonization techniques with exact diagonalization (ED) methods to calculate the reduced density matrix for one spin species, leading to the definition and analysis of an entanglement Hamiltonian.

Model / system. The primary system is the one-dimensional repulsive Hubbard model. The analysis proceeds by mapping the interacting fermionic problem onto bosonic fields via bosonization, allowing for the calculation of entanglement properties.

Key observables. Entanglement entropy, entanglement spectrum (eigenvalues of $H_{ ext{ent}}$), and Luttinger parameters ($K_c, K_s$).

Important parameters / regimes. The effective single LL parameter $\sqrt{K_c K_s}$, the repulsive interaction strength $U$, and the hopping amplitude $t$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on bosonization techniques and assumes that the reduced density matrix is Gaussian-structured, which is valid for the quadratic nature of the bosonized theory.

Figures summary. Figures visualize the universal branching hierarchy in the entanglement spectrum (Fig. 1) and show the evolution of the entanglement density of states compared to bosonization predictions (Fig. 2).

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the theoretical framework by defining the reduced density matrix for one spin species ($ ho_\uparrow$) and deriving the associated entanglement Hamiltonian ($H_{ ext{mod}}$). It then applies this formalism to the Hubbard model, using bosonization results to predict universal properties like the effective Luttinger parameter and the branching structure of the spectrum.

Abstract

We study the spin-partitioned entanglement Hamiltonian of the one-dimensional repulsive Hubbard model. By combining bosonization with exact diagonalization, we show that tracing out one spin species produces a modular Luttinger liquid, whose properties fundamentally differ from those of the physical system. While the modular spectrum is fully dispersionless and possesses a momentum-independent entanglement gap, its eigenstates exhibit algebraic correlations governed by a single effective Luttinger parameter equal to the geometric mean of the charge and spin Luttinger parameters. The resulting entanglement spectrum displays a universal branching hierarchy in excellent agreement with exact diagonalization. We further demonstrate that the modular ground state is nearly identical to that of the spinless Luttinger liquid. These results uncover a universal modular structure in interacting one-dimensional fermionic systems.

Summary. This paper presents Oraqle, a comprehensive framework to benchmark the entire readout-to-QEC pipeline for superconducting qubits. It systematically analyzes how measurement duration, machine learning discriminators, and QEC codes interact to determine the final logical error rate. The key takeaway is that hardware optimization should focus on reducing readout latency rather than over-engineering the classical decoding model.

Main problem. The performance of Quantum Error Correction (QEC) is critically dependent on the readout process, but this co-dependence between readout characteristics, ML discriminators, and QEC performance has not been deeply studied.

Main result. The study reveals that measurement duration can be significantly reduced with minimal logical error rate penalty, and discriminator complexity is largely irrelevant as residual errors are dominated by device physics.

Method. The authors introduce Oraqle, an end-to-end benchmarking framework that systematically evaluates the entire pipeline from raw qubit readout traces through ML discrimination to final QEC performance metrics.

Model / system. The work focuses on superconducting qubits operating in the dispersive regime. The system involves a full cycle: ancilla readout $ ightarrow$ ML decoding $ ightarrow$ error correction, tested across multiple QEC codes (e.g., Surface, Color).

Key observables. Logical Error Rate (LER), Readout Fidelity ($F_{5Q}$), and the raw IQ trace data used for classification.

Important parameters / regimes. Measurement duration ($\mu$s scale), discriminator complexity/model size, and the specific QEC code structure.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the QEC cycle and identifies readout as a bottleneck; it then introduces Oraqle to benchmark the entire stack across multiple codes and hardware regimes, culminating in three key findings regarding measurement time, discriminator role, and maturity dependence.

Abstract

Quantum error correction (QEC) is the most promising route toward fault-tolerant quantum computing and, thus, useful quantum computers. QEC operates as a continuous measure-decode-correct cycle: ancilla qubits are read out, a decoder infers errors from the resulting syndromes, and corrections are applied before the next round begins. Within this loop, readout occupies a uniquely critical role, as it is the sole source of ground truth available to the decoder. Yet readout is also the slowest and most error-prone operation in the stack, with characteristics that vary across qubits and drift over time; This complexity propagates directly to the classical control hardware, and in particular to the FPGA-hosted machine-learning (ML) discriminator that must classify each analog signal into a binary syndrome outcome. Despite this central role, QEC performance has not yet been studied in depth from the perspective of readout characteristics, readout length, and their co-design with an ML discriminator. We introduce Oraqle, an end-to-end benchmarking framework that evaluates qubit-state readout and its impact on QEC performance across real experimentally extracted qubit-state-readout datasets, state-of-the-art ML discriminators, multiple QEC codes, and hardware regimes spanning current to projected devices. Our study reveals three asymmetric findings: The measurement duration can be significantly reduced with nearly no penalty to the logical error rate; The discriminator complexity barely affects the QEC performance, as residual errors are written into device physics rather than the model; and the impact of qubit-state readout on the logical error rate is conditional on where the hardware sits in the QEC landscape, a window that widens as devices mature.

Summary. This paper rigorously proves that general Positive Operator-Valued Measures (POVMs) can achieve correlations strictly greater than those achievable by projective measurements (PVMs) when local dimensions are fixed. The separation is quantified using a novel Bell functional and proven analytically via advanced algebraic techniques, establishing a fundamental resource difference in quantum measurement theory.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a highly rigorous mathematical tool—an analytic Bell functional with an exact certificate—to quantify fundamental differences between measurement models in quantum information theory, which is crucial for understanding device capabilities.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to establish a rigorous, analytic separation between correlations achievable by general quantum measurements (POVMs) and those restricted to projective measurements (PVMs) in a bipartite setting.

Main result. An explicit qubit POVM was constructed whose Bell value strictly exceeds the upper bound derived for all possible qubit PVM strategies over arbitrary shared two-qubit states, establishing a certified gap greater than 1/1000.

Method. The separation is formalized using a specialized Bell functional and proven analytically through advanced mathematical techniques, including the use of exact level-3 noncommutative sum-of-squares (SOS) certificates in an algebraic framework.

Model / system. The study analyzes correlations in a finite bipartite setting involving shared two-qubit states ($ ho_{AB}$), comparing measurement outcomes from general POVMs versus restricted PVMs on qubits.

Key observables. Bell functional value $B$, the quantum maximum $\Omega = 2\sqrt{2} + 1/100$, and the upper bound for qubit PVM strategies.

Important parameters / regimes. Local dimension (fixed vs. unrestricted), the small parameter $\epsilon$ used in constructing the separating functional, and the specific structure of the two-qubit state.

Assumptions / limitations. The separation is only meaningful when local dimensions are constrained; otherwise, Naimark's theorem allows dilation to remove the distinction.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the Bell scenario comparing qubit POVMs vs. qubit projective measurements using a specific functional $B$, and Table I summarizes benchmark values for different measurement types.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining the physical problem (POVM vs PVM separation) to constructing an explicit separating POVM, deriving the necessary algebraic bounds via SOS certificates, and finally proving that this constructed value attains the dimension-unrestricted quantum optimum.

Abstract

Generalized measurements can be implemented projectively after enlarging the Hilbert space, but this dilation changes the available local dimension. We construct a Bell functional with rational coefficients that separates the two measurement models at local dimension two. An explicit three-outcome qubit positive-operator-valued measure with rational matrix entries attains $2\sqrt2+1/100$. On the other hand, all qubit-projective strategies are bounded by $2\sqrt2+\sqrt5/250+\sqrt2/32400$, giving a fully analytic certified gap greater than $1/1000$. To our knowledge, this is the first fully analytic Bell-functional separation between qubit POVMs and qubit projective measurements over arbitrary shared two-qubit states. Lean certificate for the separation theorem is provided for completeness. Separately, an exact level-3 noncommutative sum-of-squares certificate proves that the explicit qubit strategy attains the unrestricted finite-dimensional tensor-product quantum optimum.

Abstract

Abstract

The realization of high-temperature superconductivity in bilayer nickelates under epitaxial compressive strain is widely interpreted as mimicking the effects of high hydrostatic pressure. To test the equivalence of these mechanisms, we investigated a comprehensive strain continuum ranging from compressive (-2.14%) to tensile (+0.91%). Crucially, via ozone-assisted atomic-layer epitaxy, we realized high-temperature superconductivity in as-grown La2PrNi2O7 films on NdAlO3 substrates, which induce the most extreme compressive strain in this material system. Comparing our phase diagram with reported data reveals distinct lattice responses: unlike in pressurized crystals, the superconducting window in epitaxial films diverges significantly in the out-of-plane parameter c (or c/ap ratio) but remains consistent with the bulk regarding the in-plane parameter ap. Ultimately, both tuning strategies effectively modulate the underlying correlation landscape - the true driver of superconductivity - transcending the constraints of specific Fermi surface topologies. This work establishes a macroscopic platform for probing the multi-orbital physics of nickelates, offering a new dimension for investigating high-temperature superconductivity.
