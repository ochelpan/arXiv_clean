Summary. This paper theoretically investigates the pure spin photovoltaic effect in an altermagnetic topological insulator heterostructure. By developing a non-Abelian theory, the authors show that specific magnetic symmetries can suppress charge currents entirely, leading to a purely spin-generating response. This establishes the material as a versatile platform for controlling nonlinear spin transport using light.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a detailed theoretical framework linking magnetic order parameters (Néel vector) to specific quantized electronic responses (pure spin photocurrents) in topological materials, which is highly relevant for spintronics and quantum optics applications.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the Pure Spin Bulk Photovoltaic Effect (BPVE) in a complex magnetic heterostructure to understand spin current generation without accompanying charge currents.

Main result. The system realizes an intrinsically pure spin photovoltaic effect when the Néel vector is in the $xy$-plane, generating dc spin currents controllable by light polarization and magnetic symmetry.

Method. The authors develop a non-Abelian formulation of the spin BPVE, extending theory to PT-degenerate manifolds, and analyze response tensors using symmetry constraints.

Model / system. The model is a PT-symmetric 2D heterostructure combining a topological insulator with a d-wave altermagnet. The Hamiltonian incorporates SOC and magnetic exchange fields, leading to FOTI or SOTI phases depending on the Néel vector orientation.

Key observables. Pure spin photocurrent ($\sigma_{s,\mu; u\lambda}$), charge photocurrent, spin shift current (driven by linear polarization), and spin injection current (driven by circular polarization).

Important parameters / regimes. Orientation of the Néel vector ($\hat{n}_s$), local Dirac mass sign change, and symmetry protection ($C_{4z}T$ vs $C_{2z}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The theory extends conventional BPVE to PT-degenerate manifolds using a non-Abelian formalism; approximations are made regarding the spin-current operator's applicability in nonlinear optical processes.

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically shows optically induced spin transport components based on Néel vector orientation. Figure 2 illustrates energy spectra showing FOTI and SOTI topological phases under open boundary conditions.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by defining the physical system (TI/Altermagnet heterostructure), developing the generalized non-Abelian theory for spin BPVE, analyzing how symmetry dictates the pure spin response in different topological regimes (SOTI vs FOTI), and concluding with the platform's potential for controlling spin currents.

Abstract

We investigate the bulk photovoltaic effect (BPVE) in a $PT$-symmetric two-dimensional heterostructure consisting of a topological insulator coupled to a $d$-wave altermagnet. To describe the symmetry-enforced degenerate bands of this system, we develop a non-Abelian formulation of the spin BPVE, extending the conventional theory from isolated nondegenerate bands to $PT$-degenerate manifolds. We show that the orientation of the Néel vector controls both the topological phase and the character of the nonlinear optical response. When the Néel vector lies in the $xy$-plane, the heterostructure realizes a second-order topological insulator (SOTI) protected by $C_{4z}T$ symmetry. The system also retains $C_{2z}$ symmetry, which completely suppresses second-order charge photocurrents while allowing finite spin photocurrents. As a result, the BPVE becomes an intrinsically pure spin photovoltaic effect, generating a dc spin current without an accompanying charge current. We find that linearly polarized light drives a spin shift current, whereas circularly polarized light generates a spin injection current. Both responses undergo a sign reversal whenever the local Dirac mass changes sign. As the Néel vector is rotated toward the $z$-axis, the SOTI phase transforms into a first-order topological insulating phase, leading to the coexistence of charge and spin photocurrents. Our results establish $PT$-symmetric altermagnetic topological-insulator heterostructures as a versatile platform for generating and controlling pure spin photocurrents and reveal nonlinear spin transport as a sensitive probe of topology and magnetic symmetry.

Summary. This paper addresses the critical issue of 'subspace trapping' in optimizing Neural Quantum States (NQS) for many-body physics. The authors introduce Annealed Gradient Descent (AGD), a novel, sampling-aware optimization technique. Detailed structure

Main problem. Neural Quantum States (NQS) optimization suffers from 'subspace trapping' due to finite-sample instability, where important low-probability configurations are rarely sampled and receive insufficient gradient feedback.

Main result. The proposed Annealed Gradient Descent (AGD) method successfully suppresses metastable trapping, allowing simple NQS architectures to achieve chemical accuracy across molecular and spin model benchmarks.

Method. AGD is a sampling-aware update rule that temporarily reweights the influence of low-probability configurations during optimization, guiding the search away from restricted subspaces.

Model / system. The work applies to general quantum many-body systems, including molecular electronic structure calculations (using Gaussian basis sets) and quantum spin models like the 1D and 2D J1-J2 Heisenberg model.

Key observables. Variational energy expectation value ($\langle \hat{H} angle$), local energy ($E_{ ext{loc}}(\sigma)$), and convergence diagnostics showing trapping above the true ground state energy.

Important parameters / regimes. The annealing factor, learning rates (e.g., $\eta$, decay schedules), and system-specific coupling constants ($J_1/J_2$).

Assumptions / limitations. Standard gradient estimation methods fail when sampling coverage is insufficient; the authors derive enhanced gradient forms using logarithmic weight functions to correct for this bias.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate subspace trapping convergence (GD vs. AGD), showing AGD forces exploration toward lower energy states, and compare different weighting schemes ($w(p)=p$ vs $w(p)=-p \log p$).

Paper structure. The paper first identifies the theoretical failure mode (subspace trapping) in VMC/NQS optimization. It then develops AGD as a sampling-aware update rule, deriving enhanced gradient estimators that incorporate low-probability information before benchmarking on molecular and spin systems.

Abstract

Neural quantum states offer expressive representations of quantum many-body wave functions, yet their practical accuracy can be limited by stochastic optimization rather than representational capacity. Here we identify a finite-sample instability, termed subspace trapping, in which physically important configurations become strongly underestimated, remain absent from successive sampling batches and receive insufficient gradient feedback. This self-reinforcing loss of sampled support can confine optimization to an effective subspace and produce apparently stationary states above the true ground state energy. To address this problem, we introduce annealed gradient descent (AGD), a sampling-aware update with annealing factor that temporarily increases the relative contribution of sampled low-probability configurations while limiting the dominance of high-probability ones. We establish the connection between finite-sample support loss and effective subspace optimization, and then evaluate the method across molecular systems, one and two-dimensional $J_1$-$J_2$ models. Annealed gradient descent suppresses metastable trapping, preserves physically relevant configurations and enables compact neural quantum states to attain chemical accuracy and competitive state-of-the-art performance. These results establish AGD as a lightweight complement to expressive neural architectures, improved sampling strategies for scalable quantum many-body optimization.

Summary. This paper provides a critical analysis of Adlam's attempt to reconcile quantum theory with 'local friendliness' theorems using Quantum Reference Frames. The authors demonstrate that Adlam's resolution requires three non-standard modifications to physics, suggesting QRF formalism alone is insufficient. Ultimately, the work concludes that standard quantum mechanics cannot evade these fundamental no-go theorems.

Main problem. The paper critically assesses Adlam's proposal attempting to resolve tensions between quantum theory and 'local friendliness' (LF) theorems arising from Extended Wigner's Friend Scenarios (EWFS). It aims to show that standard Quantum Reference Frame (QRF) formalism cannot evade these no-go theorems.

Within standard quantum theory, QRFs do not evade EWFS no-go theorems.

Method. The authors employ critical assessment, comparing Adlam's modified mechanics (AMQM) against standard QM predictions using formalisms involving relational observables and group averaging techniques derived from QRF theory.

Model / system. The core model is the Wigner's Friend scenario (EWFS) involving entangled bipartite quantum systems (spin-1/2 particles), analyzed through the lens of Quantum Reference Frames (QRFs). The analysis uses abstract mathematical tools like group representations and projection operators to define frame-independent observables.

Key observables. Local Friendliness (LF) inequalities, spin outcomes ($AI$, $AE$), lab orientations ($AR$), and relational observables defined via reduction/relativisation maps.

Important parameters / regimes. The structure depends on the underlying symmetry group G used for frame transformations; specific calculations involve time evolution parameters like $\pi/(2g)$.

Assumptions / limitations. The critique assumes standard quantum theory holds, and it highlights that Adlam's proposal rests on misconceptions regarding QRFs and EWFS protocols.

Paper structure. The paper systematically critiques Adlam's proposal by first defining the tension (LF theorems), then detailing Adlam's three modifications, and finally using advanced QRF formalism (including appendices on recoupling theory and relational observables) to demonstrate that these modifications are necessary but insufficient to resolve the fundamental conflict within standard QM.

Abstract

Recent no-go theorems based on extended Wigner's Friend scenarios (EWFS) reveal a deep tension between the universal validity of quantum theory and local friendliness (LF), the conjunction of three natural assumptions: the absoluteness of observed events, locality, and no-superdeterminism. In a recent work, Adlam argues that this tension can be dissolved by appealing to quantum reference frames (QRFs). We present and critically assess her proposal. We show that her proposed resolution does not arise from QRFs, but instead relies on three independent modifications: certain degrees of freedom are always definite, observers can flip upon measuring spins, and the outcomes the superobservers record and use to test the inequalities are not their friends' observed results. The proposal and the arguments for the plausibility of these modifications rest on several misconceptions about QRFs and EWFS, which we rectify. We conclude that, within standard quantum theory, quantum reference frames do not evade the EWFS no-go theorems.

Abstract

We present full-dimensional quantum simulations of $H_2^+$ dissociative ionization driven by strong orthogonal laser fields. We consider equal-frequency orthogonal components, which generate elliptical or circular polarization depending on their relative phase and amplitude, as well as orthogonal $800$- and $400$-nm two-color fields. These two-dimensional fields strongly modify the fragmentation dynamics. Most notably, we identify a high-energy peak in the proton kinetic-energy-release (KER) spectrum at approximately $4-5$ eV that is absent from the corresponding single-color, linearly polarized calculations. The yield of this peak can be coherently controlled by varying the relative carrier-envelope phase of the perpendicular field component. The perpendicular field also disrupts the clear electron-proton energy-sharing pattern observed in the main $3-3.5$ eV dissociation channel, indicating more complex multichannel dynamics. Time-dependent state projections and calculations initiated from individual excited states attribute the additional peak to laser-induced vibrational excitation of $H_2^+$. Furthermore, the perpendicular field rotates the fragment angular distributions, causing the most probable proton and electron emission directions to deviate substantially from the principal $z$ axis. These findings demonstrate that the spatial and temporal geometry of orthogonal laser fields provides an additional degree of freedom for controlling ultrafast electron-nuclear dynamics.

Summary. This theoretical paper investigates what specific properties of entanglement are responsible for exponential speedups in quantum machine learning. Abstract

While entanglement is known to enable exponential improvements in the sample complexity of quantum learning, it remains unclear which properties of entangled resources are responsible for such improvements. We address this question through the reduction criterion, a condition obeyed by all bound-entangled states. In $n$-qubit Pauli-channel learning, we show that restricting either the input states or the measurement effects to satisfy this criterion rules out an exponential advantage for incoherent adaptive protocols. An exponential lower bound persists for the one-sided coherent adaptive protocols considered here, even when the unrestricted side retains quantum correlations across channel uses. Using conditional min-entropy, we further quantify how the sample-complexity lower bounds weaken as larger violations of the reduction criterion are allowed. Finally, we show that the same obstruction appears in conjugate-state learning: restricted joint measurements cannot reproduce the logarithmic-sample advantage of unrestricted joint measurements on $ρ\otimesρ^*$. These results identify violation of the reduction criterion as a necessary condition for an exponential advantage in the learning tasks considered here.

Abstract

Abstract

In quantum error correction, the error threshold provides essential quantitative guidance for the ability to bring about fault-tolerance through decoding the effects of incoherent noise, weak measurement or inference. However, the numerical value of an error threshold is typically only accessible through large-scale numerical simulations of the underlying noise model. Here we introduce an analytical estimate of error thresholds falling into the Nishimori universality class via a Fourier--Walsh projection scheme that maps the critical point of the underlying disorder-free statistical-mechanics model to the Born-disordered Nishimori critical point. Using a minimal replica theory approach, this closed-form estimate is obtained from a projection of the exact replicated single-bond weight which we find to reproduce (within a percentage point) the known numerical thresholds of random-bond and random-plaquette Ising models / $\mathbb Z_2$ stabilizer codes in spatial dimensions $d=2-5$, and extends to Potts variables with $q\le4$. Abstract

Four-spin ring (cyclic) exchange $J_{ring}$ is an essential ingredient of the Heisenberg spin Hamiltonian of cuprates and other square-lattice magnets, yet it has lacked the kind of direct, local first-principles extraction that the four-state method provides for bilinear exchange, $J$. We supply it by generalizing that method to a sixteen-state ($2^4$) scheme. Symmetry reduces the sixteen configurations to six or eight inequivalent energies, so the cost is modest. The derivation also shows that the conventional four-state magnetic coupling, $J$, is itself ring-renormalized, by $\pm 2 J_{ring} S^2$ with the sign set by the reference state. T-La$_2$CuO$_4$ confirms this quantitatively: three independent routes agree on $J_{ring}$ to $0.2\%$, giving $J_{ring}/J_1 = 0.25$, and a four-state $J_1$ quoted without naming its reference is wrong by $12\%$ in this material. SrFeO$_2$ ($S = 2$), with the same plaquette yet $J_{ring}/J = 0.006$, provides the negative control: a plaquette is necessary for ring exchange, far from sufficient.
