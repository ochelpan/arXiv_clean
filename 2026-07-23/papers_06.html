Summary. The authors investigate synthetic Caroli-de Gennes-Matricon (CdGM) states in full-shell nanowire Josephson junctions. By measuring the critical current ($I_c$) under an applied magnetic field, they observe a distinct skewness pattern. This signature is theoretically linked to CdGM analogs within the semiconductor core, providing strong evidence for their existence and utility as a diagnostic tool for superconducting physics.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete experimental realization and diagnostic tool (the $I_c$ skewness) for studying exotic superconducting phenomena like CdGM states, which are highly relevant in mesoscopic superconductivity and topological materials research.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to experimentally probe synthetic Caroli-de Gennes-Matricon (CdGM) states within full-shell nanowire Josephson junctions.

Main result. The characteristic skewness observed in the critical current ($I_c$) and zero-bias resistance ratio ($R_0/R_N$) is attributed to these CdGM analogs residing in the semiconductor core, superimposed on Little-Parks oscillations from the superconducting shell.

Method. The research combines transport measurements (measuring $dI/dV$ vs. $B$ and $I$) with microscopic theoretical modeling using Keldysh Green's functions formalism.

Model / system. The physical system is a full-shell hybrid nanowire Josephson junction ($ ext{SC}/ ext{SM}$ core). The model uses a tight-binding Bogoliubov-de Gennes Hamiltonian to calculate observables like the Local Density of States (LDOS) and critical current.

Key observables. Critical current ($I_c$) skewness, zero-bias resistance ratio ($R_0/R_N$), Little-Parks oscillations in $dI/dV$, and superconducting gap modulation.

Important parameters / regimes. Axial magnetic field ($B$), superconducting gap ($\Delta$), flux quantum ($\Phi_0$), and the level spacing of CdGM analogs relative to $\Delta$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the observed skewness is dominated by the finite population of CdGM analogs crossing zero energy, rather than just shell gap collapse.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate $dI/dV$ vs. $B$ showing Little-Parks lobes and current skewness; other figures compare theoretical predictions for $I_c$ skewness against experimental data derived from the $R_0/R_N$ ratio.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the physical platform (full-shell nanowires) and the phenomenon of CdGM analogs. It presents measurements showing characteristic current modulation and asymmetry, which are then theoretically modeled using Green's functions to confirm that the skewness fingerprint uniquely identifies the presence of these subgap states.

Abstract

Full-shell hybrid nanowires consisting of a semiconductor core fully enveloped by a superconducting shell have emerged as a platform to study Caroli-de Gennes-Matricon (CdGM) analogs. These subgap states can be considered a synthetic version of CdGM states in Abrikosov vortices. Unlike conventional CdGM states, these analogs exhibit a level spacing comparable to the superconducting gap, making them readily observable via tunneling spectroscopy techniques. The spectral density of CdGM analogs follows a characteristic skewed pattern as a function of applied axial magnetic field, an effect that is superimposed on the Little-Parks oscillations of the shell's gap induced by fluxoid quantization. Here, we provide experimental evidence for CdGM analogs through a distinctive skewness fingerprint in the critical current and zero-bias resistance of overdamped Josephson junctions based on full-shell nanowires.

Summary. This work investigates how strong, nonlinear magnon interactions in patterned magnetic films can spontaneously create multiple distinct energy coupling pathways. By observing and modeling double anticrossings whose characteristics depend strongly on pump power, the authors prove that three-magnon scattering generates two separate magnon populations, effectively creating multiple independent coupling channels within one physical system.

Why it may be interesting. The ability to dynamically generate multiple coupling channels via nonlinear magnon interactions is highly relevant to understanding engineered quantum systems where mode coupling dictates device functionality, bridging concepts from cavity QED/optomechanics into magnonics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To demonstrate that nonlinear magnon interactions can dynamically generate multiple emergent coupling channels within a single magnetic system, moving beyond channels fixed by device geometry.

Main result. The system exhibits pump-induced double anticrossings attributed to nondegenerate three-magnon splitting, which generates two distinct magnon populations and thus two effective coupling channels with the Kittel mode.

Method. A combination of microwave spectroscopy (VNA measurements) on patterned permalloy films and theoretical modeling using a three-magnon coupling Hamiltonian ($ ext{H}{ ext{nd}}$) coupled to an effective mean-field interaction ($ ext{H}$).}

Model / system. The physical system is spin waves (magnons) in patterned permalloy films. The model involves three-magnon scattering processes and the coupling of the resulting secondary magnon pairs to a primary Kittel mode.

Key observables. Double anticrossing features in the $S_{21}$ spectrum, frequency gap ($\Delta f_{ ext{gap}}$), and central frequency shift ($\delta\omega_c$).

Important parameters / regimes. Pump power ($P$), applied magnetic field ($B$), and wavevector ($k$). The dependence of observables on $P$ confirms nonlinearity.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on developing an effective coupling Hamiltonian in the mean-field approximation after deriving the three-magnon interaction terms.

Figures summary. Figures show schematic dispersion, initial single dip splitting into two dips upon pumping, and the evolution of a double anticrossing feature as the magnetic field increases.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from experimental observation (single/double anticrossings dependent on pump power) to theoretical interpretation, attributing the features to nondegenerate three-magnon splitting which creates multiple coupling channels. The analysis confirms predictions based on derived Hamiltonians and scaling laws for gap size and frequency shift.

Abstract

We observe pump-induced double anticrossings whose gap sizes and center-frequency shifts depend strongly on pump power, indicating a nonlinear mechanism. The anticrossings vanish at high magnetic fields, where energy conservation suppresses three-magnon splitting, thereby identifying the underlying process as three-magnon scattering. We attribute the double anticrossings to nondegenerate three-magnon splitting, which generates two magnon populations at distinct frequencies. Each population forms a standing-wave mode and couples independently to the Kittel mode, giving rise to two effective coupling channels. These results demonstrate that nonlinear magnon interactions can dynamically generate multiple coupling channels within a single system.

Summary. This work investigates how thermal annealing modifies the superconducting properties of non-centrosymmetric NbRe films. By analyzing both magnetotransport and critical field data, the authors demonstrate that annealing significantly amplifies spin-orbit coupling effects. This enhancement provides evidence for a mechanism protecting the unconventional superconductivity beyond standard theoretical bounds.

Why it may be interesting. The direct link established between structural modification (annealing) and the enhancement of a fundamental quantum interaction (spin-orbit coupling) that stabilizes unconventional superconductivity is highly relevant to understanding emergent quantum phenomena in condensed matter systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how thermal annealing affects superconducting and normal-state magnetotransport properties in non-centrosymmetric NbRe films, specifically focusing on the enhancement of spin-orbit effects.

Main result. Annealing significantly enhances spin-orbit-mediated quantum transport by increasing the relative spin-orbit scattering strength, leading to upper critical fields that exceed conventional theoretical limits and confirming strong antisymmetric spin-orbit coupling.

Method. The research combines structural characterization (XRD) with detailed magnetotransport measurements, analyzing data using the Werthamer–Helfand–Hohenberg framework for $B_{c2}(T)$ and the 3D Kawabata weak-localization/weak-anti-localization model.

Model / system. The physical system is $ ext{Nb}{0.18} ext{Re}$ thin films, which are non-centrosymmetric superconductors. The analysis relies on models describing quantum interference effects (WL/WAL) and superconducting pair-breaking mechanisms (WHH).

Key observables. Upper critical field $B_{c2}(T)$, normal-state magnetoconductivity ($\Delta\sigma/\sigma_0$), spin-orbit scattering strength ($B_{so}$), and the ratio $B_{so}/B_\phi$.

Important parameters / regimes. Annealing temperature, crystallite size increase (from $\sim 2$ nm to $\sim 10$ nm), and the observation that $B_{c2}(T)$ surpasses both orbital-limiting and Pauli paramagnetic limits.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that magnetotransport deviations from conventional theory are due to enhanced spin-orbit coupling protecting the superconducting state against Zeeman splitting.

Figures summary. Figures show XRD patterns confirming crystallite growth upon annealing, $R(T)$ curves showing $ ext{T}_c$ changes, and magnetoconductivity plots demonstrating pronounced Weak Anti-Localization (WAL) behavior in annealed samples compared to as-grown ones.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first characterizing structural changes via XRD; then analyzing superconducting properties using $B_{c2}(T)$ within the WHH framework, noting deviations from conventional limits; finally, confirming the underlying mechanism through normal-state magnetotransport analysis using the Kawabata WL/WAL model.

Abstract

$\text{Nb}_{0.18}\text{Re}_{0.82}$ (NbRe) is a non-centrosymmetric superconductor with a transition temperature $T_\mathrm{c}$ reaching $9\text{ K}$ in bulk form. While bulk and single-crystalline NbRe exhibit signatures of multigap superconductivity, thin films generally display a single-gap superconducting state due to structural disorder and reduced crystallite dimensions. Here, we investigate the impact of thermal annealing on the superconducting and normal-state magnetotransport properties of NbRe films. The temperature dependence of the upper critical field, $B_{\mathrm{c2}}(T)$, is analyzed within the microscopic Werthamer--Helfand--Hohenberg (WHH) framework, while the normal-state magnetoconductivity is described using the three-dimensional Kawabata weak-localization/weak-anti-localization model. Annealing drives a pronounced change in the electronic response, manifested by a strong weak anti-localization behavior in the normal state and an upper critical field that surpasses both the conventional orbital-limiting field and the Pauli paramagnetic limit. The microscopic analysis reveals a strong intrinsic increase in the relative spin--orbit scattering strength, with the annealed film showing a significantly enhanced spin--orbit-to-dephasing field ratio. These findings provide direct, independent evidence that thermal modification of the NbRe microstructure successfully amplifies spin--orbit-mediated quantum transport, which acts as the key mechanism protecting the non-centrosymmetric superconducting state against paramagnetic pair-breaking well beyond conventional theoretical boundaries.

Summary. The paper introduces 'pattern chemistry' as a method to describe any dynamical system using the language of chemical reactions and Petri nets. It formalizes this by mapping system states to counts of observable patterns (tokens). The central technical achievement is defining an 'unambiguous' chemistry, which requires that the state update must correspond to a unique, minimal set of underlying transitions.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a highly abstract mathematical formalism connecting general discrete dynamics (like CA) to chemical reaction network theory. The focus on minimizing transition counts for uniqueness is relevant to defining fundamental physical processes in complex many-body or open quantum systems where minimal energy paths are sought.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The work aims to develop a formal framework, termed 'pattern chemistry,' to describe general dynamical systems using terminology analogous to chemical reaction networks.

Main result. The authors propose that for a pattern chemistry to be well-defined and physically meaningful, the transition map must yield a unique minimal set of reactions required to transform the state's observed patterns from time $t$ to time $t+1$.

Method. They model the system using Petri nets and token maps ($\phi$), where the state is mapped to counts of observable 'patterns' (shapes in specific frames of reference). The core method involves enforcing compatibility conditions between the source and target markings derived from the dynamical update function.

Model / system. The general model is an abstract discrete dynamical system $(X, h: X o X)$. Specific examples include analyzing gliders within Conway's Game of Life cellular automaton, which serves as a concrete testbed for the framework.

Key observables. The primary observables are the 'token map' $\phi(x)$, which is a multiset counting occurrences of specific patterns (shapes) across different frames of reference at state $x$.

Important parameters / regimes. The definition relies on specifying the set of possible pattern shapes ($S$) and the frames of reference ($R$), as well as the structure of the underlying Petri net transitions.

Assumptions / limitations. The framework is currently restricted to finite dynamical systems because multisets are defined with finite support. A key assumption for 'unambiguous' chemistry is that the minimal set of required transitions must be unique.

Figures summary. Not specified in detail, but examples involve illustrating how pattern counts (markings) evolve through specific transition rules derived from cellular automaton updates.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the general chemical metaphor for dynamical systems, then formalizing this using Petri nets and token maps ($\phi$), developing the concept of 'unambiguous' chemistry via minimal transitions, and finally testing these concepts on known systems like gliders in Game of Life.

Abstract

Chemistry describes aspects of the universe in terms of molecules and their reactions. In this exploratory work we present a way to describe aspects of any dynamical system in similar terms. To describe a dynamical system in this way three decisions have to be made. The first is how many different "places" there are at which molecules or chemical species can occur; the second is how to determine the species present (or not) at each place; and the third is the set of transitions and reactions that can occur between the species in the various places. For these choices to be compatible with the state update of the dynamical system each state must be able to determine transitions that take the currently occurring molecules to those occurring in the updated state. We also propose an additional requirement that there is always a unique way to choose the least amount of transitions occurring during state updates. We discuss gliders in the game of life cellular and argue that when following their definition of according to Randall Beer they satisfy the additional criterion as well. We also point out some issues with the approach.

Main result. The study achieved a striking, anomalous PL enhancement by demonstrating that the polystyrene-assisted wet transfer process facilitates charge transfer from n-doped MoS2 to graphene, leading to trion-to-exciton conversion in MoS2.

Abstract

Van der Waals MoS2 graphene heterostructures are compelling candidates for high performance electronic and optoelectronic device applications. However, the interlayer charge transfer typically quenches the photoluminescence of monolayer MoS2, limiting the use of these heterostructures in light emitting applications. In this work, we report an anomalous photoluminescence enhancement in n-type monolayer MoS2 by integrating it with monolayer graphene via polystyrene assisted wet transfer process. Photoluminescence spectroscopy reveals a dominant trion to exciton conversion in the heterostructure. Kelvin probe force microscopy shows a 600 meV increase in the work function of MoS2 upon heterostructure formation. This work function shift, together with the higher work function of graphene, signifies electron transfer from MoS2 to graphene. Shifts in the graphene G and 2D Raman modes further corroborate the interlayer charge transfer. DFT based Bader charge analysis quantifies the role of these functional groups in facilitating interlayer charge transfer. Collectively, our findings establish polystyrene assisted wet transfer as a practical interface engineering strategy for enhancing excitonic emission in MoS2 graphene heterostructures, thereby advancing their potential for scalable optoelectronic devices.

Summary. The researchers measured the thermal conductance of topologically non-trivial states (like Chern insulators) in moiré graphene superlattices using advanced noise thermometry. They found that this heat flow is quantized based only on the system's topological Chern number, establishing thermal conductance as a universal diagnostic tool for moiré topology.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a direct, measurable signature—quantized heat flow—that serves as a stringent probe for understanding and verifying exotic topological phases predicted in moiré materials, bridging electrical and thermal transport physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To determine if heat transport in topological states (Quantum Hall, Chern Insulator) realized in moiré superlattices is universal, mirroring the established quantization of electrical transport.

Main result. The thermal conductance ($G_Q$) was found to be quantized in units determined solely by the Chern number ($t$), confirming universal topological heat transport.

Method. Thermal conductance was measured using highly sensitive Johnson-noise thermometry, analyzing excess thermal noise and dissipated power as a function of source current.

Model / system. The study uses bilayer graphene on hexagonal boron nitride (hBN) moiré superlattices, which create Hofstadter spectra hosting various topological states parameterized by Chern numbers ($t$).

Key observables. Thermal conductance ($G_Q$), quantized values relative to the thermal quantum ($\kappa_0 T$), and electron temperature ($T_M$) derived from noise measurements.

Important parameters / regimes. Moiré wavelength ($\sim 14 ext{ nm}$), magnetic field ($B_\perp$), Chern number ($t$), and the heat Coulomb blockade (HCB) effect.

Assumptions / limitations. The quantization is assumed to be robust across different microscopic origins of topological states, though deviations at high $|t|$ are attributed to HCB or measurement limits.

Figures summary. Figures show device schematics, electrical transport measurements identifying QH/CI states, and thermal conductance plots confirming quantized plateaus for various Chern numbers.

Paper structure. The work progresses by first establishing the physical platform (BLG/hBN moiré superlattice) and characterizing topological states via electrical transport. It then details the advanced noise thermometry technique to measure $G_Q$, culminating in the demonstration of universal quantization across different topological regimes.

Abstract

When electrons are subjected simultaneously to a magnetic field and a periodic potential, they form the fractal Hofstadter spectrum, whose topological gaps host quantum Hall and Chern insulating states with distinct Chern numbers. While electrical transport has established the topology of these states, whether their heat transport is likewise universal has remained unexplored. Here, we measure the thermal conductance of quantum Hall, Chern insulator, and interaction-driven symmetry-broken Chern insulator states in a bilayer graphene-hexagonal boron nitride moiré superlattice with a moirè wavelength of $\sim$14 nm using Johnson-noise thermometry. We find that the thermal conductance ($G_Q$) is quantized in units of the thermal conductance quantum ($G_Q = tκ_0T$) and is determined solely by the Chern number ($t$), independent of the microscopic origin of the topological state. By directly revealing universal topological heat transport in Hofstadter bands, our work establishes thermal conductance as a stringent probe of moiré topological matter and provides a route to investigating more exotic phases, including fractional Chern insulators.

Summary. This work determines how solutions to the defocusing Hirota equation evolve over very long times when constrained by a finite-genus algebro-geometric background. Main problem. The paper investigates the long-time asymptotic behavior of solutions to the defocusing Hirota equation when defined on a finite-genus algebro-geometric background.

Method. The analysis heavily relies on the Riemann-Hilbert (RH) formulation combined with the Deift-Zhou nonlinear steepest descent method.

Model / system. The system is governed by the defocusing Hirota equation, a higher-order integrable extension of the NLS equation. The background structure is defined by an algebro-geometric solution on a hyperelliptic Riemann surface of genus n.

Abstract

In this paper, we investigate the long-time asymptotics for the solution of the Cauchy problem of the defocusing Hirota equation on a finite-genus algebro-geometric background in the whole $(x,t)$-half-plane, whose method is mainly based on a Riemann-Hilbert (RH) formulation and Deift-Zhou nonlinear steepest descent method. The critical values of the phase function in the associated RH problem divide the space-time plane into four regions, in which the leading-order term is given by a phase-shifted finite-genus algebro-geometric solution. The subleading behavior depends on the region: the correction is of order $t^{-1/3}$ and is governed by a Painlevé-XXXIV model RH problem in the transition regions; the leading radiation is of order $t^{-1/2}$ in the Zakharov--Manakov region; and the error is $O(t^{-1})$ in the fast-decay region. These results can also be extended to other higher-order members of the AKNS hierarchy.

Fragile Topology is Unstable Under Translation Refinement

Authors: Yoonseok Hwang, Saavanth Velury, Taylor L. Hughes
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.18386v1

Summary. This theoretical work rigorously proves that 'fragile topology' is not fundamentally stable against lattice refinement. By analyzing symmetry-protected band structures using momentum-space data, the authors demonstrate that increasing the unit cell size (supercell construction) allows for an adiabatic deformation that trivializes the topological phase into a simple atomic insulator.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on condensed matter band theory rather than quantum information protocols, the rigorous mathematical tools developed for tracking topological stability under lattice changes (analogous to Hamiltonian perturbations) are highly relevant to understanding robustness in engineered quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the stability of 'fragile topological phases' under lattice enlargement (unit-cell refinement), aiming to distinguish them from stable topological phases or trivial atomic insulators.

Main result. It establishes that fragile topology possesses only finite stability, as all such phases can be shown to become adiabatically connected to an atomic insulator through a sufficiently large supercell enlargement ($N_{sc}=4$ in one example).

Method. The analysis uses a systematic framework involving Hilbert-basis analysis of momentum-space symmetry data ($\mathbf{v} = ext{BR} \cdot \mathbf{m}$) and models unit-cell enlargement as an integer linear map to track the trivialization of topological invariants.

Model / system. The study is applied to crystalline band topology, specifically analyzing 2D wallpaper groups using a tight-binding model framework derived from Wannier orbitals in an electron-positron picture.

Key observables. Symmetry-data vectors ($\mathbf{v}$), multiplicity vectors ($\mathbf{m}$), and the Wilson-loop spectrum $\{ heta_a(\mathbf{k})\}$ are key diagnostics for topological characterization and trivialization.

Important parameters / regimes. The supercell index ($N_{sc}$) is a critical parameter, as increasing it can drive the system toward a trivial state. The symmetry group (e.g., $p2$, $p4mm$) dictates the specific mathematical constraints.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that adiabatic deformations preserve both wallpaper-group symmetry and the bulk energy gap, allowing for the systematic tracking of topological invariants via symmetry data.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate Wyckoff positions, Brillouin zones with high-symmetry momenta, electron-positron configurations showing pair annihilation (trivialization), and supercell structures demonstrating band folding upon enlargement.

Paper structure. The paper develops from establishing the general instability criterion for fragile topology under unit-cell enlargement to providing detailed mathematical machinery using symmetry data vectors. It then applies this framework to specific 2D wallpaper groups, culminating in explicit calculations showing trivialization at finite supercell sizes.

Abstract

Fragile topological phases become trivial upon the addition of suitable trivial bands, distinguishing them from stable topological phases. Nevertheless, various response phenomena and material realizations have been proposed for fragile phases. At the same time, many of these phenomena can also occur in atomic insulators, leaving open the question of what properties are specific to fragile phases. Enlarging the unit cell offers a natural perspective on this question. Band folding increases the number of bands in a manner analogous to adding trivial bands. In this work, we establish a systematic framework for determining the stability of fragile topology under unit-cell enlargement. We first establish a systematic criterion for trivialization under enlargements compatible with space-group symmetry, grounded in a physical electron-positron picture and formulated through a Hilbert-basis analysis of momentum-space symmetry data. We then show that, for all two-dimensional wallpaper groups, with or without spin-orbit coupling and/or time-reversal symmetry, every symmetry-indicated fragile phase is adiabatically connected to an atomic insulator in a suitable finite supercell and can therefore be trivialized by an arbitrarily small symmetry-preserving perturbation. Our results reveal that fragile topology has only finite stability under translation-symmetry refinement. This highlights that the fate of a fragile phase can depend on translation-symmetry-breaking perturbations, such as charge-density-wave ordering, and suggests that physical signatures insensitive to translation refinement are unlikely to uniquely characterize fragile topology.

Abstract

The quadrature scaling of magnetoresistance has been widely adopted as a hallmark of the strange metal state. However, whether this scaling signals quantum criticality or reflects conventional transport behavior remains controversial. Here, by systematically investigating the magnetotransport properties of NiTe2 nanosheets, we demonstrate that the quadrature scaling is not a unique signature of strange metallicity. We find that the scaling holds only when the crossover field , marking the transition from quadratic to linear magnetoresistance, is sufficiently small relative to the applied field range.
