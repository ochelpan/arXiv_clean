Authors: Paweł Matus, Tobias Holder
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.18477v1

Summary. This work develops a sophisticated theoretical framework using elasticity-gauge duality to model two-dimensional Wigner crystals containing various lattice defects. It systematically calculates defect interaction energies and analyzes how the presence of vacancies modifies collective excitations like phonons. The results offer measurable predictions for transport properties, linking measurements of sound speed directly to vacancy density.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on solid-state correlations, the development of 'fracton-elasticity duality' provides powerful mathematical tools for analyzing systems where long-range interactions couple to topological defects, which has parallels in quantum field theory applied to condensed matter.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To systematically study how various lattice defects (vacancies, dislocations, disclinations) affect the effective action, interaction energies, and melting transitions of two-dimensional Wigner crystals.

Main result. The theory provides a comprehensive framework using duality techniques to calculate defect interactions and predicts unusual phonon dispersion relations in the metallic vacancy liquid phase. This framework is applicable to other charged systems like CDWs.

Method. Duality techniques, specifically elasticity-gauge duality, are employed to construct an effective action incorporating elastic and electrostatic contributions from various lattice defects.

Model / system. The system is a two-dimensional Wigner solid (electron gas) confined between metallic gates. The model incorporates the interaction of electrons with vacancies ($ ho_{vac}$) and treats defects as sources for electromagnetic and elastic gauge fields.

Key observables. Phonon dispersion relation, speed of sound ($c_{ph}$), interaction energies between defect pairs, and melting temperature dependence on vacancy density.

Important parameters / regimes. Vacancy density ($ ho_v$), gate separation ($d$), lattice constant ($a$), and the effective mass/density of the vacancy fluid ($m_h$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis uses linear response theory, assumes parabolic dispersion for the Fermi liquid component, and often simplifies by neglecting spin degrees of freedom or assuming infinite bulk elastic moduli for certain limits.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the fundamental types of lattice defects: vacancies, dislocations (with Burgers vector $\mathbf{b}$), and disclinations (with Frank vector $\mathbf{F}$).

Paper structure. The paper builds from defining the general quadratic action incorporating elasticity and electrostatics. It then uses duality transformations to derive a dual action describing defect physics. Subsequent sections analyze specific observables like interaction energies, collective modes (phonons/plasmons), and the dynamics of the vacancy Fermi liquid.

Abstract

The physics of Wigner solids is characterized by an interplay of elasticity and long-range electrostatics, endowing crystal defects with properties distinct from those in charge-neutral crystals. Recent experiments observing lattice melting and Wigner-crystal-adjacent phases in two dimensions necessitate an examination of how defects affect the long-wavelength properties of such solids. Here, we use duality techniques to construct a comprehensive framework for studying the contribution of vacancies and interstitials, dislocations, and disclinations to the effective action of two-dimensional charged crystals. This allows for a systematic investigation of the interaction energies for the different combinations of defect pairs. We further study melting transitions due to defect proliferation, assessing and justifying some of the assumptions present in the literature. In the metallic Wigner crystal phase, characterized by a finite ground state density of vacancies, we find phonons with a dispersion relation that varies in an unusual way with the vacancy density. Consequently, we discuss how thermodynamic properties of such a vacancy Fermi liquid can be probed in measurements of the melting temperature and speed of sound. The field theory developed here can serve as a starting point in the study of anomalous Hall crystals and charge density waves with defects.

Conductivity of the Landau levels of two-dimensional Dirac cones and gapped nodal-rings in the quantum limit under impurity-potentials

Summary. This work theoretically distinguishes the magnetotransport properties of 2D Dirac cones from those of gapped nodal-rings (GNRs) in the presence of impurities and strong magnetic fields. By applying the Kubo-Bastin formalism, the authors predict that GNRs will exhibit pronounced oscillatory behavior in both longitudinal and Hall conductivities, a signature absent in the smoothly varying response of 2D Dirac cones.

Why it may be interesting. The clear differentiation of transport fingerprints between two distinct topological/band structures (Dirac vs. GNR) under disorder provides a powerful theoretical tool to interpret complex magnetotransport experiments in condensed matter systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the dc magnetoconductivity of two-dimensional Dirac cones and gapped nodal-rings (GNRs) when subjected to perpendicular magnetic fields and impurity potentials in the quantum limit.

Main result. The GNR exhibits distinct oscillatory transport signatures, including resonant peaks in $\sigma_{xx}$ and a sawtooth pattern in $\sigma_{xy}$, contrasting sharply with the smooth, monotonic magnetoresistivity found for 2D Dirac cones.

Method. The Kubo-Bastin formalism is employed to calculate transport coefficients by analyzing the Landau level spectrum derived from the respective Hamiltonians.

Model / system. The models include isotropic 2D Dirac cones and Gapped Nodal Rings (GNRs). The analysis considers pointlike, Gaussian, and Yukawa impurity potentials in the ultraquantum limit.

Key observables. Longitudinal conductivity ($\sigma_{xx}$) and Hall conductivity ($\sigma_{xy}$)

Important parameters / regimes. Magnetic field ($B$), dimensionless ratio $ ho = 2 \epsilon_r / \epsilon_c$, and impurity potential type (pointlike, Gaussian, Yukawa).

Assumptions / limitations. Calculations are performed strictly within the ultraquantum limit, assuming a single-band, non-interacting picture.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the LL spectrum of GNR vs. $ ho$, showing its characteristic non-monotonic 'stretched-checkmark' pattern, which dictates the transport differences from Dirac cones.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the theoretical framework using Kubo-Bastin for both systems. It then derives and contrasts the resulting magnetoconductivity signatures: smooth/zero $\sigma_{xy}$ for Dirac vs. oscillatory/sawtooth $\sigma_{xy}$ for GNRs, concluding with explicit formulas for $ ho_{lx}$ based on impurity potential type.

Abstract

We investigate the dc magnetoconductivity of two-dimensional Dirac cones and gapped nodal rings (GNRs) subjected to a perpendicular magnetic field, which quantises the electronic spectrum into Landau levels (LLs). Working in the ultraquantum limit, where only the lowest LL (LLL) is partially occupied, we employ the Kubo--Bastin formalism to compute the transport coefficients for pointlike, Gaussian, and Yukawa impurity-potentials. For the Dirac case, the longitudinal conductivity is field-independent for pointlike impurities and a monotonic function of $B$ for the Gaussian and Yukawa potentials, while the Hall conductivity vanishes identically owing to the particle-hole symmetry of the two neighbouring LLs. The GNR case is qualitatively different: its non-monotonic stretched-checkmark LL spectrum causes the effective LLL to migrate to successively lower indices as the field increases, producing a pronounced oscillatory structure in both conductivities. The longitudinal response develops resonant peaks at LLL degeneracies, while the Hall conductivity traces out a sawtooth pattern with sharp zero-crossings at these same points. These results establish distinct transport fingerprints for the two systems in the extreme quantum limit, and provide a theoretical framework for interpreting magnetotransport experiments on GNRs.

Metric completion of the Bender--Brody--Müller Hamiltonian: dilation spectrum and missing eigenstates

Summary. The paper rigorously analyzes the Hilbert space completion induced by the metric associated with the BBM Hamiltonian, a model linked to the Riemann zeta function zeros. By completing the state space in an $L^2$-based norm, the authors prove that the original candidate eigenfunctions cannot exist within this mathematically defined space. This establishes a significant mathematical obstruction to the initial physical interpretation of the BBM operator.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a deep mathematical constraint on speculative quantum models derived from number theory. For theorists working on spectral problems or non-Hermitian Hamiltonians, it demonstrates how imposing standard functional analysis structures can rule out proposed physical solutions.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper analyzes the Hilbert completion induced by a candidate metric associated with the Bender--Brody--Müller (BBM) Hamiltonian, which is related to finding an operator whose spectrum yields the imaginary parts of the nontrivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function.

Main result. The rigorous $L^2$-based metric completion forces the state space to be equivalent to $L^2(\mathbb{R}^+)$, and this framework proves that the original BBM candidate eigenfunctions do not belong to the completed space, invalidating the initial mechanism for finding point-spectrum states.

Method. The analysis involves defining a pseudo-Hermitian metric $\hat{\eta}$ on the half-line $L^2(\mathbb{R}^+)$ and studying its completion. Techniques include Fourier/Mellin transforms to analyze the metric symbol, spectral theory (dilation generator), and deficiency index calculations.

Model / system. The model centers on the BBM Hamiltonian, formally written as $ ext{H}_{ ext{BBM}} = \Delta^{-1}D\Delta$, where $D$ is the dilation generator. The mathematical framework uses a metric completion based on $\hat{\eta} = \sin^2(\hat{p}/2) = \Delta^\dagger\Delta/4$.

Key observables. The spectrum of the free realization (purely continuous, $\mathbb{R}$), and the failure of the candidate eigenfunctions ($\psi_z$) to satisfy the required $L^2$ shift-difference condition.

Important parameters / regimes. The critical line $\operatorname{Re} z = 1/2$, and the metric degeneracy observed when analyzing the symbol $\eta_0(k) = |1 - e^{-ik}|^2$.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption tested is that a standard $L^2$-based Hilbert completion using the candidate metric can support the required spectral structure.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the metric degeneracy, showing $\eta_0(k)$ vanishing at specific points and the associated norm of probe functions vanishing linearly in the small-width limit.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by defining the BBM Hamiltonian and its associated metric. It then analyzes the properties of this metric completion, establishing that the resulting space is $L^2(\mathbb{R}^+)$. Finally, it tests the original physical conjecture (the existence of $\psi_z$) within this mathematically rigorous framework.

Abstract

The Bender--Brody--Müller (BBM) Hamiltonian was proposed as a non-Hermitian Hilbert--Pólya operator. We analyze the Hilbert completion induced, on the standard half-line $L^2$ core, by BBM's candidate metric $\hatη=\sin^2(\hat p/2)=Δ^\daggerΔ/4$. The form $η_0=Δ^\daggerΔ$ is positive with trivial kernel but is not coercive. Completing $C_c^\infty(0,\infty)$ in the norm $\|ψ\|_{η_0}=\|Δψ\|$ gives a Hilbert space canonically unitarily equivalent to $L^2(\mathbb R_+)$. Its free self-adjoint realization is the dilation generator, with simple, purely absolutely continuous spectrum $\mathbb R$. The analysis yields two spectral statements of interest beyond the BBM problem. First, no bounded sandwich $Δ^\dagger h(D)Δ$ is boundedly invertible. Second, the transported symmetric operator has deficiency indices $(\infty,\infty)$ and an adjoint with every real point as an eigenvalue of infinite multiplicity, while its free extension is purely continuous. The realization-independent BBM conclusion concerns the candidate eigenfunctions: $Δψ_z=x^{-z}$, so for $\operatorname{Re}z=1/2$ they do not belong to the completed space. Thus the original BBM boundary-condition/eigenfunction mechanism cannot produce point-spectrum Riemann-zero states in this $L^2$-based metric completion.

Summary. This paper introduces QEMOO, a novel multi-round quantum framework designed to tackle challenging multi-objective combinatorial optimization problems mapped onto Ising spin glasses. By iteratively refining the search using warm-started QAOA and adaptive direction updates, it achieves superior coverage of the true Pareto front compared to single-pass methods. This demonstrates a practical path toward efficient quantum computation for complex trade-off analysis.

Why it may be interesting. The use of QAOA on spin-glass Hamiltonians places this work directly at the intersection of quantum algorithms for optimization and disordered systems physics. The focus on iterative refinement (warm-start) is relevant to understanding how quantum search can overcome local minima in complex energy landscapes.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The challenge of multi-objective combinatorial optimization (MOCO), which requires finding the Pareto-optimal trade-off front among conflicting objectives.

Main result. The proposed QEMOO framework significantly improves the hypervolume approximation of the Pareto front compared to single-pass quantum methods, especially in resource-constrained regimes.

Method. QEMOO is a multi-round protocol combining Pareto-based selection with warm-started QAOA sampling and an adaptive direction update scheme inspired by PBI.

Model / system. The problem is mapped onto Ising spin-glass Hamiltonians, requiring the identification of trade-off solutions across multiple conflicting objectives.

Key observables. Hypervolume (HV) of the approximated Pareto front; Number of unique non-dominated solutions (ND).

Important parameters / regimes. Total shot budget ($s_{total}$); Warm-start coefficient ($c$); Bond dimension ($\chi$).

Assumptions / limitations. The PBI method requires linearization to maintain compatibility with the two-body Hamiltonian structure of near-term quantum processors.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate QEMOO's multi-round process, performance heatmaps comparing different weight pool geometries, and scaling plots showing HV gain vs. shot budget or bond dimension.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the MOCO problem mapped to Ising models, details the limitations of single-pass quantum approaches, proposes the QEMOO framework (multi-round protocol with adaptive updates), benchmarks its performance across various weight pool geometries and resource budgets, and analyzes the role of warm-starting.

Abstract

Multi-objective combinatorial optimization requires identifying Pareto-optimal trade-off solutions among conflicting objectives, often making it more demanding than its single-objective counterpart. Although quantum multi-objective optimization methods have begun to emerge, most existing quantum optimization workflows are still built around single-objective or fixed-scalarization settings. Building on existing weighted-sum QAOA approaches to quantum multi-objective optimization, we propose QEMOO, a quantum-enhanced multi-objective optimization framework that combines Pareto-based selection and warm-started QAOA sampling in a multi-round protocol under the same total shot budget. We further introduce a PBI-inspired adaptive direction-update scheme to improve coverage in strongly conflicting benchmark regimes. Across three benchmark stages, QEMOO improves Pareto-front hypervolume over the single-pass weighted-sum QAOA baseline under matched shot budgets, suggesting a practical route toward shot-efficient quantum-assisted multi-objective optimization and its future applications.

Absence of hidden analytic conserved quantities in harmonically confined rods

Authors: Sahil Kumar Singh, Abhishek Dhar, Sanjay Moudgalya
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.18872v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: non-equilibrium dynamics 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. The paper rigorously analyzes the existence of hidden conserved quantities in hard rods confined by a harmonic trap. By imposing symmetry constraints derived from free motion and collisions, the authors demonstrate that for finite rods, only two independent conserved quantities exist. A notable exception is the point particle limit, where more integrals are found.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous, systematic methodology for determining the full set of classical integrals of motion in complex interacting systems, which is foundational knowledge applicable to understanding integrability breaking in quantum many-body physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates whether a system of hard rods confined in a harmonic trap possesses hidden analytic conserved quantities beyond the known total energy and center-of-mass energy.

Main result. For general hard rods, conservation laws impose symmetry constraints ($ ext{U}(1)$ and $ ext{S}_N$) that force any conserved quantity to be functionally dependent only on the two known integrals of motion. However, for point particles (zero length), a larger set of such quantities is found.

Method. The authors use systematic algebraic methods by requiring that potential conserved quantities must remain invariant under symmetries dictated by free motion ($ ext{U}(1)$) and collisions ($ ext{S}_N$).

Model / system. The system consists of $N$ hard rods of equal length confined in a one-dimensional harmonic trap. The dynamics are analyzed for free motion, elastic collisions, and the special case where all rods are point particles.

Key observables. Conserved quantities (integrals of motion), ergodicity, and thermalization behavior.

Important parameters / regimes. Harmonic trap frequency ($\omega$), rod lengths ($a_i$), number of rods ($N$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is strictly limited to finding analytic conserved quantities. The comparison between finite-length rods and point particles highlights a crucial distinction in the symmetry requirements.

Figures summary. Not specified by the notes, but the text references Table 1 summarizing symmetry constraints for different dynamics (Free motion, Point particles, SMED & Rods with non-zero length).

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes four distinct dynamical regimes: free motion; collisions with finite rods; zero-length point particles; and Stochastic Momentum Exchange Dynamics (SMED), applying symmetry constraints in each case to determine the structure of conserved quantities.

Abstract

Systems of hard rods of equal length in a one-dimensional harmonic trap have been observed to exhibit peculiar non-ergodic behavior that might suggest the existence of a novel hidden conserved quantity beyond the two well known ones, i.e., the total energy and the center-of-mass energy. In this work, we investigate this possibility by systematically constraining the forms of the conserved quantities, and we rigorously rule out the existence of any extra hidden conserved quantity that is analytic in the positions and momenta of the rods involved. We do so by showing two key results: conservation during free motion demands the $U(1)$ invariance of these quantities under rotations of the position and momenta of each rod, and conservation during collisions demand an $S_N$ invariance under the permutation of the momenta of the rods as long as one of the rods have non-zero length. We then show that these conditions imply that any conserved quantity is functionally dependent on the two known conserved quantities. In addition, we show that in the special case where all rods have zero length (i.e., when they are point particles), conservation under collisions only requires invariance under a smaller $S_N$ group of permutations of the labels of the rods, which leads to a much larger set of analytic conserved quantities that we explicitly write down. In all, this rigorously clarifies the structure of conserved quantities in the hard rod problem, and motivates the application of such systematic methods to other classical systems.

Summary. By achieving high accuracy relative to numerical benchmarks, it provides valuable theoretical tools for understanding atomic structure within plasma diagnostics.

Abstract

We investigate the bound state properties of the hydrogen-like atoms in the radial screened Coulomb potential (RSCP). using three complementary analytical approaches - expectation values with Coulomb and Kratzer reference states, variational optimization with a scaled Kratzer basis, and the Hellmann-Feynman theorem - we derive approximate energy eigenvalues as function of the screening parameter c. Benchmarked against high-precision generalized pseudospectral data, the expectation value-approach with the Kratzer basis achieves relative errors of 0.63% for the first ten s-states at $c=0.1$, while the variational method improves this further. The formalism extends naturally to Positronium, demonstrating its generality for arbitrary reduced-mass systems. The complementary biases of the methods provide robust error estimation for plasma-embedded atoms.

Summary. This paper introduces MOSAIQC, a novel framework designed to overcome limitations in running large quantum algorithms on current noisy hardware by intelligently cutting and remapping circuits. It achieves this through a hybrid optimization process that jointly considers gate/wire cuts, hardware noise profiles, and sampling overhead. The results show substantial improvements in both speed and solution quality compared to existing methods.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on quantum computation, the rigorous development of scalable heuristic optimization techniques for resource allocation (like circuit partitioning) shares methodological parallels with optimizing complex physical simulations or network mapping in AMO physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is developing a scalable method for quantum circuit cutting to execute large algorithms on current, resource-limited Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) hardware.

Main result. MOSAIQC demonstrates superior trade-offs in run speed and solution quality by jointly optimizing sampling overhead, hardware placement, and mixed gate/wire cuts, achieving significant reductions in computational overhead.

Method. The framework uses a hybrid approach combining global heuristics (like METIS) for initial partitioning with local refinement optimization (Tabu Search), incorporating noise-informed cost functions.

Model / system. The system involves mapping large quantum circuits onto smaller, potentially heterogeneous quantum processors. The circuit connectivity and hardware topology are modeled as weighted graphs to calculate cutting costs.

Key observables. Local fidelity improvement (+19.56% $\pm$ 6.17%), runtime speedup ($2.88 imes$), and massive reduction in sampling overhead ($ ext{average } 5.83 \cdot 10^{11} imes$).

Important parameters / regimes. Qubit count (scaling from 6 to 1000+), cut types (gate vs. wire), hardware connectivity, and noise characteristics.

Assumptions / limitations. The framework assumes the ability to convert quantum circuit objects into lightweight graph structures for efficient computation; existing heuristics often fail to jointly optimize all necessary parameters.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate performance comparisons across various benchmark circuits (e.g., QAOA, Ising VQE) at increasing qubit scales, showing runtime and sampling overhead results for different cut placement strategies.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem of circuit cutting limitations, details the novel MOSAIQC framework using graph representations and a hybrid optimization strategy, presents specific algorithms for wire/gate cut cost calculation, and concludes with extensive comparative results across multiple scales and benchmarks.

Abstract

Current quantum computers do not yet have the required qubit resources to meet the demands of most practical quantum algorithms. To circumvent this constraint, the practice of dividing these algorithms into parts through quantum circuit cutting has been explored. Many of these works either show exponential scaling or are far from optimal solutions. In this paper, MosaiQC is presented as a novel framework to improve upon existing circuit cutting frameworks. A hybrid warmstart with refinement optimization is used to find cutting solutions, allowing the combination of both wire and gate cuts. Additionally, MosaiQC enables hardware partitions of mixed sizes. Furthermore, the refinement stage incorporates a fast approximate quadratic assignment solver to better place hardware partitions, demonstrating a mean local fidelity improvement of $19.56 \% \pm 6.17\%$ over the baseline algorithm. In runtime and sampling overhead costs, improvements of $2.88 \times$ and an average of $16.84\%$ cut reduction (resulting in an average $5.83 \cdot 10^{11} \times$ overhead reduction) are observed. MosaiQC demonstrates a superior trade-off for run speed and solution quality, while adding fundamental features excluded by most competitors. With this, MosaiQC demonstrates that scalable heuristic optimization can substantially reduce the computational overhead of circuit-cut placement for increasingly large quantum circuits.

Summary. This work proves that inherent topological features of real power grids cause the necessary matrices for solving power flow problems to become extremely ill-conditioned. This structural limitation forces both classical and quantum computation time requirements to scale poorly with grid size, effectively precluding a general end-to-end quantum advantage for these critical engineering tasks.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on electrical engineering, the core mathematical tools—analyzing computational bottlenecks via matrix conditioning and structural graph properties—are highly relevant to understanding resource limitations in simulating complex quantum many-body systems or analyzing quantum network architectures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates whether quantum computers can achieve an end-to-end computational advantage over classical methods when solving large-scale power flow problems.

Main result. Structural properties of realistic electrical grids, such as graph separators and long corridors, cause the underlying mathematical matrices to become severely ill-conditioned, precluding a general quantum advantage for these tasks.

Method. The analysis combines rigorous graph theory (analyzing network topology) with quantum complexity theory (query and readout bounds) to establish lower bounds on computational difficulty.

Model / system. The model is an electrical transmission network represented by graphs $G=(V, E)$, whose core mathematical structure involves the susceptance Laplacian matrix. The problems analyzed include DC/AC Power Flow, Optimal Power Flow, and Unit Commitment.

Key observables. Pseudo condition number ($\kappa_+(B)$), which dictates the required computational resources for solving linear systems derived from network physics.

Important parameters / regimes. Network size ($n$), graph separators, corridor lengths ($\ell$), and quantum complexity bounds related to $\epsilon$ (precision).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that realistic grid topologies exhibit structural limitations like separators or corridors, and it proves the lack of advantage at every readout level.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by analyzing the condition number growth based on network topology (separators vs. corridors), demonstrating that this growth forces quantum query complexity to exceed classical scaling, and then generalizing these obstructions across various power flow formulations.

Abstract

This letter proves realistic grid properties limit the applicability of quantum computers for power flow. Grids that split into two large regions meeting at only a few buses, common in transmission networks, force the pseudo condition number of the DC susceptance matrix to grow polynomially in the network size, and long chains of lines bridging such regions force quadratic growth, making recent empirical observations rigorous. The bounds also hold with overwhelming probability for arbitrary bounded random line susceptances. Combined with query and tomography lower bounds, this precludes end-to-end quantum advantage for DC power flow at every readout level, and these obstructions persist through AC power flow, optimal power flow, and unit commitment. All proofs are formally verified with accompanying Lean 4 source code.

Model / system. The system models quantum walks governed by the Grover walk operator $U$ on a normal Cayley graph $ ext{Cay}(\Gamma, S)$ over a finite group $\Gamma$. The state space is defined over the symmetric arcs of this graph.

Abstract

A Cayley graph $\operatorname{Cay}(Γ,S)$ over a finite group $Γ$ is said to be normal if its connection set $S$ is a union of some conjugacy classes of $Γ$. This paper investigates perfect state transfer in Grover walks on normal Cayley graphs. The Grover walk is a widely studied discrete-time quantum walk. We establish a necessary and sufficient condition for the occurrence of perfect state transfer on normal Cayley graphs. As applications, we derive explicit spectral criteria for perfect state transfer on Cayley graphs over abelian groups, dicyclic groups, and dihedral groups. These results yield several infinite families of Cayley graphs exhibiting perfect state transfer. We further obtain simple combinatorial characterizations of the existence of perfect state transfer on Cayley graphs over dihedral and dicyclic groups. Our general characterization also recovers a number of previously known results as special cases. As a further consequence, we obtain a complete characterization of perfect state transfer on unitary Cayley graphs. In particular, we prove that exactly four graphs in the class of unitary Cayley graphs exhibit perfect state transfer.
