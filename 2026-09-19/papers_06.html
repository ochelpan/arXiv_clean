Statistics, 't Hooft Anomaly, and the Else-Nayak Index: a careful comparison of concepts

Authors: Hanyu Xue
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.20813v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical paper rigorously compares the mathematical concepts of symmetry anomalies and the statistics of topological excitations. It argues that previous analogies linking the two are flawed because the derived invariants generally disagree. The work establishes a robust framework using gauge theory to clarify the necessary conditions for a consistent relationship between symmetries and topological order.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical machinery developed here—comparing topological invariants derived from different physical principles (symmetry vs. excitation)—provides a powerful, abstract tool for understanding constraints in strongly correlated systems where symmetries and topological order are key.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to establish a rigorous and reliable relationship between generalized symmetries (and their anomalies) and the statistics of topological excitations in quantum field theories.

Main result. The authors argue that the statistics and the anomaly derived from symmetry transformations and topological excitations generally do not match, suggesting that the simple identification of the two concepts is flawed.

Method. The analysis uses advanced concepts from algebraic topology, specifically comparing cocycles derived from symmetry patch operators (related to the Else-Nayak index) with those derived from the connectivity of topological spaces associated with hopping operators.

Model / system. The framework is highly abstract, involving lattice truncations, symmetry patch operators, and topological excitations defined on manifolds like $S^d$. It utilizes concepts from gauge theory by coupling boundary matter to a DW gauge field in one higher dimension.

Key observables. The Else-Nayak index, the 't Hooft anomaly, the statistics $\pi_0 M_q(X, G)$, and the filling pattern $F$ which tracks realizable pairs of $( ext{stat}, ext{anom})$.

Important parameters / regimes. The dimension $d$, the structure of the Abelian group $G$, and the level of the cocycles (e.g., $H^3(BG, \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on abstracting axioms for topological excitations and making specific assumptions about the commutation relations of operators in the presence of gauge fields.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first identifying the conceptual pitfalls in relating symmetries and statistics, then developing a rigorous framework using cochain complexes and gauge theory to calculate both quantities independently, and finally comparing the resulting invariants to show they are generally distinct.

Abstract

Generalized symmetries and topological excitations, as well as symmetry anomalies and the statistics of topological excitations, are widely believed to be related. There are, however, pitfalls in how this relation is established. A lattice truncation of a symmetry transformation to a finite patch gives a symmetry patch operator that creates symmetry defects at its boundary. This geometric picture resembles a hopping operator creating topological excitations at the boundary of its support, but does not provide well-defined statistics, let alone guarantee agreement with the symmetry anomaly. A more natural and robust relation is that the hopping operators of topological excitations are symmetric: they commute with symmetry transformations. Under suitable assumptions, this condition yields a one-to-one correspondence between statistics and anomalies. We further couple boundary matter to a DW gauge field in one higher dimension to explain this relation from the perspective of gauging. A hopping operator is, in essence, a gauge-invariant operator acting on the physical degrees of freedom after gauging. Once the gauge-field background is fixed, global symmetry comes from gauge transformations that preserve that background, while the symmetric condition on hopping is precisely the remaining gauge invariance. This distinction clarifies potential misconceptions in the literature and provides a more reliable framework for comparing symmetries and topological excitations.

Abstract

Valley-Helical Superconductivity Driven by Repulsion and Quantum Geometry: Applications to Time-Reversal Symmetric Rhombohedral Graphene

Summary. This theoretical study explores how strong electron-electron repulsion can drive a unique valley-helical superconducting state in graphene, even when time-reversal symmetry is preserved. By comparing intravalley and intervalley pairing instabilities using RPA, the authors show that the valley-helical state becomes dominant under specific coupling conditions. This work highlights the role of quantum geometry and repulsive interactions in stabilizing exotic superconducting phases.

Why it may be interesting. The mechanism of intravalley pairing overcoming intervalley pairing due to 'residual repulsion poisoning' is a sophisticated many-body effect relevant to understanding unconventional superconductivity in low-dimensional materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the possibility of realizing chiral, valley-helical superconductivity in time-reversal symmetric graphene systems without breaking valley polarization or time-reversal symmetry.

Main result. The analysis shows that overscreened repulsion (Kohn-Luttinger mechanism) can induce a valley-helical superconducting state where intravalley pairing outcompetes conventional intervalley pairing at intermediate coupling.

Method. The research employs Random-Phase Approximation (RPA) calculations to solve linearized gap equations, analyzing the competition between different pairing channels.

Model / system. The primary system is time-reversal symmetric rhombohedral graphene (RNG), modeled using a tight-binding Hamiltonian. The analysis focuses on the interplay between intravalley and intervalley pairing channels.

Key observables. Critical transition temperatures ($T_c^{ ext{intra}}$ vs $T_c^{ ext{inter}}$), the difference $\Delta T_c$, and the topological nature of the resulting edge modes.

Important parameters / regimes. Intermediate coupling strength, trigonal warping strength ($\xi_{tri}$), and the number of graphene layers ($4 \le N \le 8$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on RPA for pairing interactions and assumes certain symmetries (e.g., continuous rotation symmetry in both valleys) for simplification.

Figures summary. Figures compare the competition between intravalley and intervalley SC transition temperatures, demonstrating the dominance of the intravalley channel when repulsion exceeds a threshold set by trigonal warping.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by setting up the Hamiltonian and gap equations, calculating pairing interactions via RPA, and then analyzing the competition between intravalley and intervalley instabilities to establish the conditions for valley-helical SC.

Abstract

Motivated by the recent experimental signatures of chiral superconductivity in valley-polarized graphene- and transition-metal-dichalcogenide-based systems, we investigate the possibility of chiral pairing in the absence of valley polarization or time-reversal symmetry breaking. For systems with opposite Berry curvature in the two valleys, we find that overscreened repulsion (i.e., the Kohn-Luttinger mechanism) can indeed induce pairing of opposite chiralities in each valley, producing a valley-helical state. Moreover, the valley-helical state can outcompete more conventional intervalley paired states at intermediate coupling, despite only the latter having a weak-coupling instability. Using a realistic model of time-reversal-symmetric rhombohedral graphene, we find extended ranges of fillings and displacement fields with dominant valley-helical superconductivity. This occurs for all layer numbers considered here, $4\leq N\leq 8$. We show that the valley-helical states can display topologically projected edge modes, unusual magnetic responses, and intertwined Kekulé-like bond-order; features that can be used to distinguish them from more conventional superconductors.

A Bi-Objective Routing Framework for Hybrid Terrestrial-Satellite Quantum Networks

Authors: Yashpreet Khambay, Nitish K. Panigrahy
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.19561v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops a bi-objective routing framework for quantum networks combining terrestrial fiber and satellite links. It formulates the path selection as a Pareto optimization problem, jointly optimizing both entanglement generation rate and fidelity. The resulting framework allows network controllers to select the optimal path based on the specific trade-off required by the end application.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical formulation of multi-objective optimization (Pareto front) applied to quantum communication channels is highly relevant for designing robust quantum network protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the limitation of existing quantum network routing algorithms that optimize only a single metric, proposing a framework to jointly optimize both End-to-End Entanglement Generation Rate (EGR) and fidelity.

Main result. The proposed bi-objective framework successfully models the trade-off, showing that hybrid terrestrial-satellite networks yield substantially richer Pareto frontiers than terrestrial-only links, allowing applications to select paths based on specific utility requirements.

Method. The problem is formulated as a Pareto optimization problem and mathematically transformed into a MAXMIN–MINSUM bicriterion path problem, enabling the exact polynomial-time computation of the minimal complete Pareto set using Martins' bicriterion routing algorithm.

Model / system. The system is a hybrid quantum network combining static terrestrial fiber links with time-varying virtual links provided by Low Earth Orbit (LEO) satellites. Entanglement is distributed across these mixed links.

Key observables. End-to-End Entanglement Generation Rate (EGR) and the fidelity of the distributed entanglement.

Important parameters / regimes. The network structure includes terrestrial links (limited by attenuation) and satellite links (time-varying visibility). Performance is evaluated across 24 hours.

Assumptions / limitations. Terrestrial nodes are assumed to have perfect quantum memories, and the satellite layer assumes a dual downlink architecture without inter-satellite links.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the trade-off between EGR and fidelity for different routing policies (Pareto vs. single-objective), and one figure compares the aggregate utility (e.g., Negativity, SKR) achieved by various policies.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the bi-objective problem, models the hybrid network using time-varying graphs, derives the MAXMIN-MINSUM transformation, and then applies Martins' algorithm to compute the Pareto set, concluding with performance comparisons against single-objective baselines.

Abstract

Hybrid terrestrial-satellite quantum networks combine terrestrial fiber infrastructure with free-space links to enable long-distance entanglement distribution. Entanglement can be routed over different combinations of terrestrial and satellite links, resulting in paths with different entanglement generation rates (EGRs) and fidelities. Existing routing algorithms, however, typically reduce routing to a single-objective problem by optimizing either EGR or fidelity, or by optimizing one while constraining the other, and thus do not explicitly capture the trade-off between the two. In this paper, we present a bi-objective routing framework for hybrid quantum networks that jointly optimizes end-to-end EGR and fidelity. We formulate routing as a Pareto optimization problem and show that it possesses a special mathematical structure: the bottleneck nature of end-to-end EGR and the multiplicative nature of end-to-end fidelity allow the problem to be transformed into a MAXMIN-MINSUM bi-criterion path problem. This transformation enables the exact polynomial-time computation of a minimal complete Pareto set using Martins' bicriterion routing algorithm. Our results demonstrate that hybrid networks expose substantially richer Pareto frontiers than terrestrial-only networks and that the proposed framework outperforms representative single-objective routing policies by allowing applications to select paths based on their EGR and fidelity requirements.

Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.20690v1

Summary. This paper provides a rigorous counterexample to the quantum Hedetniemi conjecture, which relates the quantum chromatic number of a graph product to the minimum of the factors' quantum chromatic numbers. By constructing specific graphs and using advanced algebraic techniques involving $C^*$-algebras, the authors demonstrate that the conjecture fails for several related graph parameters.

Why it may be interesting. While the topic is graph theory, the use of $C^*$-algebras and projection operators connects it to algebraic quantum theory, which shares mathematical tools with quantum information theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to disprove the quantum Hedetniemi conjecture, which posits that the quantum chromatic number of a graph product equals the minimum of the quantum chromatic numbers of its factors.

Main result. The authors construct explicit finite graphs $G$ and $H$ for which the quantum chromatic number of the product, $\chi_q(G imes H)$, is strictly less than the minimum of the individual quantum chromatic numbers, $\min\{\chi_q(G), \chi_q(H)\}$.

Method. The proof involves adapting constructions from graph theory (related to Zhu's counterexample) and applying algebraic techniques using colorings defined by projections in a $C^*$-algebra, leading to explicit integer bounds.

Model / system. The study is purely mathematical, focusing on graph theory and algebraic graph parameters like the quantum chromatic number ($\chi_q$) and its $C^*$-algebraic variants ($\chi_C^*$).

Key observables. The primary observables are the graph parameters $\chi_q(G imes H)$, $\chi_q(G)$, and $\chi_q(H)$, with specific numerical counterexamples provided (e.g., $\chi(G imes H) \le 1538 < 1539 = \min\{\chi(G), \chi(H)\}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The parameters involve graph structures derived from Cayley graphs and the Lovász theta number of the complement ($ar{\vartheta}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the relationship $\chi_C^*(X) \le \pi(X) \le \chi(X)$ for various graph parameters $\pi$, and the formalization of the coloring problem using projections in a $C^*$-algebra.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the quantum coloring game setup, while Figure 2 details the projective formulation of quantum coloring using required projections in an algebra $A$.

Paper structure. The paper develops the problem by defining the quantum chromatic number and its variants, presenting the conjecture, and then constructing counterexamples using specific graph products and algebraic matrix inequalities to show the conjecture fails for multiple related parameters.

Abstract

Godsil, Roberson, Šámal and Severini conjectured that the quantum chromatic number of the categorical product of two graphs equals the minimum of the quantum chromatic numbers of the factors. We disprove this conjecture: we construct explicit finite graphs $G,H$ with \[ χ(G\times H) \leq 1538 < 1539 = \min(χ_q(G),χ_q(H)).\]The graphs are obtained from Zhu's counterexample to Hedetniemi's conjecture by using a base graph for which the Lovász theta number of the complement, and not only the fractional chromatic number, is large. The lower bound for the first factor is the theta bound. For the second factor we adapt Zhu's argument to projections that do not commute: the step that fixes the colors of a clique is replaced by identities between operators. Both lower bounds hold for colorings by projections in an arbitrary nonzero unital $C^*$-algebra. Hence the conjecture also fails for the spatial, approximate, commuting-operator and $C^*$-algebraic variants of the quantum chromatic number. We also give smaller counterexamples certified by exact integer data. The graph constructions, the certificates and the counterexample statements in the projective formulation are formalized in Lean~4.

Achieving the limits of automorphism gates

Authors: Jin Ming Koh, Shayan Majidy, Aranya Chakraborty, Anqi Gong, Shi Jie Samuel Tan, Norman Y. Yao
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.19250v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops a comprehensive theory characterizing the logical power and resource cost of using automorphism gates in quantum error correction. It shows that while automorphisms can generate large logical groups, the required physical qubit overhead scales exponentially for CX gates but only polynomially for diagonal gates. This establishes a critical resource trade-off for building scalable quantum computers.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides fundamental theoretical limits on resource overhead in quantum computation, directly impacting the design of fault-tolerant quantum hardware architectures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops a complete theory to characterize the maximum logical gate group and the minimum physical code size required to achieve it using automorphism gates in fault-tolerant quantum computing.

Main result. The sharpest physical-qubit cost divide for achieving logical gates is between diagonal and CX-type gates, not between Clifford and non-Clifford gates. Furthermore, achieving the maximum automorphism group requires an exponentially large number of physical qubits ($\Theta(2^k)$) compared to diagonal gates ($\Theta(k^2)$).

Method. The authors use theoretical group theory, analyzing the structure of logical groups generated by physical single-qubit Clifford gates and qubit permutations within the framework of stabilizer codes.

Model / system. The study focuses on fault-tolerant quantum computation implemented using stabilizer codes, specifically analyzing general, CSS, and related subclasses of codes encoding $k$ logical qubits.

Key observables. The largest attainable logical gate group, the minimum physical qubit cost ($n$), and the number of logical qubits ($k$).

Important parameters / regimes. The number of logical qubits ($k$), the physical qubit cost ($n$), and the type of physical gates used (e.g., single-qubit Cliffords, CX gates).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the code is indecomposable and relies on characterizing the automorphism group $ ext{AutL}(C)$ relative to transversal and permutation logical gates.

Figures summary. Figures summarize the comparison of logical gate implementations and their associated physical-qubit costs for different gate sets (e.g., $\Theta(2k)$ for CX vs. $\Theta(k^2)$ for CZ).

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first defining the problem using automorphism gates, then deriving bounds and constructing codes for specific gate sets (S, CX, CZ) across various code classes (CSS, PSD, SSD), culminating in a comparison of the physical cost scaling.

Abstract

Universal fault-tolerant quantum computing combines versatile but expensive operations with specialized but cheap ones. Its efficiency depends on how much computation can be pushed onto the cheap operations and on the size of the code needed to do so. Automorphism gates provide such cheap operations using only physical single-qubit Clifford gates and qubit permutations. Yet no general theory characterizes their maximum logical power or the minimum code size needed to attain it. We develop such a theory. For stabilizer codes encoding $k\geq3$ logical qubits, we show that the largest logical group attainable by automorphisms is generated by all addressable $S$ and $\mathrm{CX}$ gates, and we construct codes attaining it. While this group contains exponentially fewer gates than the full Clifford group, adding one suitable non-Clifford gate yields universality. We further classify the largest logical groups attainable using qubit permutations, physical single-qubit Cliffords, or both across general stabilizer and CSS codes, and derive refined bounds for self-dual CSS subclasses. Achieving the maximum-size logical group through automorphisms requires $n=Θ(2^k)$ physical qubits. By contrast, all addressable diagonal Clifford gates, generated by $S$ and $\mathrm{CZ}$, require only $n=Θ(k^2)$ physical qubits when implemented using physical single-qubit Cliffords alone. Both bounds are tight. This polynomial qubit cost extends beyond Cliffords to all addressable diagonal gates at any fixed level of the Clifford hierarchy, using physical single-qubit diagonal gates. Thus, for full addressability, the sharpest physical-qubit cost divide lies between diagonal and $\mathrm{CX}$-type gates, not between Clifford and non-Clifford gates.

Abstract

The concept of causal (non)separability describes whether the causal order between parties that perform local quantum operations is well-defined or indefinite. Coherent error threshold for quantum LDPC codes

Authors: Zhengyi Han, Yuanchen Zhao, Yijia Xu, Yixu Wang, Zi-Wen Liu
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.20537v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper establishes a nonzero code capacity threshold for quantum LDPC codes against coherent noise, resolving a key challenge in fault tolerance theory. Using a novel 'cluster resummation' technique, the authors prove that the logical error decays exponentially with code distance for noise below this threshold. This provides a strong theoretical foundation for building fault-tolerant quantum technologies.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides rigorous, general robustness guarantees for qLDPC codes against coherent noise, which is crucial for designing fault-tolerant quantum computation protocols that must handle realistic, structured noise sources.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of determining the code capacity threshold for quantum Low-Density Parity Check (qLDPC) codes when subjected to coherent errors, which are fundamentally different from standard stochastic Pauli noise.

Main result. It proves that general qLDPC codes possess a nonzero code capacity threshold against local coherent noise, ensuring that the logical error decays exponentially with the code distance for noise strengths below this threshold.

Method. The core technique is 'cluster resummation,' which allows the authors to exactly resum error contributions from large connected error clusters in the channel expansion, leading to exponential suppression bounds.

Model / system. The system involves quantum error correction using qLDPC codes. The noise is modeled by a local coherent error $U$ acting on the qubits, followed by syndrome measurement and recovery.

Key observables. Logical error measured in diamond distance ($\lVert \cdot Vert_{\diamond}$), and the existence of a nonzero code capacity threshold ($\eta_{th}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Code distance $d = \Omega(\log n)$, noise strength $\eta$, and the comparison between optimal recovery and minimum-weight decoding.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the noise is described by a constant-depth unitary circuit with bounded operator norm generators, and the code distance must scale at least logarithmically with the system size.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the cluster resummation process by showing how an error configuration is decomposed into a large connected cluster and smaller, resummed components.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by defining the coherent noise model, developing the proof for optimal recovery using cluster resummation, and then extending the analysis to minimum-weight decoding, comparing the resulting thresholds.

Abstract

A key appeal of quantum low-density parity check (qLDPC) codes is their ability to suppress stochastic Pauli noise below nonzero thresholds. Coherent errors are fundamentally different: they produce superpositions of error patterns whose amplitudes can interfere even after syndrome measurement. Rigorous understanding of coherent errors remains limited. Here we show that general qLDPC codes admit a nonzero code capacity threshold against local coherent noise and more generally local channel noise. For any family of qLDPC codes with distance $d=Ω(\log n)$, we show that there is a constant noise strength below which the logical recovery error in diamond distance decays exponentially with the code distance. The result is established for optimal recovery as well as the minimum-weight decoder. The key technical ingredient is what we call a \emph{cluster resummation}: rather than bounding superposed error configurations one by one, we isolate a large connected error cluster in the channel expansion and exactly resum all errors disconnected from it before taking norms. Standard cluster counting then yields exponential suppression. This work resolves a longstanding challenge in fault tolerance theory, providing general robustness guarantees for qLDPC codes against coherent noise and laying a rigorous foundation for future studies of fault-tolerant quantum technologies.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how external pressure influences the structural, vibrational, and magnetic properties of Eu-doped LaCrO3 to understand the underlying structure-property correlations.

Main result. The research reveals a pressure-induced structural phase transition from orthorhombic to rhombohedral symmetry around 10.6 GPa, alongside a distinct anomaly at 4.5 GPa suggesting the onset of long-range antiferromagnetic ordering coupled to structural changes.

Method. The investigation employs a combination of high-pressure synchrotron X-ray Diffraction (XRD), micro Raman spectroscopy, and low-temperature magnetization measurements.

Model / system. The physical system is Eu-doped LaCrO3 (ELCO), a perovskite-like oxide material whose properties are studied under extreme pressure conditions.

Key observables. Structural phase transition (Orthorhombic to rhombohedral), lattice parameters, Raman mode shifts and intensities, and the evolution of the Néel temperature ($T_N$).

Important parameters / regimes. Pressure (up to 13.5 GPa), doping concentration (5% Eu), and temperature (low-temperature magnetization).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis combines structural, vibrational, and magnetic measurements to establish a comprehensive link between pressure, structure, and magnetism.

Figures summary. Figures show pressure-dependent XRD patterns indicating peak splitting and structural transitions, volume evolution plots, and Raman spectra tracking mode changes across different pressure regimes.

Paper structure. The paper systematically presents results from XRD to map structural changes, followed by Raman spectroscopy to track vibrational anomalies, and finally integrates magnetization data to pinpoint the magneto-elastic coupling associated with magnetic ordering.

Abstract

In this study, we have carried out a detailed high pressure investigation on 5$\%$ Eu doped LaCrO$_3$ (ELCO) using synchrotron X-ray diffraction (XRD), micro Raman spectroscopy, and low-temperature magnetization measurements to correlate the structural and magnetic properties under pressure. The high pressure XRD reveals the orthorhombic to rhombohedral structural phase transition around 10.6 GPa. The high pressure Raman data corroborate this result and indicate that the lattice instabilities associated with the low-frequency soft modes play a crucial role in driving this transition. In addition, a pronounced anomaly in the Raman shift and integrated intensity of several Raman modes is observed around 4.5 GPa. By combining the low-temperature magnetization measurements with the anomalies observed in the XRD and Raman data and by comparing with the similar results in LaCrO$_3$ (LCO), a pressure-induced change in the magnetic ground state to antiferromagnetic ordering is predicted.
