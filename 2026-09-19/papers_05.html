Parallel quantum channel discrimination and numerical ranges in tensor product subspaces

Authors: Adam Bílek, Paulina Lewandowska, Ryszard Kukulski
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.20781v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work provides a comprehensive theoretical framework for analyzing perfect quantum channel discrimination in a parallel setting. It establishes that distinguishability depends on a geometric property of the operator subspace, quantified by the minimal angle of the numerical range ($ ext{sw}^*$). The paper successfully determines the minimum number of copies needed for perfect discrimination, settling a key conjecture in the field.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical tools developed (SDP, numerical range properties) provide a powerful, general framework for analyzing distinguishability in quantum channels, which has direct implications for quantum communication protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the theoretical problem of determining the necessary and sufficient conditions for perfectly discriminating between two quantum channels ($\Phi$ and $\Psi$) when the discrimination is performed in a parallel scheme.

Main result. Perfect discrimination is possible if and only if a quantity derived from the numerical range of the associated operator subspace, $ ext{sw}^*(A) > 0$. The minimal number of copies required is $N_0 = \lceil \pi / ext{sw}^*(A) ceil$.

Method. The authors develop an SDP formulation and heavily rely on the mathematical properties of the numerical range, particularly its behavior under tensor products, to establish the necessary conditions.

Model / system. The system involves general quantum channels ($\Phi, \Psi$) acting on quantum states, analyzed using their Kraus operator representations and associated operator subspaces.

Key observables. The primary diagnostic tool is $ ext{sw}^*(A)$, which measures the minimal angle of the numerical range of the relevant operator subspace $A$.

Important parameters / regimes. The minimal number of copies, $N_0$, is determined by the relationship $N_0 \approx \pi / ext{sw}^*(A)$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the key property that the minimal angle of the numerical range of a tensor product equals the sum of the minimal angles of the individual subspaces.

Paper structure. The paper builds from defining the problem using quantum channels and the numerical range, establishing necessary and sufficient conditions via SDP, proving key properties of the numerical range under tensor products, and finally applying these results to settle a long-standing conjecture regarding the minimum number of copies.

Abstract

Quantum channel discrimination plays a crucial role in quantum information theory. Of particular interest is the case in which the channels can be discriminated perfectly. In this work, we focus on the perfect quantum channel discrimination task in a parallel scheme. We develop an SDP formulation combined with a bisection procedure to compute a quantum state for perfect discrimination in time linear in the number of copies. In addition, we obtain the minimal number of copies of quantum channels to achieve perfect discrimination. Thanks to that, we settle in the affirmative Conjecture 1 of Duan, Guo, Li and Li [arXiv:1605.02294, IEEE ISIT 2016], which characterizes the number of parallel uses needed to discriminate perfectly a distinguished family of operator subspaces. All our results are obtained using the notion and basic properties of the numerical range. In particular, the key fact that we prove and use is that the minimal angle of the numerical range of a tensor product of matrix subspaces equals the sum of the minimal angles of the numerical ranges of the individual subspaces.

Plasmons in twisted bilayer graphene across dispersive and flat bands

Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 2/5

Summary. This work investigates plasmonic excitations in twisted bilayer graphene by comparing its response in highly dispersive versus quasi-flat band regimes. Using TDDFT-RPA, the authors identify distinct plasmon modes—a 2D Dirac plasmon in the dispersive case and a mid-infrared interband plasmon in the flat-band case. This highlights how the electronic band structure dictates the nature of collective charge excitations in moiré materials.

Why it may be interesting. The detailed analysis of collective excitations (plasmons) in systems exhibiting strong electronic correlations (flat bands) provides insights into how screening and charge dynamics are modified by proximity to correlated insulating states.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study explores the dynamical dielectric response of twisted bilayer graphene (TBG) across different electronic band regimes, specifically comparing dispersive and quasi-flat band behavior.

Main result. In the dispersive regime, a 2D Dirac plasmon is activated by doping, while in the quasi-flat band regime, a distinct interband plasmon is identified with an energy in the mid-infrared range.

Method. The research employs Time-Dependent Density-Functional Theory (TDDFT) within the Random-Phase Approximation (RPA) to calculate the energy-loss function.

Model / system. The physical system is Twisted Bilayer Graphene (TBG), analyzed in three regimes: large-angle dispersive bands at a reference interlayer distance, and small-angle quasi-flat bands treated at a reduced, angle-dependent magic distance.

Key observables. Electron energy-loss function (ELOSS), plasmon dispersion ($\omega$ vs. $q$), and the dielectric function $\epsilon_M(q, \omega)$.

Important parameters / regimes. Twist angle ($ heta$), interlayer distance ($d_0$ vs. $ ilde{d}$), and doping level (Fermi level shift).

Assumptions / limitations. The study uses a tractable cell approximation for the magic-angle structure and is limited by the RPA approximation, excluding correlation-driven symmetry breaking.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the geometry of the three configurations, band structures showing Dirac cones and quasi-flat bands, and ELOSS spectra identifying the $\pi$-plasmon and the doping-activated 2D Dirac plasmon.

Paper structure. The paper systematically compares the dielectric response calculated for the dispersive band regime (large angles) versus the quasi-flat band regime (magic angle approximation), contrasting the resulting plasmonic excitations.

Abstract

The dynamical dielectric response of twisted bilayer graphene is explored in large-angle dispersive-band and small-angle quasi-flat-band regimes using time-dependent density-functional theory within the random-phase approximation. At the reference bilayer-graphene interlayer distance, weak coupling in the largest-angle structures preserves Dirac dispersions and the intrinsic $π$ plasmon. Electron doping activates a two-dimensional Dirac plasmon with energies obeying approximate geometric twist-angle scaling, while acoustic-like branches remain embedded in the single-particle continuum. The prohibitively large first-magic-angle supercell is represented by a tractable cell with its interlayer separation reduced to the angle-dependent magic distance, where four quasi-flat bands emerge around the Fermi level. Their partial occupation produces a dispersive low-energy plasmon-like excitation without a clear dielectric zero at resonance. A distinct interband plasmon is instead identified, supported by transitions involving the quasi-flat manifold and neighboring high-density-of-states regions. Band-energy rescaling places its characteristic energy in the mid-infrared range of interband collective excitations measured near the magic angle.

Rapidity-Coupled Spin Dynamics in Pulsed Laser Fields from Physics-Informed Neural Networks

Authors: N. S. Akintsov, A. P. Nevecheria, S. N. Andreev, Qing-Hua Qin
Type: theory · Category: numerical methods · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.19756v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: methods for driven-dissipative 2/5

Summary. This work investigates how the spin of an electron evolves when driven by intense, short laser pulses, focusing on the role of the Carrier-Envelope-Phase (CEP). The authors use a Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) to solve the complex coupled equations, achieving high accuracy and extending the analysis to elliptical polarization where analytical solutions fail. This provides validated, powerful tools for studying CEP-resolved spin dynamics in extreme electromagnetic fields.

Why it may be interesting. The combination of relativistic quantum electrodynamics (BMT equation) with modern machine learning tools (PINNs) to solve complex, non-linear, time-dependent physics problems is highly relevant for developing next-generation computational physics tools.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Characterizing the spin dynamics of an electron driven by intense, few-cycle, laser pulses, particularly resolving the dependence on the Carrier-Envelope-Phase (CEP).

Main result. The CEP strongly controls the transient intra-pulse polarization angle, though the net spin rotation vanishes for linear polarization. A Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) successfully validates the dynamics, extending to elliptical polarization where no closed-form solution exists.

Method. The authors employed advanced numerical techniques, including light-front parameterization, high-order integrators (Dormand-Prince), and a Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) trained on the governing light-front equations.

Model / system. The system models a relativistic electron interacting with a plane-wave, few-cycle laser pulse. The dynamics are governed by the coupled equations for the electron's orbital motion and its spin evolution (BMT equation).

Key observables. Rest-frame polarization angle ($\Sigma$), peak intra-pulse angle ($\Sigma_{\max}$), net spin rotation, and peak kinetic energy spread.

Important parameters / regimes. Carrier-Envelope-Phase (CEP, $\phi_0$), pulse duration (2-cycle vs. 8-cycle), and the electron's anomalous magnetic moment ($a_e$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis often assumes an ideal plane-wave pulse, which leads to the vanishing net spin rotation, suggesting external effects are needed to observe CEP-dependent net polarization changes.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the CEP dependence of the polarization angle, showing a significant collapse in the peak angle spread ($\Delta\Sigma_{\max}$) when moving from two-cycle to eight-cycle pulses. Comparisons are made between PINN results and established reference solvers.

Paper structure. The paper develops the exact light-front solution for spin evolution, derives the closed-form expression for linear polarization, and then applies this framework to validate a PINN implementation. The PINN is trained to solve the coupled orbital and spin equations, demonstrating accuracy across various polarization regimes.

Abstract

Carrier-envelope-phase (CEP) stabilized few-cycle pulses make the sub-cycle field structure a control parameter for laser-driven polarized electron sources, yet spin solvers for such pulses are rarely tested against exact results. We show that for an electron initially at rest in a linearly polarized plane-wave pulse the rest-frame polarization angle follows the instantaneous vector potential, $Σ= 2\arctan(a_x/2) + a_e a_x$ with $a_e$ the electron anomaly: the net rotation vanishes for every CEP, while the peak intra-pulse angle varies over the CEP by 3.02 degrees for a two-cycle and by 0.22 degrees for an eight-cycle pulse. The result follows from the Volkov orbit in longitudinal and transverse rapidities. We use it to validate a light-front reference integrator and a physics-informed neural network trained only on the light-front equations, which reaches $4\times10^{-5}$ in the spin sector and carries over to elliptical polarization, for which no closed-form solution is known. This gives validated tools for CEP-resolved spin dynamics in intense fields.

Summary. This theoretical paper derives a quantum speed limit for observables undergoing non-Markovian open quantum dynamics. It analyzes how environmental dissipation affects the process of einselection, showing that non-Markovian effects can enhance or delay the approach to classicality. The findings establish a quantitative link between the accumulated negativity of the decoherence rate and the time delay in pointer state formation.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the fundamental connection between environmental coupling, the emergence of classicality (einselection), and the fundamental constraints on quantum evolution (QSLs) in non-Markovian regimes, which is central to modern open quantum systems theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the rate at which a quantum system approaches classicality (einselection) within the framework of non-Markovian open quantum dynamics.

Main result. Non-Markovianity can both accelerate and delay the einselection process, with the delay being bounded by the accumulated negativity of the canonical decoherence rate.

Method. The authors derive and analyze a quantum speed limit for observables by decomposing the time evolution into coherent and dissipative current contributions.

Model / system. The analysis concerns finite-dimensional quantum systems governed by open-system dynamics described by time-local master equations, incorporating both Hamiltonian evolution and environmental dissipation.

Key observables. Observable speed ($v_K(t)$), expectation values ($\langle K angle_t$), canonical decoherence rates ($\gamma_\mu(t)$), and coherence measures ($\mathcal{C}_{ ext{ptr}}^{\ell_1}(\varrho(t))$).

Important parameters / regimes. Non-Markovianity (signaled by $\gamma_\mu(t) < 0$), accumulated negativity ($F(0,t)$), and the time-local generator $L_t$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is restricted to differentiable time-local open dynamics, and the speed limit derivation relies on specific mathematical inequalities.

Figures summary. Figure 1 compares the exact observable speed against the theoretical bound for pure-dephasing dynamics, decomposing the speed into coherent and dissipative parts.

Paper structure. The paper builds by first defining the observable speed limit in open systems, then analyzing the decomposition of the evolution into coherent and dissipative currents, and finally comparing the speed enhancement in non-Markovian (CP-indivisible) dynamics against Markovian references.

Abstract

Among the processes governed by open-system and dissipative quantum dynamics, environment-induced superselection, or einselection, is particularly foundational, since it describes the formation of a preferred set of pointer states through environmental monitoring and ultimately the emergence of classicality. Especially in the context of Quantum Darwinism, einselection can be studied in terms of the motion of an observable towards its pointer value. To this end, we derive a quantum speed limit for observables for finite-dimensional systems governed by differentiable time-local open dynamics. We identify two mechanisms through which non-Markovianity can enhance observable speed: direct dissipative motion supported by population retention or revival, and indirect enhancement through restoration of the asymmetry available for coherent motion. For permanent einselection, this delays the emergence of the pointer basis due to accumulated negativity.

Summary. This paper analyzes the asymptotic behavior of constrained spherical integrals for non-Hermitian random matrices. It identifies a critical phase transition point determined by the largest singular value of a related matrix. The work develops conjectures for large deviations, extending established techniques from Hermitian random matrix theory to the non-Hermitian setting.

Why it may be interesting. The mathematical framework of analyzing phase transitions in random matrix integrals, especially the transition from delocalized to localized regimes, has deep parallels with localization phenomena studied in disordered systems and quantum many-body physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper studies the large-N asymptotics of constrained spherical integrals for non-Hermitian random matrices, focusing on identifying phase transitions between delocalized and localized regimes.

Main result. The constrained integral exhibits a transition governed by the largest singular value of a shifted matrix, leading to conjectures for one-eigenvalue large deviations and boundary fluctuations.

Method. The analysis employs saddle-point methods, extending techniques from Hermitian random matrices (like the Coulomb-gas picture) to the non-Hermitian setting using complex analysis and singular value decomposition.

Model / system. The model involves non-Hermitian random matrices and a constrained spherical integral $H_{q,N}(\alpha, eta)$ defined by fixing norms and mutual scalar products of associated vectors.

Key observables. The constrained integral $H_q$, the largest singular value $\sigma_{\max}(z)$, and the one-particle rate function $\Psi_V(z)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The parameters $\alpha$ and $eta$ defining the constraints, and the spectral density $ ho_\mu(z)$ of the limiting measure $\mu$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the empirical spectral measure converges to a deterministic measure $\mu$ with compact support, and relies on the validity of the large-N limit and saddle-point approximations.

Figures summary. Figure 1 compares the mean real part of the overlap $\langle u_1, v_1 angle$ with the mean top singular value $\Sigma(q)$ for the Ginibre ensemble.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the problem by comparing to Hermitian cases, develops the saddle-point analysis for the non-Hermitian integral, identifies the transition point based on singular values, and concludes with conjectures for large deviations.

Abstract

We study the large-$N$ asymptotics of a constrained spherical integral for non-Hermitian random matrices, in which the norms and mutual scalar product of two vectors are fixed. In the delocalized regime, the asymptotics are governed by the non-Hermitian transforms $\mathcal R_1$ and $\mathcal R_2$. At saddle-point level, the constrained integral exhibits a transition to a localized regime controlled by the largest singular value of a shifted matrix and by the overlap of its associated left and right singular vectors. Motivated by the Hermitian spherical-integral mechanism and by the Coulomb-gas picture, we formulate conjectures for one-eigenvalue large deviations and boundary fluctuations. Throughout, our analysis is carried out in the spirit of mathematical physics.

Summary. This paper establishes deep mathematical links between hypercontractivity and entropy contraction for quantum channels and semigroups. By analyzing these functional inequalities, the authors derive quantitative bounds on spectral properties, such as the Log-Sobolev constant. This work is crucial for understanding the rate and mechanism of thermalization in quantum many-body systems.

Why it may be interesting. The connection between functional inequalities (like hypercontractivity) and fundamental thermodynamic quantities (like entropy decay) is central to understanding thermalization and mixing in open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the mathematical relationship between hypercontractivity estimates and entropy contraction in quantum dynamical systems.

Main result. It proves that hypercontractivity implies entropy contraction for single quantum channels and derives specific lower bounds on the Log-Sobolev constant for KMS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups.

Method. The analysis employs techniques from functional analysis, including complex interpolation, weighted Hölder duality, and the use of the norm–entropy derivative.

Model / system. The study focuses on finite-dimensional quantum systems governed by quantum channels ($\Phi$) and continuous-time quantum Markov semigroups ($P_t = e^{-tL}$), assuming the existence of a faithful invariant state $\sigma$.

Key observables. Quantum relative entropy $D( ho \parallel \sigma)$, entropy contraction constant $\eta$, Log-Sobolev constant $\alpha_1$, and hypercontractivity constant $\alpha_H$.

Important parameters / regimes. The spectral gap $\lambda$ of the generator $L$, and the parameters $p$ and $q$ defining the weighted norms.

Assumptions / limitations. The system is assumed to be finite-dimensional, and for the strongest results, the QMS must be primitive and KMS-symmetric with respect to $\sigma$.

Paper structure. The paper builds logically from single-channel estimates (Section 2) to continuous-time semigroups (Section 3), culminating in applications concerning factorization and tensorization (Section 4).

Abstract

We prove that hypercontractivity implies entropy contraction for a single quantum channel, without a detailed balance condition. For primitive quantum Markov semigroups that are KMS-symmetric with respect to a faithful invariant state \(σ\), we obtain the modified Log-Sobolev bound $α_1\geq \fracλ{(2+\log\|σ^{-1}\|_\infty)}$ where $λ$ is the spectral gap. This removes the assumption of \(L_p\)-regularity for the comparison through the log-Sobolev constant. As an application, we show that the hypercontractivity of an average of two conditional expectations implies the approximate tensorization of relative entropy.

Summary. This work proposes using Variational Quantum Circuits (VQCs) as a novel, quantum-enhanced method to parametrize Fragmentation Functions (FFs) in QCD. By encoding physical symmetries and kinematic features, the VQC achieves high accuracy in reconstructing established benchmark FFs across both momentum fraction and energy scale. This demonstrates the potential of quantum circuits for creating compact, non-perturbative representations of fundamental particle physics observables.

Why it may be interesting. While the application is in high-energy particle physics, the core methodology—using VQCs to learn complex, structured functional dependencies from existing data—is a powerful example of applying quantum machine learning techniques to solve difficult, non-linear parametrization problems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of non-perturbatively parametrizing Fragmentation Functions (FFs) in Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD). It seeks a quantum method to represent these complex, multi-dimensional functions.

Main result. The Variational Quantum Circuit (VQC) framework successfully reconstructs established benchmark FFs (like DSS14) with high accuracy across both 1D and 2D kinematic domains, demonstrating the model's expressive power.

Method. The authors employ Variational Quantum Circuits (VQCs) to map the functional dependence of FFs, incorporating physics-inspired Ansätze like heavy-flavor thresholds and utilizing spectral analysis for validation.

Model / system. The physical system is governed by QCD, specifically focusing on the non-perturbative Fragmentation Functions (FFs) describing hadronization. The model uses a VQC architecture trained to reproduce known functional forms derived from global QCD analyses.

Key observables. Mean-Squared Error (MSE) loss, Correlation Coefficient ($R^2$), and the relative $L^2$ error ($E(i)$) for spectral reconstruction.

Important parameters / regimes. Momentum fraction ($z$), energy scale ($Q^2$), and the number of variational layers used in the circuit.

Abstract

We present a variational quantum-circuit model for fragmentation functions (FFs). Isospin and charge-conjugation symmetries are imposed to construct an independent six-flavor basis describing charged and neutral pion production, while physics-inspired Ansätze, including logarithmic feature maps and mass thresholds, encode the relevant kinematics. This quantum architecture substantially reduces the quantum circuit redundancies and improve optimization convergence. Using the DSS14 pion FF set as a benchmark, we first develop a one-dimensional variational representation (FF-VQR) in the momentum fraction at fixed energy scale, and show how entanglement between quark and gluon FFs yields a significant improvement, with accurate results already obtained using just two variational layers. A spectral analysis further demonstrates that the quantum model achieves high expressivity with a limited number of Fourier modes, supporting its use as a compact non-perturbative parametrization suitable for DGLAP evolution. We then extend the FF-VQR to two dimensions by incorporating the energy-scale dependence. By encoding all flavor channels within a single entangled quantum circuit, the quantum model provides a unified representation with higher accuracy than an independent encoding for each partonic species.

Summary. This theoretical work analyzes the approach to thermal equilibrium in single-particle chaotic quantum systems by examining the scaling of off-diagonal matrix elements. Using semiclassical methods based on Berry's conjecture, the authors derive that the variance follows an ETH-like scaling modified by a unique kinetic-energy factor. This provides a detailed comparison between single-particle and many-body thermalization predictions.

Why it may be interesting. The finding of a kinetic-energy-dependent factor beyond standard many-body ETH provides a novel, single-particle perspective on thermalization, which is highly relevant for understanding quantum chaos and energy transport in mesoscopic systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how isolated quantum systems approach thermal equilibrium by analyzing the off-diagonal matrix elements of real-space observables.

Main result. It shows that these matrix elements exhibit a natural banded structure, and the variance scales according to the Eigenstate Thermalization Hypothesis (ETH) multiplied by an additional kinetic-energy-dependent factor unique to single-particle dynamics.

Method. The analysis employs semiclassical approximations derived from Berry's conjecture to calculate the variance and connected autocorrelation function of the off-diagonal matrix elements.

Model / system. The study focuses on time-reversal-invariant single-particle chaotic systems, exemplified by quantum billiards such as the generalized quarter-Sinai billiard. The dynamics are governed by a single-particle Hamiltonian.

Key observables. Off-diagonal matrix elements $\langle E_i|\hat{O}|E_j angle$, the characteristic bandwidth ($\omega_b$), and the connected autocorrelation function $C_{ ext{sym}}^O(t; E)$.

Abstract

We study the off-diagonal matrix elements of real-space observables in time-reversal-invariant single-particle chaotic systems. By analyzing the semiclassical expression for the off-diagonal variance derived from Berry's conjecture, we show that the banded structure of the observable matrix emerges naturally. For local observables, we identify a characteristic bandwidth associated with a late-time timescale inversely proportional to the particle velocity. We further show that, for systems with steep-wall confinement, the predicted magnitude follows the entropy scaling of the eigenstate thermalization hypothesis (ETH), multiplied by an additional kinetic-energy-dependent factor that is independent of spatial dimension and is not captured by conventional many-body ETH. We illustrate these results through a case study of quantum billiards and verify the semiclassical scaling numerically in a generalized quarter-Sinai billiard. Our results elucidate the dynamical implications of Berry's conjecture and provide a comparison between single-particle eigenstate thermalization and many-body ETH.

Using linear spin-wave theory on a two-dimensional checkerboard lattice with nearest-neighbor and next-nearest-neighbor couplings J_1 and J_2, we demonstrate that finite J_2 produces direction-dependent magnon group velocities and splits the two magnon branches. The resulting propagation remains closer to that of an antiferromagnet than a ferromagnet, retaining an approximately circular outer wavefront, while the direction-dependent magnon velocities produce a pronounced cross-like spatial structure inside this front. We further show that changing the sign of J_2 to enter the unfrustrated regime (J_2<0) increases the characteristic propagation velocities and interchanges the diagonal directions of enhanced propagation, corresponding to a pi/2 rotation of the anisotropic spatial pattern. These results establish spacetime dynamics as a complementary probe of altermagnetic magnons, providing a route to identifying unconventional magnetic order through real-space propagation patterns in solid-state and synthetic quantum systems.

Abstract

Many signal processing tasks require intensive computations. Quantum computing promises to accelerate certain tasks, but algorithms must be designed around the limitations of quantum mechanics. This paper provides a quantum implementation of finite impulse response (FIR) filters, which are a widely used tool in classical signal processing. The filter can be parallelized and composed as part of larger quantum algorithms, with potential for speedups using quantum amplitude estimation and future fault-tolerant quantum hardware. To accomplish these properties, we introduce a state-space framework wherein sample-based signal processing is performed with unitary quantum circuits. We present the quantum delay gate as the quantum analog of the delay line in discrete-time signal processing. Unitary circuits intrinsically describe lossless systems, but we also implement lowpass and other bounded filters by emulating a projection operator. The FIR filter implementation is tested and demonstrated with a quantum circuit simulator.
