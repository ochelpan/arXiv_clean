Pressure-induced electronic and structural evolution of EuIrGe3

Authors: N. S. Dhami, V. Baledent, I. Batistic, C. M. N. Kumar, D. Kaczorowski, L. Nataf, J. M. Ablett, J. -P. Rueff, J. P. Itie, P. Fertey, S. R. Shieh, P. Popcevic, Y. Utsumi Boucher

Summary. This work comprehensively examines how high pressure alters the electronic structure and crystal lattice of the antiferromagnet EuIrGe3. By combining XAS, XRD, and resistivity measurements with DFT calculations, the authors show that pressure drives a valence change in Eu and induces a structural phase transition. These results highlight the critical role of transition metal d-electron hybridization in governing the material's response to extreme conditions.

Why it may be interesting. The strong dependence of electronic properties (valence changes, hybridization) on external pressure, particularly in systems involving localized f-electrons (Eu) coupled to itinerant d-electrons (Ir), is highly relevant to understanding pressure-induced quantum phase transitions in correlated materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the pressure-induced electronic and structural evolution of the noncentrosymmetric antiferromagnet EuIrGe3.

Main result. The material exhibits a continuous increase in the mean Eu valence and undergoes a structural phase transition above 38 GPa, demonstrating a pressure response distinct from related compounds.

Method. The research combines experimental techniques like X-ray Absorption Spectroscopy (XAS), synchrotron X-ray Diffraction (XRD), and electrical resistivity measurements with theoretical calculations using Density Functional Theory (DFT).

Model / system. The system is EuIrGe3, a member of the BaNiSn3-type EuTGe3 family, which is studied across a range of high pressures (up to 40 GPa). The electronic structure is analyzed by tracking the valence changes of Eu and the hybridization between transition metal d-electron states (Ir 5d, Ge 4p).

Key observables. Mean Eu valence, anisotropic lattice compression parameters ($a, c$), antiferromagnetic ordering temperatures ($T_N$), and the evolution of the Density of States (DOS) components.

Important parameters / regimes. Pressure (up to 40 GPa), temperature (4 K to 300 K), and the Hubbard interaction U for Eu (3.8 eV).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the structural stability within the tetragonal I4mm phase up to the transition pressure, and DFT calculations utilize the PBEsol functional with DFT+U treatment for Eu 4f states.

Figures summary. Figures show pressure-dependent Eu L3-edge XAS spectra revealing valence changes, high-pressure XRD patterns indicating structural transitions, and DFT-calculated DOS evolving with increasing pressure.

Paper structure. The paper follows a structure of presenting multiple complementary measurements: XAS tracks valence changes; XRD maps structural changes and phase transitions; resistivity measures magnetic stability; and DFT provides the underlying electronic structure calculations to interpret the experimental findings.

Abstract

We investigated the pressure-induced evolution of the electronic and crystal structure of the noncentrosymmetric BaNiSn3-type antiferromagnet EuIrGe3 using x-ray absorption spectroscopy, synchrotron x-ray diffraction complemented by density functional theory calculations, and electrical resistivity measurements. The Eu L3-edge spectra reveal a continuous increase in the mean Eu valence under compression, accompanied by modifications of the Ge and Ir electronic states. High- pressure x-ray diffraction shows anisotropic lattice compression in the tetragonal (I4mm) phase and provides evidence for a structural phase transition above 38 GPa. The experimentally deter- mined lattice and equation-of-state parameters are in good agreement with the DFT calculations. Electrical resistivity measurements reveal a monotonic increase in the antiferromagnetic ordering temperatures up to 18 GPa, indicating that the antiferromagnetic ground state remains robust despite the increasing contribution of the nonmagnetic Eu3+ configuration to the intermediate va- lence state. These results demonstrate that EuIrGe3 exhibits a pressure response distinct from EuCoGe3 and EuRhGe3, highlighting the important role of the transition metal d-electron states in the pressure-induced electronic and structural evolution of the EuT Ge3 family

Private communication from Pauli channels with no privacy

Authors: Uthirakalyani G, Pritam Halder, David Elkouss
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.20133v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical paper explores quantum channel capacity, specifically focusing on private information. The core finding is that two individual quantum channels, each incapable of transmitting private information, can be combined to create a joint channel that can transmit private information. This phenomenon, called superactivation, highlights the complex, non-additive nature of quantum communication capabilities.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides deep insights into the structural properties of quantum channels (degradability, antidegradability) and how these properties dictate fundamental communication limits, which is crucial for understanding quantum communication protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the phenomenon of 'private-capacity superactivation,' demonstrating that combining two quantum channels, each having zero private classical capacity, can enable the transmission of private information.

Main result. The authors show that two specific Pauli two-qubit channels, $F$ and $G$, can jointly transmit private information, achieving a positive private capacity even though $P(F)=P(G)=0$.

Method. The analysis relies on defining private information using mutual information and analyzing the properties of quantum channels, specifically focusing on concepts like transpose-antidegradability and antidegradability.

Model / system. The study uses abstract quantum communication channels, specifically Pauli two-qubit channels and the 50% qubit erasure channel, analyzed using Pauli operators and tensor products.

Key observables. Private classical capacity ($P(N)$), Mutual Information ($I$), and Coherent Information ($I_c$).

Important parameters / regimes. The zero private capacity condition is linked to the channel being transpose-antidegradable, while superactivation requires the joint channel to violate this property.

Assumptions / limitations. The construction relies on the specific Pauli structure of the channels and the use of Bell states to build the activating binary code.

Figures summary. The notes reference tables detailing the action of Pauli operators on specific Pauli products and the resulting environment states after channel application.

Paper structure. The paper builds from defining channel capacities and the zero-capacity condition (related to transpose-antidegradability) to constructing a pair of channels $(F, G)$ that exhibit superactivation, and finally extending this to include the 50% erasure channel $(F, H)$.

Abstract

A channel capacity quantifies the communication capability of a noisy physical process. In contrast to communication channels in the classical world, quantum theory makes this capability contextual. We show that two Pauli two-qubit channels, each with zero private classical capacity, can be used to transmit private information when used together. One is a two-qubit Pauli channel whose environment can reconstruct the receiver output up to matrix transposition; the other is an antidegradable channel. We obtain a similar result when the second channel is the 50% qubit erasure channel. A simple binary code built from rank-three mixtures of Bell states activates private communication. The main ingredient in our construction is a transpose-antidegradable channel that is not antidegradable.

Proof of a positive coherent-error threshold for topological quantum codes

Authors: Shiro Tamiya, Masato Koashi
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.20708v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper establishes a theoretical proof for a positive error threshold in topological quantum codes when facing coherent errors, a significant advance over previous stochastic-only models. By combining Fourier analysis and polymer model techniques, the authors show that the logical error rate decays exponentially with code distance for Z-rotation errors. This work deepens the theoretical understanding of quantum error correction in realistic, non-stochastic noise environments.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a rigorous theoretical framework for understanding fault tolerance beyond the standard assumption of independent, stochastic errors, which is crucial for modeling real-world quantum hardware imperfections.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to rigorously prove the existence of a positive error threshold for topological quantum codes when subjected to coherent errors, extending previous results limited to stochastic error models.

Main result. It proves that a positive threshold exists for coherent Z-rotation errors for CSS QLDPC codes, showing that the entanglement infidelity is suppressed exponentially with code distance.

Method. The proof combines Fourier analysis to handle interference effects from coherent errors with the cluster expansion technique applied to abstract polymer models.

Model / system. The system analyzed is quantum error correction (QEC) applied to topological stabilizer codes, such as the surface code, defined on local lattices. The errors considered are coherent Z-rotation errors.

Key observables. Entanglement infidelity of the recovered logical information, and the existence of a positive error threshold.

Important parameters / regimes. Code distance ($d$), the magnitude of the coherent rotation angles ($ heta_j$), and the number of physical qubits ($n$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the syndrome measurement is performed in a noiseless way (code-capacity setting) and that the rotation angles are bounded by a constant independent of code size.

Paper structure. The paper builds from preliminaries (stabilizer codes, CSS codes, QLDPC codes, Fourier analysis) to derive the main theorem using advanced mathematical tools like Fourier analysis and the cluster expansion.

Abstract

Threshold analyses of quantum error-correcting codes are well established for stochastic error models, in which errors occur randomly with given probabilities. However, errors in actual devices can also be coherent, such as unwanted $Z$ rotations due to imperfect control, which are not captured by stochastic error models. For the surface code, numerical studies have indicated threshold behavior even under coherent errors, but a rigorous proof of threshold existence is lacking. Here we prove that a positive threshold for coherent $Z$-rotation errors exists for quantum low-density parity-check codes with a bounded number of logical qubits, including the surface code and other topological codes. Specifically, we show that the maximum-likelihood Pauli recovery suppresses the entanglement infidelity exponentially in the code distance up to a prefactor linear in the number of physical qubits whenever the rotation angles lie below a constant that is independent of the code size. The proof combines Fourier analysis to retain the interference among the amplitudes of coherent errors with the cluster expansion of abstract polymer models. Our results expand the theoretical foundation of quantum error correction and offer a statistical-mechanical description of quantum error correction beyond stochastic errors.

Abstract

Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) achieve state-of-the-art performance on graph-structured data, but training and inference on large graphs are often bottlenecked by memory constraints and sparse linear-algebra workloads. Quantum computing offers an alternative set of primitives that may improve scalability for graph learning. Building on the quantum graph neural network (QGNN) framework of Liao \textit{et al.}, this work implements two representative architectures --- the Simplified Graph Convolution (SGC) and Linear Graph Convolution (LGC) models --- and evaluates them on open benchmark graph datasets and semi-supervised learning tasks using quantum simulation. We compare predictive performance and optimization behavior against classical baselines, showing that the quantum models achieve competitive performance with fewer parameters. Finally, we present a cost gradient analysis that identifies the tasks for which the models showcased are trainable. This is followed by a classical simulability study to find regimes in which the proposed circuits remain robust during training.

Quantum tree networks: complete semidefinite hierarchies and the source-transfer model

Authors: Xiangling Xu, Igor Klep, Marc-Olivier Renou
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.19310v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops a comprehensive mathematical framework to characterize correlations in quantum tree networks, resolving the difficulty of finding dimension-free bounds. It introduces the source-transfer model, proving its equivalence to the mixed quantum model, and establishes two complete SDP hierarchies for bounding these correlations. This work advances the theory of quantum correlations beyond simple tensor product assumptions.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical characterization of quantum correlations in complex network structures provides fundamental tools for understanding resource theories in quantum information, applicable to quantum communication and computation protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of finding a systematic, dimension-free method to bound correlations achievable in general quantum networks, specifically quantum tree networks.

Main result. The authors introduce and prove the equivalence of the novel source-transfer model and the mixed quantum model for tree networks, establishing two complete outer SDP hierarchies for bounding these correlations.

Method. The methodology involves developing and proving the completeness of two specialized Semidefinite Programming (SDP) hierarchies—the source-transfer and inflation-NPA hierarchies—using advanced mathematical tools like noncommutative real algebraic geometry and completely positive maps.

Model / system. The focus is on quantum tree networks, which generalize the Bell scenario. The correlations are characterized by the Mixed Quantum Model ($ ext{C}_{ ext{mix}}$) and formalized using the Source-Transfer Model, which employs $C^*$-algebras and recursive completely positive maps.

Key observables. The game-value promise problem for quantum tree networks in the mixed model, and the convergence of the SDP hierarchies to the true correlation set.

Important parameters / regimes. The structure of the network (tree topology), the dimension of the local measurements, and the number of parties involved.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that the correlation set is governed by the source-transfer model, which is shown to be equivalent to the mixed quantum model. A key limitation overcome is that simple measurement factorization is insufficient.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the hierarchy progression from Bell scenarios to quantum tree networks using the source-transfer and inflation-NPA hierarchies. Figure 2 compares the mathematical structure of the Mixed vs. Source-Transfer models for a line-of-four network.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first defining the physical problem (bounding correlations in tree networks), then introducing the source-transfer model as the necessary formalism, proving its equivalence to the mixed model, and finally establishing the completeness of the corresponding SDP hierarchies.

Abstract

Understanding the capabilities and limits of quantum systems is central to quantum information processing. This includes determining which correlations can arise in networks of quantum systems distributed by independent sources. Yet for general quantum networks, no systematic, dimension-free method is known to bound these correlations arbitrarily well. We resolve this problem for all quantum tree networks through a new source-transfer model, which we prove equivalent to a mixed quantum model. The mixed model places no restriction on dimension and reduces to the standard tensor-product model in finite dimensions; the source-transfer model instead describes these correlations through states on $C^*$-algebras and completely positive maps composed recursively along the tree. We prove the equivalence by reconstructing local measurements as Radon-Nikodym derivatives in the spatial tensor products prescribed by the network. Building on this characterization, we establish two convergent outer hierarchies of semidefinite programs (SDPs): a novel source-transfer hierarchy and the inflation-NPA hierarchy. For the source-transfer hierarchy, we extend noncommutative real algebraic geometry to multi-state polynomials with completely positive maps, prove a reconstruction theorem establishing its convergence, and give a sufficient stopping criterion for extracting finite-dimensional realizations. For the inflation-NPA hierarchy, we reconstruct source-transfer realizations from weak-operator limits of averages over source copies. Consequently, the game-value promise problem for quantum tree networks in the mixed model is $\mathsf{coRE}$-complete.

Summary. This paper establishes a rigorous framework for measuring progress toward utility-scale quantum computation by defining 'scalable logical qubits.' It characterizes progress across four coupled dimensions—reliability, scale, capability, and performance—and emphasizes that system cost, balancing error correction overhead against runtime, is the ultimate metric. This provides a necessary roadmap for researchers building fault-tolerant quantum processors.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a comprehensive, multi-dimensional framework for assessing the maturity of quantum hardware, which is crucial for theorists designing algorithms or analyzing resource requirements for future quantum simulations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Achieving utility-scale quantum computing requires error rates far below what physical qubits can support directly, necessitating robust error correction.

Main result. The authors introduce the concept of 'scalable logical qubits,' characterizing progress across four coupled dimensions: reliability, scale, capability, and performance.

Method. The paper defines a rigorous metric for progress by analyzing the trade-offs inherent in quantum error correction (QEC) schemes.

Model / system. The focus is on abstract quantum error correction codes (e.g., surface codes, LDPC codes) used to encode logical qubits redundantly across physical qubits.

Key observables. Logical error rates ($\epsilon$), number of logical qubits ($N$), logical cycle time, and physical-to-logical qubit overhead.

Important parameters / regimes. Target error rates ($\sim 10^{-12}$ to $10^{-15}$), required physical qubit count scaling ($O(N \log \epsilon^{-1})$), and decoding latency.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the existence of low-latency, real-time decoding and feedback mechanisms necessary for fault-tolerant operation.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the necessity of logical qubits, defines the four characterization dimensions (Reliability, Scale, Capability, Performance), discusses the trade-offs between these dimensions, and concludes by framing progress assessment around overall system cost.

Abstract

Utility-scale quantum computing will require executing long, complex algorithms with end-to-end error rates far below what physical qubits can support directly. Error-corrected logical qubits are needed to achieve this goal. To make the progress on logical qubits measurable and comparable we introduce the definition of scalable logical qubits: logical qubits preserved for long computations by repeated quantum error correction, capable of fault-tolerant universal operations with low-latency real-time decoding and feedback, and replicable to the hundreds or thousands, as required by applications. We characterize these scalable logical qubits along four coupled dimensions: reliability, scale, capability, and performance, and discuss trade-offs among these dimensions.

Abstract

Sensitivity of Nucleation Thermodynamics and Kinetics to the Treatment of Long-Range Interactions

Authors: Fernanda Sulantay Vargas, Kimia Sinaeian, Amir Haji-Akbari
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.19646v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work rigorously investigates the artificial sensitivity of nucleation rates to the cutoff radius used for long-range intermolecular interactions in the Lennard-Jones model. The study finds that the kinetic rate changes drastically with $r_c$, necessitating a systematic, theory-guided framework to extrapolate results to the full-potential limit. It emphasizes that the choice of truncation scheme must be treated as a fundamental convergence parameter in simulations of phase transitions.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical fluid dynamics, the rigorous treatment of convergence parameters ($r_c$) and the development of extrapolation frameworks are highly relevant to any simulation involving long-range forces, including those encountered in quantum many-body systems or complex quantum field theories.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper quantifies how the calculated nucleation rates and melting thermodynamics are artificially sensitive to the method used to truncate long-range intermolecular interactions.

Main result. The nucleation rate exhibits extreme sensitivity, changing by about ten orders of magnitude when varying the cutoff radius, suggesting that the choice of cutoff is a critical, non-trivial parameter in simulations.

Method. The study combines Molecular Dynamics (MD) simulations, jumpy forward-flux sampling (jFFS), and free-energy calculations, utilizing Classical Nucleation Theory (CNT) to analyze the cutoff dependence.

Model / system. The benchmark system is the Lennard-Jones (LJ) fluid undergoing homogeneous crystal nucleation into an Fcc structure. The potential is truncated at a cutoff radius $r_c$, leading to the need for careful long-range corrections.

Key observables. Melting temperature ($T_m$), homogeneous crystal nucleation rate ($R_{hom}$), and the chemical potential difference between liquid and crystalline phases.

Important parameters / regimes. The cutoff radius ($r_c$) is the primary variable, tested in the range $2.5\sigma \le r_c \le 6\sigma$. The simulation is run at $kT/\epsilon = 0.5$.

Assumptions / limitations. The authors assume that the kinetic sensitivity stems primarily from cutoff-induced changes in the chemical potential difference ($\Delta\mu$) between the liquid and crystal phases, and they develop a framework to extrapolate finite-cutoff rates.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate reversible paths in the p-T plane for thermodynamic integration, and show the cutoff dependence of the melting temperature and nucleation rate.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the problem of long-range interaction truncation, applies MD/jFFS to the LJ system to calculate thermodynamics and kinetics across various $r_c$, derives the kinetic sensitivity from $\Delta\mu$, and proposes a CNT-based framework for extrapolation and optimal cutoff selection.

Abstract

Nucleation rates are exponentially sensitive to the thermodynamic driving force and can therefore depend strongly on the treatment of long-range intermolecular interactions. Here, using the Lennard--Jones (LJ) system as a benchmark, we combine molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, jumpy forward-flux sampling (jFFS), and free-energy calculations to quantify the effect of potential truncation on melting thermodynamics, homogeneous crystal nucleation kinetics, and computational cost. Within the cutoff-radius range $2.5σ\le r_c\le 6σ$, the melting temperature at zero pressure varies by approximately 11%, while the nucleation rate changes by approximately ten orders of magnitude. By invoking classical nucleation theory (CNT), we show that this pronounced kinetic sensitivity originates primarily from cutoff-induced changes in the chemical potential difference between the liquid and crystalline phases. Building on this observation, we develop a CNT-based framework for extrapolating finite-cutoff rates to the full-potential limit and for estimating the expected rate deviations at other cutoff radii and temperatures. These findings also provide a systematic basis for cutoff selection: the optimal cutoff should minimize computational cost while keeping the deviation from the full-potential rate within acceptable bounds. At $kT/ε=0.5$, $r_c=4σ$ provides a reasonable compromise according to these criteria. We further demonstrate that conventional homogeneous tail corrections do not offer a reliable alternative, as they cannot consistently account for the liquid, crystalline, and interfacial environments present during nucleation. Our findings highlight the need to specify and validate the truncation scheme as an integral component of force-field development in simulations of nucleation and other interfacial phase transitions within inhomogeneous environments.

Summary. This paper introduces a novel Transformer-based intrinsic optimizer to overcome the limitations of classical optimization within the QAOA framework for quantum computing. Abstract

The Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) is a leading variational framework for combinatorial optimization on noisy intermediate-scale quantum hardware, but its practical performance depends strongly on the classical optimizer used to train its variational parameters. This outer-loop optimization is often nonconvex, initialization-sensitive, and costly when repeated across large families of related problem instances. In this work, we propose a Transformer-based intrinsic optimization framework for QAOA, in which the optimizer itself is learned and embedded directly into the hybrid quantum-classical loop. The proposed graph-conditioned Transformer processes problem structure, current QAOA parameters, measurement feedback, and recent optimization history to predict the next variational-parameter update, thereby reformulating instance-wise classical optimization as an amortized learned policy. We develop a mathematical formulation of this intrinsic-optimization perspective and evaluate the method on QAOA-based MaxCut benchmarks across multiple problem settings, with comparisons against representative classical and learned optimization baselines. The results demonstrate that Transformer-based intrinsic optimization can provide a structured and transferable mechanism for improving the classical component of hybrid quantum optimization algorithms.

Abstract

A novel class of polycatenated architected materials (PAMs) has recently emerged, exhibiting distinctive mechanical properties such as stress-strain hysteresis and a geometry-driven transition between solid-like and fluid-like behavior. This work expands the design space of PAMs and offers a predictive tool for actively controllable mechanical metamaterials.

Abstract

Bernal-stacked bilayer graphene (BLG), in which a graphene layer is stacked atop another and laterally shifted by a lattice constant, offers remarkable tunability in its single-particle states under applied magnetic and displacement fields. Owing to this tunability, recent transport and scanning tunneling microscopy experiments in the presence of a perpendicular magnetic field and finite interlayer displacement fields have observed even-denominator fractional quantum Hall (FQH) states at half-filling in the first excited, namely, $\mathcal{N}{=}2$, Landau level (LL) of BLG. In contrast, at zero displacement field, a gapless composite fermion Fermi liquid (CFFL) is realized at half-filling of the $\mathcal{N}{=}2$ LL. Motivated by these experiments, we compute the phase diagram as a function of the displacement field in the half-filled $\mathcal{N}{=}2$ LL of BLG by studying the competition between the CFFL and the Moore-Read state---a candidate even-denominator FQH state---by calculating their thermodynamic energies in this setting. We find that the modified effective Coulomb interaction, induced by changes in the single-particle states with increasing displacement field, softens the inter-electronic repulsion at short distances, thereby stabilizing the Moore-Read state over the CFFL in the $\mathcal{N}{=}2$ LL of BLG. We also study the nature of FQH states at fillings $2/5$, $3/7$, $4/9$, and $6/13$ in the $\mathcal{N}{=}2$ LL of BLG. Our results suggest that, with increasing displacement field, the Jain composite-fermion states at $3/7$, $4/9$, and $6/13$ transition into states with distinct topological order that are well-captured by parton wave functions.
