Proof of Shor's conjecture on the accessible information of quantum dichotomies

Authors: Michele Dall'Arno
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.20600v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 3/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper provides a complete and elementary proof of Shor's conjecture regarding the accessible information of quantum dichotomies. It demonstrates that this information is always attained by a simple projective measurement, regardless of the system's dimension. Furthermore, it shows that calculating this accessible information can be formulated as a convex optimization problem.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides fundamental theoretical tools for quantifying information extraction in quantum systems, which is crucial for understanding the limits of quantum communication and measurement protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To conclusively prove Shor's conjecture, which posits that the accessible information of any quantum dichotomy (an ensemble of two states) is always achieved by a projective measurement.

Main result. The conjecture is proven for arbitrary finite dimensions, showing that any measurement is informationally majorized by a projective measurement, and furthermore, the computation of accessible information for dichotomies can be framed as a convex optimization problem.

Method. The proof utilizes elementary differentiation, the Jensen inequality for operator-convex functions, and reformulates the maximization problem into a convex optimization framework.

Model / system. The study concerns quantum ensembles, specifically a quantum dichotomy $(p, ho; q, \sigma)$, where $ ho$ and $\sigma$ are quantum states prepared with probabilities $p$ and $q=1-p$. The analysis is performed in arbitrary finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces.

Key observables. Accessible information ($A$), which quantifies the maximum extractable Shannon information, and the mutual information ($I_M$) associated with various measurements.

Important parameters / regimes. The probabilities $p$ and $q=1-p$ defining the ensemble, and the quantum states $ ho$ and $\sigma$.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is the definition of accessible information as the maximum mutual information over all possible quantum measurements (POVMs).

Paper structure. The paper builds the argument by first establishing inequalities relating general measurements to projective measurements, culminating in the proof of Shor's conjecture and the convex formulation of the problem.

Abstract

The accessible information of any given quantum ensemble quantifies the maximum amount of Shannon information that can be extracted from the ensemble by any quantum measurement. Almost three decades ago, Shor conjectured that the accessible information of any quantum dichotomy, that is, an ensemble of two states, is attained by a projective measurement. Recently, a proof of this conjecture restricted to the qubit case was published by Keil. Here, we conclusively settle this longstanding open problem. First, we show that Shor's conjecture follows, in arbitrary dimension, from a recent result by Fang, Fawzi and Fawzi, and we provide a self-contained, elementary proof. Second, for any given dichotomy and measurement, we provide the explicit construction of a projective measurement that outperforms such a measurement in extracting information from the given dichotomy. Third, while the computation of the accessible information is known to be non-convex in general, we frame the computation of the accessible information of dihotomies as a convex problem.

Scalable Simulation of Quantum Dynamics on Topological Quantum Hardware

Authors: Kritanjan Polley, Mark E. Tuckerman
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.19484v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 3/5

Summary. This paper presents a comprehensive framework to simulate complex quantum dynamics for molecular and condensed matter physics using topological quantum hardware. It achieves this by mapping time evolution operators onto non-Abelian anyon braiding operations, utilizing the Solovay-Kitaev algorithm for high accuracy. The methodology proves scalable, offering a robust pathway for simulating strongly correlated and non-equilibrium quantum systems on fault-tolerant devices.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly bridges the gap between theoretical quantum simulation needs (molecular/condensed matter dynamics) and the physical realization constraints of emerging quantum hardware (topological qubits).

Detailed structure

Main problem. Establishing a scalable and robust framework for simulating quantum dynamics in molecular and condensed matter systems directly on topological quantum hardware.

Main result. The framework successfully demonstrates the ability to approximate complex quantum dynamics, including molecular kinetics and spin-boson models, using non-Abelian anyon braiding.

Method. The core method involves approximating unitary time propagators using the Solovay-Kitaev algorithm by simulating quantum gates via the braiding of Fibonacci and Ising anyons.

Model / system. The work applies to a hierarchy of systems, including two-level systems, double-well potentials, spin-boson models, and molecular reaction kinetics, all mapped onto the physics of topological quantum hardware.

Key observables. Position autocorrelation function, expectation value of sigma_z, vibronic spectra, and reaction rates (k(T)).

Important parameters / regimes. Topological anyon types (Fibonacci, Ising), target accuracy (infidelity), and system parameters like coupling strengths (J, epsilon).

Assumptions / limitations. The fidelity of the simulation relies on the accuracy of the Solovay-Kitaev approximation and the underlying assumption of fault-tolerant topological gate operations.

Figures summary. Figures compare exact dynamics (solid lines) against results from topological braiding (scattered dots/dashed lines) for various systems, showing high fidelity across different physical regimes.

Paper structure. The paper develops a theoretical framework, starting with simple models (two-level systems) to demonstrate the core methodology (anyon braiding via SK algorithm), and then scales this approach up to complex condensed matter and molecular physics problems.

Abstract

Quantum computers offer a significant advantage in simulating quantum systems compared to classical computers for certain problems, although most current applications are limited to calculating static molecular properties using hybrid quantum-classical hardware. In this work, we establish a framework for the representation of quantum dynamics in molecular and condensed matter systems, designed for execution on topological quantum hardware. By leveraging the non-Abelian braiding statistics of Fibonacci and Ising anyons, we utilize the Solovay-Kitaev algorithm to approximate unitary propagators for a range of systems. We demonstrate the efficacy of these algorithms across a hierarchy of complexity, from two-level systems and one dimensional double-well potentials to condensed phase spin-boson models, simple molecules, and molecular reaction kinetics. These algorithms provide a scalable and robust pathway for simulating many-body condensed phase chemical physics on fault-tolerant quantum devices.

Summary. This paper proposes an OFDM-based system for Simultaneous Quantum and Classical Communication (SQCC) in challenging THz frequency-selective fading channels. By processing signals in the frequency domain, the OFDM scheme maintains a practical Secret Key Rate across various power allocations. This significantly improves performance compared to single-carrier methods, making THz quantum communication more viable.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses quantum communication in a high-frequency, complex channel regime (THz), which is a major frontier in quantum optics and quantum networks, requiring advanced signal processing techniques like OFDM.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the feasibility of cost-effective Simultaneous Quantum and Classical Communication (SQCC) in realistic, frequency-selective multipath Terahertz (THz) fading channels.

Main result. The proposed Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM) based SQCC scheme achieves a practical Secret Key Rate (SKR) over various power sharing scenarios, significantly outperforming its single-carrier counterpart.

Method. The authors propose an OFDM-based protocol that superimposes classical and quantum signals in the frequency domain, utilizing LDPC coded reconciliation schemes.

Model / system. The system operates in the THz band, modeling the channel as frequency-selective multipath fading. The protocol involves superimposing classical BPSK signals with Gaussian Modulated (GM) CV-QKD signals.

Key observables. Secret Key Rate (SKR), Bit Error Rate (BER), and Block Error Rate (BLER).

Important parameters / regimes. Power sharing ratio ($\zeta = P_Q/P_C$), Ricean factor ($K$), and the channel fading characteristics (frequency-selective).

Assumptions / limitations. The investigation focuses on multipath-induced channel dispersion, treating beamforming as a link-enabling mechanism, and using LDPC CV-QKD reconciliation.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate phase-space representations of the signals, protocol diagrams for SC and OFDM approaches, and plots showing SKR vs. distance, and BLER vs. power sharing ratio ($\zeta$).

Paper structure. The paper develops the SQCC protocol by first establishing the time-domain (SC) approach, then extending it to the frequency-domain using OFDM, and finally analyzing the performance metrics like SKR and BLER under varying power sharing ratios.

Abstract

The feasibility of cost-effective simultaneous quantum and classical communication (SQCC) transmitting both the quantum key and classical information via a superimposed coherent state is investigated both in optical and Terahertz (THz) bands. Since the existing THz SQCC schemes assume single-carrier (SC) transmission over flat fading channels, we embark on investigating SQCC in realistic frequency-selective multipath THz fading channels. We then propose an orthogonal frequency division multiplexing (OFDM) based SQCC system for time-invariant frequency-selective THz scenarios, supported by low-density parity-check coded (LDPC) multidimensional QKD reconciliation schemes. Our simulation results demonstrate that the OFDM-based SQCC scheme is capable of achieving a practical secret key rate (SKR) over a wide range of power sharing scenarios between the classical and quantum signals. By contrast its single-carrier counterpart requires the classical signal to be at least 100 times stronger than the quantum signal in THz SQCC.

Unified hydrodynamics for density and spin supersolids

Summary. This paper develops a unified hydrodynamic theory for two-component supersolids, modeling systems like dipolar Bose-Einstein condensates. By deriving equations of motion from symmetry and conservation laws, the authors predict the collective excitation spectrum, including sound speeds. The theory is rigorously benchmarked against microscopic calculations, confirming its quantitative accuracy across the spin- and density-supersolid phases.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, unified theoretical framework connecting macroscopic hydrodynamic measurements (like sound speed) directly to microscopic parameters (like dipolar interactions and superfluid fractions) in complex quantum matter states.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To formulate a general hydrodynamic theory describing the collective dynamics of multicomponent supersolids, which exhibit both density and spin modulations.

Main result. The hydrodynamic predictions for sound speeds quantitatively agree with microscopic Bogoliubov–de Gennes calculations across the entire phase diagram, confirming the theory's validity.

Method. The theory is derived from symmetry and conservation laws, leading to Euler-Lagrange equations whose normal-mode solutions are used to calculate sound speeds and elastic coefficients.

Model / system. The system is a binary dipolar Bose-Einstein condensate (or quantum gas mixture) confined to 1D, exhibiting supersolid phases characterized by density and spin modulations.

Key observables. Sound speeds of the three Goldstone branches ($c_{s}, c_{d\pm}$), superfluid fraction ($f_{sf}$), and elastic coefficients ($\alpha_{d ho d ho}$, $\alpha_{s ho s ho}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Relative dipole moment ($\mu_{m2}/\mu_{m1}$), interspecies scattering length ($a_{12}$), and the component imbalance.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the long-wavelength limit ($\omega = cq$) and utilizes the symmetric-mixture approximation for the quantum-fluctuation energy density ($\epsilon_{QF}$).

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the phase diagram showing spin- and density-supersolid regions, and provide hydrodynamic predictions for sound and elasticity along specific cuts (balanced and imbalanced).

Paper structure. The paper systematically derives the Lagrangian and Euler-Lagrange equations for the hydrodynamic fields, analyzes the resulting dynamical matrix in different limits (imbalanced, balanced spin, symmetric density), and compares the resulting dispersion relations against microscopic BdG calculations.

Abstract

Two-component supersolids can exhibit in-phase or out-of-phase component density modulations, corresponding to density and spin supersolids, respectively. We formulate a general hydrodynamic theory for the collective dynamics of multicomponent supersolids, guided by their broken symmetries and conservation laws. We benchmark it against microscopic calculations for a binary dipolar condensate confined to an infinite tube, finding quantitative agreement for the sound speeds of all three Goldstone branches throughout the density- and spin-supersolid regions. The resulting relations link collective-mode measurements to elastic coefficients and superfluid fractions.

Universality of the $1/9$ Magnetization Plateau and Quantum-Disordered States in the Kagome Family $\mathrm{Cs_8AB_3Ti_{12}F_{48}}$ ($A=\mathrm{Rb},\mathrm{Li}$; $B=\mathrm{K},\mathrm{Na}$)

Jeschke, Hiroyuki Nojiri, Akira Matsuo, Koichi Kindo, Miwako Takahashi, Yukio Noda, Taku J. Sato, Shiyan Li, Hiroaki Ueda, Gia-Wei Chern, Seung-Hun Lee

Summary. This study investigates how structural tuning, achieved by chemical pressure in the Kagome material Cs_8AB_3Ti_12F_48, affects magnetic ordering. By comparing expanded and compressed compounds, the researchers found that the 1/9 magnetization plateau persists robustly in expanded systems, suggesting it is a fundamental feature of frustrated kagome magnetism, independent of the more common 1/3 plateau.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a detailed experimental and computational study of geometric frustration in a solid-state magnetic system, directly addressing the emergence of fractional magnetization plateaus, which is central to understanding quantum magnetic ground states.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to resolve the microscopic origin and universality of the low-field 1/9 magnetization plateau in spin-1/2 kagome antiferromagnets.

Main result. The 1/9 plateau is robustly observed in expanded compounds even when the conventional 1/3 plateau is absent, suggesting the 1/9 feature may be a more universal characteristic of frustrated kagome magnetism.

Method. The research combines high-field magnetization measurements, specific-heat measurements, and first-principles DFT calculations, supplemented by structural refinement using neutron and X-ray diffraction.

Model / system. The physical system is the Kagome family of compounds, Cs_8AB_3Ti_12F_48, which are spin-1/2 antiferromagnets whose magnetic interactions are modeled by a Heisenberg Hamiltonian.

Key observables. Magnetization plateaus (1/9 and 1/3), specific heat anomalies, and derived magnetic exchange constants (J_i).

Important parameters / regimes. Chemical pressure tuning via alkali ion substitution (lattice expansion vs. compression) and high magnetic fields up to 60 T.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that structural changes (compression/expansion) directly modify the magnetic exchange network, and that the magnetic behavior can be mapped onto a Heisenberg model.

Figures summary. Figures show structural refinements and polyhedral representations; magnetization curves illustrate the emergence of the 1/9 plateau in expanded samples; specific heat data contrast the behavior of compressed versus expanded compounds.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the structural basis via joint X-ray/neutron refinement, then using DFT to map exchange interactions, and finally presenting high-field magnetization and specific heat measurements to correlate structural tuning with the magnetic plateau phases.

Abstract

The microscopic origin of the low-field $1/9$ magnetization plateau in spin-$1/2$ kagome antiferromagnets remains unresolved. Here, we show that chemical pressure reshapes the hierarchy of fractional magnetization plateaus in the titanium-based kagome family $\mathrm{Cs_8AB_3Ti_{12}F_{48}}$ ($A=\mathrm{Rb},\mathrm{Li}$; $B=\mathrm{K},\mathrm{Na}$). High-field magnetization measurements up to 60 T reveal a robust $1/9$ plateau-like phase in the expanded $\mathrm{Cs_8RbK_3Ti_{12}F_{48}}$ and $\mathrm{Cs_8LiK_3Ti_{12}F_{48}}$ compounds, despite the absence of the conventionally more robust $1/3$ plateau. In contrast, compressed $\mathrm{Cs_8LiNa_3Ti_{12}F_{48}}$ exhibits neither the $1/9$ plateau-like phase nor a quantum-disordered ground state. Specific-heat measurements and first-principles calculations show that lattice expansion preserves a frustrated, fully connected kagome exchange network and gapless quantum-disordered ground states, whereas compression reorganizes the exchange network into weakly coupled quasi-one-dimensional subsystems and induces successive magnetic transitions. These results demonstrate that the $1/9$ and $1/3$ plateaus need not share a common microscopic origin and suggest that the $1/9$ plateau may represent a more universal feature of frustrated spin-$1/2$ kagome magnetism.

Vehicle-Routing Clustering by Sparse Quantum Relaxation

Authors: Farzan Moosavi, Bilal Farooq
Type: both · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.19575v1

Summary. This paper applies quantum optimization (QRAO) to the complex Vehicle-Routing Clustering problem, decomposing it into a cluster-assignment layer. It analyzes the trade-off between graph sparsity and quantum compression, showing that while quantum methods are employed, the final success hinges on a robust classical repair mechanism, leading to near-optimal results on current hardware.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a detailed, practical study of applying quantum optimization techniques to complex combinatorial problems, offering empirical benchmarks on near-term quantum hardware regarding resource scaling and error mitigation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper tackles the cluster-assignment layer of the Capacitated Pickup-and-Delivery Problem with Time Windows (CPDPTW), aiming to solve it using quantum optimization techniques.

Main result. The authors demonstrate that while QRAO provides necessary non-diagonality, the dense nature of the conflict graph poses a major obstruction, and the classical repair mechanism is crucial for achieving near-optimal solutions.

Method. The approach uses a cluster-first-route-second decomposition, employing Quantum Random Access Optimization (QRAO) on a sparsified objective Hamiltonian, followed by a two-phase classical repair procedure.

Model / system. The problem is modeled via a weighted conflict graph derived from time-window incompatibilities. The quantum computation is implemented using QRAO encoding binary variables via non-commuting QRAC observables on quantum hardware simulators (Qiskit Aer, calibrated FakeTorino, IBM Heron).

Key observables. Mean gap (0.0016), mean device-vs-ideal Total Variation Distance (TVD) (0.279), compression ratio (rho_comp), LCU 1-norm, and final routing quality.

Important parameters / regimes. Sparsification level (q), number of request pairs (4 to 12), Krylov dimension (r), and noise scale (s).

Assumptions / limitations. The authors explicitly state they make no quantum-advantage claim, relying heavily on a classical 'repair after rounding' step to enforce feasibility.

Figures summary. The notes reference a regime map linking various quantum parameters to routing quality, and show hardware performance metrics (Table I) and the efficacy of the repair step (Figure 3, Figure 4).

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the necessity of decomposition and the failure of diagonal methods (SQD). It then introduces QRAO for non-diagonality, details the sparsification/compression trade-off, and finally presents the full pipeline including two-phase repair and hardware benchmarking.

Abstract

This paper studies the cluster-assignment layer of a cluster-first-route-second decomposition for the capacitated pickup-and-delivery problem with time windows (CPDPTW), instead of a direct route-ordering quadratic unconstrained binary optimization (QUBO). Sample-based subspace diagonalization (SQD) degenerates to best-of-shots for any diagonal optimization Hamiltonian, so a useful transfer to routing requires non-diagonal relaxation. Quantum random access optimization (QRAO) provides this non-diagonality by encoding binary variables through non-commuting quantum random access code (QRAC) observables. CPDPTW-derived conflict graphs expose a routing-specific obstruction because dense objectives and penalty-encoded constraints destroy QRAO compression. We keep the Hamiltonian sparse by encoding only the sparsified objective and enforcing one-hot, capacity, and cluster time-budget feasibility by repair after rounding. We make no quantum-advantage claim. Under strong repair the classical pass alone reaches near-optimal cost at the tested sizes. The result is a regime map linking sparsification, achieved compression, linear-combination-of-unitaries (LCU) 1-norm, heavy-hex two-qubit depth, sampling drift, and final routing quality on Qiskit Aer, calibrated FakeTorino, and IBM Heron ibm_quebec. Across a 108-row ideal-calibrated-hardware evaluation grid spanning four to twelve pickup-delivery request pairs, the hardware slice has mean gap 0.0016 and mean device-vs-ideal total variation distance (TVD) 0.279; a decoder ablation at sizes six through twelve isolates the non-diagonal signal.

Higher-Order Interactions in Complex Systems: Mechanisms, Behaviour, Representation and Reducibility

Summary. This review provides a comprehensive theoretical framework for analyzing higher-order interactions in complex systems, separating the concepts of mechanism, observable behavior, and mathematical representation. It argues that these three aspects are not equivalent, demonstrating that observed collective phenomena can arise from multiple underlying physical causes. The work establishes formal criteria for determining when a complex, high-order model can be accurately simplified or reduced to a lower-order description.

Why it may be interesting. Although focused on classical statistical mechanics, the rigorous framework for distinguishing mechanism from observation, and the formal treatment of reducibility, provides powerful conceptual tools applicable to open quantum systems where distinguishing between true quantum correlations and classical approximations is paramount.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to develop a unified framework for understanding higher-order interactions in complex systems by rigorously distinguishing between the underlying physical mechanism, the resulting observable behavior, and the mathematical representation used to describe them.

Main result. The core finding is that observed collective behavior does not uniquely determine the underlying mechanism, as the same behavior can arise from different mechanisms or through mere representational artifacts.

Method. The review employs conceptual analysis, drawing distinctions between mechanistic, statistical, and representational definitions, and formalizing the concept of 'reducibility' by comparing source models to admissible lower-order approximations.

Model / system. The analysis applies to general complex systems, including coupled oscillators (like the Kuramoto model), ecological communities, and neural networks, which are modeled using generalized Hamiltonians or non-equilibrium equations of motion containing higher-order coupling terms.

Key observables. Collective behaviors such as explosive synchronization, discontinuous phase transitions, and altered stability regimes.

Important parameters / regimes. The order of interaction (pairwise vs. higher-order), the target of reduction (microscopic rule vs. observable feature), and the tolerance level for approximation.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary limitation noted is that observational data alone is generally insufficient to uniquely establish the true underlying mechanism.

Figures summary. Figure 1 conceptually structures the relationship between mechanisms, behavior, and representation, while the notes imply tables summarizing the formalisms for defining higher-order interactions.

Paper structure. The paper is structured around distinguishing three core questions: what governs the system (mechanism), what does it produce (behavior), and how is it described (representation). It then formalizes the concept of reducibility and reviews information-theoretic tools for detection.

Abstract

Higher-order interactions couple three or more elements in ways that cannot be decomposed into pairwise contributions. In diverse complex systems they produce striking collective behaviours such as explosive synchronization, discontinuous transitions and altered stability. Neither such behaviour nor a description written in many-body terms establishes a many-body mechanism, since both depend on how the system is represented. This review distinguishes three questions: what mechanism governs the system, what behaviour does it produce, and how is either represented? Representation covers both which variables are included and how relations among them are encoded. We examine direct many-body mechanisms, nonlinear but pairwise mechanisms that mimic them, and indirect routes through shared environments or eliminated variables, together with temporal aggregation, an artefact of observation rather than a mechanism. The same behaviour can arise from different mechanisms, and a given higher-order mechanism has no universal behavioural consequence. We then formulate reducibility, the question of whether a higher-order model can be replaced by a lower-order one, as a comparison between a source model and an admissible class of lower-order models, relative to a target and a tolerance. The target may be a microscopic rule, an observable or a qualitative feature. Information-theoretic and inferential methods are reviewed as complementary tools for detecting higher-order dependence, testing lower-order alternatives and reconstructing interaction structure. However, observational data alone generally cannot establish the mechanism. We close by identifying open problems in linking mechanisms to measurable signatures, coarse-graining, identifiability and causality, and in extending reductions beyond mean field. (Abridged)

Summary. This work develops a theoretical framework using martingale theory to analyze how heat is dissipated in complex, non-equilibrium physical systems described by Langevin dynamics. It establishes that the statistical behavior of dissipated heat—whether it appears conserved, increasing, or decreasing over time—is mathematically linked to the local contraction or expansion of the system's phase space. This extends stochastic thermodynamics by providing quantitative criteria for heat fluctuation analysis.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a rigorous mathematical framework (martingale theory) to characterize non-equilibrium energy dissipation, which is a core topic in modern non-equilibrium statistical mechanics, relevant for understanding energy flow in complex quantum or classical systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the martingale properties of heat dissipated during non-equilibrium overdamped Langevin dynamics in heterogeneous media.

Main result. The heat dissipation process can exhibit transient martingale, submartingale, or supermartingale behavior, which is dictated by the sign of the phase-space contraction rate. This links heat fluctuation statistics to the non-linear dynamics of the system.

Method. The analysis employs martingale theory, utilizing Itô calculus to derive stochastic differential equations for quantities like $\exp(-eta Q_t)$ and relating their behavior to the divergence of the drift term.

Model / system. The model is a generic N-dimensional overdamped Langevin system governed by an Itô equation, applicable to systems like microscopic particles interacting with walls under gravity and double-layer forces.

Key observables. Heat Dissipation ($Q_t$), Stochastic Entropy Production ($S_{env}$), and Phase-Space Contraction ($\Lambda_t$).

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature ($T$), inverse temperature ($eta$), and the sign of the divergence $ abla \cdot (\mu \cdot \mathbf{F})$.

Assumptions / limitations. The system is assumed to follow an overdamped Langevin equation, and the analysis relies on the mathematical framework of martingales for Itô diffusions.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the dependency of $\langle \exp(-Q_t) angle$ on parameters, showing transient decreases when the process behaves as a supermartingale.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the theoretical framework by defining the Langevin dynamics and heat dissipation, develops the martingale criteria linking $Q_t$ to phase-space contraction, and concludes with theoretical bounds and applications to specific physical models.

Abstract

We investigate martingale properties of heat dissipated by generic non-equilibrium overdamped Langevin dynamics with multiple dimensions and transport coefficients that may depend explicitly on time and/or space. We find quantitative criteria that establish that heat dissipation may exhibit transient martingale (time-conserved), submartingale (time increasing), and supermartingale (time decreasing) properties in Langevin dynamics with drift coefficients with spatial non-linear dependencies. Furthermore, we reveal a tight link between the heat statistics at stopping (e.g. first-passage) times with that of a functional quantifying the phase-space contraction in the system dynamics. The theoretical results are used to predict the statistical properties of the heat dissipated by a spherical microscopic particle subject to gravity and double-layer forces and hydrodynamically interacting with a rigid wall, whose validity is confirmed by numerical simulations. These results extend the scope of stochastic energetics by revealing a non-trivial relation between heat fluctuations' statistical properties and the non-linear features of non-equilibrium processes.

Disorder-induced modulation of the nonlinear Hall effect in Weyl semimetals

Authors: Juan A. Cañas, Daniel A. Bonilla, A. Martín-Ruiz
Type: theory · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.20471v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 2/5 · methods for driven-dissipative 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical work examines the impact of impurities on the nonlinear Hall effect in Weyl semimetals using the semiclassical Boltzmann approach. It derives the necessary conductivity tensors by incorporating the microscopic details of impurity scattering into the transport relaxation time. The findings show that while the dominant response is isotropic, magnetic impurities introduce a measurable, helicity-dependent anisotropic correction.

Why it may be interesting. The explicit inclusion of disorder scattering mechanisms (like magnetic impurities) into the nonlinear transport response provides a concrete, microscopic link between material imperfections and exotic topological phenomena, which is relevant for understanding realistic quantum devices.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how impurity scattering modulates the nonlinear Hall effect in Weyl semimetals.

Main result. The nonlinear Hall response is determined by the Berry curvature and the momentum-dependent transport relaxation time, with anisotropic corrections arising from magnetic impurities.

Method. The analysis employs the semiclassical Boltzmann approach, deriving rectified and second-harmonic conductivity tensors by expanding the non-equilibrium distribution function up to second order.

Model / system. The system is Weyl semimetals, modeled using a low-energy effective Hamiltonian. The analysis incorporates various impurity scattering potentials (short-range, Gaussian, screened Coulomb, and magnetic).

Key observables. Nonlinear Hall response (rectified and second-harmonic conductivity tensors), transport relaxation time ($ au_{k}$), and the anomalous velocity associated with the Berry curvature.

Important parameters / regimes. Impurity coupling strength ($J$), impurity polarization ($\mathbf{S}$), and the electric field orientation relative to the impurity polarization.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the collision integral effect is entirely encoded in the momentum-dependent transport relaxation time ($ au_{k}$), and it neglects magnetic-field-induced orbital motion.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the general framework using the Boltzmann equation, then analyzing the effect of scalar disorder, and finally detailing the qualitatively different behavior induced by polarized magnetic impurities via second-order T-matrix calculations.

Abstract

We study the effects of impurity scattering on the nonlinear Hall response of Weyl semimetals within the semiclassical Boltzmann approach. We derive the rectified and second-harmonic conductivity tensors for a general momentum-dependent transport relaxation time and evaluate this quantity microscopically for short-range, Gaussian, screened Coulomb, and magnetic impurities. For scalar disorder, the relaxation time is isotropic and chirality independent. The nonlinear Hall response is then determined by the anomalous velocity associated with the Berry curvature, and a finite net response requires energetically inequivalent Weyl nodes to avoid cancellation between opposite chiralities. Polarized magnetic impurities lead to a qualitatively different behavior. We find that interference between the first- and second-order Born amplitudes generates a helicity-dependent anisotropic correction to the relaxation time. This anisotropy changes the tensor structure of the nonlinear Hall conductivity and gives rise to a finite second-harmonic current for suitable orientations of the electric field relative to the impurity polarization. For representative parameters, however, the anisotropic contribution is several orders of magnitude smaller than the dominant isotropic response. These results establish how the microscopic form of impurity scattering enters the nonlinear Hall response of Weyl semimetals through the transport relaxation time.

These results reveal that, despite the global delocalization of molecular orbitals, the correlation structure in cyclic systems is highly non-uniform and governed by symmetry-induced interference. Although formulated in terms of cyclic lattice systems, the present results extend straightforwardly to one-dimensional fermionic chains with periodic conditions, reflecting the underlying translational symmetry of the problem. The framework therefore provides a Hamiltonian-independent and universal reference for understanding correlation patterns in one-dimensional periodic fermionic systems.

Abstract

Useful quantum computation requires the fault-tolerant implementations of a universal gate set which are generically not efficiently simulable. We devise an algebraic framework that allows for efficient sampling from the measurement distribution of fault-tolerant circuits that implement diagonal logic gates in the third level of the Clifford hierarchy. Upon successful sampling we also provide sufficient conditions for decoding success. The resulting estimate for the logical failure rate is an overestimate, which becomes more accurate for blocks that are fault tolerant against arbitrary local errors. The non-Clifford simulation overhead is independent of the number of logical qubits, making the method particularly attractive for large-scale simulations. The framework is based on viewing the non-Clifford gates in the circuit as a cohomology invariant of an underlying spacetime fault complex. The method can also be interfaced with Clifford simulators to simulate larger fault-tolerant circuits and algorithmic subroutines.
