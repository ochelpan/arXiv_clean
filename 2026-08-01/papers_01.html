Summary. This work proposes that FeSe/cuprate heterostructures are excellent platforms for studying altermagnetism—a state with spin splitting but no net magnetization. By modeling two distinct physical mechanisms (Lieb lattice coupling and substrate-induced symmetry breaking), the authors demonstrate how these systems can be engineered to host AM, which is crucial for understanding its interplay with unconventional superconductivity.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on strongly correlated magnetism, the use of proximity effects and engineered lattice structures mirrors concepts in quantum interface engineering relevant to AMO physics, particularly concerning tunable electronic couplings at boundaries.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The research aims to realize and study altermagnetism (AM) in high-temperature superconducting cuprate heterostructures to investigate spin-split electronic bands without net magnetization.

Main result. Both a Lieb lattice mechanism involving Cu-Fe hybridization and a proximity effect due to substrate-induced symmetry breaking are shown to be viable routes for engineering AM in FeSe/cuprate systems.

Method. The study employs effective tight-binding models, parameterized using first-principles Density Functional Theory (DFT) calculations, to predict electronic band structures and spin conductivity.

Model / system. The physical platform is the FeSe/cuprate heterostructure, modeled as an effective CuFe2 Lieb lattice. The Hamiltonian incorporates hopping terms ($t_0$ for Cu-Fe coupling and $\delta t$ for substrate-induced inequivalence in FeSe).

Key observables. Band splittings (d-wave splitting), spin conductivity ($\sigma_{xy}^z$), and orbital character of the electronic states.

Important parameters / regimes. $t_0$ (Cu-Fe hopping), $\delta t$ (substrate-induced hopping difference), and $U$ terms for correlation effects.

Assumptions / limitations. The authors assume a checkerboard AFM ordering state for FeSe magnetism in some analyses, and they use DFT approximations (GGA+U) to derive model parameters.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the heterostructure geometry, band splittings as functions of $t_0$ and $\delta t$, Fermi surfaces, and color plots showing enhanced spin conductivity ($\sigma_{xy}^z$).

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the physical motivation (AM in cuprates), then develops an effective tight-binding model incorporating two distinct AM mechanisms. DFT calculations are used to parameterize this model, leading to quantitative predictions of band splittings and spin transport properties.

Abstract

Realizing altermagnetism in high-$T_c$ cuprate-based systems would provide a direct route for studying spin-split electronic bands in the absence of net magnetization and investigate their interplay with unconventional superconductivity. Here, we propose that FeSe/cuprate heterostructures offer such a platform, where a 45$^\circ$ twist of Cu and Fe layers creates an effective CuFe$_2$ Lieb lattice in which Fe magnetic order and Cu-Fe hybridization through the ligands induces altermagnetic $d$-wave spin splitting. A minimal tight-binding model shows that this mechanism is generic. Furthermore, a substrate-induced inequivalence of the two Se sites in FeSe provides a second route in which altermagnetism originates in the Fe layer and is transferred to the cuprate layer by proximity. Density functional theory calculations for FeSe/Bi$_2$Sr$_2$CuO$_6$ heterostructures confirm the viability of both mechanisms and reveal ways to enhance the spin splitting. These results establish superconducting cuprate/transition metal chalcogenide heterostructures as a promising setting for engineering altermagnetism and studying its coupling to unconventional superconductivity.

Summary. The researchers report the first experimental observation of a Moiré Time Crystal (MTC) using Rydberg atomic gases driven by two distinct radiofrequency fields. This work establishes the platform as a powerful tool for engineering complex temporal order in driven quantum many-body systems.

Why it may be interesting. Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal was the experimental observation and characterization of a Moiré Time Crystal (MTC), a non-equilibrium quantum phase arising from the coherent interference of two distinct driving frequencies.

Main result. The authors successfully demonstrated the MTC in Rydberg atomic gases, evidenced by a unique comb-like pattern in the Fourier spectrum that staggers as the relative frequency difference between the two drives is tuned.

Method. The experiment uses a bichromatic driving field applied to strongly interacting Rydberg atoms and analyzes the time-domain response via Fourier transformation to map out the system's spectral phase diagram.

Model / system. The platform is Floquet-driven Rydberg atomic gases, where dynamics are governed by a Hamiltonian incorporating long-range van der Waals interactions ($C_6/|r_i - r_j|^6$) and dissipation via Lindblad channels. The system is driven using two distinct radiofrequency (RF) fields.

Key observables. The spectral response, specifically the appearance of a 'beat-note comb' in the Fourier spectra, characterized by subharmonic periodicity ($f_1/2 + n f_2/2$) and fundamental frequencies.

Important parameters / regimes. Two distinct driving frequencies ($f_1$ and $f_2$), laser detunings ($\Delta$), and the ratio between these frequencies are crucial parameters for tuning the Moiré pattern.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that spectral features correspond to nonlinear mixing and competition between subharmonic components, which define the MTC state. The persistence of the order against perturbations in laser detuning is also a key finding.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate phase diagrams showing the switching of comb structures based on frequency ratios (e.g., $f_2/f_1$), and Fourier spectra confirming the staggered subharmonic comb structure under dual-RF driving.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the theoretical basis for MTCs in this system, detailing the experimental setup using bichromatic driving fields, presenting measurements of the spectral response across varying frequency ratios, and finally analyzing specific peak structures (like a 10 kHz difference frequency) to confirm the nonlinear mixing mechanism.

Abstract

A Moiré time crystal is a non-equilibrium quantum phase emerging from the coherent interference of two distinct frequencies, at least one being the intrinsic oscillation of a symmetry-broken time crystal. Its hallmark is an ultra-long beat period, reflecting a time-domain mapping of the Moiré fringes that arise from mismatched spatial lattices. However, to date, no experimental realization of such a Moiré time crystal has been reported. In this work, by applying a bichromatic driving field with two distinct frequencies, we demonstrate that the interplay between long-range Rydberg interactions and dissipation gives rise to a unique comb-like Moiré pattern characterized by a beat-note comb, which superimposes subharmonic periodicity and fundamental frequencies. This Moiré pattern formed by two mismatched drives is staggered in the spectrum as the frequency of one driver changes. We experimentally map the phase diagram of the system and identify a robust region where the Moiré temporal order persists against perturbations in laser detuning. The reported Moiré time crystal not only provides a controllable platform for exploring emergent slow-fast dynamics and synthetic space-time symmetries but also opens avenues for engineering complex temporal order in driven quantum many-body systems.

Summary. This paper develops a universal formalism to study Floquet dynamics in quantum systems with non-relativistic conformal invariance. Detailed structure

Main problem. Developing a formalism to study Floquet dynamics for quantum systems exhibiting non-relativistic conformal invariance.

Model / system. The model concerns periodically driven strongly correlated quantum systems, with trapped fermions near unitarity and resonant anyons serving as key physical examples. The dynamics are governed by a time-dependent Hamiltonian constructed from generators of an SO(2, 1) subalgebra.

Key observables. One-point functions (e.g., expectation value of the conformal generator), state fidelity ($F$), and the trace diagnostic $T = rac{1}{2} ext{Tr} U(T)$.

Important parameters / regimes. Drive frequencies ($\omega_D$), modulation amplitudes ($\mu_2^0, eta$), and the number of periods ($\ell$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the existence of non-relativistic conformal invariance and utilizes the $ ext{SO}(2, 1)$ algebraic structure to simplify the time evolution.

Paper structure. Abstract

We develop a formalism for studying Floquet dynamics for systems with {\it non-relativistic} conformal invariance in $d$ spatial dimensions. Our analysis indicates the existence of two dynamical phases, hyperbolic and elliptic, separated by a parabolic transition surface. We demonstrate this by studying the expectation value of a conformal generator in the many body ground state during the drive, as well as the fidelity of this state. Stroboscopically, they behave exponentially in the hyperbolic phase, show oscillatory behavior in the elliptic phase, and exhibit power-law on the transition surface. Our analysis is completely universal and can be directly applied to several systems including trapped fermions near unitarity and resonant anyons. The former can provide experimental signatures of these dynamical phases. We also comment on a holographic perspective of such driven non-relativistic CFTs and demonstrate that the hyperbolic phase is associated with a timelike stationary-limit surface, such as an ergosphere, in the bulk, while the parabolic phase corresponds to an extremal Killing horizon.

Abstract
