Quantum Error Mitigation with Diffusion-Like Models

Summary. The paper introduces an AI-assisted framework to mitigate quantum noise caused by environmental coupling, modeling the process as a diffusion-like trajectory. It trains neural networks on synthetic data from weak measurements to learn an effective inverse map that reconstructs the original quantum state from noisy observations. This offers a powerful hybrid classical-quantum approach for approximating non-unitary dynamics in noisy quantum systems.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete, machine learning-driven methodology for tackling open quantum system dynamics in noisy environments, which is crucial for scaling up near-term quantum hardware by mitigating coherence loss.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses quantum error mitigation by developing an AI-assisted framework to estimate the initial state of a quantum system corrupted by decoherence.

Main result. They propose using machine learning models (like U-Nets and Transformers) trained on synthetic data from weak measurements to learn an effective, non-unitary denoising map that approximates the inverse noisy evolution channel.

Method. The core method involves training neural networks to map observed noisy density matrices ($ ho_t$) back to their estimated pre-noise state ($ ho_0$), mimicking classical diffusion model inversion techniques in a quantum context.

Model / system. The system is an arbitrary quantum state undergoing decoherence modeled by sequential local weak measurements, which generate a quantum diffusion process. The framework treats the noise as a sequence of non-unitary operations.

Key observables. Reconstructed initial density matrix ($ ho_0$), noisy intermediate states (${ ho_t}$), and the fidelity of reconstruction across single and multi-qubit registers.

Important parameters / regimes. Measurement strength parameter ($\epsilon$) and the noise process parameters derived from Pauli basis measurements.

Assumptions / limitations. The inverse process is an approximation, not a strictly physical quantum channel; local measurement independence is assumed for global reconstruction; and post-processing filters are used to enforce density matrix constraints.

Figures summary. Figures schematically illustrate the forward noisy evolution via weak measurements and the AI model's role in approximating the reverse path from $\{ ho_t\}$ back to $ ho_0$, comparing global vs. local reconstruction capabilities.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the physical problem of decoherence via weak measurements, then draws an analogy to classical diffusion models, details the training process using various ML architectures (LSTM, ViT, U-Net), and concludes with rigorous mathematical filtering techniques for ensuring output validity.

Abstract

Coupling between a quantum system and its environment causes decoherence by transferring information from the system to environmental degrees of freedom. When discretized in time, such interactions can be interpreted as sequences of weak measurements that provide an effective model of noisy quantum dynamics. Motivated by this picture, we propose an AI-assisted error-mitigation framework for quantum diffusion processes generated by sequential local weak measurements. The forward process progressively erases information from the input state through weak measurements performed in randomly selected Pauli bases, producing basis-dependent local dephasing and locally depolarizing dynamics on average. Machine-learning models are trained on exact synthetic density matrices to learn a channel- and distribution-specific denoising map and estimate the corresponding pre-noise state. We benchmark the approach on single-qubit states and separable and entangled multi-qubit registers. We also study distribution-dependent local-to-global reconstruction, in which local reduced density matrices are used to reconstruct the global state. This experimentally motivated setting relies on locally accessible information and is therefore compatible with noisy and distributed quantum systems. More broadly, the framework provides a hybrid classical-quantum approach for approximating non-unitary dynamics and mitigating coherence loss.

Summary. This theoretical paper develops generalized fluctuation relations for calculating work extracted from quantum devices that operate based on a first-detection trigger mechanism. It shows that these relations correct the standard Jarzynski equality by incorporating an entropic term related to the average detection time. The results establish fundamental, measurable limits on energy conversion in such measurement-driven quantum engines.

Why it may be interesting. This work extends fundamental theorems like the Jarzynski equality into the regime of measurement-induced quantum engines, providing new bounds on energy extraction that depend on the measurement protocol itself. This is highly relevant for understanding non-equilibrium thermodynamics in open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper derives quantum fluctuation relations characterizing the work extractable from a quantum device undergoing repeated projective measurements triggered by a first-detection event.

Main result. Two new fluctuation relations are established, correcting the standard Jarzynski equality with an entropic term dependent on the mean first-detection time. These relations provide fundamental limits on both total and extracted work.

Method. The derivation utilizes principles of quantum thermodynamics, applying Jensen's inequality to relate expectation values involving exponential functions of work, and employing time-reversal symmetry arguments.

Model / system. The model is a quantum device subjected to repeated projective measurements (a 'quantum first-detection information engine'). The analysis considers the general case where the system interacts with an external environment.

Key observables. Work extracted ($W_{ ext{ex}}$), total work exchanged ($W_{ ext{tot}}$), average first-detection time ($\langle n angle$), and survival probability ($S_\infty$).

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature ($eta$), measurement interval ($ au$), and the initial state $ ho_{in}$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is based on the Two-Projective-Measurement (TPM) scheme, and certain normalization procedures are required if the survival probability $S_\infty[ ho_{in}] > 0$.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates a 'First detection information-to-work converter' setup. Figure 2 compares extracted work ($\dot{w}_{ ext{ex}}$) against its upper bound derived from Jensen's inequality and first-detection mediated ergotropy.

Paper structure. The paper builds upon the standard Quantum Jarzynski Equality (QJE) by incorporating the non-Markovian effects of repeated measurements. It derives fluctuation relations for total work and extracted work, showing how these are modified by terms related to the mean first-detection time.

Abstract

We derive two quantum fluctuation relations for systems undergoing repeated projective measurements. These fluctuation relations characterize the work that can be extracted from a quantum device when a first-detection event triggers a mechanical operation leading to positive work production. The correction to the standard quantum Jarzynski equality depends logarithmically on the mean first-detection time for the time-reversed dynamics. Application of Jensen's inequality leads to fundamental limits on both the total work involved in the repeated measurements and final mechanical operation, and the extracted work alone. The general case of a device connected to an external environment is also considered.

Summary. The paper develops a geometric framework using state graphs and hitting times to detect slow relaxation in many-body quantum systems. By analyzing the spectral radius of the graph's hitting matrix, the authors show that this metric acts as a sensitive indicator, sharply changing at the transition into slow dynamics across multiple model types.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, unified graph-theoretic formalism to diagnose non-equilibrium dynamics in complex quantum systems, which is highly relevant for understanding thermalization and glassy behavior in AMO or condensed matter settings.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The research aims to identify and characterize slow relaxation dynamics in complex many-body quantum systems.

Main result. Slow dynamics are robustly identified by analyzing the geometry of the state graph, specifically through a sharp increase in the spectral radius of the hitting matrix at the transition point.

Method. The authors adapt tools from quantum random walks theory to construct and analyze the 'many-body state graph' using quantum hitting times derived from the unitary time-evolution operator.

Model / system. Three paradigmatic models are studied: the Rosenzweig-Porter model (RPM), the quantum East model (QEM), and the triangular lattice gas model (TLGM). The analysis is general for systems with discrete Hilbert spaces governed by a Hamiltonian H.

Key observables. Pairwise hitting times ($h_{ij}$), the kernel function $k_{ij}(T)$, and the spectral radius of the hitting matrix ($ ho(\hat{h})$).

Important parameters / regimes. Control parameters tuning the system across regimes, such as $\gamma$ in RPM or $s$ in QEM, which dictate the transition between fast and slow dynamics.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on constructing a state graph where nodes are basis states and using asymptotic limits of time-averaged quantities to characterize long-time behavior.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate how $ ho(\hat{h})$ changes across dynamical regimes, showing sharp increases at the onset of slow dynamics for QEM and RPM. Other figures show the evolution of $k_{ij}(T)$ over time T.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the state-graph formalism using quantum hitting times; it applies this framework to three distinct models (RPM, QEM, TLGM); it establishes that graph geometry metrics like $ ho(\hat{h})$ serve as universal indicators for slow relaxation transitions; and finally, it discusses the connection between these geometric measures and autocorrelation function decay.

Abstract

We adapt tools from the theory of quantum random walks to investigate slow relaxation dynamics through the many-body state graph. Specifically, we construct a probe of heterogeneity between basis states defined using hitting times derived from the unitary time-evolution operator. We find that the state-graph geometry, encoded by the pairwise hitting time of basis states, is a highly sensitive indicator of slow relaxation dynamics in a variety of systems. We study three paradigmatic models: the Rosenzweig-Porter model, the quantum East model, and the triangular lattice gas model, exhibiting a sudden onset of slow dynamics upon tuning of a control parameter. As a global characterization of the graph geometry, we analyze the spectral radius of the hitting matrix. We find that it increases sharply at the onset of slow dynamics, spanning many orders of magnitude, with a characteristic crossing point at the transition. Our work provides a geometric framework for describing and identifying phases with slow relaxation using a unified graph-theoretic formalism.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the formation, structure, and energetics of bound states between a mobile impurity and pinned quasihole excitations within Fractional Chern Insulators (FCIs). The ultimate goal is to establish controllable protocols for manipulating these anyonic composite objects.

Main result. The binding energy analysis provides a direct method to extract the fractional charge of the underlying quasihole. Furthermore, controlled transport of this composite object can be demonstrated by externally steering the pinning potential.

Model / system. The system is modeled using the interacting Harper–Hofstadter model deep in the FCI regime, applicable to quantum-engineered platforms. Important parameters / regimes. Interaction strength ($U/J$), flux ($\phi$), filling fraction ($ u=1/2, 1/3$), and the strength of external pinning potentials ($V_h$).

Abstract

Mobile impurities provide a powerful means of probing correlated and topological quantum matter, through their dressing by the surrounding medium and the practical probes granting access to the resulting composite object. Motivated by the recent observation of anyon-impurity composites in the solid state, as well as recent realizations of Laughlin-type states in engineered lattice systems, we investigate the formation of a bound state between a mobile impurity and a single pinned quasihole in the interacting Harper-Hofstadter model deep in the fractional Chern insulator regime. Combining analytical arguments with large-scale numerical simulations, we characterize the structure, energetics, and stability of hybrid anyon-impurity bound states, and show that their binding energy provides direct access to the fractional charge of the quasihole under conditions that we identify. We further demonstrate that the composite object can be coherently transported by externally steering the quasihole pinning potential. Our results establish a realistic pathway for controlled anyon-impurity manipulation in quantum-engineered platforms, enabling experimentally feasible protocols for braiding.

Summary. This paper proposes an optimal strategy for scheduling syndrome measurements in quantum error-correcting memory, treating the measurement interval as an optimizable control parameter rather than a fixed clock cycle. By deriving that the optimal interval scales inversely with code distance ($\Delta t^* \propto 1/d$), the authors show this leads to an exponential suppression of logical errors. Furthermore, they introduce an adaptive timing protocol that significantly outperforms static scheduling when noise is bursty.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides crucial theoretical guidance for designing next-generation quantum memories by moving beyond fixed operational cycles. The focus on optimizing the measurement schedule itself is a key area in open quantum systems and fault tolerance theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is optimizing the syndrome-measurement timing ($\Delta t$) in quantum error-correcting codes to minimize logical errors by balancing accumulating idling noise against measurement-induced faults.

Main result. Optimal scheduling requires setting the interval inversely proportional to the code distance ($\Delta t^* \propto 1/d$), which yields an exponential reduction in logical error rates compared to fixed-interval protocols. Adaptive strategies further improve performance, especially during noise bursts.

Method. The authors developed a phenomenological logical-noise model to analytically derive the optimal timing scaling and analyzed time-dependent noise using adaptive control theory.

Model / system. The work focuses on fault-tolerant quantum memories implemented via rotated surface codes. The error sources modeled include idling errors (exponentially dependent on $\Delta t$) and measurement faults (linearly dependent on measurement frequency).

Key observables. Logical error rate ($p_L$), optimal syndrome interval ($\Delta t^*$), relative suppression factor ($\Gamma_{ ext{opt}}$), and the time-dependent noise activity $\lambda(t)$.

Important parameters / regimes. Code distance ($d$), idling noise rate ($\lambda$), measurement fault probabilities ($p_{ ext{stab}}, p_{ ext{read}}$), and experimental parameters from Google's Willow device.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on a phenomenological logical-noise model, and the adaptive strategy assumes instantaneous knowledge of the time-dependent noise profile $\lambda(t)$.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the trade-off between error sources vs. $\Delta t$, showing that $\Delta t \propto 1/d$ yields exponentially lower errors than constant intervals; other figures compare fixed vs. adaptive protocols for dynamic noise.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the fundamental trade-off, derives the optimal scaling $\Delta t^* \propto 1/d$, and then extends this analysis to time-dependent noise using an adaptive control strategy, concluding with quantitative predictions based on current experimental hardware parameters.

Abstract

Syndrome-measurements timing is usually treated as a fixed clock cycle of a quantum error-correcting code. For quantum memories, however, the intra-measurement interval is itself an optimizable control parameter: measuring too rarely allows idling errors to accumulate, whereas measuring too often introduces measurement-induced faults. We propose a phenomenological logical-noise model for this trade-off and analytically show that the optimal syndrome-measurements interval scales inversely proportionally with the code distance and that this produces an exponential reduction of logical-error rates in the distance relative to constant-interval schedules. Furthermore, for time-dependent idling noise, we develop an adaptive timing strategy based on the measured syndrome activity that outperforms every fixed-interval protocol, with largest gains for short but strong noise bursts. Simulations of rotated surface-code memories with matching decoding validate the phenomenological model, the distance-dependent optimum, and the adaptive-strategy improvement. Moreover, with the experimental noise parameters reported by Google in Nature 638 (2025), our model predicts reductions in logical-error rates per unit time of up to $40\%$.

Summary. This theoretical paper analyzes spin-spin entanglement in Compton scattering, treating the outgoing photon and nucleon as a two-qubit system. It develops formalisms using helicity amplitudes to calculate the entanglement measure ($\lambda_{\min}$), demonstrating that polarization is crucial for generating entanglement. The findings suggest this quantum diagnostic tool can map out detailed electromagnetic structure of nucleons.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on particle physics (QCD/Electromagnetism), the core methodology—quantifying entanglement via density matrices and partial transposition criteria ($\lambda_{\min}$)—is directly transferable to analyzing quantum correlations in complex many-body or open quantum systems studied in AMO physics.

Main problem. The study investigates spin-spin entanglement generated in the final state photon-nucleon system during Compton scattering at both low and high energies.

Main result. The authors established a no-go theorem for unpolarized scattering with real amplitudes, but found that polarized scattering reveals rich patterns of maximally entangled Bell states, suggesting entanglement is a novel tool to probe nucleon electromagnetic properties.

Method. The analysis involves calculating the two-qubit spin density matrix ($ ho$) using helicity amplitudes derived from scattering cross-sections in different kinematic regimes (low/high energy).

Model / system. The physical system is Compton scattering off a spin-1/2 nucleon (proton or neutron), treated as an outgoing photon and nucleon two-qubit quantum state.

Paper structure. Abstract

We study spin-spin entanglement in the final state photon-nucleon system in Compton scattering, both at low energy below the pion threshold and at high energy in perturbative QCD to next-to-leading order. We first establish a no-go theorem showing that, for any spin-$\frac{1}{2}$ target, entanglement cannot be generated in unpolarized Compton scattering if the scattering amplitudes are real. We then consider polarized Compton scattering off the electron, the proton and the neutron. At low energy, we uncover a rich variety of maximally entangled Bell states and their unitary equivalents realized across different regions of the kinematic plane. Interestingly, the proton and neutron targets exhibit distinct patterns of entanglement. In the neutron case, the electric and magnetic polarizabilities dramatically influence the pattern and even the existence of entanglement. This suggests that entanglement can serve as a novel tool for investigating the detailed electromagnetic properties of the nucleons.

Summary. This theoretical work predicts that strange metal behavior, characterized by linear temperature dependence of resistivity ($ ho \propto T$), persists at an unconventional quantum critical point in the 2D Hubbard model. Main problem. The study investigates strange metal behavior and its connection to unconventional quantum critical points (QCPs) in two dimensions where standard Fermi liquid theory breaks down.

Main result. The authors predict that resistivity scales linearly with temperature ($ ho \propto T$) at the QCP, even when the single-particle self-energy does not exhibit a simple linear-in-$T$ dependence along the Fermi surface.

Method. Non-perturbative calculations using an improved two-particle self-consistent approach (TPSC+) are employed to analyze correlation functions and transport properties.

Model / system. The system modeled is the two-dimensional Hubbard model on a square lattice with nearest-neighbor hopping, focusing on fillings near but above half-filling ($n>1$).

Abstract

Properties of the two-dimensional Hubbard model with nearest-neighbor hopping are under scrutiny in cold-atom experiments and diagrammatic quantum Monte Carlo. Given the controlled nature of these approaches, it is timely to make predictions about strange-metal behavior and its relation to quantum-critical properties. Even for interaction strengths below the Mott transition, several peculiarities occur at the quantum critical point separating Fermi liquid and incommensurate spin-density wave order. Here, we predict, using the non-perturbative improved two-particle self-consistent approach, that for correlation lengths ranging from about one to one hundred lattice spacings, Kohn anomalies on the underlying Fermi surface lead to unconventional critical exponents. The temperature dependence of the single-particle self-energy acquires strong momentum dependence along the Fermi surface with no clear evidence of Landau quasiparticles. Nevertheless, we observe strange-metal behavior, namely, resistivity that scales linearly with temperature as $T\rightarrow{0}$. Our work shows that a linear temperature dependence of resistivity arises without a linear-in-temperature self-energy along the Fermi surface, as is often assumed.

Summary. This paper introduces the Hadamard Sensing Channel (HSC), a deterministic technique to suppress spurious signals that degrade quantum sensor readings due to imperfect microwave pulses. By leveraging the orthogonality of Hadamard matrices, HSC achieves signal cancellation superior to probabilistic methods like Phase Randomization. It offers a robust and hardware-efficient solution for high-fidelity nanoscale magnetometry.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a mathematically exact and hardware-friendly protocol for mitigating systematic errors in quantum sensing measurements, which is crucial for advancing high-resolution magnetometry experiments involving open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Spurious responses generated by finite duration microwave pulses during dynamical decoupling sequences compromise quantum sensing fidelity in nanoscale systems.

Main result. The proposed Hadamard Sensing Channel (HSC) deterministically cancels these spurious signals using a finite set of orthogonal phase patterns, matching ideal suppression while being more robust than probabilistic methods.

Method. HSC employs a deterministic phase-design framework based on Hadamard matrices to average out unwanted signal components over a finite number of runs.

Abstract

Dynamical decoupling sequences are essential for nanoscale quantum sensing, but the finite duration of microwave pulses generates spurious responses. While phase randomization (PR) protocol can suppress these artifacts, they rely on probabilistic averaging. This introduces an inherent statistical variance that demands excessive sequence lengths and stringent hardware capabilities for true random phase generation. Here, we propose Hadamard sensing channel (HSC), a deterministic phase-design framework based on Hadamard matrices. HSC completely eliminates the statistical variance of PR by exactly and deterministically canceling spurious signals using a finite set of orthogonal phase patterns. Simulations confirm that HSC matches the ideal suppression of PR but exhibits superior robustness against control errors. HSC offers a mathematically exact and hardware-friendly solution for reliable high-resolution nanoscale nuclear magnetic resonance.

Abstract

We investigate the open quantum dynamics of a system of two masses interacting with an environment of linearized gravitational waves. We formulate the analysis in terms of the observable proper distance between the two masses, and show that the canonical variables obtained from the standard Lagrangian, expressed in terms of the Fermi normal coordinates, are not suitable for an effective description of the system. We resolve this issue through a unitary transformation that provides a physically meaningful system--environment decomposition and derive the master equation to leading order in $G$. Its dissipative sector reproduces the classical energy loss due to gravitational-wave emission, while the noisy contributions suppress coherences between states with different mass quadrupole, or effectively, different proper separations. In the regime where the proper distance can be described by considering small quantum fluctuations around an average distance $l_0$, the dynamics reduces to a Caldeira--Leggett-type equation, with a decoherence rate dependent on the baseline length $l_0$.
