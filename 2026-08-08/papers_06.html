The Hidden Subgroup Problem in Semidirect Products and Quasi-Hamiltonian Groups

Authors: Mauro E. S. Morales
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.05321v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops polynomial-time quantum algorithms for solving the Hidden Subgroup Problem (HSP) in specific classes of non-Abelian finite groups, including semidirect products and quasi-Hamiltonian groups. It achieves this by exploiting deep structural properties of these groups and refining quantum measurement techniques like the block measurement protocol. The research advances the theoretical understanding of what computational problems can be efficiently solved on a quantum computer using group structure knowledge.

Why it may be interesting. This work pushes the boundaries of quantum complexity theory by extending known quantum algorithms beyond simple Abelian structures to complex, structured non-Abelian groups relevant in abstract algebra.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the Hidden Subgroup Problem (HSP) for non-Abelian finite groups, which is significantly harder than the Abelian case.

Main result. Polynomial-time quantum algorithms are provided for two structured families of non-Abelian groups: semidirect products $A times_{\varphi} \mathbb{Z}_{p^k}$ and quasi-Hamiltonian groups.

Method. The methods involve adapting techniques from Abelian HSP solvers, utilizing structural properties like modular subgroup lattices (for quasi-Hamiltonian groups), and employing advanced quantum measurement protocols like the block measurement technique.

Model / system. The theoretical model is abstract group theory applied to quantum computation; no specific physical Hamiltonian or lattice system is described. The focus is on solving a computational problem defined over finite groups $G$.

Key observables. Success probability of the quantum algorithm, which must be shown to be high enough (e.g., $\geq rac{\phi(N)}{N} 2^{mt} p$) for polynomial time complexity.

Important parameters / regimes. Group structure parameters such as $p$ (prime), $k$, and the exponent of the Abelian factor, $ ext{Exp}(A)$. Efficiency depends on conditions like $ ext{Exp}(A)/p = ext{polylog}(|G|)$.

Assumptions / limitations. The algorithms rely on specific structural assumptions about the groups (e.g., quasi-Hamiltonian structure, or $A$ having bounded generator rank) and assume black-box access to group operations.

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically illustrates the inclusion relationship between various group classes: Nilpotent $\le 2$, Quasi-Hamiltonian, Dedekind, and Abelian groups.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the context of HSP difficulty in non-Abelian settings. It then details algorithms for semidirect products (Theorem A/B), followed by a dedicated treatment for quasi-Hamiltonian groups (Theorem C). The latter sections involve deep analysis of quantum measurement protocols (PGM) and structural comparisons with other group families.

Abstract

Several early quantum algorithms, including Simon's algorithm and Shor's period-finding are instances of the hidden subgroup problem (HSP) over finite abelian groups. No polynomial-time quantum algorithm is known for the HSP over arbitrary non-abelian finite groups. The non-Abelian case is of particular interest because some instances, such as the dihedral and symmetric group HSPs, are connected to lattice problems and graph isomorphism, respectively. In this work, we give polynomial-time quantum algorithms for two further families containing non-Abelian groups. First, we consider groups of the form $G=A\rtimes_{\varphi} \mathbb{Z}_{p^k}$, with $A$ finite Abelian, $p$ prime, $k\in \mathbb{N}$ and the action of $\mathbb Z_{p^k}$ is generated by the scalar automorphism $a\mapstoμa$, for some $μ\in\mathbb Z_{\operatorname{Exp}(A)}^\times$, where $\mathrm{Exp}(A)$ is the exponent of $A$. Our algorithm is efficient when $A$ has bounded generator rank and $\mathrm{Exp}(A)/p=\mathrm{polylog}(|G|)$. This includes the case $A=\mathbb{Z}_N$ for $N\in\mathbb{N}$ and $k=1$, studied by Bacon, Childs and van Dam (FOCS 2005), and $A=\mathbb{Z}_{q^r}$ with $q$ prime and $r\in \mathbb{N}$ studied by van Dam and Dey (TQC 2014). Second, we give a polynomial-time quantum algorithm for finite quasi-Hamiltonian groups under a mild assumption on the input structure. Quasi-Hamiltonian groups are finite nilpotent groups with modular subgroup lattice, or equivalently the finite groups in which every subgroup is permutable. As far as we know, this is the first quantum algorithm to exploit the modularity of the subgroup lattice for solving the HSP. This extends, under the aforementioned structured input assumption, the quantum algorithm for Dedekind groups given by Hallgren, Russell, and Ta-Shma (SIAM J. Comput. 32, 2003).

Time-Reversal Selection Rules for Quantum Error Correction

Authors: Eric Kubischta, Ian Teixeira
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.06304v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work applies time-reversal symmetry to the theory of quantum error correction, showing that this symmetry imposes strong selection rules on the underlying error algebra. These rules automatically satisfy many necessary conditions for fault tolerance, simplifying the requirements for reliable quantum computation. The framework unifies concepts from spectral protection (Kramers pairs) and algebraic constraints (Knill-Laflamme conditions).

Why it may be interesting. It provides a deep structural constraint on quantum codes derived from fundamental symmetries, which is relevant for designing robust quantum memories or computation protocols in physical systems exhibiting time-reversal symmetry.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how applying time-reversal symmetry ($\Theta$) imposes structural selection rules on quantum error correction (QEC) codes.

Main result. Time-reversal invariance forces all even-weight Pauli operators to act as scalars, automatically satisfying the even-weight Knill–Laflamme conditions and simplifying error detection requirements.

Method. The authors use the antiunitary time-reversal operator $\Theta$ to derive parity selection rules for Pauli strings and reinterpret Rains' shadow enumerator through this symmetry.

Model / system. The analysis is performed on quantum codes defined over spin-1/2 particles (qubits) and generalizes to multi-spin systems. The focus is on the time-reversal invariance of the code subspace itself.

Key observables. Kramers degeneracy, Pauli weight parity, Knill–Laflamme conditions satisfaction, and coefficients of the Rains shadow enumerator ($S_w$).

Important parameters / regimes. The degree of error correction distance ($d$) and the number of spins ($n$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes working with spin-1/2 particles and relies on the antiunitary nature of time reversal ($\Theta$).

Paper structure. The paper establishes the link between spectral protection (Kramers degeneracy) and algebraic error protection by applying $\Theta$ to QEC codes, deriving selection rules, and reinterpreting shadow invariants.

Abstract

We apply time-reversal symmetry to quantum codes and show that it imposes parity selection rules on the physical error algebra. A time-reversal-invariant logical qubit on an odd number of spins is a Kramers doublet, forcing every even-weight Pauli to act as a scalar. Consequently, all even-weight Knill--Laflamme conditions hold automatically, so single-qubit error detection implies correction. We then reinterpret the Rains shadow enumerator through time reversal: each coefficient is a sum of error-resolved overlaps between a code and its time-reversed image.

Summary. This work develops a generalized time-dependent Ginzburg-Landau theory for disordered, noncentrosymmetric superconductors using the Keldysh formalism. It determines that nonreciprocal Lifshitz invariants appear in both the free energy and the dynamic fluctuation terms. The results unify conflicting theoretical predictions by introducing a single crossover function controlling the system's response to disorder.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous application of fluctuation-dissipation theorems and symmetry principles (Onsager reciprocity) to derive non-equilibrium dynamics in a superconducting context provides advanced tools for understanding dissipation beyond simple mean-field treatments.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study derives a generalized time-dependent Ginzburg-Landau theory for disordered noncentrosymmetric superconductors, focusing on the appearance of Lifshitz-type nonreciprocal couplings in the quantum (Keldysh) sector.

Main result. The kinetic Lifshitz invariants are governed by a single crossover kernel dependent on the ratio of the spin-relaxation rate to temperature, reconciling previous conflicting results for magnetoelectric couplings.

Method. The theory is derived from the Keldysh nonlinear sigma model and utilizes principles like the Fluctuation-Dissipation Theorem (FDT) and Onsager reciprocity to determine dynamics.

Model / system. The minimal model is a disordered noncentrosymmetric superconductor realized via a two-dimensional electron gas with Rashba spin-orbit coupling and an in-plane Zeeman field. The analysis uses the Keldysh formalism to construct the effective action.

Key observables. Superconducting diode efficiency, fluctuation-induced magnetochiral anisotropy, relaxation rate of fluctuations ($\mathbf{q}$ dependence), and Langevin noise power (which acquire $\mathbf{q}$-odd and $B$-field odd terms).

Important parameters / regimes. The ratio of the Dyakonov-Perel spin-relaxation rate ($\Gamma_t$) to temperature ($T$), which dictates the crossover between weak-relaxation and relaxation-dominated regimes.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is performed in the diffusive regime and relies on gauge covariance arguments within the Keldysh formalism.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the general context of Lifshitz invariants, applies this to superconductors using the Keldysh NLSM, derives the full dynamical theory (TDGL equation), and computes specific nonreciprocal couplings in various limits defined by $\Gamma_t/T$.

Abstract

We derive the generalized time-dependent Ginzburg-Landau theory of a disordered noncentrosymmetric superconductor from the Keldysh nonlinear sigma model, using a two-dimensional electron gas with Rashba spin-orbit coupling and an in-plane Zeeman field as a minimal model. On the thermodynamic side we construct the Lifshitz invariants of the free energy, the linear and cubic gradient terms and the momentum-odd part of the quartic vertex, and trace their dependence on disorder. These couplings are governed by a single closed-form kernel controlled by the ratio of the Dyakonov-Perel spin-relaxation rate to temperature, interpolating between the weak-relaxation regime, where the invariants are suppressed, and the relaxation-dominated regime, where the helical modulation of the order parameter saturates at a universal, disorder-independent value. This crossover reconciles conflicting results for the magnetoelectric couplings of dirty Rashba superconductors. Because the theory is formulated on the Keldysh contour, it also determines the dissipative dynamics: the relaxation rate of a fluctuation with pair momentum $\mathbf{q}$, and hence, by the fluctuation-dissipation theorem, the Langevin noise power, acquires a term odd in $\mathbf{q}$ and odd in the magnetic field. The structure of this kinetic Lifshitz invariant is dictated by Onsager reciprocity: friction and noise renormalize in lockstep, so equal-time fluctuations remain Gibbsian while the dynamics are nonreciprocal. As applications we compute the superconducting diode efficiency near $T_c$, where the cubic invariant competes with the quartic vertex and with even higher-gradient terms rendered odd by the helical shift, reversing the sign of the diode coefficient, and the fluctuation-induced magnetochiral anisotropy above $T_c$, where the current-resolved nonreciprocal resistance forms a plateau across the Gaussian regime.

Summary. This work theoretically analyzes the Electric Hall Effect in magnetic Weyl Semimetals, revealing unique scaling behaviors dictated by topology. The key findings include universal $E_F^{-1}$ scaling at zero temperature and a logarithmically corrected scaling at finite temperatures. This suggests a robust, all-electric mechanism for sensing weak electric fields.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on condensed matter topology, the concept of field-induced topological transitions and universal scaling laws are highly relevant in understanding quantum transport phenomena that can be modeled using effective Hamiltonians common in AMO physics (e.g., superconducting qubits or cavity QED systems exhibiting topological phases).

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the unconventional scaling laws governing the Electric Hall Effect (EHE) in 2D magnetic Weyl Semimetals (WSMs). The goal is to understand how topology dictates this response, especially under weak electric fields.

Main result. The EHE exhibits universal scaling at zero temperature ($\chi_{xy} \propto E_F^{-1}$) and an unconventional logarithmically corrected scaling at finite temperature ($\sigma_{xy} \propto E_z \ln(1/|E_z|)$).

Method. Theoretical derivation using the Kubo formula, Berry curvature calculations, and detailed scaling analysis involving momentum-to-parameter space mappings.

Model / system. The system is modeled as 2D magnetic Weyl semimetals hosting doubly degenerate nodal points. The physics is captured by a two-band Hamiltonian subjected to an out-of-plane electric field ($E_z$) which acts as a mass term, inducing topological phase transitions.

Key observables. Electric Hall conductivity ($\sigma_{xy}$) and the intrinsic EHE coefficient ($\chi_{xy}$), both dependent on $E_F$ and $E_z$.

Important parameters / regimes. Fermi energy ($E_F$), out-of-plane electric field strength ($E_z$), and the global topological charge ($C$) of the Weyl points.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on a two-band model approximation near the nodal points, and specific mathematical mappings between momentum space and parameter space are utilized for integration.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the $E_z$-driven topological phase transition (gap closing) and compare numerical/analytical results showing the characteristic logarithmic scaling of $\sigma_{xy}(T)$ versus field strength.

Paper structure. The paper systematically derives the EHE coefficient ($\chi_{xy}$) at zero temperature using Berry curvature methods, then extends this analysis to finite temperatures by incorporating thermal effects into the conductivity calculation, revealing the unconventional scaling laws.

Abstract

Electric Hall Effect (EHE), a unique phenomenon in two-dimensional (2D) magnetic systems, refers to the generation of Hall current by an out-of-plane electric field $\Ez$. Here, we demonstrate that for 2D magnetic Weyl semimetals that host doubly degenerate nodal points, the EHE features multiple unconventional scaling laws. At zero temperature, the EHE exhibits a topological $E_F^{-1}$ Fermi-energy scaling. Remarkably, the prefactor of the scaling is determined by the global topological charge of the point without any dependence on the local parameters of the system, leading to a universal and significant enhancement of Hall response in any species of Weyl points as the Fermi energy approaches the Weyl point. This significant response enables a weak electric field to be directly converted into a measurable Hall signal. Surprisingly, this enhanced Hall response is not diminished by temperature, but evolves into an unconventional logarithmically corrected scaling at finite temperature $σ_{xy}\propto\Ez\ln(1/|\Ez|)$ for weak $\Ez$, still yielding a divergent electric-field susceptibility. Thus, our work not only unveils intriguing scaling laws resulting from the interaction between magnetism and topology, but also suggests a novel scaling-enhanced and temperature-robust mechanism that may enable weak electric-field sensing through a practical and all-electric route.

Summary. This paper models nonlinear waves in a 2D mechanical topological insulator using coupled nonlinear Schrödinger equations. It predicts the existence and properties of various vector edge solitons and domain walls, which are topologically protected. The findings suggest that these systems can be engineered for advanced functionalities like collision-based computing.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The research investigates nonlinear edge waves and domain walls within a two-dimensional mechanical topological insulator (MTI). The goal is to characterize the complex soliton dynamics arising from this system.

Main result. The study predicts the existence of various vector edge solitons, including bright-bright and dark-dark types, which are topologically protected when carrier frequencies lie within a band gap. Model / system. The physical system is a 2D MTI built using pendulums with on-site cubic nonlinearity, connected by linear springs to realize the quantum spin Hall effect. The dynamics are modeled via coupled nonlinear equations of motion that reduce to the CNLS equation for edge modes.

Key observables. Bright-bright (BB) and dark-dark edge solitons, domain walls, energy transfer during soliton collisions, and topological protection confirmed by passing through defects.

Important parameters / regimes. The system depends on parameters defining the cubic nonlinearity ($\sigma$), coupling strengths ($f$), and the quasi-momentum space structure that allows for Equal Group Velocity (EGV) points.

Abstract

We report nonlinear edge waves in a 2D mechanical topological insulator. A bulk lattice consists of pendulums with on-site cubic nonlinearity connected by linear springs realizing quantum spin Hall effect. We show that the nonlinear interaction between two edge modes with equal group velocities (EGV) is described by a 1D two-component coupled nonlinear Schrödinger (CNLS) equation. On the interface separating two bulk lattices with opposite spin Chern numbers, we construct linear springs such that the dispersion relation exhibits EGV points with favorable CNLS coefficients. Thus, we realize nonlinear edge waves propagating along the interface, including bright-bright (BB) edge solitons for focusing CNLS coefficients, and dark-dark edge solitons, edge domain walls, and dark-bright edge solitons for defocusing CNLS coefficients. In terms of the site amplitudes, these solutions resemble bright and dark breathers. These solutions should be topologically protected when both carrier frequencies lie within a band gap, which we explicitly show by passing BB edge solitons through compact defects on the interface. We also show energy transfer in BB edge soliton collisions with potential application to collision-based computing. Generally, vector edge solitons exhibit a large parameter space for soliton collisions, which endows mechanical devices with greater potential for information processing and other functionalities.

Abstract

Abstract

Controlling nanoparticle size during synthesis remains a central challenge in nanoscience, particularly in systems where external magnetic fields are used as continuous control parameters. Existing descriptions of magnetic-field-assisted nucleation are typically material-specific or rely on computationally intensive atomistic methods. Here, we reformulate classical nucleation theory as a geometrically closed thermodynamic framework by introducing a sphere-packing representation of atomic assembly. This construction establishes a direct link between discrete atomic structure and continuum free-energy contributions under applied magnetic fields, yielding a field-driven evolution equation for the critical nucleus size. The resulting theory provides a unified description of nanoparticle nucleation across superparamagnetic, paramagnetic, and diamagnetic systems within a single formalism. It quantitatively reproduces previously unresolved experimental observations for magnetite and nickel nanoparticles, namely the systematic reduction of mean particle size and narrowing of size distributions with increasing magnetic field. In the diamagnetic limit, the framework recovers our earlier analytical susceptibility-based description of silver nanoparticles, in which the field-dependent critical radius is governed by the induced-magnetization contribution to the nucleation free energy. Beyond modeling the reduction of mean particle size with increasing magnetic field, the framework reveals that the narrowing of size distributions emerges naturally from the curvature of the field-modified free-energy landscape. These results establish magnetic-field-assisted nucleation as a geometrically constrained thermodynamic process, providing a computationally efficient route for controlling nanoparticle size across distinct magnetic material classes.

Summary. This theoretical work calculates how electron-electron interactions modify the density of states in highly anisotropic 2D conductors subjected to spin-orbit coupling. The key finding is that SOC dramatically alters the energy dependence of this correlation correction, leading to a sign reversal at critical strengths. This provides a distinct spectroscopic tool for characterizing SOC effects beyond standard Altshuler-Aronov physics.

Main problem. The study investigates Altshuler-Aronov type interaction corrections to the single-particle Density of States (DOS) in a strongly anisotropic 2D conductor.

Main result. The DOS anomaly exhibits distinct dimensional crossovers (2D log dip vs. quasi-1D $\sqrt{\cdot}$ singularity), and SOC can induce a sign reversal of this correction, providing a unique spectroscopic signature.

Method. Calculations are performed in Matsubara space using diagrammatic techniques to compute the exchange contribution in the diffusion channel, incorporating dynamically screened Coulomb interactions.

Important parameters / regimes. Transverse coupling scale ($\varepsilon_c$), SOC strength, disorder scattering rates, and the energy relative to the Fermi level ($|\epsilon - \epsilon_F|$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes a weak-disorder diffusive regime ($k_F l \gg 1$) and utilizes approximations like the Random Phase Approximation (RPA) for screening.

Abstract

We study Altshuler-Aronov-type interaction corrections to the single-particle density of states (DOS) in a strongly anisotropic 2D conductor with an open Fermi surface (FS) and weak disorder, in the presence of coexisting Rashba and Dresselhaus spin-orbit couplings (SOCs) constrained to the longitudinal direction. The low-energy band consists of two warped sheets weakly tunnel-coupled transversely; SOC splits the sheets into helicity branches with a fixed spin axis. Working in a Matsubara space, we compute the exchange contribution in the diffusion channel with dynamically screened Coulomb interaction and an impurity ladder. The resulting DOS anomaly exhibits a dimensional crossover governed by the transverse coupling scale $\varepsilon_c$. Close to the Fermi level ($|\varepsilon-\varepsilon_F|<\varepsilon_c$), the system behaves two-dimensionally, featuring a logarithmic DOS dip whose magnitude is enhanced by intrinsic SOCs. Further from the Fermi level ($|\varepsilon-\varepsilon_F|\!>\!\varepsilon_c$), the system behaves quasi-one-dimensionally, featuring a sharper square-root singularity whose amplitude is remarkably enhanced by the SOCs. Notably, we identify a critical SOC strength at which these spin-orbit effects exactly cancel the electron-correlation correction, perfectly restoring the unperturbed density of states. Furthermore, increasing the SOC beyond this critical point inverts the sign of the anomaly entirely, yielding a positive DOS correction. This sign reversal fundamentally alters the energy dependence, such that at energies beyond $\varepsilon_F + \varepsilon_c$, the positive correction decays to smaller values as energy increases, opposite to the standard negative correction. This contrasting trend provides a distinct spectroscopic signature of SOC-modulated correlation effects.

Main problem. The paper predicts a novel, stable Majorana zero-energy mode characterized by a ring shape (annular) within superconducting vortices in topological insulators.

Method. The analysis involves solving the Bogoliubov-de Gennes (BdG) Hamiltonian for superconducting vortices and performing symmetry analysis on the resulting bound states.

Abstract

When the surface states of a topological insulator becomes superconducting, topological superconductivity can be obtained, and each vortex on the surface can host one single Majorana zero-energy mode which is usually a wave packet decaying exponentially off the vortex core. Here, we predict stable Majorana zero-energy mode whose wave function is ring-shape, dubbed as annular Majorana mode, in the superconducting vortex in topological insulators respecting $3$-fold or $6$-fold rotational symmetry. Such topological insulators are featured with a single nonlinear Dirac cone located at $\barΓ$ or three linear Dirac cones at $\bar{\text{M}}$ in the surface Brillouin zone. The annular Majorana mode originates from the effective chiral $f$-wave superconductivity on the nonlinear Dirac cone in the former case and the interference of the effective chiral $p$-wave superconductivity on the three linear Dirac cones in the latter. In both cases, the annular Majorana mode is stabilized by the rotational symmetry and the winding number $3$ carried by the surface states. Candidate materials supporting the annular Majorana mode are predicted. Our work provides new insights into the topological superconductivity in superconducting topological insulators.

Abstract

We investigate pseudovorticity generation in Gaussian beams undergoing self-focusing under amplitude and phase noise, using the cubic-quintic nonlinear Schrödinger equation. Abstract

We investigate the electronic band topology of recently fabricated pentagonal IV-VI semiconductor nanowires, which contain five radial $\{111\}$ twin planes meeting at the nanowire axis. Tight-binding calculations show that, when the bulk band structure is inverted and the twin planes in the nanowire are cationic, the spectrum contains two spatially separated helical Dirac crossings near $\overlineΓ$: one localized at the core and the other at the outer surface. When the twin planes are anionic, the corresponding spectra remain gapped. The crossings originate from the hybridization of five helical channels associated with the twin-plane edges, whose odd number leaves one Kramers pair near the nanowire axis and the other at the outer boundary. These results establish pentagonal SnTe-class nanowires as an experimentally accessible realization of spatially separated helical channels bound to the axial defect and the outer boundary.

Abstract

Excitons possess internal structure absent from single-particle Bloch particles, allowing their band topology to emerge from the bound-state structure rather than being inherited from their constituents. This raises the question of how the internal structure of a bound state can provide a microscopic origin of exciton topology. Here we show that the real-space embedding of charge-transfer excitons can generate an intrinsic manifold of symmetry-related off-site composite orbitals whose coupling supports topological exciton bands. Lateral electron-hole separation embeds the localized exciton on the bond connecting its constituent sites rather than on either site. We demonstrate this mechanism in a honeycomb lattice, where three bond-centered charge-transfer exciton orbitals form a Kagome lattice. By solving the Bethe-Salpeter equation, we show that this emergent multi-orbital manifold supports a topological exciton flat band upon time-reversal symmetry breaking, even when the electron and hole bands are topologically trivial. The resulting band exhibits nearly uniformly distributed quantum geometry, favorable for interaction-driven bosonic states. Our results establish a general route toward topological bands of localized composite bound states and unconventional strongly correlated bosonic phases.

Abstract
