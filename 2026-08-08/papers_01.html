Abstract

Spin ensembles are considered as potential candidates for quantum memory and quantum enhanced sensing applications. Here, we explore the controlled coupling of multiple superconducting microwave cavities to a spin ensemble, which shows signatures of strong coupling and, due to the multi-mode character, the formation of dark states. Abstract

Quantum metrology harnesses entanglement to improve measurement precision beyond classical limits. While standard protocols rely on two-level qubits to estimate a single parameter, extending them to entangled multi-level qudits enables the optimal simultaneous estimation of multiple parameters within a single probe. However, generating such multi-level entanglement and harnessing it for joint multi-parameter estimation in atomic clocks has remained an outstanding challenge. Here, we experimentally demonstrate genuine qutrit entanglement and joint multi-parameter estimation in an optical clock platform. We observe a joint estimation variance below the ideal individual two-level sensing threshold, and show theoretically that this advantage persists at state-of-the-art atom numbers under circuit-level noise. These results demonstrate the key building blocks towards quantum information science with high-dimensional states encoded in the internal energy levels of neutral atoms.

Abstract

We study dynamical phase transitions in the generalized dissipative Dicke model in an array of trapped Rydberg ions, where their density-density interactions compete with the collective spin-phonon coupling, laser driving and dissipation. This setting offers a versatile approach to study equilibrium as well as non-equilibrium many-body phenomena, as parameters, such as the Ising interaction, laser-ion and spin-phonon coupling can be tuned. Through analyzing the mean-field phase diagram, we find a variety of distinct phases and the emergence of a tricritical point that are sensitively dependent of the interaction between Rydberg ions. We then study the quantum dynamics for a finite system size and characterize parameter dependent dynamics using the spin average, entropy, and Loschmidt echo. Distinctive signatures of the dynamical phases, such as slow relaxation and metastability, arise near the phase transition. This analysis predicts rich quantum dynamics of the finite system that link to the non-equilibrium mean-field phases. Our study widens the exploration of collective and non-equilibrium phases in Dicke models, and reveals that the Rydberg ion interaction drastically affects the phase diagram and dynamics.

Summary. This paper develops a theory detailing how local measurements perturb the critical behavior of gapless quantum matter like Tomonaga-Luttinger liquids. Using replica calculations, the authors show that this measurement-induced randomness causes correlation functions to decay with specific power laws featuring logarithmic corrections. This establishes a new regime of criticality beyond standard theoretical models.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the non-trivial interplay between quantum measurements and fundamental critical phenomena, providing a quantitative framework for how measurement backaction modifies universal properties in 1D systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The theory investigates how local measurements perturb and alter the universal long-range correlations of gapless quantum matter, specifically Tomonaga-Luttinger liquids.

Main result. Average correlators decay with universal power laws accompanied by logarithmic corrections when measurements are relevant, a signature distinct from both forced measurements and conventional critical scaling.

Method. The authors employ replica instanton calculations to handle the intrinsic randomness of measurement records, complementing this with matrix-product-state calculations for verification.

Model / system. The primary model is the Tomonaga-Luttinger liquid (TLL), a 1D quantum critical state described by a continuum field theory derived from lattice Hamiltonians. The analysis focuses on weakly monitoring these systems.

Key observables. Connected density and phase fluctuation correlators ($\langle\hat{n}(x)\hat{n}(0) angle$, $\langle e^{i[ heta(x)- heta(0)]} angle$), and the full multifractal spectrum of correlation moments.

Important parameters / regimes. The Luttinger parameter ($K$) dictates the algebraic decay, while the measurement strength ($\gamma$) controls the perturbation regime; the focus is on $K < 1/2$ where perturbations are relevant.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the low-energy physics can be described by 1+1d Conformal Field Theories (CFTs) and utilizes the replica trick to average over measurement outcomes.

Figures summary. Figures compare theoretical predictions for density and phase correlators under weak measurements against those from forced measurements, illustrating the unique decay behavior induced by randomness.

Paper structure. The paper develops a general theory of measurement-altered criticality, applying it first to TLLs using replica methods to calculate Born-averaged observables. It contrasts these results with standard critical scaling and provides numerical support via MPS calculations.

Abstract

Local measurements can alter long-range correlations in gapless quantum matter. We propose a theory of weakly-monitored Tomonaga-Luttinger liquids, a broad class of quantum critical states in one dimension. In order to address the intrinsic randomness of the measurement record, we develop a replica instanton calculation to study Born-averaged observables. We find that when measurements are relevant, average correlators of density and phase fluctuations decay at long distances as universal power laws with logarithmic corrections, a feature we argue is peculiar to measurement-induced randomness. We characterize the full multifractal spectrum of moments of correlations functions, revealing broad, strongly non-gaussian fluctuations across the ensemble of post-measurement states. We support these analytic results with matrix-product-state calculations, and provide a general picture of measurement-altered criticality for ground states described by 1+1d conformal field theories. Our results establish that physical measurements alter critical quantum states in a manner that lies beyond both forced measurements and conventional critical scaling.

Summary. This paper theoretically analyzes how interference patterns in the readout statistics of a meter used in a weak measurement can reveal information about the system's underlying quantum properties. By relating interference terms in the post-selection probability to fluctuations in the meter reading, the authors demonstrate that these statistical details allow for the objective determination of weak values. This provides a rigorous framework for interpreting measurements where standard assumptions about classical statistics break down.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a deep theoretical understanding of measurement contextuality and weak measurements, which is highly relevant for quantum optics and open quantum systems where continuous monitoring or weak coupling is central to the physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how quantum interference effects, specifically those arising from meter readout statistics, can reveal fundamental details about weak measurements.

Main result. It establishes a universal relationship between quantum interference in the post-selection probability and the interference observed in the meter readout statistics. This analysis shows that these interference terms correct for measurement fluctuations, allowing objective values to be determined by weak values.

Method. The authors perform a careful theoretical analysis of the quantum dynamics of an interacting system (the observable) and a continuous variable meter during post-selection. They utilize quasi-probability distributions to track interference effects.

Model / system. The model involves a general weak measurement setup where a system observable $\hat{A}$ interacts unitarily with a meter whose position is continuously measured. The analysis focuses on the resulting joint probability distribution $P(f, x)$ after post-selection ($f$).

Key observables. Quantum interference in the post-selection probability; quantum interference in the meter readout statistics; weak values ($\langle A \rangle_w$); conditional fluctuations (e.g., Ozawa-Hall uncertainty).

Important parameters / regimes. Measurement strength ($s$), which dictates whether the measurement is weak or projective.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that quantum interference effects are measurable in the meter readout statistics and that these statistics provide more information than just conditional averages.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the general framework by analyzing the joint probability of post-selection and meter readout. It then details how interference terms modify both the average value and, crucially, the conditional fluctuations (variance) observed in the meter.

Abstract

It is widely assumed that the quantum fluctuations of the meter readout in a weak measurement make it impossible to identify the contributions originating from the individual values of the physical property observed in the measurement. Here, we show that a careful analysis of the quantum dynamics of the meter system allows us to identify a universal relation between quantum interference in the post-selection probability, and quantum interference in the statistics of the meter readout. The analysis reveals that quantum interference modifies the readout distribution of the meter in two ways, a diffusion term that identifies the appropriate operator ordering in the post-selected variance of the observed system property, and a wavefunction-dependent update of the initial meter statistics based on the effect of back action on the post-selection probability. Our results show that the statistical patterns described by meter interference provide important details about the physics of post-selection in weak measurements, allowing us to exclude the possibility of statistical artefacts in the experimental observation of weak values.

Abstract

Strong light-matter coupling in optical microcavities can transport energy ballistically across an emitter array, but the same coupling buries most of the excitation in a manifold of dark states that grows with system size and traps energy outside the transport channel. We show that the off-diagonal (non-Condon) part of the exciton-phonon coupling opens a one-way escape route from this trap, driving population irreversibly from dark states into the radiative channel. This rate is fixed by a photonic-weight conservation law rather than by dark-bright overlap which evacuates the dark manifold at a rate independent of system size. The mechanism contributes to transport with near-complete efficiency with four signatures being single exponential dark state decay, a size scaling efficiency gap, distinct temperature behavior, and a resonance in the escape rate at vibrational bath modes. Beyond polariton transport, it recasts dark states from a parasitic loss channel into an engineered dissipative resource, with implications for light harvesting and dissipation based quantum control.

Abstract
