Improved regret bounds for structured online learning of quantum states

Authors: Akshay Bansal, Jiahui Liu
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.05740v1

Summary. This paper tackles the challenge of online quantum state tomography by analyzing how structural assumptions on measurements can drastically improve learning guarantees. Using advanced online optimization techniques like OGD, the authors derive regret bounds that depend on intrinsic properties (like rank) rather than the exponential Hilbert space dimension. The results show that under favorable conditions, the learnability of quantum states is significantly enhanced.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the practical limitations of quantum state characterization by providing theoretically rigorous methods to achieve high accuracy with limited measurement data, which is crucial for building scalable quantum devices.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core problem is improving the regret bounds for online quantum state tomography, which is necessary because full state reconstruction is infeasible at scale.

Main result. By exploiting structural assumptions on measurements (like bounded Frobenius norm or low rank), the achievable regret bounds are significantly improved compared to general settings. Furthermore, using squared L2 loss in multi-outcome measurements yields a logarithmic regret bound independent of system size.

Method. The authors employ online learning frameworks, primarily Projected Online Gradient Descent (OGD) and Regularized Follow-the-Leader (RFTL), adapted for the constraints of quantum state space projection.

Model / system. The model involves estimating an unknown quantum state $ ho$ over time $T$ using sequential measurements. The process is framed as an online learning task where the learner minimizes regret against an adversarial measurement sequence.

Key observables. Regret ($R_T$), which measures performance relative to the best fixed quantum state in hindsight, and the predicted measurement outcomes.

Important parameters / regimes. The structural properties of the measurements, such as bounded Frobenius norm ($\|E_t\|_F \le B$), low rank ($ ext{rank}(E_t) \le r$), or $\kappa$-sparsity.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies heavily on assuming specific structural constraints (low rank/sparsity) for the adversarial measurement operators, and assumes convexity and Lipschitz continuity of the loss function.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the general online learning framework using OGD, derives bounds based on structural assumptions (Frobenius norm, rank), and presents a complementary result showing logarithmic regret for multi-outcome measurements under squared L2 loss.

Abstract

Quantum state tomography is fundamental to quantum information processing but becomes infeasible at scale due to the exponential growth of the state space. Shadow tomography alleviates this challenge by focusing on predicting measurement outcomes rather than reconstructing the full state. Its online variant models adaptive and potentially adversarial measurement scenarios, where a learner sequentially predicts outcomes while competing with the best fixed quantum state in hindsight. We show that exploiting additional structure in the measurements leads to significantly stronger regret guarantees. In particular, under the assumption that the adversarial measurements have bounded Frobenius norm, we analyze Projected Online Gradient Descent and derive regret bounds that depend on intrinsic structural properties, such as rank or sparsity, rather than the ambient Hilbert space dimension. As a complementary result, we show that one can achieve logarithmic regret, independent of both the number of qubits and measurement outcomes, for multi-outcome measurements under squared $L_2$ loss. These results demonstrate that incorporating realistic structural assumptions can substantially enhance the learnability of quantum states in online environments.

Nitrogen Vacancy Centers in Hexagonal Diamond Exhibit Long Coherence Times

Summary. The authors theoretically investigate NV centers in hexagonal diamond (lonsdaleite) to find better spin qubit candidates than those in cubic diamond. They found that a specific, symmetry-reduced configuration (AB) significantly enhances the coherence time ($T_2$) by leveraging its unique zero-field splitting properties. This establishes lonsdaleite as a promising material for quantum information science applications.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses hardware platforms for solid-state qubits by predicting enhanced coherence times based on crystal symmetry, which is crucial knowledge for designing robust quantum memory elements.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To investigate Nitrogen Vacancy (NV) centers in hexagonal diamond (lonsdaleite) as potential spin qubits with enhanced coherence times compared to cubic diamond counterparts.

Main result. The AB configuration of the NV center in lonsdaleite exhibits an approximate fourfold enhancement of the Hahn-echo coherence time ($T_2$) at zero magnetic field due to a finite transverse zero-field splitting.

Method. First-principles calculations (DFT) combined with advanced quantum defect theory methods (QDET, gCCE) were used to model electronic structure and spin coherence times.

Model / system. The study models negatively charged NV centers ($ ext{NV}^-$) in two configurations (AA and AB) within the hexagonal diamond polymorph lonsdaleite, comparing them against the established cubic-diamond NV center.

Key observables. Hahn-echo coherence time ($T_2$), Zero-Field Splitting (ZFS) tensor components ($D$ and $E$), Photoluminescence spectra, and Debye-Waller factors.

Important parameters / regimes. Zero magnetic field ($ ext{B}=0$), specific lattice configurations (AA vs. AB), and the magnitude of the enhancement factor (approximate fourfold).

Assumptions / limitations. The study assumes that symmetry-broken centers in lonsdaleite are viable candidates, though calculating intersystem crossing rates for full optical cycle efficiency was beyond scope.

Figures summary. Figures compare crystal structures and defect configurations; one figure shows energy splittings due to symmetry reduction (AB case); another compares PL/Abs spectra for AA and AB configurations.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the electronic structure, coherence properties, and optical signatures of NV centers in lonsdaleite. It contrasts two specific defect symmetries (AA vs. AB) against the cubic standard to pinpoint the optimal configuration for quantum applications.

Abstract

We show that negatively charged nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers in the hexagonal diamond polymorph lonsdaleite offer a route to spin qubits with enhanced coherence relative to their cubic-diamond counterparts. Using first-principles calculations, we examine two distinct defect configurations, AA, with the same symmetry as in cubic diamond and AB, with reduced symmetry. We find that the AB configuration of the NV center exhibits a finite transverse zero-field splitting, giving rise to an approximate fourfold enhancement of the Hahn-echo coherence time $T_2$ at zero magnetic field. The AA configuration, by contrast, closely reproduces the electronic structure and coherence properties of the cubic NV center. We further characterize the many-body electronic structure, vertical excitation energies, and photoluminescence spectra of both configurations, providing spectral fingerprints for their experimental identification. Our results establish symmetry-broken NV centers in lonsdaleite as promising candidates for quantum sensing and information science applications.

Maximally entangled states are not complete for pseudo-telepathy

Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.05378v1

Summary. This paper addresses an open problem in quantum nonlocality concerning whether maximally entangled states are sufficient for all pseudo-telepathic games. The authors construct a specific counterexample game, $G_{4,3,6,6}$, and rigorously prove that this game requires entanglement beyond what is provided by any maximally entangled state. This settles the long-standing question negatively.

Why it may be interesting. This work pushes the boundaries of understanding quantum correlations by providing a concrete counterexample in nonlocal games, which has deep implications for characterizing genuine multipartite entanglement beyond simple state characterizations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The central question is whether maximally entangled quantum states are sufficient (complete) to guarantee a perfect strategy for every bipartite pseudo-telepathic nonlocal game.

Main result. The authors provide a counterexample, $G_{4,3,6,6}$, demonstrating that maximal entanglement is not always sufficient for such games, settling the open question negatively.

Method. The proof involves constructing an inner product game and using advanced mathematical techniques, including the NPA hierarchy relaxation solved via SDP, to show the non-existence of a perfect maximally entangled strategy.

Model / system. The study uses bipartite nonlocal games, specifically a newly defined class called 'inner product games,' involving input/output sets of sizes 4, 3, and 6 respectively. The analysis is based on quantum correlation measures derived from unitary operators.

Key observables. The existence or non-existence of a perfect strategy (probability one) using either an entangled state or a maximally entangled state for the game $G_{4,3,6,6}$.

Important parameters / regimes. Input set sizes (4 and 3), output set size (6), and the specific unitaries defining the inner product games.

Assumptions / limitations. The proof relies on solving a complex SDP derived from assuming the existence of such a state, which is computationally intensive and formalized in Lean.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem context regarding completeness for pseudo-telepathy, constructs the specific counterexample game $G_{4,3,6,6}$ (an inner product game), and then proceeds through mathematical machinery (NPA hierarchy/SDP) to prove that no perfect maximally entangled strategy exists.

Abstract

One of the longstanding open problems in quantum nonlocality is to determine if maximally entangled states are complete for bipartite pseudo-telepathic games: namely, if every nonlocal game which admits a perfect entangled strategy admits such a strategy which uses a maximally entangled state. We exhibit a counterexample to this in the form of a bipartite nonlocal game with input sets of size 4 and 3 and output sets both of size 6. This game is part of a new class of nonlocal games, which we call inner product games, which could be of independent interest.

Summary. This work develops an effective field theory toolbox for understanding dynamically moving quantum Hall interfaces. It rigorously links the interface's geometric motion to its electrical charge transport using a worldsheet formalism derived from Chern-Simons theory. The resulting framework cleanly separates universal topological kinematics from material-dependent physics.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on QH physics, the development of a general worldsheet formalism for constrained boundary dynamics is highly relevant to modeling interfaces in other strongly correlated systems or topological materials where geometry dictates charge transport.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of formulating an effective, geometrically consistent theory for quantum Hall (QH) interfaces where interface motion is coupled to charge dynamics.

Main result. A systematic framework is derived that separates the universal Hall kinematics of the moving interface from its microscopic geometric and neutral energy contributions.

Method. The authors employ a worldsheet description, starting from the two-sided Chern-Simons response and introducing a relative-area construction to derive an equal-time constraint linking charge transport to interface shape.

Model / system. The system models Abelian quantum Hall interfaces separating incompressible phases. The analysis uses concepts from topological field theory (Chern-Simons action) applied to the geometry of the moving boundary.

Key observables. Normal charge current ($\mathbf{J} \cdot \mathbf{n}$), interface velocity ($v_n$), relative magnetic-area density ($a_\sigma$), and geometric energy terms.

Important parameters / regimes. Filling fraction difference ($\Delta u = u_1 - u_2$) and the background magnetic field strength (implied by Chern-Simons coupling).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is restricted to the electromagnetic Chern–Simons response in a flat sample, excluding terms like Wen–Zee or gravitational corrections.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the Frenet frame and level-set description used for analyzing the QH interface geometry.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by establishing the kinematic constraints from the Chern-Simons response, deriving an equal-time constraint via relative area construction, and finally separating the resulting dynamics into universal Hall kinematics and microscopic geometric terms.

Abstract

A freely moving quantum Hall interface is fundamentally different from an ordinary edge fixed by an external confining potential. Since a normal displacement changes the areas occupied by the adjacent incompressible phases, the interface geometry and charge dynamics cannot be treated as independent degrees of freedom. We formulate this problem for interfaces between Abelian quantum Hall phases using a spatially reparametrization-invariant worldsheet description, in which tangential motion is a relabeling of the interface while normal motion is physical. Starting from the two-sided Chern--Simons response, we derive the relation between normal charge transport and interface motion. We then introduce a relative-area construction, defined with respect to a material reference curve, that converts this velocity relation into an equal-time constraint linking the charged boundary sector to the interface shape. Combined with the folded $K$-matrix current algebra, this identifies the universal Hall kinematics of the moving interface while leaving its geometric energy and neutral dynamics dependent on microscopic interface physics. The resulting framework provides a systematic basis for effective theories of dynamical quantum Hall interfaces.

Summary. This theoretical paper develops an exact formalism for analyzing finite-time quantum histories governed by cyclical unitary evolution. It shows that key physical observables like the spectral gap depend only on the global monodromy operator, not the individual steps. Furthermore, it rigorously classifies the symmetries (clock changes) that preserve the underlying information structure of the history.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a highly rigorous mathematical framework for understanding how global constraints (like cyclic boundary conditions or total evolution) dictate local observable properties in quantum systems, which is relevant to open system dynamics and measurement theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper solves the problem of characterizing cyclic quantum histories defined by arbitrary time-dependent unitary steps, removing restrictive assumptions about the order of individual steps.

Main result. It establishes that spectral properties (gap, trace) are determined solely by the monodromy operator's eigenphases, and it rigorously classifies exact symmetries (clock changes) preserving the history code structure.

Method. The analysis employs spectral theory applied to a cyclic history Hamiltonian, relating observables like the spectrum and heat trace to the gauge-invariant properties of the total monodromy operator $M$.

Model / system. The system models quantum evolution over a finite cycle time $L$, governed by a propagation Hamiltonian derived from sequential unitary steps $U_t$ acting on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space.

Key observables. Monodromy operator ($M$), spectral gap ($\Delta(U)$), history code ($ ext{K}_0$), and the predictive quotient of event alphabets.

Important parameters / regimes. Cycle length $L$, monodromy eigenphases $ heta_a$, estimation error bounds ($\epsilon, \delta$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes finite-dimensional unitary steps and focuses on 'sharp' (unitary) clock changes; it distinguishes these from general basis rotations.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the lowest spectral branches for $L=12$, demonstrating how a nonzero monodromy phase shifts the clock momentum and controls the frustration energy relative to zero energy.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by first analyzing the Hamiltonian structure via the monodromy operator, then deriving closed forms for spectral quantities (gap, trace), followed by characterizing operational equivalence classes of clocks, and finally classifying the symmetries that preserve the history code.

Abstract

We solve cyclic finite-dimensional quantum histories for arbitrary time-dependent unitary steps, without assuming that one step has finite order. The propagation Hamiltonian is a unitary connection Laplacian on a cycle; its complete gauge invariant is the monodromy $M=U_{L-1}\cdots U_0$. Its spectrum is $λ_{a,k}=1-\cos((2πk-θ_a)/L)$, where $e^{iθ_a}\in\mathrm{spec}(M)$. Thus the exact history sector is isomorphic to $\mathrm{Fix}(M)$, while frustration, the gap above a nonempty zero-energy sector, the determinant, and the finite-temperature trace are obtained in closed form. Ordinary spectral data recover the multiset of monodromy phase cosines but not phase orientation; low energy certifies proximity to an exact relational history. We then define the predictive quotient of a sharp finite clock relative to an accessible operator system as the unique coarsest event alphabet preserving all conditional statistics on a history sector. A finite-error theorem shows that threshold clustering recovers this quotient when the minimum diamond separation of inequivalent event channels exceeds four times the estimation error, and proves an optimal record-count bound. With full matrix access and homogeneous step $U$, the minimal number of clock events is the projective order of $U$. We distinguish the normalizer of the clock algebra from transformations preserving the coherent history code and classify oriented exact sharp clock changes by $U(r)\times\mathbb{Z}_L$ on a rank-$r$ history sector; without orientation the cyclic factor becomes dihedral. Reversible changes of full-information clock fibers are necessarily unitary, so irreversible coarse-graining is not exact clock covariance. Minimal realizations of a complete history Gram kernel are uniquely unitarily equivalent, with a finite-data Procrustes bound. Independent finite-matrix code verifies the main results.

Summary. This paper establishes a multi-state geometric framework using the cQAC tensor to analyze quantum state manifolds. It derives a general sum rule connecting the second-order DC rectification response of insulators to fundamental quantities like the ground state third cumulant and a complex distortion tensor. This provides a powerful, geometry-based tool for understanding nonlinear transport in interacting many-body systems.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a powerful geometric tool (cQAC tensor) to constrain measurable transport properties (rectification/currents) in complex many-body systems, linking geometry directly to non-equilibrium response.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To develop a theory of multi-state geometry for density matrices and use it to derive general sum rules for second-order rectification response in quantum materials.

Main result. A zero-temperature sum rule is derived relating the frequency-integrated DC rectification response to the ground state third cumulant minus a complex distortion tensor, generalizing known single-particle results to many-body systems.

Method. The authors introduce the cQAC tensor, a quantum generalization of the Amari-Chentsov tensor, and use time-dependent perturbation theory on thermal density matrices to establish geometric connections.

Model / system. The work applies to general many-body systems, extending beyond clean, noninteracting zero-temperature limits. Specific applications include insulators modeled by a generalized Kane-Mele model.

Key observables. Second-order DC rectification response, ground state third cumulant, complex distortion tensor, shift current, and nonlinear Hall currents.

Important parameters / regimes. Zero temperature (T=0) is the primary focus for deriving exact sum rules; low-temperature measurements are discussed in relation to gap corrections.

Assumptions / limitations. The theory aims to generalize beyond assumptions of clean, noninteracting systems, but derivations rely on perturbation theory and specific geometric formalisms like the QAC tensor.

Figures summary. Not specified by notes; however, numerical verification is mentioned using a generalized Kane-Mele model.

Paper structure. The paper develops the multi-state geometry framework via the cQAC tensor, derives the general zero-temperature sum rule for rectification response, specializes this rule to currents like the shift current, and finally discusses the temperature dependence of the result.

Abstract

The geometry of quantum states has emerged as a key ingredient in understanding the linear and nonlinear responses of quantum materials. To date, however, the connection between geometry and nonlinear response is best understood for clean, noninteracting systems at zero temperature. In this work, we develop a theory of multi-state geometry for density matrices and use it to derive sum rules for second-order rectification, making no assumptions about the strength of disorder or interactions. We first show that perturbation theory for thermal density matrices gives rise to two dual information-theoretic connections and an almost complex structure. We introduce a complex, quantum generalization of the Amari-Chentsov tensor of classical information theory, the cQAC tensor, which captures the multi-state geometry of the perturbed density matrix. We derive a zero-temperature sum rule for the frequency-integrated DC rectification response of an insulator as a difference between a ground state third cumulant and the complex distortion tensor, a multi-state geometric quantity built from the cQAC tensor. This generalizes known single-particle sum rules for the shift and nonlinear Hall currents to many-body systems and general perturbations. Specializing to the shift current, we resolve the geometric contribution for multiband insulators into particle-like and hole-like terms. We verify the sum rule numerically in a generalized Kane-Mele model, finding that the geometric contribution can dominate the integrated response. Finally, we show that although the splitting of the sum rule into cumulant and geometric contributions does not survive at nonzero temperature, the measured sum rule for insulators differs from its zero-temperature form by corrections exponentially small in the gap, allowing low-temperature rectification measurements to probe the multi-state geometry of insulators.

Towards fault-tolerance with universal phase-error-transparent gates for high-spin cat codes

Summary. This paper develops the theory required to achieve fault-tolerant quantum computing using high-dimensional nuclear spins encoded in a spin cat code. The core contribution is the construction of universal logical gates that are 'Error-Transparent' (ET) to phase errors, ensuring that operational noise remains correctable by subsequent Quantum Error Correction steps. This provides a concrete path toward scalable quantum computation in solid-state systems.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a highly detailed theoretical roadmap for realizing fault-tolerant quantum computation in solid-state spin qubits, directly addressing the critical issue of noise resilience in physical implementations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is constructing quantum gates for high-dimensional nuclear spin systems that are fault-tolerant against phase errors, which dominate noise in donor-in-silicon architectures.

Main result. The authors construct a universal set of Error-Transparent (ET) logical gates and demonstrate that ET operations are crucial for achieving optimal error correction capacity necessary for full fault-tolerant quantum computation.

Method. The work involves theoretical construction of gate sets, formalizing the Error Transparency criterion ($\left[\hat{\mathcal{E}}_j, \hat{H}(t) ight]|\psi_L angle= 0$), and analyzing the fault tolerance of measurement and error correction gadgets.

Model / system. The system utilizes high-dimensional nuclear spins (e.g., in donor atoms in silicon) encoded into a spin cat code, which provides intrinsic robustness against phase errors. Control is achieved via ESR and NMR/NER driving fields.

Abstract

High-dimensional nuclear spins offer a hardware-efficient route to quantum error correction (QEC), with the spin cat code providing intrinsic robustness against phase errors -- the dominant noise channel in donor-in-silicon architectures. However, realizing the full potential of this encoding requires gate operations that preserve its error-correcting properties. In this work, we construct a universal logical gate set that is error-transparent (ET) to phase errors, and discuss its practical implementations and challenges. The ET gates ensure that phase errors occurring stochastically during gate operations are propagated in a systematically traceable manner and remain correctable in a subsequent QEC step. Among the universal gate set constructed, we identify the logical $X$ gate as the primary challenge and discuss potential realization schemes. In addition, to fully leverage the spin cat code's advantage over an unencoded qubit, multi-tone microwave driving of the logical $CZ$ gate is essential. Our simulations show that ET gates significantly outperform non-ET gates and may be necessary to surpass the break-even point. We further show how logical measurement and recovery can be constructed from ET operations, and explain why state-preparation cannot be made ET. In particular, ET measurement in the computational basis is realizable via spin parity measurement, and that error correction circuits constructed from ET operations achieve optimal error correction capacity. Our work charts a concrete path toward full fault-tolerant quantum computation with high-dimensional nuclear spin systems.

Abstract

We propose and analyze a programmable near-field radiative thermal network that emulates convolutional and recurrent operations through heat exchange. By integrating radiative thermal diodes and transistors composed of phase-change materials such as VO2 and GST, we construct two fundamental architectures: the Thermal Convolutional Neural Network (T-CNN) and the Thermal Recurrent Neural Network (T-RNN). The T-CNN executes spatial pattern recognition via temperature-dependent emissivity modulation, where each gate acts as a tunable radiative filter that amplifies or suppresses localized heat flux. The system is externally programmable via gate temperature distributions in VO2/GST, enabling weighted summation and nonlinear activation; hysteretic transitions provide state dependent responses that can be harnessed for memory. The T-RNN extends this functionality temporally, embedding radiative feedback and hysteretic phase transitions to realize memory and sequential inference. Together, these systems exhibit tri-state logic behavior, radiative gain, and non-volatile thermal storage, key attributes of physical learning. Each logical node operates without charge carriers or electrical circuits, relying solely on near-field photon tunneling between nanoscale surfaces. The resulting framework enables direct implementation of convolution, memory retention, and feedback learning within a contactless, energy-conserving platform. This study establishes the first radiative realization of thermal deep learning, revealing a new computing paradigm where temperature fields function as carriers of intelligence, uniting heat transfer, memory, and adaptive inference in a single non-electronic system.

Abstract

Abstract

Bottom-up coarse-graining expands the length and time scales accessible to molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, but information loss can hinder accurate representation of multistate phenomena in complex biomolecular dynamics. Ultra-Coarse-Graining (UCG) projects discrete "quantum-like" extended degrees of freedom, or "internal states," onto coarse-grained (CG) molecules, extending CG model expressiveness. The rapid-local-equilibrium (RLE) approximation in UCG depends on user-defined collective variables (CVs, e.g., local density) and neglects correlations between internal states within and between CG molecules. We present Self-Consistent UCG (SC-UCG), which uses the underlying UCG interactions directly to assign internal states without designing CVs in the CG ensemble. During simulation, internal state probabilities are determined self-consistently through graph message passing. We enhance the RLE Hamiltonian with the Bethe approximation and AI-based inference to represent explicit correlations between UCG beads. For force-field training, we develop Multilayer Internal State Consistency (MISC), a machine-learning method derived from relative entropy minimization that avoids iterative sampling of intermediate force fields. We apply SC-UCG to a tetramer exhibiting a second-order symmetry-breaking phase transition from a supercritical racemic fluid to subcritical D-rich and L-rich fluids. SC-UCG captures collective switching of internal states in the subcritical region and recapitulates the phase transition across temperatures, despite being trained on a single-temperature dataset.
