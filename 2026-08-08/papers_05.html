A platform for nuclear symmetry-violation searches with laser-coolable molecules carrying spinful nuclei

Authors: Tatsam Garg, Jakob Weiß, Tesse Tiemens, Charly Beulenkamp, Andreas Schindewolf, Tim Langen
Type: both · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.06138v1

Summary. This paper introduces a general, high-precision experimental platform for searching for nuclear symmetry violations using laser-cooled molecules containing spinful nuclei. By leveraging techniques like Zeeman tuning near state crossings, the authors project sensitivities significantly beyond current limits. This work provides both the theoretical framework and the practical roadmap for next-generation searches for physics beyond the Standard Model in molecular systems.

Why it may be interesting. This work represents a sophisticated integration of quantum optics (laser cooling/trapping) with fundamental physics searches. The detailed theoretical modeling of complex molecular Hamiltonians to predict optimal measurement points is highly relevant to advanced AMO research.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is searching for new physics by measuring nuclear P- and CP-violating effects, such as NSD-PV, in molecular systems.

Main result. The authors propose a comprehensive experimental toolbox achieving projected sensitivities two orders of magnitude better than current molecular beam methods, applicable to various spinful molecules.

Method. The method involves using external magnetic fields (Zeeman tuning) to bring opposite-parity molecular states into near-degeneracy, enhancing the measurement sensitivity via an interrogation technique like Stark-interferometry.

Model / system. The system is laser-coolable diatomic molecules carrying spinful nuclei (e.g., ${}^{137} ext{BaF}$). The theoretical framework involves detailed calculations of molecular Hamiltonians incorporating rotational, hyperfine, and Zeeman interactions in Hund's case ($\alphaeta S$).

Key observables. The key observable is the mixing element $ar{W}$ (or matrix element $ ilde{C}$) derived from parity-violating couplings, measured via an asymmetry $A$ proportional to $1/\Delta$.

Important parameters / regimes. Key parameters include the magnetic field strength ($B$), the energy detuning ($\Delta$), and molecular constants like the permanent electric dipole moment (e.g., 3.17 Debye for BaF).

Assumptions / limitations. Major assumptions include neglecting contributions from weak electric fields in certain Hamiltonians and assuming that the dominant uncertainty source is the control over the energy detuning $\Delta$.

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically shows the entire experimental apparatus, from laser ablation to MOT capture. Figure 2 details the state structure of ${}^{137} ext{BaF}$ in a magnetic field, illustrating relevant state crossings.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the general physical platform and required techniques (cooling, trapping). It then applies these methods to benchmark measurements like NSD-PV using specific molecular species (${}^{137} ext{BaF}$), detailing Hamiltonian derivations and identifying precise magnetic field locations for maximal sensitivity.

Abstract

Cold heavy molecules are promising systems for exploring nuclear $\mathcal{P}$- and $\mathcal{CP}$-violating phenomena in search of new physics beyond the Standard Model. However, most proposed experimental strategies and their early realizations to date have been limited to proof-of-principle molecular species with effectively spin-zero nuclei that are not sensitive to nuclear symmetry-violating phenomena. Here, we introduce a comprehensive experimental toolbox that integrates cooling, trapping, coherent state manipulation, and a complete precision-measurement protocol that is applicable to molecules carrying relevant nuclear spins. Using ${}^{137}$Ba${}^{19}$F and nuclear-spin-dependent parity violation (NSD-PV) as representative species and benchmark application, respectively, our approach achieves a projected statistical sensitivity roughly two orders of magnitude beyond comparable molecular beams by combining techniques already demonstrated individually in current experiments. This level of precision could provide realistic experimental access not only to the enhanced NSD-PV signals arising from the heavy ${}^{137}$Ba nucleus within this molecule but also to the contributions from the lighter ${}^{19}$F nucleus, bringing direct benchmarks of nuclear \textit{ab initio} theory within reach. We further identify a candidate magic wavelength as a route to second-scale rotational coherence in future experiments. The techniques developed here can be transferred to measurements of nuclear Schiff and magnetic quadrupole moments in molecules containing deformed nuclei, establishing a general platform for laboratory searches for nuclear symmetry violations.

Summary. The authors present a compact Dual-Faraday-laser-pumped (DFP) Cesium beam clock designed to achieve superior frequency stability for portable timing systems. By integrating advanced laser stabilization techniques like Modulation Transfer Spectroscopy with Faraday filtering, they successfully suppressed noise sources inherent in previous designs. This results in record short-term stability of $7.7 imes 10^{-13}/\sqrt{ au}$, advancing the state-of-the-art for field-deployable atomic clocks.

Why it may be interesting. This work represents a significant engineering achievement in precision measurement using atomic physics principles. The successful suppression of laser noise to reach $10^{-13}/\sqrt{ au}$ stability is highly relevant for quantum sensing and metrology applications that require robust field operation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is improving the short-term frequency stability of compact Cesium (Cs) beam clocks for use in field-deployable timing systems, overcoming limitations imposed by laser noise.

Main result. The developed Dual-Faraday-laser-pumped (DFP) Cs clock achieved a fractional Allan deviation of $7.7 imes 10^{-13}/\sqrt{ au}$, pushing stability into the highly desirable $10^{-13}/\sqrt{ au}$ regime.

Method. The technique involves using two lasers stabilized via Modulation Transfer Spectroscopy (MTS) and employing an intracavity Faraday anomalous dispersion optical filter (FADOF) for inherent resonance alignment.

Model / system. The system is a compact Cs beam clock utilizing two distinct laser pumping transitions ($ ext{F}=4 o ext{F}'=5$ and $ ext{F}=3 o ext{F}'=2$). It relies on low-frequency-noise, atom-referenced lasers stabilized through complex optical feedback loops.

Key observables. Clock SNR (46,365 in 1 Hz), fractional Allan deviation ($7.7 imes 10^{-13}/\sqrt{ au}$), and Hadamard deviation ($7.7 imes 10^{-15}$ at 10,000 s).

Important parameters / regimes. Lorentzian linewidth of 2.12 kHz; stability improvement from $1.1 imes 10^{-12}/\sqrt{ au}$ (SFP) to $7.7 imes 10^{-13}/\sqrt{ au}$ (DFP).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary limitation addressed is the conversion of laser frequency noise into fluorescence noise in standard two-laser pumping schemes.

Abstract

Compact cesium beam clocks are major frequency references for deployable timing systems. However, further improvement of their short-term frequency stability is limited by the clock signal-to-noise ratio (SNR). Although two-laser optical pumping can increase the effective atomic utilization, the achievable clock SNR has long been limited by laser-induced frequency-to-amplitude noise conversion. Here, we demonstrate a compact dual-Faraday-laser-pumped (DFP) Cs beam clock enabled by a low-frequency-noise atom-referenced laser architecture. The intracavity Faraday anomalous dispersion optical filter provides inherent alignment to the Cs D$_2$ resonances, while modulation transfer spectroscopy offers suppressed frequency noise and drift. The resulting laser system supports robust turnkey operation with a Lorentzian linewidth of 2.12 kHz. The DFP Cs clock achieves a clock SNR of 46,365 in a 1-Hz bandwidth and a fractional Allan deviation of $7.7\times 10^{-13}/\sqrtτ$ , with Hadamard deviation reaching $7.7\times 10^{-15}$ at 10,000 s. This work pushes the fractional frequency stability of a compact Cs beam clock into the $10^{-13}/\sqrtτ$ regime, providing a pathway toward high-performance Cs frequency references for field-deployable precision timing, navigation, and synchronization.

Summary. This theoretical paper analyzes the fundamental limits of phase estimation using heralded non-Gaussian states generated from Gaussian resources in optical systems. By defining an Effective Quantum Fisher Information (EQFI) that weights by success probability, the authors prove that this resource cannot surpass the QFI limit set by the original deterministic Gaussian input state. This highlights the critical importance of accounting for generation efficiency in quantum metrology.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous resource-counting benchmark for quantum sensing, showing that mere non-Gaussianity is insufficient for advantage if generation probabilities are ignored in linear optics settings.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates whether using heralded, probabilistic non-Gaussian quantum states offers a genuine metrological advantage over deterministic Gaussian states for single-parameter phase estimation.

Main result. It is proven that the Effective Quantum Fisher Information (EQFI) of the heralded non-Gaussian states cannot exceed the Quantum Fisher Information (QFI) achievable with the original Gaussian input state, when resource cost (success probability) is accounted for.

Method. The authors define and calculate the EQFI as a success-probability-weighted average of the conditional QFI over all possible detection outcomes from photon number resolving detectors.

Model / system. The analysis uses passive linear optical circuits acting on Gaussian input states (like squeezed vacuum) to generate heralded non-Gaussian states, with phase estimation performed by applying a global phase rotation operator.

Key observables. Quantum Fisher Information (QFI), Effective Quantum Fisher Information (EQFI), Success Probability ($P(n_B)$).

Important parameters / regimes. The comparison is between $ ext{EQFI}$ and the input $ ext{QFI}$, particularly in regimes involving photon number conservation.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes linear phase estimation using passive linear optics, and crucially incorporates the success probability of the heralding process into the performance metric.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate quantum sensing schemes comparing non-Gaussian vs. Gaussian states, showing how the probabilistic nature (heralding) is mapped to an effective measurement strategy.

Paper structure. The paper establishes a formal mapping between heralded state preparation and postselection after parameter encoding. It then derives the EQFI metric and proves the upper bound ($ ext{EQFI} \le ext{QFI}_{ ext{input}}$), using commutation relations to justify this result.

Abstract

Non-Gaussian states can exhibit large quantum Fisher information (QFI) in quantum sensing. In optical systems, however, its generation is often probabilistic via the boson-sampling type conditional operation and thus its generation rate is limited. This probabilistic generation of non-Gaussian resource should be taken into account for evaluation of the sensing performance. Then a natural question arising is whether the use of heralded probabilistic non-Gaussian states is better than that of the original deterministic Gaussian states for quantum sensing. In this paper, we answer to this question for single-parameter phase-estimation. By using photon-number conservation in passive linear optical systems, we show that heralded state preparation before parameter encoding can be mapped to a postselection problem after parameter encoding for phase estimation. This mapping allows the success probability of heralding to be included naturally in the metrological performance. We introduce an effective quantum Fisher information (EQFI), defined as the success-probability-weighted QFI of the heralded outputs, and prove that it cannot exceed the QFI of the original Gaussian inputs. The result highlights the importance of resource counting in quantum sensing toward better understanding of the resource efficient advantage of optical quantum sensing.

Phonon spectral functions of low-density polaron metals

Summary. This work calculates the phonon spectral function for low-density polaron metals using DMRG. The results demonstrate that the spectral weight shifts significantly across energy scales, contradicting predictions based on the Migdal limit's $2k_F$ kink. The study proposes a 'dressed RPA' as an effective approximation while cautioning about the pitfalls of inconsistent diagrammatic expansions.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on lattice models, the study of spectral weight renormalization due to coupling (electron-phonon interaction) is a core concept in understanding emergent collective excitations relevant to strongly correlated systems and superconductivity.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to compute the phonon spectral function of low-density polaron metals, contrasting these results with predictions from established theoretical limits like the Migdal limit.

Main result. The calculated spectra show significant phonon weight transfer both below and above the bare phonon energy, crucially lacking the $2k_F$ kink signature expected in standard Kohn anomaly phenomenology.

Method. The primary calculation uses Density Matrix Renormalization Group (DMRG), supplemented by theoretical comparisons using the Random Phase Approximation (RPA) and a 'dressed RPA' scheme.

Model / system. The system modeled is a one-dimensional spinless Holstein model doped with a low but finite carrier concentration ($x \le 0.15$), describing polaron metals interacting via electron-phonon coupling ($\lambda$).

Key observables. Phonon spectral function, $B(q, \omega) = - rac{1}{\pi} ext{Im} D_R(q, \omega)$.

Important parameters / regimes. Low carrier concentration ($x \le 0.15$), electron-phonon coupling ($\lambda$), and the bare phonon energy ($\Omega$).

Assumptions / limitations. The 'dressed RPA' is proposed as an efficient approximation, but including lowest-order vertex corrections leads to unphysical negative spectral weight, signaling necessary consistent non-perturbative treatment of dressings.

Figures summary. Figures compare DMRG results (for $q<0$) against polarization calculations using RPA and dressed RPA variants (for $q>0$), illustrating the transfer of phonon weight across different energy regimes.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the physical problem by comparing low-density polaron behavior to known limits. It presents DMRG results, contrasts them with lowest-order RPA predictions, develops an improved 'dressed RPA' scheme, and concludes by discussing the limitations imposed by vertex corrections.

Abstract

We use the density matrix renormalization group (DMRG) to compute the phonon spectral function of a one-dimensional spinless Holstein model doped with a low but finite carrier concentration, $x \leq 0.15$, as a function of the electron-phonon coupling $λ$. To the best of our knowledge, these are the first such results in this regime, complementing extensive prior work at the single-polaron level ($x\to 0$). We find that significant phonon spectral weight is transferred both below, all the way down to $ω=0$, and above the bare phonon energy $Ω$, in stark contrast with the Kohn-anomaly phenomenology expected in the Migdal limit, where weight remains centered near $Ω$ with a kink at $q=2k_F$. No signature of this $2k_F$ kink appears in our results. This behavior is captured qualitatively by the Random Phase Approximation (RPA), and semi-quantitatively, at negligible extra computational cost, by a ``dressed RPA'' scheme in which the electron addition propagator is renormalized using the Momentum Average (MA) approximation for the low-density electron-polaron. By contrast, adding the lowest-order vertex correction to this dressed scheme produces unphysical negative spectral weight, signaling that vertex and propagator dressings must be treated consistently once the propagators are dressed nonperturbatively. Our results provide an efficient approximation for the phonon spectral functions of low-density polaron metals, a regime relevant to weakly doped insulators.

Abstract

Sample-based quantum diagonalization (SQD), equivalently quantum-selected configuration interaction (QSCI), has in two years become a pragmatic centre of gravity of pre-fault-tolerant quantum chemistry: a quantum processor samples electronic configurations, and the many-electron Hamiltonian is diagonalized classically in the resulting determinant subspace. Its accuracy is set entirely by which configurations enter that subspace, a selection problem for machine learning made acute by a coupon-collector bottleneck. We critically review the ecosystem of generative and learned selectors, organizing it by the object each method generates and the importance signal it exploits, and expose one conspicuous gap: a reward-proportional generative-flow-network proposer built for tail discovery. We then confront the field's central question -- whether the quantum sampler beats classical selected configuration interaction -- and report a carefully scoped negative: across published same-active-space comparisons, strong classical selected CI matches or beats the quantum-sampled subspace, and the flagship single-layer circuits now admit polynomial-time classical energy estimation. We distil a benchmarking standard and turn the negative into a regime map, then test it with FCI-exact experiments that confirm one prediction and refute another: the cheap prior's rank correlation with the exact weights declines with multireference character (a usable coordinate), but a controlled single-molecule noise sweep shows the one generative advantage we find, robustness to valid-shot starvation, to be generic rather than the multireference-specific effect a confounded contrast first suggested. Finally, we flag learning from quantum experiments, whose classical sample-complexity lower bound is an unconditional theorem, as the one adjacent frontier where a quantum advantage is provable but not yet bridged to chemistry.

Abstract

Computing Shear Viscosities from Molecular Dynamics Simulation: Comparing the OrthoBoXY Approach with the Green-Kubo Method

Authors: Marcel Brandt, Ralf Ludwig, Dietmar Paschek

Summary. This work compares two methods for calculating liquid shear viscosity from MD simulations, confirming strong agreement between them. The authors demonstrate that the results are robust against finite size effects and propose practical guidelines for scaling simulation parameters ($\tau_{\text{block}}$) to achieve massive computational savings without losing numerical accuracy.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical liquids, the rigorous statistical analysis of error propagation and the development of scaling rules (like adjusting $\tau_{\text{block}}$ by $1/8$ or $1/4$) are highly relevant to optimizing resource-intensive simulations in quantum many-body systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the accurate computation of shear viscosities ($\eta$) for molecular liquids using Molecular Dynamics (MD) simulations, comparing two established theoretical approaches.

Main result. Both the OrthoBoXY and Green-Kubo methods yield highly consistent results across a wide range of liquid viscosities. Furthermore, the study provides significant computational recommendations to reduce simulation time without sacrificing accuracy.

Method. The viscosity is calculated using MD simulations in the NVT ensemble, comparing the stress tensor autocorrelation function (Green-Kubo) against an approach derived from self-diffusion coefficients (OrthoBoXY).

Model / system. The study investigates 15 neat molecular liquids modeled using the OPLS force field. The analysis covers systems ranging across three orders of magnitude in viscosity.

Key observables. Shear viscosity ($\eta$), self-diffusion coefficients ($D_0$, $D_{PBC,ii}$), and their associated standard errors.

Important parameters / regimes. System size (down to 250 molecules), simulation block length ($ au_{ ext{block}}$), and temperature/density conditions for the liquids.

Assumptions / limitations. The OrthoBoXY approach relies on a specific geometric relationship involving box lengths, and statistical robustness is achieved by using Variant C averaging method.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the agreement between different viscosity calculation methods (OrthoBoXY vs. Green-Kubo) and show how error estimates are independent of system size due to compensation effects.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the problem and compares the two main computational methods. It then analyzes the dependence on system size, confirming robustness down to small systems. Finally, it proposes detailed, quantitative refinements for setting the simulation block length ($ au_{ ext{block}}$) based on liquid viscosity.

Abstract

We calculated shear viscosities of 15 neat molecular liquids from equilibrium molecular dynamics (MD) simulations using the OrthoBoXY approach and compare them to viscosities calculated via the Green-Kubo method. Data from both methods agree very well. Here, we show how to avoid pitfalls while computing the OrthoBoXY-data to obtain optimal results. From simulations of multiple system sizes, we could verify that the viscosity of molecular liquids is not influenced by finite size effects down to systems as small as 250 molecules. Moreover, we demonstrate that also the standard error of the viscosity is nearly independent of the system size. Abstract

Reaching fault tolerance means scaling qubit counts by orders of magnitude, a jump that conventional superconducting architectures cannot sustain without solving the so-called `wiring problem'. Global control sidesteps this bottleneck, but implementing quantum error correction (QEC) on previously proposed global architectures incurs extremely steep overhead costs, due to the need for separate correction procedures for the computational and auxiliary qubits that comprise the global device. We resolve this by introducing the first globally-controlled architecture with zero qubit overhead. Every physical qubit is a computational qubit, and thus, every qubit is protected under a single error correcting scheme. We identify a class of cyclic stabilizer codes realizable through global iSWAP and single-qubit gates, yielding QEC thresholds nearly seven orders of magnitude larger than previous estimates for globally-controlled arrays. We further show these thresholds improve systematically as the global architecture is augmented with a limited amount of local measurement sites, demonstrating a trade-off between wiring simplicity and fault-tolerant performance.

Abstract

Quantum circuit cutting enables the execution of large circuits on devices with a limited number of qubits by partitioning circuits into independent subcircuits. However, this introduces a sampling overhead, which grows exponentially with the number of cuts, rendering the choice of cut placements critical for practical circuit cutting. Determining optimal cut placements remains computationally challenging, particularly as circuits grow in size. Additionally, existing circuit cutting approaches typically treat gate and wire cuts independently. Those combining both cutting approaches, however, do not take advantage of joint cutting, i.e., identifying common gate groups and cutting them jointly for a reduced overhead. This work presents a unified framework that combines gate and wire cutting within a single partitioning strategy, enabling more efficient circuit decompositions. Moreover, our approach incorporates joint cutting via a novel gate grouping technique, further reducing sampling overhead. By formulating the cut placement problem as a scalable graph partitioning task, our method efficiently identifies near-optimal cut placements for large circuits, also providing diagnostic feedback on whether circuits are suitable for cutting.

Abstract

School network reorganization is a strategic planning problem that requires balancing demographic trends, territorial accessibility, educational requirements, and institutional constraints while ensuring an efficient allocation of public resources. This paper proposes an optimization framework for school dimensioning decisions based on a novel Integer Linear Programming formulation integrating geographical, administrative, and educational criteria. The proposed approach effectively identifies optimal aggregation plans under different policy scenarios while preserving the structural characteristics of the educational system. Furthermore, the model is reformulated as a constrained quadratic model and implemented within a hybrid quantum optimization environment, demonstrating its compatibility with emerging quantum technologies. The results highlight the robustness of the proposed methodology and its potential as a decision-support tool for sustainable and equitable school network planning.
