Geometric decomposition of the $d$-dimensional hard-sphere partition function

Authors: Luke K. Davis
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.16483v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work introduces a novel geometric decomposition for calculating the hard-sphere partition function in $d$ dimensions. It factors the total partition function into an upper bound derived from close-packing geometry and an exponential penalty term related to void space fragmentation. This provides a powerful, geometrically controlled framework for understanding fluid thermodynamics across all densities.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical statistical mechanics, the rigorous geometric bounding techniques used to control fluctuations in the void space are highly relevant for developing controlled approximations in quantum many-body systems where local correlations dominate.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To derive a rigorous geometric decomposition for the $d$-dimensional hard-sphere partition function, $Q(N, V, T)$.

Main result. The partition function is shown to factorize into a reference upper bound ($Q^*$) and an exponential penalty term: $Q \propto Q^* \exp(-NJ)$, where $J$ depends on the boundary-to-volume ratio of voids.

Method. The authors use geometric bounds, inspired by close-packing arguments and formalized via local density certificates (Blichfeldt-type), to establish an upper bound on the configurational integral.

Model / system. The system is a classical hard-sphere fluid in $d$ dimensions. The analysis focuses on calculating the partition function based on the available insertion volume ($V_I$) for adding particles without overlap.

Key observables. Hard-sphere partition function ($Q$), Insertion Probability ($\Pi_I$), Packing Fraction ($\phi$), and Compressibility Factor ($Z$).

Important parameters / regimes. Dimensionality ($d$), Close-packing density ($\phi_{cp}$), and the ratio of surface area to volume of excluded regions.

Assumptions / limitations. The rigorous derivation relies on the existence of a local density certificate that allows bounding the exclusion set volume, particularly at high densities.

Figures summary. Figures compare various bounds on the insertion probability ($\Pi_I$) against known exact solutions for low dimensions ($d=1$), and illustrate the decomposition of $Q$ into a reference bound ($Q^*$) and a geometric penalty factor.

Paper structure. The paper systematically develops the factorization, first establishing upper bounds using geometry (Chunk 1), then deriving the finite-$N$ exact relationship involving insertion volume ratios (Chunk 2), and finally confirming thermodynamic consistency across low-density (van der Waals) and high-density (close-packed limit) regimes (Chunks 3 & 4).

Abstract

We introduce a geometric decomposition of the hard-sphere partition function. Using a close-packing-inspired geometric bound on the available insertion volume, made rigorous when a corresponding local density certificate is available, we establish a reference upper bound $Q^\ast$ on the configurational integral. Factoring this upper bound out of the statistical geometric partition function of Speedy yields a new form for the $d$-dimensional partition function, $Q(N,V,T)=Q^\ast \exp(-N \mathcal{J})$, where $\mathcal{J}$ depends strictly on the boundary-to-volume ratio of the voids and the close-packing density. Overall, this work deepens our statistical geometric understanding of the hard-sphere system.

Summary. This paper investigates the behavior of electron energy levels in super-heavy atoms ($Z>100$) where standard quantum electrodynamics predicts unphysical singularities. By incorporating the electron's Anomalous Magnetic Dipole Moment (AMDM) into the theoretical framework, the authors demonstrate that this contribution regularizes the wave functions and yields physically acceptable results for high Z numbers.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly tackles fundamental theoretical limitations in QED applied to extreme matter (super-heavy elements), providing an alternative regularization mechanism that has implications for understanding vacuum stability and atomic structure beyond current standard models.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study addresses the singularity issue encountered in standard Quantum Electrodynamics (QED) point-nucleus models when calculating electron energy levels for super-heavy atoms ($Z > 137$).

Main result. Incorporating the electron's Anomalous Magnetic Dipole Moment (AMDM) provides a sufficient condition to obtain regular wave functions and physically acceptable energy values even in the idealized point-nucleus limit for very high atomic numbers.

Method. The analysis involves modifying Dirac's equation with an effective Pauli Hamiltonian term representing the AMDM, leading to coupled differential equations solved using techniques like Whittaker functions and perturbation theory.

Model / system. The system is a single electron orbiting a super-heavy nucleus ($Z > 100$). The model uses a point-nucleus approximation but incorporates the AMDM contribution via an effective Hamiltonian modification to regularize the wave function at the origin.

Key observables. Energy eigenvalues $E(Z)$, critical atomic number $Z_{ ext{crit}}$ where $E=-m$, and the behavior of radial wave functions near the nucleus.

Important parameters / regimes. Fine structure constant ($\alpha$), AMDM parameter ($a = (g-2)/2$), and the atomic number $Z$.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that including the AMDM contribution via an effective theory is sufficient to regularize the wave functions, even though finite nuclear size effects are usually required for full accuracy.

Figures summary. Figure 1 compares numerically obtained eigenvalues from the AMDM model against results from finite-nucleus models. Figure 2 illustrates the behavior of a specific function related to the wave function in the point nucleus + AMDM model.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the problem of singularities in QED for super-heavy atoms. It then modifies the Hamiltonian using the AMDM, derives coupled differential equations, and solves them by analyzing near-origin behavior and transforming the problem into a Schrödinger-type equation to determine energy dependence on Z.

Abstract

Super-heavy atoms ($Z > 100$) are usually studied in the context of the so-called ``Quantum Electrodynamics of Strong Fields''. In this theory the problem of the singularity in the electron energy whenever $Z > 137$ is overcome. This is done by considering the finite size of the nucleus and leads to interesting phenomena, such as the spontaneous production of positrons. Here, we show that taking into account the contribution from the Anomalous Magnetic Dipole Moment of the electron (by means of an effective theory), within a point-nucleus model, is a sufficient condition to obtain regular wave functions and physically acceptable energy values for $Z > 137$.

QALM: Escaping Local Minima via Interleaved Exploration and Exploitation in Quantum Circuit Optimization

Authors: Aidan Wagner, Mingkuan Xu, Pengyu Liu, Zhihao Jia, Umut A. Acar
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.16221v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. The paper introduces QALM, a novel hybrid optimizer designed to improve quantum circuit synthesis. It combines the speed of rule-based optimization with the thoroughness of search algorithms by interleaving these two approaches. This allows QALM to achieve superior gate reduction rates compared to existing methods without incurring prohibitive computational overhead.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses algorithmic bottlenecks in quantum computation resource estimation, which is crucial for designing efficient circuits for near-term hardware devices.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Quantum circuit optimization aims to find an equivalent quantum circuit with minimum cost (e.g., gate count) by overcoming the limitations of local minima encountered by existing optimizers.

Main result. The proposed QALM optimizer outperforms existing rule-based and search-based methods in circuit reduction rates while maintaining high computational efficiency across a comprehensive benchmark suite.

Method. QALM employs a hybrid framework that interleaves exhaustive, targeted search capabilities with efficient, greedy rule-based optimization passes.

Model / system. The system modeled is quantum circuits represented as Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs), where qubits are wires and gates are nodes. Optimization requires maintaining circuit equivalence while minimizing cost metrics like gate count or depth.

Key observables. Circuit fidelity ($F$), total gate count reduction, two-qubit gate ($ ext{CX}$) reduction percentage, and optimization runtime.

Important parameters / regimes. The exploration depth parameter $k$ controls the balance between search (exploration) and rule application (exploitation).

Assumptions / limitations. It is assumed that interleaving search and rules provides a superior trade-off compared to using either method in isolation, despite the problem's inherent computational hardness.

Figures summary. Figures compare optimization trajectories showing QALM's ability to escape local minima; other figures illustrate performance comparisons across various benchmarks (e.g., fidelity vs. gate reduction).

Paper structure. The paper introduces the fundamental limitations of greedy and pure search optimizers, proposes the interleaved QALM framework parameterized by an exploration depth $k$, details its iterative algorithm, and validates it against multiple state-of-the-art benchmarks.

Abstract

Quantum circuit optimizers face a fundamental limitation in how they tolerate temporary cost increases. At one extreme, greedy rule-based optimizers immediately apply any cost-reducing transformation, achieving high efficiency but quickly becoming trapped in local minima. At the other extreme, search-based optimizers accept cost-increasing moves to explore the circuit space and escape such minima. However, because search-based optimizers cannot determine within a reasonable time budget whether a given point is promising, that is, whether its neighborhood contains a deeper local minimum, they must blindly explore higher-cost regions. As a result, escaping the current basin to reach a promising point takes exponentially many steps. In this work, we show that this limitation can be overcome with a hybrid framework that interleaves the exhaustive exploration capabilities of search algorithms with the efficiency of rule-based optimization. We implement this framework as QALM, a novel optimizer designed to escape local minima without incurring the runtime penalties of pure search. Crucially, our results demonstrate that QALM does not merely strike a balance; it outperforms existing rule-based and search-based optimizers in circuit reduction rates while operating with the computational efficiency of rule-based systems. In a comprehensive evaluation across 248 circuits, QALM matches or exceeds the fidelity of the strongest baseline on 83.9% of these circuits, given the same time budget.

Abstract

Quantum computing has rapidly emerged as a powerful paradigm for tackling computationally demanding problems. In particular, quantum optimization shows strong promise for hard combinatorial problems in power systems, where increasing distributed energy penetration heightens the need for intentional islanding to maintain grid reliability and resilience. This study develops a resource-efficient hybrid QAOA islanding framework that brings physics-constrained power-system partitioning into the quantum optimization workflow. This study establishes QAOA-based islanding as a viable quantum approach for critical infrastructure, with structured post-processing as the key enabler of quantum resource efficiency.

The limits of interpretability in multiple linear regression

Summary. The paper rigorously analyzes why interpreting weights in linear regression fails when input features are highly correlated, even if predictions are accurate. Using eigenmode decomposition, it shows that multicollinearity generates unstable and oscillatory weight patterns. The key takeaway is that while regularization stabilizes the estimates, this stabilization does not equate to true physical interpretability.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous mathematical framework connecting statistical concepts like multicollinearity and model instability to the physical concept of 'interpretability' in data-driven science. It suggests that prediction performance (low MSE) is insufficient for claiming physical understanding.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the fundamental limits of interpretability when using multiple linear regression models, particularly when input features are strongly correlated (multicollinearity). It seeks to explain why weight estimates become unstable or oscillatory even if prediction accuracy remains high.

Main result. Multicollinearity causes weight instability through small-eigenvalue modes in the feature correlation matrix. While regularization like Ridge helps stabilize weights by suppressing these modes, it does not guarantee physical interpretability, as the resulting weights are highly dependent on the chosen regularization parameter alpha.

Method. The analysis combines theoretical linear algebra (analyzing eigenmodes of the feature correlation matrix) with statistical diagnostics (VIF calculation and analyzing weight fluctuations across bootstrap samples). It compares OLS and Ridge regression estimates.

Model / system. The model is Multiple Linear Regression ($\hat{\mathcal{Y}} = \sum \hat{w}(f) X(f)$), applied to diverse physical datasets such as superconductivity properties and glassy-dynamics structural descriptors. The analysis focuses on the mathematical structure imposed by highly correlated input features.

Key observables. Weight fluctuations (standard deviation across samples), weight oscillations across physically similar features, Maximum Variance Inflation Factor ($ ext{max } f{ ext{VIF}(f)}$), and the roughness measure ($S$).

Important parameters / regimes. Regularization strength $\alpha$, eigenvalues ($\lambda(k)$) of the feature correlation matrix $C$, and sample size $N_S$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that interpretability requires both statistical robustness (low weight variance) and physical consistency (stable signs/magnitudes for similar features). It models correlations using Toeplitz matrices in some sections.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the three scaling regimes of $ ext{max } f{ ext{VIF}(f)}$ vs. $\alpha$ ($\propto \alpha^{-1}$ to $\propto \alpha^{-2}$), show weight oscillations decreasing with increasing regularization strength, and display eigenvectors corresponding to small eigenvalues.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the problem (weight instability under multicollinearity) to developing theoretical tools (eigenmode analysis, VIF generalization for Ridge). It then applies these tools across various datasets, culminating in a detailed mathematical derivation showing how regularization suppresses oscillatory modes associated with small eigenvalues.

Abstract

Interpreting machine-learning models has attracted increasing attention, particularly in the physical sciences, where one often seeks to understand the underlying mechanisms rather than merely make predictions. Multiple linear regression is often regarded as an interpretable alternative to more complex models, such as deep neural networks, because its predictions are expressed as explicit weighted sums of input features. However, when input features are strongly correlated, namely in the presence of multicollinearity, the learned weights can exhibit large dataset-to-dataset fluctuations and oscillatory behavior across physically similar features, making their interpretation difficult or even impossible. Although the instability of the weights under multicollinearity is well known in statistics, its consequences for physical interpretation, in particular its connection to oscillatory weights across physically similar features, have not been systematically clarified. Here, we theoretically discuss the mechanism behind this loss of interpretability by analyzing the eigenmodes of the feature correlation matrix. We show that small-eigenvalue modes associated with multicollinearity amplify fluctuations in the weights and generate oscillatory patterns that do not necessarily reflect meaningful contributions. We test this theoretical picture numerically on physics datasets and show that Ridge regularization suppresses these unstable modes, although the resulting weights must still be interpreted with caution. We further confirm the generality of our findings beyond physics by analyzing a diverse collection of publicly available datasets. Our results clarify why, in the presence of multicollinearity, physical interpretation can remain difficult even for linear regression models.

Worst-case depth hierarchy for shallow quantum circuits

Authors: Min-Hsiu Hsieh, Michael de Oliveira, Sathyawageeswar Subramanian, Xingjian Zhang
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.16425v1

Summary. The paper establishes an explicit and unconditional depth hierarchy theorem for constant-depth quantum circuits ($ ext{QNC}^0$). By mapping computational tasks to nonlocal games defined by operator constraints, the authors prove that increasing circuit depth strictly increases computational power. This provides a rigorous measure of quantum advantage based solely on the required depth of the quantum circuit.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides fundamental complexity results by quantifying the resource cost of quantum computation in terms of circuit depth, which is a key metric for physical implementation constraints.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The central problem is establishing an explicit, unconditional depth hierarchy for constant-depth quantum computation ($ ext{QNC}^0$), demonstrating that increasing circuit depth strictly increases computational power.

Main result. The authors prove a depth hierarchy theorem showing that certain two-round interactive problems require quantum circuits of depth $\Omega(d)$ to solve perfectly, while shallower circuits fail with significant probability. This establishes an unconditional quantum advantage over classical $ ext{NC}^0$ computation based purely on circuit depth.

Method. The methodology involves translating the computational problem into a rigid operator-valued constraint system derived from group theory and nonlocal games. Lower bounds are established by showing that perfect solutions require implementing multi-controlled phase operations, which necessitates deep circuits.

Model / system. The model is quantum computation realized via bounded fan-in quantum circuits over the Clifford+T gate set. The complexity is analyzed using two-round interactive problems and constraint systems derived from abelian groups like $\mathbb{Z}_m^2$.

Key observables. Circuit Depth ($d$), Success Probability ($\epsilon(d)$), Multi-controlled phase operations (e.g., $C_{m-1}(\mathbb{Z})$).

Important parameters / regimes. Depth $d$, precision $\epsilon$, and the number of qubits/labels $m$.

Assumptions / limitations. The lower bound proofs rely on constructing constraint systems whose unique faithful solutions require specific non-Clifford gates, and later sections address robustness under approximate state preparation.

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically represents the established quantum depth hierarchies. Figure 2 shows logical dependencies for analyzing the quantum-input relation problem; Figure 3 shows dependencies for the classical verifier construction.

Paper structure. The paper develops from establishing the basic hierarchy using nonlocal games (Chunk 1), refining this by connecting it to operator constraints and self-testing in semi-quantum settings (Chunks 2, 4, 6), deriving specific depth lower bounds related to multi-controlled gates (Chunk 7), and finally analyzing upper bounds for generalized Toffoli gates (Chunk 8).

Abstract

Circuit depth is a central resource in complexity theory. While bounded-depth classical circuits admit well-understood hierarchy theorems, the internal structure of constant-depth quantum computation remains comparatively unexplored. We prove an explicit depth hierarchy theorem for $\mathsf{QNC}^0$. For each $d\ge 12$, we construct a family of two-round interactive problems on which no depth-$(d-1)$ quantum circuit can achieve near-perfect success, regardless of gate set, circuit size, or ancillary qubits. In contrast, we prove that our construction admits realizations by simple bounded fan-in quantum circuits of depth larger than $d$ by a small constant factor. Moreover, all bounded fan-in classical circuits of sublogarithmic depth (in the input size) fail to achieve perfect success on these tasks for every $d$, yielding a hierarchy of problems that show unconditional quantum advantage of $\mathsf{QNC}^0$ over $\mathsf{NC}^0$. A key obstacle is the scarcity of lower bound techniques for quantum circuits. To address this, we develop methods to analyze how depth affects a circuit's ability to realize nonlocal correlations amongst its output qubits in a fine-grained manner. Our approach exploits the correspondence between constraint systems and nonlocal games, translating group-theoretic constructions into rigid operator-valued constraint systems and then into non-local games. In particular, we construct constraint systems whose unique faithful operator-valued solutions require every perfect strategy, and every near-perfect strategy to a fixed precision, to implement multi-controlled phase operations. This reduces to a nonlocal unitary-synthesis problem, yielding depth lower bounds for both shallow quantum and classical circuits. These results show that increasing depth strictly increases computational power within $\mathsf{QNC}^0$, establishing a genuinely quantum hierarchy.
