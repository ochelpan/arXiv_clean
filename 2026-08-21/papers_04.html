Sign-problem-resilient singular-value probe in determinant quantum Monte Carlo

Authors: Wen Chen, Rubem Mondaini
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.19028v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work introduces the adjacent gap ratio of singular values of the fermion matrix as a robust diagnostic for phase transitions in DQMC simulations. By analyzing Hubbard models, the authors demonstrate that this spectral statistic remains sensitive to underlying physics even when the simulation encounters a sign or phase problem. This establishes a powerful, sign-problem-resilient probe for strongly correlated electron systems.

Why it may be interesting. The technique provides a powerful, purely spectral diagnostic tool that bypasses the fundamental sign problem inherent in simulating fermionic quantum many-body systems, which is crucial for understanding correlated materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The sign problem limits the use of determinant quantum Monte Carlo (DQMC) for studying strongly correlated fermion systems, necessitating a sign-problem-resilient diagnostic for phase transitions.

Main result. The adjacent gap ratio of low-lying singular values of the space-time fermion matrix successfully tracks interaction-driven phase transitions in both sign-free and complex-weight Hubbard models, remaining robust against phase reweighting.

Method. The authors use the adjacent gap ratio of low-lying singular values ($ ext{err}$) of the space-time fermion matrix, derived from the Hubbard-Stratonovich decoupling, as a diagnostic tool.

Model / system. The study focuses on half-filled honeycomb-lattice Hubbard models, including the sign-free and complex-weight Haldane-Hubbard models, analyzed via DQMC simulations.

Key observables. Adjacent gap ratio of low-lying singular values ($ ext{err} = \min(\delta_n, \delta_{n+1}) / \max(\delta_n, \delta_{n+1}$)), and the average phase $\langle e^{i heta} angle_{ ext{ref}}$.

Important parameters / regimes. Interaction strength ($U/t_1$), lattice geometry (honeycomb), and the auxiliary-field coupling strength ($\lambda$).

Assumptions / limitations. The method relies on the local spectral correlations of singular values, which are assumed to be insensitive to the sign/phase of the determinant itself.

Figures summary. Figures show the gap ratio $\langle ext{err} angle_{L au}$ for synthetic ensembles comparing different paths and models, and also compare the gap ratio for the spin-channel versus the SU(2) charge channel.

Paper structure. The paper develops the diagnostic using the gap ratio, applies it to the honeycomb and Haldane-Hubbard models to demonstrate transition tracking, and contrasts this spin-channel response with an SU(2) charge-channel decomposition to validate the specificity of the spin-based probe.

Abstract

The sign problem limits determinant quantum Monte Carlo studies of strongly correlated fermion systems. In the spin-channel Hubbard-Stratonovich decoupling, spin correlations are exactly related to auxiliary-field correlations. This relation implies that an antiferromagnetic transition reorganizes auxiliary-field configurations and thereby changes the statistical structure of the resulting fermion matrices. We use the adjacent gap ratio of low-lying singular values of the space-time fermion matrix to probe interaction-driven transitions in two half-filled honeycomb-lattice Hubbard models. In the sign-free honeycomb Hubbard model, the statistic tracks the established transition from a Dirac semimetal to an antiferromagnetic Mott insulator. In the complex-weight Haldane-Hubbard model, the transition-sensitive feature remains visible in the phase-quenched reference ensemble and occurs near previous estimates of the transition. Moreover, phase reweighting only weakly modifies the gap ratio over the regimes investigated, despite the rapid suppression of the average phase. These results establish singular-value statistics as a sign-problem-resilient probe of interaction-driven transitions in determinant quantum Monte Carlo.

Ultrasonic attenuation in inhomogeneous superconductors

Authors: Aman Sardwal, Andreas Kreisel, Shantanu Mukherjee
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.19079v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical work calculates ultrasonic attenuation in inhomogeneous superconductors to probe the nature of subgap bound states. By using a BdG formulation, the authors show that the attenuation signal is highly sensitive to the spin structure and symmetry of the bound states. This allows ultrasound to distinguish between different types of electronic states, such as those induced by non-magnetic versus magnetic impurities at grain boundaries.

Why it may be interesting. The sensitivity of the attenuation to spin structure and symmetry provides a powerful, non-spectroscopic probe for understanding exotic pairing symmetries and local magnetic moments in strongly correlated superconductors.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how ultrasonic attenuation can be used to distinguish between different types of superconducting bound states (e.g., magnetic vs. non-magnetic) in inhomogeneous superconductors.

Main result. The attenuation signature is highly sensitive to the symmetry and spin structure of the bound states; specifically, local magnetism can induce a clear attenuation peak where non-magnetic impurities cause only weak signals due to coherence factor cancellation.

Method. The authors use a real-space Bogoliubov-de Gennes (BdG) formulation combined with linear response theory to calculate the first-order correction to the phonon decay rate.

Model / system. The study models superconductors, focusing on inhomogeneous junctions like phase-biased SNS junctions and grain boundaries in $d_{x^2-y^2}$ superconductors. The calculation involves the electron-phonon interaction derived from hopping parameter variations.

Key observables. Ultrasonic attenuation coefficient ($\alpha(q, \lambda)$) and the phonon lifetime ($ au_{q\lambda}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Low temperatures, phonon frequency matching subgap level separation, and the presence/absence of local magnetism.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the first-order correction in the decay rate calculation and uses an effective mean-field model for the superconducting gap.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate gap structures, the comparison of attenuation from non-magnetic vs. magnetic bound states, and the temperature dependence of attenuation in s-wave and d-wave systems.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theoretical framework using BdG formalism and linear response theory, applies it to specific inhomogeneous geometries (SNS junctions, grain boundaries), and contrasts the resulting attenuation signatures based on the nature of the impurity/disorder.

Abstract

Subgap bound states can produce pronounced features in the density of states without necessarily giving rise to a corresponding signal in ultrasonic attenuation. We show that the coherence factors act as a filter for the contribution of bound states to ultrasonic attenuation. In a phase-biased superconductor--normal--superconductor junction, the Andreev bound states produce a resonance in the attenuation when the phonon frequency matches the phase-dependent separation between the subgap levels. The situation is different at a $(110)$ grain boundary in a $d_{x^2-y^2}$ superconductor. There, nonmagnetic impurity-induced bound states remain weak in the attenuation despite their large spectral weight, because the relevant coherence factors nearly cancel. When local magnetism develops near the boundary, this cancellation is removed, the bound states become spin split, and a clear attenuation peak appears, which we argue is governed by a process involving emission of phonons by pair of quasiparticles at low temperatures. We obtain these results using a real-space Bogoliubov--de Gennes formulation of ultrasonic attenuation and benchmark the method against the known low-temperature behavior of homogeneous $s$- and $d$-wave superconductors. The results show that ultrasound can distinguish bound states with similar spectral signatures but different symmetry and spin structure.

Unconventional bond- and current-density waves on hexagonal lattices

Authors: Andras Szabo, Hannes Braun, Laura Classen
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.19185v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

Summary. This work establishes a comprehensive theoretical framework to classify unconventional Charge-Density Wave (CDW) orders on hexagonal lattices by integrating local symmetry constraints (little group) with multiple ordering wave vectors. Using RPA on the triangular lattice, the authors derive Landau free energies whose coefficients determine whether the ground state favors a single-momentum-vector (single-Q) or multi-momentum-vector (multi-Q) order. This provides a systematic route for predicting exotic electronic ground states in correlated quantum materials.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous application of group theory to classify complex ordered phases like CDWs provides a powerful, generalizable theoretical tool for understanding electronic instabilities in correlated materials, which is relevant to understanding exotic quantum states.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops a systematic framework to describe unconventional Charge-Density Wave (CDW) phases that incorporate both local symmetries (little group) and multiple symmetry-related ordering wave vectors (the 'star').

Main result. The stability of the CDW state (single-Q vs. multi-Q) is determined by the sign of the coefficient $2eta_1 - eta_2$ in the Landau free energy, allowing for a systematic classification of ground states.

Method. The analysis combines group-theoretical symmetry analysis (using little groups and space groups) with mean-field theory, specifically calculating Landau free energy coefficients via the Random Phase Approximation (RPA).

Model / system. The study focuses on two-dimensional hexagonal lattices, using the triangular lattice as a minimal example, analyzed via a tight-binding model and Hubbard-type interactions.

Key observables. Order parameters ($\Delta, \psi, \eta$), Landau free energy coefficients ($\alpha, eta, \gamma$), and the resulting CDW structure (bond or current density).

Important parameters / regimes. Chemical potential ($\mu$), temperature ($T$), and the relative signs of the quartic free energy coefficients ($eta_1, eta_2$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on mean-field approximations (RPA) and the systematic incorporation of local symmetries via the little group.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the hexagonal Brillouin zone and the classification of form factors based on symmetry. Other figures show the dependence of free energy coefficients ($eta_1, 2eta_1 + eta_2$) on temperature and chemical potential, determining the phase stability.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by establishing the general symmetry framework (LG/SG) for unconventional CDWs, deriving the relevant form factors and symmetry-constrained Landau free energies, and finally applying this framework to the triangular lattice using RPA calculations to determine the ground state phase.

Abstract

Charge-density wave (CDW) orders are conventionally described as modulations of on-site charge at an ordering wave vector $\boldsymbol{Q}$ with symmetry-related wave vectors typically forming a multicomponent order-parameter manifold. Recent developments significantly broadened this phenomenology to bond and loop-current density waves, which possess nontrivial, potentially symmetry-breaking textures within the unit cell in addition to their spatial modulation at $\boldsymbol{Q}$. Such textures arise from particle-hole condensates with nonzero angular momentum, analogous to unconventional superconductivity, and their symmetries are described by the little group $G_{\boldsymbol{Q}}$. In this work, we develop a framework for unconventional CDW phases that simultaneously incorporates the local symmetries described by little group and the presence of multiple symmetry-related ordering wave vectors, also known as the star. The resulting multicomponent order parameter transforms under representations of the full space group induced from irreducible representations of $G_{\boldsymbol{Q}}$. We apply this framework to two-dimensional lattices with sixfold symmetry and ordering wave vectors along high-symmetry lines, and derive the corresponding Landau free energies. As a microscopic example, we demonstrate the emergence of unconventional bond and loop-current orders from electronic interactions on the triangular lattice within the random phase approximation, and determine their ground states by microscopically evaluating the relevant coefficients in the free energy. Our framework provides a systematic route to describing unconventional modulated phases and can be readily extended to more complex lattices.

(Almost) quadruply optimal unitary designs in 1D

Authors: Guoding Liu, Jonas Helsen
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.18650v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper presents a method to construct approximate unitary k-designs on 1D quantum hardware that achieves near-optimal scaling for both circuit depth and the number of required magic gates. By refining previous techniques and combining them with the Gluing Lemma, the authors demonstrate efficient resource utilization for generating highly mixed quantum states on linear quantum processors.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides concrete, resource-efficient circuit constructions for achieving highly mixed quantum states (unitary designs) on physically constrained 1D hardware, which is crucial for quantum simulation and computation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The central problem is constructing approximate unitary k-designs on 1D quantum architectures with minimal resource overhead regarding circuit depth and magic gates.

Main result. The authors achieve simultaneous near-optimality in circuit depth and T-gate count for 1D systems, matching known lower bounds up to a logarithmic factor.

Method. The construction combines and refines existing results, utilizing a two-layer block-brickwork architecture and the Gluing Lemma to extend local designs to global ones.

Model / system. The system is modeled as a 1D nearest-neighbor quantum architecture (qubits) implementing unitary operations, involving Clifford group operations and random permutations.

Key observables. Circuit depth, number of magic gates (T gates), and the relative error ($\epsilon$) of the unitary k-design.

Important parameters / regimes. Design order $k$, system size $n$, relative error $\epsilon$, and locality $\ell$.

Assumptions / limitations. The construction relies on the Gluing Lemma and assumes the block size is an integer multiple of the locality parameter.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the two-layer block-brickwork circuit architecture for the design, and Figure 2 relates to implementing the local generators using CPZPC generators.

Paper structure. The paper builds by first improving the magic block size reduction, then constructing a constant-spectral-gap generating set for the Clifford group, and finally combining these elements using the gluing lemma to prove the main near-optimal design result.

Abstract

We construct $n$-qubit approximate unitary $k$-designs in 1D systems, achieving circuit depth $O(\log(n/\varepsilon) + k\log k)$ with relative error $\varepsilon$ and requiring $O(nk\log k)$ magic gates. This matches existing lower bounds $Ω(\log(n/\varepsilon) + k)$ for circuit depth, and $\widetildeΩ(nk)$ for the required number of $T$ gates, up to a $\log k$ factor, achieving simultaneous near-optimality in all parameters. Our construction is based on a combination and refinement of two existing results. We reduce the required magic block size for breaking Clifford symmetries in the magic-augmented circuit construction of Zhang et al. from $O(k\log k)$ to $O(\log k)$. We also improve the breakthrough construction of Chen et al. to construct a generating set of 1D local constant-depth circuits for the unitary group with a constant spectral gap, making $O(\log k)$-local random unitaries realizable in depth $O(k\log k)$. As a by-product, we provide a constant-size 1D-local generating set for the Clifford group, which we expect to be of independent interest. Combining the two results with the gluing lemma, we prove the final result.

Summary. This paper introduces AlphaClifford, a novel model-based Reinforcement Learning framework that uses Monte Carlo Tree Search to efficiently synthesize and optimize Clifford quantum circuits. By modeling the problem using the algebraic structure of the symplectic group, it significantly outperforms existing heuristics in minimizing gate counts for both general optimization and hardware-constrained transpilation.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on abstract computation, the rigorous mathematical modeling of the state space using group theory (symplectic group) and the application of advanced AI search techniques (MCTS) provide powerful computational tools applicable to modeling complex quantum dynamics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Standard methods for synthesizing Clifford quantum circuits yield sub-optimal gate counts, which is inefficient for fault-tolerant quantum computation.

Main result. AlphaClifford achieves significant reductions (up to 56% total gate reduction) in gate counts compared to state-of-the-art heuristics for both unconstrained optimization and hardware-constrained transpilation.

Method. The authors introduce AlphaClifford, a model-based Reinforcement Learning framework utilizing Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) to explore the combinatorial space of Clifford circuits.

Model / system. The system involves Clifford circuits, which are mathematically characterized by their action on the symplectic group $ ext{Sp}(2n, ext{F}_2)$ via the symplectic matrix representation $M = [X Z]$.

Key observables. Total gate count and two-qubit (CNOT) gate count are minimized; the goal is to match a target symplectic matrix $M_{ ext{target}}$.

Important parameters / regimes. The fundamental gate set is {H, S, CNOT}; the state space is modeled using the algebraic properties of the symplectic group.

Assumptions / limitations. The synthesis guarantees equivalence only up to a Pauli layer ($P \in P_n$), relying on the homomorphism $\phi: C_n o ext{Sp}(2n, ext{F}_2)$.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the process: (a) Training uses MCTS on the symplectic matrix tree; (b) Synthesis uses the model to find a circuit matching a target matrix; (c) Shows applications in optimization, transpilation, and logical synthesis.

Paper structure. The paper details the mathematical formulation using symplectic matrices, introduces the AlphaClifford RL framework powered by MCTS, presents results across general optimization and transpilation tasks, and discusses future directions.

Abstract

Clifford circuits play a foundational role in quantum computing, particularly due to their importance in quantum error correction and fault-tolerant logical synthesis. While these circuits can be efficiently simulated and represented as symplectic matrices, standard synthesis methods-such as the Aaronson-Gottesman algorithm-often yield sub-optimal circuits with excessively high gate counts. In this work, we introduce AlphaClifford, a model-based Reinforcement Learning framework powered by Monte Carlo Tree Search, designed to efficiently synthesize Clifford circuits from the fundamental gate set composed of H, S, and CNOT. By modeling the state space through the algebraic properties of the symplectic group, AlphaClifford effectively explores this combinatorial space to minimize overall circuit cost. For unconstrained Clifford optimization, our approach achieves a consistent reduction in both total and two-qubit (CNOT) gate counts compared to state-of-the-art synthesis heuristics, despite operating with a strictly less expressive gate set. Furthermore, we demonstrate the broad applicability of our framework on two additional tasks: hardware-constrained Clifford transpilation, where we outperform existing RL-based compilers, and as a post-synthesis optimization component within a full Clifford+T logical synthesis pipeline. Our results underscore that model-based RL is highly effective at addressing the combinatorial complexities of quantum compilation, offering a scalable pathway to mitigate hardware constraints in both near-term and future fault-tolerant quantum devices.

Abstract

Anti-altermagnets exhibit odd-parity nonrelativistic spin splitting, yet unlike even-parity altermagnets, their momentum-space symmetries lack a reduced classification analogous to spin Laue groups. Here, we introduce anti-spin Laue groups, organized into three distinct classes, and identify 21 groups describing the odd-parity partial wave character of this unconventional class. Together with the 10 spin Laue groups of altermagnets, they complete the classification of nonrelativistic unconventional magnets with collinear momentum-space spin polarization. Anti-spin Laue groups also provide a many-to-one reduction of spin space (point) groups by retaining only their action on the collinear momentum-space spin polarization, thereby directly encoding the symmetry-enforced nodal spin-splitting character. Based on this we develop a systematic model-construction algorithm yielding minimal, material-oriented four-band models. This framework places odd- and even-parity unconventional magnets on equal footing within a unified momentum-space symmetry description.

Abstract

Copy-protection encodes a functionality in a reusable quantum state that cannot be split into two states (freeloader adversaries) which remain simultaneously useful. Prior plain-model results handle only independently sampled challenges; the more natural identical-challenge notion, also tied to unclonable bits and copy-protection of point functions, has remained open. We strengthen these definitions and prove plain-model security for our new stronger notions. For single-decryptor encryption (SDE) we define correlated challenge security, show it implies all previous SDE notions including identical-challenge security, and prove that the construction of Kitagawa and Yamakawa (TCC'25) achieves it assuming iO and one-way functions. We also obtain the first plain-model copy protection for point functions, $k$-point functions, and compute-and-compare programs, and identical-challenge copy protection for general puncturable functionalities.

Entropic Rigidity in Quantum Memories: How Geometry and Algebra Control the Onset of Degeneracy Corrections

Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.18420v1

Summary. This theoretical work establishes a quantitative measure called 'entropic rigidity depth' ($r$) to characterize the performance gap between two decoding strategies (MP vs. MLD) in quantum error correction. By relating this depth to the code's geometry and algebra, the authors provide a universal benchmark for understanding the fundamental limits of quantum memory reliability under noise.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on QEC, the rigorous mathematical framework for quantifying 'entropic rigidity' provides a general tool for understanding how geometric constraints (like topology or algebraic structure) impose fundamental limits on information processing capacity.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper determines the minimum physical error weight ($m$) at which the decision criteria from Maximum-Probability (MP) decoding and Degenerate Maximum-Likelihood (MLD) decoding diverge for quantum error-correcting codes.

Main result. The divergence onset is governed by an entropic rigidity depth $r$, leading to a quantifiable hierarchy ($r=0, 1, 2$) that provides an exact benchmark for low-noise decoder selection.

Method. The authors compare the peak probability (MP) against the total probability mass (MLD) using algebraic combinatorics and analyzing the lexicographic spectra of logical sectors.

Model / system. The study analyzes quantum error-correcting codes, including planar surface codes, toric codes, and qLDPC codes, subjected to code-capacity Pauli noise.

Key observables. Entropic rigidity depth ($r$), minimum error weight ($m$), and the gap $\Delta(t)$ between MP and MLD performance.

Important parameters / regimes. Code distance ($d$), physical noise strength ($t$), and the universal bound $h = \lceil d/2 ceil$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the use of code-capacity Pauli noise to isolate intrinsic entropy effects and relies on defining the winner sets via set-valued comparisons.

Figures summary. Figures compare MP vs. MLD performance, illustrate the entropic rigidity depth ($r$), and show finite noise curves and validity windows for planar and toric geometries.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by defining the statistical comparison between MP and MLD, deriving the entropic rigidity depth $r$ via the relation $m=h+r$, and then certifying this depth for specific families of quantum codes.

Abstract

Maximum-probability (MP) decoding selects the most probable microscopic error, whereas degenerate maximum-likelihood (MLD) decoding includes the configurational entropy of an entire logical sector. Using code-capacity Pauli noise to isolate rigidity intrinsic to the code, we determine the first physical error weight $m$ at which their logical winner sets become disjoint, even under optimal MP tie resolution. Code distance imposes the universal bound $m\geq h=\lceil d/2\rceil$. We define the entropic rigidity depth $r$ through $m=h+r$ and certify a three-level hierarchy: $r=0$ for planar surface codes and two concatenated families, $r=1$ for odd-distance square toric codes and the Gross $[[144,12,12]]$ quantum low-density-parity-check code, and $r=2$ for a separable family with hypergraph product and bivariate bicycle descriptions. The onset fixes the leading operational failure gap, proportional to the $m$th power of the physical noise strength. Geometry and algebra therefore provide quantifiable controls of configurational entropy and an exact benchmark for low-noise decoder selection.

Good Stabilizer Codes from Shallow Clifford Circuits with Random Matchings

Summary. This paper develops a method to construct high-performance quantum error-correcting codes using shallow random circuits. This advances the feasibility of fault-tolerant quantum computation.

Abstract

Encoding quantum information with low circuit overhead is a fundamental challenge in fault-tolerant quantum computation. Random circuits provide a natural mechanism for rapidly spreading logical information through simple gates applied in parallel. Brown and Fawzi showed that random Clifford circuits on two-qubit Clifford gates provide such encoders that achieve the quantum Gilbert-Varshamov rate-distance tradeoff with depth $O(\log^3 n)$. We show that the same asymptotic tradeoff is attained in optimal $O(\log n)$ depth under a gate distribution with a more restricted support. Abstract

Two-dimensional (2D) electron/hole gases confined in semiconductor heterostructures suffer from weak Rashba spin-orbit coupling (SOC) for manipulating spin degreee of freedom via an electric rather than a magnetic field. Here, we show that complementary metal-oxide-semiconductor technology-accessible strain could substantially enhance the linear Rashba SOC of the top hole subband in Ge/SiGe quantum wells (QWs) to a level comparable to that of 2D Rashba materials through enhancing the mixture of the light-hole and heavy-hole bands. We further show that strongly enhanced Rashba SOC boosts the Rabi frequency of hole spin qubits confined in Ge/SiGe QWs by two orders of magnitude to an unprecedented 40 GHz, more than one order of magnitude faster than other qubit platforms. We also demonstrate that the hole spin rotation with Rabi frequency > 25 GHz enters a new regime being immune to gate control-induced electric noise, opening a new avenue to simultaneously improve the gate speed and gate fidelity. Our findings provide a new routine to substantially enhance the Rashba SOC in 2D semiconductor hole gases to a level that is great for spintronic applications.

Abstract

As quantum circuits grow beyond small toy examples, preparing them for execution on physical devices becomes increasingly complex. Design automation is therefore essential for scalable quantum computing: Compilation procedures optimize resource requirements and transform circuits to a format compatible with specific hardware; resource estimation evaluates execution cost; verification methods prove circuit correctness. However, these concepts present a steep learning curve for novices, particularly when quantum circuits are introduced through low-level textual representations. To address this, we present a block-based programming framework for quantum design automation, implemented as an extension to the Scratch programming platform. This system allows users to build quantum circuits as a sequence of blocks and embed them in classical control logic to perform evaluations, compare simulation results, and directly apply different design automation techniques. We evaluated the approach in a user study with computer science students, who completed guided exercises using the platform and provided structured feedback in the form of self-reports and short knowledge assessments. Results demonstrate strong understanding and confidence in quantum design automation concepts, suggesting that the block-based approach successfully lowers the entry barrier to quantum design automation. The implemented framework is open-source and available at https://github.com/munich-quantum-toolkit/scratch-quantum.
