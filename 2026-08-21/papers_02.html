Direct fidelity estimation through joint fiducial grouping

Authors: Júlia Barberà-Rodríguez, Arthur Strauss
Type: both · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.18548v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: QC/QI experiment 3/5 · quantum measurements 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper presents joint fiducial grouping, a technique to drastically reduce the experimental overhead required for quantum gate fidelity estimation by grouping commuting Pauli pairs. This grouped estimator provides a practical, context-preserving method to estimate entanglement fidelity, which is then leveraged as a reward function to train quantum gates using reinforcement learning.

Why it may be interesting. The focus on reducing experimental overhead in quantum characterization is crucial for scaling up quantum computation, directly impacting the feasibility of implementing complex quantum algorithms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the high experimental overhead associated with direct fidelity estimation (DFE) for characterizing quantum gates, especially when the target gate is non-Clifford.

Main result. Joint fiducial grouping is introduced to partition Pauli pairs such that multiple transfer coefficients can be estimated using a single preparation-measurement setting, significantly reducing experimental overhead.

Method. The authors derive an unbiased grouped estimator for entanglement fidelity by grouping commuting input-output Pauli pairs, which is then applied in a context-sensitive reward function for reinforcement learning gate calibration.

Model / system. The work is applied to characterizing general quantum channels (CPTP maps) and specifically demonstrates its utility for calibrating the parametrically controlled two-qubit gate, fSim(theta, phi).

Key observables. Pauli transfer coefficients ($\chi_A(\alpha, eta)$), Entanglement fidelity ($F_e(U, E)$), and the context-sensitive reward $J( heta)$ used in RL.

Important parameters / regimes. Error parameters ($\epsilon, \delta$), the number of required settings ($M$ vs. $|\Omega|$), and the gate parameters ($ heta, \phi$).

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes the ability to measure Pauli transfer coefficients and that grouping is possible based on commuting input/output Paulis. Truncation introduces a quantifiable bias ($W_{disc}$).

Figures summary. The notes reference Tables I and II, detailing the noise model parameters (e.g., $T_1/T_2$, readout matrices) for the fSim comparison and the specific settings used for PPO/DFE calibration runs.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the limitations of standard DFE, then introduces the theoretical framework of joint fiducial grouping to derive the grouped estimator. It then validates this estimator by applying it to the fSim gate and using the resulting fidelity estimates as a reward signal for a model-free reinforcement learning calibration routine.

Abstract

Fault-tolerant quantum computation hinges on the requirement for low physical error rates. Reaching below threshold regime requires the accounting of circuit dependent noise, that is inherent to the execution context in which a quantum gate is usually embedded. Direct fidelity estimation is a technique that offers natural context preservation as it solely requires the insertion of local Pauli preparation and measurement fiducials around the window of interest. However, each sampled input-output Pauli pair demands its own preparation and measurement setting, an overhead that grows rapidly once the target gate is no longer Clifford. We introduce joint fiducial grouping, which partitions Pauli pairs into sets with commuting input and output operators, allowing several Pauli-transfer coefficients to be estimated within the same preparation-measurement setting. We derive an unbiased grouped estimator and finite-sample guarantees showing that grouping always reduces the number of distinct input-output settings and can also reduce the required channel uses when the target weight is concentrated within compatible groups. We characterize these gains for the parametric two-qubit gate $\mathrm{fSim}(θ,\varphi)$, and use the grouped estimator as a context-sensitive reward for reinforcement-learning-based gate calibration. Our results provide a practical route to lower-overhead, context-preserving fidelity estimation for continuously parameterized quantum gates.

Finite-temperature Green's function cluster expansion from thermofield doubles: Breakdown of the polaron picture

Authors: M. R. Carbone, S. Fomichev, B. Kloss, A. J. Millis, M. Berciu, D. R. Reichman, J. Sous
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.18267v1

Summary. This work presents a numerically exact method, the finite-temperature Green's function cluster expansion, to calculate polaron properties at finite temperatures. By combining cluster expansion techniques with the thermofield double formalism, the authors show that the simple picture of a single quasiparticle peak breaks down as temperature increases. This has significant implications for understanding charge transport in materials where electron-phonon coupling is strong.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous treatment of finite-temperature effects in strongly coupled electron-phonon systems, particularly the identification of spectral features beyond simple quasiparticle poles, is highly relevant for understanding charge transport in molecular and organic semiconductors.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to compute the momentum- and frequency-resolved single-particle Green's function of a polaron at finite temperature, addressing the breakdown of simple quasiparticle descriptions.

Main result. The method successfully computes the spectral function across coupling regimes and temperatures, revealing that at finite T, the single-Lorentzian polaron picture breaks down due to hybridization with thermal-satellite continua.

Method. It introduces the finite-temperature Green's function cluster expansion (TGCE), which combines the generalized Green's function cluster expansion (GGCE) with the thermofield double (TFD) formalism.

Model / system. The study focuses on the polaron problem, modeled using the Holstein Hamiltonian, describing a charge carrier coupled to dispersive phonons. The TFD formalism maps the thermal trace onto a pure-state expectation value in an enlarged, doubled Hilbert space.

Key observables. Single-particle Green's function G(k, omega), spectral functions A(k, omega), polaron dispersion, lifetimes, and effective masses.

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature relative to phonon frequency (T/Omega), coupling strength ($\lambda$), and phonon frequency ($\Omega$).

Assumptions / limitations. The method is numerically exact in principle, but convergence requires careful handling of the non-trivial configuration structure in the doubled phonon Hilbert space.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the matrix size scaling, the convergence of the spectral function at various temperatures, and show the transition of the spectral line shape from a clean Lorentzian at T=0 to multi-peak structures at finite T.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the TGCE method by combining GGCE and TFD. It then benchmarks the method on the 1D Holstein polaron, calculating key observables across varying coupling strengths and temperatures, culminating in a discussion of the breakdown of the simple polaron picture.

Abstract

We introduce a method, numerically exact in principle, for computing the momentum- and frequency-resolved single-particle Green's function of a polaron at finite temperature. The method, which we refer to as the finite-temperature Green's function cluster expansion, combines two ingredients: the generalized Green's function cluster expansion, a numerically exact extension of the momentum average family of methods that solves the polaron problem at zero temperature through a hierarchy of equations of motion for restricted phonon cloud configurations; and the thermofield double formalism, which maps the thermal trace onto a pure-state expectation value over a doubled Hilbert space. The resulting equations of motion have the same algebraic structure as those of the multi-boson zero-temperature theory, with the temperature entering through a Bogoliubov-type mixing angle that controls the coupling to a set of fictitious bath bosons. We implement the method in our open-source software package and benchmark it on the one-dimensional Holstein polaron, computing spectral functions, dispersions, lifetimes, and effective masses across coupling regimes and temperatures up to $T/Ω\sim 1$. Where finite-temperature density matrix renormalization group results are available, we find quantitative agreement at affordable computational cost. The method recovers momentum-resolved spectra directly in frequency space, with no time evolution or analytic continuation. We also discuss the practical costs of the approach. In particular, since the doubled phonon Hilbert space has a non-trivial configuration structure in which real and fictitious clouds compete, convergence in the corresponding cloud parameters requires care.

Robustness of spin state superpositions for noisy quantum metrology

Summary. This theoretical paper analyzes the robustness of quantum metrology using spin state superpositions under correlated dephasing noise. By deriving perturbative expressions for the Quantum Fisher Information, the authors characterize a fundamental trade-off: the very moments that boost sensitivity also increase noise susceptibility. The findings guide the selection of optimal quantum states for high-precision measurements in noisy environments.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides deep analytical insight into the fundamental limitations imposed by noise on quantum sensing protocols, which is crucial for designing robust quantum measurement strategies in physical systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how the precision limits in quantum metrology, quantified by the Quantum Fisher Information (QFI), degrade when the quantum probe state is subjected to noisy environments, specifically spatially correlated dephasing.

Main result. A key trade-off is identified where the collective-spin moments that enhance the noiseless sensitivity also increase the leading noise-induced decay of the QFI, though certain states like SCCSs show enhanced robustness.

Method. The authors employ a perturbative framework to derive analytical expressions for the QFI dynamics, comparing these results against the quantum Cramér–Rao bound and measurement-specific sensitivity bounds.

Model / system. The system modeled is an ensemble of N spins symmetric under particle exchange, analyzed within the fully symmetric subspace of fixed angular momentum J=N/2. The noise is modeled as spatially correlated dephasing.

Key observables. Quantum Fisher Information (QFI), sensitivity and degradation indicators derived from QFI, and the ultimate precision bound $\Delta^2\Phi_{ ext{opt},t_{ ext{opt}}}$ for phase estimation.

Important parameters / regimes. Noise strength ($\gamma$), interrogation time ($t$ or $T$), system size ($J$), and squeezing parameter ($\sigma$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on a perturbative treatment valid for weak noise and short interrogation times, and specific approximations are used for the continuum limit of certain state superpositions.

Figures summary. Figures compare error-propagation and quantum Cramér–Rao bounds for spin-squeezed states, and compare classical and quantum Cramér–Rao bounds for GHZ-like states.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the QFI dynamics for various Gaussian Spin States (GSS) superpositions (SCS, Dicke, spin-squeezed, GHZ-like) by deriving perturbative expressions for the QFI, identifying the sensitivity/degradation trade-off, and comparing these bounds to measurement-specific limits.

Abstract

Quantum metrology faces major challenges in noisy environments, where decoherence rapidly degrades useful quantum resources. We investigate the dynamics of the precision limits given by the quantum Fisher information (QFI) for phase estimation under spatially correlated dephasing. We characterize the dynamics of the QFI by the sensitivity and degradation indicators that can be obtained as analytical expressions derived using perturbative theory treatment. These short-time and weak-noise formulas yield analytic insight into how collective-spin moments govern both (i) the noiseless sensitivity and (ii) the leading noise-induced degradation of metrological usefulness. We identify a trade-off that is intrinsic to our commuting encoding-noise structure. We analyze the QFI dynamics for the Gaussian spin state (GSS) superpositions, encompassing spin coherent state (SCS), Dicke state superpositions, spin-squeezed states, and GHZ-like states. Predictions from indicators of the QFI dynamics are compared to both the quantum Cramér--Rao bound and the measurement-specific sensitivity bounds for an optimal parameter and interrogation time under a finite total time resource. When possible, we analytically derive the measurement-specific sensitivity bounds for spin-projection and parity-based measurements.

Distinct Modes of Quantum Information Transfer in Power-Law Long-Range Spin Networks

Summary. This work analyzes how the range of interactions in a spin chain dictates quantum information transfer dynamics. By varying the power-law decay exponent $\alpha$, the authors distinguish between standard wavepacket spreading and novel, faster transfer modes arising from spectral localization. This has implications for designing robust quantum communication channels.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a theoretical framework for designing quantum channels by tuning interaction decay rates, showing how spectral properties dictate the speed and mechanism of quantum information transport.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the distinct mechanisms governing quantum state transfer fidelity in spin networks whose interactions decay according to a power law.

Main result. Long-range interactions (low power-law exponent $\alpha$) fundamentally change the transfer dynamics, leading to emergent, faster oscillations confined to a few high-energy eigenmodes, contrasting with the ballistic spread seen in short-range systems.

Method. The study uses theoretical analysis of the XX Hamiltonian, calculating quantum state transfer fidelity via single-particle transition amplitudes derived from the system's spectrum.

Model / system. The model is a spin-1/2 chain governed by an XX Hamiltonian with long-range couplings decaying as $J_{i,j} \propto r_{\alpha}^{i,j}$. The coupling range is controlled by the power-law exponent $\alpha$.

Key observables. Average fidelity $\langle F(t) \rangle$, arrival time $\tau$, and the spectral distribution of the initial state.

Important parameters / regimes. The power-law exponent $\alpha$ (controlling interaction range), chain length $N$, and the coupling strength $J_0$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis focuses on the transfer of an arbitrary single-qubit quantum state injected at one end of the chain.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the dependence of transfer time ($\tau$) on chain length ($N$) and $\alpha$, comparing few-mode oscillations ($\alpha \le 1.5$) to wavepacket propagation ($\alpha \ge 2.0$).

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the dynamics across different $\alpha$ regimes, contrasting short-range (ballistic) vs. long-range (few-mode coherent) transfer, and details the optimization methods used to maintain high fidelity.

Abstract

We identify different regimes of quantum state transfer in long-range coupled spin-$\frac{1}{2}$ systems, where naturally occurring power-law interactions enable rapid, high-fidelity transfer with minimal engineering. Across a broad range of interaction profiles, from effectively nearest-neighbour coupling to Coulomb interactions, we show how long-range connectivity fundamentally reshapes the mechanisms underlying information propagation within such systems. For effectively short-range interactions, transfer follows familiar ballistic transfer dynamics: an initially localised excitation spreads across many eigenmodes concentrated within the approximately linear region of the spectrum, enabling robust wavepacket motion. In contrast, increasing long-distance interactions via lowering the power-law exponent $α$ ($α=1-2$) drives a striking transformation, where the initial state becomes confined to progressively fewer eigenmodes, ultimately reducing the dynamics to the coherent participation of only a few states corresponding to the highest eigenenergies. This spectral localization gives rise to emergent long-range oscillations between distant sites, revealing a distinct -- and faster -- transfer mechanism arising from the intrinsic structure of long-range quantum interactions rather than from full-system engineering pathways.

The exact price of local realism in CHSH experiments: a measurement-dependence-detection trade-off surface, a moiré phase-locking mechanism that saturates it, and an unmeasured fringe in the fourfold coincidence sum

Authors: Aaron Alai

Summary. This theoretical paper quantifies the 'price'—the required measurement dependence—that local hidden-variable theories must pay to reproduce experimental CHSH violation values given detector inefficiencies. It derives a precise trade-off surface and predicts a specific, unmeasured $\cos 4(a-b)$ fringe in the fourfold coincidence sum, offering a new, falsifiable diagnostic tool beyond standard Bell inequality tests.

Why it may be interesting. Detailed structure

Main result. A precise formula for the minimal measurement dependence $M(S, \eta_{ ext{eff}})$ is derived, and the analysis identifies a residual $\cos 4(a-b)$ fringe in the fourfold coincidence sum as a falsifiable fingerprint of local realism violations.

Method. The analysis uses linear programming over local strategies and involves solving functional equations related to detection profiles, alongside Fourier analysis of coincidence rates.

Model / system. The study analyzes Bell tests using a polarization singlet state in CHSH experiments, modeling the constraints imposed by local hidden variables (LHV) on measured coincidence rates.

Key observables. CHSH value ($S$), Measurement dependence ($M$), Fourfold coincidence sum modulation ($\cos 4(a-b)$ fringe), and detection efficiency ($\eta_{ ext{eff}}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The quantum point ($S=2\sqrt{2}$), the effective detector efficiency ($\eta_{ ext{eff}}$), and the measurement dependence measure $M$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the LHV accounts are 'faithful' (reproducing singles and coincidence rates) and investigates the implications of assuming angle-independent coincidence rates.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the price surface $M(S, \eta_{ ext{eff}})$, comparing the derived formula against known bounds, and show the residual correlation pattern in the fourfold coincidence sum.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the minimal measurement dependence price surface via linear programming. It then proves impossibility results for pure-detection models and introduces the moiré phase-locking mechanism as a saturating local account. Finally, it analyzes the residual fringe structure and presents computational verification of the trade-off.

Abstract

For local hidden-variable accounts of the CHSH experiment that are faithful -- reproducing the observed singles and coincidence rates and unbiased marginals of a polarization singlet with symmetric detector efficiency $η_{\rm eff}$ -- I determine the minimal measurement dependence $M$ (Hall's variational measure) needed to achieve a CHSH value $S$. Linear programming over all local strategies yields, to machine precision at 32 grid points, $M(S,η_{\rm eff})=\max\{0,η_{\rm eff}((S+2)η_{\rm eff}-4)/6\}$, whose edges reproduce Hall's tight bound at $η_{\rm eff}=1$, the Garg-Mermin detection threshold, and the postselection ceiling $S=4/η_{\rm eff}-2$. The quantum point is certified exactly: $M(2\sqrt{2},9/10)=(27\sqrt{2}-33)/100$, with primal and dual certificates in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ arithmetic. I solve the unique detection profile $D(m)=\sqrt{m}\,h(m)$ under which a deterministic sign model reproduces the singlet exactly, derive the $\sqrt{m}$ edge law, and prove exact quantum correlations and angle-independent coincidence rates jointly impossible for any pure-detection model of this class. Surviving local accounts trade off measurement dependence against a $\cos 4(a-b)$ modulation of the fourfold coincidence sum, of relative amplitude up to 12.4%, which vanishes identically at the CHSH angles and appears never to have been bounded below 1%. A settings-torus protocol reaches $5σ$ sensitivity at 0.1% within hours: a flat result forces $M\gtrsim 95\%$ of Hall's floor; a fringe would contradict the flat-rate prediction of quantum mechanics. Finally I exhibit a local mechanism -- moire phase locking -- with deterministic phase evolution and all randomness quenched in frozen lattice offsets and flight times, which attains the certified floor exactly at $η_{\rm eff}=1$ (10% uniform fidelity); its softening of the correlation extremes is a falsifiable fingerprint.

High-field fate of the Kitaev quantum spin liquid in $α$-RuCl$_3$

Summary. This work investigates how the Kitaev quantum spin liquid state in $\alpha$-RuCl$_3$ behaves under very strong magnetic fields. This reveals a complex, nonperturbative high-field phase where the underlying fractionalized excitations remain active.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is to determine the fate of the Kitaev quantum spin liquid (KQSL) state in $\alpha$-RuCl$_3$ when subjected to strong external magnetic fields, especially beyond the regime where simple perturbative theories apply.

Main result. KQSL signatures persist well beyond the perturbative window, indicating a nonperturbative regime where Majorana and vison energy scales merge, contrasting sharply with predictions for a simple spin-polarized state.

Method. The study relies on high-field specific heat measurements ($C/T$) and in-plane magnetization measurements, analyzing the field and angle dependence of thermodynamic quantities.

Model / system. The system is the layered honeycomb magnet $\alpha$-RuCl$_3$, which is investigated as a realization of the Kitaev quantum spin liquid (KQSL) model, featuring itinerant Majorana quasiparticles and gapped $Z_2$ fluxes (visons).

Abstract

Kitaev quantum spin liquids (KQSLs) host fractionalized excitations described by itinerant Majorana quasiparticles and gapped $Z_2$ fluxes (visons), providing a platform for emergent topological matter. Whether such a state survives under strong magnetic fields, however, remains an open question. The layered honeycomb magnet $α$-RuCl$_3$ is a leading candidate material: an in-plane field of $\sim$ 7 T suppresses antiferromagnetic order and induces a quantum-disordered phase exhibiting signatures consistent with Majorana excitations, including an anomalous thermal Hall effect and field-angle-dependent specific heat. At higher fields, the magnetization approaches saturation, suggesting a transition to a spin-polarized state, yet the microscopic evolution between these limits remains unresolved. Here we report high-field specific heat measurements up to 24 T that reveal a distinct crossover at $μ_0H^*\approx$15 T, beyond which the perturbative Kitaev description breaks down. Above $H^*$, the characteristic six-fold angular modulation of the specific heat collapses and the excitation gap deviates from the predicted $H^3$ scaling. Meanwhile, the gap decreases with increasing field and the in-plane magnetization anisotropy persists up to $\sim$ 24 T, both in sharp contrast to a trivial spin-polarized state, indicating that KQSL signatures are preserved even at $\sim$ 90 % of magnetization saturation. These results reveal that the KQSL in $α$-RuCl$_3$ extends well beyond the perturbative window, persisting as a nonperturbative regime in which the Majorana and vison energy scales merge, before eventually giving way to spin polarization. This thermodynamic roadmap provides a basis for understanding how fractionalized phases evolve under strong magnetic fields.

Abstract

Non-local search-to-decision asks whether two noncommunicating parties, given the two shares of a bipartite encoding of a uniformly random string $x\in \mathbb{F}_2^n$, can both predict the same random parity $\langle r,x\rangle$ without there also being local measurements with which both parties recover $x$. We show that entanglement-generation speed across a fixed network interface is governed by two distinct resources: the entangling capacity of the interface itself and the ability of the surrounding architecture to replenish it with fresh degrees of freedom. For fermionic Gaussian dynamics, we derive the coefficient-sharp bound $\sum_k|\dotθ_k|\leq\frac12\|K_{AB}\|_*$ on the collective speed of the canonical entanglement angles. Explicit Ising-chain rematching trajectories saturate this bound, thereby certifying exact minimum interaction times under the stated control model. Beyond the Gaussian setting, exhaustive optimization of the complete $N=8$ tree--tree family shows that, at fixed interface capacity, first-layer entanglement, connectedness, and edge budget, the saturation depth is exactly classified by rooted architecture. With higher-resolution $x$-only control, variational entanglement-enhancing-field (VEEF) optimization reaches the numerically resolved fast-$X$ optimum in a two-channel benchmark. Across all 21 symmetry-reduced rooted orbits, a pre-specified two-time VEEF growth diagnostic recovers the complete replenishment partition directly from optimized dynamics. Interface capacity therefore sets how much entangling flux is available, whereas architecture determines whether fresh degrees of freedom can continually replenish the interface and sustain repeated use of that capacity.

Abstract

Abstract

This paper extends a recently proposed Variational Quantum Algorithm framework for nonlinear elasticity to a broader class of constitutive nonlinearities involving rational powers of the stretch. One-dimensional incompressible Ogden and Mooney-Rivlin models are employed as representative examples to demonstrate the proposed methodology. Nonlinear constitutive terms are transformed into forms compatible with the available quantum algorithmic primitives through the introduction of auxiliary variables and penalty constraints, yielding approximate solutions via a Variational Quantum Algorithm. An iterative correction strategy based on a sequence of Variational Quantum Algorithms is then introduced to improve solution accuracy. A Numerical example demonstrates the proposed approach.
