Quantum channels on duals of von Neumann algebras in the Schrödinger picture

Summary. This paper develops a general theory for quantum channels acting on the state spaces associated with von Neumann algebras in the Schrödinger picture. It achieves this by constructing these channels as Pettis integrals involving unitary representations over topological groups. This mathematical generalization allows the framework to handle complex, infinite-dimensional open quantum systems beyond traditional finite-dimensional models.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous mathematical framework for describing open quantum system dynamics in infinite dimensions by generalizing standard channel definitions to the dual space, which is crucial for modern theoretical treatments of continuous systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper extends the study of quantum channels from finite-dimensional state spaces or the Heisenberg picture to the duals of general von Neumann algebras in the Schrödinger picture.

Main result. Quantum channels can be systematically constructed as completely positive maps on $M^*$ via Pettis integrals involving representations of topological groups, and these constructions preserve semigroup properties under convolution semigroups of measures.

Method. The core method involves defining quantum channels $\Phi: M^* o M^*$ using the integral form $\int_G \pi(g) ho\pi(g)^* d\mu(g)$, which generalizes unitary averaging techniques to infinite dimensions.

Model / system. The model concerns open quantum systems whose dynamics are described by completely positive maps (quantum channels) acting on the state space $\Sigma(M)$ associated with an infinite-dimensional von Neumann algebra $M \subset B(H)$.

Key observables. Quantum states $ ho \in \Sigma(M)$, observables $A \in M$, and the action of the channel $\Phi$ on these elements.

Important parameters / regimes. The structure of the underlying topological group $G$ and the measure $\mu$ used in the Pettis integral construction are critical parameters.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that the dynamics can be modeled by averaging unitaries derived from representations $\pi: G o U(H)$; a key limitation noted is the potential failure of SOT-continuity for such representations.

Paper structure. The paper builds upon known results for finite systems, introduces the general framework on $M^*$, proves that unitary evolution and Pettis integral constructions yield valid channels (Theorems 3.1 & 3.2), and then explores semigroup properties using convolution semigroups of measures.

Abstract

The theory of quantum channels is traditionally studied either on finite-dimensional state spaces or within the Heisenberg picture as completely positive maps on C^*-algebras. In this paper, we consider quantum channels as completely positive maps on the duals of general von Neumann algebras in the Schrodinger picture. We investigate the construction of such channels through Pettis integrals using representations of topological groups.

The preferred-time problem in the conditional interpretation of time

Summary. This paper tackles the 'preferred-time problem' in timeless quantum formulations like the Page-Wootters proposal. It argues that assuming a separate clock system leads to ambiguity because infinitely many valid time operators exist for the entangled state. The resolution is that time must be defined intrinsically by the world's own observables, making the explicit separation of a 'clock' subsystem unnecessary.

Why it may be interesting. Although focused on foundational quantum gravity/time, the concept of emergent dynamics from internal degrees of freedom is highly relevant to open quantum systems theory, particularly in defining effective Hamiltonians or decoherence mechanisms where time parameters are not externally imposed.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the 'preferred-time problem' within the Page-Wootters (PW) formalism, which arises from the ambiguity in selecting a unique time operator when deriving dynamics from timeless quantum states.

Main result. The correct interpretation requires that time emerges from intrinsic pointer observables of the world itself, rendering the explicit separation into a separate clock subsystem redundant for defining physical time evolution.

Method. The analysis involves comparing mathematically valid decompositions of entangled states against physically necessary constraints, particularly by identifying which observable induces the temporal structure.

Model / system. The model is quantum theory formulated in a timeless manner, using the PW proposal where the total system is treated as an entanglement between a clock subsystem ($c$) and the world subsystem ($r$).

Key observables. Clock observable ($T_c$), global time observable ($\hat{b}T_r$), and the state decomposition $|\Psi angle$.

Important parameters / regimes. The structure of the Hamiltonian $H = H_c \otimes I_r + I_c \otimes H_r$ is central, as are the continuous vs. discrete nature of time spectra.

Assumptions / limitations. The paper critiques two key assumptions of the standard PW interpretation: (1) that dynamics emerge solely from clock-world entanglement, and (2) that the world state is passively conditioned by a pre-selected clock basis.

Paper structure. The structure moves from defining the problem (PW formalism leading to ambiguity) to analyzing the mathematical source of the ambiguity (infinite time operators) and finally proposing a resolution where intrinsic world observables dictate the temporal structure, thus invalidating the need for an external clock.

Abstract

In timeless formulations of quantum theory, the Page-Wootters proposal (PW) recovers time and dynamics as relative clock-and-world states. By interpreting the total timeless system as a clock entangled with the world, the states having a definite clock reading appear to recover the temporal states of the world as relative states, and dynamics emerges. But an entangled state admits infinitely many decompositions as a superposition of product states, each decomposition corresponding to a different but equally valid time operator of the same clock system. Different valid choices of the time operator for the same clock system lead to different physical histories, where the world states in a history are superpositions of the world states from another history. Fixing one operator as "the" clock time would import the temporal meaning that the Page-Wootters proposal is meant to explain in the first place. Therefore, the interpretation of PW that the world state is passively conditioned on the clock state cannot hold. The timeless state is resolved into time-dependent states by the intrinsic pointer observables of the world, without having to outsource the role of the time operator to a separate clock. Fortunately, the formalism of PW remains valid, provided that it is interpreted in terms of intrinsic pointer observables of the world itself.

Summary. This work analyzes a theoretical thermal machine powered by the probabilistic discrimination of non-orthogonal quantum states. It maps out the operational boundaries in the parameter space defined by the Carnot efficiency and state overlap, revealing distinct phase transitions. The findings establish how quantum information constraints fundamentally limit the maximum extractable work from heat gradients.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a rigorous framework connecting non-equilibrium thermodynamics (heat engines) directly with fundamental principles of quantum information theory (state distinguishability), which is highly relevant for understanding energy extraction in quantum devices.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the fundamental limits on energy conversion when a thermal machine utilizes probabilistic discrimination of non-orthogonal quantum states.

Main result. The system exhibits phase-transition-like switching in its performance region within the Carnot efficiency ($\eta_C$) and state overlap ($\mu$) plane, revealing anomalous reentrant transitions under weak driving.

Method. The analysis uses a six-step thermodynamic cycle model to derive efficiencies by jointly considering quantum information bounds (Holevo-Helstrom) and classical thermodynamics constraints.

Model / system. A two-reservoir quantum machine modeled using an ideal gas with internal quantum degrees of freedom, operating between hot ($T_h$) and cold ($T_c$) reservoirs.

Key observables. Efficiency ($\eta$), Net Work ($W_{net}$), State Overlap ($\mu$), Discrimination Accuracy ($\delta$).

Important parameters / regimes. Carnot Efficiency ($\eta_C = 1 - T_c/T_h$); critical thresholds like $\eta_C \ge 0.5$ and $\eta_C \lesssim 0.13$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes reversible operation for the joint measurement-resetting process.

Figures summary. Figure 1(b) plots the required operational region for discrimination accuracy ($\delta$) between the thermodynamic lower bound ($\delta_{lhc}$) and the quantum indistinguishability limit ($\delta_{QI}$). Figure 3 maps a phase diagram showing three distinct operating phases in the $(\eta_C, \mu)$ space.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the physical model, derives efficiency formulas incorporating entropy terms, determines the operational constraints by comparing quantum and thermodynamic bounds, and finally maps out the resulting phase diagram to characterize functional switching.

Abstract

While the impossibility of perfectly identifying non-orthogonal states is a cornerstone of quantum information science, their probabilistic discrimination is nonetheless permissible. Here, we propose a two-reservoir quantum machine driven by this mechanism to map its functional boundaries across the parameter space of the state overlap $μ$ and the Carnot efficiency $η_C$. Within this $η_C$-$μ$ plane, the machine exhibits phase-transition-like functional switching among a pure heat-engine phase, a mixed phase, and a dissipative phase. We identify critical thresholds governing these transitions: strong thermal driving ($η_C \ge 0.5$) unconditionally guarantees positive work extraction, whereas weak driving ($η_C \lesssim 0.13$) induces an anomalous reentrant transition, where increasing $μ$ unexpectedly restores engine functionality after a purely dissipative regime. Our results explicitly demonstrate how quantum mechanics and thermodynamics jointly constrain information-to-energy conversion.

Summary. This paper extends stochastic quantization by incorporating jump-diffusion processes, allowing simulations to overcome topological barriers that trap standard diffusion methods. By developing rigorous mathematical constructions for the jump probabilities, the authors demonstrate a powerful tool to efficiently sample complex action landscapes in lattice field theories.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical development of ensuring detailed balance for non-local jumps is highly relevant to any simulation technique relying on Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC), including those used in quantum field theory or complex many-body systems where barriers are present.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To construct a natural generalization of stochastic quantization by incorporating jump-diffusion processes to efficiently explore action landscapes with barriers.

Main result. The framework successfully generalizes stochastic quantization by providing concrete, detailed balance preserving methods (additive proposals, invertible maps) for designing the necessary jump probabilities.

Method. The authors derive and apply a generalized master equation for Poisson jump-diffusion processes, ensuring convergence to the desired equilibrium distribution $P(\phi) \propto e^{-S(\phi)}$ via specific construction strategies for the jump kernel.

Model / system. The theory is applied to Euclidean Lattice Field Theories, using 2D U(1) gauge theory as a benchmark problem to address topological freezing. The dynamics are governed by a generalized Fokker-Planck master equation incorporating both diffusion and non-continuous jumps.

Key observables. Topological charge dynamics histogram, autocorrelation time reduction, and the acceptance rate of the jump process.

Important parameters / regimes. The jump rate ($\lambda_0$), the size/extent of the update ($L_w$), and the parameters defining the proposal distributions (e.g., $\sigma$ for Gaussian approximations).

Assumptions / limitations. Boundary terms are neglected in deriving the Fokker-Planck equation; certain constructions rely on assuming measure-preserving maps whose inverses are also available.

Figures summary. Figure 4 compares topological charge dynamics histograms, showing that state-informed jumps (red bars) restore efficient transitions between topological sectors compared to uninformed updates (blue bars).

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the need for jump-diffusion processes over standard diffusion. It then derives the generalized master equation and presents three construction strategies (A, B, C) to enforce detailed balance in the jump term. Finally, it applies this framework to lattice gauge theory simulations, demonstrating improved sampling efficiency.

Abstract

We construct the natural generalization of stochastic quantization (in the Markovian sense) by considering jump-diffusion processes. This class of stochastic processes exhibits non-continuous paths, so-called Lévy flights. In the presence of jumps, action landscapes with barriers can be efficiently explored, improving and even restoring ergodicity where traditional diffusion approaches become inefficient. We explore different strategies for constructing efficient jump updates, which we deploy to address the benchmark problem of topological freezing in 2d U(1) gauge theory.

Summary. This theoretical work uses Renormalization Group techniques to map out which pairing instability dominates in a nuclear Fermi liquid under varying conditions. By analyzing the pole scale of competing channels, it shows that both the geometry of the Fermi surface and the asymmetry between neutron and proton populations dictate the superconducting state formed.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on nuclear physics, the methodology—using RG flow to determine the instability order among competing channels based on geometry and composition—is highly relevant to understanding phase transitions in strongly interacting quantum systems like ultracold Fermi gases (e.g., superfluidity onset).

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study determines which competing pairing instability develops first in a nuclear Fermi liquid, considering various spin, isospin, and orbital channels.

Main result. The dominant pairing channel is determined by the pole scale of the Renormalization Group (RG) flow; this selection depends critically on the Fermi surface geometry and the degree of neutron-proton asymmetry.

Method. A Fermi-surface Renormalization Group (RG) treatment is employed, using one-loop corrections to determine the running couplings and the divergence scale ($\Lambda^*$) for different pairing channels.

Model / system. The system modeled is a nuclear Fermi liquid, analyzed using a minimal S/P-wave interaction model. The analysis extends to include effects of axial deformation and neutron-proton momentum splitting.

Key observables. Pole scale ($\Lambda^*_A$) of the RG flow; competition boundary parameter $r_P$; running coupling constants for different channels (nn, pp, np).

Important parameters / regimes. Isospin asymmetry $\delta = (k_{F,n} - k_{F,p}) / k_{F0}$; deformation parameter $eta_2$; initial coupling strengths ($g_A(\Lambda_0)$).

Assumptions / limitations. The calculations are controlled one-loop RG illustrations and not quantitative nuclear matter predictions; the analysis uses minimal S/P-wave truncations.

Figures summary. Figure 1 shows the spherical channel selection diagram based on $r_P$. Figure 2 illustrates competition on an axially deformed surface. Figure 3 maps the dominant pairing channel in the $(\delta, r_P)$ plane for six components.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the RG flow by first establishing the spherical benchmark (S vs P wave), then incorporating geometric effects via axial deformation ($eta_2$), and finally analyzing the impact of isospin asymmetry ($\delta$) which introduces finite threshold scales for $np$ pairing.

Abstract

A nuclear Fermi liquid exhibits competing pairing instabilities in different spin, isospin, and orbital channels. In a Fermi-surface renormalization group (RG) treatment, the channel that develops a pole first is determined not only by its tree-level attraction but also by its one-loop RG coefficient. We illustrate this mechanism in a minimal $S/P$-wave model. A spherical Fermi surface establishes the reference competition between the lowest even- and odd-parity interactions. Axial deformation changes the relevant Fermi-surface integrals and lifts the degeneracy between longitudinal and transverse $P$-wave components. In isospin-asymmetric matter, neutron--proton Fermi-momentum splitting restricts the simultaneous low-energy contribution of the two species and can terminate the $np$ running at finite threshold scales. Our calculations are intended as controlled one-loop RG illustrations rather than as quantitative nuclear-matter calculations. We show how the Fermi-surface geometry and composition can change the ordering of competing pairing instabilities.

Dynamical spectral functions from bitstring-sampled quantum subspaces: entanglement, not one-body magic, tracks the sampling cost

Summary. This work develops a method to calculate dynamic properties, such as spectral functions, in strongly correlated electron systems using limited quantum resources. By sampling subspaces via simple measurements, the authors show that the computational cost is dictated by entanglement structure ($\chi$), not by easily measurable one-body quantities. This advances the field by providing a resource-aware roadmap for simulating complex electronic dynamics on current hardware.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a concrete pathway for simulating complex many-body dynamics (like spectral functions) on near-term quantum hardware by identifying the true scaling bottleneck (entanglement) rather than relying on simplified local observables.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To extend quantum subspace diagonalization from static ground-state energies to continuous, frequency-resolved dynamics for electronic structure calculations.

Main result. The sampling cost is governed by the entanglement (minimal bond dimension $\chi$), not by one-body invariants like Fermionic AntiFlatness ($F_1$).

Method. Reconstruct spectral functions and dynamical structure factors classically from sampled subspaces using only computational-basis measurements on a quantum processor.

Model / system. The methods are applied to electronic structure problems, validated against Hubbard chains and molecular systems, focusing on charged and neutral sectors of many-electron Hamiltonians.

Key observables. Single-particle spectral functions $A(\omega)$, momentum-resolved $A(k, \omega)$, and dynamical structure factors $S(q, \omega)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The resource cost is tracked by the determinant support size $|\mathcal{S}|$ and lower-bounded by entanglement ($\chi$).

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes moment exactness can be achieved with a sampling bound polynomial in $|\mathcal{S}|$, independent of Hilbert-space dimension.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the methodology by moving the target from ground state to dynamics, details the reconstruction process using shallow measurements, and then analyzes the resource cost, contrasting one-body diagnostics with entanglement measures.

Abstract

Sample-based quantum diagonalization (SQD) and quantum-selected configuration interaction (QSCI) are the electronic-structure methods with most hardware traction, yet their canonical target -- the ground-state energy -- is where classical methods have caught up. We move the target to dynamics and the resource question. From one bitstring-sampling primitive -- computational-basis measurements of a shallow real-time circuit, with no Hadamard or controlled unitaries -- we reconstruct, from sampled subspaces, the single-particle spectral functions $A(ω)$ and $A(k,ω)$ and the neutral-sector dynamical structure factors $S(q,ω)$ and $S^{zz}(q,ω)$, each built classically in the Lehmann representation from its own subspace. The reconstruction matches exact diagonalization on Hubbard chains and, for $A(ω)$, across nineteen molecules (FCI-verified to $<10^{-5}$ Ha), and runs on the IBM Heron processor. Second, we ask which resource controls the cost -- the determinant support $|\mathcal{S}|$ the sampler must populate. On number-conserving states the fermionic AntiFlatness collapses to one 1-RDM invariant, $\mathcal{F}_1 = 4\,\mathrm{tr}[γ(1-γ)] = 2N_u$. An orbital-rotation (Gaussian) invariant while $|\mathcal{S}|$ is basis dependent, $\mathcal{F}_1$ is provably decoupled from the cost; the cost is instead lower-bounded and tracked by the entanglement -- the minimal bond dimension $χ$ (Spearman $ρ= 0.90$). One-body magic is thus a faithful multireference diagnostic but an unreliable cost predictor; any genuine advantage lives in the non-Gaussianity of the higher-body cumulants. We prove moment exactness and a sampling bound polynomial in $|\mathcal{S}|$, independent of Hilbert-space dimension. Self-consistent configuration recovery improves the subspace under device noise, while a learned generative model does not beat that classical baseline.

Temperature encoding rates of two-level probes in de Sitter spacetime

Authors: Yao Jin
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.15607v1

Summary. This theoretical paper uses quantum metrology to determine the precision limit for estimating temperature parameters encoded in two-level atoms placed in de Sitter spacetime. It finds that the total measurable temperature rate is additive from intrinsic and acceleration-induced (Unruh) sources, and crucially, it identifies an optimal inherent acceleration that maximizes the measurement of the background temperature. The results suggest these measurements remain feasible even far from the cosmological horizon.

Why it may be interesting. This work bridges quantum information theory (metrology) with fundamental physics in curved spacetime (de Sitter space), providing concrete predictions about how thermal effects can be measured using quantum probes, which is highly relevant to open quantum systems research.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study quantifies the rate at which temperature parameters (Gibbons-Hawking and Unruh) are encoded in two-level quantum probes interacting with a fluctuating scalar field in de Sitter spacetime.

Main result. The total encoding rate is additive, and non-zero inherent acceleration does not suppress intrinsic temperature encoding; instead, an optimal acceleration maximizes this rate.

Method. Quantum metrology principles are applied by optimizing the initial state, evolution time, and measurement basis to maximize the estimation rate of temperature parameters.

Model / system. The system involves a two-level atom (probe) coupled to a massless scalar field in de Sitter spacetime. The dynamics are governed by an open quantum system approach using a Lindblad master equation.

Key observables. Temperature encoding rate ($R$), effective temperature ($T_{ ext{eff}}$), intrinsic Gibbons–Hawking temperature ($T_{GH}$), and position-dependent Unruh temperature ($T_U$).

Important parameters / regimes. Separation distance from the horizon, inherent acceleration ($a$), total probe time ($T$), and initial state parameters ($ heta, \phi$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on a weak coupling assumption for the evolution of the reduced density matrix.

Figures summary. Not specified in the notes; the text describes mathematical relationships between encoding rates and dependencies on acceleration/distance.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the quantum-metrological framework, deriving the effective temperature as a sum of intrinsic and Unruh components. It then calculates the encoding rate by optimizing parameters, revealing the non-monotonic dependence of $R_{X_\alpha}$ on $T_U$, and finally assessing the feasibility of measurements at large separations.

Abstract

We investigate the temperature encoding process of two-level probes in de Sitter spacetime within the framework of open quantum systems. From a quantum-metrological perspective, we quantify the temperature encoding rate, which characterizes the precision of estimating a temperature parameter with a fixed total probe time. The maximal encoding rate is obtained by optimizing the initial state, evolution time, and measurement basis of each probe. For a static probe separated by a finite distance from a freely falling observer, both the intrinsic Gibbons--Hawking temperature and the position-dependent Unruh temperature are encoded in the probe state. We find that the encoding rate of the effective temperature is equal to the sum of the encoding rates of the intrinsic temperature and the Unruh temperature associated with the probe's inherent acceleration. Interestingly, when the intrinsic temperature is sufficiently small, the encoding rate of the intrinsic temperature initially increases with the Unruh temperature, reaches a maximum, and then decreases to zero. Thus, a nonzero inherent acceleration does not necessarily suppress the encoding of the intrinsic temperature. Instead, there exists an optimal inherent acceleration at which its encoding rate is maximized. Furthermore, we investigate the encoding rate of the Unruh temperature and the minimum total probe time required to estimate it with sufficient precision as functions of the separation distance. We find that the required total probe time and the corresponding number of probes remain experimentally feasible even when the probes are located relatively far from the cosmological horizon, with a radial position as large as $0.1$ times the horizon radius.

Although this FDR profile was previously demonstrated in BBO only in the nondegenerate regime, our results establish its generality: the FDR dip is also present at exact degeneracy in QPM crystals, an unexpected feature that was previously thought to deliver a resolution advantage in the nondegenerate regime alone. Our treatment applies to all SPDC-based quantum-imaging regimes (including CPM and QPM crystals, walk-off and non-walk-off axes, degenerate and nondegenerate emission, and signal and idler filtering) and offers enhanced quantum-imaging resolution via the design rules presented-most prominently along the walk-off axis of CPM crystals (in far field) and across all transverse axes in the near field.

Abstract

We study the emergence of altermagnetic topological phases stabilized by Ising spin-orbit coupling in a prototypical Lieb lattice model of a correlated altermagnet. Treating the electron interactions within the Hartree-Fock and the exact diagonalization techniques, we establish the co-existence of a quantum spin Hall effect with altermagnetic spin order over a wide region of the parameter space for average electron densities of 2 and 4 per unit cell. We explore how the magnetic structure along edges influences the electronic behavior of the associated topological edge modes, demonstrating their robustness against inversion-symmetry breaking terms.

Abstract

Electronic position controls how a crystal polarizes and responds to an external field. In crystals, it is usually described through local changes of electronic states in momentum space. This Berry framework has reshaped modern solid-state physics, but strong fields drive electrons across a finite momentum range, where coherence between different momenta becomes part of the response. Here we establish a first-principles theory of electronic position at finite momentum that retains this missing information. We show that unequal-momentum coherence can cancel under spatial averaging and still produce polarization, forming a coherence dipole. We obtain the matrix directly from material wave functions, without model bands or fitted transition elements. In Si, the finite-momentum geometry sets a material momentum scale. Comparing this scale with the momentum change driven by the field predicts when finite-momentum physics becomes active. Crossing the scale strongly reorganizes the fifth and higher harmonics, showing that momentum-space geometry, rather than emitted photon energy alone, controls the nonlinear response. HHG is the first demonstration, but the theory applies whenever driven electrons explore a finite momentum range. It therefore extends quantum geometry beyond the local Berry limit and provides a general basis for predicting field-driven phenomena in real materials.
