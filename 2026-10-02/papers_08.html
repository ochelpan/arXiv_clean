Cycle Codes and Decoded Quantum Interferometry

Authors: Anuj Apte, Shouvanik Chakrabarti, Andi Gu, Stephen P. Jordan, Ojas Parekh, Ruslan Shaydulin, Jacob Watkins, Noureldin Yosri, Adam Zalcman
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.40036v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work analyzes Decoded Quantum Interferometry (DQI) applied to optimization problems modeled by cycle codes. The authors derive rigorous bounds on DQI's achievable performance by studying decoding guarantees for these codes over finite fields. The results constrain the potential quantum advantage by establishing performance limits based on classical decoding capabilities.

Why it may be interesting. While the core topic is coding theory applied to optimization, the rigorous analysis of error thresholds and recovery bounds using advanced mathematical tools (LP, random walks) is highly relevant to understanding the limits of quantum error correction and quantum computation feasibility.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the performance limits of Decoded Quantum Interferometry (DQI) when applied to solving optimization problems constrained by cycle codes.

Main result. The analysis establishes upper and lower bounds on the approximate optima achievable by DQI using classical decoders, ruling out quantum advantage in the analyzed regimes but identifying specific instances where a nontrivial guarantee exists.

Method. The authors derive satisfaction guarantees by analyzing the decoding process for homological cycle codes over finite fields, employing techniques like Linear Programming (LP) formulations and bounding interference terms.

Model / system. The model centers on cycle codes, which reduce optimization problems like MaxCut to decoding problems. The analysis is performed over arbitrary finite fields $\mathbb{F}_q$ and utilizes the Linial–Simkin ensemble of regular graphs.

Key observables. Satisfaction fraction guarantee, reliability threshold ($\delta_{\text{rel}}$), and the minimum-weight decoding complexity.

Important parameters / regimes. Field size $q$, graph degree $D$, error probability $p$, and the girth of the underlying graph.

Assumptions / limitations. The initial 'no-go' results relied on minimum distance assumptions that underestimate true DQI performance; the analysis also assumes deterministic syndrome-consistent mappings.

Figures summary. The notes mention Table 1 comparing binary vs. non-binary cycle codes, and Table 2 comparing algorithmic performance metrics for different decoding techniques.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the problem context (DQI and cycle codes), then deriving general satisfaction guarantees under imperfect decoding, followed by detailed analysis of ML decoding bounds using Fourier transforms, and concluding with LP formulations and trellis constructions to prove recovery guarantees.

Abstract

Decoded Quantum Interferometry (DQI) reduces optimization problems with two-variable constraints to decoding cycle codes. For one such problem, namely MaxCut, prior work showed that DQI achieves a nontrivial satisfaction fraction guarantee only on linear-girth graphs, for which MaxCut is classically easy. However, these no-go results rely on minimum distance assumptions that do not represent true decodability thresholds for common noise channels, thus underestimating actual DQI performance. To estimate the true performance, we derive DQI satisfaction guarantees in the presence of imperfect decoding for fixed instances and generalize prior results for random instances. We then study homological cycle codes over arbitrary finite fields, and show that minimum-weight decoding is NP-hard for every field of size $q>2$, in contrast with known results for binary cycle codes. On the Linial--Simkin ensemble of regular graphs with logarithmic girth, we prove maximum-likelihood recovery bounds and a strong converse for a family of non-uniform additive noise channels. Moreover, LP decoders provide polynomial-time recovery of a positive fraction of randomly located errors with arbitrary values. These results allow us to prove upper and lower bounds on the approximate optima achievable by DQI when restricted to classical decoders. These bounds rule out quantum advantage in the regimes we analyze but also identify a family of regular Max-$k$-Cut instances on which DQI efficiently achieves a nontrivial satisfaction fraction guarantee.

Does the Readout Bypass Leak the Input? A Feature-Visibility Audit of Hybrid Quantum-Classical Models

Authors: Guilin Zhang, Kai Zhao, Xiquan Cui, Henry Heng, Xu Chu, Aletta Johanna Blanken

Summary. This work provides a privacy audit for hybrid quantum-classical models, demonstrating that the readout mechanism leaks raw input data through standard linear layer vulnerabilities. The authors show that simply appending quantum features does not guarantee privacy protection against gradient-based reconstruction attacks. This highlights that the leakage stems from the classical readout structure rather than the quantum processing itself.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on ML privacy, the underlying concept of analyzing information leakage through complex, non-linear quantum feature maps relates to understanding the information capacity and robustness of quantum representations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper audits the privacy leakage when raw classical inputs are concatenated with measured quantum features in readout-side residual hybrid Quantum Machine Learning (QML) models.

Main result. The readout bypass mechanism leaks raw input coordinates because the initial linear layer is analytically vulnerable, regardless of the quantum circuit's complexity.

Method. The authors use gradient matching attacks and reconstruction metrics (PSNR, MSE) to quantify how well raw inputs can be recovered from the model's gradients.

Model / system. The model is a readout-side residual hybrid QML architecture where the readout vector $z$ is formed by concatenating raw input $x$ and measured quantum features $Q(x)$.

Key observables. PSNR (Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio) for reconstruction, Membership Inference Attack (MIA) AUC, and reconstruction fidelity across full-vector vs. shared-six coordinates.

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis is limited to specific six-input, six-observable circuits and individual gradient leakage, not aggregation.

Assumptions / limitations. The findings are specific to the tested small datasets and circuit structures, and the leakage is attributed to the linear layer's analytic vulnerability.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the visibility-conditioned audit setup, comparing reconstruction performance across different feature sets and architectures.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the model setup, details the gradient matching attack methodology, presents quantitative results comparing different architectural leakage levels (Residual vs. Input-only), and concludes with a privacy audit framework.

Abstract

Readout-side residual hybrids concatenate raw inputs with measured quantum features. Under single-example gradient sharing, a biased first linear layer admits standard analytic recovery of its input, so the bypass exposes raw coordinates without requiring inversion of the quantum circuit. We audit this mechanism using two tabular datasets, four architectures, and metrics conditioned on feature visibility. Iterative gradient matching gives median full-record PSNR of 73-96 dB for residual and input-only heads. Quantum-only heads score 8-11 dB on the full record but 54-96 dB on the six input coordinates they actually encode. These are reconstruction results for the tested six-input, six-observable circuits, not a general statement about quantum encodings. A loss-threshold membership attack remains near chance. The contribution is a visibility-conditioned privacy audit: omitted coordinates must not be credited as protection supplied by quantum processing, and near-exact PSNR differences must not be interpreted as meaningful privacy rankings. Our findings concern individual gradients and do not establish leakage under aggregation or multiple local training steps.

Fast and Sure-ious Quantum Process Tomography

Authors: A. Afham, Sayantan Sen, Marco Tomamichel
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.39941v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5

Summary. This paper develops a theoretically rigorous and computationally efficient method for Quantum Process Tomography (QPT). It proves that a simple, closed-form partial normalization heuristic is mathematically equivalent to the optimal fidelity projection onto the set of physical quantum channels. This breakthrough allows QPT algorithms to inherit the favorable sample complexity guarantees of state tomography while avoiding the computational bottlenecks of traditional semidefinite programming methods.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a computationally tractable and theoretically sound method for characterizing open quantum systems (channels), which is fundamental for quantum process characterization in quantum computing and quantum optics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of Quantum Process Tomography (QPT), which requires estimating an unknown quantum channel while enforcing the trace-preserving (TP) constraint.

Main result. The authors prove that the standard 'folklore partial normalization' heuristic is mathematically equivalent to the exact projection onto the set of valid Choi states using fidelity, allowing QST algorithms to be directly lifted to QPT with inherited sample complexity.

Method. The core method involves establishing the mathematical equivalence between the partial normalization and the fidelity projection, enabling the use of closed-form, computationally efficient regularization techniques for QPT.

Model / system. The system under study is an unknown quantum channel $\Phi: \mathcal{L}(A) o \mathcal{L}(B)$, which is characterized by its Choi state $C(\Phi)$. The estimation process involves reconstructing this state from measurement data.

Key observables. Fidelity ($F$), Choi Rank, Sample Complexity ($N$), and computational runtime/memory usage.

Important parameters / regimes. The dimension of the systems ($d_A, d_B$), the target infidelity ($\epsilon$), and the required number of channel uses ($N \propto 1/\epsilon$).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that the fidelity projection is the correct regularization method, which is shown to be equivalent to the partial normalization formula.

Figures summary. Figures compare the runtime of various TP regularization methods (Fidelity projection vs. SDP vs. HIP) against Choi dimension, showing the fidelity projection is significantly faster. Other figures compare rank recovery and infidelity vs. shot count.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the theoretical equivalence between partial normalization and fidelity projection. It then applies this result to lift QST protocols to QPT, demonstrating improved performance over existing projection-based methods in terms of speed and accuracy.

Abstract

Quantum process tomography (QPT) protocols are often designed by lifting quantum state tomography (QST) protocols through the Choi-Jamiolkowski isomorphism, at the cost of an extra step enforcing the trace-preserving (TP) constraint. Existing approaches fall into two classes. The folklore partial normalization, which sandwiches the density estimate between inverse square roots of its input marginal, has a closed form but is used as a heuristic, its only analyses being asymptotic and losing a factor polynomial in the dimension. Projection-based methods are principled but have no closed forms and scale poorly with dimension. We resolve this dichotomy by showing that the partial normalization is the exact projection onto the Choi states of quantum channels in fidelity (equivalently in Bures or purified distance). Consequently, any QST algorithm with guarantees in fidelity lifts to a QPT algorithm that inherits its sample complexity, through a single closed-form congruence that preserves the Choi rank and is computed in Kraus form without any eigendecomposition of Choi dimension. We lift two single-copy protocols and prove that the non-adaptive one (Fidelity Projected Least Squares) recovers the Choi rank and reaches infidelity $\varepsilon$ from a number of channel uses proportional to $1/\varepsilon$. Numerically, for channels of low Choi rank, it improves on the state-of-the-art projected least squares in accuracy, and by orders of magnitude in the time and memory of the TP-regularization step and of the returned estimate.

Local Automorphism-Aware Syndrome Compilation for General Quantum LDPC Codes

Authors: Eugenio Durazo Rocha, Olai Å. Mostad, Hsuan-Yin Lin, Eirik Rosnes
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.40319v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces Local Automorphism-Aware Syndrome Compilation (LocalASC) to optimize the circuit depth required for syndrome extraction in quantum LDPC codes. By exploiting the symmetries (automorphisms) of the code's Tanner graph, LocalASC simplifies the complex scheduling constraints. This leads to the discovery of depth-optimal and lower-error-rate syndrome extraction schedules compared to prior state-of-the-art methods.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on quantum error correction, the underlying mathematical machinery involving graph automorphisms, constraint satisfaction, and optimization techniques is highly relevant to designing efficient quantum circuits for any complex quantum system.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of finding low-depth syndrome extraction circuits for general quantum LDPC codes.

Main result. The proposed Local Automorphism-Aware Syndrome Compilation (LocalASC) method successfully finds depth-optimal syndrome-extraction schedules for several tested quantum codes, often outperforming existing compilation techniques.

Method. LocalASC reduces the complex quantum-constrained edge coloring problem to edge-orbit variables by leveraging subgroups of the Tanner graph's automorphisms, simplifying the constraint system.

Model / system. The work analyzes Calderbank-Shor-Steane (CSS) quantum LDPC codes, which are modeled using their associated Tanner graphs, incorporating constraints from X- and Z-check measurements.

Key observables. Minimum two-qubit depth ($\lambda_{ ext{SEC}}^{\star}$), Logical Error Rate (LER), and Circuit Distance ($d_{circ}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The maximum degree of the Tanner graph ($\Delta(G)$) serves as a key lower bound for the required depth.

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes the existence of appropriate automorphism subgroups ($H \leq ext{Aut}(G)$) to simplify the constraint system.

Figures summary. Figures compare the performance (e.g., LER) of LocalASC against other scheduling methods for specific codes like [[48, 4, 8]] and [[72, 12, 6]], demonstrating superior performance.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the problem as a quantum-constrained edge coloring problem, introduces LocalASC by reducing constraints to edge orbits, and then validates its effectiveness by comparing its results against established methods and analyzing specific code families.

Abstract

Low-depth syndrome extraction for Calderbank-Shor-Steane (CSS) quantum low-density parity-check codes can be formulated as a proper ordered edge-coloring problem subject to quantum parity constraints. A proper edge-coloring of the CSS Tanner graph ensures that each data or ancilla qubit participates in at most one two-qubit gate per layer, but does not guarantee a valid interleaving of the X- and Z-check measurements as for every overlapping X/Z check pair, the number of shared data qubits on which the X interaction precedes the Z interaction must be even. The minimum number of colors in a proper ordered edge-coloring satisfying the quantum parity constraints equals the minimum two-qubit depth when each stabilizer check is measured with a single ancilla. We introduce local automorphism-aware syndrome compilation (LocalASC), which reduces the constraint system to edge-orbit variables under a subgroup of the Tanner graph automorphisms and lifts each feasible orbit assignment to the full graph. Although the $6$-layer degree lower bound is unattainable for the published weight-$6$ IBM bivariate bicycle codes, we show that this is not universal among two-block CSS codes. Among code instances for which the maximum check weight equals the maximum Tanner graph degree, LocalASC finds depth-optimal syndrome-extraction schedules for several two-block CSS codes with odd component weights, including instances with unequal odd weights. We also obtain lower-bound-saturating syndrome-extraction schedules for several quantum Tanner codes satisfying the same degree condition. To obtain the subgroups used by LocalASC without computing the full automorphism group of the Tanner graph, we construct translation subgroups for two-block group-algebra CSS codes over abelian groups. For quantum Tanner codes, we give conditions under which square-complex symmetries extend to Tanner graph automorphisms.

The authors derive a sharper bound on the state deviation, which translates directly into a higher certified secret key rate from the same experimental data. This work highlights how precise mathematical analysis can boost the practical utility of quantum communication systems.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on QKD, the rigorous mathematical techniques for bounding information leakage and extracting randomness from noisy quantum measurements are fundamental to understanding the limits of quantum communication protocols.

Main result. By refining privacy amplification bounds using the leftover hash lemma, the authors demonstrate a provable increase in the certified key rate compared to state-of-the-art techniques.

Model / system. The analysis is applied to Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) protocols, using security proofs based on entropic uncertainty relations and finite-block analysis.

Abstract

The amount of secret key that can be obtained from a quantum key distribution run depends on both the physically observed error rates and the mathematical bounds used to certify security. For finite datasets, conservative bounds force users to discard a substantial fraction of the potentially available key. Here we further refine and extend the privacy amplification bounds achievable through the recent leftover hash lemma of Regula and Tomamichel [arXiv:2603.04493] and incorporate them into the security analysis of quantum key distribution based on entropic uncertainty relations. This improves on state-of-the-art key rates in finite-block regimes, certifying more secret key from the same experimental data without changes to the protocol, and outperforming techniques based on entropy accumulation. The results illustrate how sharper mathematical estimates can directly increase the usable output of a quantum communication system.

More is Less:Optimal Security for Haar Quantum Money and More

Authors: Zihan Hao, Xingjian Li, Qipeng Liu, Wei Zhan
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.40184v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper rigorously analyzes the security of quantum money by showing that the difficulty of counterfeiting remains constant even as the number of existing banknotes increases, provided the total count is small relative to the system size. It achieves this by developing a novel framework based on the compressed-oracle technique to establish tight lower bounds on cloning complexity. The work confirms that quantum money schemes can achieve optimal security where wealth does not confer counterfeiting power.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on quantum cryptography, the techniques developed—such as progress measurement using compressed oracles—are fundamental tools in quantum information theory applicable to analyzing resource constraints in complex quantum protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the security of quantum money and copy-protection schemes, specifically determining if possessing more copies provides an asymptotic advantage in counterfeiting.

Main result. It proves that for Haar-random state constructions, the query complexity to counterfeit additional banknotes remains constant (independent of existing wealth) as long as the total number of banknotes is sub-extensive relative to the system size.

Method. The authors develop a framework using the compressed-oracle technique to analyze progress measures for quantum tasks like Haar state cloning, establishing tight lower bounds via matching attacks.

Model / system. The model involves quantum states, specifically Haar-random states in $C^N$, and security is analyzed using a controlled reflection oracle. The analysis is framed within the Quantum Random Oracle Model (QROM).

Key observables. Query complexity ($T$), success probabilities for learning and cloning, and the asymptotic security threshold ($k+r = o(N)$).

Important parameters / regimes. Number of existing banknotes ($k$), number of additional copies ($r$), and the system dimension ($N=2n$).

Assumptions / limitations. The results are established for the Haar-state reflection-oracle construction and rely on the asymptotic condition $k+r = o(N)$ to guarantee security.

Paper structure. The paper builds its argument by first developing the compressed-oracle framework, then applying it to establish lower bounds for cloning, and finally synthesizing these results to prove the optimal security bounds for quantum money and copy-protection.

Abstract

Quantum cryptography leverages unclonability to enable a wide range of cryptographic applications that are impossible classically, such as digital currency protected against counterfeiting by quantum mechanics. Security requires that no efficient user can produce even one additional valid banknote beyond those already in their possession. However, existing security bounds weaken as the number of banknotes available to a user increases. In this paper, we study a construction of quantum money whose asymptotic query security does not deteriorate as long as the number of banknotes available to a user remains below the scale required for state tomography. In particular, we show that the construction based on an $n$-qubit Haar-random state and a reflection oracle achieves optimal query security: unless a user holds $Ω(2^n)$ banknotes, their existing banknotes cannot asymptotically speed up counterfeiting --- their best possible attack is the same as if they do not have any banknotes. To establish this result, we develop a framework based on the compressed-oracle technique for defining and analyzing progress measures for quantum tasks such as Haar state cloning. Furthermore, we prove a tight lower bound for generating $r$ additional copies, give a matching attack, and apply our results to quantum copy-protection.

On The Simplest Quantum-Secure Block Cipher

Authors: Gorjan Alagic, Joseph Carolan, Christian Majenz, Saliha Tokat
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.40350v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper establishes the quantum security of a specific two-round block cipher construction (Even-Mansour) within the Ideal Permutation Model. It proves that this cipher acts as a quantum pseudorandom permutation against adaptive quantum adversaries. The work is significant for advancing the theoretical understanding of quantum-secure primitives in cryptography.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on cryptography, the techniques developed—especially the use of isometries and bounding quantum state differences—are highly relevant to analyzing the security and distinguishability of quantum processes in quantum information theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to construct and prove the quantum security of Pseudorandom Permutations (PRPs) using the Key-Alternating Cipher (KAC) framework in the quantum-query setting.

Main result. The two-round Even-Mansour cipher is proven to be information theoretically secure (a qPRP) in the Ideal Permutation Model against polynomially-many adaptive forward and inverse quantum queries.

Method. The security proof utilizes a hybrid argument, employing an isometry and bounding the distinguishability advantage between the real and ideal quantum distributions.

Model / system. The model is the Ideal Permutation Model (IPM) extended to the Q2 setting, involving quantum oracles for permutations ($P_1, P_2$) and the cipher structure $E_{k_1,k_2,k_3}(x)$.

Key observables. The distinguishability advantage of a quantum algorithm distinguishing the real world from the ideal world, and the success probability of the quantum distinguisher.

Important parameters / regimes. Query complexity ($q$), domain size ($N$), and the number of rounds (specifically two rounds).

Assumptions / limitations. The proof relies on the use of compressed permutation oracles (CPO) and specific algebraic manipulations of quantum states in the Hadamard basis.

Figures summary. Not specified, but the notes detail complex algebraic derivations involving bounding differences between quantum operators ($ ext{cORVp}\Gamma^2 -Vp\Gamma^2 ext{cOI}$).

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the general framework for quantum security, proving the two-round security via a hybrid argument, and then detailing the complex algebraic steps required to bound the difference between the ideal and real world quantum operations.

Abstract

Pseudorandom permutations are ubiquitous in theoretical and applied cryptography. PRPs that offer security even against adversaries making quantum queries are of increasing interest, and used in applications ranging from constructing pseudorandom unitaries to separating SZK from BQP. A successful framework for constructing classically-secure PRPs is the key-alternating Even-Mansour approach, which interleaves applications of public permutations with additions of round keys. The single-round construction is already classically secure in the ideal permutation model (IPM), with added rounds offering improved concrete security. However, in the quantum-query setting, the status of this framework is presently unclear. A simple quantum-query attack based on Simon's algorithm breaks the one-round cipher. For two or more rounds, security is only known against non-adaptive adversaries who must prepare all queries in advance. In this work, we show that the two-round Even-Mansour cipher is information theoretically secure in the IPM against adversaries making polynomially-many adaptive forward and inverse quantum queries to all available oracles. Our proof uses compressed permutation oracles and a specially crafted isometry relating the ideal and real experiments. We also show that this construction is minimal, in the sense that essentially any cipher constructed via a single call to a public permutation is quantumly insecure.

Optimal learning of covariant quantum states and channels

Authors: Satoshi Yoshida, Kazuki Okigami, Pietro M. Posta, Dmitry Grinko

Summary. This work develops optimal theoretical and practical protocols for quantum state and channel tomography when symmetries are known. By leveraging techniques like random purification and constructing efficient quantum circuits, the authors establish optimal query scaling bounds for learning these quantum objects. This is crucial for designing resource-efficient quantum characterization experiments.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous determination of optimal query complexity for tomography, especially when symmetries are exploited, provides fundamental resource bounds for quantum characterization protocols relevant to quantum hardware testing.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the theoretical problem of optimally learning (tomography) quantum states and channels, particularly when the underlying system possesses known symmetries.

Main result. It derives optimal query scalings for learning permutation-covariant channels and constructs efficient quantum circuits for state/channel learning, achieving polynomial gate complexity.

Method. The authors employ techniques like random purification and dilation, reducing the problem to pure-state estimation, and utilize advanced mathematical tools including bosonic encoding, the quantum Hermite transform, and asymptotic combinatorial analysis.

Model / system. The focus is on general quantum states ($ ho$) and quantum channels ($\Lambda$) acting on qudits, specifically those exhibiting symmetries related to compact groups ($G$-covariant or permutation-covariant).

Key observables. Trace-distance error ($\epsilon$), diamond-distance error ($\epsilon$), and the required number of queries (sample complexity) or circuit depth (gate complexity).

Important parameters / regimes. The complexity scales depend on the system size ($k$), local dimension ($d$), and the desired error $\epsilon$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the ability to exploit known symmetries and assumes the existence of efficient quantum circuit approximations for key measurements like the Hayashi measurement.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing general bounds for symmetric states and covariant channels, to detailing the construction of efficient quantum circuits for the necessary measurements, and finally deriving explicit, optimal query lower bounds using combinatorial methods.

Abstract

We give collective tomography protocols for quantum states and channels with known symmetries, using random purification and dilation to reduce learning to pure-state estimation. For states commuting with a compact-group representation with multiplicities $m_λ$, the optimal copy complexity is $Θ((\sum_λm_λ^2+\logη^{-1})/\varepsilon^2)$ for sufficiently small trace-distance error $\varepsilon$ and failure probability $η$ for the nontrivial case $\sum_λm_λ^2>1$. For $G$-covariant channels with finite-dimensional unitary representations of a compact group $G$, parallel $\widetilde{O}(D_G/\varepsilon^2)$ queries achieve a diamond-distance error $\varepsilon $ at fixed success probability $2/3$, where $D_G$ counts the real parameters of covariant Choi operators before imposing trace preservation. For permutation-covariant channels on $k$ qudits of fixed local dimension $d$, at sufficiently small fixed error and fixed success probability $2/3$, we obtain the optimal query scalings $Θ_d(k^{d^4-1})$ for diamond distance and $Θ_d(k^{d^4-d^2})$ for Choi trace distance. Furthermore, we resolve an open problem in quantum tomography of constructing efficient quantum circuits that approximate the Hayashi measurement for optimal pure-state estimation. We encode states in the symmetric subspace into bosonic occupation modes, realize the measurement by heterodyne detection and normalization, and approximate this procedure on qubits using the quantum Hermite transform. Combined with our symmetry-compatible purification and dilation circuits, this yields query-optimal and gate-efficient learners of permutation-covariant states and channels with gate complexity $O_d(\mathrm{poly}(k,\varepsilon^{-1},\logη^{-1}))$.

Oscillator-Qubit Primitives for a Molecular Quantum Dynamics Simulator

Authors: Jungsoo Hong, Joonsuk Huh
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.40078v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops optimized quantum circuit primitives for simulating molecular quantum dynamics using oscillator-qubit architectures. By employing generalized Jacobi-Anger expansions within QSP, the authors create efficient gates for modeling complex vibronic interactions. The work sets a crucial benchmark for near-term quantum processors aiming to simulate chemical reactions.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the quantum computational implementation of fundamental chemical physics problems, providing concrete, optimized quantum circuits for simulating complex molecular interactions.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is simulating molecular quantum dynamics that requires treating both electronic and nuclear degrees of freedom quantum mechanically, which is exponentially hard classically.

Main result. The authors propose near-optimal quantum primitives for this simulation, achieving benchmark circuits significantly shorter than previous direct QSP approaches.

Method. They synthesize nonlinear and multimode bosonic phase gates using generalized Jacobi-Anger (GJA) expansions within the Quantum Signal Processing (QSP) framework.

Model / system. The system is a molecular quantum dynamics problem modeled using Oscillator-Qubit (OQ) processors, where electronic states are qubits and nuclear vibrations are bosonic oscillators. The Hamiltonian includes electron-nuclear and electron-electron interactions.

Key observables. Energy exchange between stretching and bending vibrations of a simplified CO2 model; joint-state error ($ ext{e}_{ ext{joint}}$); trace distance $ ext{D}(t)$.

Important parameters / regimes. Qubit and oscillator lifetimes (e.g., 2 ms and 5 ms); interaction strength and precision settings.

Assumptions / limitations. The simulation is limited by current qubit and oscillator coherence lifetimes; the model uses approximations for the full molecular Hamiltonian.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the mapping from molecular interactions to OQ circuits, showing how coordinate-dependent terms are compiled into bosonic phase gates.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the need for OQ processors for molecular dynamics. It then details the gate synthesis methods (Product-formula vs. QSP) and proposes the GJA-OQ-QSP compiler. Finally, it applies this to simulate energy exchange in a simplified CO2 model.

Abstract

Molecular quantum dynamics simulations that treat both electrons and nuclei quantum mechanically are crucial for predicting chemical reactions. With classical computation, a full wave-function representation of both types of particles requires resources that grow exponentially with their number. Oscillator-qubit processors, including trapped-ion and circuit QED devices, represent electronic states with qubits and nuclear vibrations with oscillators. Their native operations produce linear vibronic interactions, from which programmable anharmonic interactions can be built. Product-formula approaches build the corresponding phase gates by repeating small noncommuting phase steps, and quantum signal processing (QSP) approaches approximate each gate directly with a Fourier series. Simulations apply many such gates in sequence, so accumulated cost and error limit how long the dynamics can be followed. We propose near-optimal quantum primitives for molecular quantum dynamics simulation, covering the representations of both types of particles and their interactions. We synthesize nonlinear and multimode bosonic phase gates using generalized Jacobi-Anger (GJA) expansions within QSP, with a cost that grows near-optimally in both interaction strength and precision. Our interaction model and gate synthesis each have their own accuracy setting. Chosen together, these settings make our multimode benchmark circuits about one-third shorter than those of the previous direct QSP approach. We then simulate energy exchange between the stretching and bending vibrations of a simplified carbon dioxide model. With circuit-QED noise, a short simulation narrowly misses our accuracy criteria at qubit and oscillator lifetimes near 2 ms and 5 ms. These lifetimes mark a concrete checkpoint on the way to simulating anharmonic multimode molecular quantum dynamics on near-term oscillator-qubit processors.

Perfect non-local quantum computation is impossible

Abstract

We show that periodically modulating the self-propulsion of active particles in an asymmetric potential can both enhance rectified transport and reverse its direction, even when the propulsion speed itself never changes sign. Current enhancement results from the nonlinear response of active transport to propulsion strength, whereas current reversal emerges from the interplay between activity-induced spreading and passive relaxation. Our theoretical analysis identifies the regimes governed by these distinct rectification mechanisms and predicts the finite range of driving periods over which current reversal occurs. These results establish cyclic activity as a means of dynamically controlling rectified active transport, with potential applications to the selective sorting of active particles.

Summary. This paper introduces QuLoC, a novel method for compressing large language models by using photonic quantum circuits to generate trainable gating coefficients. These gates modulate the low-rank components during training, allowing the resulting model to achieve state-of-the-art performance gains on classical hardware. Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is efficiently deploying large language models (LLMs) by compressing their parameters while minimizing the degradation of downstream performance.

Main result. The proposed QuLoC method achieves significant relative accuracy improvements (e.g., 9.73% on Qwen3.5-4B) over state-of-the-art baselines, even when running on physical photonic quantum hardware.

Method. QuLoC introduces a quantum-assisted training mechanism where quantum circuit outputs generate trainable gating coefficients that modulate the contributions of low-rank components during training.

Model / system. The work applies to compressing large transformer-based LLMs (like Qwen3.5-4B and LLaMA-7B) by targeting their MLP blocks. The quantum aspect utilizes photonic quantum circuits (e.g., MZI structures) to generate quantum response features used for gating.

Key observables. Relative improvement in average downstream accuracy, Perplexity (PPL), and the fidelity of reconstruction compared to state-of-the-art methods.

Important parameters / regimes. The low-rank dimension ($r$), the compression ratio, and the number of compressed layers are key parameters varied in the experiments.

Assumptions / limitations. The final, deployed model runs entirely on classical hardware, as the quantum gating coefficients are absorbed into the low-rank factors after training, eliminating the need for quantum circuits during inference.

Abstract

As LLMs grow in size, compression becomes increasingly important for efficient deployment. SVD-based low-rank compression reduces parameter counts but can degrade downstream performance. To improve performance after compression, we introduce QuLoC, a photonic quantum-assisted LLM compression algorithm that uses quantum circuit outputs to gate the retained low-rank components during training. Model performance is recovered through local functional reconstruction followed by end-to-end knowledge distillation. After training, the gating coefficients are absorbed into the low-rank factors, allowing the compressed model to run on classical hardware without executing quantum circuits during inference. Experiments on Qwen3.5-4B show that QuLoC achieves a 9.73\% relative improvement in average accuracy over state-of-the-art baselines across multiple downstream tasks, demonstrating its effectiveness. We further evaluate QuLoC on LLaMA-7B at different parameter compression ratios. It consistently achieves higher average downstream accuracy than state-of-the-art baselines, supporting its applicability to a larger model across different compression settings. Notably, experiments using the photonic quantum hardware retain these benefits with a small accuracy loss relative to simulation, suggesting robustness to hardware noise. These results motivate further exploration of photonic quantum-assisted compression for larger models and more complex agentic tasks.
