Summary. This work reports on the successful creation and detailed characterization of negatively charged boron vacancy color centers in bulk h-BN using high-energy helium ion irradiation. By employing ODMR and PL spectroscopy, the authors confirmed the spin properties of the defect and measured its physical depth. This research advances the use of 2D materials for solid-state quantum memory and sensing applications.

Why it may be interesting. The $ ext{B}_{ ext{V}}^-$ center acts as a solid-state spin qubit candidate. The detailed characterization of its spin Hamiltonian parameters ($D, E, \gamma$) and its integration into a robust fabrication protocol are highly relevant for quantum information research.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper focuses on demonstrating the reliable fabrication and characterizing the negatively charged boron vacancy ($ ext{B}_{ ext{V}}^-$) color center in bulk hexagonal boron nitride ($ ext{h-BN}$).

Main result. The authors successfully confirmed the creation of $ ext{B}_{ ext{V}}^-$ centers using characteristic photoluminescence and optically detected magnetic resonance (ODMR) measurements.

Method. The research combines experimental techniques like high-energy $ ext{He}^+$ ion irradiation, PL, and ODMR with theoretical modeling (SRIM) to characterize the defect.

Model / system. The system is the $ ext{B}_{ ext{V}}^-$ color center in bulk $ ext{h-BN}$. The electronic structure is modeled by a Hamiltonian involving zero-field splitting ($D$) and orthorhombic splitting ($E$), which governs its spin $S=1$ behavior in a magnetic field.

Key observables. Photoluminescence (PL) spectrum (peak at 800–850 nm), Zeeman shift in ODMR resonance lines, and the depth profile of the color center along the c-axis.

Important parameters / regimes. Zero-field splitting parameter ($D \approx 3.48 ext{ GHz}$), orthorhombic splitting ($E \approx 50 ext{ MHz}$), and the measured resonance width ($180 ext{ MHz}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary discrepancy noted is that the experimental depth of the vacancy is greater than the depth predicted by standard SRIM modeling, suggesting unaccounted physical processes like ion channeling.

Abstract

While color centers in diamond and other three-dimensional crystals are nowadays key elements of several types of sensors, color centers in two-dimensional materials are rapidly developing and promise high-tech applications. One of the interesting centers in 2D materials is the negatively charged boron vacancy in hexagonal boron nitride (h-BN), which has already shown some potential for magnetometry, but the reliable creation of boron vacancies remains challenging. Here, we demonstrate the fabrication of negatively charged boron vacancy color centers in bulk h-BN via implantation of helium ions at ~1 MeV, as confirmed by characteristic photoluminescence and optically detected magnetic resonance. The resonance has a width of 180 MHz and exhibits the expected Zeeman shift of the resonance lines. The depth of the color center along the c-axis of the h-BN crystal was measured and compared with predictions from Stopping and Range of Ions in Matter modeling. The experimental value and the modeled prediction are consistent within the reported uncertainties, as their 1σ intervals overlap, although the experiment shows a greater depth and a wider distribution.

Experimentally realized local-global tradeoff in quantum resources

Summary. This paper experimentally establishes a fundamental tradeoff inequality linking local quantum resources (maximum coherence) and global quantum resources (quantum mutual information) in bipartite quantum states. Using entangled photons, the authors demonstrate that these resources are complementary, showing that enhancing one necessarily constrains the other. This provides crucial physical insight into the resource management within quantum networks.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides concrete experimental evidence for fundamental resource constraints in quantum information, linking local properties (coherence) to global correlations (QMI) in a way that has implications for quantum network design.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates and experimentally demonstrates a fundamental tradeoff relationship between local quantum resources (measured by maximum coherence) and global quantum resources (measured by quantum mutual information).

Main result. A tradeoff inequality is established showing that the sum of local maximum coherence and quantum mutual information is bounded by a definitive upper limit. This implies that local and global quantum resources are mutually exclusive, meaning enhancing one necessarily limits the other.

Method. The authors use quantum state tomography on experimentally generated entangled photon pairs to measure various quantum resource quantities. They compare experimental measurements against theoretically derived tradeoff inequalities.

Model / system. The study uses an optical quantum system employing polarization degrees of freedom of entangled photon pairs generated via Spontaneous Parametric Down-Conversion (SPDC). The system is analyzed for both pure and mixed bipartite quantum states.

Key observables. Local maximum coherence ($C_{\max}( ho_A) + C_{\max}( ho_B)$) and Quantum Mutual Information ($I( ho_{AB})$).

Important parameters / regimes. The tradeoff inequality itself, $C_{\max}( ho_A) + C_{\max}( ho_B) + I( ho_{AB}) \leq \log_2(d_A d_B)$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the basis-independent measure of maximum coherence, $C_{\max}( ho)$, and the derived inequality holds with equality if and only if the state is pure.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the local-global tradeoff curve, showing the relationship between local coherence and QMI for both pure and mixed states, comparing experimental data points to theoretical bounds.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the concept of the local-global tradeoff, then details the experimental setup using entangled photons. It derives the general tradeoff inequality, experimentally verifies this inequality for pure states, and analyzes how mixed states fall below the theoretical bound due to entropy.

Abstract

The interconversion among different quantum resources has attracted considerable attention, exemplified by the tradeoff between coherence and entanglement. However, the relationship between local and global quantum resources remains largely unexplored. Here, we propose and experimentally demonstrate a local global tradeoff in quantum resources, quantified respectively by the local maximum coherence and the quantum mutual information. Our experiments show that the sum of the local maximum coherence and the quantum mutual information is bounded by a definitive upper limit. These results not only establish a fundamental tradeoff inequality, but also provide direct experimental evidence of the complementarity between local and global quantum resources. Moreover, our findings reveal an important constraint in quantum networks: the local quantum resources available at individual nodes and the global quantum resources of the network are mutually exclusive, implying that enhancing one necessarily limits the other.

Summary. The authors present a microwave-driven, reservoir- and measurement-free method to initialize semiconductor spin qubits into a target singlet state. By exploiting spin-charge physics near a Pauli blockade, they achieve high-fidelity initialization using only fixed microwave pulse sequences. This establishes a crucial, scalable control primitive for building large quantum processors.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides an experimentally validated, solid-state method for state preparation that circumvents major scaling bottlenecks in quantum computing architectures, directly relevant to solid-state quantum information processing.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Developing scalable methods for initializing semiconductor spin qubits without relying on complex, hard-to-distribute local reservoirs or measurement feedback.

Main result. A fixed-sequence microwave initialization protocol was demonstrated, achieving a high singlet-associated charge outcome probability of 99.4% and establishing a scalable control primitive for spin-qubit processors.

Method. The protocol uses a fixed sequence of microwave and baseband pulses to drive the system through the triplet manifold, enabling conversion to the singlet charge state.

Model / system. The work uses silicon spin qubits in an industrially fabricated Si/SiGe quantum-dot device, operating near the (1, 3)–(0, 4) Pauli-blockade transition.

Key observables. Singlet-associated charge outcome probability ($P_S$), Rabi oscillation visibility, and charge-signal difference (CSD) measurements.

Important parameters / regimes. Initialization time scale ($\approx 12 \mu ext{s}$ initially, projecting sub-microsecond), and the singlet-triplet mixing time ($ au_{mix}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The method relies on singlet-triplet physics within the Pauli spin blockade regime, suggesting applicability to other platforms with similar physics.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the spin-charge spectrum, the initialization cycle energy schematic, Rabi oscillations, and the dependence of initialization probability on repetition number and pulse parameters.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from characterizing the physical system and coherent control (Rabi oscillations) to detailing the fixed-sequence initialization protocol, analyzing its repetition dependence, and finally establishing its viability as a scalable control primitive.

Abstract

Scalable quantum processors require repeated qubit initialization throughout large arrays. In semiconductor spin qubits, fast initialization commonly relies on local reservoir access or measurement-based feedback, requiring dedicated infrastructure that becomes increasingly difficult to distribute as processors scale. Here, we demonstrate reservoir- and measurement-free initialization of a silicon spin-qubit pair in an industrially fabricated Si/SiGe quantum-dot device using a fixed sequence of microwave and baseband pulses. Odd spin-parity states relax to the singlet charge state, whereas blocked even spin-parity states are microwave-driven through the triplet manifold and subsequently converted to the singlet by singlet-triplet mixing and charge hybridization. Repeated cycles produce the singlet-associated charge outcome with a median probability of 99.4% across the sampled preparation states, while exchange spectroscopy independently verifies mapping to the target $|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle$ operational state. Microwave spectroscopy and time-domain measurements identify the dark-state-limited single-cycle transfer and the subsequent blockade-lifting dynamics that set the initialization time scale. The demonstrated pumping sequence uses approximately $12\,μ\mathrm{s}$ of microwave bursts and mixing dwells, while we project sub-microsecond initialization under improved device conditions. These results establish fixed-sequence microwave initialization as a scalable control primitive for semiconductor spin-qubit processors, based on singlet-triplet physics that can be adapted to platforms with suitable Pauli-blockade transitions.

Abstract

In mean-field theory, the ground state of a two-component Bose-Einstein condensate (BEC) in a uniform system is either uniformly mixed or totally separated. Beyond-mean-field effects introduce an additional phase, the mixed-bubble state, in which the two components remain mixed within one of the separated domains. Here we show that a mixed-bubble state can emerge in a binary mixture of spin-1 ${}^{7}{\rm{Li}}$ and ${}^{23}{\rm{Na}}$ BECs even within the mean-field framework. We further demonstrate the existence of a metastable uniformly-mixed state, which undergoes a transition to the mixed-bubble state triggered by a local perturbation.

Abstract

Summary. This paper provides an algebraic framework to characterize the high-dimensional spatial entanglement of biphotons generated by SPDC using LG-pumped beams. By decomposing the process into beam-splitter and squeezing operations, the authors derive the Schmidt number as a function of the pump mode indices. This offers a deep theoretical understanding of entanglement dimensionality and associated conservation laws in structured light quantum sources.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous, mathematically unified description of high-dimensional entanglement resources generated optically, which is crucial for designing quantum communication protocols based on structured light.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to develop an algebraic framework to characterize the complex spatial-mode entanglement generated in biphotons via Spontaneous Parametric Down-Conversion (SPDC) when the pump beam uses higher-order Laguerre-Gaussian (LG) modes.

The authors derive an analytical expression for the Schmidt number, showing how the radial and azimuthal indices of the LG pump modes govern the dimensionality of the spatial entanglement. They also establish conservation laws when the pump beam radius matches the crystal correlation width.

Method. The core method involves factorizing the SPDC process into a spatial-mode beam-splitter transformation followed by a spatial two-mode squeezing operation, analyzed in the transverse wavevector space.

Model / system. The system is the biphoton state $|\Psi_{N^-}^{N+} angle$ generated in an SPDC process using a higher-order LG-pumped beam. The analysis utilizes the LG-mode basis and sum/difference coordinates for the signal and idler photons.

Abstract

High-dimensional spatial entanglement generated via spontaneous parametric down-conversion (SPDC) provides a powerful resource for quantum information processing, yet its mode structure becomes increasingly complex when the pump occupies a higher-order Laguerre--Gaussian (LG) mode. Here, we develop an algebraic framework for characterizing biphoton spatial-mode entanglement by factorizing LG-pumped SPDC into a spatial-mode beam-splitter transformation followed by spatial two-mode squeezing. By choosing the biphoton LG basis radius as the geometric mean of the pump-beam radius and the crystal-induced correlation width, we obtain a natural modal basis in which the two operations can be treated separately. When the pump-beam radius matches the correlation width, spatial squeezing vanishes, and the two circular-mode components of the pump are independently conserved across the signal and idler photons, leading to conservation of both orbital angular momentum and the total spatial mode number. We further derive an analytical expression for the Schmidt number for arbitrary LG pump modes and squeezing strengths, revealing how the radial and azimuthal pump indices govern the dimensionality of the spatial entanglement. This algebraic characterization provides a unified description of the mode structure, conservation laws, and entanglement dimensionality of higher-order-LG-pumped SPDC.

Abstract

Abstract

In this work, we consider the fundamental task of estimating the purity of an unknown state to within $\textit{multiplicative}$ error $\varepsilon$. When one can perform general collective measurements, $Θ\left(\frac{\sqrt{d}}{\varepsilon^2} + \frac{d}{\varepsilon}\right)$ copies are known to be necessary and sufficient for this problem [AISW20]. However, implementing collective measurements on such a large number of copies can be experimentally demanding, and we thus aim to characterize the copy complexity of this problem with incoherent measurements. In this setting, the only non-trivial result is a non-adaptive algorithm that uses $O\left( \frac{d}{\varepsilon^2} + \frac{d^2}{\varepsilon} \right)$ copies [PTTW26], which is polynomially larger than the best-known lower bound. Our first result is a new algorithm performing non-adaptive incoherent measurements that succeeds using $O\left( \frac{d}{\varepsilon^2} + \frac{d^{3/2}}{\varepsilon} \right)$ copies, improving on the latter term in the copy complexity. Moreover, we show that for any algorithm restricted to non-adaptive measurements, the above copy complexity is optimal. We also develop a new adaptive estimator for the purity of a state that improves on the above complexity in the high-precision regime, i.e., for $\varepsilon = o(1/d)$. We also show that the non-adaptive and adaptive estimators together yield the optimal complexity for incoherent purity estimation; in particular, we show that the copy complexity of this problem is $$ Θ\left(\min\left\{ \frac{d}{\varepsilon^2} + \frac{d^{3/2}}{\varepsilon}, \frac{d^2}{\varepsilon} + \frac{\sqrt{d}}{\varepsilon^2}\right\} \right). $$

Summary. This paper introduces a novel quantum amplitude amplification protocol that enhances Grover's search by incorporating weak measurements within iterative loops. Main problem. To develop quantum amplitude amplification algorithms that improve upon standard Grover search by incorporating tunable weak measurements in iterative loops.

Main result. The proposed weakly measured loop achieves an expected oracle cost that improves upon standard Grover search, showing a leading constant of $1/\sqrt{2}$ in certain regimes and providing explicit fixed-point control.

Method. The authors analyze the expected oracle cost by modeling the process as an interleaving of ordinary Grover rotations and weak measurements, solving resulting differential/difference equations in various parameter regimes.

Model / system. The model is quantum amplitude amplification operating in a two-dimensional subspace, utilizing weak measurements defined by tunable strength $\kappa$ to probe the target subspace.

Key observables. Expected oracle cost (expected number of Grover iterations), which is analyzed in terms of the target probability $p$.

Important parameters / regimes. Target probability $p$, weak measurement strength $\kappa$, and the lower bound $p_0$ for the target probability.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the process retains the Grover-type quadratic speedup and relies on bounding omitted tail sums for rigorous proofs.

Figures summary. Figure 2 plots the normalized expected cost $2C(b)/\pi$ vs. $b$, showing a minimum near $b \approx 5.2$. Figure 3 compares the finite-$p$ normalized expected cost for various adaptive schedules, highlighting the authors' schedule's performance.

Abstract

We present a family of quantum amplitude amplification algorithms that interleave ordinary Grover rotations with tunable weak measurements in loops. A successful measurement yields the target state and stops the algorithm, while after a failure, the loop resumes from the post-measurement state without restarting. We express the leading costs in expected Grover iterations as $p \to 0$. For a known target probability $p$, an exact weak measurement-conditioned loop for states near the angle $π/4$ uses $(π/8+1/4+o(1))/\sqrt{p}$ iterations, improving on the standard Grover constant $π/4$ and on optimised restart Grover, and matching the corresponding infimum in our continuous-angle analysis. When only a lower bound $0 < p_0 \leq p < 1/2$ is available, a discrete Lyapunov equation gives the exact expected oracle cost for every fixed measurement strength and a closed-form optimal strength. Using the strength selected from $p_0$, the expected number of Grover iterations is at most $\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{p_0}})$, where the expected cost decreases as the actual $p$ increases, and gives the leading constant $1/\sqrt{2}$ at the promise boundary $p = p_0$. For completely unknown $p$, we identify measurement strength $κ(t)=Θ(1/t)$ as the critical scale within the regular schedules considered here and analyse $κ_b(t)=\min\{1/2,b/t\}$. For each fixed $b > 2$, we rigorously derive a closed-form Gamma-function expression $C(b)$ giving $(C(b)/2+o(1))/\sqrt{p}$ expected iterations. Numerical minimisation of this explicit expression gives $b \approx 5.2$ and $C(b)/2 \approx 1.01$. The results show that weak measurements preserve the $Θ(1/\sqrt{p})$ search scale while adding an explicit fixed-point control mechanism and provable oracle savings.

Abstract

Abstract

Finite-bandwidth reservoir engineering can suppress unwanted transitions in a quantum thermal machine, but a physical filter also introduces a finite response time. We study this competition in a continuous three-level heat engine whose hot environment couples parasitically to the cold transition. The corresponding three-state rate network is solved exactly, showing that the parasitic transition produces a hot-to-cold thermodynamic short circuit and yielding a closed threshold for the loss of positive-power operation. We then retain a damped auxiliary mode explicitly as a physical spectral filter. Its Lorentzian response suppresses the detuned parasitic transition, whereas excessive narrowing limits the useful energy throughput. Independent local-GKSL and nonsecular Bloch--Redfield calculations both recover the no-leak Markovian engine in the weak-coupling limit and predict a finite maximum-power bandwidth, although its precise location is model dependent. A frequency-resolved Lorentzian-rate reduction, by contrast, has no interior optimum. The resulting design principle is that spectral selectivity can protect the useful thermodynamic cycle, but a real filter cannot be narrowed without a dynamical throughput cost.
