Complex Quantum Dynamics Versus Classical Simulability of Noisy Random Circuits

Authors: Anjali Waghmare, Sergii Strelchuk, Sathyawageeswar Subramanian
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.39734v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper demonstrates that in the presence of noise, standard quantum complexity diagnostics (like magic or scrambling) fail to reliably track the limits of efficient classical simulation. The separation is attributed to the fact that these diagnostics probe higher-order statistical moments than the moments relevant to the best classical simulation algorithms. This suggests that measuring such diagnostics on a noisy device is insufficient evidence for true quantum computational advantage.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly challenges the assumption that quantum complexity measures are universally reliable indicators of computational hardness, providing crucial theoretical nuance for assessing quantum advantage claims in realistic, noisy settings.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates whether standard quantum dynamical diagnostics (like magic or scrambling) reliably predict the limits of efficient classical simulation when quantum circuits are subjected to noise.

Main result. The diagnostics and classical simulability do not reliably track each other in noisy mixed-state circuits, as they probe different statistical moments that decay at different rates under noise.

Method. The authors compare the behavior of structural diagnostics (e.g., fourth-order moments) against the computational cost derived from best-known classical simulation algorithms (which often depend on second-order moments).

Model / system. The study analyzes noisy random quantum circuits, specifically two families: noisy Clifford+$T$ circuits and noisy matchgate circuits, subjected to local depolarizing noise.

Key observables. Magic, operator scrambling (OTOCs), anticoncentration, and fermionic non-Gaussianity are used as diagnostics, alongside the computational cost derived from classical simulation algorithms.

Important parameters / regimes. Noise strength, circuit depth, and system size ($N$) are key parameters used to map out the separation regimes.

Assumptions / limitations. The initial connection between diagnostics and simulability was established for noiseless circuits, and the analysis highlights that diagnostics measured on a noisy device do not constitute evidence for classical hardness by themselves.

Figures summary. Figure 1 summarizes the central result, showing that the boundary where the resource diagnostic becomes trivial does not coincide with the boundary separating regions of efficient classical simulation for both circuit families.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theoretical framework by analyzing the statistical moments probed by various diagnostics and comparing these to the decay rates governing the efficiency of known classical simulation techniques for the specified noisy circuit models.

Abstract

Claims of quantum advantage rest on the classical hardness of simulating quantum circuits. Magic, operator scrambling, anticoncentration, and non-Gaussianity for fermionic circuits are standard diagnostics of complex quantum dynamics. For pure states, some of these have been rigorously connected to classical simulability. Whether these diagnostics reliably track the limits of efficient classical simulation under noise remains unclear. Here, we show that in noisy Clifford+$T$ and nearest-neighbour matchgate+SWAP circuits, dynamical diagnostics and classical simulability can separate in both directions. In particular, the diagnostics can remain nontrivial after classical simulation becomes efficient, or become trivial before known efficient classical algorithms apply. We trace this mismatch to the different statistical properties they probe: magic and scrambling depend on fourth-order statistics of the Pauli spectrum, whereas the simulation algorithms depend primarily on second-order moments, which local noise suppresses at different rates. Thus, dynamical diagnostics measured on a noisy quantum device do not by themselves constitute evidence for classical hardness.

Computing electron overlap integrals for Gausslet orbitals on cubic lattices

Type: theory · Category: numerical methods · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.39176v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

Summary. This paper presents advanced numerical algorithms for calculating electron overlap integrals required for quantum electronic structure simulations using Gausslet orbitals on a cubic lattice. By rigorously exploiting lattice symmetries, the authors achieve massive computational reductions in the number of integrals needed. This efficient methodology is demonstrated by accurately calculating the ground state energies of the hydrogen atom and molecule.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a highly optimized, scalable computational framework for quantum chemistry calculations, which is crucial for applying advanced electronic structure methods to larger, more complex molecular or solid-state systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is developing highly efficient algorithms to compute the necessary overlap integrals (kinetic, nuclear, and electron repulsion) for electronic structure simulations using Gausslet orbitals on cubic lattices.

Main result. The authors successfully developed symmetry-exploiting algorithms that drastically reduce the number of required electron repulsion integrals on a $5 imes 5 imes 5$ grid from $244$ million to under $330$ thousand, achieving rapid computation times.

Method. The methodology relies heavily on exploiting translational, permutation, and octahedral symmetries inherent in the Gausslet basis set to systematically reorder and precompute nested summations for the various integral types.

Model / system. The work focuses on electronic structure calculations using Gausslet orbitals defined on a cubic lattice ($\mathbb{Z}^3$). Demonstrations include calculating the ground state energy of the hydrogen atom and the hydrogen molecule.

Key observables. Overlap integrals ($k_{i,j}$, $nZ_{i,j}(R)$, $(ij|k\ell)$), ground state energy ($E_0$), and the rank of the resulting wavefunction coefficient matrix.

Important parameters / regimes. The grid size (e.g., $5 imes 5 imes 5$), the truncation tolerance ($10^{-5}$), and the scaling factor ($s$) used for basis set rescaling are key parameters.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary approximations include using the Integral Diagonal Approximation (IDA) for the electron repulsion integral tensor and assuming the factorized form of the Gausslet orbitals.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the asymptotic decay of ERIs, showing expected scaling, and show the ground state energy curves for the H atom and molecule as a function of the grid scaling factor $s$.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the physical problem and the Gausslet basis. It then details the mathematical derivation and reorganization of the three types of overlap integrals, heavily emphasizing the symmetry reduction techniques (Algorithm 1). Finally, it applies the developed framework to benchmark calculations for the H atom and H molecule.

Abstract

Gausslet orbitals on a cubic lattice, introduced in [Steven R. White, J. Chem. Phys. 147, 244102 (2017)], represent a localized, smooth, and systematically refineable basis set featuring a "diagonal" approximability of the electron repulsion integral tensor. The present work develops efficient algorithms for evaluating overlap integrals required for electronic-structure simulations using these Gausslets, specifically the kinetic, nuclear, and electron repulsion integrals (both with and without the diagonal approximation). Computational efficiency improvements rest on the exploitation of translation, permutation, and octahedral symmetries, a reordering and precomputation of nested sums, as well as an early truncation of small coefficients. Our algorithms reduce the number of electron repulsion integrals on a $5 \times 5 \times 5$ grid from $125^4 = 244140625$ to $324275$ due to symmetries, and achieve a wall-clock runtime for evaluating the remaining integrals with a truncation tolerance of $10^{-5}$ in under 2 seconds on a laptop computer. We apply the developed methodology to compute the ground state of the hydrogen atom and molecule as a demonstration.

Conditioning-Free Non-Uniform Quantum Fourier and Chebyshev Transforms

Authors: Chaowen Guan, Akshit Katiyar
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.40277v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper presents an efficient quantum algorithm for the non-uniform Chebyshev transform (NUCT), overcoming limitations in previous quantum Fourier transform methods. By constructing a 'conditioning-free' NUQFT, the authors achieve a polylogarithmic gate complexity circuit for the NUCT. This advancement is significant for quantum simulation tasks requiring high-precision spectral analysis.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete, resource-efficient quantum circuit implementation for a mathematically important transform (Chebyshev) that is critical in quantum simulation and spectral analysis, advancing the field of quantum signal processing.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Developing an efficient quantum algorithm for the non-uniform Chebyshev transform (NUCT), which is difficult because its sampling nodes are non-uniform in the angle $ heta$.

Main result. The authors provide an $\epsilon$-accurate block encoding of the NUCT matrix using $O(L)$ qubits and $\widetilde O(L^2)$ gates, where $L = \log N + \log(1/\epsilon)$, crucially removing dependence on geometry-dependent parameters from prior work.

Method. The NUCT is related to the average of two Type-II non-uniform DFTs, which are implemented using an improved, 'conditioning-free' Non-uniform Quantum Fourier Transform (NUQFT) circuit.

Model / system. The model involves quantum registers storing fixed-point numbers used to compute the transform coefficients based on Chebyshev polynomials sampled at specific nodes $x_k$.

Key observables. The output state fidelity and the circuit complexity in terms of qubits and gates.

Important parameters / regimes. The precision $\epsilon$, the transform size $N$, and the derived complexity parameter $L = \log N + \log(1/\epsilon)$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the explicit construction of oracles for node and row access, and the equivalence of the NUCT to two Type-II NUDFTs.

Figures summary. Not specified.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the NUCT problem, improving the general NUQFT by removing $\kappa$ dependence, and finally applying this improved transform to construct the full NUCT circuit.

Abstract

We present an efficient quantum algorithm for the non-uniform Chebyshev transform. It is defined as the projection of a function onto Chebyshev polynomials sampled at given nodes that are uniform in $x\in[-1,1]$, and hence non-uniform in the angle $θ=\arccos x$, a setting that QFT-based quantum Chebyshev transforms cannot handle. Our construction is based on an improvement of an existing Non-uniform Quantum Fourier Transform (NUQFT) whereby we remove the conditioning from non-uniform node sampling. Hence, error bounds are independent of the geometry-dependent parameter $κ$ of prior work. We use the fact that Chebyshev transform matrix is the average of two Type-II non-uniform DFTs, which we implement with a single controlled NUQFT circuit. We provide explicit oracle constructions, including the row-access oracle previously left as an assumption. The resulting $\varepsilon$-accurate block encoding has $O(1)$ normalization and uses $O(L)$ qubits and $\widetilde O(L^2)$ gates, where $L=\log N+\log(1/\varepsilon)$. We give an end-to-end implementation with complexity analysis, including the success probability and output-state error.

Summary. This paper develops advanced theoretical constructions for quantum error-correcting codes, focusing on achieving constant rate while supporting fully addressable transversal gates like the T gate. By combining algebraic geometry and coding theory, the authors establish new bounds on code performance and address the critical challenge of implementing arbitrary logical gates fault-tolerantly.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the fundamental limitations in fault-tolerant quantum computation by providing explicit constructions for high-performance quantum codes with necessary gate symmetries.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to construct quantum error-correcting codes (specifically CSS codes) that possess constant rate, fully addressable transversal gates (like the T gate), and maintain good asymptotic parameters.

Main result. The authors construct asymptotically good CSS codes with fully addressable transversal T gates, and also provide constructions for sparse protected subsystems and Pauli-LDPC codes with transversal T.

Method. The work employs advanced techniques from algebraic geometry (e.g., rational functions on curves) and coding theory to enforce specific phase identities required for transversal gate implementation.

Model / system. The model is quantum error correction, focusing on stabilizer codes and CSS codes defined over binary subspaces. The physical system involves encoding logical qubits into physical qubits to protect against errors while allowing selective gate operations.

Key observables. Code parameters include rate, distance ($d$), and the ability to realize specific logical gates (e.g., transversal T gate).

Important parameters / regimes. Key parameters include the code rate, the distance scaling ($\Omega(n)$ or $\Omega(\sqrt{n})$), and the gate order ($\ell$) for the implemented gates.

Assumptions / limitations. The general case for arbitrary rational rotations requires the use of nonadditive codes, and the construction of Pauli-LDPC codes with fully addressable non-Clifford gates remains an open challenge.

Figures summary. Not specified in the notes.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing constructions for fully addressable transversal T gates in CSS codes, then detailing the use of algebraic geometry to enforce necessary phase identities, and finally presenting concrete constructions for sparse protected subsystems and distance lower bounds.

Abstract

Recent work by Wills constructed codes with constant rate and transversal $T$. Extending this line of work, we construct asymptotically good CSS codes with \textbf{fully addressable} transversal $T$, permitting selective control over which logical qubits receive a $T$ gate. We also give Pauli-LDPC codes with transversal $T$ and growing distance at vanishing rate; and a protected-subsystem construction with fully addressable transversal $T$, constant protected rate, distance $Ω(\sqrt n)$, and sparse (non-Pauli) checks. More generally, we obtain asymptotically good binary codes with full transversal addressability for every fixed finite-order one-qubit gate, retaining CSS realizations for dyadic phase gates such as $\sqrt T$ and using generally nonadditive codes for rotations such as $R_z(π/7)$. The remaining challenge is to construct asymptotically good Pauli-LDPC codes with fully addressable transversal non-Clifford one-qubit gates.

Constant-Overhead Injection into Quantum Codes

Authors: Louis Golowich, Venkatesan Guruswami
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.39345v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces a groundbreaking fault-tolerant scheme for quantum error correction that solves the problem of injecting and ejecting quantum states between physical qubits and encoded code blocks. By achieving constant space and time overhead, it significantly improves the feasibility of building large-scale quantum processors. The methodology relies on advanced code constructions using hypergraph products and rigorous gadget analysis.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides concrete, resource-efficient protocols for interfacing physical qubits with logical quantum memory, which is a critical bottleneck for scaling up quantum computation architectures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is to construct a fault-tolerant method for injecting bare physical qubits into quantum error-correcting codes and subsequently ejecting encoded states back into physical qubits.

Main result. The authors present the first known fault-tolerant scheme achieving this injection/ejection with constant space and time overhead, succeeding under locally stochastic noise.

Method. The construction utilizes high-dimensional hypergraph products of classical LDPC codes, which are then concatenated to achieve resilience against uniform noise. Fault-tolerance is maintained via specialized 'gadgets' implemented with constant circuit depth.

Model / system. The system involves quantum error-correcting codes, specifically built using hypergraph products of classical LDPC codes derived from linear-time-encodable codes. The analysis is performed under the model of circuit-level locally stochastic noise.

Key observables. Logical error probability (bounded by a small constant for injection/ejection), constant space and time overhead ($O(1)$ depth).

Important parameters / regimes. The physical qubit count grows linearly with the number of logical qubits; the scheme assumes the ability to run polynomial-sized noiseless classical circuits.

Assumptions / limitations. The scheme assumes the availability of noiseless classical side-computations and that the physical qubits are exposed to a constant noise rate.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the process of code switching, showing the flow from one code block ($Q_1$) through an intermediate fault-tolerant block ($Q_2$) to an output block ($Q'_1$) using injection and ejection.

Paper structure. The paper follows a structure moving from motivation to technical overview, defining preliminaries, detailing the code construction via hypergraph products, presenting the decoder, and finally detailing the fault-tolerant gadgets and their rigorous proof of operation.

Abstract

We construct the first known fault-tolerant scheme for injecting states into quantum error-correcting codes with constant space and time overhead. That is, we construct a family of constant-rate quantum error-correcting codes for which a set of bare physical qubits can be injected, i.e. fault-tolerantly encoded, into a code block. Similarly, a code state can be ejected, i.e. fault-tolerantly decoded, back into bare physical qubits. We show that these injection and ejection procedures succeed under circuit-level locally stochastic noise, while incurring just a small constant probability of corrupting each qubit, which is unavoidable for bare physical qubits. We also show how to perform fault-tolerant error correction and code-state preparation under locally stochastic noise. All of our gadgets can be implemented with constant quantum circuit depth (i.e. are single-shot), and with a number of physical qubits growing linearly with the number of logical qubits, assuming the ability to run polynomial-sized noiseless classical circuits on the side. We construct our quantum codes by taking a high-dimensional hypergraph product of classical LDPC codes, which in turn are a simplified version of Spielman's linear-time encodable codes (STOC'95). As our resulting product codes are only resilient to physical errors occurring with non-uniform probabilities across qubits, we then show how to concatenate with inner codes of various sizes to obtain fault-tolerance against uniform noise.

EPR Count for Runtime Prediction in Distributed Quantum Computing

Authors: Fatih E. Bilgen, Ozgur B. Akan
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.39851v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper rigorously tests the assumption that minimizing the required EPR pair count is equivalent to minimizing the runtime in distributed quantum computing. By analyzing several benchmark circuits under varying communication conditions, the authors demonstrate that the two metrics are distinct, showing that minimum-EPR mappings can be suboptimal and that runtime differences are highly sensitive to communication latency and concurrency.

Why it may be interesting. This work is highly relevant as it provides critical theoretical constraints on resource estimation in large-scale quantum computation, guiding the development of more accurate compilation and scheduling tools for quantum hardware.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates whether the count of required EPR pairs (EPR cost) is a reliable proxy for the actual execution time in distributed quantum computing.

Main result. EPR cost and execution time are distinct optimization objectives; minimizing EPR cost does not guarantee minimum runtime, and the reliability of the proxy depends strongly on the specific quantum workload and communication operating regime.

Method. The authors systematically evaluate mappings of benchmark quantum circuits (QFT, QAOA, CDKM) onto two Quantum Processing Units (QPUs) by comparing the minimum EPR cost against the calculated distributed execution time under various concurrency models.

Model / system. The model simulates Distributed Quantum Computing (DQC) executing circuits across two interconnected QPUs. The complexity is analyzed by mapping circuits and calculating both the entanglement demand (EPR cost) and the runtime based on circuit dependencies (DAG scheduling).

Key observables. EPR Cost (CEPR), Distributed Execution Time (T_dist), Spearman's rank correlation ($ ho_s$), Strict Rank Reversal Fraction (Frev), and Runtime Regret.

Important parameters / regimes. Circuit size ($N_q \in \{4, 6, 8, 10, 12\}$), EPR-generation latency ($ ho_{ ext{EPR}}$), and communication concurrency limit ($N_{ ext{comm}}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes a baseline model where one EPR pair is consumed per non-local operation, ignoring entanglement reuse or advanced teleportation techniques.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the variation of the Strict Rank Reversal Fraction (Frev) across different circuits and qubit numbers, and show how the maximum runtime spread among equal-EPR mappings increases with EPR-generation latency.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the problem by comparing EPR cost to runtime across three benchmark circuits (QFT, QAOA, CDKM). It then systematically analyzes the relationship by varying communication latency and concurrency, concluding that explicit communication-aware runtime modeling is necessary.

Abstract

EPR-pair consumption is commonly used as a communication-cost objective in distributed quantum computing, but minimizing EPR cost does not necessarily minimize distributed execution time. Despite its widespread use, the reliability of EPR count as a runtime surrogate has received limited direct characterization across different workloads and communication conditions. This work addresses this gap by systematically evaluating the relationship between EPR cost and runtime over all balanced two-QPU mappings of QFT, QAOA, and CDKM circuits. The results show that mappings with identical EPR cost can have substantially different execution times, lower-EPR mappings can be slower, and minimum-EPR mappings can be runtime-suboptimal. The reliability of EPR count is strongly workload dependent and also changes with the communication operating regime: increasing EPR-generation latency amplifies runtime differences hidden by equal EPR cost, while increased communication concurrency can reduce the ability of EPR count to preserve runtime ordering. These results show that EPR count and execution time are related but distinct optimization objectives, and identify regimes in which explicit communication-aware runtime modeling is necessary.

Equality in the Bosonic Quantum Entropy Power Inequality

Authors: Yonglong Li
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.40061v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper rigorously characterizes the equality conditions for the bosonic quantum entropy power inequality (qEPI) for independent inputs. It proves that saturation of this inequality requires the input quantum states to be Gaussian and possess identical covariance matrices. The methodology combines advanced techniques from quantum information theory, including doubling constructions and the quantum Darmois–Skitovich theorem.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous characterization of when fundamental entropy bounds are saturated in bosonic systems, linking information-theoretic inequalities directly to the Gaussian nature of the underlying quantum states, which is crucial for understanding quantum channels.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to determine the complete equality class for the bosonic quantum entropy power inequality (qEPI) for independent input states.

Main result. Equality in the qEPI (both linear and exponential forms) holds if and only if the input bosonic states are Gaussian and share the same covariance matrix.

Method. The proof uses a 'doubling construction' involving thermal auxiliary states, leading to an injectivity argument that forces the inputs to be product states, followed by applying the quantum Darmois–Skitovich theorem to establish Gaussianity.

Model / system. The system involves bosonic quantum modes (m-mode) interacting via a beam splitter with transmissivity $\eta$ ($0 < \eta < 1$), analyzing the relationship between input and output entropies.

Key observables. Von Neumann entropy ($S$), quantum relative entropy ($D$), and the entropy power inequality itself.

Important parameters / regimes. Transmissivity $\eta$ ($0 < \eta < 1$), finite mean photon number, and the covariance matrix of the input states.

Assumptions / limitations. The inputs $\rho_A, \rho_B$ are assumed to be independent, finite-energy $m$-mode states.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the 'doubled circuit' setup using beam splitters and auxiliary thermal states to facilitate the proof.

Paper structure. The paper develops the proof by first analyzing the vanishing of entropy deficits ($L_C=0$ and $L_E=0$) via a doubling construction, which implies output independence. This leads to Gaussianity via the quantum Darmois–Skitovich theorem, and finally, a central-limit argument equates the exponential and linear forms.

Abstract

The bosonic quantum entropy power inequality bounds the entropy of a beam-splitter output in terms of the input entropies. We determine its complete equality class among independent inputs of finite mean photon number: for any number of modes and any transmissivity strictly between zero and one, equality in either the linear or the exponential form holds if and only if the inputs are Gaussian with the same covariance matrix, allowing arbitrary displacements. The main step shows that equality for one output forces the two outputs to be independent: a thermal auxiliary converts equality into preservation of mutual information by a fixed noisy channel, and a two-outcome instrument shows that preservation of mutual information requires a product state with the reference. The quantum Darmois--Skitovich theorem then gives Gaussianity, while a central-limit argument reduces exponential equality to linear equality.

Exact Diagonal Completion on Reachable Subspaces: Application to QAOA Placement

Authors: Owen Friedewald, Ali Shiri Sichani, Chi-Ren Shyu

Summary. This paper develops a method called 'exact diagonal completion' to simplify quantum circuits used in QAOA for placement problems by optimizing unused diagonal operator entries. Using weighted-$\ell_1$ optimization, the authors significantly reduced gate counts in simulations, demonstrating a powerful tool for quantum circuit compilation. However, the study concludes that while the circuit optimization is effective, it does not translate into a clear, end-to-end quantum advantage over classical solvers.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on quantum computation, the techniques for optimizing circuit structure via linear programming and sparse recurrence relations are mathematically rich and could inspire novel circuit optimization techniques applicable to other quantum information problems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates optimizing quantum circuits by performing 'exact diagonal completion' on unreachable subspaces, specifically applying this to the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) for placement problems.

Main result. Weighted-$\ell_1$ completion successfully reduced Controlled-NOT (CX) counts in many tested geometries, though no end-to-end advantage was established over classical search methods in full integration.

Method. The authors construct exact Manhattan-distance operators using weighted-$\ell_1$ linear programming and a sparse recurrence relation to simplify the required quantum cost phases.

Model / system. The model involves quantum circuits used for solving placement problems, utilizing token permutation encoding and distance phases derived from weighted pairwise nets. The state preparation involves sequences of unitary operators.

Key observables. Controlled-NOT (CX) gate counts, circuit depth, and percentage reduction in gate counts compared to alternative circuit extensions.

Important parameters / regimes. The number of sites (e.g., 6, 9, or 12 sites) and the structure of the underlying geometry (e.g., 160 geometries tested).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that optimizing unspecified diagonal entries on unreachable states does not alter the ideal computation, and the structured completion methods provide exact results for certain coordinate systems.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the prepared token circuit and the gate-level expansion of its mixer block using controlled register swaps. Other figures likely compare gate counts or circuit structures across different encoding methods.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by defining the problem (QAOA placement), developing the mathematical tools (weighted-$\ell_1$ completion, sparse recurrence), demonstrating the technique's efficacy through multiple simulations (160 geometries, 60-case cohort), and concluding with an integration study (OpenROAD) showing feasibility but limited quantum advantage.

Abstract

Unused encoding states offer opportunities to simplify quantum circuits. For algorithms restricted to reachable subspaces, unspecified diagonal-operator entries can be optimized without changing ideal computation. We investigate exact diagonal completion for the quantum approximate optimization algorithm (QAOA) applied to placement, using permutation-preserving register swaps. We construct exact Manhattan-distance operators through weighted-$\ell_1$ optimization of Walsh coefficients and a sparse recurrence requiring $O(\sqrt{m})$ terms on balanced rectangles, avoiding $O(m^4)$ dense constraint storage. Across 160 geometries, weighted-$\ell_1$ completion reduces controlled-NOT (CX) counts relative to four alternative extensions in all 96 cases with unused binary codes under Gray-code synthesis. On an independently specified 60-case cohort, median reductions relative to virtual-coordinate extension are 28.0\%, 53.9\%, and 21.6\% at six, nine, and twelve sites. Under generic diagonal synthesis, reductions decrease to 10.9\%, 1.3\%, and 0.7\%, demonstrating compiler dependence. Additional ancillas reduce mixer serialization, but token circuits remain deeper than one-hot baselines. Ideal placement simulations show baseline-dependent solution quality, with classical search performing better. OpenROAD integration takes 72 QAOA and 216 classical placements across six RTL designs through clock-tree synthesis and global routing with zero overflow. Completion improves phase construction; no end-to-end advantage is established.

Exact Maximum Likelihood Decoding beyond Treewidth via Rank-Decomposition Dynamic Programming

Authors: Bin Cheng, Feng Pan
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.39556v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper presents a novel decoding algorithm, Rank-Decomposition Dynamic Programming (Rank DP), to calculate exact maximum-likelihood probabilities for quantum error correction. By exploiting the algebraic structure of the problem, it bypasses the exponential time complexity associated with standard tensor network contractions based on treewidth. This breakthrough allows for polynomial-time decoding for several important quantum codes, significantly advancing the field of quantum noise characterization.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a powerful algebraic tool (Rank DP) to tackle classically hard problems in quantum information theory, offering a path to simulating quantum error correction performance beyond the limits of standard tensor network contraction methods.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core challenge is computing the exact maximum-likelihood decoding probabilities for quantum error correction codes, which is computationally intractable due to exponential scaling with the treewidth of the associated tensor network.

Main result. The authors introduce Rank-Decomposition Dynamic Programming (Rank DP), an algorithm that evaluates the decoding partition function in time exponential only in the rank-width, which is superior to the standard treewidth-based contraction.

Method. The method reformulates the decoding problem as evaluating a partition function expressed as a quadratic sum of powers (SOPs) and applies Rank DP, which leverages the algebraic structure related to the rank-width of the Tanner graph.

Model / system. The work analyzes quantum error correction under stochastic Pauli noise, focusing on stabilizer codes like Reed-Muller and Steane-concatenated families. The complexity is analyzed using graph parameters like treewidth and rank-width.

Key observables. Decoding likelihoods (partition functions), conditional failure probabilities, and the comparison between computational complexities derived from rank-width versus treewidth.

Important parameters / regimes. The rank-width ($ ext{rw}$) of the Tanner graph, the noise model (stochastic Pauli noise), and the size of the code (e.g., up to 1,023 qubits for RM codes).

Assumptions / limitations. The approach assumes independent local fault factors for the Rank DP formulation, and the complexity bounds are derived within an arithmetic model.

Figures summary. The notes do not detail specific figures, but mention tables comparing failure probabilities and numerical enclosures for various codes and error rates.

Paper structure. The paper develops by first establishing the difficulty of exact MLD via TN contraction, then introducing the Rank DP framework by mapping the problem to SOPs, proving the complexity improvement using rank-width, and finally demonstrating its application to specific quantum codes.

Abstract

Maximum-likelihood decoding provides an optimal decoding strategy for quantum error correction under stochastic Pauli noise. However, computing logical-class probabilities is challenging, and the leading exact tensor-network contraction requires time exponential in treewidth. In this work, we introduce a new decoding algorithm based on rank-decomposition dynamic programming (Rank DP). We express decoding partition functions with independent local fault factors as quadratic sums of powers and apply Rank DP. The resulting exact evaluator has arithmetic complexity polynomial in the input size and exponential in the Tanner graph's rank-width with respect to the fault partition, including decomposition construction. After Gaussian elimination, it gives polynomial-time decoding for punctured quantum Reed-Muller codes and a Steane-concatenated family with growing distance under independent single-qubit Pauli noise. Standard contraction of the corresponding tensor networks requires superpolynomial time. A nonnegative realization gives relative floating-point error bounds under explicit arithmetic assumptions. Numerical experiments demonstrate runtime advantages over the tested tensor-network implementations on selected code-capacity and circuit-level instances, with full likelihood evaluation for Reed-Muller codes up to $1{,}023$ qubits. We further use these likelihoods to learn circuit noise parameters from syndromes, evaluate rare postselection probabilities, and quantify decoder optimality gaps. Our work opens new avenues for exploiting algebraic structure in quantum decoding and noise characterization.

Summary. This paper provides exact formulas for the spectral gaps associated with random Pauli rotations on both the special unitary group SU(d) and the projective unitary group PU(d). By combining graph theory, polynomial bounds, and representation theory, the authors disprove previous conjectures and establish rigorous bounds for quantum mixing processes.

Why it may be interesting. The precise determination of spectral gaps for random quantum circuits is crucial for understanding quantum error correction codes and the mixing time of quantum walks on quantum state spaces.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper resolves the spectral-gap problem for random Pauli rotations acting on the special unitary group SU(d) and the projective unitary group PU(d).

Abstract

We give an efficient algorithm for learning $k$-dimensional brickwork random quantum circuits using only copies of the output state obtained by applying $U$ to the all-zero input. For a depth-$d$ circuit on $n$ sites with random $2\ell$-qubit gates, the algorithm learns the original circuit $U$ with high probability in $\text{poly}(n,2^{\ell d})$ time for every constant dimensional lattice. In particular, the algorithm is polynomial time as long as $\ell d = O(\log n)$. In one dimension, this reaches the natural boundary suggested by pseudorandomness: pseudorandom states require $\ell d=ω(\log n)$, and structured cryptographic constructions suggest that this scale may be achievable from above. In higher dimensions, it remains plausible that the same $\ell d=ω(\log n)$ scale remains the threshold for ancilla-free pseudorandomness, and our results help clarify the conditions under which pseudorandomness can arise in this setting. The main idea behind our algorithm is a local correlation criterion that identifies gates in the final layer without learning their entire backward light cones, avoiding a bottleneck in the previous approaches. A key technical ingredient is a Carbery-Wright type anticoncentration inequality for low-degree polynomials of Haar random unitaries whose small-ball exponent is independent of the matrix dimension. The dimension-independent exponent is crucial for handling gates of growing locality. This anticoncentration result may also be of independent interest.

Abstract

We investigate the phase diagram and critical properties of the two-dimensional classical XY model with interactions decaying as $1/r^{2+σ}$. At the marginal case $σ=2$, we show that the low-temperature phase is characterized by logarithmic quasi-long-range order (log-QLRO), where spin correlations decay as a power of the logarithm of distance, $C(r)\sim(\ln r)^{-η_\ell}$ with $η_\ell\propto T$, and that the transition into this phase is a continuous transition beyond both the Ginzburg-Landau-Wilson paradigm and the Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless mechanism. Our analysis combines a near-exact Gaussian spin-wave theory, an adiabatic renormalization-group analysis, and large-scale Monte Carlo simulations. We find that the $1/r^4$ interaction logarithmically modifies the spin-wave stiffness kernel as $γ(k)\simeqκk^2\ln(1/k)$, which suppresses vortex proliferation and gives rise to a nonuniversal correlation-length exponent $ν\propto1/\sqrt{2πκ}$. Crucially, our simulations reveal that the short-range algebraic QLRO phase is unstable against a weak long-range perturbation for $σ\le2$. These results establish $σ=2$ as the crossover boundary between the long-range and short-range universality classes and provide a critical evaluation of recent theoretical proposals.
