Quantum Query Complexity for List Search

Authors: Niranka Banerjee, Akinori Kawachi
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.38736v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper characterizes the quantum query complexity for searching a linked list whose structure is hidden within a much larger address space. It proves that the optimal quantum complexity scales as $\Theta(\min\{\ell, (N\ell)^{1/4}\})$, demonstrating a specific regime where quantum computation offers a speedup over classical traversal time. This work precisely maps the conditions under which the ambient address space size influences the quantum search cost.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a precise, quantitative understanding of when and how quantum algorithms can achieve speedups over classical methods in structured data access problems, which has implications for quantum data structures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper analyzes the quantum query complexity for searching a hidden simple path (linked list) of length $\ell$ embedded within a large ambient address space of size $N$.

Main result. The tight quantum query complexity is $\Theta(\min\{\ell, (N\ell)^{1/4}\})$, revealing a genuine quantum advantage when the ambient space $N$ is small relative to $\ell^3$.

Method. The analysis uses the quantum query complexity model, employing both upper bounds (via quantum algorithms) and lower bounds (using the negative-weight adversary method).

Model / system. The model involves an address universe $[N]$, a public start symbol $s$, a successor oracle $f$, and a marking oracle $g$ that defines a hidden simple path structure.

Key observables. Quantum query complexity (number of oracle calls required for decision or search).

Important parameters / regimes. Ambient address space size $N$ and hidden list length $\ell$; the critical threshold is $N \sim \ell^3$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the existence of successor and marking oracles defining the list structure.

Figures summary. Figure 1 summarizes three search regimes: unstructured search (Grover), fully structured search (explicit linked list), and the intermediate hidden linked-list model.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the problem definition, derives upper bounds by combining global access to $[N]$ with local exploration, and proves lower bounds using the negative-weight adversary method for both single and double linked lists.

Abstract

Searching in a linked list is one of the most basic problems in classical algorithms. Although the nodes of the list come with memory addresses, classically those addresses play no role in the cost of search: one simply starts at the head and follows successor pointers to search for an element. In this paper, we show that the quantum setting is different. Here, the ambient address space from which the list vertices are drawn can itself affect the query complexity. We study the following problem analogous to search in a linked list in the query complexity model: the input consists of an address universe $[N]$, a public start symbol $s$, a successor oracle $f$ whose non-$\perp$ values trace a hidden simple path $s \to a_1 \to a_2 \to \cdots \to a_\ell \to \perp$, and a marking oracle $g$ that marks at most one list vertex. The task is to decide whether the list contains a marked vertex. We prove that both the decision and search versions of this problem for all $N \ge \ell \ge 1$ have quantum query complexity $Θ\!\bigl(\min\{\ell,(N\ell)^{1/4}\}\bigr)$. Thus, quite surprisingly, when $N < \ell^3$ the optimal quantum complexity is $(N\ell)^{1/4}$, which is strictly smaller than the $Θ(\ell)$ cost of ordinary linked-list traversal. This gives a precise characterization of when the ambient address space yields a genuine quantum advantage for linked-list search. We extend our results and give the same tight asymptotic bounds for the natural double linked-list version as well.

Quantum Sampling of Random Spanning Trees via Amortized Data Structures

Authors: Yassine Hamoudi, Adrian Tanasa, Shrinidhi Teganahally Sridhara
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.40314v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper presents a quantum algorithm to efficiently sample a uniform superposition over the spanning trees of a graph. It achieves a sub-linear query complexity by employing quantum walks over a sequence of slowly changing Markov chains. This work provides a quantum advantage over classical methods for graph sampling and has implications for quantum counting algorithms.

Why it may be interesting. The use of quantum walks and spectral sparsifiers to solve a complex combinatorial sampling problem demonstrates advanced techniques in quantum algorithms applicable to many physical systems modeled by graphs.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Developing an efficient quantum algorithm to generate a uniform superposition over all spanning trees of a given graph.

Main result. The authors achieve a quantum sampling complexity of $ ilde{O}(\sqrt{mn})$ queries for the q-sample, which is shown to be essentially optimal via a matching lower bound.

Method. The algorithm employs quantum walk sampling over a sequence of slowly-changing Markov chains, utilizing an amortized data structure to maintain spectral sparsifiers and leverage score samplers.

Model / system. The system is a weighted connected graph $G=(V, E, w)$ with $n$ vertices and $m$ edges. The target state is a uniform superposition over the spanning trees, $|\pi angle$.

Key observables. Query complexity, spectral gap ($\gamma_{ ext{ph}}$), and the resulting q-sample state $|\pi angle$.

Important parameters / regimes. Graph size ($n, m$), number of samples ($k$), and the required query complexity bounds ($ ilde{O}(\sqrt{mn})$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the existence of an admissible spanning tree $S$ and the ability to compute necessary spectral sparsifiers.

Paper structure. The paper builds from general quantum walk theory and spectral analysis (showing phase gap bounds) to the specific application of quantum sampling via a cooling schedule and amortized data structures.

Abstract

We present a quantum algorithm that generates a uniform superposition over the spanning trees of a graph -- also known as a q-sample -- using only a sub-linear number of queries to the graph. This goes beyond previous work that focuses on generating classical samples, including a recent quantum algorithm by Apers, Gao, Ji, and Liu [ICALP 2025]. For an $n$-vertex, $m$-edge graph, our algorithm admits a pre-processing step of $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{mn} + m^{1-δ})$, after which each q-sample can be generated in $\tilde{O}(n^{1+2δ})$ for any $δ$. Alternatively, $k$ independent q-samples can be generated at a total cost of $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{kmn})$. We also prove a matching lower bound up to logarithmic factors, showing that our algorithm is essentially optimal. Further tradeoffs are given in the paper. In comparison, the optimal classical algorithms (Anari, Liu and Vuong [FOCS 2022]) need an $\tilde{O}(m)$ pre-processing step, after which each sample costs $\tilde{O}(n)$ operations. These results yield faster quantum walk-based algorithms for counting spanning trees or finding a marked one. Our result is obtained via quantum walk sampling over a sequence of slowly-changing Markov chains. Each chain is an isotropized up-down walk that mixes rapidly to the spanning tree distribution of the input graph. A key ingredient is an amortized data structure supporting fast implementation of the associated quantum walk operators throughout the sequence. This data structure maintains access to a spectral sparsifier and a leverage score sampler that evolve with the underlying graph.

Quantum-echo Markov process for combinatorial optimization

Authors: Tatsuhiko Shirai
Type: theory · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.38943v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work introduces a quantum-echo Markov process to tackle combinatorial optimization by engineering structured transition kernels using QA or QAOA. The key insight is that optimal performance requires a balance between quantum exploration (Hamming nonlocality) and controlled energy changes (energy locality). The framework shows that combining this quantum process with classical greedy descent significantly boosts optimization capability.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a concrete framework for using finite-resource quantum dynamics to engineer structured transition kernels for optimization, linking quantum dynamics directly to classical search heuristics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops and analyzes a quantum-echo Markov process designed to efficiently explore the configuration space of combinatorial optimization problems.

Main result. The performance of the optimization hinges on balancing Hamming-space nonlocality (for exploration) and energy-space locality (for suppressing large energy jumps), with greedy descent providing crucial exploitation.

Method. The process uses quantum dynamics from QA or QAOA to engineer a transition kernel, characterized by analyzing locality in both Hamming and energy spaces.

Model / system. The model applies to general combinatorial optimization problems, using a cost function $C_z$ on binary spin configurations $z$. The dynamics are governed by quantum annealing (QA) or QAOA circuits.

Key observables. Hamming-space locality ($d_H$), Energy-space locality ($d_E$), and the effective indicator ($\xi$) quantifying energy-space correlation.

Important parameters / regimes. Annealing time ($ au$) or QAOA layers ($p$), which control the degree of quantum evolution and thus the locality structure.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is performed in the finite-resource regime, and various approximations (like Random-Phase Approximation) are used to calculate expectation values.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates desired move types: nonlocal in Hamming space but local in energy space, contrasting with trapped or purely random moves.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the quantum-echo process, defines locality measures in both spaces, analyzes how QA/QAOA dynamics generate specific locality structures, and finally proposes and evaluates the combined quantum-echo local optimization with greedy descent.

Abstract

We introduce a quantum-echo Markov process for combinatorial optimization. Quantum dynamics based on quantum annealing (QA) or the quantum approximate optimization algorithm (QAOA) is used to engineer the transition kernel. Increasing the annealing time in QA or the number of layers in QAOA enables transitions to explore distant configurations while suppressing large energy changes. The Hamming-space delocalization originates from operator spreading, whereas the energy-space localization arises from a dynamically generated correlation. For optimization, we propose quantum-echo local optimization with and without greedy descent. We find that its performance is governed by the interplay between Hamming-space nonlocality and energy-space locality, and that excessive energy-space localization can degrade optimization performance. Incorporating greedy descent substantially improves the performance, highlighting the complementary roles of quantum dynamics for exploration and greedy descent for exploitation. These results establish quantum-echo dynamics as a framework for engineering structured transition kernels and provide a route to using finite-resource quantum many-body dynamics as a computational primitive for iterative optimization.

Randomness of exact unitary designs under symmetry

Authors: Christopher Vairogs, Felix Leditzky
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.40227v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical paper analyzes how physical symmetries constrain the randomness of unitary operations in quantum systems. It introduces the concept of 'symmetric design strength,' showing that this strength can exceed the standard unitary design strength. This has implications for designing quantum protocols that must respect underlying symmetries while maintaining high levels of randomness.

Why it may be interesting. The concept of 'symmetric design strength' provides a powerful tool to quantify how much randomness is preserved in quantum protocols when physical symmetries are imposed, which is crucial for understanding noise resilience and resource estimation in quantum computation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the relationship between the unitary design strength and the symmetry-constrained (symmetric) design strength of finite unitary ensembles.

Main result. The authors prove that for high dimensions and broad symmetries, there exist ensembles whose symmetric design strength can be strictly higher than their unitary design strength.

Method. The analysis uses concepts from representation theory and the definition of statistical moments (t-designs) over unitary groups, comparing integrals over the full group versus the symmetry-compatible sub-ensemble.

Model / system. The model involves finite unitary ensembles (subsets of U(H)) acted upon by a symmetry group S. The core mathematical objects are the unitary design strength (tmax) and the symmetric design strength (t_sym).

Key observables. Unitary design strength (tmax) and symmetric design strength (t_sym), which are maximal integers quantifying moment matching to the Haar measure.

Important parameters / regimes. The dimension of the Hilbert space (H), the structure of the symmetry group S (e.g., U(1), SU(d)), and the weighting probability distribution p of the ensemble.

Assumptions / limitations. The results often rely on the assumption of sufficiently high dimension and the existence of symmetries satisfying specific representation-theoretic conditions.

Paper structure. The paper develops definitions for both design strengths, proves the existence of ensembles where t_sym > tmax, and derives specific bounds for ensembles forming finite groups under global symmetries.

Abstract

We define the unitary design strength of a finite unitary ensemble to be the maximal integer $t$ for which its $t$-th statistical moment is identical to that of the unitary group. In the presence of physical symmetry, not all operators of an ensemble are symmetry-compatible. This motivates us to define the symmetric design strength of a finite unitary ensemble to be the maximal integer $t$ for which the $t$-th moments of its symmetry-compatible sub-ensemble match those of the group of symmetry-compatible unitaries. We show that in sufficiently high dimension and for a broad family of symmetries, which includes the global on-site $\mathrm{U}(1)$ and $\mathrm{SU}(d)$ symmetries, there exist finite unitary ensembles of arbitrarily high design strength whose symmetric design strength is strictly higher. Ensembles with this property may be more versatile under symmetry constraints for protocols that rely on unitary randomization than they are without symmetry. Our results extend the previous work [Mitsuhashi and Yoshioka, PRX Quantum 4.4 (Nov. 2023)] showing that the symmetric design strength of the Clifford group is strictly upper bounded by its unitary design strength for symmetries that do not trivialize the symmetry-compatible unitaries. Due to the special significance of unitary designs with a group structure, we also derive a variety of bounds on the unitary and symmetric design strengths of finite unitary ensembles whose operators form a group. In particular, we provide a simple proof that an arbitrary ensemble of operators forming a finite group must have a symmetric design strength of at most two under the global on-site $\mathrm{U}(1)$ and $\mathrm{SU}(d)$ symmetries. Along the way, we show that there exist uniformly weighted groups of arbitrarily high symmetric design strength in arbitrary dimension and analyze their structure.

Rate 1/5 Non-Malleable Codes against Entangled Split-State Tampering

Authors: Naresh Goud Boddu
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.39589v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops a new construction for non-malleable quantum codes, proving they can securely encode classical messages at a rate approaching 1/5. It achieves this by modifying existing constructions to provide worst-case security guarantees against complex, entangled quantum tampering attacks. The methodology involves advanced quantum information theory tools to rigorously bound the information leakage during the attack.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete, high-rate bound for quantum information security primitives (NMCs) against sophisticated, entangled attacks, which is foundational for quantum cryptography and secure quantum communication protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the construction of efficient information-theoretic non-malleable codes (NMCs) for classical messages that are secure against two noncommunicating local quantum tampering operations with arbitrary pre-shared entanglement.

Main result. The authors establish a worst-case security guarantee for such codes, achieving a rate of at least $1/5 - \xi$ for sufficiently large share lengths $n$. This resolves the constant-rate question for worst-case classical messages in the entangled two-split-state model.

Method. The construction builds upon prior work using a permutation-based two-split structure, modifying it by concatenating the message with fresh uniform padding to achieve worst-case security. The security proof involves complex comparisons between actual and ideal quantum states using CPTP maps.

Model / system. The model involves encoding a classical message into a quantum state structured in a 'two-split-state' form, which is then subjected to local quantum tampering operations. Security is quantified by bounding the trace distance between the actual and ideal states.

Key observables. Rate ($\geq 1/5 - \xi$), Error ($2^{-n\Omega(1)}$), and the trace distance ($\Delta(\mu, \sigma)$) quantifying security against tampering.

Important parameters / regimes. The rate $1/5$, the small positive constant $\xi$, and the large share length $n$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on techniques like the 'scalar mean-one lemma' in the quantum setting and the ability to replace maximum loss with an average deficit through padding randomness.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the setup involving two local quantum channels and the message-selection step, comparing state evolution ($ ho_0 o \mu Z \Lambda E_2$) and detailing the process of computing and normalizing the selected message state.

Paper structure. The paper develops the security proof by first analyzing the classical adversary using averaging over source-independent tapes, and then extending this to the quantum adversary by comparing actual and ideal quantum states using CPTP maps and smoothing techniques.

Abstract

We construct efficient information-theoretic non-malleable codes for classical messages that are secure against two noncommunicating local quantum tampering operations with arbitrary pre-shared entanglement. For every sufficiently small fixed $ξ>0$ and all sufficiently large first-share lengths $n$, the codes have rate at least $1/5-ξ$, perfect correctness, and error $2^{-n^{Ω(1)}}$. Security holds for every message, with a single message-independent simulator for each attack. This resolves the constant-rate question for worst-case classical messages in the entangled two-split-state model. Our construction builds on the permutation-based two-split construction of Batra, Boddu, and Jain, which achieves rate approaching $1/5$ for uniformly random messages. We retain their architecture but replace the uniform message input to the permutation with a prescribed message concatenated with fresh uniform padding. Our main contribution is a worst-case security reduction for this modification.

Reducing the Entanglement Cost of Distributed Bipartite Quantum Computation with Constant Qubit Overhead

Authors: Kosuke Matsui, Jun-Yi Wu, Min-Hsiu Hsieh, Mio Murao
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.39865v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper tackles the resource optimization challenge in distributed quantum computing by minimizing entanglement consumption. It shows that for Clifford unitaries, the minimum entanglement cost is achievable while keeping the required auxiliary qubits constant. For more complex unitaries, it provides a tight upper bound on the excess entanglement cost relative to the theoretical minimum.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides concrete, resource-efficient protocols for large-scale quantum computation, directly impacting the feasibility of networking quantum processors.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses how to minimize the entanglement cost required for distributed bipartite quantum computation while strictly limiting the number of auxiliary qubits available at each processing unit.

Main result. They prove that the theoretical lower bound on entanglement cost is attainable for all bipartite Clifford unitaries using only two auxiliary qubits per QPU, and provide a tight bound for non-Clifford unitaries.

Method. The authors utilize the operator Schmidt rank ($ ext{OpSch}$) as a lower bound and construct explicit quantum circuit implementations based on decomposing the target unitary into elementary blocks.

Model / system. The model is distributed quantum computation connecting multiple Quantum Processing Units (QPUs) via quantum communication, analyzed in an entanglement-assisted setting using shared Bell pairs.

Key observables. Entanglement Cost (measured in Bell pairs), Qubit Overhead (number of auxiliary qubits required), and the Operator Schmidt Rank ($ ext{OpSch}(U)$).

Important parameters / regimes. The T-count ($t$) for non-Clifford unitaries, and the constant overhead limit (e.g., $\le (2, 2)$ qubits).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes perfect Bell pairs and focuses on exact deterministic implementations of the quantum gates.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the implementation of Clifford unitaries using specific Bell pairs and qubit overheads, showing decompositions into elementary blocks.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the general lower bound using $ ext{OpSch}(U)$, then proves attainability for Clifford unitaries with constant overhead, and finally derives an upper bound for non-Clifford unitaries based on their T-count.

Abstract

Distributed quantum computation connects multiple quantum processing units (QPUs) through quantum communication to jointly perform large-scale quantum computations. Since the number of qubits available at each QPU is limited, it is important to reduce quantum communication while keeping the qubit overhead small. To this end, we study an entanglement-assisted model, in which entanglement consumption serves as a measure of quantum communication cost. For exact deterministic implementations in this model, the operator Schmidt rank of the target bipartite unitary provides a general lower bound on entanglement cost when qubit overhead is unrestricted. However, it has remained unclear how closely this bound can be approached with constant qubit overhead. In this work, we show that this lower bound is attainable for every bipartite Clifford unitary using at most two auxiliary qubits per QPU. In addition, for non-Clifford unitaries specified by exact Clifford+$T$ decompositions with $T$-count $t$, we construct implementations with the same qubit overhead whose entanglement cost is at most $2t$ Bell pairs above this lower bound.

Relative entropy of entanglement and tripartite minimal surface

Authors: Takato Mori, Beni Yoshida
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.38815v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work establishes a holographic duality connecting the relative entropy of entanglement for tripartite states to the geometry of minimal surfaces in the bulk spacetime. By applying this to random tensor networks, the authors derive a leading-order formula relating entanglement to the minimal tripartition surface area. This connects advanced quantum information theory measures to concepts from general relativity.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a concrete, geometric realization of a multipartite entanglement measure (REE) using tools from quantum gravity (minimal surfaces), which is highly relevant for understanding entanglement structure in complex quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper proposes a holographic dual for the relative entropy of entanglement (REE) for a tripartite quantum state ($ ho_{ABC}$) defined on a boundary.

Main result. The leading order holographic dual relates the REE to the difference between the minimal bulk tripartition surface area ($ ext{Area}(\Gamma_{\min})$) and the minimal surface homologous to a bipartite cut ($ ext{Area}(\gamma_{AB})$).

Method. The proof involves establishing upper and lower bounds by constructing specific separable states associated with the minimal surface and by bounding the maximal tripartite product-state overlap.

Model / system. The analysis is performed using Random Tensor Networks (RTNs) as a toy model for holographic boundary theories, where the entanglement structure is encoded in the geometry of the bulk minimal surfaces.

Key observables. Relative Entropy of Entanglement ($ ext{ER}( ho_{AB})$), Minimal bulk tripartition surface area ($ ext{Area}(\Gamma_{\min})$), and minimal surface homologous to $AB$ ($ ext{Area}(\gamma_{AB})$).

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis is conducted in the large-$n$ limit, where edge lengths $\ell_e$ scale linearly with $n$.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is the validity of the holographic correspondence relating entanglement measures to geometric minimal surfaces, and the use of RTNs as a tractable model.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the geometric relationship between the minimal tripartition surface and standard RT surfaces for bipartite cuts. Figure 2 shows a Haar random tensor network with an embedded bulk tripartition $\Gamma$.

Paper structure. The paper develops the holographic proposal, rigorously proves the upper and lower bounds for random tensor networks, and discusses the geometric interpretation of the minimal tripartition surface, drawing analogies to replica action minimization.

Abstract

We propose a holographic dual of the relative entropy of entanglement for a boundary tripartition $A:B:C$. Our proposal is that, at the leading order, \begin{align} E_R(ρ_{AB}) = \frac{1}{4G_N}\Big[\mathrm{Area}(Γ_{\min}) - \mathrm{Area}(γ_{AB})\Big] \notag \end{align} where $Γ_{\min}$ is the minimal bulk tripartition surface and $γ_{AB}$ is the minimal surface homologous to $AB$. For a general random tensor network, we prove the corresponding upper bound by constructing a separable state associated with $Γ_{\min}$ and evaluating its relative entropy. We also establish the matching lower bound rigorously for networks of one, two, and three Haar random tensors by bounding the maximal tripartite product-state overlap. In appropriate geometries, the minimal tripartition surface can form a nontrivial tri-junction resembling the Mercedes logo. The proof proceeds by sequential optimization over candidate product states, which has a natural geometric interpretation as a local search for the minimal tripartition surface.

Resonances control when multiterminal Josephson currents reduce to two-terminal couplings

Authors: A. Barış Özgüler
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.40346v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work analyzes how well multi-terminal Josephson currents can be described by simple two-terminal couplings. The authors find that the error is dominated by a specific normal-region electronic mode. By understanding and detuning this mode using a gate voltage, they demonstrate a mechanism to restore high accuracy in the description of these complex superconducting junctions.

Why it may be interesting. The identification of a specific normal-state mode that dictates the deviation from simple pairwise coupling provides a powerful, experimentally verifiable control mechanism for superconducting quantum devices, linking normal-state properties to superconducting transport.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the accuracy of approximating complex Josephson currents in multi-terminal junctions using only pairwise (two-terminal) coupling terms, and identifies the physical mechanism responsible for any resulting error.

Main result. The error is controlled by a specific normal-region mode near the Fermi level; detuning this mode with a gate voltage can suppress the nonpairwise currents, restoring high accuracy.

Method. The study uses a microscopic scattering model and compares exact supercurrents derived from the Bogoliubov-de Gennes formalism against the best pairwise approximation.

Model / system. The system is a planar, multi-terminal Josephson junction (weak link coupling three or more superconductors) modeled on a tight-binding lattice. The physics is governed by superconducting proximity effects and Andreev bound states.

Key observables. Supercurrents, energy variation, current error percentage, and the gate dependence of the error.

Important parameters / regimes. Number of terminals (up to sixteen), gate detuning ($\delta$), and the superconducting gap ($\Delta$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on comparing the exact solution to the best pairwise approximation, and the identification of the error mechanism is tied to specific normal-state modes.

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically compares exact vs. pairwise energy and current for a three-terminal junction, showing the error dependence on gate detuning.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by establishing the theoretical framework for multi-terminal currents, quantifying the error between exact and pairwise models, identifying the specific normal-mode mechanism causing the error, and finally demonstrating how gate tuning can suppress this error, confirming the theoretical predictions.

Abstract

Multiterminal Josephson junctions, in which one weak link couples three or more superconductors, are studied as hosts of topological Andreev bands, multi-pair supercurrents and tunable circuit elements. Their currents are modeled both as pairwise networks of two-terminal couplings and through genuinely multiterminal processes such as quartets. We ask how accurate the pairwise description is and what controls its error. In a microscopic scattering model of planar junctions, we compare exact currents with the best pairwise approximation, which allows arbitrary nonsinusoidal couplings. In ensembles of disordered three- and four-terminal junctions, the pairwise terms capture nearly all of the energy variation, yet the median current error ranges from 8.0 to 21.2 percent, about twice the energy error, because currents weigh the multiterminal harmonics more strongly. Designed devices extend the comparison to sixteen terminals. We identify a mechanism that controls this error. A normal-region mode near the Fermi level that couples to three or more terminals produces large nonpairwise currents, and detuning it with a gate suppresses them. Shifting only this mode, selected from normal-state properties, predicts the gate dependence of the error in eight three- and four-terminal devices without fitting currents. Finite-gap calculations in two clean devices confirm a drop from tens of percent near resonance to below one percent. An analytic single-level model gives a sufficient detuning for pairwise accuracy at any terminal count. Planar junctions thus inherit the resonant and cotunneling regimes known from quantum dots, and normal-state properties identify the mode that selects between them.

Resource-Tunable Quantum Circuit Implementation of Nonlinear Element-Wise Transformations

Authors: Chunlin Yang, Yuqi Li, Hongmei Yao, Zhaobing Fan

Summary. This paper presents a resource-tunable quantum framework for implementing element-wise nonlinear transformations by approximating them with polynomials. It significantly improves upon existing methods by decomposing the polynomial into factors, allowing for a controlled trade-off between circuit depth and query complexity. The results demonstrate highly parallel constructions that approach the theoretical query lower bounds for computing these complex matrix functions.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the resource scaling limitations in quantum machine learning algorithms that require nonlinear activation functions. The comparison between different circuit constructions (LCU vs. binary-tree) provides concrete, quantifiable improvements in depth and gate count.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core problem is efficiently implementing element-wise nonlinear transformations, $f(A)$, on quantum-encoded data by approximating the function with a polynomial $P(x)$. The challenge is reducing the circuit depth, which is typically linear in the polynomial degree, to achieve better resource scaling.

Main result. The authors propose a resource-tunable quantum framework that decomposes the high-degree polynomial into multiple lower-degree factors, allowing a trade-off between query complexity, circuit depth, and ancilla count. This framework achieves a depth scaling of $O(n+d)$ or better, matching the query lower bound in certain regimes.

Method. The method involves polynomial approximation (via Mergelyan's theorem) followed by decomposing the resulting polynomial into factors. These factors are then implemented using parallel Hadamard products and controlled unitaries, comparing the efficiency of binary-tree versus LCU-based constructions.

Model / system. The model is the quantum circuit implementation of a Quantum Hadamard Matrix Function (QHMF), $P(A)$, where $A$ is an $n$-qubit matrix representing the input data. The focus is on the algorithmic structure required to compute this function efficiently.

Key observables. Query complexity ($Q_l^q$), circuit depth ($D$), size ($S$), ancilla count ($N$), and the total approximation/implementation error ($\epsilon$).

Important parameters / regimes. The degree of the polynomial ($2d-1$), the number of factors ($m$), and the system size ($n$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on prior results regarding the block-encoding of matrix powers ($A^{\circ k}$) and the decomposition of complex controlled gates into simpler components.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate quantum circuits for the Hadamard product of two or multiple matrices, the block encoding of the all-ones matrix, and the final circuits for the factorization-based QHMF and the LCU-based QHMF.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the problem (nonlinear transformations), then detailing the factorization approach (using Hadamard products), and finally comparing the resource costs and complexities of different advanced implementations, such as the LCU-based method versus the binary-tree method.

Abstract

Nonlinear transformations are important in quantum machine learning and other quantum numerical applications, which can be reduced to the implementation of element-wise polynomials through polynomial approximation. Existing works establish the feasibility of transformations and improve query or auxiliary-space efficiency, but circuit depth remains linear in scale with degree. In this work, we develop a resource-tunable quantum framework that decomposes a $(2^d-1)$-degree polynomial into $m$ lower-degree factors, with $m$ controlling the degree of parallelism. By varying $m$ from $1$ to $2^d-1$, the framework provides a trade-off among query complexity, additional gate complexity, ancilla count, and normalization, ranging from a binary-tree implementation to a $\mathcal{O}(n+d)$-depth construction with highly parallel oracle queries that match the query lower bound. The effects of polynomial approximation and implementation errors are further analyzed. We demonstrate the framework for nonlinear activation functions such as sigmoid and tanh, as well as pixel-wise image transformations.

Summary. This work numerically studies the scalability of variational quantum algorithms for combinatorial optimization problems. This validates the potential of VQAs for large-scale quantum computation.

Why it may be interesting. The use of the matchgate ansatz, which is related to free evolution of 1+1D transversal Ising models, provides a concrete, hardware-implementable quantum circuit structure for benchmarking quantum algorithms.

Quantum Random Access Memory (qRAM) is a critical component for achieving quantum advantage in algorithms ranging from database search to quantum machine learning. In a recently introduced model [arXiv:2508.02855], we proposed a resource-efficient qRAM architecture based on discrete-time quantum walkers. This article serves as a comprehensive technical follow-up, providing the full mathematical derivations, detailed protocol specifications, and in-depth resource analysis. Moreover, we extend the original proposal with novel techniques for the purpose of making the qRAM implementation more realistic. Our model resolves the primary drawbacks of leading qRAM proposals: it avoids the exponential number of active nodes $\mathcal{O}(2^n)$ required by the "Bucket Brigade" architecture by employing a number of quantum walkers that scales linearly with the address and message sizes, $n$ and $m$, respectively. Simultaneously, it overcomes the spatial bottlenecks of previous quantum-walker schemes by eliminating the need for multiple parallel trees. We propose two algorithmic paradigms: the long- and the short-range approaches, which differ by the length of interaction of the main routing gates employed in the qRAM. We formalize the routing, message-copy, and walker retrieval phases for both variants and we show how, while the long-range scheme employs controlled gates having a multitude of target systems, the short-range approach decomposes these interactions into sequences of 2- and 3-body local gates, which improves the architecture's feasibility for near-term experimental implementation. Finally, our comprehensive resource analysis confirms that the short-range approach achieves the optimal $\mathcal{O}(n+m)$ circuit depth. Within these two paradigms, we explore the potential of different types of quantum walkers, namely bosons, dual-rail qubits and four-level qudits.

The Generalized Semi-Clifford Conjecture Holds at Level 4

Authors: Maxwell Marcus, Sathyawageeswar Subramanian, Marcel Dall'Agnol
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.38751v1

Summary. This paper proves a major result concerning the structure of quantum gates in the Clifford hierarchy up to level 4. By introducing and analyzing 'conjugation groups,' the authors use advanced group theory and fixed-point theorems to show that these gates must possess a simple, diagonal structure (generalized semi-Clifford). This advances the mathematical understanding of gate sets crucial for quantum computation.

Why it may be interesting. This work is highly relevant as it provides deep structural characterizations of quantum gates within the context of fault-tolerant quantum computation, directly impacting the understanding of gate sets usable in quantum error correction.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to prove the Generalized Semi-Clifford Conjecture for quantum gates belonging to the Clifford hierarchy up to level $k=4$. This conjecture characterizes gates that are structurally simple, being equivalent to a permutation times a diagonal matrix up to Clifford corrections.

Main result. The authors prove that for prime dimension $d$ and level $k \le 4$, every gate in the Clifford hierarchy $C_k$ is generalized semi-Clifford. This extends previous results for $k=3$ and $d=2$.

Method. The proof centers on analyzing inductively defined 'conjugation groups' $\Gamma_j(U)$ associated with the gate $U$. By showing these groups are finite $d$-groups and applying fixed-point theorems to their action on Lagrangian subspaces, they establish the required structural properties.

Model / system. The system involves quantum gates $U$ acting on $n$-qudits of prime dimension $d$. The mathematical framework utilizes the Clifford hierarchy $C_k$, Pauli matrices, and the associated Pauli Maximal Abelian Subalgebras (MASAs) defined by Lagrangian subspaces.

Key observables. The primary observables are the structure of the conjugation groups $\Gamma_j(U)$ and the existence of fixed points under their action on the set of Lagrangian subspaces $\mathcal{L}$.

Important parameters / regimes. The key parameters are the level $k$ of the hierarchy ($k=4$ is the focus), the prime dimension $d$, and the number of qudits $n$.

Assumptions / limitations. The proof relies on the assumption that $\Gamma_{k-2}(U) \subseteq C_2$ to establish the necessary structural properties for the fixed-point arguments to hold.

Paper structure. The paper builds upon prior results, first establishing the general method using conjugation groups $\Gamma_j(U)$. It then proves the theorem for $k=4$ by showing that the action of $\Gamma_2(U)$ on the space of Lagrangians yields a fixed point, thereby proving the conjecture for $k \le 4$.

Abstract

The Clifford hierarchy $\mathsf{C}_1 \subset \mathsf{C}_2 \subset \cdots$ was introduced by Gottesman and Chuang (arXiv:quant-ph/9908010) to characterize gates that admit fault-tolerant implementation by gate teleportation. Yet, despite its rich mathematical structure and the attention it has received in recent years, little is known about $\mathsf{C}_k$ for $k > 3$. Most progress has focused on identifying structural properties of restrictions of the hierarchy, such as diagonal gates and gates on systems of small dimension $d$ or with few qudits. The generalized semi-Clifford conjecture, proposed by Zeng et al. (arXiv:0712.2084), states that every gate in $\cup_k \mathsf{C}_k$ is, up to multiplication by Cliffords, the product of a permutation and a diagonal matrix. Beigi and Shor proved the case $d=2$, $k=3$ (arXiv:0810.5108) and Pllaha et al. found an alternative proof by exploiting fixed points of the conjugation map induced by (a Clifford correction of) $U \in \mathsf{C}_3$ on the span of maximal stabilizer subgroups (arXiv:2006.14040). By extending their fixed-point arguments to the group $Γ_1(U)$ generated by $U \mathsf{P} U^\dagger$ and beyond, we prove the conjecture for $k \leq 4$ and any prime dimension $d$. Our proof centers on conjugation groups $Γ_1(U), Γ_2(U), \ldots$ of $U \in \mathsf{C}_k$, which we expect to be a useful tool in the study of the Clifford hierarchy more generally. We also show a natural sufficient condition on such groups for gates in higher levels to be generalized semi-Clifford.
