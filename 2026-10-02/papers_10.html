Distillation of N-Qubit Stabilizer States on a Star Network Topology

Authors: Theodore M. Mahaffey, Chaohan Cui, Saikat Guha, Murphy Yuezhen Niu
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.39916v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work introduces a scalable entanglement distillation protocol for N-qubit stabilizer states, specifically targeting GHZ states, operating over a star network. By leveraging the stabilizer formalism, the authors generalize previous techniques to cover any CSS stabilizer state. This is crucial for distributing high-fidelity quantum resources across multiple, potentially distant quantum processors.

Why it may be interesting. The generalization of distillation protocols to arbitrary CSS stabilizer states and the explicit formulation on a star network topology provide powerful, general tools for quantum network resource management.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops an entanglement distillation protocol to convert multiple noisy copies of an N-qubit Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger (GHZ) state into fewer, higher-fidelity logical copies.

Main result. The protocol is shown to be generally applicable to any N-qubit Calderbank-Shor-Steane (CSS) stabilizer state and is efficiently scalable on a star network topology.

Method. The scheme is formulated using the stabilizer formalism, relying on local operations and classical communication (LOCC) to perform syndrome measurements and error correction.

Model / system. The system involves N parties sharing multiple copies of an N-qubit stabilizer state, utilizing an arbitrary $[[n, k, d]]$ stabilizer code structure.

Key observables. Final GHZ state fidelity, success probability $p(N)_{ ext{succ}}$, and the structure of the post-measurement stabilizer group $G'$.

Important parameters / regimes. Depolarizing noise rates (up to or above 6%), the number of qubits N, and the code parameters ($n, k, d$).

Assumptions / limitations. The protocol assumes the initial state is an N-qubit stabilizer state and that local operations and classical communication are noiseless.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the GHZ distillation scheme on a star topology for small N, contrasting party-major and copy-major direct sums for parity check matrices.

Paper structure. The paper builds from defining the problem on a star network, detailing the use of the stabilizer formalism for state evolution, proving the general applicability to CSS states, and deriving the success probability for local QEC.

Abstract

We introduce an entanglement distillation protocol that utilizes an arbitrary $[[n,k,d]]$ stabilizer code to convert $n$ raw copies of an $N$-qubit Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger (GHZ) state into $k$ logical copies in the presence of Pauli noise. Our explicit formulation of the scheme on a star network topology is efficiently scalable to arbitrary $N$, requires only local operations, and minimizes the impact of errors on idle physical qubits, enabling the distribution of high-fidelity entangled logical states between any number of quantum processors. We report the results of numerical experiments using the 5-qubit code, toric code, and $[[144,12,12]]$ bivariate bicyle code to protect against independent single-qubit depolarizing noise on all qubits. Our encoding scheme improves the final GHZ state fidelity relative to a single bare GHZ state sent over the same noisy channel for depolarizing rates up to or above $6 \%$ for all codes tested. Additionally, we use the stabilizer formalism to prove that our protocol can be applied to any $N$-qubit Calderbank-Shor-Steane (CSS) stabilizer state, from which the GHZ state emerges as a special case. This result explicitly establishes the working principle of previous stabilizer-based Bell pair and GHZ state distillation schemes, and generalizes such protocols beyond the traditionally considered states. We also show that some stabilizer codes can distill any stabilizer state with our protocol.

Effective Contact Theory for Exotic Loosely Bound States in Strongly Interacting Expanding Matter

Authors: Xiaofeng Wang, Zebo Tang, Zhangbu Xu, Chi Yang, Wangmei Zha
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.38579v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5

Summary. This work introduces an effective contact theory to unify the description of exotic bound states (like Kmu atoms) and continuum particle correlations in the extreme environment of heavy-ion collisions. By replacing sharp cutoffs with a regulated kernel, the theory posits that both observables stem from a single underlying two-particle density matrix. This establishes a powerful, general methodology for analyzing strongly interacting, expanding matter.

Why it may be interesting. The concept of relating bound states and continuum correlations via a universal contact term is highly relevant to non-equilibrium many-body physics, echoing ideas used in quantum optics and open quantum systems where short-range interactions dominate.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops a theory to relate the production of loosely bound states to continuum two-particle correlations in strongly interacting, expanding matter, addressing the challenge of scale separation.

Main result. The effective contact theory unifies bound and continuum observables as projections of a common two-particle density matrix, providing a general framework for coalescence and correlation studies.

Method. It replaces the hard momentum cutoff of conventional coalescence models with a regulated Bethe-Peierls kernel, derived from an effective contact theory based on Tan's universal relations.

Model / system. The study uses relativistic heavy-ion collisions (like STAR) as the physical laboratory, focusing specifically on the analysis of Coulomb-bound Kmu atoms.

Key observables. Atom yield, two-particle correlations (e.g., STAR dLambda correlations), and the common two-particle density matrix.

Important parameters / regimes. Localization momentum scale ($p_0$), source scale ($\Lambda_{fo}$), and the effective contact residue ($C$).

Assumptions / limitations. The core assumption is that bound and continuum populations originate from projections of a single, common two-particle density matrix at freeze-out.

Paper structure. The paper builds from the general concept of Tan's contact to develop the effective theory, applying it first to the idealized case of Kmu atoms, and finally testing the framework using continuum correlation data from heavy-ion collisions.

Abstract

Short-distance correlations generate universal relations that can be independent of microscopic details. Tan's contact is a prominent example, connecting close pairs, high-momentum constituents, and bound-state formation across atomic, condensed-matter, and nuclear systems. Whether an analogous universality governs the nonequilibrium freeze-out of relativistic QCD matter is an open question. We develop an effective contact theory that relates the production of loosely bound states to continuum two-particle correlations in heavy-ion collisions. The construction replaces the hard relative-momentum cutoff of conventional coalescence by a regulated Bethe--Peierls kernel: freeze-out localization fixes its momentum scale, while the composite yield fixes its contact residue. Bound and continuum observables then become projections of a common two-particle density matrix rather than independent phenomena. Coulomb-bound $Kμ$ atoms provide an ideal realization because their atomic, production, and source scales are widely separated. A contact-theory analysis of STAR $dΛ$ correlations supplies a first bound--continuum consistency test and demonstrates how inferred near-threshold parameters can depend on the finite expanding source. The framework establishes a general strategy for relating coalescence, correlations, and exotic bound states in strongly interacting expanding matter.

Entropy threshold: A simple proxy for performance of quantum error correction

Authors: Diego Ruiz, Aleksander Kubica

Summary. This work introduces an entropy-based heuristic to predict the performance threshold of quantum error correction codes. By comparing the entropy generated by physical noise against the information extracted via syndrome measurements, the authors provide a simple, generalizable tool. This method successfully predicts thresholds across diverse code types and noise models, offering significant phenomenological insight into QEC performance.

Why it may be interesting. The development of simple, generalizable heuristics that match complex simulation results provides powerful, 'back-of-the-envelope' tools for assessing QEC feasibility without running massive simulations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to develop simple, predictive heuristics for determining the performance threshold of various Quantum Error Correction (QEC) protocols, circumventing the intractability of exact analytical calculations.

Main result. An entropy-based proxy successfully captures the main trends across diverse QEC settings, yielding threshold estimates in good agreement with computationally intensive numerical simulations.

Method. The core method involves developing an entropy decomposition that locally compares the noise entropy (from physical errors) with the syndrome entropy (information gained from stabilizer measurements).

Model / system. The analysis applies to various QEC codes, including Clifford-deformed surface codes (XY, XZZX) and color codes, tested under different noise models such as biased Pauli noise, erasure noise, and circuit-level noise.

Key observables. Threshold ($p_{th}$), Noise Entropy ($H(E)$), Syndrome Entropy ($H(S)$), and Logical Error Rate ($p_L$).

Important parameters / regimes. Physical error rate ($p$), noise bias ($\eta$), and code size ($n$).

Assumptions / limitations. The proxy neglects correlations between stabilizer outcomes and the contribution from code degeneracy, and for soft information, it relies on modeling measurement outcomes with Gaussian distributions.

Figures summary. Figures compare the entropy threshold lines derived from the proxy against numerical results for surface and color codes under various noise conditions (e.g., Fig. 1 and Fig. 3).

Paper structure. The paper introduces the entropy-based proxy by deriving bounds on the logical error rate using conditional entropy, then applies this heuristic to various code types and noise models, culminating in discussions on incorporating soft information.

Abstract

The rapidly growing landscape of quantum error-correction (QEC) protocols has produced a wealth of numerical data, but comparatively few heuristics for understanding and predicting their performance. Here, we develop a simple entropy-based proxy that predicts the thresholds of a variety of QEC protocols, ranging from the code-capacity setting of Clifford-deformed surface codes with biased Pauli or erasure noise to the circuit-level noise model of the surface or color codes with flag qubits. Our proxy estimates the threshold by locally comparing the noise entropy with the error information gained via stabilizer measurements (or spacetime detectors and flag outcomes in the circuit-level settings). Despite neglecting correlations between stabilizer outcomes and the contribution from code degeneracy, the proxy captures the main trends across diverse settings and yields threshold estimates in good agreement with numerical results. Our work develops much-needed phenomenology that enables simple back-of-the-envelope estimates of QEC performance, recovering and providing an explanation for the results of computationally intensive detailed simulations.

Exponential strong converse for blind quantum data compression

Authors: Kohdai Kuroiwa
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.38328v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work rigorously establishes exponential strong converse theorems for blind quantum data compression of mixed-state sources. It resolves the difficulty of analyzing mixed states by introducing a new overlap quantity that connects source information preservation to the transmitted system dimension. The results provide sharp fundamental limits for quantum communication tasks.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on quantum information theory, the rigorous analysis of structural decomposition (KI) and the establishment of fundamental limits (strong converse) provide powerful mathematical tools applicable to characterizing resource constraints in complex quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper establishes exponential strong converse theorems for the blind quantum data compression of finite-dimensional mixed-state sources, considering both unassisted and entanglement-assisted scenarios.

Main result. It proves that for general mixed-state sources, the compression fidelity decays exponentially with the block length when the rate falls below the optimal threshold, resolving a long-standing open question in the field.

Method. The proof introduces a new KI overlap quantity and combines it with fidelity bounds derived from analyzing the kernel of an operator $L_\Phi$, leading to exponential decay estimates.

Model / system. The model involves quantum data compression of mixed-state sources $\Phi = \{(p_x, ho_x)\}_{x \in \Sigma}$ where the sender (Alice) does not know the classical label $x$. The analysis uses the Koashi–Imoto (KI) decomposition to characterize the source structure.

Key observables. Compression Fidelity ($F_n$), achievable rate region $(R, E)$, and the KI overlap quantity.

Important parameters / regimes. Compression rate ($R$), entanglement assistance ($E$), and the block length ($n$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the structural decomposition provided by the KI decomposition and the existence of informationally complete measurements.

Paper structure. The paper builds from preliminaries defining the rate region via KI decomposition, then proves the unassisted exponential strong converse, followed by the result for entanglement assistance, and finally combining both bounds.

Abstract

We establish exponential strong converse theorems for blind quantum data compression of finite-dimensional mixed-state sources, with and without entanglement assistance. Blind compression is a fundamental quantum information processing task in which the sender aims to transmit quantum states without direct access to the classical label. Strong converse is a notable aspect of data compression: the accuracy of the compression abruptly drops down to zero once the compression rate goes below a threshold. Strong converses for data compression have been established for pure-state sources; however, for mixed-state sources, the optimal compression rate is determined by a structural decomposition of quantum states and can change substantially under small perturbations. This sensitivity makes it difficult to quantitatively analyze the trade-off between compression rate and error. Despite recent progress, whether a strong converse holds at the optimal rate has remained unresolved in general. In this work, we resolve this question affirmatively. For unassisted compression, we show that at every rate below the optimum, the accuracy decays exponentially with the block length for general mixed-state sources without restrictions on protocols. We also prove exponential strong converse for blind compression with unlimited entanglement assistance at its optimal rate. Combining the two strong converse bounds yields exponential decay at every compression-entanglement rate pair outside the complete achievable rate region. Our proof introduces a new overlap quantity based on the structure of quantum states; this quantity connects preservation of the source information to the dimension of the transmitted system. We expect that this approach provides useful tools for converse analyses of other quantum information-processing tasks involving mixed states.

Fast Quantum Algorithms for Learning Linear Threshold Functions

Summary. This paper develops quantum algorithms to efficiently learn Linear Threshold Functions (LTFs) under various query models, including real-domain queries and quantum examples. It achieves significant quantum advantages, such as exponential speedups for Boolean LTFs and polynomial speedups for Gaussian-based LTFs. The work advances the theory of quantum computation applied to fundamental problems in machine learning and pattern recognition.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the problem of learning Linear Threshold Functions (LTFs), which are fundamental in machine learning for linear discrimination, using various quantum access models.

Main result. The authors provide quantum algorithms achieving significant speedups over classical bounds for learning LTFs, particularly showing exponential improvements for Boolean LTFs and polynomial improvements for LTFs accessed via quantum examples.

Method. The techniques involve adapting quantum query algorithms for continuous domains (real membership queries) and leveraging the efficient Hermite transform for accessing quantum examples under Gaussian measures.

Model / system. The model is abstract, focusing on learning the function $f_{w, heta}(x) = ext{sign}(\langle w, x angle - heta)$ over domains like $\mathbb{R}^n$ or $\{-1, 1\}^n$. The quantum access is modeled via membership queries or a unitary $U$ generating quantum examples.

Key observables. The primary observables are the function $f_{w, heta}(x)$ itself, and the complexity measures are the number of queries (membership queries or $U$ applications) and the number of quantum gates.

Important parameters / regimes. Key parameters include the dimension $n$, the error $\epsilon$, the support size $k$ (for Boolean cases), and the threshold $ heta$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on specific access models, such as the availability of real-domain membership queries or the existence of a unitary $U$ for generating quantum examples under Gaussian measure.

Figures summary. No figures or tables were explicitly described in the notes, but the analysis heavily references numbered equations to establish lower and upper bounds on query complexity.

Paper structure. The paper is structured around three distinct access models: 1) Real-domain membership queries for general LTFs; 2) Boolean membership queries for structured LTFs (Majority function); and 3) Quantum examples accessed via a unitary $U$ under Gaussian measure.

Abstract

Linear threshold functions are $f_{w,θ}(x)=\text{sign}(\langle x,w \rangle -θ)$, where the weight vector $w\in\mathbb{R}^n$ is a unit vector, $θ\in\mathbb R$ is a threshold, and typically $x\in\mathbb{R}^n$ or $x\in\{-1,1\}^n$. When $θ=0$, the LTF is called homogeneous, and we write $f_w:=f_{w,0}$. Such functions are among the most important objects in machine learning, since they serve to linearly discriminate positive and negative examples. We give three positive results about learning LTFs: 1. Suppose we can make real-domain queries, meaning we can compute $f_{w,θ}(x)$ at any $x\in\mathbb{R}^n$ of our choice. We give a quantum algorithm that learns $f_{w,θ}$ up to Euclidean error~$ε$ using $O(\log(n/ε))$ membership queries and $\widetilde{O}(n)$ other gates. Then we have also learned $f_{w,θ}$ up to error $O(ε)$ when $x$ is Gaussian. Classical algorithms need $Ω(n\log(1/ε))$ queries. 2. A homogeneous LTF $f_w$ on domain $\{-1,1\}^n$ where $w$ has only $k$ nonzero entries of the same value, is the Majority function on the support of $w$. Belovs gave a bounded-error quantum algorithm that identifies the hidden support exactly (and hence learns $f_w$) using $O(k^{1/4})$ queries. We give an exponential improvement, using $O(\log k)$ queries. 3. Suppose we have a unitary $U$ that can produce (discretized) \emph{quantum examples} under Gaussian measure, corresponding to $\int_x \sqrt{γ_n(x)}|x\rangle |f_w(x)\rangle dx$. This is a weaker access model than membership queries. We give a quantum algorithm based on the efficient \emph{Hermite transform} of Jain et al.\ to learn homogeneous LTFs~$f_w$ with error~$ε$ under the Gaussian distribution, using $O(n^{1/4}/\sqrtε)$ applications of $U$ and $U^\dagger$ and $\widetilde{O}(n^2/ε^4)$ other gates.

Abstract

Quantum measurements and channels determine how information is extracted, encoded, and transmitted in quantum protocols. Optimizing their performance often requires numerical methods that remain practical as Hilbert space dimensions increase. The Reimpell-Werner iteration offers a practical approach to these tasks through repeated matrix updates that respect the constraints. These results provide a rigorous foundation for the iteration and broaden the class of optimization problems to which its convergence guarantees apply.

Improved Quantum Random Self-Reduction for Linear Problems

Authors: Vahid R. Asadi, Shuichi Hirahara, Nobutaka Shimizu
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.39823v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

Summary. This paper presents an improved quantum random self-reduction for solving linear problems over finite fields when the underlying linear map is only accessible via an imperfect oracle. By utilizing the Bogolyubov–Ruzsa subspace and quantum search techniques, the authors achieve a time complexity of $\widetilde{O}(n T^{1/3})$, which is a substantial improvement over existing quantum bounds.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a significant theoretical advancement in the complexity of quantum algorithms for solving average-case problems, demonstrating a superior time complexity compared to prior quantum reductions in the field.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the development of quantum random self-reductions to solve linear problems over finite fields when access to the underlying linear map is only available on a small fraction of inputs.

Main result. A uniform quantum reduction is established that computes the matrix-vector product $Mx$ with high probability in time $\widetilde{O}(n T^{1/3})$, significantly improving upon previous bounds.

Method. The reduction leverages the Bogolyubov–Ruzsa subspace from additive combinatorics and employs quantum search techniques based on amplitude amplification to avoid explicitly learning the subspace structure.

Model / system. The model is an abstract computational setting involving a linear map $M \in \mathbb{F}^{n imes n}$ and an imperfect oracle $\mathcal{O}$ that provides access to $Mx$ only on an $\epsilon$-fraction of inputs.

Key observables. The primary observable is the success probability of computing $Mx$ for an arbitrary input $x$, which is maintained at at least $2/3$.

Important parameters / regimes. Key parameters include the dimension $n$, the query cost $T$ of the oracle, and the error fraction $\epsilon$. The running time depends critically on the relationship between $n$ and $T$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes constant field size and a fixed error fraction $\epsilon$. The improvements rely on avoiding computationally expensive steps like explicitly recovering a basis for the orthogonal complement of the Bogolyubov–Ruzsa subspace.

Figures summary. Figure 1 compares the derived running time bound, $e^{O(n T^{1/3})}$, against the running times of prior state-of-the-art reductions across different regimes of $T$.

Paper structure. The paper builds upon previous work by first establishing the necessary theoretical tools (like the BR subspace) and then constructing a multi-stage quantum algorithm. This involves defining an amplified oracle $\mathcal{O}'$, using it to build a 'BR table,' and finally executing a quantum search procedure to achieve the desired complexity bounds.

Abstract

We study quantum random self-reductions for linear problems over finite fields. Let $M\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}$ be an arbitrary matrix, and let $\mathcal{O}$ be an oracle that agrees with the linear map $x\mapsto Mx$ on an $\varepsilon$-fraction of inputs $x\sim\mathbb{F}^n$. Given coherent access to $\mathcal{O}$ and coherent entry access to $M$, we give a uniform quantum reduction that computes $Mx$ on any prescribed input $x$ with probability at least $2/3$ in time $\widetilde{O}(nT^{1/3})$, for $n\le T\le n^{3/2}$ and constant field size and $\varepsilon$, where $T$ is the cost of one coherent query to $\mathcal{O}$. In particular, when $T=\widetilde{O}(n)$, the reduction runs in time $\widetilde{O}(n^{4/3})$, improving the $\widetilde{O}(n^{3/2}+T)$ reduction of Asadi, Golovnev, Gur, Shinkar, and Subramanian (SODA 2024). Our reduction uses the Bogolyubov--Ruzsa subspace guaranteed by additive combinatorics, but it avoids learning this subspace explicitly, which was computationally expensive for the previous reduction; in particular, it does not recover a basis for its orthogonal complement. The main technical step is to decompose the inputs into sparse pieces and find a vector that lies outside the Bogolyubov--Ruzsa subspace via a quantum search based on amplitude amplification. This yields a tunable tradeoff between the cost of querying the average-case oracle and the cost of verifying matrix-vector products.

Abstract

The volume estimation problem is a classic task in computational geometry. The development of randomized algorithms for this problem spurred the development of many influential algorithmic techniques related to Markov Chain Monte Carlo and simulated annealing, and the problem connects to several important geometrical results, like the recently-resolved KLS conjecture. In this work, we quantize the state-of-the-art $\widetilde{O}(d^{3.5}+d^3/\varepsilon^2)$-query randomized algorithm developed by Cousins and Vempala, and obtain a $\widetilde{O}(d^{3.5} + d^{1.75}/\varepsilon)$-query quantum algorithm, improving over the $\widetilde{O}(d^{3.5} + d^{2.25}/\varepsilon)$ state-of-the-art bound. It is this amortized quantum walk framework that allows us to exploit the amortized analysis of the ball walk by Cousins and Vempala, thus overcoming the key barrier that previously barred its quantum implementation.

Layer codes as quantum memories: syndrome extraction, thresholds and idle robustness

Authors: Zhu Sun, Zhenyu Cai
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.39522v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

Summary. This work analyzes layer codes as advanced quantum memories by simulating their performance under realistic circuit-level noise. The authors introduce a novel scheduling technique that improves circuit depth and achieves superior idle robustness compared to surface codes. The findings suggest layer codes are highly competitive, provided the associated hardware overheads are manageable.

Why it may be interesting. It provides concrete, quantitative benchmarks for next-generation QEC hardware by rigorously analyzing circuit overheads and noise resilience beyond simple code distance arguments.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper evaluates layer codes as active quantum memories under realistic circuit-level noise, focusing on the complex syndrome extraction circuit required for repeated measurements.

Main result. Layer codes achieve lower logical error rates than general-purpose schedulers at half the CNOT depth per round and significantly outperform surface codes in idle robustness, though this comes with higher overhead.

Method. The authors developed a novel, decoder-free scheduler that constrains hook propagation first and compresses depth afterward, comparing the resulting circuits against standard surface code implementations.

Model / system. The system utilizes layer codes, which are 3D local CSS codes formed by quasi-concatenating a CSS input code with unrotated surface code patches, for quantum error correction.

Key observables. Logical error rate (LER), circuit depth (CNOT depth per round), idle robustness (sustainable idle interval), and physical overhead (qubits, gates per logical qubit).

Important parameters / regimes. Circuit-level noise threshold ($p_{th}$), distance ($d$), and the ratio of idle time tolerance (1.7-3.0 times longer than rotated surface code).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis focuses on active circuit-level fault-tolerance, and the cost analysis emphasizes that the speed of beyond-2D cross-plane CNOTs is more critical than their fidelity.

Abstract

Layer codes are three-dimensional local CSS codes of check weight at most six, obtained by quasi-concatenating a CSS input code with unrotated surface code patches. What is established about them describes the code, not the circuit that measures it. In this work, we evaluate them as active memories under circuit-level noise. Doing so first requires a syndrome extraction circuit, and the junction stabilizers that couple the patches rule out borrowing a surface code patch's CNOT schedule. We present a decoder-free scheduler that constrains hook propagation first and compresses depth afterwards, generate circuits for eighteen layer codes across four input codes, and compare them with surface codes of the same distance, simulated and decoded in the same way. The circuits lose no distance to hook errors on [[4,2,2]]-input codes wherever a circuit-distance proof is affordable, and reach a lower logical error rate than those of a general-purpose scheduler for CSS codes at roughly half its CNOT depth per round. In this work, we introduce quantum secure non-interactive reductions (QSNIR), a natural extension of SNIR, in which the source and target resource are each a bipartite quantum state. Specifically, when the target resource is classical, our framework enables us to analyze the problem of disseminating private correlations from entanglement, in addition to classical resources. Our primary technical result is that QSNIR privacy in this context can be computed exactly via a semidefinite program (SDP) pair that quantifies an error operationally rooted in a simulation-based cheating advantage. We prove that QSNIR privacy is lower-bounded by a decision problem related to minimum-error state discrimination (MED), and use both definitions to compute exact one-shot privacy errors for universal two-party computation (2PC) preprocessing correlations.

Abstract

Abstract

We introduce the quantum Černý complexity $\mathrm{qc}(w)$ of a binary word $w$: the least dimension $d$ for which there exist quantum channels $A_0,A_1$ on $d\times d$ density matrices and a start state $ρ_0$ such that $w$ is the unique shortest word whose associated channel is constant on the reachable set. We show that $2\le\mathrm{qc}(w)\le\lceil\sqrt{|w|+1}\,\rceil$ for every nonempty $w$, a quadratic saving over the classical analogue, and that constant words are extremal: $\mathrm{qc}(0^m)=\lceil\sqrt{m+1}\,\rceil$. In contrast, $\mathrm{qc}(01^n0)=2$ for every $n\ge 1$, realized by a single qubit whose rotation angle acts as a counter; consequently there is no quantum analogue of the Černý function, and $\mathrm{qc}$ is strongly anti-correlated with intuitive notions of descriptive complexity. We further study the variant $\mathrm{qcp}$ in which the synchronization target is required to be a pure state. We prove that in dimension $2$ no word of length at least $2$ can be a unique shortest synchronizing word with pure target, and we exhibit an explicit qutrit instance, combining a coherent rotation with a measure-and-funnel channel, achieving $\mathrm{qcp}(01^n0)=3$ with target a computational basis state and with synchronization holding universally over all input states. Thus purity of the reset state costs exactly one dimension on this family. We also observe that $\mathrm{qc}$ is computable, by reduction to the first-order theory of the reals.
