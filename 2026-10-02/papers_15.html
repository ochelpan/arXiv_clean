The power of constant-depth quantum circuits of unbounded size

Authors: Sergii Strelchuk, Sathyawageeswar Subramanian, Máté Weisz
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.40294v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper explores the depth limitations of implementing arbitrary quantum unitaries using restricted gate sets. It shows that while some related tasks can be achieved in constant depth with unlimited resources, the general unitary case remains open. The authors propose a scalable, depth-bounded approximation method using port-based teleportation.

Why it may be interesting. The work directly addresses fundamental limits on quantum computation complexity, which is crucial for designing efficient quantum algorithms and understanding the resource overheads of universal quantum computation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates whether arbitrary unitary transformations can be implemented exactly in constant depth using quantum circuits built from single-qubit gates and generalized Toffoli gates, even when circuit size and ancillary qubits are unrestricted.

Main result. While constant-depth constructions exist for state preparation and basis permutations, implementing arbitrary unitaries remains open; however, a port-based teleportation scheme achieves an approximation with depth $O(\sqrt{d})$.

Method. The authors use reductions to simplify the problem (e.g., to traceless unitary involutions) and develop a port-based teleportation protocol to approximate the required unitaries.

Model / system. The model is quantum circuits operating on qubits, utilizing single-qubit gates and generalized Toffoli gates, with analysis focusing on circuit depth and resource scaling.

Key observables. Depth (constant depth is the goal), Entanglement Fidelity ($F_e$), and Circuit Size.

Important parameters / regimes. Input dimension ($d$), number of ports ($M$), and the level ($\ell$) in the Clifford hierarchy.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption for some results is the availability of unbounded circuit size and ancillary qubits to achieve constant depth for specific subtasks.

Figures summary. Figures summarize the ingredients for state preparation constructions and compare exact unitary reductions against the approximation provided by port-based teleportation.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by establishing constant-depth constructions for simpler tasks (permutations, state preparation), then reducing the general unitary problem to known subproblems, and finally presenting the port-based teleportation scheme for approximation.

Abstract

Classical circuits with unbounded fan-in can compute any Boolean function in constant depth when their size is unrestricted. We ask whether removing the restrictions on circuit size and ancillary qubits also allows quantum circuits built from arbitrary single-qubit gates and generalised Toffoli gates to implement every unitary in constant depth. We give exact constant-depth constructions for arbitrary permutations of computational basis states, diagonal unitaries and the preparation of arbitrary pure states. These connect quantum state preparation to reversible classical computation and the preparation of probability distributions. With fanout in the gate set, they use exponentially many gates and ancillary qubits and return all ancillary qubits to zero. Replacing fanout by an exact circuit over the original gate set preserves constant depth, although the size bounds can become doubly exponential. The implementation of arbitrary unitaries in constant depth remains open. We give equivalent formulations in terms of copying the vectors of a specified orthonormal basis, extracting their labels and implementing restricted families of unitaries. We also reduce arbitrary unitary implementation to that of traceless unitary involutions using one additional clean qubit. With adaptive measurements, gate teleportation gives depth proportional to the level of a gate in the Clifford hierarchy. Towards arbitrary unitary implementation in constant depth, we use port-based teleportation: for input dimension $d$ and $M\geq d^2-1$ ports, we construct a unitary circuit of depth $O(\sqrt d)$, independent of $M$ and including resource preparation and port selection, with entanglement fidelity at least $(1-(d^2-1)/(2M))^2$. Thus, at fixed $d$, the approximation can be made arbitrarily accurate without increasing depth. Whether the dependence on $d$ can also be removed remains open.

The quantum query complexity of the semigroup product problem

Authors: Troy Lee, Miklos Santha
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.39974v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work determines the quantum query complexity for computing the product of elements in a semigroup. It introduces and utilizes the concept of 'product breadth' ($eta$) to characterize the complexity, providing tight bounds for various classes of semigroups, including a significant improvement over previous general bounds.

Why it may be interesting. While the subject is abstract algebra, the techniques used—query complexity analysis and deriving tight bounds based on structural invariants ($eta$)—are foundational tools in quantum computation theory, relevant for understanding resource limitations in quantum algorithms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper studies the quantum query complexity required to compute the product of $n$ elements in a semigroup, given access to the multiplication table.

Main result. For finite commutative aperiodic monoids, the quantum query complexity is characterized by the product breadth $eta$, achieving $\Theta(\min\{n, \sqrt{n}eta\})$.

Method. The analysis employs quantum query complexity theory, relating the problem to algebraic structure via the concept of product breadth ($eta$) and leveraging structural properties of semigroups.

Model / system. The model is a finite aperiodic semigroup or monoid $S=(S, \otimes)$, where the computation involves finding $x_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes x_n$. The complexity depends heavily on the algebraic structure of $S$.

Key observables. Quantum query complexity, Product breadth ($eta$), Aperiodicity index ($\iota(M)$).

Important parameters / regimes. $n$ (number of inputs), $N$ (size of the semigroup), $eta$ (product breadth).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis focuses on the bounded-error quantum query complexity for aperiodic semigroups/monoids.

Paper structure. The paper develops bounds by analyzing specific cases (commutative monoids, monoids with stable partial orders) and culminates in an improved upper bound for arbitrary finite aperiodic semigroups, comparing it against known lower bounds.

Abstract

We study the quantum query complexity of computing a semigroup product $x_1\cdots x_n$, when one query reveals one input element and the multiplication table is given. For a finite aperiodic semigroup of size $N-1$, the argument of Aaronson, Grier, and Schaeffer gives an upper bound of $\sqrt n\,(N\log(nN+2))^{O(N)}$ queries. To obtain query bounds that reflect algebraic structure, we study the product breadth $β$: the smallest bound such that every input word has a subsequence of at most $β$ letters with the same product. For nontrivial finite commutative aperiodic monoids, the bounded-error quantum query complexity is $Θ(\min\{n,\sqrt{nβ}\})$, and is thus characterized by product breadth. We further show that if such a monoid $M$ has aperiodicity index $k$ (the least positive integer satisfying $x^k=x^{k+1}$ for every $x\in M$), then $β=O(k\log(|M|+1)\log\log(|M|+2))$. For monoids with a stable partial order in which the identity is the minimum element, we prove that the bounded-error quantum query complexity is at most $\sqrt{n+1}((β+2)\log(n+2))^{O(\log(β+2))}$. For arbitrary finite aperiodic semigroups of order $N-1$, we improve the bound of Aaronson, Grier, and Schaeffer, obtaining a bounded-error quantum query complexity of at most \[ \min\left\{n,\sqrt n\, \log^{O((N\log(N+2))^{1/3})}(n+2)\right\}. \] The dependence on semigroup size is nearly tight: the bounded-depth Dyck lower bound of Ambainis et al. yields aperiodic monoids requiring $\sqrt n\,2^{Ω(N^{1/3})}$ quantum queries in the relevant parameter range.

The Structure of Higher-Order Quantum Resources with an Application to Coherence

Authors: Felix Ahnefeld, Thomas Theurer, Martin B. Plenio

Summary. This paper introduces a comprehensive resource theory framework for supermaps, unifying the study of quantum resources across states, channels, and processes. It develops mathematical tools like the 'quantum comb' and analyzes supermap distillation/dilution protocols. The findings advance the understanding of resource quantification in complex, multi-copy quantum information tasks.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides advanced mathematical tools for quantifying non-classical correlations in multi-copy quantum processes, which is fundamental for understanding resource limitations in quantum computation and communication protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops a unified, general framework for quantifying higher-order quantum resources, extending resource theories from states and channels to supermaps (processes).

Main result. It characterizes the largest sets of compatible supermaps for dynamical coherence and establishes relationships between various supermap distance measures, showing that the set of compatible supermaps is strictly larger than the free network set.

Method. The authors generalize resource theory concepts, utilizing the Choi-Jamiołkowski representation and defining supermap resource monotones, distillation, and dilution protocols.

Model / system. The focus is on quantum states, quantum channels ($\mathcal{E}$), and quantum supermaps ($\mathcal{S}$), which are necessary for describing multi-copy quantum processes. The analysis is generalized to include concepts like free supermaps and compatible supermaps.

Key observables. Supermap resource monotones, supermap distillation/dilution rates, generalized diamond distance ($ ext{d}{\diamond, ext{N}}$), and the quantum comb ($ ext{J}$).}

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis considers the parallel and adaptive settings for distillation and dilution tasks.

Assumptions / limitations. The work assumes a definite causal order for the supermap processes and is restricted to finite-dimensional quantum systems.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates a quantum network implementing a supermap. Figure 2 shows the composition of quantum supermaps using a link product.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the general framework for supermaps, then deriving mathematical tools like the comb representation and distance measures, and finally applying this framework to specific resource theories, such as dynamical coherence.

Abstract

We develop a general framework for higher-order quantum resources that treats the resourcefulness of quantum states, channels, and supermaps (also called processes) in a unified manner. By generalizing well-established concepts from resource theories of states and channels, we introduce and study supermap resource monotones and supermap distillation and dilution. As free supermaps, we consider free networks (supermaps that can be implemented with free channels), compatible supermaps (sets of supermaps closed under arbitrary composition), and maximally free superchannels. Specializing to approximate channel-to-channel conversions, we show that under mild assumptions, the conversion error with respect to the diamond distance and the worst-case infidelity coincide. We then apply the general framework to resource theories of dynamical coherence, where we establish the existence of largest sets of compatible supermaps. By characterizing these sets with semidefinite constraints, we show that they are strictly larger than the corresponding free networks but do not contain all maximally free superchannels. We finish by investigating the operational differences of these sets in one-shot channel distillation and dilution, providing solutions for both tasks in the parallel setting and for dilution in the adaptive setting.

Transmitting algebras through quantum channels

Authors: Robert Salzmann, Satvik Singh
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.40054v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work develops a rigorous theory for determining which complex information structures (C*-algebras) can be perfectly transmitted through a quantum channel. The central finding is that a single 'optimal' structure may not exist, forcing users to select different optimal strategies based on the specific task. This advances the understanding of quantum channel capacity by introducing algebraic invariants and characterizing when such universal structures fail to exist.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a deep algebraic framework for understanding information capacity limits, which is fundamental to quantum communication theory and quantum error correction.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops a theory to characterize the exact transmission of finite-dimensional C*-algebras through arbitrary quantum channels.

Main result. The existence of a unique 'dominating' algebra that encompasses all optimal transmission strategies is not guaranteed, and the failure of domination implies complex, task-dependent choices for the user.

Method. The authors use algebraic methods involving embedding preorders on the set of transmittable algebras and introduce 'hybrid capacities' to characterize the structure.

Model / system. The system involves quantum channels $\Phi: L(H) o L(K)$ acting on finite-dimensional C*-algebras, which model hybrid classical-quantum information.

Key observables. Transmittable algebras $T(\Phi)$, hybrid capacities, and the growth rate of maximal algebra types under tensor products.

Important parameters / regimes. The dimension of the algebras ($d_k$), the block-length of the code, and the structure of the quantum channel $\Phi$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the algebraic structure of the channel and the definition of transmittability via encoding/decoding channels.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the Hasse diagrams showing when a dominating algebra exists for specific channels, and classification diagrams relating channel structures to the existence of capacity algebras.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the mathematical framework for transmittable algebras, investigates the existence and properties of a dominating algebra, derives structural theorems for domination, and concludes with examples showing the failure of domination and superadditivity analogues.

Abstract

We develop a theory of exact transmission of finite-dimensional $C^*$-algebras through quantum channels. These algebras describe hybrid classical-quantum information, allowing the dimension of the quantum system to depend on the classical message. Allowing arbitrary encodings and decodings yields a transmission set of algebra types, ordered by embedding, that unifies zero-error information theory with operator-algebraic error correction. We ask if this set admits a dominating algebra into which every transmittable algebra embeds. Our central finding is that domination can fail even in small dimensions. In its absence, several incomparable maximal algebras can describe different optimal uses of the same channel, forcing the user to select one depending on the operational task and the type of information to be preserved. We introduce hybrid capacities that reconstruct the only possible dominating algebra type and present a complete finite characterization of domination using minimal forbidden algebra types. We identify dominating algebras for channels whose operator systems are graph-isomorphic to $*$-algebras, including highly divisible channels, and for channels with zero one-shot zero-error quantum capacity. Under tensor products, we prove that joint coding can produce new algebra types, giving an algebraic analogue of superadditivity from Shannon theory. Domination can fail for the joint use of two channels, even when both channels separately admit dominating algebras. Finally, we construct a channel whose $n$-fold tensor powers have doubly exponentially many maximal algebra types, attaining the largest possible growth scaling for finite-dimensional channels. Consequently, new transmittable algebra types appear at arbitrarily large block-lengths for this channel, so its full transmission structure cannot be generated from any finite collection of bounded-block-length codes.

Unconditional quantum advantage with noisy planar architectures

Summary. This paper proves an unconditional quantum advantage for planar quantum devices by showing that shallow, 2D-local quantum circuits can solve problems intractable for classical AC0 circuits, even when the circuits are imperfectly implemented with local noise. The authors achieve this by compiling a fault-tolerant 1D circuit into a 2D structure and demonstrating the resulting computational separation.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete, physically relevant demonstration of quantum advantage that is robust to noise and does not rely on unproven complexity assumptions, making it highly relevant for near-term quantum hardware.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To establish an unconditional quantum advantage by demonstrating that shallow, 2D-local quantum circuits are computationally more powerful than classical AC0 circuits, even when subject to realistic local stochastic noise.

Main result. The authors prove a complexity separation showing that a specific relation problem can be solved with high probability by a noisy 2D quantum circuit, while any AC0 circuit solving it must have exponentially large size.

Method. The proof involves constructing a classically hard relation $R_n$ and showing that the quantum circuit solves it with high probability under noise, while the classical simulation requires an exponentially large circuit size.

Model / system. The model considers quantum devices restricted to local operations in 2D and subject to local stochastic Pauli noise below a constant threshold. The quantum computation is compiled from a 1D-local Clifford circuit into a non-adaptive, 2D-local, constant-depth circuit.

Key observables. Success probability of the quantum circuit (high probability) versus the failure probability of the classical circuit (constant probability).

Important parameters / regimes. The noise strength must remain below a constant fault-tolerance threshold ($p_{th}$); the depth $D$ is constant, and the locality is 2D.

Assumptions / limitations. The advantage is unconditional, meaning it does not rely on unproven complexity assumptions, but it requires the noise to be below a specific fault-tolerance threshold.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the process of compiling a 1D-local circuit into a 2D plane using gate teleportation, showing how the circuit unfolds in the (x, y) plane.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the physical model (2D, noisy, local). It then details the compilation process (1D $ o$ 2D, fault-tolerant) and the post-processing map. Finally, it uses this construction to prove the complexity separation by comparing the quantum solution to the known classical hardness of the problem.

Abstract

We consider quantum devices restricted to local operations in 2D and subject to local stochastic noise below a constant threshold. We show that such circuits are computationally more powerful than AC0-circuits, i.e., noise-free, geometrically-unconstrained constant-depth classical circuits with unbounded fan-in AND, OR and NOT gates. To this end, we exhibit a computational problem with the following properties: (i) Any instance of the problem is correctly solved with high probability by a certain geometrically 2D-local quantum circuit even if the latter is imperfectly implemented, but (ii) any polynomial-size AC0-circuit fails to solve certain instances of the problem with constant probability. To our knowledge, this isthe first complexity-theoretic separation which applies to planar quantum devices, incorporates noise-resilience and is unconditional, i.e., does not rely on complexity-theoretic assumptions. This brings the experimental demonstration of an unconditional quantum advantage closer to experimental realities.

Universal distillation of quantum entanglement

Authors: Ryuji Takagi, Kaito Watanabe, Takaya Matsuura, Hayato Arai, Masahito Hayashi
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.40215v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

Summary. This work addresses the limitation of entanglement distillation theory that previously required knowing the exact input state. The authors introduce a universal protocol that achieves the maximum distillable entanglement rate using only local operations and classical communication, making $E_d$ a robust measure for unknown quantum states.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a robust, operational definition for entanglement distillation that holds even in realistic scenarios where the quantum state is unknown, which is crucial for quantum communication protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To establish distillable entanglement as a fundamental operational quantity even when the parties do not know the exact description of the input quantum state (state-agnostic distillation).

Main result. The authors prove that a universal LOCC protocol exists that achieves the distillable entanglement rate for any unknown input state, confirming its operational significance.

Method. The protocol is constructed using state merging techniques applied to multiple copies of the state, combined with the uniform convergence property of finite-block estimates.

Model / system. The study concerns bipartite quantum states $\rho_{AB}$ acting on a tensor product Hilbert space, characterizing the rate of extracting Bell states.

Key observables. Distillable Entanglement ($E_d$), Coherent Information ($I_c$), and the asymptotic rate of entanglement extraction.

Important parameters / regimes. The number of copies ($n$), the estimation error ($\epsilon_n$), and the noise strength parameter ($\xi_k$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the ability to perform local informationally complete measurements and the convergence of finite-block estimates to the true distillable entanglement.

Paper structure. The paper first reviews the standard state-aware definition of $E_d$, then introduces the universal protocol based on state merging and local estimation, culminating in the proof that the rate converges to $E_d( ho_{AB})$ regardless of prior state knowledge.

Abstract

Distillable entanglement---the maximum rate of Bell states extractable from the given noisy entangled states via local operations and classical communication (LOCC)---has been a central quantity to characterize the operational value of entangled states. However, this operational meaning had been known to be valid only under a crucial assumption: both parties know the description of the input state and can choose the protocol tailored to it, which does not necessarily reflect the natural operational setting. Here, we show that the distillable entanglement is the fundamental operational quantity even without this assumption. We present a universal LOCC protocol---whose description does not depend on the input state and thus works universally for any input---that achieves the distillable entanglement of an arbitrary unknown input state. Our result therefore lifts the distillable entanglement to a robust notion of an operationally meaningful quantity and clarifies that input-state knowledge is not necessary to achieve the optimal rate of entanglement distillation. Our protocol is based on state merging for compound states, together with the uniform convergence property of the finite-block quantity toward distillable entanglement.

Verifiable Quantum Advantage and Computation via Quantum Circuit Obfuscation

Summary. This work establishes rigorous cryptographic protocols for proving and verifying quantum computational advantage. By leveraging Quantum Indistinguishability Obfuscation (qiO), the authors construct schemes that allow classical parties to verify the results of BQP computations, achieving both private and publicly verifiable standards. This provides a strong theoretical foundation for the concept of quantum advantage.

Why it may be interesting. While highly theoretical, the rigorous cryptographic framework developed for verifying quantum computation provides deep insights into the computational separation between classical and quantum resources, which underpins much of modern quantum information theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of creating protocols that allow classical verification of quantum computational advantages and the results of BQP computations.

Main result. They construct a publicly verifiable two-message quantum-advantage protocol and develop protocols for both private and public classical verification of arbitrary BQP computations.

Method. The core method relies on utilizing Quantum Indistinguishability Obfuscation (qiO) to build cryptographic foundations for quantum advantage proofs and verification schemes.

Model / system. The model involves abstract quantum circuits and the complexity class BQP (Bounded-error Quantum Polynomial time). The security is analyzed using quantum indistinguishability and the diamond distance.

Key observables. Quantum indistinguishability ($ ext{qiO}$), quantum advantage proof success probability, and the ability to distinguish between quantum and classical computation.

Important parameters / regimes. The security parameter $\lambda$, the negligible error bounds ($\epsilon, \delta, \kappa$), and the computational assumption $ ext{BQP} eq ext{BPP}$.

Assumptions / limitations. The existence of quantum indistinguishability obfuscation ($ ext{qiO}$), a slightly stronger version of $ ext{BQP} eq ext{BPP}$, and post-quantum one-way functions for the public verification protocol.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the technical structure of the constructions, showing implications between various protocols, such as public verification leading to bounds on quantum advantage.

Paper structure. The paper builds from the general problem of verifiable quantum advantage, first constructing a two-message protocol, then detailing private verification using only $ ext{qiO}$, and finally achieving the first publicly verifiable protocol by adding post-quantum assumptions.

Abstract

We construct protocols for classically verifiable quantum advantage and classical verification of $\mathsf{BQP}$ computations using \emph{quantum indistinguishability obfuscation} (qiO). Specifically, given qiO and assuming a slightly stronger version of $\mathsf{BQP}\neq\mathsf{BPP}$, we construct a two-message quantum-advantage protocol that is efficiently and publicly verifiable. Our result can be viewed as a rigorous cryptographic foundation for the heuristic quantum advantage proposals based on \emph{peaked random circuit sampling} of Aaronson and Zhang (arXiv:2404.14493). We also construct two simple protocols for classically verifying arbitrary $\mathsf{BQP}$ computations. The first protocol is privately verifiable and assumes only the existence of qiO. This gives a rare example of a nontrivial cryptographic application of (quantum) iO that does not make additional computational hardness assumptions. The second protocol additionally assumes post-quantum one-way functions and is \emph{publicly verifiable}. To our knowledge, this is the first publicly verifiable protocol for classical verification of $\mathsf{BQP}$ computations under computational assumptions in the standard model. We show that all our results hold when qiO is assumed only for ancilla-free unitary circuits. As evidence supporting this assumption, we prove a worst-to-average-case reduction for obfuscating such circuits. This reduction extends the local-mixing framework of Canetti, Chamon, Mucciolo and Ruckenstein (TCC 2024) under quantum analogues of their assumptions.

Verifiable quantum advantage based on polynomials with planted structures

Summary. This paper proposes a method to achieve verifiable quantum advantage by embedding secret, planted structures into quantum circuits defined by cubic polynomials. By using a specialized cross-entropy test, the authors show that a verifier can check the quantum computation's output much faster than any classical adversary, leading to a provable computational gap. This has implications for generating classically certifiable randomness.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the crucial issue of verifiability in quantum advantage claims, providing a concrete, mathematically rigorous framework based on algebraic structures (polynomials/tensors) that could underpin future quantum-classical cryptographic primitives.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Developing quantum advantage protocols that are verifiable using only classical outputs, addressing the lack of classical verifiability in current quantum advantage demonstrations.

Main result. The authors propose a scheme using IQP circuits with planted structures that yields a polynomial gap between the verification time and the time a classical adversary needs to pass the test.

Method. The core method involves using 'simulation secrets' and a cross-entropy test (XEB) to verify quantum advantage by exploiting hidden, planted structures within the quantum circuit's polynomial description.

Model / system. The model uses IQP circuits defined by cubic Boolean polynomials acting on $n$-bit strings. The structure relies on identifying and exploiting 'planted independent spaces' within the polynomial's algebraic representation.

Key observables. The cross-entropy test statistic ($\chi$), the dimension of the independent space ($h$), and the resulting computational gap between verification and simulation times.

Important parameters / regimes. The scheme is estimated to be implementable using 100 logical qubits at logical error rates around $10^{-6}$. The gap is conjectured to be exponential.

Assumptions / limitations. The argument relies on conjectures regarding the computational invisibility of large independent spaces to bounded adversaries, and fine-grained complexity conjectures regarding random polynomials.

Figures summary. Not specified.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the need for verifiable advantage, studies the PlantedIndSp problem for cubic Boolean polynomials, details verification using IQP circuits and the XEB test, and discusses the worst-case hardness and cryptanalysis of the independent space problem.

Abstract

A central question in the theory of quantum advantage is whether there are quantum advantage protocols with similar resource requirements as random circuit sampling that are also verifiable just from the classical outputs of the quantum computation. Here, we develop the idea of simulation secrets for verifiable advantage. A verifier can use a simulation secret to evaluate a cross-entropy test faster than it would take a classical adversary to pass the test. We instantiate this idea using IQP circuits described by cubic polynomials with planted independent spaces. These correspond to the largest independent set in the orbit of a polynomial under the general linear group and yield a low-rank stabilizer decomposition of the corresponding state. We conjecture that large independent spaces are invisible to a computationally bounded adversary, and therefore they cannot exploit them to pass the protocol. A second conjecture regards the fine-grained complexity of producing samples that pass the cross-entropy test for uniformly random polynomials. Under these conjectures, our scheme results in a polynomial gap between the verification time and the time a classical adversary would need to pass the protocol---both are exponential. It has a potential application to generating classically certifiable randomness, since the output distributions have high min-entropy. We estimate that the planted polynomial scheme is implementable using 100 logical qubits at logical error rates around $10^{-6}$.

Abstract

We present a hybrid quantum-classical algorithm for distribution network reconfiguration, a combinatorial optimization problem on power distribution networks, that combines a radiality preserving branch-exchange encoding with iterative warm-start quantum imaginary-time evolution to minimize active line losses. The encoding uses a fixed-width binary representation of sequential branch-exchange actions, ensuring that every register outcome decodes to a radial configuration. The quantum subroutine is informed by a surrogate model fit to classically-solved alternating current power flow (ACPF) labels. After a set budget of ACPF solves and circuit samples is exhausted, the algorithm returns the lowest-loss configuration seen. We employ our algorithm on ten test cases, including seven commonly-used benchmark cases and three cases we modified from these and similar standard systems. We include two methods to reduce problem size, which enable extensions of the algorithm to larger networks. We successfully reach minimal-loss configurations for six cases and find configurations with losses from 0.2 percent to 2.18 percent above the reported reference minimal loss for the other four.

Abstract

This paper investigates electron transport properties across N/AM interface or N/AM/N heterojunction. Based on Hamiltonians in the three regions, general wave functions are proposed and combined linear equations are constructed. Reflection and transmission coefficients as well as spin polarizatioin of the altermagnet heterostructure are obtained. When electron incident from normal metal to altermagnet, it is scattered and split into + branch and _ branch transmitting into altermagnet zone with separate refractive angles. Total internal reflection might happen to + branch. Critical angle of total reflection is determined by incident conditions and properties of AM material. In the heterostructure case, spin-up and spin-down electrons transmitted with same propagation directions but different transmission probabilities. Transmission and polarization periodically change with length of the junction. Exchange field and spin-splitting strength have pronounced effect on spin-polarized transmittance while the effect of spin-orbit coupling is quite small. Optimized system can be found by tuning parameters of the AM material. This study will lay the theoretical groundwork for experimental research on spin-selective outputs and spintronic devices.

Summary. This work analyzes the magnetization relaxation in chains of interacting nanomagnets, modeling the competition between single-particle and collective reversal mechanisms. Using both advanced analytical theory and Monte Carlo simulations, the authors derive expressions for the relaxation rate and show that the reversal mechanism is controlled by the external field, transitioning from uniform to edge-nucleation propagation. This provides a computationally efficient framework for predicting the dynamics of dipolar-coupled magnetic assemblies.

Why it may be interesting. The study provides a detailed, quantitative framework for understanding collective dynamics in interacting magnetic systems, which shares mathematical parallels with phase transitions and non-equilibrium relaxation processes studied in statistical mechanics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the magnetization dynamics crossover from single-particle to collective behavior in interacting chains of nanomagnets.

Main result. The analysis derives a field-controlled crossover in the reversal mechanism, shifting from uniform (macrospin-like) reversal to edge-nucleation propagation as the external field increases.

Method. The study employs an Intermediate-to-High Damping (IHD) analytical approach based on Langer's theory and validates results using Time-Quantified Monte Carlo (TQMC) simulations.

Abstract

We investigate the magnetization dynamics crossover from single-particle to collective behavior in a one-dimensional chain of dipolar-coupled nanomagnets with uniaxial anisotropy. Using both an intermediate-to-high damping (IHD) analytical approach based on Langer's theory and time-quantified Monte Carlo (TQMC) simulations, we derive and validate semi-analytical expressions for the relaxation rate and the magnetization relaxation curves. Our main results include: (i) a closed-form expression for the relaxation rate accounting for (weak) dipolar interactions, (ii) a two-exponential semi-analytical formula for the magnetization dynamics $m(t)$ of an interacting chain, and (iii) a systematic comparison with TQMC simulations, showing good agreement for a wide range of parameters. The analysis reveals a field-controlled crossover from uniform (macrospin-like) reversal to edge-nucleation propagation, driven by the spatial inhomogeneity of dipolar stabilization. The derived expressions provide a computationally efficient framework for predicting the relaxation behavior of dipolar-coupled nanomagnetic assemblies.
