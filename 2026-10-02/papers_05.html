A Quantum Algorithm for $st$-Transport on Flat Connection Graphs

Authors: Stacey Jeffery, Tobias J. Osborne, Galina Pass
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.40231v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 3/5 · quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops a quantum algorithm to solve the $st$-transport problem on graphs whose edges carry unitary quantum operations, provided these operations form a 'flat connection.' The algorithm achieves near-optimal time complexity, and the authors prove an $\Omega(n)$ quantum query lower bound, confirming its efficiency for simulating quantum transport phenomena.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a quantum algorithmic framework for simulating physical processes modeled by gauge theories (like lattice gauge theory) on quantum computers, connecting quantum complexity theory to fundamental physics structures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the $st$-transport problem on graphs where edges are labeled by unitary quantum operations, requiring the determination of connectivity and the estimation of the state overlap between source and target states.

Main result. A bounded-error quantum algorithm for $st$-transport is developed with time complexity $\widetilde{O}(n/\varepsilon)$ and space complexity $O(\log n + \log k + \log(1/\varepsilon))$.

Method. The algorithm utilizes quantum transducers and extends the Metropolis-Hastings reweighting technique from classical $st$-connectivity problems to the unitary-labeled setting.

Model / system. The model is an undirected graph $G=(V,E)$ where each edge $\{u,v\}$ is labeled by a unitary $U_{uv} \in \mathbb{C}^{k imes k}$. The crucial assumption is that these labels form a 'flat connection,' meaning path products are path-independent.

Key observables. The primary observable is the squared overlap $|\langle\psi_t|U_{s o t}|\psi_s angle|^2$, which quantifies the fidelity of state transport from $s$ to $t$.

Important parameters / regimes. Key parameters include $n$ (number of vertices), $k$ (dimension of the unitaries), and $\varepsilon$ (the required additive error for the overlap estimation).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis critically relies on the assumption that the edge labels define a flat connection (pure gauge).

Figures summary. One figure illustrates the $st$-transport instance on an unweighted path graph, where edge labels are unitaries $X_{x_i}$ designed to encode a parity check, demonstrating the $\Omega(n)$ lower bound.

Paper structure. The paper first defines the $st$-transport problem on unitary-labeled graphs under the flatness assumption. It then presents the quantum algorithm using transducers and Metropolis-Hastings reweighting to achieve an upper bound. Finally, it establishes an $\Omega(n)$ quantum query lower bound by reducing the parity problem to this transport problem.

Abstract

We study a generalization of undirected $st$-connectivity to graphs whose edges carry quantum operations. Let $G=(V,E)$ be an undirected graph on $n$ vertices in which each edge $\{u,v\}$ is labeled by a unitary $U_{uv}\in\mathbb{C}^{k\times k}$, with $U_{vu}=U_{uv}^\dagger$. We assume the labels form a \emph{flat} connection: the ordered product of labels along any path between a pair of vertices $u$ and $v$ is independent of the path. Equivalently, the connection is pure gauge, i.e., gauge-equivalent to the trivial connection; such graphs are exactly the consistent connection graphs of spectral graph theory and the noiseless instances of group synchronization. Consequently, whenever $s$ and $t$ are connected, transporting a state from $s$ to $t$ defines a unique unitary $U_s(t)$. Given states $|ψ_s\rangle,|ψ_t\rangle\in\mathbb{C}^k$ and an oracle that returns the neighbours of a vertex while coherently applying the corresponding edge unitaries, the \emph{$st$-transport problem} is to decide whether $s$ and $t$ are connected and, if so, to estimate the squared overlap between $U_s(t)|ψ_s\rangle$ and $|ψ_t\rangle$ to additive error $\varepsilon$. When $k=1$ and all labels are trivial, this is exactly undirected $st$-connectivity. We give a bounded-error quantum algorithm for $st$-transport that runs in time $\widetilde{O}(n/\varepsilon)$ and uses $O(\log n+\log k+\log(1/\varepsilon))$ space. We do this by designing a transducer and applying a Metropolis-Hastings reweighting to the input graph. We also prove an $Ω(n)$ quantum query lower bound that holds even when $s$ and $t$ are promised to be connected, so for constant $\varepsilon$ our algorithm is optimal up to polylogarithmic factors.

An uncertainty principle for entanglement

Authors: Mitali Nanda, Daochen Wang
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.39126v1

Summary. This paper introduces a quantitative uncertainty principle for entanglement, bounding the sum of Shannon entropies in two different bases by the difference in entanglement Rényi entropies. This result has a direct physical implication, showing that weakly-entangled states cannot perfectly represent the ground states of systems whose energy eigenstates are highly entangled, demonstrated using the SYK model.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a fundamental mathematical constraint linking entanglement structure (basis localization) to energy structure (eigenbasis uncertainty), which is crucial for understanding quantum simulation and ground state preparation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper establishes a quantitative uncertainty principle for entanglement, generalizing known principles from position/momentum measurements.

Main result. It derives a lower bound on the sum of Shannon entropies of a state's coefficients in two orthonormal bases, relating it to the difference between the entanglement Rényi entropies of the basis states.

Method. The proof relies on establishing new lower and upper bounds on the entanglement of superpositions, utilizing functional analysis tools like Schatten norms and interpolation theorems.

Model / system. The primary application is to the Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev (SYK) model, which is used to illustrate how the uncertainty principle limits the ability of weakly-entangled states to represent ground states of Hamiltonians with strongly-entangled energy eigenstates.

Key observables. Shannon entropies of coefficient distributions ($H(p)$), Entanglement Rényi entropies ($E_\gamma(\psi)$), and the energy gap/expected energy ($\langle E angle_\psi$).

Important parameters / regimes. The parameters $\alpha \in (1, \infty]$ and $eta \in [1/2, 1)$ govern the specific form of the inequality.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis often approximates the state $|\psi angle$ as either a Matrix Product State (MPS) or a Configuration Interaction State (CIS) for practical bounds.

Figures summary. Figure 1 plots the eigenstate entanglement Rényi entropies versus energy for the SYK model. Figure 2 plots derived lower bounds for the support size and the energy gap for various $\alpha$ values.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the general uncertainty principle using mathematical tools, followed by an application section where the principle is used to constrain the representation of ground states in the SYK model.

Abstract

Consider two orthonormal bases of a bipartite Hilbert space such that states in one basis are weakly entangled and states in the other are strongly entangled. We establish a quantitative version of the following uncertainty principle: every state that is localized in one basis must be delocalized in the other. More specifically, we lower bound the sum of the Shannon entropies of any state's coefficient distributions in the two bases by the difference between the entanglement Rényi entropies of their basis states. The result follows from new lower and upper bounds on the entanglement of superpositions that may be of independent interest. As an application, our result limits the power of weakly-entangled states in representing ground states of Hamiltonians with strongly-entangled energy eigenstates. We illustrate this application numerically for the Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev model.

Copy-scarce learning of ground states

Authors: Kaito Wada, Nobuyuki Yoshioka
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.38513v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 3/5 · analog quantum simulation 1/5

Summary. This work establishes optimal resource trade-offs for estimating multiple ground-state observables in copy-scarce regimes. By developing catalytic readout protocols, the authors achieve near-optimal measurement accuracy while crucially returning the initial quantum copies nearly unchanged. This advances the understanding of quantum resource management in quantum sensing and characterization.

Why it may be interesting. The focus on resource trade-offs (copies vs. time) and the development of catalytic protocols that preserve quantum resources are highly relevant to quantum computation and quantum metrology.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of estimating multiple ground-state observables when conventional readout methods are too costly due to the need for many independent copies.

Main result. They derive a unifying inequality and construct catalytic readout protocols that achieve near-optimal trade-offs between initial copies and evolution time while returning the initial copies nearly unchanged.

Method. The approach involves deriving lower bounds via a static–dynamic distinguishability inequality and constructing parallel catalytic readout protocols utilizing controlled Hamiltonian evolution.

Model / system. The analysis focuses on a quantum system with a unique, gapped ground state $|\psi_0 angle$ governed by a Hamiltonian $H$. The task is estimating expectation values $\mu_j = \langle\psi_0|O_j|\psi_0 angle$ for multiple observables $O_j$.

Key observables. Expectation values of multiple observables ($\mu_j$), the number of initial copies ($P$), and the elapsed Hamiltonian time ($T$).

Important parameters / regimes. The spectral gap ($\Delta$) of the Hamiltonian, the desired error ($\epsilon$), and the number of observables ($M$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the existence of a unique gapped ground state and controlled access to Hamiltonian evolution $e^{-itH}$.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the optimal copy–dynamics trade-off with catalytic return, showing the general measurement circuit and the return of copies. Figure 2 details the readout process, including the joint phase-kickback mechanism for multi-signal extension.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the core problem, derives the fundamental lower bound inequality, constructs the catalytic readout protocols achieving the bound, and concludes with numerical benchmarks comparing the new method to specialized, copy-consuming protocols.

Abstract

Probing multiple properties of ground states is central to understanding quantum matter, yet conventional readout can be prohibitively costly because it consumes many independently prepared copies. Here, in copy-scarce regimes, we establish optimal trade-offs between the number of initial ground-state copies and elapsed Hamiltonian-evolution time required for estimating multiple observables or reconstructing a full classical description of a unique gapped ground state. We derive a unifying inequality that quantifies how well ground states can be distinguished using initial copies and controlled dynamics, even when all copies may be consumed. It yields worst-case lower bounds on elapsed Hamiltonian time that any protocol must incur to perform either task. We construct parallel catalytic readouts that return all initial copies nearly unchanged while attaining these bounds up to poly-logarithmic factors. Our protocols require no coherent preparation circuit or its inverse, and their elapsed Hamiltonian time depends only logarithmically on the inverse of the desired return error. Thus, optimal copy--dynamics trade-offs can be attained together with catalytic return. Numerical benchmarks further show substantial reductions in end-to-end elapsed Hamiltonian time relative to a task-specialized copy-consuming protocol, even under an optimistic preparation-cost model. Our results reveal a copy--dynamics resource landscape connecting copy-scarce learning to copy-only endpoints realized by known protocols for shadow tomography and pure-state tomography.

Entangling Atomic Quantum Memories Using High-Order Modulated Coherent States

Summary. This work proposes using high-order Quadrature Amplitude Modulation (QAM) on optical modes to significantly boost the rate of entanglement distribution between quantum memories. By optimizing the measurement scheme (POVM), the authors demonstrate that this approach surpasses conventional single-qubit protocols, paving the way for scalable quantum networking architectures.

Why it may be interesting. It provides concrete, quantitative results on scaling entanglement distribution beyond simple single-photon protocols by leveraging high-dimensional coherent state modulation, which is crucial for scaling quantum networks.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of distributing high-rate, high-fidelity entanglement between distant quantum memories using optical interconnects.

Main result. A high-order M-QAM modulation scheme, implemented with an SRM-inspired receiver, can distribute multiqubit entanglement, achieving rates significantly higher than single-qubit-per-mode protocols.

Method. The authors model the entanglement distribution using a collective midpoint POVM optimized for distillable entanglement, comparing the achievable rate against theoretical capacity bounds.

Model / system. The system involves reflective cavity-coupled atomic quantum memories (Alice and Bob) communicating via optical modes to a central receiver (Charlie). The protocol maps a q-qubit register onto an M=2^q QAM alphabet.

Key observables. Distillable entanglement rate (ebits/mode), achievable rate vs. loss, and the improvement factor over single-photon baselines.

Important parameters / regimes. End-to-end channel loss (L_ch), QAM order (M=2^q), and systematic conditional-phase error ($\delta\phi$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on constructing an explicit achievable receiver (SRM-inspired) and assumes the use of a strongly coupled reflective-cavity qubit–photon interface.

Figures summary. Figures compare entanglement generation rates across different modulation orders and losses, illustrate the optimization of QAM spacing versus loss, and compare the performance of different receiver designs (SRM vs. variational POVM).

Paper structure. The paper builds from defining the physical setup and the entanglement objective (maximizing distillable entanglement) to developing the mathematical framework using M-QAM states, deriving the achievable rate via the SRM-inspired POVM, and finally comparing this rate against theoretical benchmarks and alternative measurement strategies.

Abstract

High-order coherent-state modulation with reflective cavity-coupled atomic memories can distribute multiple heralded ebits per transmitted optical mode, but requires a collective midpoint POVM optimized for maximum distillable entanglement rather than minimum-error state discrimination. An SRM-inspired 16-QAM receiver reaches $1.75$ ebits per mode at $0.5$~dB end-to-end loss, $3.8$~dB below the half-link capacity benchmark. For 4-QAM, a variational POVM with more outcomes than constellation points improves the SRM rate by $4.0\%$ yet remains below capacity, revealing measurement-design headroom. The advantage over single-qubit-per-mode midpoint protocols persists for per-interface loss $<0.2$~dB and calibrated phase error $<0.2π$.

Experimentally Testable Quantum Advantage in Shallow Circuits

Authors: Kishor Bharti, Adán Cabello
Type: both · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.38775v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: QC/QI experiment 3/5 · quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work establishes a rigorous, experimentally testable method to prove quantum advantage in shallow quantum circuits by analyzing nonlocal games. It refines classical bounds to show that the classical success probability vanishes rapidly ($O(\log N/N)$) as the number of sites $N$ increases. This provides a clear roadmap for near-term quantum hardware demonstrations.

Why it may be interesting. It provides concrete, resource-aware protocols for quantum advantage testing, which is crucial for bridging theoretical quantum complexity with near-term experimental hardware capabilities.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of experimentally demonstrating quantum advantage using shallow quantum circuits by refining the classical bounds for nonlocal games.

Main result. They provide an explicit, experimentally testable protocol showing that the classical winning probability decays as $O(\log N/N)$ for large host graphs, while the quantum device wins with certainty.

Method. The method involves a two-round protocol using quantum teleportation across a host graph of $N$ sites, combined with disjoint-player repetition to suppress classical correlations.

Model / system. The model is based on testing nonlocal games ($G$) using $m$ disjoint copies played on a host graph of $N$ sites. The quantum advantage is demonstrated by comparing the quantum winning probability (unity) against the derived classical bound.

Key observables. Classical winning probability ($p_c$), quantum winning probability ($\omega_q$), and the required number of copies ($m$).

Important parameters / regimes. The depth ($D$) and fan-in ($K$) of the classical response circuit, and the number of sites ($N$) in the host graph.

Assumptions / limitations. The classical competitor is modeled as a randomized circuit with limited online depth ($D$) and fan-in ($K$), and the quantum implementation is assumed to be ideal (noiseless) for the theoretical bound.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the two-round protocol involving teleportation and measurement, and tables list classical thresholds ($B_c$) for various circuit depths ($D$) and qubit numbers ($Q$).

Paper structure. The paper develops the theoretical framework by refining the classical soundness bound, details the two-round protocol, derives the vanishing classical bound $O(\log N/N)$, and concludes with an explicit proposal for a 99-qubit test.

Abstract

Experimental tests of shallow-circuit quantum advantage require explicit classical bounds at finite circuit sizes. We refine the finite-size classical soundness bound of Aasnaess's graph-distributed construction to depend linearly on the number of players. Combined with standard disjoint-player repetition, this gives a two-round test on a single processor for any finite nonlocal game with a finite-dimensional perfect quantum strategy and classical winning probability $γ<1$, with arbitrarily small classical success. The method is based on playing $m$ copies with disjoint players and teleporting each player's register to a uniformly random one of $N$ sites before the questions are revealed. Each answer bit of a depth-$D$, fan-in-$K$ classical response with fixed wiring depends on at most $K^D$ question wires, making cross-player dependencies unlikely when $N$ is large. The quantum implementation has constant depth per round and wins with certainty. A classical device wins with probability at most $γ^m+O(mK^D/N)$, which vanishes as $O(\log N/N)$ for $m=\lceil\log_{1/γ}N\rceil$ at fixed $D$ and $K$. We present an explicit proposal for an experimentally testable quantum advantage with 99 qubits.

Exponential separation in sensing continuous signals via squeezing

Authors: Francesco Anna Mele, Nadine Meister, Haimeng Zhao, Senrui Chen, Hsin-Yuan Huang
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.38678v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 3/5 · quantum optics experiment 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical work quantifies the advantage of quantum squeezing in sensing continuous, time-varying signals. It proves that achieving polynomial sensing time requires the squeezing resource to scale at least as fast as $\omega(\sqrt{\log T})$ relative to the signal pattern size $T$. Below this threshold, the required sensing time explodes exponentially, marking a significant resource-dependent barrier in quantum sensing.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the resource scaling in quantum metrology for time-varying signals, providing concrete quantitative thresholds for when simple quantum resources like squeezing provide an exponential advantage over classical or unsqueezed quantum methods.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper establishes a fundamental separation in the required sensing time for continuously evolving quantum signals based on the amount of available quantum squeezing.

Main result. Sufficient squeezing ($z \geq \omega(\sqrt{\log T})$) allows for polynomial sensing time, whereas insufficient squeezing ($z \leq o(\sqrt{\log T})$) forces the sensing time to scale exponentially with the pattern size $T$.

Method. The authors analyze the sensing task using Gaussian quantum protocols, comparing the achievable time scaling against theoretical lower bounds derived from information-theoretic arguments.

Model / system. The system is a single bosonic mode (CV sensor) whose evolution is governed by a time-varying Hamiltonian $\hat{H}(t) = f(t) \hat{p}$, where $f(t)$ represents the unknown, continuously varying signal.

Key observables. The primary observables are the homodyne measurement outcomes ($ ilde{x}$) and the accumulated counts derived from these measurements, used to distinguish between signal hypotheses.

Important parameters / regimes. Key parameters include the pattern size $T$, the available squeezing parameter $z$, and the required sensing time $T_{ ext{sensing}}$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the ability to perform adaptive measurements and controls at arbitrary times, which is crucial for establishing the separation boundary.

Figures summary. Figures compare the sensing performance for coherent versus squeezed protocols, visually demonstrating the transition from polynomial to exponential time scaling based on the squeezing level relative to $\sqrt{\log T}$.

Paper structure. The paper develops the problem by first setting up the continuous signal model, then establishing the upper bound using Gaussian sensing protocols, and finally deriving the lower bound by showing that any strategy must overcome the exponential barrier unless sufficient squeezing is present.

Abstract

Quantum sensing traditionally focuses on using quantum resources such as squeezing and entanglement to improve precision for sensing fixed signals. However, in many applications such as gravitational-wave detection and electromagnetic-field sensing, the signal evolves continuously and varies through time. Additionally, the learner is free to prepare, control, and measure the sensor at arbitrary times, possibly chosen adaptively. In this work, we establish exponential separation in sensing time for continuously evolving signals due to the available squeezing. The signals we study are characterized by a pattern size $T$, and we find that a sensor with squeezing at least $ω(\sqrt{\log T})$ offers a $\mathrm{poly}(T)$ sensing time, whereas those with squeezing at most $o(\sqrt{\log T})$ must use an exponential sensing time in $T$.

Harnessing problem structure for end-to-end quantum speed-ups

Summary. This work introduces a novel framework to make quantum speed-ups robust by encoding the inherent structure of real-world problems into quantum circuits. By translating structural information into efficient state preparation, the authors demonstrate that previously lost quantum advantages can be recovered for combinatorial optimization tasks. This establishes problem structure as a quantifiable computational resource for quantum algorithms.

Why it may be interesting. The explicit use of structured state preparation circuits, particularly those involving Rydberg-blockade chains, directly connects theoretical quantum complexity with physical implementations in AMO platforms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To determine if quantum speed-ups persist in real-world applications when the full overhead cost of encoding classical data is accounted for (end-to-end cost accounting).

Main result. By introducing structure-aware quantum data encoding, the authors show that exploiting the problem's inherent structure can restore quantum speed-ups that were otherwise eliminated by encoding overhead.

Method. They propose a general, recursive framework that compiles compact recursive descriptions of problem structure directly into efficient state-preparation circuits, reducing encoding complexity.

Model / system. The framework is applied to combinatorial optimization problems, such as the uncapacitated facility-location problem, and general structured state preparation $|\psi_\pi angle = \sum_{x \in F} \sqrt{\pi(x)} |x angle$.

Key observables. The quantum speed-up factor, the circuit depth, and the gate count scaling relative to the problem structure's expansion dimension $d$.

Important parameters / regimes. The expansion dimension $d$, which governs the complexity of the structural description; the size of the feasible set $F$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the strong exponential time hypothesis for proving asymptotic speed-ups, and for some applications, the assumption of non-negative costs is made.

It then details the resource scaling for six representative structured data families using a local-update principle. Abstract

Quantum algorithms can offer substantial computational speed-ups, yet these advantages may disappear once the cost of structure-agnostic classical data encoding is taken into account. Real-world problem instances, however, often possess rich internal structure. This raises a fundamental question: can such structure be harnessed to make quantum speed-ups survive end-to-end cost accounting? Here we show that it can. We introduce a general framework for structure-aware quantum data encoding that compiles compact recursive descriptions of problem structure into efficient state-preparation circuits, translating structural information directly into reduced encoding complexity. For the uncapacitated facility-location problem, structure-agnostic encoding admits a classical dequantization that eliminates the quadratic quantum speed-up, whereas exploiting the underlying problem structure restores this advantage even when state-preparation costs are included. More broadly, the same framework prepares structured quantum states for combinatorial optimization that capture non-trivial relations among constraints, including group balance, conflicts and synergistic rewards, extending previously reported super-polynomial quantum advantages to broader classes of objectives. These results establish exploitable problem structure as a computational resource for quantum algorithms and provide a systematic route towards realizing quantum advantage in data-intensive problems.

Multipartite entanglement hidden in vector-chiral correlations of spin-1/2 chains

Summary. This paper develops a method to quantify multipartite entanglement in quantum spin chains by relating it to the Quantum Fisher Information (QFI) of the vector-chiral correlation. By establishing rigorous upper bounds on this QFI, the authors show that exceeding these bounds certifies a specific depth of entanglement. This provides a powerful, experimentally accessible diagnostic tool for quantum materials research.

Why it may be interesting. This work bridges the gap between abstract entanglement measures and measurable magnetic responses (dielectric loss), which is highly relevant for experimentalists studying quantum materials and strongly correlated systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The central challenge is characterizing and measuring multipartite entanglement in quantum materials using only experimentally accessible observables.

Main result. The study demonstrates that multipartite entanglement is encoded in vector-chiral correlations in the $J_1-J_2$ XXZ chain, providing a new diagnostic route via dielectric spectroscopy.

Method. The authors establish rigorous bounds on the Quantum Fisher Information (QFI) associated with the total nearest-neighbour vector chirality, combining iDMRG and thermal quantum-state calculations.

Model / system. The system analyzed is a spin-1/2 chain, specifically an extended $J_1-J_2$ XXZ chain, which is relevant to multiferroic magnets. The Hamiltonian includes nearest-neighbor and next-nearest-neighbor interactions.

Key observables. Vector Chirality ($\kappa_i$), Total Vector Chirality ($K_{tot}$), and the Quantum Fisher Information (QFI) associated with $K_{tot}$.

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature ($T$), chain length ($N$), and the entanglement depth parameter ($k$).

Assumptions / limitations. The QFI-based criterion is sufficient but not necessary for multipartite entanglement. The analysis relies on bounding the QFI for $k$-producible states.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the theoretical bound for the QFI related to vector chirality. It then applies this bound to the $J_1-J_2$ XXZ chain at both zero and finite temperatures, culminating in a comparison with experimental measurement techniques.

Abstract

Characterizing entanglement in quantum materials through experimentally accessible observables remains a central challenge. Quantum Fisher information associated with sums of one-site spin operators provides one route by linking entanglement to measurable magnetic response functions. Extending this approach to local composite observables requires new entanglement bounds. Here we establish a rigorous bound for the total nearest-neighbour vector chirality of periodic spin-$1/2$ chains with an even number of sites. The quantum Fisher information per spin cannot exceed $k+1$ for any $k$-producible state, so exceeding this bound certifies entanglement depth of at least $k+1$. Combining infinite density-matrix renormalization group and thermal pure quantum-state calculations, we demonstrate multipartite entanglement encoded in vector-chiral correlations at zero and finite temperatures in an extended $J_1$-$J_2$ XXZ chain. The spin-current coupling between vector chirality and electric polarization offers, in principle, access to this witness through dielectric loss spectroscopy in multiferroic magnets, extending entanglement diagnostics beyond dipolar spin correlations.

Qantum Well Resonant Tunneling Diode Probe of Correlated States in Twisted Bilayer MoS$_2$

Summary. This paper reports on the use of a novel Quantum Well Resonant Tunneling Diode (QWRTD) to investigate correlated electronic states in twisted bilayer MoS2. Why it may be interesting. This work provides a highly controlled, local experimental realization of strongly correlated physics in 2D van der Waals materials, offering a platform to test theoretical models of Mott and Wigner insulators.

Main problem. The study aims to probe emergent quantum phases, specifically strongly correlated insulating states, within moiré superlattices formed in twisted bilayer MoS2.

Main result. The authors report direct evidence of a robust integer correlated insulating state at filling factor $\nu=1$ and a displacement-field-induced fractional insulating state at $\nu=3/4$, which is characterized as a potential generalized Wigner crystal.

Model / system. The system is near-AB-stacked twisted bilayer MoS2 forming moiré superlattices. The physics is governed by strong electronic correlations, expected to be in the regime where the on-site Coulomb interaction $U$ is comparable to or larger than the bandwidth $W$.

Key observables. Differential conductance ($dI/dV_{ ext{Probe}}$) measured as a function of filling factor ($ u$) and displacement field ($D$).

Abstract

Moiré superlattices formed in transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDs) offer a versatile platform for exploring emergent quantum phases arising from strong electronic correlations. In this work, we develop a new experimental platform, the quantum well resonant tunneling diode (QWRTD), to probe the electronic landscape of $\approx 57^\circ$ twisted bilayer MoS$_2$ (tMoS$_2$). By measuring the differential conductance ($dI/dV_{\text{Probe}}$) as a function of filling factor $ν$ and displacement field $D$, we observe a robust integer correlated insulating state at $ν= 1$ that persists across the measured displacement field range. Our results provide direct evidence of correlated states in near-AB-stacked tMoS$_2$ and establish QWRTD as a powerful experimental tool for investigating strong correlations and topology in van der Waals heterostructures.

Abstract

Mutually unbiased bases (MUBs) describe quantum measurements for which certainty in one basis gives uniform outcomes in the others. Their maximum number remains unknown in dimensions that are not powers of a prime. We introduce an ansatz that, in many dimensions, allows us to construct more MUBs than have previously been found. Each basis in the ansatz consists of a diagonal phase matrix applied to a fixed unitary, which is a tensor product of Fourier matrices and, optionally, any real Hadamard matrix. This construction yields five MUBs in dimension 12, six in dimensions 48, 96, and 192, seven in dimensions 36 and 108, and ten in dimension 648. These bases can then be extended to exceed the tensor product bound, asymptotically, in every eighteenth dimension. Moreover, we show that Paley's real Hadamard matrix can be used to construct $q+1$ bases in dimension $d = q(q+1)$ for every prime power $q\equiv3\pmod4$. This count grows as $\sqrt d$ and cannot be exceeded by tensor products of smaller sets.

Abstract

Understanding the complexity of low-energy quantum states is a central problem in quantum complexity theory and many-body physics. Here we prove the no low-energy trivial magic (NLTM) conjecture, constructing a family of local Hamiltonians for which every state within a positive energy density window requires circuit depth $Ω(\log n)$ to prepare from arbitrary stabilizer state that may themselves be highly entangled. Notably, our result is intrinsic in the sense that the same Hamiltonian family and energy density threshold apply to arbitrary qudit stabilizer inputs with any bounded local dimensions. Our construction builds on non-Abelian gauged sheaf codes obtained using the cup product structure on good quantum locally testable codes. We establish constant operator soundness for the associated Hamiltonians, enabling their protected logical structure to constrain all states at sufficiently low energy density. By excluding shallow stabilizer-based classical witnesses, Our result substantially strengthens NLTS and advances our understanding of low-energy complexity relevant to quantum PCP.
