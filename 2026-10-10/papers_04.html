Spin-outcome correlations and memory-assisted entropic uncertainty in Bhabha scattering with an entangled spectator

Authors: Zan Cao, Meng-Long Song, Xue-Ke Song, Liu Ye, Dong Wang
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.11629v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 3/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical paper analyzes how initial entanglement between two particles (B and C) affects the measurement uncertainty of a third particle (A) during Bhabha scattering. By quantifying memory-assisted entropic uncertainty, the authors show that the spectator particle C acts as a quantum memory, whose initial entanglement can reduce the overall uncertainty in the scattering process near backscattering angles.

Why it may be interesting. It applies advanced quantum information concepts (uncertainty relations, entanglement) to a high-energy particle physics process, providing insights into how quantum correlations govern measurement limits in scattering experiments.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates spin-outcome correlations and memory-assisted entropic uncertainty in Bhabha scattering when one particle is initially entangled with a spectator.

Main result. Initial entanglement between the scattering positron and the spectator can lower the total memory-assisted uncertainty in high-energy backscattering by strengthening spin-outcome correlations with the spectator.

Method. The analysis uses quantum information diagnostics, including entanglement entropy, memory-assisted entropic uncertainty, and outcome-memory mutual information sums.

Model / system. The system is tree-level QED Bhabha scattering ($e^-e^+ o e^-e^+$) where the positron (B) is initially entangled with a spectator electron (C). The analysis focuses on the post-selected three-particle scattering state.

Key observables. Entanglement entropy $S( ho_A)$, total memory-assisted uncertainty $U_A$, outcome-memory mutual-information sums $G_B$ and $G_C$.

Important parameters / regimes. Initial entanglement weight $\eta$, scattering angle $ heta$, and high-energy backscattering limit ($ heta \approx \pi$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is restricted to an idealized fixed-angle post-selected spin state, and some calculations neglect forward-scattering interference terms.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the angular dependence of $U_A$ and $S( ho_A)$ for varying entanglement parameters, and density plots show the relationship between these quantities in the $( heta, \mu)$ plane.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theoretical framework by defining the initial entangled state, calculating the relevant quantum information measures ($U_A, S( ho_A), G_B, G_C$), and then analyzing the behavior of these measures in specific physical limits, such as high-energy backscattering.

Abstract

We investigate spin-outcome correlations and memory-assisted entropic uncertainty in tree-level Bhabha scattering, where an incident electron $A$ scatters with a positron $B$ initially entangled with a spectator electron $C$. For the fixed-angle post-selected final state, we compare the total memory-assisted uncertainty $U_A$ for three alternative Pauli measurements on $A$ with the entanglement entropy $S(ρ_A)$ across the $A|BC$ partition. We resolve $U_A$ into the separate uncertainties $U_B$ and $U_C$ and evaluate the corresponding outcome-memory mutual-information sums $G_B$ and $G_C$. This separates changes in the randomness of the outcomes on $A$ from changes in their correlations with each memory. In the parameter regions studied, enhanced $A|BC$ entanglement is accompanied by lower $U_A$, most prominently near high-energy backscattering. An analytic treatment of this limit shows that $B$ factorizes from $AC$: $G_B$ vanishes, whereas $G_C$ increases with the initial $B$-$C$ entanglement. An entropic complete complementarity analysis characterizes the accompanying loss of local helicity predictability in $ρ_A$ and changes in the coherence, predictability, and mixedness of the interacting pair $ρ_{AB}$. These results show that initial $B$-$C$ entanglement can lower the total memory-assisted uncertainty in high-energy backscattering by strengthening spin-outcome correlations with the spectator $C$, thereby identifying the distinct roles of the interacting particle and the spectator as quantum memories.

Symmetric Mode Ansatz: Theory of Magnon Collective Modes in Chern Insulators

Authors: Miguel Gonçalves, Jonah Herzog-Arbeitman, Juan Felipe Mendez-Valderrama, Andreas Feuerpfeil, B. Andrei Bernevig, Nicolas Regnault
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.10706v1

Summary. This work introduces the Symmetric Mode Ansatz (SyMA) to theoretically describe the low-energy magnon excitations in Chern and fractional Chern insulators found in Moiré materials. By applying the SyMA to Hubbard models, the authors derive rigorous quantum geometric bounds on the magnon gap. This provides a powerful, unified theoretical tool for understanding magnetism stabilized by topological band structures.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous derivation of bounds on the magnon gap using topological invariants (Chern number, Berry curvature) connects fundamental concepts from band theory and topology to the stability of magnetic order in strongly correlated systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to clarify the nature and origin of the magnon gap in Chern insulators (CIs) and fractional Chern insulators (FCIs) stabilized by ferromagnetism in Moiré materials.

Main result. The Symmetric Mode Ansatz (SyMA) provides a highly accurate theoretical description of the low-energy magnon spectrum, leading to quantum geometric bounds on the magnon gap.

Method. The authors develop and apply the generalized single-mode approximation (SyMA) to the magnon Hamiltonian derived from Hubbard-type models.

Model / system. The study focuses on Moiré materials, specifically twisted MoTe2, which host FCIs due to spontaneous time-reversal symmetry breaking. The theoretical framework is tested using Hubbard models and the First-Harmonic (FH) model.

Key observables. Magnon spectrum/gap ($\Delta$), Chern number ($C$), Berry curvature ($F(k)$), and quantum metric ($G$).

Important parameters / regimes. Filling fractions ($ u$), magnetic coupling strength ($U$), and the twist angle ($ heta$).

Assumptions / limitations. The SyMA is assumed to be exact or highly accurate for certain solvable Hubbard models; approximations are made when neglecting single-particle terms in the low-energy magnon analysis.

Figures summary. Figures compare the exact magnon spectrum against the SyMA predictions across various fillings ($ u$), showing agreement at integer filling and providing gap energy dependence on $ u$.

Paper structure. The paper develops the SyMA theory, tests it on analytically solvable Hubbard models to derive general bounds, and then applies it to the specific FH model of MoTe2 to analyze the magnon gap across integer and fractional fillings.

Abstract

Moiré materials host integer and fractional Chern insulators (FCIs) where time-reversal is broken spontaneously by flat band ferromagnetism. In twisted MoTe2, unlike in quantum Hall, opposite spins carry opposite Chern numbers and realize Ising ferromagnetism with gapped magnon excitations. Clarifying the nature of these collective modes and the origin of the magnon gap is key to understanding the stability of the Chern magnets in these materials. To do so, we develop a generalized single-mode approximation, which we call the symmetric mode ansatz (SyMA), to describe the low-energy magnon spectrum. We find it to be in exceptional agreement with numerical calculations at integer filling, and qualitatively accurate across fractional fillings including FCIs. To explain the accuracy of the SyMA, we present two families of Hubbard models (including the Haldane model and continuum ideal bands) whose collective modes are analytically solvable and for which the SyMA is either exact or an accurate approximation. For a class of these models where SyMA is exact, we prove two-sided bounds on the zero-momentum magnon gap in terms of the Chern number, Berry curvature, and quantum metric, revealing that uniform quantum geometry is sufficient for stabilizing magnetism in Chern bands.

Transducing quantum-spin-ice correlations into Weyl Fermi-arc transport at a synthetic Kondo lattice interface

Authors: Tsung-Chi Wu, Michael Terilli, Christian Zaprianov, Eun Sang Choi, David Graf, Qinghua Zhang, Lin Gu, Mikhail Kareev

Summary. The authors demonstrate a novel method to probe quantum-spin-ice correlations by coupling them to Weyl semimetal surface states via a synthetic Kondo lattice interface. By measuring the angular magnetoresistance, they observe a unique twelvefold symmetry reconstruction only when coupled to a quantum spin-ice candidate ($ ext{Tb}_2 ext{Ti}_2 ext{O}_7$). This confirms that the electronic transport acts as a sensitive transducer for the underlying multipolar quantum magnetic order.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a highly tunable, solid-state platform to probe exotic quantum magnetic correlations (multipolar moments) by mapping them onto measurable electronic transport symmetries, which is relevant for understanding strongly correlated interfacial physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to demonstrate a method to encode and detect quantum-spin-ice correlations, particularly those involving multipolar degrees of freedom, into measurable electronic transport properties.

Main result. The quantum-spin-ice interface ($ ext{Tb}_2 ext{Ti}_2 ext{O}_7$) exhibits a unique $ ext{6-fold} o ext{12-fold} o ext{6-fold}$ reentrant anisotropy in the angular magnetoresistance, a signature absent in the classical spin-ice reference ($ ext{Dy}_2 ext{Ti}_2 ext{O}_7$).

Method. The research uses in-plane and out-of-plane angular magnetoresistance measurements on a synthetic Kondo lattice interface at ultra-low temperatures.

Model / system. The platform is a synthetic Kondo lattice interface formed between a Weyl semimetal ($ ext{Eu}_2 ext{Ir}_2 ext{O}_7$) and a quantum-spin-ice candidate ($ ext{Tb}_2 ext{Ti}_2 ext{O}_7$), allowing the coupling of itinerant Weyl surface states to frustrated local moments.

Key observables. Angular magnetoresistance ($ ext{MR}$), specifically the symmetry reconstruction observed as a transition from sixfold to twelvefold anisotropy ($ ext{MR}_{12}/ ext{MR}_6$).

Important parameters / regimes. Ultra-low temperatures ($ ext{20 mK}$), varying magnetic field strength, and the comparison between quantum ($ ext{Tb}$) and classical ($ ext{Dy}$) spin-ice couplings.

Assumptions / limitations. The electronic transport is sensitive to the specific magnetic degrees of freedom (dipolar vs. multipolar) present in the adjacent spin-ice material.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the structural interface, the field evolution of angular magnetoresistance showing the $ ext{6-fold} o ext{12-fold} o ext{6-fold}$ sequence for the quantum case, and direct comparisons with the classical case.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the physical system, details the measurement techniques (ADMR), presents the key comparative results showing the distinct symmetry signatures ($ ext{12-fold}$ vs. $ ext{2-fold}$), and concludes by establishing the synthetic Kondo lattice as a transduction route.

Abstract

Quantum spin ice hosts collective excitations and quantum fluctuations that are largely electrically silent because the materials supporting them are insulating. Coupling this magnetic environment to surface-localized Weyl states offers a route to encode its correlations directly into Fermi-arc transport. Experimental access to this regime, however, has remained largely unexplored. Here, we realize a synthetic Kondo lattice interface between the Weyl semimetal $\mathrm{Eu_2Ir_2O_7}$ and the quantum-spin-ice candidate $\mathrm{Tb_2Ti_2O_7}$, and demonstrate that quantum-spin-ice correlations are imprinted on Weyl Fermi-arc transport. At ultra-low temperature, electronic transport evolves from a sixfold anisotropic response at low field into a twelvefold response over an intermediate field window, before entering a reentrant sixfold anisotropy at high field. No analogous twelvefold response occurs at the classical-spin-ice interface. The quantum--classical contrast shows that the same itinerant electronic channel acquires qualitatively different symmetry information when interacting with the quantum-fluctuating Tb moments, consistent with an interfacial coupling between the Weyl surface states and the transverse, multipolar degrees of freedom, in addition to the longitudinal, dipolar contribution. Our work establishes synthetic Kondo lattice interfaces as a route for transducing frustrated quantum magnetism with multipole correlations into distinct Weyl Fermi-arc transport responses.

Summary. This paper solves a major theoretical hurdle in quantum gravity by developing the algebraic Petz map to reconstruct information trapped inside a black hole's 'island' from Hawking radiation. It achieves this by leveraging advanced tools from modular theory, bypassing the need for direct causal connections between the interior and the exterior. The result provides a concrete, computable dictionary proving that black hole information is preserved and accessible.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on gravity, the reliance on modular theory, algebraic reconstruction, and the mapping between different quantum algebras provides deep mathematical tools applicable to open quantum systems and entanglement structure in general.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the black hole information paradox by providing an operational, algebraic method to reconstruct quantum operators trapped inside a black hole's 'island' from measurable Hawking radiation.

Main result. The authors construct a rigorous dictionary using the algebraic Petz map, demonstrating that information inside the black hole is mathematically recoverable from the asymptotic radiation.

Method. The reconstruction relies on Tomita–Takesaki modular theory, mapping the island operators to their algebraic complement, and then using a sequence of modular conjugations and a $ ho$-dual map to project them onto the radiation algebra.

Model / system. The system is an evaporating black hole coupled to a non-gravitating bath, analyzed using the framework of quantum field theory in curved spacetime and holographic duality (AdS/CFT).

Key observables. Island operators ($\phi_I$), Hawking radiation operators ($\Phi_R$), and the resulting reconstructed mirror operators ($\Phi_A$).

Important parameters / regimes. The Page time, the large-$N$ limit, and the background quantum state $|\psi_g angle$.

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes the relevant state is cyclic and separating, and it relies on the algebraic duality relation $A'_I = ar{A}_I$ to bypass the need for a direct global isometry.

Paper structure. The paper builds the reconstruction protocol by first establishing the failure of standard entanglement wedge methods, then introducing the algebraic Petz map via modular theory, and finally demonstrating its application to yield the mirror operator structure.

Abstract

The island proposal guarantees a unitary Page curve for evaporating black holes, implying that operators deep behind the horizon must be fully reconstructable from asymptotic Hawking radiation. However, because the interior is causally disconnected and highly dynamical, standard entanglement wedge reconstruction methods, relying on global isometries or continuous modular flow, fail to provide this mapping. We resolve this by constructing a protocol for island reconstruction using the algebraic Petz map. By using modular theory, we reflect island operators into the accessible complement, transport them to the boundary, and map them into the radiation algebra. Furthermore, we demonstrate that this purely algebraic derivation naturally yields the structure of Papadodimas-Raju mirror operators. This framework provides a rigorous operational dictionary for the island formula, proving that information trapped inside a black hole can be decoded.

Mutual Linearity of Complexes in Chemical Reaction Networks

Authors: Pedro E. Harunari, Guilherme Fiusa, Matteo Polettini
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.11970v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 2/5 · Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5 · correlated / nonlocal dissipation 1/5 · driven-dissipative phase transition 1/5 · methods for driven-dissipative 1/5 · non-equilibrium universality 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work establishes a powerful structural property called 'mutual linearity' for the activities of complexes in chemical reaction networks. It proves that this linearity holds exactly, even far from equilibrium, provided the network has zero deficiency. This relationship serves as a powerful diagnostic tool, as its violation signals structural flaws like non-zero deficiency.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical chemical kinetics, the mathematical machinery—relating structural invariants (deficiency) to algebraic constraints (linearity)—is highly relevant to understanding constraints in open quantum systems or quantum field theories where non-equilibrium steady states are studied.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates finding exact, non-linear relationships (mutual linearity) describing how a system far from equilibrium responds to changes in its reaction rate constants.

Main result. Mutual linearity holds not for species concentrations, but for the activities of the reaction complexes ($\psi_c$), yielding a three-term linear relation independent of the controlled rate constants.

Method. The proof utilizes concepts from Markov chain theory, specifically analyzing the coplanarity of spanning-tree polynomials derived from the complex-space Laplacian.

Model / system. The system is Chemical Reaction Networks (CRNs) operating under mass-action kinetics, analyzed in the context of jump processes and complex-balance equations.

Key observables. Stationary activities of complexes ($\psi_c$), which are mass-action monomials; the relationship $\Delta_{bc}\psi_a + \Delta_{ca}\psi_b + \Delta_{ab}\psi_c = 0$.

Important parameters / regimes. Zero deficiency ($\delta=0$) and the condition of having a single linkage class are crucial structural requirements for the theorem to hold.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the network is zero-deficiency and that the controlled reaction is bidirectional and not a bridge.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the theorem using a Michaelis-Menten cycle, showing the linear collapse of the mutual linearity product. Figure 2 demonstrates the relation holding for a complex network and its violation when the deficiency increases.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the problem of non-equilibrium response, proves the existence of mutual linearity in complex activities for specific network structures, derives the general linear relation, and uses this relation to test structural invariants like network deficiency.

Abstract

Exact statements about how a system far from equilibrium responds to changes in its rate constants are scarce beyond linear response, yet they are central to the control of biochemical networks. A notable exception is mutual linearity: in a jump process, controlling the rates of a single transition makes any two stationary probabilities obey an exact affine relation, arbitrarily far from equilibrium. With nonlinear mass-action kinetics, and conservation laws in place of a normalization, chemical reaction networks seem to defy mutual linearity since species concentrations obey no such relation. We show that it was there all along, in the complexes' activities (mass-action monomials) rather than in the concentrations of species. For zero-deficiency networks with a single linkage class, controlling one reaction makes any three stationary activities obey a linear relation whose coefficients are independent of the controlled rate constants, the conserved quantities (hence the initial state), and even of the stoichiometry of complexes. This predicts hidden activities and bounds the activity ratios the control can reach, while a violation signals nonzero deficiency or multiple linkage classes.

Summary. This paper introduces a non-perturbative method to model how molecular vibrations are excited by strong, time-varying electric fields. By mapping the dynamics onto a complex phase-space coordinate, the authors provide a unified description that accurately predicts vibrational populations in both resonant and non-resonant regimes. This framework is crucial for understanding and controlling molecular energy transfer in intense light fields.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, non-perturbative phase-space picture for controlling molecular vibrations using intense light fields, which is central to ultrafast spectroscopy and quantum control.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Developing a non-perturbative quantum framework to describe vibrational excitation in molecular systems driven by arbitrarily time-dependent external electric fields.

Main result. The dynamics are exactly mapped onto the evolution of a complex phase-space coordinate, yielding a Poisson distribution for vibrational populations that correctly captures both resonant enhancement and non-resonant suppression.

Method. The authors utilize an exact coherent-state solution for the driven harmonic oscillator, allowing the analysis to remain valid in the strong-field regime where standard time-dependent perturbation theory fails.

Model / system. The system models a single normal mode of a molecule with a harmonic potential and a linear dipole coupling ($\mu(q) = \mu_0 + \mu'_0 T \cdot q$) interacting with an external electric field $\vec{E}(t)$.

Key observables. Vibrational populations ($P_n(t)$), the time-dependent phase-space displacement ($S_t = |z_t|^2$), and the phase-space trajectory governed by $z_t$.

Important parameters / regimes. Resonant vs. non-resonant driving ($\omega$ vs. $\omega_0$), field strength ($E_0$), and detuning ($\Delta$).

Assumptions / limitations. The dipole operator is approximated by an affine function of the normal coordinate ($q$).

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the phase-space trajectories for resonant (outward spiral) and non-resonant driving, and compare the results of the exact coherent-state method against Time-Dependent Perturbation Theory (TDPT) in both weak and strong-field limits.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the non-perturbative coherent-state formalism, derives the closed-form expression for the phase-space coordinate $z_t$, analyzes the distinct behaviors in resonant and non-resonant regimes, and validates the approach by showing its reduction to TDPT in the weak-field limit.

Abstract

We present a non-perturbative framework for the quantum treatment of vibrational excitation in molecular systems driven by electric fields with arbitrary time dependence. For harmonic potentials with linear dipole coupling, the field-induced dynamics is exactly described by a displaced coherent state, whose evolution is completely determined by a complex phase-space coordinate. Closed-form expressions for this coordinate reveal a clear distinction between resonant and non-resonant regimes. The resulting vibrational populations follow a Poisson distribution determined by a dimensionless time-dependent phase-space displacement, for both resonant and non-resonant driving. Resonant driving results in an enhanced phase-space displacement, whereas finite detuning between the driving and vibrational frequencies leads to an exponential suppression of the phase-space displacement and vibrational excitation. While the approach reduces to the standard time-dependent perturbation theory in the weak-field limit, it remains valid in the strong-field regime, where perturbative treatments fail. Our results provide a transparent phase-space picture of vibrational control and enable non-perturbative state preparation in ultrafast spectroscopy.

Summary. This paper tackles the challenge of efficiently simulating flow models on quantum computers by leveraging the formal connection between flow dynamics and quantum wavefunction evolution. Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the computational bottleneck of simulating continuous physical processes (like quantum evolution or fluid dynamics) on near-term quantum hardware by integrating advanced generative ML techniques (flow matching) with quantum circuit design.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to develop an efficient quantum circuit implementation for simulating flow models, which are used to model probability path transport.

Method. The core method involves simulating wavefunction flow, which is connected to flow models via a continuity Hamiltonian, using a phase kickback framework enhanced by QNNs for data encoding.

Model / system. The system involves flow models generating trajectories via an ODE solved by a velocity field, which is formally linked to quantum dynamics via the continuity Hamiltonian driving Schrödinger evolution.

Abstract

Flow models generate trajectories from an initial distribution to a target distribution by solving an ordinary differential equation defined by a velocity field. Flow matching learns this velocity field by modeling the transport dynamics between the two distributions. Wavefunction flow establishes a formal connection between flow models and quantum dynamics by introducing a continuity Hamiltonian, which drives the Schrödinger evolution of quantum states. In this paper, we investigate accurate and efficient quantum simulation of the wavefunction flow, thereby realizing the efficient implementation of flow models on quantum computers. We first leverage a quantum read-only memory (QROM)-based phase kickback framework for the wavefunction flow simulation, generating probability densities that closely match those produced by the corresponding conventional flow model. To address the high circuit-resource cost, we further incorporate a trained quantum neural network (QNN) into the phase kickback framework, replacing QROM for data encoding. Numerical experiments demonstrate that our proposed method implements flow models on quantum computers more efficiently, since it maintains the accuracy of wavefunction flow simulation compared with the QROM-based framework, and significantly reduces the circuit resources.

While this construction has appeared in the literature, a suitable mathematical justification is lacking. In this work, we show that the security of such protocols, under certain simple conditions, follows from a slightly modified notion of secrecy of the protocol without the final error-verification step. In particular, we show that for most entropy-based proofs, the combined protocol can be implemented with no change to the key rate, and a minor change to the security parameter.

Abstract

We study bond percolation on the one-dimensional lattice in which two sites at distance $r$ are connected with probability $p_r=C/r^{1+σ}$, where $0 < σ< 1$. An infinite cluster exists if $C$ exceeds a critical value $C_c(σ)$, which is not known exactly: the available results are the lower bound of Schulman, $C_c\ge1/[2ζ(1+σ)]$, and numerical estimates. Here we prove explicit upper bounds. We apply a second-moment argument to a family of random monotone paths whose steps have a heavy-tailed (Sibuya) distribution. Two such paths meet only a finite number of times, and their overlap is computed exactly by renewal theory. The result is a closed-form bound, which gives $C_c\leσ+O(σ^2)$ as $σ\to0$ and is below $1$ for $σ< 0.667$. Since the paths are monotone, the bound holds also for oriented percolation, and it determines the oriented threshold asymptotically: $C_c^{\rightarrow}ζ(1+σ)\to1$ as $σ\to0$. A coarse-grained version of the argument, with blocks of sites in place of sites, gives an explicit bound $C_c < 1$ for every $σ< 1$. We compare the bounds with the known numerical values of $C_c$.
