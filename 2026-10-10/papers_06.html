Authors: Dong-Ping Xuan, Hua Nan, Zhi-Xi Wang, Shao-Ming Fei

Summary. This paper introduces the unitary-accessible coherence-erasure distance, a metric measuring the minimum unitary effort to make a quantum state incoherent while keeping its spectrum fixed. The authors derive exact formulas for qubits and establish a tight universal bound for qutrits. This distance has direct operational meaning as the minimum time required for coherence erasure under bounded unitary driving.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous, quantifiable measure of how much 'coherence' must be erased via unitary control, which is fundamental for understanding quantum state preparation and decoherence processes in controlled quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper introduces and analyzes the unitary-accessible coherence-erasure distance, $D_{U|I}( ho)$, which quantifies the minimum unitary control cost to transform a quantum state into an incoherent state while preserving its spectrum.

Main result. For qutrits, the authors derive a tight universal bound for this distance: $\arccos\left[ rac{2\sqrt{2} - 1}{4} ight]$, and provide an explicit eigenbasis that achieves this bound.

Method. The analysis involves establishing relationships between this distance, orbit-restricted quantum transport, and the Bures geometry, culminating in a detailed optimization over the monomial unitary group for the qutrit case.

Model / system. The study concerns general quantum states $ ho$ on a $d$-dimensional Hilbert space, focusing on the dynamics restricted to unitary evolution. The target states are restricted to the intersection of the unitary orbit and the set of incoherent states ($I_ ho$).

Key observables. Unitary-accessible coherence-erasure distance ($D_{U|I}( ho)$), coherence-erasure cost, and the minimum unitary-control cost.

Important parameters / regimes. Qubit and qutrit dimensions ($d=2, 3$), the specific bound $\arccos[(2\sqrt{2}-1)/4]$, and the state's eigenbasis orientation.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on restricting the target state set to $I_ ho = I \cap O_ ho$, and for the qutrit case, it involves extensive optimization over the monomial unitary group $M_3$.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the restriction of the optimization to $I_ ho$ and show the reduction of the distance calculation to an optimization problem over the eigenbasis relative to the monomial unitary group.

Paper structure. The paper first defines the distance and relates it to existing metrics. It derives exact results for qubits. For qutrits, the problem is reduced to an optimization over the monomial unitary group, leading to a systematic, constraint-based proof to establish the tight universal bound.

Abstract

We introduce the unitary-accessible coherence-erasure distance, which measures the minimum unitary-control cost required to transform a quantum state into an incoherent state without changing its spectrum. The target states are restricted to incoherent states on the same unitary orbit, leading to a geometry different from conventional state-space proximity. We establish inequalities relating orbit-restricted quantum transport, Bures geometry, and the unitary-control distance. These quantities coincide for pure states but can differ for mixed states. For qubits, we derive an exact expression and show that, in the nondegenerate case, the coherence-erasure cost depends on the eigenbasis orientation rather than on the eigenvalues. For nondegenerate qutrits, we reduce the problem to an optimization over the monomial unitary group. We derive the tight universal bound $\arccos[(2\sqrt2-1)/4]$ and construct an explicit eigenbasis that attains it. The resulting distance also determines the minimum coherence-erasure time under bounded unitary driving, giving it a direct operational meaning.

Summary. This theoretical paper connects the spectral quantization of a singular sextic oscillator to fractional-power potentials by identifying a common 'power-Liouville Hermite orbit.' Using advanced techniques like coordinate transformations and semiclassical matching, the authors prove that these diverse potentials share an identical underlying quantization structure. This work provides a unified mathematical framework for understanding spectral properties across different physical models.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical connection established between multiple exactly solvable potentials via a single spectral orbit is highly relevant for understanding the underlying symmetries and quantization rules in quantum mechanics, applicable to systems modeled by complex differential equations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to establish a deep mathematical connection between the spectral quantization of a singular sextic oscillator and various fractional-power potentials.

Main result. It demonstrates that these potentials share a common 'power-Liouville Hermite orbit,' characterized by an exact discrete sequence of quartic Sturmian eigencouplings for the sextic oscillator.

Method. The analysis employs power-Liouville coordinate transformations and analyzes the invariance of the Wronskian zero set across different spectral charts derived from the biconfluent-Heun equation.

Model / system. The core model is the singular sextic oscillator, analyzed within the framework of the biconfluent Heun equation. This system is shown to be spectrally equivalent to potentials like $x^{2/3}$ and inverse-square-root potentials.

Key observables. The exact discrete sequence of quartic Sturmian eigencouplings, the vanishing of the degree-five accessory compatibility obstruction, and the common physical zero set (Wronskian zero set).

Important parameters / regimes. The index $N=4$ of the Hermite hierarchy, and the quantization parameter derived from the Frobenius index ($ u=5$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on selecting a specific 'physical slice' and assumes that spectral equivalence is defined by the preservation of the physical zero set under reparametrization.

Figures summary. Not specified, but the notes reference Table 2 showing numerical verification of eigencouplings via two-sided shooting methods.

Paper structure. The paper builds by first defining the spectral problem via the biconfluent Heun equation, then applying power-Liouville transformations to generate the orbit, and finally using advanced semiclassical methods (matched large-action analysis) to derive the precise quantization rules.

Abstract

A singular sextic oscillator in the finite-Hermite biconfluent-Heun hierarchy generates an exact four-member power-Liouville Hermite orbit. Power coordinate transformations with the Liouville amplitude map the hierarchy member $N=4$ to four half-line Schrödinger spectral charts while preserving the physical regular-recessive Wronskian zero set under energy-coupling reparametrization. Two charts are the previously studied $x^{2/3}$ and inverse-square-root conditionally integrable potentials; the orbit embeds this known power-dual pair together with the singular sextic and linear-plus-quadratic charts. On the selected physical slice, the degree-five accessory compatibility obstruction vanishes identically, while the transformed Frobenius index reproduces both published fractional-power centrifugal coefficients. The common endpoint condition yields an exact discrete sequence of quartic Sturmian eigencouplings for the sextic oscillator. Independent two-sided shooting confirms representative eigencouplings and node counts. A matched large-action analysis further derives the first matched-endpoint correction within the leading Langer action and improves the Maslov-shifted effective-level approximation. The construction thus connects exact Hermite solvability, energy quantization, coupling quantization, and power duality within a single spectral orbit.

Attainable Boundaries versus Operational Equivalence: Dual Benchmarks for the Hierarchical Assessment of Quantum Resources

Summary. This paper develops a novel quantum resource theory framework that moves beyond idealized free states by benchmarking quantum resources against the set of states actually preparable in current experiments ($E$). It introduces a dual-benchmark approach—one for immediate performance and one for intrinsic potential—to provide a more realistic measure of resource utility. This is vital for advancing quantum technologies by grounding theory in physical operational constraints.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a rigorous, operational framework for quantifying quantum resources that directly addresses the gap between theoretical ideals and experimental reality, which is crucial for designing practical quantum protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Conventional quantum resource theories benchmark utility against idealized free states, failing to account for the practical constraints of current experimental apparatus.

Main result. The authors propose a dual-benchmark framework that quantifies relative resource utility by comparing states against the set of practically accessible resources ($E$), demonstrating its utility for resource management.

Method. The framework establishes two benchmarks: a strategy-dependent measure for achievable performance and a strategy-independent lower bound for intrinsic potential, using $E$ as the reference set.

Model / system. The system involves general quantum states and resource quantification, extending resource theories to incorporate the physical limitations imposed by the preparable set $E$ of states.

Key observables. Relative Superiority ($ ext{RE}$), Distillable Entanglement ($D( ho)$), and the residual resource entropy ($E_{rde}^E( ho)$).

Important parameters / regimes. The reference set $E$ (the attainable boundary), and the asymptotic limits ($\epsilon o 0, n o \infty$) used in derivations.

Assumptions / limitations. The core assumption is that defining $E$ as the reference point resolves the challenge of strategy-dependent operational efficacy in QRTs.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the construction of two key reference sets: $E$ (currently accessible states) and $ ext{RFO}(E)$ (states reachable via free operations) and $ ext{Rmax}(E)$ (performance-equivalent states).

Paper structure. The paper systematically introduces the limitation of free-state benchmarks, proposes the framework using $E$, develops the dual-benchmark methodologies, and validates the approach using entanglement distillation as a concrete example.

Abstract

While conventional quantum resource theories quantify utility relative to an idealized set of free states, practical implementations are confined to a specific supply environment---a framework encompassing all states realizable or preparable via current experimental apparatus and design choices, not limited to free states. This work proposes a novel framework for evaluating the relative utility of arbitrary quantum states against the resource set made accessible by these current selections. We specifically focus our investigation on two canonical representations: the set of states reachable via free operations as the attainable boundary imposed by current experimental constraints, and the set defined through numerical optimization as the criterion for operational equivalence. Our framework establishes two core evaluation methodologies. First, we provide a strategy-dependent benchmark by assessing a state's performance relative to the reference set under its individually optimized strategy, thereby quantifying achievable performance. Second, we derive a computable lower bound on this superiority by considering optimal strategies for each state individually, enabling the quantification of strategy-independent relative performance independent of specific experimental setups. Beyond relative efficiency metrics, we demonstrate the practical utility of this approach for resource management scenarios, including the selective extraction of states advantageous for storage and distribution, and the concentration of residual resources. Using quantum entanglement as a paradigmatic example, we prove that distillable entanglement can be fully recovered via extraction and concentration when the reference set is restricted to pure states, thereby demonstrating the practical relevance of our framework.

Beyond QAOA: A Review of AI and Quantum Computing for Adaptive Combinatorial Optimization

Authors: Hoong Chuin LAU
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.11759v1

Summary. This review surveys the rapidly evolving field of using Artificial Intelligence to enhance quantum computation for solving hard combinatorial optimization problems. It categorizes current research into three paradigms and critically assesses the evidence, concluding that the primary challenge is managing the allocation of classical and quantum resources rather than just qubit count. The paper provides a structured framework for future research in the field.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a high-level, critical survey of the intersection between ML and quantum computation, which is crucial for understanding the practical roadmap for quantum algorithm development beyond simple circuit implementations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The review addresses the challenge of adaptive combinatorial optimization on near-term quantum processors, where resource constraints and complex decision-making limit current approaches.

Main result. Evidence is stronger for using AI to assist quantum optimization (AI-for-QO) than for using quantum computation to improve learned classical optimizers (Q-for-AI). Scaling is identified as a systems problem of resource allocation.

Method. The paper reviews the literature by organizing it around the specific decision being learned (e.g., parameter initialization, circuit design, penalty selection) rather than by application.

Model / system. The focus is on mapping combinatorial problems (like MaxCut or VRP) to QUBO/Ising models, which are then solved using parameterized quantum circuits (PQCs) like QAOA or VQE on NISQ hardware.

Key observables. Solution quality (optimality gap, approximation ratio), feasibility (feasible-sample rate), and resource costs (shot budget, quantum evaluations).

Important parameters / regimes. The M0-M5 evidence hierarchy is used to grade maturity, and key parameters include the QAOA depth ($p$) and the shot budget ($S$).

Assumptions / limitations. The review notes that experimental controls are thin, and many studies lack proper classical baselines or fully isolate the quantum contribution.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the increasing scope of learned decisions, progressing from fixed quantum algorithms to adaptive solver policies across heterogeneous resources.

Paper structure. The review systematically examines three paradigms (AI-for-QO, Q-for-AI, Co-Optimization) and structures the literature review by the decision being learned, culminating in a detailed evidence matrix (Table 7).

Abstract

Near-term quantum approaches to combinatorial optimization are limited by qubit counts, circuit fidelity, sampling cost, and the difficulty of encoding constraints, while machine learning is increasingly used to configure and control quantum optimization workflows. We call such workflows adaptive: decisions conventionally fixed in advance, from formulation and penalties to shot budgets, backends, and whether to invoke a quantum processor at all, are made by learned policies that respond to the instance, the progress of the solve, or the hardware. This review examines three paradigms, AI for quantum optimization, quantum for AI-driven optimization, and AI-quantum co-optimization, and organizes the literature by the decision being learned rather than by application. A structured review of 119 papers, 67 coded in detail, shows that the evidence is considerably stronger for AI-assisted quantum optimization than for the reverse direction: learning already reduces quantum evaluations, improves initialization, supports decomposition and penalty control, and mitigates noise, whereas evidence that quantum computation improves learned optimizers remains largely confined to small-scale simulation. Experimental controls are thin: 25 of 57 studies include no classical baseline, the quantum contribution is fully isolated in 10 of 24 studies where an ablation applies, and the median experiment uses 17 qubits. We introduce an M0-M5 evidence hierarchy, from simulation to matched-resource practical advantage, and find no broadly convincing result at the highest level. We argue that scaling is increasingly a systems problem: the question is not only whether a problem fits on a quantum processor, but how classical and quantum resources should be allocated across the optimization process. The review is aimed at researchers in quantum computing, machine learning, and operations research.

Summary. This work introduces a novel, scalable cryptographic protocol to assure the security of communication paths in large QKD networks. It allows a provider to prove that a valid, policy-compliant route exists without revealing the network's sensitive internal topology. By certifying nodes and edges independently, the method ensures that proof complexity scales only with the path length, not the entire network size.

Why it may be interesting. While highly cryptographic, the focus on quantum networks and secure communication protocols places it directly within the domain of quantum information security, which is a key application area for quantum physics.

Main problem. The paper addresses the need for a scalable, zero-knowledge mechanism to prove the existence of policy-compliant paths in large-scale Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) networks without revealing the network's internal topology.

Main result. They propose a topology-certification mechanism that certifies nodes and edges independently using multi-message signatures, achieving proof sizes and computational costs independent of the overall network size.

Method. The approach uses advanced zero-knowledge proof techniques, combining multi-message signatures and NIZK proofs to prove path existence while hiding the specific route and topology details.

Model / system. The context is large-scale QKD networks, which are modeled as directed graphs where security assurance depends on the entire communication path.

Abstract

Large-scale Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) networks rely on trusted repeaters, making the security properties of the selected communication path an essential part of end-to-end assurance. At the same time, network operators may be unwilling to disclose their internal topology. We present a topology-certification mechanism that lets a provider prove in zero knowledge that policy-compliant routes between two endpoints exist, without disclosing the routes in an individual presentation. Our main idea is to certify nodes and edges independently using multi-message signatures, rather than signing the complete graph as one object. For a route chosen in advance, proof size and cryptographic proving and verification work depend on its positions and attributes, independently of the overall network size, whereas route discovery and certification have separate graph-dependent costs. The construction hides the actual route length up to a public bound and sketches extensions to node-disjoint routes and monotonic additions within a graph epoch. We give a formal security model and conditional proofs of unforgeability and graph hiding for a generic construction. For a BBS-based construction, presentation-size estimates remain below $300$\,KiB at $\ell=m=n=50$, and measured generation and verification times remain below $400$\,ms at $\ell=64$ and $m=n=8$.

Summary. This paper introduces scalable methods to quantify entanglement entropy and 'magic' (non-stabilizerness) directly from ZX-diagrams, bypassing costly full state contractions. By decomposing the circuit into a graph-state backbone and non-Clifford gadgets, the authors derive additive bounds for these key quantum resources. This establishes a crucial link between the graphical ZX-calculus and the characterization of quantum many-body resources.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a powerful, diagrammatic tool to characterize complex quantum states arising from quantum computation models, offering insights into resource quantification that is crucial for understanding computational complexity.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of efficiently estimating quantum resources, specifically entanglement entropy and 'magic' (non-stabilizerness), directly from the structure of ZX-diagrams without resorting to computationally expensive full tensor contractions.

Main result. The authors derive scalable, additive upper and lower bounds for entanglement entropy and magic based on decomposing the ZX-diagram into a graph-state backbone and non-Clifford Pauli gadgets.

Method. They utilize a 'Flow' criterion to transform the ZX-diagram into a normal form, allowing the separation of the graph-state part from the non-Clifford contributions, which then yields the resource bounds.

Model / system. The model is quantum states and dynamics represented by ZX-diagrams, which are used to describe quantum circuits, including those involving random unitaries, Trotterized Hamiltonian evolution, and Clifford layers.

Key observables. Entanglement Entropy ($S$, including Rényi variants $S_\alpha$), Magic (logarithmic stabilizer extent $M$), and the entanglement entropy of the graph-state backbone ($ ext{rank}{\mathbb{F}_2}\Gamma$).

Important parameters / regimes. Circuit depth, number of qubits, and the phases ($\varphi_i$) associated with the non-Clifford Pauli gadgets.

Assumptions / limitations. The derived strict bounds assume that there is no coherent interference between the Pauli gadgets, although the method remains informative even when such interference occurs.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the decomposition of the circuit into a normal form (Fig. 1), and compare the derived bounds for entanglement entropy and magic against exact calculations for random and Trotterized circuits (Figs. 2 and 3).

Paper structure. Abstract

Entanglement and non-stabilizerness are complementary resources underlying the complexity of quantum states, yet extracting either quantity from large quantum circuits generally requires exponentially scaling resources. In diagrammatic approaches to quantum computation, such as the ZX-calculus, both quantities likewise typically require expensive tensor contractions that are not native to graphical rewriting. Here we show that both entanglement and magic can be efficiently estimated directly from ZX-diagrams. Flow enables efficient extraction of a normal form separating a potentially extensively entangled graph-state backbone from non-Clifford Pauli gadgets. This structure yields additive upper and lower bounds on bipartite entanglement entropy, which we further tighten through a preprocessing procedure that removes or merges redundant non-Clifford contributions. The same normal form gives an upper bound on logarithmic stabilizer extent. We benchmark these methods in random unitary and monitored circuits, and a circuit combining Trotterized Hamiltonian evolution with Clifford layers, finding that the resulting bounds remain informative even at large qubit numbers, circuit depths, and internal spider counts. Our results provide scalable probes of entanglement and non-stabilizerness of ZX-diagrams and establish a bridge between ZX-calculus and quantum many-body resource characterization, with applications across quantum computing, quantum information, and condensed-matter physics.

Summary. This paper calculates the exact quantum value for the $2 imes3$ magic rectangle, a nonlocal game, finding it to be $(1+\sqrt{2/3})/2$. The work provides a deep classification of all optimal quantum strategies, linking the game to quantum random access codes.

Main result. The quantum value of the game is proven to be $ rac{1 + \sqrt{2/3}}{2}$, and the optimal strategies are shown to be highly rigid, involving maximally entangled pairs of four-dimensional systems.

Method. The analysis uses techniques from nonlocal game theory, establishing upper bounds via exact sum-of-squares identities and classifying optimal structures by analyzing the equality conditions of these bounds.

Abstract

The $2\times3$ magic rectangle is a nonlocal game in which two players match entries subject to incompatible parity constraints. We prove that its quantum value is $(1+\sqrt{2/3})/2$ and classify all optimal strategies in arbitrary finite local dimensions, allowing general measurements. Every optimal strategy contains a maximally entangled pair of four-dimensional systems, with fixed measurements on the local state supports up to local isometries and ancillary systems. The attaining construction was previously known from quantum random access codes. An exact sum-of-squares identity gives the upper bound, and its equality relations determine the measurement algebra needed for the classification. The rectangle determines the largest magic-square score compatible with perfect prediction of Alice's first row by an adversary with quantum side information. We classify all finite-dimensional strategies attaining this score with perfect prediction and show that one minus the best compatible guessing probability has linear order in the score excess above it. The same rigidity theorem classifies all optimal three-bit random access codes that hide the full input parity.

Abstract

These results reveal a field-resolved hierarchy of superconducting energy scales in PdTe and indicate superconducting responses with distinct magnetic-field robustness.

Abstract

The entanglement structure of a bipartite pure state is characterized by its entanglement spectrum, or equivalently, by the family of Rényi-$k$ entanglement entropies with integer $k$. Taking the difference of two such entropies defines a quantity known as an entanglement spread, that is monotonous under local operations, and that vanishes if and only if the entanglement spectrum of the state is flat. We introduce a multipartite generalization of this notion which, for a fixed number $D\geq 3$ of parties, results in a collection of maps indexed by pairs of $D$-edge-colored graphs (obeying some condition). Each member of this collection takes the form of a difference of two multipartite Rényi entanglement entropies, each associated to a local unitary polynomial invariant (known in this context as a trace-invariant), and is shown to be monotonous under local operations. We then argue that a previously introduced family of so-called hypergraph-tensor (HT) states can be understood as a multipartite counterpart to the family of flat bipartite states. Indeed, we first prove that, when $D=3$, a tripartite pure state is HT if and only if it minimizes a particular (infinite) family of entanglement spreads. Second, we embed the set of HT states into the much larger family of spectral hypergraph-tensor (SHT) states, defined by a new Ansatz we introduce. We then prove that HT states can be uniquely characterized as the minimizers of some fixed entanglement spread among SHT states, for arbitrary $D\geq 3$. We also investigate SHT states in their own right: in particular, we characterize their orbits under local unitary transformations. Finally, along the way, the asymptotic large-$N$ expectation values of a number of entanglement spreads are computed in the Haar-random state of local dimension $N$.

Abstract

We study entanglement distillation under operations that remain completely positive under partial-transpose conjugation, a.k.a. PPT channels, in the standard quantum Shannon theory regime of asymptotically vanishing but non-zero error. Our main results are: (a) a regularised formula for the PPT distillable entanglement in terms of a measured-relative-entropy-like quantity; and (b) two different single-letter converses, one based on operator quadratic forms and the other on Hirschman's strengthening of the Hadamard three-line theorem. As an immediate application of (b), we show that the PPT distillable entanglement can be strictly smaller than the regularised Rains bound, thereby resolving an open problem in the theory of entanglement manipulation [Regula et al., NJP 21:103017, 2019]. A gap appears already for $3\times 3$ Werner states: at antisymmetric weight $25/26$, a certified upper bound of $0.62107$ ebits lies below the (regularised) Rains bound, which equals $\frac{25}{26} \log_2 5 - \log_2 3 \approx 0.64766$ ebits. On a different note, (a) implies a faithful lower bound on the PPT distillable entanglement in terms of the entanglement negativity, which provides a quantitative counterpart to the qualitatively known fact that any NPT state is PPT distillable [Eggeling et al., PRL 87:257902, 2001].
