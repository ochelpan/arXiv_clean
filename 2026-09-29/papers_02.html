Emergent frustrated magnetism in strain-patterned graphene

Summary. This paper reports the realization of emergent frustrated magnetism in non-magnetic graphene by applying lithographically controlled strain patterns. The strain creates a pseudo-magnetic field that induces strongly correlated electronic states, leading to magnetic behavior analogous to spin ice. This establishes strain engineering as a versatile, scalable method for creating programmable quantum matter.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a concrete, tunable solid-state platform (graphene) to engineer complex quantum magnetic interactions, which is highly relevant for simulating exotic quantum phases without relying on rare magnetic elements.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The research addresses the fundamental challenge of realizing and studying emergent frustrated magnetism within an intrinsically non-magnetic material.

Main result. The authors demonstrate that lithographically patterned strain in graphene can induce correlated electronic states exhibiting robust, emergent frustrated magnetism, establishing a scalable platform for programmable quantum matter.

Method. The study combines advanced experimental techniques, including transport measurements and cryogenic magnetic force microscopy, with theoretical modeling using spin-ice-like effective Hamiltonians and Monte Carlo simulations.

Model / system. The physical system is graphene patterned into a superlattice via strain engineering, which generates a pseudo-magnetic field (PMF). This PMF induces flat electronic bands and strong electron correlations, leading to magnetic frustration analogous to spin ice.

Key observables. Anisotropic magnetic hysteresis, slow relaxation dynamics, insulating behavior characterized by Efros–Shklovskii variable-range hopping (ES-VRH), and spatially complex magnetic textures observed via MFM.

Important parameters / regimes. The effective magnetic moment ($\mu_{eff} \approx 9.0 \mu_B$), the critical magnetic fields ($H_{c,1}, H_{c,2}$) governing charge ordering phases, and the bandwidth of the induced flat bands ($< 6$ meV).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that the strain patterning is perfectly programmable via lithography, and the magnetic response can be effectively modeled using spin-ice-like constraints derived from the PMF landscape.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the device structure and PMF profiles, temperature-dependent resistivity showing insulating behavior, magnetotransport hysteresis loops, and MFM images confirming field-dependent magnetic textures.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the physical platform (strain-patterned graphene) to characterizing the electronic state (flat bands, correlated regime) via transport measurements, and finally modeling the emergent magnetic behavior using spin-ice analogies and simulating field-dependent charge ordering.

Abstract

Geometrically frustrated magnetism conventionally arises from pre-existing magnetic moments on lattices whose geometry prevents their interactions from being simultaneously satisfied, giving rise to highly degenerate states and rich collective behavior. Creating such frustration in an intrinsically non-magnetic material presents a fundamentally different challenge, requiring both the magnetism and the competing interactions to emerge from correlated electrons. Here we show that this can be realized in graphene through lithographically programmable strain engineering. Patterning strain and the associated pseudo-magnetic field (PMF) into superlattices creates a correlated electronic system with flat bands and strong interactions. Transport measurements reveal interaction-driven insulating behavior, anisotropic magnetic hysteresis, and slow relaxation dynamics reminiscent of spin freezing, qualitatively captured by Monte Carlo simulations of competing magnetic moments on the PMF-defined ruby superlattice. Cryogenic magnetic force microscopy further reveals magnetic textures associated with the PMF landscape. These observations demonstrate frustrated magnetism emerging from correlated electrons in otherwise non-magnetic graphene. Our results establish lithographic strain engineering as a general and scalable route to flat bands and correlated states in van der Waals materials, providing a versatile platform for programmable quantum matter.

Summary. This theoretical paper establishes a general mechanism for the Rényi hierarchy of Decoherence-Induced Phase Transitions (DIPTs) by analyzing correlation inequalities in replicated statistical models. Using models like topological codes and rotor systems, the authors prove that the critical decoherence strength increases monotonically with the Rényi index $R$ for $R \ge 2$. This provides a deep mathematical understanding of how entanglement diagnostics reveal the structure of quantum phase boundaries under noise.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, general mathematical tool—the correlation inequality approach—to predict and understand the structure of quantum phase transitions driven by environmental coupling, which is crucial for understanding quantum error correction and open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to identify a general, underlying mechanism responsible for the Rényi hierarchy observed in Decoherence-Induced Phase Transitions (DIPTs). This hierarchy dictates that the critical decoherence strength increases monotonically with the Rényi index $R$ for $R \ge 2$.

Main result. A general mechanism is established using correlation inequalities within replicated statistical models, proving that the critical decoherence strength $p_c(R)$ is non-decreasing with $R$ for $R \ge 2$ in various models.

Method. The analysis involves mapping the decohered quantum system to a replicated statistical model (e.g., Ising-type model) and proving the ordering of expectation values of spin products, $\langle \sigma(A)_V angle_R \geq \langle \sigma(A)_V angle_{R'}$, which serves as the core diagnostic.

Model / system. The study applies to $Z_2$ topological stabilizer codes subjected to phase-flip decoherence, and a decohered rotor model exhibiting strong U(1) symmetry. The physics is analyzed via replicated partition functions $Z_R$.

Key observables. Rényi entropy $S_R$, expectation values of Ising spin products $\langle \sigma(A)_V angle_R$, and the critical decoherence strength $p_c(R)$ or $\alpha_c(R)$.

Important parameters / regimes. Rényi index $R$ (integer, $R \ge 2$), decoherence strength $p$ or $\alpha$, and coupling constants $J$ or $J_0, J_1$.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary mathematical tool relies on establishing correlation inequalities analogous to GKS inequalities, which are proven for integer $R \ge 2$. The extension to the $R o 1$ (von Neumann) limit remains an open question.

Figures summary. The notes do not provide a summary of figures, but the analysis focuses on deriving correlation inequalities (Eq. (8) and Eq. (18)) that establish the required ordering.

Paper structure. The paper systematically develops the mechanism by first analyzing $Z_2$ codes using Ising spin products, then extending the proof to correlated decoherence, and finally applying the framework to the U(1)-symmetric decohered rotor model.

Abstract

Decoherence-induced phase transitions (DIPTs) are associated with singular changes in a quantum system's entanglement structure as the decoherence strength varies. In many cases, their critical strengths depend monotonically on the Rényi index of the entanglement diagnostic, forming a Rényi hierarchy. We identify a general mechanism for this hierarchy based on correlation inequalities in replicated statistical models that describe these DIPTs. We demonstrate it in $\mathbb{Z}_2$ topological stabilizer codes subject to independent phase-flip decoherence on each qubit and in a decohered rotor model with strong $\text{U}(1)$ symmetry. When the relevant correlations diagnose the DIPTs, these inequalities imply that the critical decoherence strength is nondecreasing with integer Rényi index $R\geq2$. We show that this mechanism also applies to a class of models with correlated decoherence.

Crucially, the same engineered process simultaneously generates strong even-parity entanglement, unifying two key quantum control mechanisms.

Main result. The authors demonstrate that adiabatically encircling a second-order exceptional point induces chiral transfer between $|\Phi^+ angle$ and $|\Phi^- angle$, and that the same loop can deterministically generate strong even-parity entanglement from a separable state.

Method. The dynamics are analyzed using a hybrid Liouvillian description, which interpolates between conditional no-jump dynamics and standard Lindblad evolution, coupled with the theoretical framework of non-Hermitian physics.

Model / system. The system is a two-qubit platform driven by coherent two-photon processes and engineered two-photon loss. The dynamics are confined to the even-parity subspace, which is structurally protected by parity symmetry.

Abstract

We investigate chiral transfer and dissipative generation of even-parity Bell states in a two-qubit system with coherent two-photon driving and engineered two-photon loss. We find that adiabatic encirclement of a second-order exceptional point induces direction-dependent transfer between the Bell states $|Φ^+\rangle$ and $|Φ^-\rangle$, governed by a time-integrated low-loss branch-selection mechanism. We develop a hybrid-Liouvillian description with a control parameter $q$ that interpolates between conditional no-jump dynamics and unconditional Lindblad evolution, and use it to assess how exceptional-point-induced chirality survives in the presence of quantum jumps. When the same parameter loop is initialized in the separable state $|00\rangle$, it directly generates strong even-parity entanglement. Together, these results extend dissipative Bell-state control beyond the single-excitation manifold. They further demonstrate that exceptional-point-based protocols can unify chiral state transfer and entanglement generation within a single engineered two-photon platform.

Summary. This work demonstrates high-gain vortex light transfer in molecular magnets by exploiting symmetry-breaking mechanisms to activate forbidden optical transitions. The theoretical analysis shows that the Autler-Townes splitting effect surpasses EIT in enhancing the signal gain, challenging the universal dominance of EIT in nonlinear optics. The results suggest potential applications for quantum information processing in solid-state quantum systems.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a concrete, solid-state platform (molecular magnets) to study fundamental quantum optics phenomena like forbidden transition enhancement, offering a novel comparison between EIT and ATS in a highly controllable solid-state environment.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to achieve high-gain vortex light transfer in molecular magnets by enabling optical transitions that are otherwise forbidden by selection rules.

Main result. The Autler-Townes splitting (ATS) effect was shown to yield a significantly larger signal field gain than Electromagnetically Induced Transparency (EIT) in this specific nonlinear three-wave mixing process.

Method. The study employs a theoretical analysis using a three-level ladder-type configuration and solving the Maxwell-Bloch equations under perturbative approximations.

Model / system. The physical system is the molecular magnet $[ ext{Cr}( ext{C}_3 ext{S}_5)_3]^{3-}$, modeled by a spin Hamiltonian. The dynamics are analyzed using a three-level ladder configuration, where symmetry breaking allows nominally forbidden transitions.

Key observables. Intensity and phase of the generated vortex signal field; the topological charges of the incident and generated fields.

Important parameters / regimes. Detuning of the probe field, strength of the control field, and the ratio of signal gain between ATS and EIT.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the absence of nuclear spins to suppress decoherence and relies on the perturbative approximation for weak probe and signal fields.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the three-level ladder configuration with broken symmetry, showing the nonlinear three-wave mixing process generating a signal field.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the physical model using a three-level ladder system, derives the coupled equations for field propagation, calculates the field amplitudes using steady-state solutions, and compares the gain mechanisms (ATS vs. EIT).

Abstract

Vortex light has garnered considerable interest in recent years owing to its distinctive properties and broad application potential. In this paper, we employ a conventional three-level ladder-type configuration in molecular magnets to realize high-gain vortex light transfer by enabling otherwise forbidden transitions. We demonstrate that the intensity and phase of the generated vortex signal field are governed by the detuning of the probe field and the strength of the control field, and that the topological charges of the incident and generated fields obey a well-defined algebraic relation during the vortex transfer process. Furthermore, we show that, in molecular magnets, the Autler-Townes splitting (ATS) effect yields a larger vortex signal field gain than electromagnetically induced transparency (EIT) over a broad parameter range in nonlinear three-wave mixing. This result suggests that the widely accepted view of EIT as the dominant enhancer of nonlinear optical effects may not hold universally. In addition, we revisit previous studies and re-examine the characterization of vortex beam transfer efficiency, emphasizing the need for a more careful interpretation in multi-beam interaction systems. Leveraging the long spin coherence and microwave transitions of molecular magnets, our results may enable quantum information transfer, storage, computing, and radar imaging in solid-state platforms.

Abstract

We show experimentally obtained frequency- and power-dependent optical response of a room-temperature cesium vapor cell used as a Rydberg microwave receiver based on electromagnetically induced transparency. An applied microwave field couples the ${70S_{1/2}}$ Rydberg state to the nearby ${70P_{1/2}}$, ${70P_{3/2}}$, ${69P_{1/2}}$, and ${69P_{3/2}}$ states, modifying the probe-transmission spectrum. Probe transmission is recorded over a 800 MHz range of coupling laser detunings. These EIT spectra are measured for RF frequencies from 9 to 13.5 GHz and over a 40 dB range of RF power. We analyze the RF induced splittings in the recorded EIT spectra. We observe both limiting cases, where the EIT peak separation is either dominated by the RF detuning or by the RF power. At higher powers we are also able to observe complex features due to coupling of multiple nearby Rydberg states. We further analyze the probe-transmission signal at zero coupling-laser detuning. Sigmoidal fits to the power-response curves are used to determine the midpoint power and the 10-90% transition interval as functions of RF frequency.

Abstract

We propose a new $q$-derivative operator built directly from Jackson's $q$-number formulation, designed to preserve the structural properties of standard differential calculus while incorporating deformation effects. By analyzing $q$-deformed Heisenberg algebras, we demonstrate that this formulation maintains the consistency of thermodynamic quantities-such as internal energy, particle number, and specific heat-in dilute gas limits without requiring ad-hoc chain-rule modifications. Furthermore, we explore the physical implications of algebraic deformation using the Biedenharn-Macfarlane realization, showing how $q$-deformation induces intrinsic anharmonicity in quantum oscillator spectra and affects multi-level quantum systems ($d \ge 3$). Applying this algebraic scheme to superconducting transmon qubits, we derive analytical pulse-shaping corrections that generalize the Derivative Removal by Adiabatic Gate (DRAG) technique, offering a robust method to suppress computational leakage in ultra-fast quantum logic operations.

Abstract

Dissipative algorithms approach the problem of ground state preparation by mimicking the natural thermalization of a quantum system in contact with a large, low-temperature thermal environment. Abstract

Motile active matter systems, from animal groups to synthetic particles, exhibit spontaneous self-organized transitions between disordered and ordered collective states. These transitions are driven by continuous energy injection, yet how energy consumption and dissipation change during these transitions remains poorly understood due to the challenge of measuring individual energetic fluxes. Leveraging this energetic reorganization, we construct an active engine powered by collective motion, capable of performing mechanical work against an external load. These findings establish energetic fluxes as quantitative observables for understanding collective state transitions and demonstrate how collective order can be harnessed for work extraction in active matter.

Abstract

In this work, we define $(3+1)$-dimensional topological quantum field theories (TQFTs) and their lattice realizations with input data given by a pivotal fusion 2-category; more precisely, our framework applies in wider generality to presemisimple locally fusion pivotal tensor $2$-categories, which are equipped with a so-called \textit{shadow trace}. We introduce a decoration of triangulated manifolds by the input data, and develop a $20j$-symbol formalism for computing 4-simplex scattering amplitudes. We verify the topological invariance of 20$j$-symbol summations under the 4-dimensional Pachner moves. Furthermore, we also construct explicitly a 3-dimensional Levin-Wen-type lattice Hamiltonian model realizing the state-sum invariant. Our construction can be understood as a higher-dimensional analogue of the spin-networks construction for the Turaev-Viro invariant. Examples arising from $2$-groups are discussed from the perspective of higher gauge theory.
