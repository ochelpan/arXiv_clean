Quantum interaction can superactivate cheating under parallel repetition

Authors: Archishna Bhattacharyya, Laura Mančinska, Yuming Zhao
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.31223v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper explores the counterintuitive phenomenon of 'superactivation' in quantum interactive proof systems, showing that quantum communication can cause the cheating probability to increase dramatically upon parallel repetition. The authors prove that while the single-shot cheating value can be low, repeating the game multiple times forces the cheating probability to one. This work has significant implications for quantum complexity theory and the security analysis of quantum protocols.

Why it may be interesting. The finding that quantum resources can be 'superactivated' by repetition challenges standard assumptions about error amplification in quantum protocols, which is a core topic in quantum information theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how quantum communication affects the soundness error of interactive multiprover games (QMIP) under parallel repetition, contrasting this with classical results.

Main result. The authors demonstrate that quantum communication can cause 'superactivation' of cheating, where the cheating probability increases from a value less than one for a single instance to exactly one under parallel repetition.

Method. The work establishes a correspondence between quantum games and quantum channels, using techniques like teleportation-based reductions and analyzing the behavior of zero-error capacities.

Model / system. The model involves quantum interactive proof systems (QMIP) and quantum channels, analyzing the success probability of cheating provers (the 'value' of the game).

Key observables. The local value $\omega(G)$ versus the parallel repetition value $\omega(G^n)$, and the zero-error classical/quantum capacities ($C_0, Q_0$).

Important parameters / regimes. The number of parallel repetitions ($n$), and the number of messages exchanged (limited to 3 for certain impossibility results).

Assumptions / limitations. The impossibility results for entangled values are shown only for a limited number of messages (at most 3), and the analysis relies on establishing specific orthogonality relations.

Paper structure. The paper builds by first contrasting classical and quantum parallel repetition effects, then introducing the quantum game-channel correspondence, proving the superactivation phenomenon for the local value, and finally proving the impossibility of superactivation for the entangled value under certain constraints.

Abstract

We study interactive multiprover games and many-round protocols in which the communication between the verifier and the provers is quantum. Parallel repetition is known to suppress soundness error of classical two-prover games arbitrarily close to zero, as shown by Raz (STOC '95), even with quantum strategies as shown by Yuen (ICALP '16), and Bavarian, Vidick and Yuen (STOC '17). Yet, we show that quantum communication can have the opposite, unexpected effect. Specifically, we exhibit a one-round quantum game and two-round $\mathsf{QMIP}$ protocols whose local (unentangled) value is strictly less than one for a single instance, yet equals one under $n$-fold parallel repetition for every $n \geq 2$. The key is that parallel repetition not only imposes additional winning conditions but also supplies additional quantum resources as entanglement in the exchanged states can be exploited jointly across copies. Our multiround examples build on superactivation of zero-error capacities of quantum channels. To establish this connection, we introduce quantum games and interactive protocols which capture the one-shot zero-error classical and quantum capacities, both with and without entanglement assistance. Our constructions use quantum-state verification and a teleportation-based reduction from two rounds to one. In contrast, when shared entanglement is allowed, we show that parallel repetition cannot increase the entangled value when at most $3$ messages are exchanged, consistent with classical results of Bellare, Impagliazzo and Naor (FOCS '97), and that of single-prover systems by Kitaev and Watrous (STOC '00). Combining this monotonicity with our quantum game-channel correspondence, we show that entanglement-assisted zero-error classical and quantum capacities cannot be superactivated.

Representation-Aware Transport-Information Measure for Non-inclusive Discrete Supports

Authors: Koretaka Yuge
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.30944v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work develops a sophisticated information-theoretic measure to compare discrete probability distributions whose supports may not overlap. It achieves this by moving beyond comparing only the final discrete states, instead incorporating information about the continuous distributions and the specific discretization processes used to generate them. This provides a powerful tool for analyzing physical systems where the underlying continuous physics is more informative than the resulting coarse-grained data.

Why it may be interesting. While the core topic is information geometry, the rigorous mathematical framework connecting extrinsic geometric operations (discretization) to intrinsic information metrics is highly relevant for developing robust comparison tools in complex many-body systems where approximations or coarse-graining are inevitable.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of comparing discrete probability distributions, particularly when their supports are non-inclusive, while retaining crucial information about the underlying continuous distributions and the discretization schemes used to generate them.

Main result. A novel representation-aware transport-information measure ($D_{RA}$) is introduced that successfully quantifies the difference between two discrete distributions by combining the divergence between their continuous references with a term quantifying the information deformation caused by their respective discretization processes.

Method. The measure is formulated using the framework of unavoidable canonical nonlinearity (UCN), linking the 2-Wasserstein transport cost of discretization to the information-geometric indistinguishability of nearby continuous distributions.

Model / system. The theory is applied to general comparison of probability distributions, finding relevance in physical systems like configurational degrees of freedom (e.g., crystal structures) where the resulting discrete supports may be non-inclusive.

Key observables. The representation-aware transport-information measure ($D_{RA}$), the 2-Wasserstein-based transport cost ($ ext{eW}^2/2$), and the local information deformation terms ($ ext{Tr}(M_1A) + ext{Tr}(M_2C)$).

Important parameters / regimes. The continuous reference distributions ($P_{c1}, P_{c2}$), the discretization schemes ($D_1, D_2$), and the vanishing discretization scale limit ($ ext{V}_k ightarrow 0$).

Assumptions / limitations. The derivation assumes the local-map (LM) scheme for discretization and relies on the local UCN correspondence, which equates the discretization cost to the information-geometric indistinguishability at the leading order.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the limitations of standard information measures for non-inclusive supports. It then introduces the UCN framework to relate discretization geometry to information geometry. Finally, it derives and presents the concrete, second-order form of the representation-aware measure, separating the continuous difference from the discretization-induced deformation.

Abstract

Information-theoretic measures for comparing probability distributions are widely used across physics and other fields. When two discrete distributions have non-inclusive supports, however, the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence is in general not directly applicable, and various alternative divergences and distances have been introduced. These measures compare the resulting distributions themselves, but do not generally retain information about the representation transformations by which the discrete distributions are generated from underlying continuous ones. Here we introduce a representation-aware transport-information measure for discrete distributions with non-inclusive supports, formulated based on the standard KL divergence. We consider two continuous reference distributions, each transformed into a discrete representation through its own discretization scheme. Rather than comparing only the resulting discrete distributions or their continuous references, we additionally retain local information associated with the representation-change schemes. The resulting measure can therefore distinguish discrete representations that may have identical discrete probability landscapes but originate from different continuous references or discretization schemes. The construction is based on the transport-information cost of continuous-to-discrete representation in the framework of unavoidable canonical nonlinearity (UCN). UCN provides a non-arbitrary correspondence between the transport cost of discretization as an extrinsic geometric operation and the information-theoretic indistinguishability of nearby continuous distributions, thereby allowing a discrete representation to be associated with a local family of underlying continuous distributions on the statistical manifold.

Sharp Data-Processing Region for Quantum Conditional Rényi Entropies

Authors: Milad M. Goodarzi, Yucheng Bao, Marco Tomamichel
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.31350v1

Summary. This paper provides a comprehensive mathematical characterization of the data-processing properties for a generalized family of quantum conditional Rényi entropies. By proving the sharpness of the data-processing region and deriving necessary conditions from universal monotonicity and entropic duality, the authors uniquely determine the structural constraints on these entropy measures. This work solidifies the mathematical understanding of information flow in quantum systems.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous characterization of fundamental information-theoretic quantities like conditional entropy and their associated inequalities provides deep structural insights into quantum correlations, which underpins quantum computation theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to characterize the data-processing region, the club-sandwiched subfamily, and the entropic duality for a three-parameter family of quantum conditional Rényi entropies.

Main result. It proves that the data-processing region is sharp, that universal monotonicity under coarse-graining forces the parameter $z$ to equal $\alpha$, and that the entropic duality relations are rigid.

Method. The analysis uses rigorous mathematical proofs involving the application of specific test states (e.g., pure states, quantum-classical marginals) to derive necessary and sufficient conditions for various inequalities to hold.

Model / system. The study focuses on the generalized quantum conditional Rényi entropy, $H_{\lambda}^{\alpha, z}(A|B) ho$, defined for bipartite quantum states $ ho_{AB}$ in finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces.

Key observables. Quantum conditional Rényi entropies ($H_{\lambda}^{\alpha, z}$), the data-processing inequality (DPI), and the entropic duality relation.

Important parameters / regimes. The entropy depends on three parameters: $\alpha > 0, \alpha eq 1$, $z > 0$, and $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces and relies on testing the functional against specific classes of pure and mixed states to constrain the parameters.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by first establishing the sharpness of the DPI region using various test states, then uses the condition of universal coarse-graining monotonicity to constrain the parameters, and finally uses the duality relation to uniquely characterize the entire structure.

Abstract

We study the three-parameter family of quantum conditional Rényi entropies $H_{α,z}^λ$ introduced in \textit{Commun. Math. Phys.} 407, 180 (2026). We prove that the previously established data-processing region is sharp. We then characterize the club-sandwiched family through a classical coarse-graining principle: within the full data-processing region, universal monotonicity under deterministic coarse-graining of the classical conditioned register holds if and only if $z = α$. Finally, we establish rigidity of entropic duality by showing that any universal duality relation for finite-dimensional tripartite pure states must satisfy the known parameter relations. Thus, the data-processing region, the club-sandwiched subfamily, and the duality transformation are each uniquely characterized by their respective universal properties.

Supporting functionals and singular boundary geometry of two-qubit entanglement of formation

Authors: Wei Song, Xiao-Lan Zong
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.31097v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical paper provides a precise mathematical criterion for determining if the entanglement of formation ($ ext{EF}$) for a two-qubit state possesses a global supporting affine functional. The existence hinges on the nondegeneracy of the state's support with respect to the spin-flip bilinear form. Failure of this condition leads to a characteristic square-root decay of $ ext{EF}$ under perturbation, demonstrating a fundamental limitation in the measure's global differentiability.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical characterization of when entanglement measures are well-behaved under small perturbations is crucial for developing robust quantum information protocols and understanding the boundaries of quantum correlations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to establish necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of a global supporting affine functional for the entanglement of formation ($ ext{EF}$) for an entangled two-qubit state.

Main result. A global supporting affine functional exists if and only if the spin-flip bilinear form restricted to the state's support is nondegenerate. In the degenerate case, $ ext{EF}$ decreases as the square root of the mixing parameter, violating the required bound.

Method. The analysis combines mathematical criteria involving the nondegeneracy of the spin-flip bilinear form with perturbation theory applied to the $ ext{EF}$ functional along specific paths.

Model / system. The study focuses on two-qubit quantum systems, characterized by density matrices $ ho$. The mathematical tools involve the concurrence, the spin-flip bilinear form ($eta$), and the state's support ($S$).

Key observables. Entanglement of Formation ($ ext{EF}$), Concurrence ($C$), Spin-flip bilinear form ($eta$), and Concurrence roots ($\lambda_i$).

Important parameters / regimes. The degeneracy of $eta|_S$ (nondegenerate vs. rank two/three), and the small perturbation parameter $t$ or $\epsilon$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the state $ ho$ is entangled and relies on local Lipschitz continuity established for nondegenerate supports.

Figures summary. Not specified in the provided notes.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the mathematical criterion for the supporting functional's existence, analyzes the nondegenerate case where local Lipschitz continuity holds, and then investigates the degenerate case using perturbation analysis to show the failure of the global bound.

Abstract

We give a criterion for the existence of a global supporting affine functional for the entanglement of formation at an entangled two-qubit state. It exists if and only if the spin-flip bilinear form is nondegenerate on the support of the state, or equivalently, if the last concurrence root associated with the support is nonzero. In this case the entanglement of formation is locally Lipschitz continuous. In the degenerate case the state has rank two or three, and its projective support is tangent to the product-state quadric. For suitable perturbations outside the support, the entanglement of formation decreases as the square root of the mixing parameter. The leading term is obtained explicitly. This square-root decrease violates the one-sided Lipschitz bound required for a global supporting affine functional.

Summary. This work experimentally investigates how the magnetic anisotropy and Crystal-Electric-Field (CEF) ground states evolve across the $ ext{SmTr}_2 ext{Ge}_2$ series by varying the transition metal $ ext{Tr}$. By measuring magnetization, specific heat, and susceptibility, the authors demonstrate a clear transition in magnetic behavior linked to the underlying CEF state and the local structural environment around the $ ext{Sm}$ ion.

Why it may be interesting. The systematic tuning of magnetic anisotropy via ligand environment (changing $ ext{Tr}$) provides a clear experimental platform to study how crystal fields dictate the low-energy magnetic excitations in $4f$ electron systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To systematically investigate the evolution of magnetic anisotropy and crystal-electric-field (CEF) ground states in the $ ext{SmTr}_2 ext{Ge}_2$ series.

Main result. The magnetic anisotropy systematically changes from easy $ab$ plane ($ ext{Co}, ext{Ni}$) to easy $c$ axis ($ ext{Cu}, ext{Ag}$), correlating with the evolution of the CEF ground state and local structural parameters.

Method. Measurements include magnetization, specific heat, and magnetic susceptibility ($\chi$) as a function of temperature and applied field, complemented by structural analysis.

Model / system. The study focuses on the $ ext{SmTr}_2 ext{Ge}_2$ compounds ($ ext{Tr} = ext{Co}, ext{Ni}, ext{Cu}, ext{Ag}$) with the $ ext{ThCr}_2 ext{Si}_2$-type structure, where magnetism originates from the $ ext{Sm}^{3+}$ $4f$ electrons.

Key observables. Magnetic anisotropy (easy plane vs. easy axis), CEF ground state evolution, magnetic entropy, and resistivity ($ ho(T)$) changes below $T_N$.

Important parameters / regimes. Transition metal identity ($ ext{Tr}$), applied magnetic field ($H=1 ext{ T}$), and temperature ($T$).

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes magnetic measurements ($\chi, C_p, M$) across the $ ext{Tr}$ series, correlating the observed changes in magnetic anisotropy and CEF ground state with the local structural environment determined by X-ray measurements.

Abstract

We investigated the crystal-electric-field (CEF) states in SmTr2Ge2 (Tr= Co-Cu and Ag) with the ThCr2Si2-type structure by magnetization and specific heat measurements. The magnetic anisotropy evolves systematically with Tr, changing from an easy ab plane for Tr = Co and Ni to an easy c axis for Tr = Cu and Ag. Combined analyses of the low-temperature susceptibility and magnetic entropy indicate a level crossing of the CEF ground state between Ni and Cu, together with a systematic evolution of the first excited CEF energy. The evolution of the magnetic properties correlates with changes in local structural parameters, providing an experimental basis for discussing how the ligand environment around the Sm ion is related to the 4f electronic state.

Summary. This paper introduces a general Non-Markovian Electronic Friction and Langevin Dynamics framework to simulate molecular dynamics coupled to metal leads. It demonstrates that retaining non-Markovian electronic memory effects is essential for accurately describing non-equilibrium charge transport and vibrational energy exchange. The method successfully reproduces exact quantum benchmarks where simpler Markovian approximations fail.

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of accurately simulating the non-equilibrium vibronic dynamics of molecules coupled to metal surfaces, particularly when electronic memory effects are significant.

Method. The authors develop a general framework utilizing a Markovian embedding scheme to sample coordinate-dependent quantum colored noise, allowing the simulation of non-Markovian electronic friction effects.

Abstract

We present a general framework for simulating the nonequilibrium vibronic dynamics of molecules interacting with metal surfaces, which utilizes non-Markovian electronic friction and the corresponding generalized Langevin equation. The method employs a Markovian embedding scheme to sample coordinate-dependent quantum colored noise, with the underlying memory kernels represented systematically by exponential decompositions obtained directly from the electronic forces. In doing so, the approach extends the widely used electronic friction and Langevin dynamics formalism beyond the conventional Markovian approximation while retaining the coordinate dependence of the electronic back-action. We demonstrate the method for nonequilibrium charge transport through a vibrationally coupled molecular junction, where comparisons with numerically exact hierarchical equations of motion simulations show that the non-Markovian formulation is both more accurate and more robust than its Markovian counterpart. In particular, we find that non-Markovian electronic forces play a decisive role in the nonequilibrium vibrational dynamics, stabilizing regimes in which the conventional Markovian approximation fails.

Abstract

Summary. This work develops a theoretical framework showing how strain and magnetic fields can control the topology of electronic states at a crystal-field crossing in Ce3+. Main result. The combination of strain and field detuning can generate an isolated diabolical point with unit-magnitude Chern charge in the 3D control space of a cubic Ce3+ ion.

Method. Theoretical derivation using an effective two-level Hamiltonian, analyzing the topological features (Chern charge) generated by independent strain and magnetic field controls.

Abstract

Field-tuned crystal-field crossings provide a route to synthetic topology in localized rare-earth degrees of freedom when symmetry-resolved perturbations can independently control the crossing states. Here we show that a field-induced crossing in cubic Ce$^{3+}$ has precisely this structure. For $B\parallel[001]$, the crossing is protected by a twofold rotation, while the $T_{2g}$ shears $ε_{xz}$ and $ε_{yz}$ break that protection and mix the two states through orthogonal pseudospin components. Together with magnetic-field detuning, these controls generate an isolated diabolical point with unit-magnitude Chern charge in a three-dimensional parameter space. Using the CeTe crystal-field scale gives an ideal single-ion crossing near $35.5$ T. A calibrated point-charge calculation gives a representative projected shear coupling $|α_{T_{2g}}|\simeq18.1$ meV per unit tensor strain, corresponding to gaps of $0.036$ and $0.072$ meV for tensor shears of $0.10\%$ and $0.20\%$. We identify the associated elastic and magnetic signatures, discuss the limitations of the electrostatic estimate in a hybridizing Ce compound, and determine the adiabatic and coherence requirements for cyclic geometric control. The result is a symmetry-based framework that connects high-field crystal-field reconstruction, strain response, and parameter-space topology in rare-earth systems.

Abstract

Influenza A persists by continually changing the antigens that its host population recognizes. How fast it evolves and how many hosts it infects are not independent, because the immunity that selects new variants is produced by the infections the pathogen has already caused. We analyze a model of Andreasen et.\ al.\ that captures this eco-evolutionary feedback, and we extend it to the case where infection may kill the host. In the model infected and recovered hosts are distributions over a one-dimensional antigenic space coupled by cross-immunity. The endemic state is a traveling pulse: the infected distribution keeps a fixed shape and advances through antigenic space at constant speed. In the limit of weak antigenic mutation we construct the pulse asymptotically and find that: (i) the speed of antigenic evolution decreases as prevalence rises, (ii) less standing antigenic variation accompanies either faster or slower antigenic evolution, depending on whether pathogens differ in immune evasion or host lifespan (iii) the total number of infected hosts is largest at an intermediate virulence when the host birth rate is sufficiently low.

Electrically controlled spin-splitting and asymmetric tunnel magnetoresistance in anti-altermagnets

Abstract

Anti-altermagnets (AAMs) are a recently identified class of layered magnetic materials where opposite spin-splitting in adjacent layers creates a globally spin-degenerate band structure, rendering conventional spectroscopic detection highly difficult. In this paper, we theoretically demonstrate an all-electrical method to manipulate and probe this hidden magnetic order using a dual-gated transport junction. By applying a perpendicular displacement field, we break the spatial inversion symmetry of the lattice, explicitly lifting the global spin degeneracy. We attach ferromagnetic (FM) leads on either side of the AAM. Using quantum transport calculations, we show that this gate-induced spin-splitting manifests as a strongly asymmetric tunnel magnetoresistance (TMR) as a function of the lead magnetization. We identify specific crystallographic orientations where the TMR retains its symmetry despite the fully split bands, a direct consequence of exact momentum-space compensation. We further reveal that Rashba spin-orbit coupling guarantees robust, highly directional transport asymmetries even in the absence of an explicit chemical potential mismatch between the layers. Our findings establish a clear, electrically tunable framework for exploiting AAMs in next-generation spintronic architectures.
