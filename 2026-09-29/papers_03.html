Front-based construction of quantum droplets, bubbles, and hole states in a Bose mixture

Authors: Sherzod R. Otajonov, Fatkhulla Kh. Abdullaev
Type: theory · Category: quantum gases · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.30807v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 3/5 · Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5

Summary. This paper theoretically explores nonlinear structures like quantum droplets and bubbles in Bose mixtures governed by an extended Gross-Pitaevskii equation. By analyzing the stability of background states and constructing solutions from nonlinear fronts, the authors map out a unified picture connecting these self-bound states. The findings are crucial for understanding the complex dynamics and stability of interacting quantum gases beyond mean-field theory.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a comprehensive theoretical framework connecting mean-field interactions, quantum fluctuations (LHY), and topological defects (kinks/holes) in Bose-Einstein condensates, which is central to modern AMO physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the existence and properties of nonlinear structures, such as quantum droplets, bubbles, and hole states, within a Bose mixture.

Main result. The paper establishes a unified front-based picture connecting these localized states, showing that collisions exhibit phase-dependent dynamics and that deep bubbles possess finite-time robustness.

Method. The research combines analytical methods, including Bogoliubov-de Gennes spectrum analysis and grand-potential calculations, with real-time numerical simulations of the time-dependent Gross-Pitaevskii equation.

Model / system. The system is an elongated Bose mixture modeled by an extended Gross-Pitaevskii equation incorporating both attractive cubic mean-field interactions and repulsive quartic Lee-Huang-Yang contributions.

Key observables. Modulational stability gain, density deficit/bubble depth, collision dynamics (merging, particle transfer), and the background amplitude $a$.

Important parameters / regimes. The chemical potential ($\mu$), the relative phase between components, and the separation distance between nonlinear fronts.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on classifying constant-amplitude backgrounds and assumes the quasi-one-dimensional regime for the effective LHY term.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the modulational instability gain for the lower background branch, the time evolution of perturbed asymmetric holes showing stability near the coexistence limit, and the phase-dependent merging dynamics during droplet collisions.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the stable background states, determines their stability via the Bogoliubov-de Gennes spectrum, constructs kink/antikink solutions, and then applies these fronts to model holes, bubbles, and droplet collisions in both scalar and two-component systems.

Abstract

We study nonlinear holes, bubbles, kink fronts, and quantum-droplet-like states in an elongated Bose mixture governed by an extended Gross-Pitaevskii equation with attractive cubic mean-field interactions and repulsive quartic Lee-Huang-Yang contribution. We classify the constant-amplitude backgrounds: the Bogoliubov-de Gennes spectrum shows that the upper branch is modulationally stable, whereas the lower branch is unstable to long-wavelength perturbations. Grand-potential and pressure determine the vacuum-finite-density coexistence point and the unique background supporting stationary kink and antikink fronts. Symmetric holes on the stable background have a density deficit and integral width that diverge near coexistence as the hole separates into two nonlinear fronts. A multiplicative kink-antikink construction connects bell-shaped states to broad flat-top droplets and approaches the stationary quantum-droplet family as the front separation increases. An antikink-kink sum with equal signs generates bubble-like density depletions without a phase jump, whereas their difference produces dark-hole-like profiles with a $π$ phase difference between the asymptotic backgrounds. Bubble depth increases with front separation, distinguishing gray-like and dark-like profiles. Real-time simulations show that deep bubbles persist under weak random perturbations, demonstrating finite-time robustness without establishing spectral stability. In the asymmetric two-component model, localized relative-density modes can induce spinor-driven breakup on a modulationally stable background, while robust holes persist near the kink limit. Collisions of kink-antikink droplet-like states display phase-dependent merging, asymmetric particle transfer, and effective reflection. These results connect nonlinear fronts with bubbles, phase-jump holes, and self-bound droplets in beyond-mean-field Bose mixtures.

Summary. This work analyzes a frustrated quantum spin chain, revealing the emergence of Wigner magnon crystals in the highly frustrated regime. By modeling the system using an effective lattice-gas approach, the authors demonstrate that the thermodynamic performance of a quantum Stirling engine is strongly dictated by these crystal phases, achieving peak efficiency within their stable regions.

Why it may be interesting. The direct connection drawn between the sequence of field-induced quantum phases (Wigner crystals) and the maximum efficiency of a quantum heat engine provides a concrete, measurable signature of quantum phase transitions in a many-body system.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the ground state, thermodynamics, and heat engine performance of a fully frustrated spin-1/2 Heisenberg four-leg tube.

Main result. The system exhibits Wigner magnon crystals in the highly frustrated regime, and the efficiency of a quantum Stirling engine is maximized deep within the stability regions of these crystal phases, not at the phase boundaries.

Method. The research employs a combination of exact diagonalization, density matrix renormalization group (DMRG), and mapping the low-energy physics onto an effective interacting lattice-gas model.

Model / system. The system is a fully frustrated spin-1/2 Heisenberg four-leg tube described by a Hamiltonian involving inter-plaquette ($J_1$) and intra-plaquette ($J_2$) couplings, subjected to a magnetic field ($h$).

Key observables. Ground state phase diagram, magnetization curves, low-temperature thermodynamics (entropy, specific heat), work output, and efficiency ($\eta$) of a quantum Stirling heat engine.

Important parameters / regimes. Frustration ratio ($J_2/J_1$), magnetic field strength ($h$), and temperature ratios ($T_H/J_1, T_L/J_1$).

Assumptions / limitations. The low-energy physics is accurately captured by an effective interacting lattice-gas model of two monomer quasi-particle species, which neglects higher-energy excitations.

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically illustrates the fully frustrated four-leg tube structure, detailing the $J_2$ and $J_1$ couplings.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the system's behavior across different frustration regimes, first establishing the magnon spectrum and Wigner crystal states using localized-magnon theory, then deriving an effective lattice-gas model, and finally applying this framework to calculate the thermodynamic properties and heat engine performance.

Abstract

The ground state, magnetization curves, low-temperature thermodynamics, and heat-engine performance of the fully frustrated spin-$1/2$ Heisenberg four-leg tube with antiferromagnetic inter- and intra-plaquette coupling constants J1 and J2 are examined using exact diagonalization, density matrix renormalization group, and localized-magnon theory. In the unfrustrated to weakly frustrated regime J2/J1 << 2, the system exhibits a continuous field-driven quantum phase transition between the gapped Haldane phase and a gapless Tomonaga-Luttinger quantum spin liquid. In the highly frustrated regime J2/J1 > 2, the system contrarily displays discontinuous field-driven quantum phase transitions between the Wigner magnon crystals, which are manifested in zero-temperature magnetization curves as intermediate plateaus at zero, one-quarter, one-half, and three-quarters of the saturation magnetization. The low-temperature magnetic and thermodynamic properties in this regime are accurately captured by an effective interacting lattice-gas model of two monomer quasi-particle species constructed from localized one- and two-magnon states. Finally, we explore a quantum Stirling heat engine using the fully frustrated four-leg tube as the working medium. The work output and efficiency are strongly suppressed near discontinuous field-induced transitions and reach pronounced local maxima well inside the stability regions of the Wigner magnon crystals.

Measurement-Based Uncomputation from an Error Correction Perspective

Authors: Minjun Jeon, Po-Wei Huang, Zhenyu Cai
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.31605v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 3/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper proposes Measurement-Based Uncomputation (MBU) as an efficient alternative to complex inverse quantum circuits. By interpreting the measurement process through a Quantum Error Correction lens, the authors show that post-selection on zero-syndrome outcomes yields the desired uncomputation state. This approach significantly reduces overhead by linking success probability to the low-dimensional affine rank of the system's support.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on computation, the rigorous mathematical framework connecting measurement outcomes to error syndromes and the use of algebraic rank to bound resource requirements is highly relevant to understanding noise and error mitigation in any physical quantum system.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Developing an efficient method for Measurement-Based Uncomputation (MBU) to replace the costly coherent inverse circuits required in fault-tolerant quantum computation.

Main result. Post-selected MBU achieves the necessary uncomputation by accepting only zero-syndrome measurement outcomes, with a success probability determined by the low-dimensional affine rank of the garbage support.

Method. The authors recast MBU into a Quantum Error Correction (QEC) framework, analyzing measurement outcomes as syndrome measurements that induce relative phases.

Model / system. The model involves a quantum computation with a main register and a garbage register ($G$). The process is analyzed by measuring $G$ in the X basis, relating the outcome to error syndromes.

Key observables. Success probability ($P_{ ext{succ}}$), relative phases induced by measurement outcomes, and the affine rank ($r_\Lambda$) of the populated garbage support.

Important parameters / regimes. The affine rank $r_\Lambda$ dictates the success probability ($P_{ ext{succ}} \propto 2^{-r_\Lambda}$), which is much smaller than the number of garbage qubits.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on post-selecting on outcomes that yield zero relative phase, and later extends to bounding deviations from the ideal orthogonal case using coherence measures.

Figures summary. Figure 1 conceptually maps the process from standard computation to MBU and its QEC interpretation. Other figures detail the comparison between standard and MBU implementations in algorithms like HHL.

Paper structure. The paper builds from the conceptual problem of uncomputation to the algebraic framework of MBU via QEC, deriving the success probability based on the affine rank, and finally detailing structural conditions for deterministic correction.

Abstract

Coherent uncomputation can be as costly as the computation itself, creating substantial spacetime overheads that can be prohibitive on early fault-tolerant quantum computers. We develop an algebraic framework for measurement-based uncomputation (MBU) through a quantum error-correction picture: measurements of the garbage register induce relative phases analogous to errors causing non-trivial syndromes. Accepting only zero-syndrome outcomes achieves post-selected MBU, removing the need for an uncomputation oracle at the cost of a probabilistic success rate determined by the affine rank of the populated garbage support. This rank can be much smaller than the number of garbage qubits, as illustrated through an example of the HHL algorithm. We then derive a structural condition under which the original uncomputation oracle can be reused to correct the phase for all measurement outcomes. No correction is required for the zero syndrome, while corrections for non-zero syndromes can be further adapted to individual measurement outcomes for simpler circuits, as illustrated by existing modular-arithmetic, QROM, and QRAM MBU constructions. Phase-correction circuits can be further simplified by co-optimising their implementation with the representation of the measurement-induced phases. Selecting which syndrome classes to correct interpolates between post-selected, partially corrected, and deterministic MBU, enabling trade-offs among correction cost, workspace, and repetitions.

Non-vanishing Density of States in Disordered Weyl Semimetals

Authors: Justin H. Wilson
Type: theory · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.30372v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 3/5

Summary. This theoretical work resolves a major conflict in the literature concerning the density of states in disordered Weyl semimetals. By performing a comprehensive saddle-point calculation including quantum fluctuations, the authors prove that the zero-energy density of states remains finite regardless of the disorder strength. This provides a crucial theoretical benchmark for understanding topological materials in realistic, imperfect environments.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous application of advanced field theory (supersymmetry, instanton calculus) to determine a fundamental transport property ($ ho(0)$) in a topological material under disorder is highly relevant for understanding robustness in quantum devices.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper resolves a long-standing theoretical disagreement regarding whether the zero-energy density of states ($ ho(0)$) in a 3D Weyl semimetal vanishes when subjected to weak short-range disorder.

Main result. The authors establish that $ ho(0)$ is finite for any disorder strength, providing an analytical expression that matches exact numerical calculations over several orders of magnitude.

Method. The calculation employs a full saddle-point approximation within a supersymmetric field-theoretic framework, combining instanton calculations with the computation of one-loop fluctuations via a convergent Fredholm determinant.

Model / system. The system is a three-dimensional Weyl semimetal described by a Hamiltonian subjected to weak, short-range, Gaussian-correlated disorder. The analysis utilizes advanced techniques from supersymmetric quantum field theory and replica methods.

Key observables. Zero-energy density of states, $ ho(0)$, and the associated non-vanishing prefactor $\mathcal{A}$ and exponent $s^*$.

Important parameters / regimes. Disorder strength $w$, and the calculated constants $s^* = 6.4163(2)$ and $\mathcal{A} = 27.47(8)$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the supersymmetric formulation of disorder and the validity of the saddle-point approximation for the functional integral.

Figures summary. Figure 1 compares the calculated $ ho(0)$ with exact numerics, showing agreement over four orders of magnitude. Other figures illustrate the radial profiles of the hedgehog solution and the convergence of determinant calculation methods.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first setting up the problem using supersymmetric functional integrals, solving the nonlinear Weyl equation for the instanton solution, and then calculating the fluctuations by determining the reduced superdeterminant as a Fredholm determinant. The final result is the explicit, finite expression for $ ho(0)$.

Abstract

There is disagreement in the literature concerning whether the nodal point of a three-dimensional Weyl semimetal survives weak short-range disorder: instanton calculations including fluctuations claim that the zero-energy density of states $ρ(0)$ vanishes, while exact numerics find a systematically finite number. We settle this disagreement with a full saddle-point calculation, including both instanton and fluctuations. Within the supersymmetric formulation for disorder, the instanton obeys a nonlinear Weyl equation whose solution is an exact $j=1/2$ hedgehog whose full functional form we numerically compute. We then compute the fluctuations about this saddle point, carefully identifying all zero modes, and computing the reduced superdeterminant as a convergent Fredholm determinant. No fermionic zero mode beyond a Kramers doublet exists, and at finite disorder $w$, the fluctuations become a finite one-loop prefactor, $ρ(0)=\mathcal{A} w^{-4} \exp(-s^*/w^2)(1+O(w^2))$, in normalized units with $s^* = 6.4163(2)$ and $\mathcal{A} = 27.47(8)$ for Gaussian-correlated disorder. This expression matches exact numerics on a single Weyl cone over four orders of magnitude with no fitted parameters (the amplitude is $\times 0.76$ the one-loop value), establishing that the density of states is finite for any disorder strength.

On the applicability of Kolmogorov's theory of probability to the description of quantum phenomena. Part II: Bell inequalities

Authors: Maik Reddiger
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.31075v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 3/5

Summary. This paper re-examines Bell inequalities by analyzing them through the lens of Kolmogorov's probability theory, introducing the concept of contextuality. It demonstrates that even 'classical' models can be contextual, and crucially, that contextual models can violate Bell inequalities even under strong locality assumptions. This suggests that the physical conclusions drawn from Bell tests might stem from a conceptual misunderstanding regarding the nature of probability itself.

Why it may be interesting. It challenges the fundamental assumptions underlying the interpretation of quantum nonlocality, which is crucial for understanding measurement processes in quantum information and open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper analyzes the applicability of Kolmogorov's theory of probability to quantum phenomena, specifically focusing on the implications of violating Bell inequalities.

Main result. Contextuality reveals a conceptual misunderstanding in the physical conclusions drawn from Bell inequality violations, suggesting that 'nonlocality' does not necessarily follow from empirical violations while respecting locality.

Method. The analysis involves a comprehensive mathematical study of Bell experiments, focusing on the BCHSH inequality and introducing the notion of contextuality into the probabilistic framework.

Model / system. The study uses the framework of Bell experiments, analyzing correlations between detector outcomes (A and B) for various settings (a, b), and comparing classical probabilistic models against quantum predictions.

Key observables. Expectation values, such as $\langle A(a), B(b) angle$, and the violation magnitude of the BCHSH inequality.

Important parameters / regimes. The settings of the detectors ($a, b$) and the underlying assumptions of locality and noncontextuality.

Assumptions / limitations. The work critically examines the assumption of noncontextuality, which is a core assumption in the standard derivation of Bell inequalities.

Figures summary. Not specified.

Paper structure. The work is presented as Part II of a series, building upon foundational discussions of quantum mechanics and probability theory, and focusing specifically on the mathematical and physical interpretation of Bell inequalities.

Abstract

Among the various "no-go theorems" in the foundations of quantum mechanics, Bell inequalities are among the few, which do not implicitly rely on the mathematical structure of quantum mechanics. Instead, Bell inequalities are commonly understood to rest on Kolmogorov's theory of probability and a "locality" assumption. Physical evidence supporting their violation is hence taken as evidence against at least one of these two assumptions. This work provides a comprehensive analysis of the physical debate on Bell inequalities from the point of view of Kolmogorov's theory of probability, focusing on the Bell-Clauser-Horn-Shimony-Holt inequality (BCHSH inequality). The central contribution is the motivation and mathematical study of the notion of contextuality in Bell experiments: in so-called contextual Bell models the detectors themselves behave randomly and the distribution of the random detector parameters depends on the chosen detector settings. It is shown how even "classical" Bell experiments motivate such a description. The need to account for contextuality was pointed out before by de la Peña, Cetto, Brody, Lochak, Bohm, Hiley and others. A broad class of contextual models is proven to satisfy the BCHSH inequality, thereby generalizing a previous result by Gill and Lambare. Nonetheless, even under strong locality requirements, contextual Bell models may violate the BCHSH inequality: based on work by Popescu and Rohrlich, we consider a "proof of concept" example exhibiting the maximum possible violation. Contrary to the much debated "loopholes" in empirical tests of Bell inequalities, contextuality thus reveals a conceptual misunderstanding in the physical conclusions commonly drawn from Bell inequality violations.

Abstract

Summary. This paper presents quantum algorithms to compute the exponential of banded Toeplitz matrices, which arise when discretizing PDEs. By relating these matrices to circulant generators diagonalized by the QFT, the authors circumvent the computational bottleneck associated with large matrix norms. This provides an efficient quantum simulation tool for physical systems like the heat equation.

Main problem. The paper develops quantum algorithms to efficiently approximate the matrix exponential of banded Toeplitz matrices, which are crucial for simulating time evolution governed by Partial Differential Equations (PDEs).

Abstract

We present quantum algorithms to approximate the exponential of banded Toeplitz matrices. These matrices have central importance in many PDE related problems, but their quantum implementation is hindered by their possibly large norms. Constructing directly the exponential we can circumvent this limitation. By relating the lower/upper shift operators to circulant and skewcirculant generators, which are diagonalised by the QFT, we construct (i) an LCU-based block encoding of banded Toeplitz matrices, (ii) an efficient, controllably truncated Pauli-string decomposition of the circulant eigenphases with a closed-form error bound, and (iii) a specialized QFT-Trotter product formula. As an application we build a block encoding of the propagator of the discretised heat equation with periodic, Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions, using a frequency cutoff.

Summary. This paper introduces Oracle Distillation, a protocol designed to purify an unknown, noisy quantum oracle by querying it many times. It proves that this distillation process can successfully protect quantum advantages for key algorithms, such as Grover search, even when the error rate is non-zero but below a critical threshold. This opens a path for robust quantum sensing and computation.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a robust theoretical framework for quantum sensing and machine learning by showing how quantum advantages can be maintained against realistic noise models, which is crucial for experimental quantum information science.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The central challenge is developing a method to preserve quantum computational advantages in learning and sensing tasks when the underlying system interaction (the oracle) is unknown and corrupted by noise.

Main result. The authors introduce Oracle Distillation, proving that quantum advantage for problems like Grover search is retained at low error rates, provided the noise is below a specific constant threshold.

Method. The core technique is Oracle Distillation (OD), which consumes many noisy queries to synthesize a single, high-fidelity oracle. This relies on using 'weak queries' to facilitate error correction while preserving necessary information.

Model / system. The system is modeled by an unknown unitary 'oracle' acting as the interface between the quantum device and the external system. Noise is modeled using both adversarial corruption and i.i.d. depolarizing noise.

Key observables. Fidelity of the distilled oracle ($\epsilon$-closeness), quantum advantage over classical methods, and the threshold error rate ($p^*$).

Important parameters / regimes. The per-qubit depolarizing rate ($p$), the required fidelity ($\epsilon$), and the query complexity ($TOD$).

Assumptions / limitations. The protocol assumes a universal quantum computer with ideal gates, focusing noise only on the oracle itself.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the OD process, showing noisy queries distilling to a high-fidelity oracle, and Figure 1b plots the advantage threshold versus noise parameters.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the problem of noise in unknown-oracle tasks, then constructs the OD protocol for adversarial noise, establishes its near-optimality, and finally proves a threshold theorem for i.i.d. depolarizing noise, extending the concept to continuous time.

Abstract

Learning and sensing are among the most promising applications of quantum technology, often with provable quantum advantages. In any realistic experiment, however, noise threatens to erase these advantages as the problem size grows. We introduce oracle distillation, a procedure that distills many queries to a noisy oracle into a single high-fidelity oracle. Here the oracle, an unknown unitary from a known family, models the interface through which a quantum device learns about an unknown system. We construct an explicit protocol that distills any Boolean oracle, both under adversarial noise that corrupts a constant fraction of the input qubits and under i.i.d. depolarizing noise at a constant rate on every qubit. At the heart of the protocol is the weak query, in which the device deliberately queries the oracle only weakly, trading response strength for the ability to correct errors. Our protocol is near-optimal among all protocols that distill the oracle while keeping its errors correctable, revealing a fundamental tension between these two requirements. An important implication of the protocol is a threshold theorem: every Boolean oracle problem whose quantum advantage is polynomial in the domain size, including Grover search, $k$-forrelation, and Simon's problem, retains a quantum advantage whenever the per-qubit depolarizing rate is below a constant threshold. In particular, the quadratic quantum advantage of Grover search, long believed fragile under noise, survives nearly intact at low error rates even when almost every query involves errors on one or more qubits. We further extend the protocol to fractional and continuous-time Boolean oracles. Quantum error correction can thus protect not only prescribed operations but also unknown dynamics. Our results make a large family of quantum advantages in learning robust against noise and open a path toward robust computational sensing.

Summary. This paper presents Stabilized Analytic Continuation (SAC) as a robust method to calculate the von Neumann entropy from limited integer Rényi entropy measurements. The authors provide deep theoretical insights into the limitations of this process, particularly identifying how zeros of the trace function $ ext{Tr} ho^z$ can cause analytic continuation to fail, leading to observable phase transitions.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical framework connecting entanglement entropy calculation to complex analysis (RKHS theory) provides powerful, general tools for analyzing quantum correlations in strongly correlated systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The central challenge is extracting the physically meaningful von Neumann entropy ($S_{ ext{vN}}$) from numerical data that only provides access to integer Rényi entropies ($S_n$).

Main result. The authors developed and rigorously analyzed Stabilized Analytic Continuation (SAC), showing it is robust but can fail when the Rényi function develops singularities (Fisher zeros) due to zeros of $ ext{Tr} ho^z$.

Method. The work combines analytic continuation techniques, mapping the problem onto a Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS) framework, and deriving universal bounds on the location of singularities based on spectral properties.

Model / system. The analysis is applied to general many-body quantum systems, benchmarking performance on the toric code, Kagome Heisenberg models, and $(1+1)d$ compact-boson CFTs.

Key observables. Von Neumann entropy ($S_{ ext{vN}}$), mutual information, integer Rényi entropies ($S_n$), and the function $ ext{Tr} ho^z$ in the complex $z$-plane.

Important parameters / regimes. The rank ($r$) of the density matrix, the number of input Rényi samples ($N$), and the location of zeros of $ ext{Tr} ho^z$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis often assumes a noiseless setting for the theoretical derivations, and the SAC method relies on the minimum-norm interpolant within a specific RKHS structure.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the SAC construction, the extraction of topological entanglement entropy for the Kagome model, and the reconstruction of mutual information across various physical benchmarks.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces SAC as a robust alternative to conventional extrapolation. It then provides theoretical derivations establishing zero-free domains for $ ext{Tr} ho^z$ and deriving bounds on singularities. Finally, it benchmarks SAC performance across three distinct physical settings: topological entropy, mutual information, and quantum dynamics.

Abstract

Extracting operationally meaningful quantities such as von Neumann entropy and mutual information is central to characterizing many-body quantum systems, yet experiments and numerics often provide direct access only to integer Rényi entropies. Whereas conventional extrapolation relies on a prescribed fitting ansatz, in PRL 137, 100202 we introduced an alternative approach based on stabilized analytic continuation (SAC), which avoids such an ansatz and is inherently robust to noise. Here, we further develop this framework in the noiseless setting and benchmark its performance across several nontrivial many-body problems. We first show that, besides the intrinsic ambiguity of reconstructing the von Neumann entropy from finitely many Rényi samples, analytic continuation can also fail because of genuine nonanalyticities in the Rényi function arising from zeros of $\text{Tr}ρ^z$. We derive universal zero-free domains for $\text{Tr}ρ^z$ and systematically sharpen these bounds using additional spectral information about $ρ$. We then benchmark the performance of SAC in three settings: ($i$) extracting topological entanglement entropy from DMRG Rényi data for the toric code and Kagome Heisenberg models; ($ii$) reconstructing the mutual information between disjoint intervals in a $(1+1)d$ compact-boson CFT; and ($iii$) recovering finite-time ballistic growth of the von Neumann entropy from integer Rényi entropies that cross over toward subballistic growth in diffusive quantum dynamics. In the latter setting, analytic continuation is expected to fail in the long time limit. We illustrate this mechanism with a physically motivated two-sector model, where the asymptotic separation between von Neumann and higher-Rényi growth is accompanied by a zero of $\text{Tr}ρ^z$ pinching the real axis at $z=1$, thereby producing a first-order Rényi phase transition.
