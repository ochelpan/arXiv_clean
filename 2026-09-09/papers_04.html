Renormalization of One-Dimensional Semirelativistic Bosons with Contact Interactions

Authors: Fatih Erman, O. Teoman Turgut
Type: theory · Category: quantum gases · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09068v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 2/5

Summary. This theoretical paper develops a renormalization scheme for one-dimensional bosons with semirelativistic dispersion and contact interactions. By systematically handling logarithmic divergences using advanced resolvent techniques, the authors prove that the full interacting Hamiltonian converges to the established Lieb-Liniger model in the nonrelativistic limit. This provides a powerful, rigorous framework for studying strongly interacting quantum gases.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous, field-theoretic approach to handling strong, short-range interactions in low-dimensional quantum systems, connecting a relativistic-like dispersion to a standard nonrelativistic model (Lieb-Liniger).

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the renormalization of a quantum many-body system consisting of one-dimensional spinless bosons interacting via attractive contact potentials, which leads to ultraviolet divergences.

Main result. The theory successfully constructs a cutoff-independent, self-adjoint Hamiltonian whose nonrelativistic limit precisely reproduces the well-known attractive Lieb-Liniger Hamiltonian.

Method. The methodology involves advanced renormalization group techniques using an enlarged Fock space and the Schur-complement representation of the resolvent to systematically absorb divergences.

Model / system. The system models spinless bosons in one dimension with a semirelativistic dispersion relation ($\hat{p}^2 + m^2$) and attractive contact interactions ($\delta(x)$). The Hamiltonian is studied in the context of quantum field theory renormalization.

Key observables. The zero-total-momentum two-body bound-state energy ($E_B$), the renormalized resolvent $R(E)$, and the mean-field binding scale $|E_{MF}|$.

Important parameters / regimes. The bare coupling constant ($\lambda$), the particle number ($N$), and the momentum cutoff ($\Lambda$ or $c$).

Assumptions / limitations. The procedure assumes the existence of a unique self-adjoint Hamiltonian defined by the resolvent, and convergence is established by comparing the semirelativistic result to the known Lieb-Liniger model.

Figures summary. The notes reference mathematical equations (e.g., Eq. 1.1, Eq. 5.15, Eq. C.102) detailing the formal structure of the Hamiltonians, resolvents, and convergence proofs, rather than describing visual figures.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining the divergent Hamiltonian and the renormalization procedure (using the resolvent) to explicitly calculating the two-body sector, and finally demonstrating the convergence of the full $N$-body theory to the nonrelativistic Lieb-Liniger model.

Abstract

We study one-dimensional spinless bosons with semirelativistic spinless-Salpeter dispersion and attractive pairwise contact interactions. Because the dispersion becomes linear at large momentum, the contact interaction is marginal by power counting and produces a logarithmic ultraviolet divergence. We construct the renormalized many-body theory using an enlarged Fock space and a Schur-complement representation of the resolvent, eliminating the bare coupling in favor of the physical zero-total-momentum two-body bound-state energy. The resulting cutoff-independent resolvent defines a self-adjoint Hamiltonian in each fixed particle-number sector. We treat the two-body problem explicitly and show, in the norm-resolvent sense, that the nonrelativistic limit reproduces the attractive Lieb--Liniger Hamiltonian. We also formulate a mean-field approximation directly within the renormalized theory. In the massless and deeply bound large-particle-number regimes, it predicts an exponentially increasing binding scale whose exponent is determined by a one-dimensional variational problem.

Snakes on a Hyper-Plane: Dynamical and Ontological Locality in Everettian Quantum Mechanics

Authors: Travis Norsen

Summary. This paper critically examines whether Everettian Quantum Mechanics, which posits non-local ontological states, can remain compatible with relativistic causality. By analyzing how local interventions affect global, non-local properties, the authors argue that the theory likely violates the principle of dynamical locality. This forces a re-evaluation of how non-local beables must be treated in relativistic quantum theories.

Why it may be interesting. It challenges fundamental assumptions about how quantum theories incorporating non-local ontologies can respect relativistic causality, which is a major topic in quantum foundations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper questions the consensus that Spacetime State Realism (SSR) Everettian Quantum Mechanics (EQM) is compatible with relativistic causality, despite positing ontological non-locality.

Main result. The involvement of non-local beables introduces complications for dynamical locality, suggesting that SSR EQM might violate relativistic causality.

Method. The argument modifies thought experiments (like the snake analogy) and analyzes the behavior of local versus non-local beables (like reduced density matrices) under measurement interventions.

Model / system. The analysis focuses on Everettian Quantum Mechanics (EQM) under the Spacetime State Realism (SSR) interpretation, using conceptual models of localized and non-localized quantum states.

Key observables. Local beables (Reduced Density Matrices for localized regions) and non-local beables (joint/holistic states for non-localized regions).

Important parameters / regimes. The causal structure, specifically the relationship between causes and effects relative to the future light cone.

Assumptions / limitations. The core assumption being challenged is that dynamical locality can be maintained when the underlying ontology includes non-separable, non-local beables.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the original snake thought experiment (Figure 1) and a modified version (Figure 2) showing a localized cause affecting a global, irreducible holistic property partially outside the future light cone.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the conflict between ontological non-locality and dynamical locality, then formalizing the conflict using modified thought experiments, and finally applying this framework to the EPR setup to show the effect on the joint state.

Abstract

An apparently emerging consensus holds that Everettian Quantum Mechanics (EQM), in the Spacetime State Realism (SSR) version of Wallace and Timpson, involves ontological non-locality (i.e., non-separability) but is fully compatible with the dynamical notion of locality usually taken to be required by relativity. The claim of dynamical locality is based on the fact that the local beables postulated by the theory (namely, Reduced Density Matrices, RDMs, associated with localized regions) are not affected by interventions outside their past light cones. However, the theory's non-separability means that it posits non-local beables (namely RDMs for non-localized regions) in addition to the local ones. We argue here that the involvement of non-local beables introduces complications for extant formulations of dynamical locality and at least suggests, contrary to the emerging consensus, that SSR EQM should perhaps not be considered compatible with relativistic causality after all.

Summary. This work establishes a Fisher-information speed limit that quantifies the maximum rate at which a neural network can learn about underlying latent variables during stochastic training. By modeling SGD as a stochastic process, the authors decompose this rate into deterministic (drift) and fluctuating (noise) components. This provides a powerful, information-theoretic diagnostic tool for understanding the efficiency and limits of machine learning algorithms.

Why it may be interesting. The use of information-theoretic speed limits to characterize the dynamics of complex, non-equilibrium learning processes provides a quantitative physical analogy to relaxation dynamics seen in many-body systems or open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To derive a Fisher-information flow speed limit that bounds the rate at which trainable parameters in neural networks acquire information about latent variables during stochastic learning.

Main result. The rate of information acquisition is bounded by the sum of drift and noise contributions, and this bound is shown to be tight at the peak of the flow in analytically tractable models.

Method. The authors formulate Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) as a Markovian stochastic process and utilize the Fisher information framework to derive an upper bound on the information flow.

Model / system. The model is stochastic learning dynamics, specifically analyzing SGD applied to supervised learning where data is generated from a latent variable $Z$. The analysis is specialized to basis-function linear regression, whose dynamics near convergence reduce to an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process.

Key observables. Fisher-information flow ($J_{ heta, \chi}^{Z,t}$), Fisher information ($F_{ heta}^{Z,t}$), Drift information budget ($I_{ ext{drift}, \chi}^{Z,t}$), and Noise information budget ($I_{ ext{noise}, \chi}^{Z,t}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Relaxation rates ($\lambda_i$), characteristic time scales ($ au_{\chi, i}$), and the ratio of initial to final covariance values.

Assumptions / limitations. The mini-batch fluctuation is approximated as Gaussian, and the analysis relies on deriving an upper bound, which is not necessarily an exact decomposition of the actual flow.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the time evolution of Fisher information and the instantaneous flow, comparing the measured flow against the theoretical speed limit derived from the drift and noise components, showing the bound is tight at the peak.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the general Fisher-information flow speed limit for SGD, then specializes this framework to linear regression. It analyzes the dynamics using modal decomposition, showing that the peak time of the information flow matches the relaxation time of the drift budget for each mode.

Abstract

Neural networks acquire internal representations through learning. In this work, we formulate stochastic gradient descent (SGD) as a Markovian stochastic process and derive a Fisher-information flow speed limit that bounds the rate at which trainable parameters can acquire information about latent variables in the data-generating process. The resulting inequality decomposes the information flow into drift and noise contributions, thereby quantifying the roles of deterministic learning forces and SGD-induced fluctuations from an information-theoretic perspective. We verify the bound in analytically tractable basis-function linear regression, where the information budget predicted by the bound reproduces the ordering and characteristic time scales with which different latent variables are encoded in the learned parameters. These results establish Fisher-information speed limits as a quantitative framework for diagnosing when and how different aspects of the data-generating mechanism are acquired during stochastic learning.

Summary. This paper addresses the challenge of tracking complex quantum many-body dynamics by introducing the 'Scrooge ensemble.' By constraining this maximum-entropy ensemble only with the expectation values of slow, conserved modes, the authors demonstrate a principle called 'global restricted typicality.' This allows for the accurate reconstruction of nonlinear quantum properties, like entanglement entropy, even when standard hydrodynamic methods are insufficient.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a powerful, non-standard statistical tool (the Scrooge ensemble) to extract global, non-linear information about non-equilibrium quantum states, bridging the gap between local observables (hydrodynamics) and full quantum state characterization.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Characterizing the time evolution of generic quantum many-body systems is computationally intractable due to the exponential scaling of state representation.

Main result. The authors introduce 'global restricted typicality,' showing that complex, nonlinear quantum properties can be accurately reconstructed by constraining the maximum-entropy Scrooge ensemble using only the system's slow, conserved modes.

Method. The core method involves constructing the Scrooge ensemble, which maximizes information entropy subject to fixing the first statistical moments (expectation values) of the slow modes, thereby bypassing standard coarse-graining assumptions.

Model / system. The work applies to generic quantum many-body systems, particularly those exhibiting a timescale separation where dynamics are governed by the transport of conserved charges, such as those studied in quantum hydrodynamics.

Key observables. Entanglement entropy (half-chain) and participation entropy in the computational basis.

Important parameters / regimes. Timescale separation, slow modes (conserved charges), and the constraints derived from the system's slow dynamics.

Assumptions / limitations. The approach assumes a timescale separation and that the dynamics are governed by the transport of conserved charges, allowing the slow modes to constrain the global state.

Figures summary. Figure 1 conceptually illustrates global restricted typicality, showing the time-evolved state lying on a dynamically restricted manifold, and outlines the operational pipeline from microstate to the Scrooge ensemble.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theoretical framework by defining the Scrooge ensemble, applying it to systems with slow dynamics, and demonstrating its ability to recover nonlinear quantum properties by constraining the ensemble solely with slow mode expectation values.

Abstract

Characterizing the time evolution of generic quantum many-body systems is a fundamental challenge, as representing the exact state requires exponentially scaling computational resources. While hydrodynamics and statistical mechanics successfully simplify this task by predicting the expectation values of local observables, these macroscopic frameworks provide no information about nonlinear characteristics of the quantum state. In this work, we demonstrate that for systems exhibiting a timescale separation, with dynamics governed by the transport of conserved charges, this lost information can be systematically recovered. By constraining the maximum-entropy Scrooge ensemble solely by the system's slow modes, we accurately reconstruct complex, nonlinear quantum properties of the global time-evolved state, including the half-chain entanglement entropy and the participation entropy in the computational basis. Our results generalize the paradigm of canonical typicality, revealing that a hydrodynamically bottlenecked system behaves as a typical pure state within a dynamically restricted submanifold of the Hilbert space - a phenomenon we term global restricted typicality.

Abstract

Quantum speed limits impose intrinsic lower bounds on the shortest time scale for quantum system evolution. As an emerging paradigm in quantum resource theory, quantum-state texture has attracted research interest amid the rapid advancement of quantum theory. Herein, we investigate the interplay between quantum speed limits and quantum-state texture via several canonical quantifiers, including trace distance, state rugosity and Jensen-Shannon divergence. Our results reveal that quantum speed limits derived from quantum-state texture capture the fundamental constraints on quantum evolutionary speed, with promising applications in quantum computing, quantum control and quantum metrology.

Summary. This paper rigorously investigates how topological invariants, such as the modular commutator and Hall conductance, maintain their robustness when the idealized local quantum Markov condition is only approximately met. By combining continuity proofs with finite-time dynamical analysis, the authors establish that these invariants remain asymptotically invariant in gapped phases. This work extends theoretical no-go theorems by providing quantitative bounds on the necessary entanglement structure required to support non-zero topological responses.

Why it may be interesting. It provides rigorous mathematical tools to test the stability of topological invariants in realistic, non-ideal physical systems where exact symmetries are broken by finite-size effects or dynamics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To determine the robustness and invariance of the modular commutator and Hall conductance estimator as topological invariants when the exact local quantum Markov condition is violated.

Main result. The modular commutator and Hall conductance estimator remain asymptotically invariant under topology-preserving deformations if the local quantum Markov property holds approximately. Furthermore, a quantitative lower bound is established for the conditional mutual information required to sustain a non-zero invariant.

Method. The analysis combines proofs of asymptotic invariance using trace-norm continuity, the study of finite-time dynamics generated by modular Hamiltonians, and deriving quantitative bounds on entanglement measures.

Model / system. The study focuses on two-dimensional gapped topological phases, particularly chiral topological phases characterized by the chiral central charge ($c_-$) and systems exhibiting Hall conductance ($\sigma_{xy}$).

Key observables. Modular commutator ($J$), Hall conductance estimator ($\Sigma$), and Conditional Mutual Information ($I(A:C|B)$).

Important parameters / regimes. The degree of violation of the local quantum Markov condition (A1), and the exponential decay rates of entanglement measures.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the existence of an 'approximately local quantum Markov property' and relies on asymptotic limits and continuity arguments.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the geometry for the three-sector disk partition used for calculating $J$ and $\Sigma$, alongside the local geometry related to the A1 condition.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing asymptotic invariance under approximate Markov conditions, then proving trace-norm continuity, and finally deriving quantitative lower bounds on the required conditional mutual information by analyzing finite-time dynamics.

Abstract

The modular commutator provides a bulk, local, single-wave-function probe of the chiral central charge $c_-$ for gapped ground states. Its invariance under deformations was previously established under a local quantum Markov condition---namely, the conditional mutual information $I(A:C|B)$ is zero for all tripartitions of a disk into three consecutive strips A, B, and C \cite{Modular-commutator-Gapped}. However, the local quantum Markov condition also forces the probe to vanish, leaving open whether modular commutator remains robust in physically relevant states where the Markov property holds only approximately. In this paper, we resolve this tension for finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces: the approximate local quantum Markov property implies the change of the modular commutator under topology-preserving deformations vanishes asymptotically. We next prove trace-norm continuity of the modular commutator. Combining deformation invariance with trace-norm continuity, we establish that the modular commutator remains asymptotically invariant within gapped quantum phases connected by quasi-local unitary paths that preserve the approximate local quantum Markov condition. Conversely, by analyzing finite-time dynamics generated by modular Hamiltonians, we derive a quantitative lower bound on the conditional mutual information required for a non-zero modular commutator: $I(A:C|B)$ across such strip tripartitions cannot decay faster than exponentially with the width of B given that the state satisfies entanglement area law, extending the exact no-go theorem of \cite{strict-J-2024} to a quantitative finite bound . Finally, we demonstrate that those conclusions apply equally to the Hall conductance estimator \cite{FanSahayVishwanath2023}.

Summary. This paper develops a theoretical framework to quantify gravitational decoherence arising from the emission of soft gravitons. Main result. The decoherence exponent is derived to be gauge-invariant and non-negative, showing that soft gravitons carry away which-path information, leading to an enhancement proportional to the gravitational-wave energy density and the fourth power of the observation time in certain regimes.

Method. The analysis employs Weinberg's soft theorem, calculating the decoherence functional via the overlap of coherent radiation states and extending the formalism to include squeezed graviton states.

Abstract

We revisit gravitational decoherence of matter superpositions using the leading soft sector of the graviton $S$-matrix. Starting from Weinberg's soft theorem and the overlap of coherent radiation states, we derive, for branch configurations with the same total four-momentum, a gauge-invariant, nonnegative leading-soft decoherence exponent and evaluate the polarization sum and angular integrals exactly at this order. In the vacuum, coherence between branches with distinguishable soft radiation vanishes as the infrared cutoff is removed. The leading nonrelativistic term is purely quadrupolar as a consequence of energy--momentum conservation. We extend the analysis to single- and two-mode squeezed graviton states, including the two-mode case motivated by inflation. Squeezing can enhance or suppress decoherence depending on the relative phase, while phase averaging gives an enhancement governed by the graviton occupation. For a phase-averaged relic background with an approximately flat energy-density spectrum over the relevant soft frequency band, the sharp-cutoff identification of the infrared scale with the inverse observation time gives a contribution that grows with the gravitational-wave energy density and with the fourth power of the observation time. These results connect soft-graviton scattering, infrared quantum information, and decoherence in nonvacuum gravitational backgrounds.

Summary. This work explores how localized defects can fundamentally alter the topology of electronic systems, leading to the formation of Topological Impurity Bands (TIBs). By analyzing impurity-induced bound states, the authors derive an effective low-energy theory applicable to lattice models like the Haldane model. This provides a local, defect-mediated route to realizing topological quantum phases.

Why it may be interesting. The concept of disorder-induced topology, particularly through localized defects, provides a concrete mechanism for realizing topological phases that might be inaccessible via uniform disorder averaging, which is relevant for understanding engineered quantum materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how localized defects, unlike long-wavelength disorder, can induce novel topological phase transitions in quantum materials.

Main result. A finite density of defects can generate Topological Impurity Bands (TIBs), leading to new topological phase transitions even when the clean system is trivial.

Method. The theory employs T-matrix calculations to find impurity bound states and derives an effective low-energy Hamiltonian by considering the hybridization of these states.

Model / system. The study applies to lattice models like the Qi-Wu-Zhang and Haldane models, analyzing the effect of a local mass defect impurity potential on the electronic structure.

Key observables. Topological Impurity Bands (TIBs), Bott index, Inverse Participation Ratio (IPR), and the vorticity ($\ell$) of impurity bound states.

Important parameters / regimes. Defect density (e.g., 3%), critical impurity strength ($h_c$), and the nature of the disorder potential.

Assumptions / limitations. The standard TAI theory's reliance on perturbative averaging is insufficient for localized defects; the analysis assumes the impurity term breaks certain symmetries while preserving others.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates TIB formation via impurity bound states with differing vorticity; Figure 2 shows numerical evidence of TIB transitions matching analytical predictions.

Paper structure. The paper moves from establishing the limitations of standard disorder theory to deriving the TIB mechanism using bound states, and finally applying this theory to specific lattice models (QWZ, Haldane) to predict observable topological transitions.

Abstract

The effects of disorder on topological phases of matter are typically either preservation of topology or its destruction. For example, the quantum Hall effect's quantization and chiral edge mode remain robust until a strong-disorder percolation transition exits the topological phase. Disorder can also enlarge a preexisting topological phase, yielding a topological Anderson insulator (TAI). However, the reliance of TAI and other theoretical treatments on perturbatively averaging out long-wavelength randomness suggests that additional effects might appear when the disorder involves localized defects. Here we show that a finite density of randomly distributed defects can generate various new topological phase transitions. This route to disorder-induced topology can be viewed as the formation of topological impurity bands (TIB) and is applicable even if the initial clean model is a trivial band insulator. The theory relies on an orbital-dependent vorticity of impurity bound states, which we derive analytically near a generic Dirac cone phase transition as well as using the Chern insulator lattice models of Qi-Wu-Zhang and Haldane. Their numerically computed phase diagrams at 3% defect density show robust topological transitions in distinct TIB and TAI regimes, suggesting a local route to topological phases.

A theoretical study of subcarrier-wave quantum key distribution system integration with an optical transport network utilizing dense wavelength division multiplexing

Authors: Fedor Kiselev, Natalia Veselkova, Roman Goncharov, Vladimir Egorov

Summary. This paper theoretically analyzes how noise from classical DWDM channels degrades Subcarrier-Wave Quantum Key Distribution (SCW QKD) systems. By calculating QBER and key rates, the authors demonstrate that placing the quantum channel at 1310 nm (O-band) significantly outperforms the C-band, primarily due to reduced nonlinear noise, which is crucial for practical network deployment.

Why it may be interesting. This work is highly relevant to quantum optics and quantum networks, as it provides quantitative guidelines for integrating quantum communication protocols into existing, high-capacity classical infrastructure.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the performance limitations of Subcarrier-Wave Quantum Key Distribution (SCW QKD) when integrated into a classical optical transport network utilizing Dense Wavelength Division Multiplexing (DWDM).

Main result. Quantum channel allocation at the 1310 nm wavelength (O-band) is superior to the C-band because it mitigates nonlinear noise effects, leading to better achievable distances despite higher fiber attenuation.

Method. The study involves theoretical calculations of the Quantum Bit Error Rate (QBER) and the secure key generation rate by modeling various noise sources.

Model / system. The system models SCW QKD operating over an optical fiber shared with multiple classical DWDM channels. Noise sources analyzed include Spontaneous Raman Scattering (SpRS), Four-Wave Mixing (FWM), and Linear Channel Crosstalk (LCXT).

Key observables. Quantum Bit Error Rate (QBER), secure key generation rate, and various noise power levels ($P_{ram}, P_{ijk}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Fiber length (L), attenuation ($\xi$), number of classical channels ($N_{ch}$), and the quantum channel allocation wavelength (1310 nm vs. C-band).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the use of output power for noise calculations and models the impact of noise sources on the overall detector click probability.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the dependence of noise sources (SpRS, FWM) and the secure key generation rate on fiber length and the number of classical channels for both O-band and C-band configurations.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the impact of classical DWDM noise on SCW QKD, deriving expressions for noise powers, calculating QBER and key rates, and comparing performance metrics across different wavelength allocations (O-band vs. C-band).

Abstract

In this paper we study the performance of subcarrier-wave quantum key distribution (SCW QKD) in the presence of classical channels of optical transport network utilizing dense wavelength division multiplexing (DWDM). We consider the impact of spontaneous Raman scattering noise as well as the four-wave mixing and channel isolation efficiency. We calculate quantum bit error rate as well as the secure key generation rate of SCW-QKD protocol for different parameters of DWDM system and quantum channel allocations. Our calculations show, that quantum channel allocation at the wavelength of 1310 nm is preferable to allocation at C-band due to lower nonlinear noise, despite fiber's larger attenuation at this wavelength.
