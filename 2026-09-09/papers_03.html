First fault-tolerant quantum memory demonstration for a generalized superfast encoding

Authors: James Brown, Kenny Heitritter
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.08957v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 3/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work introduces a novel, constant-weight Generalized Superfast Encoding (GSE) designed for fault-tolerant quantum memory of fermionic systems. By simulating performance under circuit noise, the authors report the first observation of threshold-like scaling for such a fermion-mapping, achieving a threshold around $4 imes 10^{-3}$. This advances the practical feasibility of using quantum error correction for simulating fermionic quantum matter.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a concrete, simulated demonstration of fault tolerance for a fermion-mapping, which is crucial for quantum simulation efforts involving fermionic Hamiltonians.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of demonstrating fault-tolerant quantum memory specifically for a fermion-to-qubit mapping.

Main result. They achieved a simulation showing a threshold-like scaling for fault tolerance at a physical error rate of approximately $4 imes 10^{-3}$ for two specific code instances.

Method. The authors constructed an even-distance, constant stabilizer-weight Generalized Superfast Encoding (GSE) and simulated quantum memory operations under circuit-level depolarizing noise.

Model / system. The system involves encoding fermionic modes into qubits using the GSE, which utilizes stabilizer generators with weights of 4 or 6. The simulation tests two specific stabilizer codes: $[[48, 8, 6]]$ and $[[64, 8, 8]]$.

Key observables. Logical error rate ($ar{\epsilon}_L(p)$) as a function of the physical depolarizing error rate ($p$).

Important parameters / regimes. The observed threshold is $\approx 4 imes 10^{-3}$. The tested codes have distances $d=6$ and $d=8$, and logical qubits $k=8$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on simulating circuit-level depolarizing noise and assumes the applicability of the developed GSE structure for fault tolerance.

Figures summary. Figure 1 shows the logical error rate ($ar{\epsilon}_L(p)$) for both tested codes versus $p$, illustrating the approach to a pseudo-threshold near $p \approx 4 imes 10^{-3}$.

Paper structure. The paper details the construction of the constant-weight GSE stabilizers, analyzes the operator weights using 'a' and 'b' families of local Majorana operators, and simulates the memory performance using AlphaSyndrome extraction and Relay-BP decoding.

Abstract

The Generalized Superfast Encoding (GSE) is a fermion-to-qubit mapping that has error-correcting/detecting properties. To this point, all demonstrations have been relegated to error-detection only, as no fault-tolerance under circuit-level noise has been observed. Here, we introduce an even-distance $d$ constant stabilizer-weight GSE where each of $N$ modes is assigned a $d$-qubit block arranged on a ring. The resulting stabilizer generators have constant weight 4 or 6 for any even distance $d$. Furthermore, the full stabilizer set of this construction can always be partitioned into four qubit-wise commuting groups, which enables compact syndrome-extraction scheduling. We simulate quantum memory experiments under circuit-level depolarizing noise for two instances of this code, $[[48,8,6]]$ and $[[64,8,8]]$ and the threshold is observed to be $\approx 4\times10^{-3}$. This is, to our knowledge, the first fault-tolerant quantum-memory characterization of a fermion-mapping with threshold-like scaling.

High-resolution angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy with tunable magnetic field

Summary. This work presents a methodology to overcome severe spectral distortions in magneto-ARPES by employing detailed electron trajectory simulations. By accurately modeling how magnetic fields distort photoemitted electron paths, the authors successfully reconstruct the true electronic structure of $ ext{Bi}_2 ext{Se}_3$. This establishes critical technical guidelines for performing high-resolution ARPES measurements in strong magnetic fields.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous development of post-processing techniques to correct for complex, field-dependent electron optics effects is highly valuable for any experiment probing quantum states in external fields, bridging experimental measurement limitations with theoretical models.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge addressed is mitigating spectral distortions and broadening in magneto-ARPES measurements caused by significant electron trajectory aberrations when strong magnetic fields are applied.

Main result. The authors demonstrated that the electronic structure can be accurately reconstructed in post-processing, maintaining high momentum resolution even in the presence of strong trajectory aberrations.

Method. The technique involves using detailed electron trajectory simulations (based on the equations of motion) to correct the measured photoemission angles and energies.

Model / system. The study uses $ ext{Bi}_2 ext{Se}_3$, a topological insulator, measured using a high-resolution, laser-based ARPES system equipped with a tunable dipole magnetic field.

Key observables. Angle contraction ($ heta_f$), angle rotation ($\phi_f$), momentum distribution curves (MDCs), and the field-induced gap opening at the Dirac point.

Important parameters / regimes. The critical technical requirements identified are 2D momentum mapping, a micron beam spot size, and precise numerical field simulations. Scaling laws relating field dependence are also derived.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the electronic structure can be recovered via post-processing corrections, and residual field effects inside the analyzer lens column are neglected.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the magneto-ARPES setup, electron trajectory simulations showing contraction and rotation, and comparative plots showing the successful reconstruction of the electronic structure by applying trajectory corrections.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the technical limitations of magneto-ARPES due to trajectory aberrations, details the simulation methodology using electron equations of motion, applies this correction procedure to $ ext{Bi}_2 ext{Se}_3$ data, and finally discusses the physical implications for probing spin and orbital coupling.

Abstract

The control and perturbation of quantum phenomena with magnetic fields is an indispensable tool in materials research. Recently, in-situ field tuning was implemented into angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (magneto-ARPES), which provides direct momentum and energy-resolved information of the field-dependent electronic structure. However, aberrations of the electron trajectories were shown to be substantial, leading to significant spectral distortions and broadening even in small fields. Here we show that the electronic structure can be recovered from magneto-ARPES spectra with high accuracy while maintaining high momentum resolution even when strong trajectory aberrations are present. We studied Bi$_2$Se$_3$ in a dipole field of a coil using a laser-based ARPES system. The electronic structure was reconstructed in post-processing using detailed electron trajectory simulations. We identify two-dimensional (2D) momentum mapping, a micron beam spot size, and precise numerical field simulations as critical technical requirements for high-resolution magneto-ARPES. We show how circular dichroism can provide additional information about the coupling of the magnetic field to the spin. The experimental achievements and the scaling laws from our simulations provide a road map towards magneto-ARPES in larger fields.

Summary. This work establishes a fundamental impossibility result for quantum computation, proving that no one-way one-round quantum algorithm can reliably 4-color directed cycles. The authors achieve this by developing a deep connection between quantum graph coloring and noncommutative extremal combinatorics, using matrix energy bounds to derive the necessary lower bound.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a strong, non-trivial lower bound for quantum computation models, showing limitations beyond classical or simpler quantum models, which is fundamental for understanding quantum algorithm complexity.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper proves a quantum lower bound demonstrating that a one-way one-round quantum LOCAL algorithm cannot 4-color directed cycles with high probability.

Main result. The impossibility of such a quantum coloring is established by connecting the problem to a lower bound on the $\Lambda$-energy of matrix-space decompositions.

Method. The proof employs noncommutative extremal combinatorics, mapping the quantum coloring problem to bounding the $\Lambda$-energy of a set of POVM elements.

Model / system. The model is the quantum LOCAL algorithm applied to graph coloring, specifically directed cycles. The analysis uses quantum information tools like POVMs and matrix energy bounds.

Key observables. Collision Probability ($ ext{Pr}[c_i = c_{i+1}]$) and $\Lambda$-energy ($E_\Lambda(W)$).

Important parameters / regimes. The number of colors ($q=4$), the locality (one-way, one-round), and the size of the local random-seed space ($N$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the quantum coloring problem can be mapped to the energy bound, and the lower bound is shown to be stronger than those from bounded-dependence models.

Figures summary. Not specified.

Paper structure. The paper develops the argument by first establishing classical lower bounds, then generalizing this to the quantum setting using matrix energy bounds, culminating in the proof that the energy lower bound implies the coloring impossibility.

Abstract

We show that one-way one-round quantum LOCAL algorithms cannot $4$-color directed cycles with high probability, even with unbounded local computation and quantum message length. This is the first lower bound in the high-probability quantum LOCAL setting that goes beyond the non-signaling and bounded-dependence models, exploiting the structure of distributed quantum algorithms. Our proof connects distributed quantum computing with noncommutative extremal combinatorics by identifying local collision probabilities with the weighted multiplicative energy of matrix-space decompositions. We obtain our lower bound by proving a dimension-independent weighted stability theorem for a directed noncommutative analogue of Mantel's theorem.

Kane-Lubensky phonons in Maxwell lattice frustrated Mott insulators

Authors: Hong-Hao Song, Gang v. Chen
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.08262v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 3/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work demonstrates the emergence of topologically protected, gapless Weyl-line phonons in frustrated Mott insulators modeled on the Maxwell lattice. The mechanism involves spin-lattice coupling distorting the lattice into a topological state. The key finding is the unique $T^2$ specific heat signature associated with these topological phonons, suggesting new avenues for phonon-mediated quantum phenomena.

Why it may be interesting. The finding of topologically protected, non-Goldstone zero-frequency modes provides a novel platform for studying emergent quantum phenomena mediated by lattice vibrations, which can couple strongly to electronic degrees of freedom.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the existence and topological nature of zero-energy gapless Weyl-line phonons in frustrated Mott insulators modeled on the Maxwell lattice.

Main result. The system hosts topologically protected, bulk zero-frequency Weyl-line phonons, which yield a characteristic low-temperature specific heat of $C_{ ext{ph}} \sim T^2$, distinguishing them from conventional Goldstone modes.

Method. The analysis employs magnetopological mechanics and lattice dynamics, calculating the phonon spectrum using the dynamical matrix and characterizing topology via winding numbers.

Model / system. The system is a pyrochlore antiferromagnet modeled as a frustrated Mott insulator, whose lattice is distorted by spin-lattice coupling into a topological Maxwell lattice.

Key observables. Zero-frequency Weyl-line phonons, winding numbers ($n_W$), and the low-temperature specific heat ($C_{ ext{ph}} \sim T^2$).

Important parameters / regimes. Spin-lattice coupling strength, magnetic field strength, and the lattice structure (Maxwell lattice).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the quadratic approximation for lattice dynamics and assumes the spin interaction is primarily Heisenberg-like.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the spin configuration inducing the topological lattice, winding number diagrams showing Weyl line projections, and real-space cuts demonstrating boundary zero-mode localization.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by establishing the physical model (spin-lattice coupling in pyrochlores), deriving the effective topological lattice, calculating the phonon spectrum using topological invariants (winding numbers), and finally predicting the unique low-temperature thermal signature.

Abstract

We show that zero-energy gapless Weyl-line phonons of the Kane-Lubensky's type can arise in the three-dimensional Maxwell lattice frustrated Mott insulators through the magnetopological mechanics. In a pyrochlore antiferromagnet with the spin-lattice coupling, a magnetic field selects the spin state whose lattice distortion generates a $P4_3 32$ topological lattice. More crucially, the spin-lattice coupling and the spin configuration cause the bending of the neighbouring bonds, and converts the system into the topological Maxwell lattice. Remarkably, the resulting system is found to host the bulk zero-frequency Weyl-line phonons protected topologically, and these gapless phonons are not Goldstone modes. Unlike the conventional ${C_{\rm ph}\sim T^3}$ for the Goldstone phonons, these one-dimensional zero-mode manifolds yield a characteristic low-temperature phonon specific heat ${C_{\rm ph}\sim T^2}$. Our results could find applications in the Cr-based spinel systems, and moreover, we establish a low-energy platform where an extensive number of topological zero-frequency phonons can strongly couple to other degrees of freedom, opening a route to exotic phonon-mediated phenomena.

On the Power of Adaptivity in Testing Quantum States in Fidelity

Authors: Jan Seyfried, Sayantan Sen, Marco Tomamichel
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.08733v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 3/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work analyzes the sample complexity for testing quantum properties like state equality and independence using limited measurements. It compares the efficiency of adaptive versus non-adaptive protocols for both trace distance and fidelity metrics. The findings show that fidelity testing reveals structural differences, particularly for unknown states, suggesting that adaptivity can provide significant advantages over non-adaptive methods.

Why it may be interesting. The results provide rigorous, quantitative bounds on the resources needed to verify quantum properties, which is fundamental for quantum characterization protocols in experimental quantum information science.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper studies the sample complexity required to certify, equivalently test, or test for independence of unknown quantum states using limited measurements.

Main result. For fidelity testing, adaptive algorithms provide better sample complexity bounds for equivalence and independence testing compared to non-adaptive methods, demonstrating a separation from state certification.

Method. The authors develop a framework using partial learning and reducing the testing problems to an $\ell_2$-distance testing subroutine, comparing results across trace distance and fidelity metrics.

Model / system. The focus is on quantum states $ ho$ and $\sigma$ in $d$-dimensional Hilbert spaces, including bipartite ($ ho_{AC}$) and tripartite ($ ho_{ABC}$) systems, analyzed in the single-copy measurement setting.

Key observables. Quantum states ($ ho, \sigma$), fidelity ($F$), trace distance ($|\cdot - \cdot|_{ ext{tr}}$), and quantum mutual information $I(A:C) ho$.

Important parameters / regimes. Decision gap $\varepsilon$, state rank $r$, and system dimensions ($d, d_A, d_C$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is performed in the single-copy measurement setting, and results are compared between adaptive and non-adaptive measurement protocols.

Figures summary. The notes mention tables comparing sample complexities for identity and equivalence testing across different distance measures (Trace distance vs. Fidelity) for both classical and quantum settings.

Paper structure. The paper systematically compares sample complexities for quantum state certification, equivalence testing, and independence testing under both trace distance and fidelity metrics, culminating in specific bounds for Markov chain testing.

Abstract

We study the problems of quantum state certification, equivalence testing and independence testing. In certification, given samples of an unknown quantum state $ρ$ and the description of a state $σ$, the goal is to test whether $ρ=σ$, or whether $ρ$ and $σ$ are far in a given distance measure. In equivalence testing, $σ$ is also unknown and only accessible via samples. Independence testing decides whether $ρ_{AC}=ρ_A\otimesρ_C$, or is far from being a product. The sample complexities of these problems are now well-understood for a decision gap $\varepsilon$ in trace distance: in the single-copy measurement setting with $d$-dimensional states, all three tasks can be solved using the same non-adaptive approach, which uses $Θ(d^{3/2}/\varepsilon^2)$ samples and is optimal in general, even without adaptivity. In this work, we consider decision gaps expressed in fidelity and study possible separations between these problems and how adaptivity can help. We prove that certification with respect to fidelity for a state $σ$ of rank $r$ does not benefit from adaptivity and requires $\widetildeΘ(r^{3/2}/\varepsilon)$ samples. For equivalence testing and independence testing, we provide adaptive algorithms using $\widetilde{O}(\min\{d^{3/2}/\varepsilon^2,d^{9/4}/\varepsilon\})$ and $\widetilde{O}(\min\{(d_Ad_C)^{3/2}/\varepsilon^2,d_A^{9/4}d_C^{3/4}/\varepsilon\})$ samples, for $d_A\geq d_C$, respectively. Our main technique is a framework that uses partial learning and a reduction to testing in $\ell_2$-distance, adapted from the distribution testing literature. We show that adaptivity matters for equivalence testing in fidelity by proving that $\widetildeΩ(1/\varepsilon^2)$ samples are necessary in the non-adaptive case even for qubits, showing a separation from certification.

Sklar's Theorem and Quantum State Reconstruction from All-Context Dependence

Authors: Yi-Yu Lin

Summary. This theoretical work adapts Sklar's theorem from classical statistics to quantum mechanics to determine if a quantum state can be uniquely reconstructed from its dependence structure measured across all possible local measurement contexts. The authors prove that for two qubits, this all-context information is sufficient to identify the state up to a simple ambiguity, and they show how global quantum discord emerges from this framework.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a powerful, information-theoretic tool to characterize quantum correlations by demanding consistency across all possible local measurements, linking fundamental concepts like quantum discord to classical dependence structures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how much quantum state information ($ ho$) can be recovered by analyzing the dependence structure derived from all possible local quantum measurements, analogous to Sklar's theorem in classical statistics.

Main result. For two-qubit states, the full set of context-dependent dependence structures uniquely determines the state up to a discrete double-spin-flip ambiguity. Furthermore, global quantum discord can be reproduced by optimizing over these local contexts.

Method. It adapts classical copula theory to quantum measurements, defining an invariant quantity (like the odds ratio $Q$) that characterizes the dependence structure across different measurement bases.

Model / system. The focus is on two-qubit quantum states ($ ho$), analyzed using local projective measurements defined by vectors on the Bloch sphere ($x, y \in S^2$). The theory is conceptually extended to multiqubit and continuous-variable systems.

Key observables. Dependence nucleus, invariant $Q$, Connected Correlation ($\Gamma_ ho$), Quantum Discord ($D_{GQ}$), and the Copula structure.

Important parameters / regimes. Two-qubit states, local measurement bases ($x, y$), and the double-spin-flip ambiguity ($e ho$).

Assumptions / limitations. The reconstruction theorem is rigorously established for two-qubit systems; generalizations to higher dimensions or continuous variables face open mathematical challenges.

Paper structure. The paper builds from the classical analogy of Sklar's theorem (copula decomposition) to the quantum setting. It first establishes the invariant measures for two qubits, derives the reconstruction theorem based on all-context compatibility, and finally discusses conceptual extensions to higher-dimensional and continuous systems.

Abstract

Sklar's theorem separates the marginals of a joint distribution from its dependence structure. We apply this viewpoint to Born statistics generated by local quantum measurements. For non-product two-qubit states, the dependence nuclei over all local binary projective measurement contexts determine the state up to at most a discrete double-spin-flip ambiguity. The corresponding scalar optimization, combined with the quantum total correlation, reproduces global quantum discord.

Summary. This paper extends nonequilibrium statistical thermodynamics by incorporating memory effects via boundary functionals into Zubarev's statistical operator method. It achieves this by mapping non-Markovian history dependence onto the geometry of an extended phase space using information potentials. This provides a rigorous tool for analyzing rare events and memory in complex stochastic systems.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, non-calculus-based method to handle memory effects in open systems, which is crucial for modeling complex dynamics in nanoscale or biological environments where history matters.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To develop a generalized framework for nonequilibrium stochastic thermodynamics that rigorously incorporates non-Markovian memory effects arising from boundary functionals (like first-passage times or absolute extrema) into the statistical description.

Main result. The authors show that incorporating history-dependent functionals allows the non-Markovian memory effects to be encoded into the geometry of an extended phase space via an information potential, thus generalizing Zubarev's formalism.

Method. The method generalizes Zubarev's nonequilibrium statistical operator approach by employing the maximum information entropy method, which is shown to be equivalent to the Donsker-Varadhan large deviation formalism and the Escher shift.

Model / system. The theory is applied to open stochastic systems, modeled by Langevin equations, which are analyzed by extending the state space to incorporate history-dependent functionals (e.g., residence time, FPT, absolute extremum).

Paper structure. Abstract

This paper develops a generalization of Zubarev's nonequilibrium statistical operator method for the case of simultaneous inclusion of additive and nonlocal boundary functionals of trajectories. Using a unified thermodynamic approach, a three-parameter model of nonequilibrium systems is constructed, including the first-passage time, the dwell time above a given level, and the absolute extremum of the process. A correspondence is demonstrated between the maximum information entropy method for trajectories and the Donsker-Varadan large deviation formalism. Using the Doob`s h-transform, it is demonstrated that fixing extremal functionals of history induces efficient non-Markovian transport in the system with dynamic adaptation to historical records. Criteria for the applicability of the developed apparatus in the "large time" domain and the possibilities of its use for optimizing stochastic particle separation in periodic potentials are discussed.

Summary. This paper develops a comprehensive theoretical framework to calculate the noise (Fano factor) in RNA copy number resulting from stochastic gene expression. It models the system as doubly stochastic, linking the noise directly to the statistics of the fluctuating transcription rate. The work provides general formulas and verifies them across various complex rate dynamics models, including those with different boundary conditions.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical framework for calculating noise in open quantum systems (like gene expression) by coupling a fluctuating rate process to a decay process is highly relevant to modeling quantum noise sources in open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to understand the noise (fluctuations) in RNA copy number resulting from stochastic transcription and degradation processes in gene expression.

Main result. It derives the Fano factor, which quantifies noise, showing it is composed of an intrinsic Poisson contribution and a second term dependent on the variance and autocorrelation of the fluctuating transcription rate.

Method. The authors develop a doubly stochastic framework, deriving analytical results for the Fano factor using general stochastic calculus and applying it to various rate dynamics models.

Model / system. The system models gene expression where RNA production is driven by a time-varying, random transcription rate $\lambda(t)$ and subject to degradation. The analysis uses continuous-time Markov chains and diffusion approximations.

Key observables. Fano factor ($F[n] = V[n]/E[n]$), RNA copy number $n(t)$, Mean copy number $E[n]$, and the autocorrelation function $ ho(h)$ of $\lambda(t)$.

Important parameters / regimes. Degradation rate ($\mu$), Variance of $\lambda$ ($V[\lambda]$), Mean of $\lambda$ ($E[\lambda]$), and the timescale ratio between rate fluctuation and degradation.

Assumptions / limitations. The general derivation assumes independent, finite-mean mRNA lifetimes. Simplified results often rely on the assumption of stationarity or the exponential lifetime approximation.

Figures summary. The notes describe results derived for different boundary conditions (Reflecting, Periodic, First-Passage Resetting) for the rate dynamics, showing how the noise factor $F[n]$ is calculated for each case.

Paper structure. The paper systematically develops the noise factor $F[n]$ by first establishing general relations, then applying them to specific rate dynamics models (M/M/1, Drift-Diffusion with various boundaries), and finally presenting data-driven estimation techniques.

Abstract

Stochastic models of gene expression typically begin with a microscopic model of transcription and propagate its statistics to the RNA distribution. Here we develop a doubly stochastic framework in which RNA production is Poisson conditional on a time-varying transcription rate $λ(t)$. For RNA lifetimes drawn independently from an arbitrary finite-mean distribution, we derive the RNA Fano factor in terms of the lifetime survival function and the autocovariance of $λ(t)$. For a Poisson degradation process with rate $μ$, the survival kernel provides an exponential temporal filter, and the result depends only on the mean, variance, and normalized autocorrelation of $λ(t)$. These quantities may be calculated from an explicit rate model or, under ergodicity and adequate sampling, estimated from a sufficiently long rate trajectory. We validate the data-driven estimator using simulated transcription-rate trajectories, without supplying the known autocorrelation to the estimator. We also obtain exact analytical results for transcription-rate dynamics modeled by a discrete M/M/1 process and by drift-diffusion with reflecting, periodic, or first-passage-reset boundaries, and verify each result by direct simulation of the coupled transcription-rate and RNA copy-number processes.

Summary. This theoretical work analyzes how the causal structure of multi-step quantum operations affects the efficiency of channel transformation under coherence constraints. Using SDPs, the authors prove that for some channels, the causal order matters significantly, while for others, the structure collapses, indicating that parallelization can negate any potential causal advantage.

Why it may be interesting. The findings provide deep structural insights into the limitations of quantum operations, showing that the advantage of complex causal structures can be entirely nullified by the underlying resource properties of the channels involved.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates whether increasing causal freedom (from fixed-order sequential to general higher-order processes) improves quantum channel transformation when the process is constrained by a resource theory.

Main result. A strict causal hierarchy is proven for amplitude-damping channels, but this hierarchy collapses for mixed-Pauli channels, suggesting free program-state parallelization is a structural obstruction to causal enhancement.

Method. The analysis employs a unified semidefinite-programming (SDP) framework to minimize the transformation error under various causal constraints (Parallel, Sequential, General) and coherence resource constraints (MISC, DISC).

Model / system. The study models quantum channel transformations involving two resource channels and a target channel, focusing on two-qubit amplitude-damping channels and mixed-Pauli channels.

Key observables. The optimal error in channel transformation, $D_{ ext{Class}}^F(\dots)$, and the coherence coefficients ($x_c, \mu$).

Important parameters / regimes. The damping strength ($\epsilon$) of the amplitude-damping channels, and the specific coherence constraints (MISC or DISC).

Assumptions / limitations. The collapse condition for causal enhancement is shown to be a sufficient, not necessary, characterization of structural obstructions.

Figures summary. The notes do not provide a summary of figures, but mention the use of various equations (e.g., (201) through (239)) containing core mathematical derivations.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by defining the SDP framework, establishing the causal classes, deriving the necessary constraints for different orders, and finally applying these tools to prove the hierarchy for amplitude-damping channels and the collapse condition for mixed-Pauli channels.

Abstract

Higher-order quantum transformations allow multiple channel uses to be combined through different causal architectures, from parallel and fixed-order sequential networks to general higher-order processes. Whether this causal freedom improves channel transformation when the higher-order operation is also constrained by a resource theory remains largely unexplored. We study this question in the dynamical resource theory of coherence using a unified semidefinite-programming framework. For two qubit amplitude-damping channels and the identity target, we prove a strict causal hierarchy at every nontrivial damping strength under both maximally incoherent superchannels (MISC) and dephasing-covariant incoherent superchannels (DISC). In contrast, mixed-Pauli channels admit a common teleportation simulation that transfers the channel dependence to Bell-diagonal program states prepared in parallel. The remaining processing can then be absorbed into a single quantum operational, so parallel, fixed-order sequential, and general higher-order strategies achieve the same optimal error for any target. These results identify free program-state parallelisation as a structural obstruction to causal enhancement.

Abstract

Quantum-state texture quantifies structural irregularities of a state in a selected basis and has emerged as a valuable resource for gate characterization in universal circuits. Consequently, a class of contractive distance based texture measures and a framework for constructing convex-roof extended texture measure have been proposed. Here, we extend this theory in several directions. We examine the convertibility of a pure state to any state under texture-free operations, and fully solve the deterministic transformation problem between any two states in the qubit case. We then propose two classes of texture measures: convex-function-based measures and texture cost defined via minimal cost of texture contained in pure states. Moreover, we show that every texture monotone gives rise to a non-negative function which admits the same properties as the function used in the convex-roof extension---thus providing a converse to that construction. Our work thereby offers a relatively comprehensive resource-theoretic formulation of quantum-state texture.
