Accelerating Fourier--Motzkin elimination: redundancy removal and the choice of variable elimination order

Authors: Shashaank Khanna
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.07960v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops advanced computational techniques to accelerate Fourier-Motzkin elimination, a method for projecting polyhedra described by linear inequalities. By proposing a smart variable elimination order rule, the authors achieve significant speedups over standard methods, making the analysis of complex entropic constraints from causal structures computationally feasible.

Why it may be interesting. While the core topic is computational geometry/optimization, the application to entropic constraints derived from causal structures has implications for quantum information theory by providing tools to analyze marginalization over latent variables in complex physical models.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the computational bottleneck of Fourier-Motzkin (FM) elimination, which is used to find the inequality description of the projection of a polyhedron onto a subset of its coordinates.

Main result. A novel variable elimination order rule was proposed that significantly reduces the running time (by factors of 6 to 25) compared to fixed-order elimination, especially for large systems arising from entropic constraints.

Method. The authors analyze redundancy removal techniques (LP testing vs. Imbert's test) and propose a look-ahead rule for selecting the variable elimination order to minimize the number of intermediate inequalities.

Model / system. The mathematical framework involves polyhedra defined by linear inequalities, with specific applications to obtaining entropic constraints derived from causal structures via marginalization over latent variables.

Key observables. The number of redundant vs. non-redundant inequalities retained at each step, and the wall-clock running time of the elimination process.

Important parameters / regimes. The number of variables and the number of inequalities in the initial system (e.g., >100 variables, >250 inequalities for causal structures).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the assumption that the projection is full-dimensional for some results, and it notes that the optimal ordering problem is analogous to an NP-complete problem.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the projection of a polyhedron in 3D and the comparison of inequality counts and running times between fixed-order and look-ahead elimination strategies for causal structure instances.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the mathematical background of FM elimination and redundancy testing, then details the flaws in combining these tests, and finally proposes and analyzes the variable elimination order rule using random polytopes and large causal structure examples.

Abstract

Fourier-Motzkin elimination computes an inequality description of the projection of a polyhedron onto a subset of its coordinates by eliminating one variable at a time. It is used in several areas of optimisation and computer science, and it is a standard way of obtaining the entropic constraints of a causal structure, where the marginalisation over the latent variables produces such a projection. Its limitation is the growth of the intermediate systems of inequalities, which can be doubly exponential in the number of eliminated variables even though the projection itself grows only as a single exponential. In practice the computational overload of the method therefore depends on two choices: how the redundant inequalities are removed after each step, and the order in which the variables are eliminated. We consider both. We first show, by an explicit example, that Imbert's redundancy test cannot be interleaved with redundancy removal by linear programming. We show that the two methods, however, can be combined soundly if the derivation records used by Imbert's test are re-initialised after every step at which linear programming is used. We then propose a rule for choosing the elimination order of the variables that gives a significant computational advantage, however, at the cost of increased resource usage. We demonstrate this advantage on some random polytopes, where the rule reduces the running time by factors of between 6 and 25 compared with the same elimination under a fixed order. For entropic descriptions of causal structures, with more than 250 inequalities and more than 100 variables to eliminate, our rule keeps the number of inequalities handled at each step one to two orders of magnitude lower than a fixed order.

Classical Active-Space Hybrid Quantum Subspace Expansion (CASH-QSE): Quantum Corrections without Remeasuring the Classically Calculable Energy

Authors: Artur F. Izmaylov
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.08170v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces CASH-QSE, a novel quantum algorithm for electronic structure calculations that separates the energy calculation into a classically solvable active-space component and a quantum-corrected component. By enforcing orthogonality and solving the problem via a generalized eigenvalue problem, it achieves chemical accuracy with significantly reduced quantum circuit depth and sampling requirements compared to existing VQE methods.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a sophisticated, resource-aware quantum algorithm for quantum chemistry, directly addressing the resource scaling and measurement overhead that limits the application of VQE to strongly correlated systems, which is a major focus in AMO physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the bottleneck in Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) methods by developing a way to calculate quantum corrections to electronic energies without re-measuring the energy contribution already determined by a classical reference state.

Main result. The proposed CASH-QSE method achieves chemical accuracy while keeping the largest required quantum circuits manageable (a few hundred CNOT gates) and significantly reducing the required sampling cost compared to standard VQE approaches.

Method. CASH-QSE expands the wave function as a combination of a classically calculated Active-Space (CAS) reference state and orthogonal quantum states constructed in the remaining correlation subspace. The energy is then solved via a generalized, non-overlapping Hermitian eigenvalue problem.

Model / system. The method is applied to electronic structure calculations for small molecules, specifically $ ext{H}_2 ext{O}$ and $ ext{N}_2$, across bond-stretching coordinates. The formalism relies on partitioning the Hilbert space into a classically tractable CAS subspace and orthogonal quantum subspaces.

Key observables. Energy ($E$), CAS coefficients, circuit depth (measured in CNOT gates), and the ratio of required quantum sampling shots (compared to ADAPT-VQE).

Important parameters / regimes. Chemical accuracy, bond-stretching distances (e.g., 0.96 Å to 3.0 Å for $ ext{H}_2 ext{O}$), and the size of the required quantum circuit (e.g., 70-CNOT network for $ ext{H}_2 ext{O}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes the remaining correlation can be exactly decomposed into a set of mutually orthogonal, structurally constrained quantum subspaces, allowing the global energy minimization to be treated as a linear Ritz problem.

Figures summary. Table VIII compares the required quantum sampling resources (shots) for $ ext{H}_2 ext{O}/ ext{cc-pVDZ}$ at various bond lengths, showing the significant reduction in shots achieved by CASH-QSE relative to ADAPT-VQE.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the core problem, details the mathematical formalism involving the first-defect decomposition to create an orthogonal basis, describes the iterative optimization procedure involving orbital relaxation and CAS coefficient updates, and concludes with benchmark results on molecular systems.

Abstract

The wave function prepared by a variational quantum eigensolver (VQE) can contain a substantial classically tractable component, yet its energy contribution is sampled on the quantum device. Representing the wave function as a combination of a classical reference and separately prepared quantum states allows the reference energy to be evaluated without quantum sampling. However, near-linear dependence among these components can amplify measurement errors and destabilize the resulting energy estimate. We introduce Classical Active-Space Hybrid Quantum Subspace Expansion (CASH-QSE), which retains a complete-active-space self-consistent field (CASSCF) reference classically and uses occupation structure to construct quantum states exactly orthogonal to the reference and to one another. The energy follows from an ordinary Hermitian eigenvalue problem without overlap measurements, while the same occupation constraints simplify the measured operators. Using full configuration interaction to guide component selection, we test CASH-QSE along H$_2$O and N$_2$ bond-stretching coordinates spanning weakly to strongly correlated regimes. The benchmarks use STO--3G for both molecules and a restricted cc-pVDZ orbital space for H$_2$O. CASH-QSE reaches chemical accuracy while limiting the largest complete measurement circuits to a few hundred all-to-all logical controlled-NOT gates. In favorable cases, it also reduces the idealized final-energy sampling cost by several orders of magnitude relative to VQE with an adaptive derivative-assembled pseudo-Trotter ansatz (ADAPT-VQE).

Closing the Loop: Non-Causal Computation, Partial Traces, & Postselected Entanglement

Authors: Mark Carney
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.08716v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

Summary. This paper mathematically interprets non-causal quantum computation using category theory, linking it to postselected quantum teleportation. It proves that the logical consistency of these circuits is equivalent to specific trace conditions on the underlying process matrices. Ultimately, it shows this consistency manifests physically as a uniform probability distribution ($1/d^2$) in the postselected Bell measurement outcomes.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides deep mathematical tools (category theory) to analyze the fundamental constraints of causality and information flow in quantum computation, which is highly relevant for understanding resource theories and computational limits.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to provide a rigorous categorical interpretation of non-causal quantum computation models, specifically connecting them to the physical process of postselected quantum teleportation.

Main result. It establishes that classical logical consistency in these non-causal circuits is mathematically equivalent to the postselected Bell outcome having a uniform probability of $1/d^2$ for all classical inputs.

Method. The authors employ a categorical framework using traced monoidal categories ($ ext{MatR}^{\ge 0}$) to define and analyze the process's consistency conditions.

Model / system. The model involves non-causal circuits, which are analyzed via a categorical trace operation ($ ext{Tr}_C(P)$) applied to a stochastic map $P$. This is physically mapped onto a process involving postselection on a maximally entangled Bell state.

Key observables. The stochastic nature of the traced process ($ ext{Tr}_C(P) \in ext{Stoch}$), the trace of the induced loop transition matrix ($ ext{tr}(H_a) = 1$), and the probability of the postselected Bell outcome ($1/d^2$).

Important parameters / regimes. The loop dimension $d = |C|$, which dictates the required probability $1/d^2$ for classical consistency.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the process $P$ is stochastic for the trace to be well-defined in the context of the category $ ext{Stoch}$.

Paper structure. The paper develops the categorical framework, proves the equivalence between the categorical trace being stochastic and the loop transition matrix having trace 1 (Theorem 1), and then extends this to show the equivalence between this condition and the specific probability distribution arising from postselected Bell measurements (Theorem 3).

Abstract

This paper gives a categorical interpretation of Baumeler \& Wolf's logically consistent non-causal circuits, connecting them to postselected quantum teleportation. Looped feedback is represented by a trace in the category of non-negative matrices, and it is shown that the traced process is stochastic precisely when the induced loop transition matrix has trace $1$ for every external input, a condition shown to be equivalent to a unique fixed point for the loop for each input to a deterministic circuit. A classical non-causal circuit is represented by a measure-and-prepare quantum channel with an internal register utilising a maximally entangled Bell-state with postselection. The main result is that classical logical consistency is equivalent to the postselected Bell outcome having, for loop dimension $d$, probability exactly $1/d^2$ for each classical input distribution. The subsequent normalised conditional output then agrees exactly with the classical categorical trace. This work identifies a class of quantum Bell-postselection constructions whose conditional evolution maintains linear dependence on classical input distributions.

Distributed Quantum Property Testing with Quantum Carrier Pigeons

Authors: Kenny Chen, Mina Doosti, Ryan Sweke, Chirag Wadhwa
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.08864v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops a general framework for distributed quantum inference, focusing on certifying unknown quantum states under strict communication limitations. It rigorously characterizes the required quantum resources, proving that shared randomness is crucial for achieving optimal complexity in state certification. The results establish fundamental limits for distributed quantum computation protocols.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous characterization of resource trade-offs (e.g., quantum vs. classical communication, public vs. private randomness) in distributed quantum tasks provides fundamental limits for quantum networking and distributed quantum computation protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses distributed quantum inference, specifically characterizing the complexity of quantum state certification—determining if an unknown state $ ho$ equals a target state $\sigma$ or if they are far apart—under communication constraints.

Main result. It establishes the copy complexity for state certification, showing that shared randomness is necessary for optimal performance, and developing near-optimal private-coin algorithms matching the lower bounds.

Method. The work uses information-theoretic techniques to derive both upper bounds (algorithms) and lower bounds (proofs) on the required quantum resources (copy complexity) for the certification task.

Model / system. The model is the $(n_c, n_q, R, E)$-model for distributed quantum inference, where $m$ nodes each hold a copy of $ ho$ and communicate with a central node $N_c$ via limited classical bits ($n_c$) and qubits ($n_q$).

Key observables. Copy complexity, trace distance ($\| ho - \sigma\|_1$), and the success probability of the certification test.

Important parameters / regimes. Dimension $d$, error threshold $\epsilon$, communication limits $n_c$ and $n_q$, and the randomness source $R$ (public vs. private).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis focuses on the case where there is no communication allowed between the distributed nodes themselves, only one-way communication to the central node.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the $(n_c, n_q, R, E)$ model, detailing the information flow from distributed nodes to the central node under different randomness and entanglement assumptions.

Paper structure. The paper first defines the general framework and the specific state certification problem. It then derives complexity bounds for the public-coin and private-coin settings, establishing the necessity of shared randomness, and concludes by presenting near-optimal algorithms.

Abstract

We introduce a framework for distributed quantum inference under communication constraints. In our model, $m$ distributed nodes each receive one copy of an unknown $d$-dimensional quantum state $ρ$, before communicating via a constrained one-way communication channel with a central node, which aims to infer some property of $ρ$. This framework generalizes the classical distributed inference framework introduced by Acharya, Canonne, and Tyagi [COLT 2019], by allowing quantum resources such as quantum communication and shared entanglement. Within this setting, we focus on the fundamental problem of quantum state certification: Given a complete description of some state $σ$, decide whether $ρ=σ$ or $\|ρ-σ\|_1\geq ε$. Additionally, we focus on the case of limited communication between distributed nodes and the central node: we assume each communication channel is limited to only $n_c$ bits and $n_q$ qubits with $n_c + n_q \leq \log d$. When all nodes can make use of a shared source of randomness, we show that the copy complexity of distributed state certification is $Θ(\frac{d^2}{2^{n_q} 2^{n_c/2}ε^2})$. We further demonstrate that shared randomness is necessary to achieve the above complexity, by proving an $Ω(\frac{d^3}{4^{n_q} 2^{n_c} ε^2})$ lower bound in the $\textit{private-coin}$ setting. Moreover, we develop a private-coin algorithm that matches this bound up to a $\sqrt{\log d}$ factor, showing this complexity is near-optimal. Together, our work establishes a general framework for distributed quantum inference with communication constraints and characterizes the complexity of distributed state certification with limited communication.

Divergent Orbital Diamagnetism from Chiral Edge States in Chern Insulators

Authors: Nobuyuki Okuma

Summary. This work analyzes the orbital diamagnetism in Chern insulators using lattice models, finding that the susceptibility diverges linearly with system size when open boundary conditions are applied. This giant diamagnetism is robust against disorder and is attributed to the topological nature of the chiral edge states, distinguishing it sharply from the finite response predicted by bulk theory.

Why it may be interesting. The robust, size-dependent topological response observed here provides a clear, measurable signature (divergent diamagnetism) that distinguishes topological edge physics from bulk properties, which is a key concept in characterizing topological phases.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates whether orbital diamagnetism can distinguish between topologically non-trivial (Chern insulator) and trivial massive Dirac insulators, focusing on the role of chiral edge states.

Main result. Both Chern insulators and massless Dirac systems exhibit giant diamagnetism that scales linearly with system size under open boundary conditions, a feature absent in the bulk calculation or trivial insulators.

Method. The authors use numerical calculations of the normalized orbital susceptibility derived from the total energy dependence on magnetic flux, comparing open and periodic boundary conditions.

Model / system. The system is modeled using a lattice massive Dirac Hamiltonian, specifically the Qi–Wu–Zhang (QWZ) model, applied to Chern insulators and related Dirac systems on a square lattice.

Key observables. Orbital diamagnetic susceptibility ($\chi_{ ext{orb}}$ or $\chi_{ ext{norm}}$), and its scaling with system size ($L$).

Important parameters / regimes. System size ($L$), magnetic flux ($\phi$), mass term ($m$), and disorder strength ($w$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes zero temperature and fixed particle number, and relies on comparing finite-size calculations (OBC) against bulk formulas (PBC).

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the phase diagram of the QWZ model, the system-size dependence of $\chi_{ ext{norm}}$ for different mass regimes, and the robustness of the diamagnetism against disorder.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by establishing the context of divergent diamagnetism in massless Dirac systems, then applying the method to Chern insulators under OBC, contrasting this with PBC results, and finally analyzing the persistence of the divergence under symmetry breaking or changes in filling.

Abstract

Two-dimensional massless Dirac systems, exemplified by graphene, are known to exhibit a divergent orbital diamagnetic susceptibility that scales linearly with system size. Motivated by viewing a chiral edge state as one half of an enlarged analogue of a benzene ring, we study Chern insulators under open boundary conditions and find the same divergent scaling. This giant diamagnetism is robust against disorder, revealing its topological nature. Our results further suggest a profound connection to the divergent diamagnetism of massless Dirac systems.

Summary. This paper computationally studies how the range of molecular interactions influences the critical behavior of a fluid system using an interconverting lattice model. By varying the interaction range, the authors demonstrate that the system's critical points evolve, showing a systematic convergence toward mean-field theory predictions. This work provides a detailed framework for understanding the crossover between short-range and long-range critical phenomena in condensed matter systems.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical statistical mechanics, the study of critical phenomena and phase transitions in lattice models provides foundational insights into how microscopic interactions dictate macroscopic behavior, which underpins many quantum many-body theories.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how varying the range of intermolecular interactions affects the nature of fluid multicriticality.

Main result. The system exhibits a clear convergence of simulation results toward mean-field predictions as the interaction range increases, revealing different critical archetypes depending on the interaction range.

Method. The research employs both mean-field theory analysis and extensive three-dimensional Monte Carlo simulations.

Model / system. The model is an interconverting lattice model (DBC model) designed to exhibit both Ising-like liquid-gas criticality and symmetric fluid tricriticality. The Hamiltonian includes terms for intermolecular interactions and interconversion energy.

Key observables. Phase coexistence, critical exponents, phase diagrams (showing LGCP, $\lambda$-EP, and TCP), and density/composition profiles.

Important parameters / regimes. The primary variable is the interaction range, controlled by the number of interacting neighbors ($Z_n$), which is systematically varied from 6 to 388.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis often focuses on the degenerate case ($ ilde{e} = ilde{s} = 0$), and the mean-field theory is known to be exact only in the infinite range limit.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the physical model and the role of interaction range, details the simulation methodology (MC moves, observables), presents results showing the dependence of critical behavior on $Z_n$, and concludes by comparing findings to theoretical limits.

Abstract

The range of intermolecular interactions plays a central role in determining the nature of phase behavior and critical phenomena. It is well established through studies of the Ising model that as interaction range increases, Monte Carlo simulations progressively approach meanfield predictions as the effects of critical fluctuations are suppressed. In this work, we investigate how varying interaction range influences fluid multicriticality using an interconverting lattice model that exhibits both Ising-like liquid-gas criticality and symmetric fluid tricriticality (similar to that in the superfluid $^4$He-$^3$He mixture). This minimal model serves as a representative system for exploring the evolution of competing critical points within a generic framework. We analyze the model using both meanfield theory and three-dimensional Monte Carlo simulations while systematically varying the number of interacting neighbors, $Z_n$, from 6 to 388. We find that the system with nearest-neighbor interactions ($Z_n=6$) reveals only two types of multicritical behavior, while for larger interaction ranges, four distinct archetypes emerge. We demonstrate the convergence of the simulation results to those of the meanfield theory as the number of interacting neighbors tends to infinity, and we discuss the results within the framework of crossover critical phenomena.

Nuclear slowing-down factors in alkali-metal vapors

Authors: Vasiliki Koutrouli, Georgios Vasilakis, Kostas Mouloudakis
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.08261v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical work refines the understanding of spin dynamics in alkali-metal vapors by deriving separate slowing-down factors for longitudinal and transverse spin components in the SERF regime. The authors show that standard single-factor models overestimate relaxation rates at high polarization. These findings are vital for improving the accuracy of atomic magnetometers used in fundamental physics measurements.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a rigorous, quantitative refinement of the effective Bloch description for alkali-metal spin dynamics, which is crucial for accurately modeling high-precision atomic magnetometers and comagnetometers.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the discrepancy in describing alkali-metal spin dynamics in the SERF regime by analyzing the nuclear slowing-down factors for longitudinal versus transverse spin components.

Main result. Longitudinal and transverse spin dynamics are governed by distinct slowing-down factors at finite polarization, invalidating the single-factor description used in standard Bloch equations.

Method. The analysis employs perturbation theory on the linearized density-matrix dynamics, treating the spin-exchange interaction as the unperturbed system.

Model / system. The system involves alkali-metal vapors undergoing spin-exchange optical pumping (SEOP) in the SERF regime, modeled using the density-matrix formalism and effective Bloch equations.

Key observables. Longitudinal and transverse spin relaxation rates (1/T1, 1/T2), and the polarization-dependent slowing-down factors ($q_L(p)$ and $q_T(p)$).

Important parameters / regimes. Electron spin polarization ($p$), magnetic field strength ($B$), and the ratio of various relaxation rates (e.g., $\gamma_e B_z/R_{ ext{se}}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes an axially symmetric configuration and uses perturbation theory valid to specific orders in small rate ratios.

Figures summary. The notes reference tables providing explicit, closed-form polynomial expressions for the second-order coefficients ($ ext{S}_{\alphaeta}$) governing the field-dependent corrections to the transverse SERF eigenvalue.

Paper structure. The paper builds from the general Bloch description to a detailed density-matrix analysis, deriving distinct factors for longitudinal and transverse dynamics, and culminates in calculating the second-order field corrections to the transverse SERF eigenvalue.

Abstract

Nuclear slowing-down factors account for the sharing of angular momentum between the electron and the nucleus in the effective Bloch description of alkali-metal spin dynamics in the spin-exchange-relaxation-free (SERF) regime. For collinear optical pumping and magnetic field, we find that longitudinal and transverse spin dynamics are characterized by different polarization-dependent slowing-down factors. Transverse dynamics are governed by the conventional factor $q(p)$, with $p$ the electron spin polarization, whereas longitudinal relaxation is governed by $q(p)+p\,dq(p)/dp$. The two factors coincide at zero polarization but differ substantially at high polarization, where the single-factor description overestimates the underlying pumping and relaxation rates inferred from longitudinal transients by factors approaching two to four, depending on the nuclear spin. We further derive closed-form expressions, valid at arbitrary polarization, for the residual spin-exchange relaxation of the transverse spin components at finite magnetic field along the pumping axis. This contribution is quadratic in the magnetic field and can become comparable to the zero-field linewidth at fields well within the SERF regime. The results are obtained by perturbation theory on the density-matrix dynamics linearized around the stationary state and are verified by numerical solutions of the microscopic density-matrix equation. These findings refine the effective Bloch description of alkali-metal spin dynamics and are directly relevant to atomic magnetometers and alkali-metal--noble-gas comagnetometers, including those used in precision searches for physics beyond the Standard Model.

Summary. This paper develops a mean-field theory for the Kondo lattice to explain the emergence of spatially modulated Kondo hybridization waves (KHW). By modeling the order parameter with a wavevector $\mathbf{Q}$, the authors predict that the $\mathbf{Q}=(0,\pi)$ phase generates uniaxial stripe modulations and anisotropic conductivity. This provides a unified theoretical foundation for understanding these novel ordered states in heavy fermion materials.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a concrete, microscopic theoretical framework connecting abstract many-body physics (Kondo lattice) to specific, measurable experimental signatures (STM modulation, transport anisotropy) in complex correlated materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the experimental observation of spatially modulated Kondo hybridization wave (KHW) order in heavy fermion superconductors, challenging the assumption of uniform hybridization.

Main result. A microscopic theory successfully predicts the stable $\mathbf{Q}=(0,\pi)$ KHW phase, which yields uniaxial stripe modulation matching STM data in $ ext{UTe}_2$ and $1 ext{T}/1 ext{H-TaS}_2$, and predicts anisotropic transport as a fingerprint.

Method. The authors developed a theory using the large-$N$ fermionic mean-field approximation applied to the canonical square-lattice Kondo lattice model.

Model / system. The system is the Kondo lattice model, described by a Hamiltonian involving conduction electrons and local moments. The theory treats the Kondo hybridization order parameter $V_i$ as spatially modulated: $V_i = V_0 + V_1 e^{i\mathbf{Q}\cdot\mathbf{R}_i}$.

Key observables. Kondo hybridization gap modulation (uniaxial stripe), in-plane conductivity anisotropy ($\sigma_{xx} eq \sigma_{yy}$), quasiparticle band structure, and Local Density of States (LDOS).

Important parameters / regimes. The ordering wavevector $\mathbf{Q}$, the Kondo coupling strength $J$, and the conduction electron filling $n_c$.

Abstract

Recent scanning tunneling microscopy experiments have discovered emergent spatially modulated Kondo hybridization wave (KHW) order in the heavy fermion superconductor UTe$_2$ and the artificial Kondo lattice system 1T/1H-TaS$_2$, challenging the conventional paradigm of spatially uniform Kondo hybridization in heavy fermion physics. Here, we develop a microscopic theory for KHW order based on the canonical square-lattice Kondo lattice model within the large-$N$ fermionic mean-field approximation. We systematically identify stable modulated KHW phases and establish their ground-state phase diagram. The prominent $\boldsymbol{Q}=(0,π)$ KHW phase yields uniaxial stripe modulation of the Kondo hybridization gap, which faithfully reproduces the spatial modulation pattern observed in UTe$_2$. Moreover, its inherent unit-cell-doubling modulation precisely accounts for the spectroscopic features measured in 1T/1H-TaS$_2$. We further predict a characteristic in-plane conductivity anisotropy that serves as a definitive transport fingerprint to discriminate KHW states with distinct ordering wavevectors. Our work provides a microscopic foundation for the newly observed KHW order and establishes a unified theoretical framework for understanding emergent modulated hybridization phenomena in heavy fermion materials.

Abstract

In this work, we investigate the electronic, topological, and transport properties of a Creutz-Su-Schrieffer-Heeger (CSSH) ladder. Using a tight-binding model within the Green's function formalism, we calculate the energy spectrum, local density of states (LDOS), and electronic transmission. Our results establish a direct connection between the topological properties, flat-band formation, and electronic transport in the CSSH ladder.

Abstract

Abstract

Single crystals of Ce$_5$RhGe$_2$ and Ce$_5$IrGe$_2$ have been systematically investigated by electrical resistivity, specific heat, and magnetization measurements. Together with Ce$_5$CoGe$_2$, all three compounds crystallize in the orthorhombic \emph{Pnma} structure, with the lattice parameters increasing monotonically from Co to Rh to Ir, consistent with the effect of negative chemical pressure. Magnetization measurements along the three principal crystallographic axes identify the \emph{a} axis as the easy magnetization direction throughout the series. Ce$_5$RhGe$_2$ exhibits ferromagnetic ordering with a Curie temperature of approximately 11.5 K and shows magnetic behavior closely resembling that of Ce$_5$CoGe$_2$. In contrast, Ce$_5$IrGe$_2$ undergoes two successive magnetic transitions at $T_{\rm M1}=12.7$ K and $T_{\rm M2}=11.8$ K, and there are multiple metamagnetic transitions under magnetic fields, giving rise to magnetization plateaus at fractions of the saturation magnetization $M_{\rm s}$ of approximately $M_{\rm s}/5$ and $M_{\rm s}/3$. The low-field metamagnetic transition along the easy axis shifts to lower field with decreasing temperature, and eventually a pronounced hysteresis loop is observed about zero-field, establishing that Ce$_5$IrGe$_2$ exhibits a ferrimagnetic ground state at the lowest measured temperatures.
