Deriving the Kijowski Arrival-Time POVM from the Schrödinger Current: Minimal Positivity and Uniqueness

Authors: Avi Marchewka
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.08458v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: 🔥 quantum measurements 4/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper derives the Kijowski time-of-arrival POVM by imposing a minimal positivity constraint on the standard Schrödinger current, which suffers from 'quantum backflow' (negative values). The authors show that the required modification yields a positive, normalized current that naturally separates into independent directional components, providing a physical justification for the POVM's structure.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous, current-based physical derivation for the structure of the Kijowski arrival-time POVM, connecting fundamental principles of current conservation and positivity to a key observable in quantum measurement theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the issue of 'quantum backflow,' where the standard Schrödinger current becomes negative even for states with purely positive momentum support, and seeks to derive a physically meaningful, non-negative arrival-time Probability Operator-Valued Measure (POVM).

Main result. The minimal modification to the free Schrödinger current, derived by enforcing positivity, results in a current that exactly matches the known Kijowski time-of-arrival POVM. Furthermore, the requirement of no backflow in mixed-sign sectors forces the cross-sector kernel to vanish, leading to a natural separation of the directional components.

Method. The authors use a minimal positivity constraint on the momentum-space representation of the current, modifying the free-particle kernel from the standard form to a geometric mean kernel, $K_{\min}(p,p') = \sqrt{pp'}/m$.

Model / system. The analysis is performed for a free particle in one dimension, governed by the Schrödinger equation, focusing on the time-of-arrival observable derived from the momentum-space current.

Key observables. Schrödinger current ($j_{ ext{Sch}}$), minimal positive current ($J_{ ext{min}}$), and the associated momentum-space kernels ($K_{ ext{Sch}}$ vs $K_{ ext{min}}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Momentum ($p$), mass ($m$), and time ($t$). The analysis distinguishes between positive-momentum ($p>0$) and negative-momentum ($p<0$) sectors.

Assumptions / limitations. The construction is based on finding the smallest modification that ensures non-negativity while preserving individual momentum components; it explicitly notes that this construction does not impose a first-arrival condition.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the coherence attenuation factor $C(r) = 2\sqrt{r}/(1+r)$. Figure 2 compares the standard Schrödinger current (which can be negative) to the minimal current (which remains non-negative) for a two-mode superposition.

Paper structure. The paper first defines the problem of quantum backflow in the free Schrödinger current. It then derives the minimal positive kernel modification for single-sign sectors. Finally, it extends the analysis to mixed-sign sectors, showing that the no-backflow condition forces the cross-sector kernels to zero, yielding the sum of two independent directional contributions matching the Kijowski POVM.

Abstract

Quantum backflow refers here to the appearance of a negative Schrödinger current for a state whose momentum support is entirely positive. We ask for the smallest modification of the free Schrödinger current that makes it nonnegative for every such state, while preserving the current of each individual momentum component. We show that the required minimal modification changes the free-particle momentum kernel according to \[ K_{\rm Sch}(p,p')=\frac{p+p'}{2m} \;\longrightarrow\; K_{\min}(p,p')=\frac{\sqrt{pp'}}{m}. \] The resulting current is positive and normalized and therefore defines an arrival-time POVM. Extending the directional no-backflow requirement to states containing both momentum signs forces the cross-sector kernel to vanish, \[ K_{\min}^{+-}=K_{\min}^{-+}=0, \] so that the full current is the sum of two independent directional contributions. The resulting POVM is exactly the Kijowski time-of-arrival POVM, providing a current-based physical motivation for both its directional kernels and their separation. Within the diagonal-preserving pairwise-minimal construction considered here, the result is unique. The construction itself does not impose a first-arrival condition.

Measurement-induced remote activation of nonclassicality

Authors: Sudip Chakrabarty
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.07875v1

Summary. This work demonstrates that nonclassical quantum features, such as Wigner negativity, can be remotely activated in a quantum system simply by performing local measurements on a correlated partner. By quantifying this 'steered nonclassicality' using advanced measures, the authors establish measurement-induced steering as a quantifiable resource for quantum information processing.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a concrete, operational resource (measurement steering) for generating nonclassicality, which is a key resource in quantum information protocols, linking measurement theory directly to fundamental quantum resources.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the operational mechanism by which local measurements on one subsystem can remotely activate nonclassical quantum features in a correlated partner subsystem.

Main result. Local measurements can drive a state from a classically described reduced state into a genuinely nonclassical conditional state, establishing measurement-induced steering as a resource for nonclassicality.

Method. The authors developed formal measures for 'steered nonclassicality' (maximal and average) using quasiprobability distributions (Wigner, KD) and optimized these measures over possible local measurements.

Model / system. The analysis covers general two-qubit systems and extends to a hybrid qubit-oscillator model for continuous variable (CV) extensions. The core is the bipartite state $\rho_{AB}$ shared between two parties.

Key observables. Nonclassicality, quantified by Wigner negativity (CV) or KD sum-negativity (DV), and the resulting anomalous weak values.

Important parameters / regimes. The nature of the local noise channel (unital vs. non-unital) and the alignment of the noise relative to the system's axis.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the convexity of the nonclassicality measure and the ability to restrict the optimization to rank-one POVM elements.

Figures summary. Not specified, but the notes indicate optimization results for two-qubit and CV quantities, including finding global maxima and verifying numerical stability.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by defining the problem using nonclassicality diagnostics, developing mathematical tools (Maximal/Average Steered Nonclassicality), optimizing these measures for specific models (two-qubit, qubit-oscillator), and analyzing the physical implications (weak values, noise effects).

Abstract

Nonclassicality, defined through nonpositivity of quasiprobability distributions such as the Wigner and Kirkwood-Dirac distributions, underlies key quantum advantages. We show that it can be remotely activated: local measurements by a correlated partner alone can drive a party whose reduced state is classical into a genuinely nonclassical conditional state. For two-qubit systems we obtain closed forms for the maximal single-branch effect and for its probability-weighted average under the best two-outcome projective measurement, the latter proven optimal over all measurements when the shared state carries no local bias. Local noise on the steered party, evaluated relative to its own noise-updated reference basis, can enhance the effect when non-unital and misaligned with that party's axis, whereas no unital channel ever helps. This nonclassicality is shown to unlock, for the same reference observable, anomalous weak values unavailable to the unsteered state under arbitrary postselection. An exactly solvable hybrid qubit-oscillator model shows the same mechanism generates Wigner negativity. These results identify measurement-induced steering as an operational resource for remotely activating nonclassicality.

Saving resources through repeat-until-success positive-operator-valued-measure measurements in quantum computation

Summary. This paper proposes a quantum computation scheme that uses intermediate Positive-Operator-Valued-Measure (POVM) measurements within a Repeat-Until-Success (RUS) framework. By performing measurements step-by-step rather than accumulating them, the protocol achieves a polynomial reduction in the required number of qubits and unitary gates compared to standard methods. This demonstrates a significant resource optimization technique for quantum circuit design.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a concrete, resource-saving protocol for implementing complex quantum algorithms by strategically placing measurements, which is crucial for scaling quantum computation beyond idealized models.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the resource inefficiency in quantum computation when intermediate measurements are required during a multi-step process.

Main result. Implementing intermediate measurements using a Repeat-Until-Success (RUS) POVM approach provides a polynomial reduction in required qubits and unitary operations compared to deferring all measurements to the end.

Method. The core method involves structuring the computation path such that each step is realized as a probabilistic, repeat-until-success measurement on the working qubits, mediated by an ancillary qubit.

Model / system. The model uses $n$ working qubits and one ancillary qubit. The POVM measurement is physically implemented by applying a controlled unitary operation on the whole system followed by a projective measurement on the ancillary qubit.

Key observables. Success probability ($P_{ ext{succ}}$), number of required qubits, and count of unitary operations (e.g., Toffoli gates).

Important parameters / regimes. The success probability $p_k$ of each step, and the time evolution parameters ($t_k$) used in the Quantum Resonance Transfer (QRT) method.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the principle of deferred measurement and assumes that the overlap between adjacent states and the energy gap are polynomially large for the QRT implementation.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the quantum circuit for the RUS-POVM process (Fig. 1) and compare it conceptually to the circuit where intermediate measurements are deferred to the end (Fig. 2/3).

Paper structure. The paper details the RUS-POVM approach (Sec. II), its physical implementation via the QRT method (Sec. III), error analysis (Sec. IV), and a direct comparison of resource scaling against deferred measurement protocols (Sec. V).

Abstract

We present a quantum computation approach in which computation is guided by positive-operator-valuedmeasure (POVM) measurements following a given computation path in multisteps. In this approach, one ancillary qubit is coupled to a register of working qubits, and a POVM measurement is implemented effectively on the working qubits by applying a unitary operation on the whole system followed by a projective measurement performed on the ancillary qubit. Each step of the computation is a repeat-until-success procedure such that the desired state of the step is obtained deterministically on the working qubits via POVM measurements. The principle of deferred measurement states that measurements can always be moved from an intermediate stage of a quantum circuit to the end of the circuit without affecting the efficiency of the computation.We demonstrate that in our approach, by introducing intermediate measurements on the ancillary qubit in the computation process, both the number of qubits and unitary operations can be reduced polynomially, compared to the case where the intermediate measurements are deferred to the end of the computation. We also provide a method for implementation of the approach.

Summary. This paper theoretically analyzes how a qubit interacts with the edge of a photonic graphene lattice, finding that the unique zero-energy flat band fundamentally changes the decoherence dynamics. Instead of exhibiting expected long-time power-law decay, the system displays damped Rabi oscillations. This provides crucial insights for designing quantum devices where edge effects dominate coupling to the environment.

Why it may be interesting. This work is highly relevant as it models open quantum systems dynamics (qubit coupled to a structured bath) using advanced techniques, directly impacting understanding of decoherence in solid-state and photonic platforms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the non-Markovian dynamics of a qubit coupled to the zigzag edge of a photonic graphene lattice, contrasting it with bulk coupling.

Main result. The zero-energy flat band at the zigzag edge suppresses long-time branch-cut tails, leading instead to damped Rabi oscillations in the qubit population.

Method. The analysis employs an analytical resolvent operator approach combined with exact lattice Green's functions to calculate the qubit population dynamics.

Model / system. The system consists of a qubit coupled to a 2D honeycomb lattice bath modeled with a zigzag edge. The Hamiltonian includes the qubit, the bath, and the coupling term, $H = \Delta\sigma_+\sigma_- + H_B + g(\sigma_+a_{00} + H.c.)$.

Key observables. Qubit population $P_e(t)$, spectral components (bound states, unstable poles, branch cut detours).

Important parameters / regimes. Coupling strength ($g$), detuning ($\Delta$), and hopping rate ($J$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis requires analytically continuing Green's functions beyond the first Riemann sheet to manage branch cuts.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the energy spectrum showing the zero-energy flat band, and plot the qubit population dynamics over time for various coupling regimes.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by setting up the Hamiltonian, deriving the self-energy using Green's functions, and then using the resolvent operator to calculate the time evolution of the qubit population, comparing edge vs. bulk dynamics.

Abstract

We present an analytical study of the dynamics of a qubit coupled to the zigzag edge of a photonic honeycomb lattice. Leveraging a resolvent operator approach and exact lattice Green's functions, we map the spectrum of qubit-photon bound states and complex poles to uncover the long-time scaling laws of the qubit population. Unlike a qubit coupled to the bulk of graphene, the presence of a zero-energy flat band connecting the Dirac points suppresses long-time branch-cut-induced tails, instead driving damped Rabi oscillations. Potential experimental platforms are briefly discussed.

Summary. This paper rigorously studies spatial decoherence in many-body systems interacting with a bosonic environment. It derives an effective evolution map by analyzing the system dynamics in specific limits of coupling strength and time scaling. The key finding is the identification of a fine-grained time scale $\tau = \lambda t$ that resolves the decoherence process, providing a theoretical description of macroscopic quantum localization.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous, quantitative description of how environmental coupling drives quantum systems toward classical localization, which is central to understanding quantum-to-classical transitions in open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the dynamics of a many-body quantum system coupled strongly to a bosonic reservoir, focusing specifically on the mechanism and timescale of spatial decoherence.

Main result. The decoherence process is shown to be exactly resolved at a fine-grained time scale $\tau = \lambda t$, leading to an effective evolution map that is generally Markovian but not described by a standard dynamical semigroup.

Method. The analysis uses time evolution calculations involving the full Hamiltonian, employing Dyson series expansions and introducing a projection operator $P$ to analyze the system's evolution in different limits of coupling strength ($\lambda$) and time ($t$).

Model / system. The system consists of an $N$-body quantum system in $d$ dimensions, coupled via a linear potential operator $G$ to a bosonic quantum field reservoir. The dynamics are governed by a total Hamiltonian $H = H_S + H_R + \lambda G \otimes \phi(g)$.

Key observables. The expectation value of system observables $\langle A \rangle_t$, the density matrix evolution $\rho_t$, and the degree of off-diagonal coherence.

Important parameters / regimes. The coupling strength $\lambda$, the time $t$, and the crucial scaling parameter $\alpha$ defining the time dependence $t \propto \lambda^{-\alpha}$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on considering the limit of infinite coupling strength ($\lambda \to \infty$) and assumes the reservoir state $\omega_R$ is regular (e.g., Gaussian).

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the time evolution under different scaling limits of $\lambda$ and $t$, establishing the Zeno limit ($\alpha=0$), the static limit ($\alpha>1$), and deriving the exact effective dynamics for the critical scaling $\alpha=1$.

Abstract

We study the dynamics of a many-body quantum system strongly interacting with a bosonic reservoir. The coupling is given by a potential operator for the system and it is linear in the field. We show that in the limit of infinite coupling strength the system undergoes instantaneous spatial decoherence. To resolve the decoherence process in time we consider large coupling strengths $λ$ and short times scaling as $t\propto λ^{-α}$, with $α\ge0$, in the limit $λ\rightarrow\infty$. On short time scales $α>1$ the dynamics is trivial while on longer ones $0\leα<1$ the decoherence is instantaneous. We show that the decoherence process is resolved exactly for $α=1$, defining the fine-grained time scale $τ=λt$. We construct an approximate effective evolution map of the many-body system with controlled error estimates. Generically, the effective dynamics is markovian, but not given by a dynamical semigroup. As a physical application we give a rigorous description of the phenomenon of localization of macroscopic quantum objects in position space.

Abstract

Nonreciprocity is increasingly recognized as a unifying concept linking diverse nonequilibrium phenomena found across physics, chemistry, and biology. However, nonreciprocity and its nonequilibrium signatures are fundamentally scale-dependent, and may emerge or disappear under coarse-graining. A central challenge is therefore to understand when and how nonreciprocity manifests itself on different scales, for example via irreversible fluctuations or macroscopic currents. In this Perspective, we discuss the physical origin and fate of effective nonreciprocal interactions and the characteristic irreversible dynamics they give rise to across scales.

Summary. This theoretical paper investigates how measurement-induced nonlocality (MIN) behaves in two detectors undergoing uniform acceleration in the presence of a quantum field. Contrary to previous expectations that MIN vanishes in strong acceleration, the authors show that the response is highly dependent on the initial quantum state. This demonstrates that acceleration does not universally destroy quantum correlations, offering a nuanced view of quantum information in curved or accelerating spacetime.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses quantum correlations (MIN) in non-inertial frames, providing insights into how fundamental physical effects like acceleration modify quantum information measures, which is highly relevant to quantum optics and open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To determine if the suppression of measurement-induced nonlocality (MIN) in accelerating Unruh-DeWitt detectors is a universal consequence of the Unruh effect or if it depends on the specific initial state of the system.

Main result. The suppression of MIN under strong acceleration is not universal; its behavior can be state-dependent, showing monotonic suppression, recovery, or enhancement, and can approach a nonzero value at high temperatures.

Method. The authors analyzed the evolution of the reduced density matrix using a master equation approach, calculating MIN as a function of the Unruh temperature and initial state parameters.

Model / system. The system consists of two uniformly accelerating Unruh-DeWitt detectors coupled to a massless scalar field in Minkowski spacetime. The dynamics are governed by a master equation incorporating the Kubo-Martin-Schwinger (KMS) condition.

Key observables. Measurement-Induced Nonlocality (MIN), which quantifies the maximal global disturbance induced by local projective measurements.

Important parameters / regimes. Unruh temperature ($T = a/(2\pi)$), detector energy spacing ($\omega$), coupling strength ($\mu$), and the initial-state parameter ($\Delta_0$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the weak coupling ($\mu \ll 1$) and Markovian approximations.

Figures summary. The figures illustrate MIN versus temperature ($T$) for different initial correlation parameters ($\Delta_0$), showing cases of monotonic decrease, nonmonotonic behavior (minimum followed by increase), and monotonic increase.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the theoretical framework using the detector model and master equation, derives the expression for MIN, and then analyzes the temperature dependence of MIN by examining specific limits and parameter choices.

Abstract

Uniform acceleration induces a thermal response to the Minkowski vacuum and can thereby modify quantum correlations. For measurement-induced nonlocality (MIN), a form of nonclassical correlation distinct from entanglement, previous field-mode analyses of bosonic fields found that it decreases with increasing acceleration and vanishes in the infinite-acceleration limit. This raises the question of whether the disappearance of MIN is a generic consequence of the Unruh effect or depends on the physical description of the accelerated quantum system. To address this question, we study two uniformly accelerating Unruh-DeWitt detectors interacting with a massless scalar field. We find that the response of MIN to the Unruh temperature depends sensitively on the initial detector state: it can decrease monotonically, vanish at an intermediate temperature and subsequently recover, or increase monotonically. Moreover, for a broad class of initial states, MIN approaches a nonzero value in the high-temperature limit. These results show that the suppression of MIN under strong acceleration is not universal. Instead, Unruh-induced detector dynamics gives rise to a state-dependent response in which measurement-induced nonlocality can be suppressed, restored, or enhanced.

Abstract

Symmetry-protected topological (SPT) phases are conventionally formulated for pure states protected by exact symmetries. In open quantum systems, however, decoherence generates mixed-state ensembles in which average symmetry can instead emerge only after averaging over microscopic trajectories. In this work, we realize decoherence-induced averaged SPT (ASPT) order on programmable quantum processors. Starting from a one-dimensional cluster SPT, we engineer sublattice-selective Pauli-$Z$ dephasing through ancilla-assisted quantum circuits. We observe the resulting symmetry conversion through the decay of the symmetry charge. Moreover, a two-replica Renyi-2 string correlator measured through destructive SWAP readout remains nontrivial, revealing the ASPT structure encoded at the density-matrix level. We further show that the engineered dephasing redistributes spectral weight among reduced-stabilizer sectors while preserving the characteristic twofold pairing of the half-chain entanglement spectrum. These results establish a gate-based route to engineering and probing ASPT order and demonstrate structured decoherence as a programmable resource for realizing mixed-state topological quantum matter.

Summary. This work explores the concept of hydrodynamic attractors, which describe how complex, non-equilibrium systems approach a simple, low-dimensional late-time behavior. It rigorously distinguishes this 'attractorization' from the 'hydrodynamization' process, comparing results from nuclear collisions and ultracold atomic gases. The findings provide a unified theoretical framework for understanding how fluid dynamics emerge from microscopic interactions.

Why it may be interesting. The explicit inclusion and comparison with ultracold Fermi gases provides a direct theoretical link between high-energy heavy-ion physics and controllable AMO systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to distinguish between 'attractorization' (loss of initial state sensitivity) and 'hydrodynamization' (onset of hydrodynamic validity) in non-equilibrium systems.

Main result. Hydrodynamic attractors are effective low-dimensional structures that govern late-time evolution, and this concept extends constitutive relations beyond local thermal equilibrium.

Method. The authors compare multiple theoretical frameworks—including kinetic theory, holography, and Israel-Stewart-type theories—using conformal, boost-invariant Bjorken flow as the primary setting.

Model / system. The study applies to high-energy nuclear collisions (QGP) and ultracold atomic gases, utilizing theoretical tools like divergent gradient expansions and transseries completions.

Key observables. Particle production, transverse energy, flow observables, shear/bulk viscosity, and the evolution of the stress-energy tensor.

Important parameters / regimes. Proper time ($ au$), temperature ($T$), and dimensionless parameters related to viscosity ($\eta/s$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis often relies on the conformal, boost-invariant Bjorken flow approximation, and it distinguishes between attractorization and hydrodynamization.

Paper structure. The paper is structured by comparing different theoretical approaches (MIS, kinetic theory, holography) to understand the transition from far-from-equilibrium dynamics to hydrodynamic behavior, detailing different types of attraction.

Abstract

Attractors are effective low-dimensional structures, defined in a chosen set of observables, that trajectories from different initial states approach; hydrodynamic attractors are those on which the late-time evolution is governed by hydrodynamic constitutive relations. Motivated by nuclear collisions and ultracold atomic gases, this chapter distinguishes attractorization (the loss of sensitivity to some directions in the space of initial states) from hydrodynamization (the onset of validity of hydrodynamic constitutive relations). Using mainly conformal, boost-invariant Bjorken flow, we compare Müller-Israel-Stewart-type theories, kinetic theory, holography, and classical Yang-Mills fields. Forward attraction, produced by the decay of non-hydrodynamic modes, is distinguished from pullback attraction, which can select a unique regular solution, and from expansion-driven attraction, which can suppress initial-state sensitivity before microscopic relaxation, with or without a subsequent fluid regime. Divergent gradient expansions and their transseries completions, the state-space picture, and adiabatic hydrodynamization provide complementary descriptions. Applications to nuclear collisions include particle production, transverse energy and flow, the initialization of and linear response around attracting backgrounds, jet quenching, and attractor-informed modifications of hydrodynamic models. For ultracold Fermi gases, we review theoretical proposals for bulk-channel attractors under scattering-length drives and distinguish them from recent measurements of short-time contact and momentum-distribution dynamics.

Abstract

We report simultaneous sub-Doppler cooling and optical dipole trapping of $^{39}$K-Cs and $^{41}$K-Cs mixtures. Both mixtures are cooled to temperatures $\sim10$ $\mathrmμ$K, achieving performance comparable to that obtained with each species individually. To the best of our knowledge, this constitutes the first realization of a laser-cooled and optically trapped $^{41}$K-Cs mixture. For the $^{39}$K-Cs mixture, we additionally implement parallel spin-resolved Feshbach spectroscopy enabled by Stern-Gerlach separation. For potassium isotopes, we implement sub-Doppler cooling with $D_1$-line gray molasses, while for cesium the entire cooling sequence is implemented using $D_2$ transitions. We spin-polarize the atoms and confine Bose-Bose mixtures in a 1064 nm optical dipole trap. Using the resulting $^{39}$K-Cs samples, we observe 14 heteronuclear Feshbach loss features. Five agree with resonances reported previously, while nine have, to the best of our knowledge, not been observed experimentally before, including $p$-wave features and resonances in additional spin channels. The shorter lifetime of the $^{41}$K-Cs mixture in the optical dipole trap currently hinders systematic Feshbach spectroscopy, which we therefore do not pursue in this work. To characterize this limitation quantitatively, we study the decay dynamics of $^{39}$K-Cs and $^{41}$K-Cs mixtures under comparable temperature and density conditions, revealing a substantially stronger nonexponential loss in the $^{41}$K-Cs mixture. The demonstrated preparation of ultracold $^{41}$K-Cs mixtures provides a starting point for Feshbach-resonance and photoassociation spectroscopy of this previously unexplored isotopologue. Such measurements are essential for identifying suitable pathways for magnetoassociation and coherent optical transfer, and ultimately for the production of ultracold ground-state $^{41}$KCs molecules.

Abstract

Certifying entanglement in high-dimensional systems usually requires full state tomography, whose cost grows rapidly with the system dimension. Local randomized measurements offer a scalable alternative, but existing tests based on second-order correlations access only limited information about the state. Here, we derive a finite-size entanglement certificate that extends local randomized measurements to third order. The additional third-order information reveals entanglement that remains undetected at second order, while a dimension-independent concentration bound provides rigorous control of finite-sample errors. Our result opens a practical route to extracting stronger entanglement information from experimental platforms without the dimension-dependent overhead of state tomography.
