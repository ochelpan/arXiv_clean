Summary. This work establishes tight, near-optimal quantum lower bounds for solving convex optimization problems using quantum oracles. By developing a novel technique based on Fourier-rank, the authors show that solving these problems requires a query complexity scaling as $\Omega(n / \log n \log \log n)$. This resolves long-standing open questions regarding the quantum speedup for optimization.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous characterization of quantum query complexity for fundamental optimization problems provides deep insights into the computational power of quantum algorithms in practical settings.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Establishing near-optimal quantum query lower bounds for high-accuracy convex optimization over explicit families of n-dimensional ellipsoids.

Main result. The quantum query complexity for this optimization problem is characterized by a lower bound of $\Omega\left( rac{n}{\log n \log \log n} ight)$ membership queries, up to logarithmic factors.

Method. The proof utilizes a novel polynomial method based on Fourier-rank, which is applied to derive lower bounds for determinant computation and subsequently reduces this hardness to the convex optimization setting.

Model / system. The model is a black-box quantum query model where the objective function and feasible set (ellipsoids) are accessed via oracles (membership, phase, or gradient).

Key observables. Query complexity (number of oracle calls), additive objective error ($\Theta(n^{-2})$), determinant value, and minimum eigenvalue.

Important parameters / regimes. Dimensionality ($n$), accuracy ($\epsilon$), and the condition number ($\kappa$) for gradient bounds.

Assumptions / limitations. The lower bounds are established for specific hard families (e.g., centered ellipsoids) and the analysis relies on advanced tools from functional analysis and Fourier analysis in the continuous setting.

Figures summary. Not specified.

Paper structure. The paper develops the Fourier-rank based lower bound for determinant computation, applies this to membership queries for ellipsoids to solve the optimization lower bound, and separately derives gradient-query lower bounds for strongly convex functions.

Abstract

We establish a near-linear quantum query lower bound for high-accuracy convex optimization over an explicit family of $n$-dimensional ellipsoids. We focus on linear optimization with an explicitly given objective, where the feasible set is accessed through a membership oracle. We show that any algorithm that, for every unit linear objective, returns an exactly feasible point with additive objective error $Θ(n^{-2})$ requires $Ω\!\left(\frac{n}{\log n\,\log\log n}\right)$ membership queries. The same lower bound can be shown to hold if the returned point is only required to be approximately feasible, within $Θ(n^{-2})$ distance from the feasible set. This resolves, up to logarithmic factors, an open question posed by Chakrabarti, Childs, Li, and Wu~(\textit{Quantum}, 2020) and by van Apeldoorn, Gilyén, Gribling, and de Wolf~(\textit{Quantum}, 2020). Coupled with the upper bounds in these papers, the query complexity of high-accuracy convex optimization is characterized tightly up to logarithmic factors. The proof is built around a lower bound for determinant computation that is derived via a novel polynomial method based on Fourier-rank. In the continuous matrix phase-query model, computing the determinant of a real $n\times n$ matrix requires at least $n/2$ matrix-vector product queries. The construction also yields an $Ω(n)$ phase-query lower bound for estimating the minimum eigenvalue of a real symmetric $n\times n$ matrix to additive accuracy $Θ(n^{-2})$. These results extend the determinant and minimum-eigenvalue lower bounds of Childs, Hung, and Li~(ICALP 2021) from finite fields to the real-valued setting. Based on the same constructions, we also prove a near-optimal gradient-query lower bound for constant-accuracy optimization of smooth and strongly convex functions.

Summary. This paper rigorously proves exponential strong converse theorems for quantum and classical communication over arbitrary quantum channels. By utilizing integral representations of Rényi information, the authors demonstrate that any attempt to transmit information above the channel's capacity fails exponentially fast. Why it may be interesting. The rigorous characterization of capacity limits via strong converse theorems provides fundamental, non-trivial bounds on what is physically possible for information processing using quantum resources.

Main problem. The paper aims to establish an exponential strong converse theorem for information transmission through quantum channels, proving that communication rates exceeding the channel capacity fail catastrophically.

Main result. The authors prove that at rates beyond the quantum or classical capacity, the entanglement-generation fidelity and success probability decay exponentially with the number of channel uses.

Method. The proof adapts the classical Arimoto strategy, crucially employing integral representations of Rényi information measures to establish asymptotic continuity bounds for Rényi capacities.

Model / system. The analysis concerns arbitrary finite-dimensional memoryless quantum channels, considering both classical and quantum information transmission protocols.

Key observables. Capacity ($C$ or $Q$), entanglement-generation fidelity ($F_{eg}$), and the success probability for classical communication.

Important parameters / regimes. The Rényi order ($\alpha > 1$) and the transmission rate ($r$) relative to the capacity.

Assumptions / limitations. The core technical steps involve bounding fidelity using the sandwiched Rényi divergence and adapting the Leung–Smith telescoping argument.

Paper structure. The structure builds from defining the problem (strong converse) to introducing the mathematical machinery (Rényi measures, integral representations), deriving the converse bounds for both quantum and classical settings, and finally proving the equivalence of various rate bounds.

Abstract

We show that the capacity of a quantum channel demarcates a phase transition: while reliable transmission below capacity is always possible, any attempt to transmit information above it fails catastrophically. Specifically, we prove exponential strong converse theorems for unassisted quantum and classical communication over arbitrary finite-dimensional memoryless quantum channels. At rates beyond the respective capacity, the entanglement-generation fidelity and the success probability for classical communication decay exponentially with the number of channel uses. This rules out transmission above capacity even when one tolerates arbitrarily large errors. Our proof follows the classical Arimoto strategy, augmented by a crucial new ingredient: integral representations of Rényi information measures that lead to asymptotic continuity bounds for Rényi capacities.

Promises should be taken seriously: On relativization with promise problems

Summary. This paper tackles the complex theoretical issue of how computational power changes when moving from standard language problems to promise problems, especially when using black-box oracles. The authors show that standard complexity results fail to transfer to the promise setting, establishing fundamental separations between classes like $ ext{PromiseBQP}$ and $ ext{PromiseP}$. It provides deep insights into the structure of quantum and classical complexity hierarchies under these generalized computational models.

Why it may be interesting. While highly theoretical, the rigorous analysis of computational models and the limitations of standard complexity results (like those transferring from languages to promises) informs the fundamental limits of quantum computation, which is crucial for understanding the feasibility of quantum algorithms in physical systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the computational differences between standard language problems and promise problems when using black-box oracle access, particularly concerning how unconstrained 'off-promise' inputs affect complexity classes.

Main result. It demonstrates that results proven for languages do not necessarily transfer to promise problems, establishing separations like $ ext{PromiseBQP}^O ot\subseteq ext{PromiseP}^O_{ ext{/poly}}$ for a specific oracle $O$. Furthermore, it establishes that $ ext{PromiseYQP}^* = ext{PP}$ under certain assumptions.

Method. The authors employ advanced techniques from complexity theory, defining and comparing various oracle access semantics (robust, loose) and adapting proof techniques, such as those used for the Quantum-Classical Polynomial Hierarchy, to the promise setting.

Model / system. The model is purely theoretical, focusing on abstract computational complexity classes ($ ext{P}, ext{BQP}, ext{PP}$, etc.) and their generalizations to promise problems ($ ext{PromiseP}, ext{PromiseBQP}$). The 'oracle' represents an external computational source whose behavior is constrained by the problem's promise.

Key observables. The primary observables are the containment and separation relationships between various complexity classes (e.g., $ ext{PromiseBQP}$ vs. $ ext{PromiseP}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The key parameters are the definitions of oracle access semantics (robust vs. loose) and the structure of the promise sets ($\Pi_{ ext{yes}}, \Pi_{ ext{no}}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on constructing specific oracles $O$ to demonstrate separations, and the results often depend on the specific definition of oracle access used (e.g., robust queries).

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the three types of oracle access (Smart, Robust, Loose) to promise problems, comparing the required success probabilities for different query types.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by first establishing the necessity of defining oracle access carefully (Observation 1.1). It then proves separations between language and promise settings (Result 1), improves bounds on the Quantum-Classical Polynomial Hierarchy using loose access (Result 2), and finally establishes relationships between specialized promise classes like $ ext{PromiseYQP}^$ and $ ext{PromisePostBQP}^$ (Result 3).

Abstract

Relativization is concerned with comparing computational models with black-box access to an oracle. For promise problems, black-box access is not canonical due to inputs outside of the promise being unconstrained. We study two semantics for such access. Under robust queries, a machine must correctly answer regardless of the completion of the problem,, while loose access requires that the internal choices of a machine do not change based on off-promise queries. Our first result separates the language and promise settings. Namely, we construct an oracle $O$ such that $\mathsf{P}^O = \mathsf{BQP}^O = \mathsf{AWPP}^O$, but $\mathsf{PromiseBQP}^O\not\subseteq\mathsf{PromiseP}^O_{\mathsf{/poly}}$. In particular, $\mathsf{BPP}^O = \mathsf{BQP}^O$, but $\mathsf{PromiseBQP}^O \neq \mathsf{PromiseBPP}^O$, showing that results for languages need not transfer to promises. Next, we use loose queries to strengthen the upper bound on the Quantum-Classical Polynomial Hierarchy from $\mathsf{P}^{\mathsf{PP}^{\mathsf{PP}}}$ to $\mathsf{QCPH} \subseteq \mathsf{BP\cdot PP} \subseteq \mathsf{PromiseBPP}^{\mathsf{PP}}$. The same proof also shows $\mathsf{PP}^\mathsf{PromiseBQP} = \mathsf{PP}$. Additionally, we show that $\mathsf{PromiseBQP}$, even when given quantum advice, is self-low under robust queries. Finally, we exhibit an obstruction to transferring language-level counting results to promise classes. Although $\mathsf{AWPP}$ and $\mathsf{APP}$ are low for $\mathsf{PP}$, a corresponding promise analogue would collapse the counting hierarchy as $\mathsf{GapP} \subseteq \mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{PromiseAWPP}}$. This motivates the introduction of $\mathsf{PromisePostBQP^*}$, which restricts $\mathsf{PostBQP}$ to input-indepencent postselection. By showing that it is low for \PP, we obtain $\mathsf{PP}^{\mathsf{PromiseYQP^*}} = \mathsf{PP}$.

Summary. This paper develops a moment-based methodology to quantify the nonclassicality of quantum states using the Margenau--Hill quasiprobability distribution. By focusing on low-order moments rather than full distribution reconstruction, the authors establish a hierarchy of rigorous lower bounds for the negativity. This provides a practical, experimentally accessible framework for characterizing quantum features.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, measurable, and computationally tractable diagnostic tool (moment-based) to quantify nonclassical features of quantum states, which is highly relevant for characterizing quantum resources in open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to quantify the nonclassicality associated with the Margenau--Hill quasiprobability (MHQ) distribution without needing to reconstruct the full distribution.

Main result. A moment-based framework is established, yielding a hierarchy of rigorous lower bounds for the MHQ negativity, with the fourth-moment bound being the strongest among even-moment bounds.

Method. The authors utilize the moments (both standard and absolute) of the MHQ distribution, deriving lower bounds for the negativity using techniques related to R\'enyi entropy and moment-matrix conditions.

Model / system. The study concerns quantum states ($ ho$) on a $d$-dimensional Hilbert space, specifically analyzing the MHQ distribution derived from two observables, $A$ and $B$, defined by spectral projectors.

Key observables. Logarithmic MHQ negativity, low-order moments ($m_n, e_n$), and the moment-matrix condition for positivity.

Important parameters / regimes. The order of the moment ($r$ or $2n$), and the dimensionality of the Hilbert space ($d$).

Assumptions / limitations. The approach assumes that the relevant moments admit exact multi-copy representations, allowing estimation via interferometry or classical shadow techniques.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the MHQ distribution and the concept of nonclassicality via its negativity. It then develops the moment-based quantification, deriving the lower bound hierarchy, and finally applies these results to qubits to find tight bounds and optimal thresholds.

Abstract

Quasiprobability distributions offer a useful way of describing nonclassical features of quantum systems through their departure from classical probability theory. In this work, we investigate the quantification of nonclassicality associated with the Margenau--Hill quasiprobability (MHQ) distribution using its moments, without requiring reconstruction of the full distribution. First we introduce the logarithmic MHQ negativity as a quantifier of nonclassicality, and then derive a hierarchy of rigorous lower bounds in terms of low-order moments. Within the resulting hierarchy, the fourth-moment bound is the strongest among the bounds based on even moments. For qubits, we further derive a tight upper bound on the exact MHQ negativity and identify the corresponding extremal state. We also obtain an optimal positivity threshold based on the fourth moment for arbitrary pairs of qubit observables. Finally, we show that the relevant moments admit exact multicopy representations, providing a route to their estimation using interferometry or classical shadow techniques. Our results establish a moment-based framework for extracting quantitative information about MHQ negativity from a finite set of low-order observables.

Abstract

However, its accuracy degrades in strongly correlated regimes where the relevant determinant space exceeds what finite-shot sampling can capture. In this work, we introduce Quantum Wavefunction Augmentation via Variational Autoencoders (Q-WAVE), a hybrid method that combines determinants sampled via SqDRIFT Krylov circuits and configuration interaction singles and doubles (CISD) determinants with generative machine learning. Finally, we establish Q-WAVE's scalability on a 52-qubit ethylene system (achieving sub-millihartree accuracy versus CCSD(T)) and a highly correlated 60-qubit $\text{Cr}_2$ stress test that attains chemical accuracy upon a final perturbative correction.

Abstract

Josephson tunnel junctions (JJs) are the fundamental building blocks of today's most advanced superconducting electronic components, such as qubits and superconducting quantum interference devices (SQUIDs). Given the ongoing demand for scalability of these devices on the wafer-scale, it is crucial to control the fabrication process as precisely as possible to ensure uniform quality and reproducibility. Abstract

Freezing of supercooled water is a classic non-equilibrium problem, yet the influence of thermal history on crystallization remains unclear. Using molecular dynamics simulations with the TIP4P/Ice model, we investigate how the initial temperature $T_i$ shapes freezing following rapid quenching to 250 K. By monitoring the evolution of hydrogen-bonded ring structures, we find a non-monotonic dependence of the freezing time $t_F$ on $T_i$, with the slowest crystallization occurring near 300 K.
