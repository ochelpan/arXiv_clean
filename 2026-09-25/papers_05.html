Summary. This paper develops a spectral transfer formalism to analyze nonlinear synchronization in coupled oscillator networks, moving beyond simple global measures. By projecting dynamics onto the graph Laplacian eigenbasis, the authors quantify how activity redistributes across structural scales via a transfer matrix. This reveals hidden, structured spectral reorganizations that persist even when the overall system appears macroscopically stable.

Why it may be interesting. The concept of spectral reorganisation and the separation of global coherence from underlying spectral dynamics provides a powerful, multiscale diagnostic tool applicable to analyzing complex, non-equilibrium dynamics in many-body systems, which is relevant to open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The work aims to resolve how internal dynamical activity redistributes across network scales during nonlinear synchronization, moving beyond traditional global observables like the Kuramoto order parameter.

Main result. The study demonstrates a clear separation between macroscopic coherence and microscopic spectral interactions, showing that structured, fluctuating internal redistribution dynamics persist even when global synchronisation is nearly stationary.

Method. A spectral transfer formalism is introduced by projecting Kuramoto phase dynamics onto the eigenbasis of the graph Laplacian, constructing a time-dependent transfer matrix.

Model / system. The model uses the Kuramoto oscillator system coupled on a general graph structure, with analysis focusing on modular and hierarchical network architectures.

Key observables. Spectral flux, Spectral Modal Energy ($E_m(t)$), Transfer Matrix elements ($T_{m o k}(t)$), Net Spectral Flux ($\Pi(t, K_0)$), and the conventional Kuramoto order parameter ($R(t)$).

Important parameters / regimes. Coupling strength ($K$), frequency heterogeneity ($\sigma_{\omega}$), and the structural scales defined by the graph Laplacian eigenmodes.

Assumptions / limitations. The spectral flux quantifies redistribution between structural dynamical scales defined by the Laplacian eigenbasis, not physical transport across nodes.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate graph spectral decomposition (showing mode hierarchies) and the temporal evolution of spectral activity, including cross-sections of the transfer matrix and the spectral flux ($\Pi$).

Paper structure. The paper builds the spectral transfer formalism by decomposing the state onto graph eigenmodes, constructing the transfer matrix to quantify mode exchange, and analyzing the resulting spectral flux and various transfer observables to characterize different dynamical regimes.

Abstract

This work introduces a spectral transfer formalism for nonlinear synchronisation dynamics to resolve how internal dynamical activity redistributes across network scales, moving beyond traditional global observables like the Kuramoto order parameter. By projecting Kuramoto phase dynamics onto the eigenbasis of the graph Laplacian, we construct a time-dependent transfer matrix and derive novel network metrics, including the spectral flux, which quantifies directional redistribution between structural dynamical scales rather than physical transport across nodes. Numerical simulations on modular and hierarchical networks reveal highly structured, intermittent spectral transfer episodes characterised by forward and inverse spectral cascades, directional reversals, and complex interaction geometries. Crucially, we demonstrate a clear separation between macroscopic coherence and microscopic spectral interactions, showing that highly organised, fluctuating internal redistribution dynamics persist even beneath nearly stationary and featureless global synchronisation states. This framework extends conventional synchronisation analysis and establishes a multiscale approach for interpreting network dynamics as an evolving process of spectral reorganisation.

Abstract

Numerous quantum devices require lasers strictly locked to atomic transitions. Atom-filtered lasers (AFLs) are considered a leading candidate for quantum device laser sources due to their ability to self-align to atomic transitions. However, the sub-GHz sharp transmission spectra of conventional atomic filters impose constraints on both laser miniaturization and output stability. Herein, we demonstrate a micro Faraday anomalous dispersion optical filter (μFADOF) operating within the extreme hyperfine Paschen-Back regime, generating a 7.5 GHz flat-top transmission window. By integrating this filter, the miniaturization bottleneck of the AFLs is overcome, achieving a 30-fold volume reduction to a compact package volume of 75 mL. Simultaneously, investigations into the optical self-feedback characteristics of the μFADOF reveal a stable operating regime, where the optical self-feedback of the atomic filter acts as a stabilizing mechanism, enabling the laser to achieve a power instability of 9 x 10^-6 at 1 s and 3.2 x 10^-5 at 8400 s. The optical frequency standard based on this 75-mL AFL achieves a further improvement in long-term frequency stability to 3 x 10^-13 at 10000 s and maintains turn-key operation under environmental shocks, highlighting the critical role of this laser in the development of deployable quantum devices.

Summary. This theoretical paper analyzes the boundary information encoded in the full Pauli spectrum of critical quantum chains, showing it goes beyond what is captured by standard Rényi entropy measures. By mapping the system to classical root systems, the authors derive exact lattice invariants that serve as precise targets for understanding defect-junction amplitudes in boundary conformal field theory. The results confirm that the full spectrum retains crucial boundary details lost in the continuum limit.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a deep, mathematically rigorous link between critical quantum systems, boundary conformal field theory, and algebraic structures (root systems), which is highly relevant for understanding boundary effects in quantum many-body systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates what boundary information is retained in the full Pauli distribution of critical quantum states, which may be invisible when only considering single Rényi entropy moments or the infrared fixed point.

Main result. The full Pauli spectrum resolves boundary information by generating specific multiplicative invariants related to classical root systems (A, B, C, D), providing concrete lattice targets for defect-measurement-boundary junction amplitudes.

Method. The analysis uses advanced techniques involving Selberg ensembles, Cauchy determinants, and the comparison of ratios of partition functions derived from different boundary conditions.

Model / system. The study focuses on critical Ising chains, whose properties are analyzed using Pauli strings and mapped onto structures governed by classical root systems A, B, C, and D.

Key observables. Stabilizer Rényi Entropy moments, Pauli magnitudes, and ratios of moments $R(\eta)_{\alpha,L}$ and $R( imes)_{\alpha,L}$.

Important parameters / regimes. The Renyi index $\alpha$, the system length $L$, and boundary field parameters $\eta_b$ are crucial parameters explored across various limits.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the lattice ratios converge to universal continuum junction targets in the thermodynamic limit, despite microscopic differences between boundary realizations.

Figures summary. Figures compare the convergence of ratios for different root system realizations (B, C, D) towards common theoretical limits, and show the flow of observables away from exactly solvable points.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the connection between Pauli distributions and root systems, deriving exact lattice invariants, and then performing detailed finite-size and strong-boundary limit analyses to confirm the universality of the derived junction amplitudes.

Abstract

Universal information extracted from a critical quantum state depends on how the state is probed. Spatial entanglement, participation statistics, and stabilizer Rényi entropy correspond to different replica geometries and need not isolate the same boundary data. We show that the complete distribution of Pauli magnitudes in critical Ising chains carries an exact geometric fingerprint of boundary termination, distinguishing lattice realizations that flow to the same infrared boundary fixed point. This structure is organized by discrete Selberg ensembles associated with the classical root systems $A$, $B$, $C$, and $D$: three open chains sharing the free--free Ising boundary condition retain distinct $B_L$, $C_L$, and $D_L$ trigonometric wall geometries. The standard symmetry-breaking representatives instead produce rectangular, pinned, and character-inserted $C$ ensembles. Their four boundary moments contain exactly two independent multiplicative combinations invariant under local endpoint normalizations. One of these reduces exactly at finite rank to a moment of the fundamental symplectic character and provides a lattice target for a defect--measurement-boundary junction amplitude. A conditional Gaussian-character law predicts its values throughout the unlocked phase, while exact-arithmetic finite-rank results at the $α=4$ transition depart sharply from the Gaussian continuation. These results show that the full Pauli spectrum resolves boundary information invisible to the infrared boundary fixed point alone and provide concrete targets for replicated boundary conformal field theory.

Bridging two families of non-Hermiticity: from non-reciprocal couplings to an imaginary potential using the Lanczos transformation

Summary. This paper introduces a novel mathematical technique using the Lanczos transformation to bridge two distinct physical descriptions of non-Hermiticity: non-reciprocal couplings and imaginary potentials. It demonstrates that a generalized Hatano-Nelson model can be mapped to an equivalent model with real couplings and an imaginary on-site potential. This provides a versatile framework for designing and understanding complex non-Hermitian quantum systems.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful mathematical tool (the Lanczos mapping) to unify the understanding of different non-Hermitian physics phenomena, which is highly relevant for designing and analyzing open quantum systems in optics and condensed matter.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to establish an isospectral mapping between two distinct families of non-Hermiticity: those arising from non-reciprocal couplings and those involving an imaginary on-site potential.

Main result. The Lanczos transformation, when applied with specific initial vectors, successfully maps a generalized Hatano-Nelson model to an equivalent tight-binding model featuring real symmetric couplings and an imaginary on-site potential.

Method. The core method is the bi-Lanczos transformation, adapted for non-symmetric Hamiltonians, which constructs an isospectral mapping between the two non-Hermitian forms.

Model / system. The study focuses on 1D and 2D tight-binding lattice models, specifically generalizing the Hatano-Nelson model, which incorporates non-reciprocal couplings and potentially imaginary on-site potentials.

Key observables. The primary observables are the couplings (real vs. imaginary) and the resulting energy spectrum, which is shown to be isospectral between the original and transformed Hamiltonians.

Important parameters / regimes. The initial vectors $|u_0 angle$ and $\langle v_0|$ are crucial parameters, as their specific choice dictates whether the resulting model has an imaginary potential or real couplings.

Assumptions / limitations. The success of the mapping relies on choosing non-standard initial vectors for the bi-Lanczos transformation, and the method is shown to hold even when the original Hamiltonian already contains imaginary potentials or exceptional points.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the transformation on a 1D lattice, showing how different choices of initial vectors control the real/imaginary nature of the on-site potential and couplings. Further figures demonstrate the mapping in 2D (triangular ribbon) and the behavior of exceptional points.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the two non-Hermitian families, develops the Lanczos mapping technique using specific initial vectors, proves the isospectrality, and then applies this framework to construct novel non-Hermitian systems with desired spectral properties (e.g., real spectrum without PT symmetry).

Abstract

Two types of non-Hermiticity found in physical systems, i.e., non-reciprocal couplings and complex potentials, have gained considerable interest recently. The former leads to the non-Hermitian skin effect, while the latter gives rise to a range of non-Hermitian symmetries such as parity-time ($PT$) and particle-hole symmetries. Although these two non-Hermitian forms have been incorporated in the same models, so far they have been treated as distinct families of non-Hermiticity. In this work, we establish an isospectral mapping between them using the Lanczos transformation. More specifically, we show that a generalized Hatano-Nelson model $H$ with non-reciprocal and spatially varying couplings can be mapped to a tight-binding model $H_L$ with real symmetric couplings and an imaginary on-site potential. This result holds even when the original $H$ already has an imaginary potential and exceptional points. Our work provides a refreshed understanding of the connection between different forms of non-Hermiticity. It also offers an approach to obtaining an intuitive physical understanding of exceptional points in $H_L$ that are elusive in $H$. Finally, our findings also warrant a versatile approach to constructing non-Hermitian systems with a complex potential and a real spectrum, without $PT$ symmetry.

Uphill and downhill first passage of an active Brownian particle: Asymmetry and exact path reweighting

Authors: Mykola Tasinkevych, Xuan My Le, Artem Ryabov
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.27425v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Frenkel-Kontorova 1/5 · Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5 · non-equilibrium universality 1/5

Summary. This work analyzes how self-propulsion introduces asymmetry in the passage times of an active particle moving against or with an external bias. The core finding is that the path ensembles are related by a spatial reflection symmetry, which yields an exact reweighting functional. This functional allows researchers to calculate rare event statistics (uphill) using more common measurements (downhill).

Why it may be interesting. The concept of exact path reweighting based on symmetry is a powerful tool applicable to non-equilibrium statistical mechanics problems, suggesting general protocols for analyzing directional biases in active matter.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the asymmetry in first passage processes (uphill vs. downhill) for an Active Brownian Particle (ABP) driven by an external force between absorbing boundaries.

Main result. While self-propulsion breaks the equality of unweighted first passage time distributions, the two directional path ensembles remain exactly related by spatial reflection, allowing for an exact reweighting to reconstruct rare uphill statistics from common downhill trajectories.

Method. The analysis employs path-weighting techniques, deriving an exact reweighting functional based on spatial reflection symmetry, and uses perturbative/asymptotic expansions to calculate mean first passage times.

Model / system. The system is an Active Brownian Particle (ABP) moving in 2D under a constant external force, whose dynamics are reduced to a 1D stochastic process $(x(t), \phi(t))$ in the overdamped limit.

Key observables. Splitting probabilities, conditional first passage time distributions, and the path-dependent asymmetry functional.

Important parameters / regimes. Self-propulsion speed ($U$), external force bias ($F$), and rotational diffusion coefficient ($D_r$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is performed in the overdamped regime, and later sections analyze the rapid angular relaxation limit ($\epsilon o 0$).

Figures summary. Figures illustrate planar trajectories showing both uphill and downhill paths, and projected $X( au)$ trajectories demonstrating the effect of persistence ($U$) on directional contrast.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the ABP model, derives the exact path-reweighting relation based on spatial reflection, and then uses this relation to calculate the directional first passage time distributions and mean times via expansions.

Abstract

First passage processes in active systems combine stochastic transport with self-propulsion and orientational persistence, making motion along and against an external bias sensitive to the internal active dynamics. For passive biased diffusion, opposite exits can have different splitting probabilities while their conditional first passage time distributions remain identical. We study how this relation changes for an active Brownian particle driven by a constant external force between two absorbing boundaries. Self-propulsion breaks the equality of the uphill and downhill first passage time distributions and modifies the splitting probabilities. Nevertheless, the two directional path ensembles remain exactly related by spatial reflection, which pairs downhill and uphill first passage paths of the same duration while preserving their orientational history. The log-ratio of the probabilities of a path and its reflected partner defines a path-dependent asymmetry functional and provides an exact reweighting between the two ensembles. In the symmetric half-weighted representation, the uphill and downhill first passage time distributions coincide for arbitrary orientational persistence. The same path relation also allows rare uphill statistics to be reconstructed from the more frequently sampled downhill trajectories. Numerical simulations confirm the weighted equality across the explored bias and persistence regimes, while perturbative and asymptotic analyses clarify how orientational persistence produces the directional asymmetry of the unweighted statistics. The exact relation between the two directional path ensembles suggests that similar symmetry-based reconstruction protocols may be found in other nonequilibrium first-passage problems.

Summary. This paper enhances the mathematical tool known as the Fer expansion, which calculates quantum time evolution operators using a product of unitary matrix exponentials. By optimizing the bounding technique for commutators, the authors increase the guaranteed convergence radius from 2 to approximately 2.6058. This improvement is vital for accurately simulating quantum dynamics governed by time-dependent Hamiltonians.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a significant quantitative improvement to a fundamental mathematical tool (the Fer expansion) used for time evolution in quantum mechanics, which is crucial for simulating dynamics in AMO physics and quantum information.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to improve the convergence radius for the Fer expansion, a method used to calculate the time-evolution operator for quantum systems governed by time-dependent Hamiltonians.

Main result. The authors successfully improve the known convergence radius for Hermitian generators from $2$ to approximately $2.6058$, representing an improvement of about $30\%$.

Method. The improvement is achieved by optimizing the bound on the commutator $\left[e^{-\mu X}, Y ight]$ over a free parameter $c$, which is then used to refine the recursive bound governing the expansion's convergence.

Model / system. The analysis concerns a quantum system described by a time-dependent Hamiltonian $H(t)$, whose time evolution is captured by the operator $U(t)$. The mathematical framework involves comparing the Dyson series with the unitary-preserving Fer expansion.

Key observables. The convergence radius, defined by the maximum value of the time integral $\int_0^t \|H(s)\| ds$ for which the expansion is guaranteed to converge.

Important parameters / regimes. The convergence radius $ ho$, which is determined by the fixed point of the recursion map $\Psi(x)$, and the Hamiltonian $H(t)$ itself.

Assumptions / limitations. The convergence is only guaranteed within a finite radius, and the derivation relies on bounding the commutator using spectral properties of $e^{-\mu X}$ on the unit circle.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the spectrum of $e^{-\mu X}$ for anti-Hermitian $X$ lying on an arc of the unit circle. Figure 2 depicts the recursion relation as a map showing the improvement from the old bound ($ ho_{old}$) to the new bound ($ ho_{new}$).

Paper structure. The paper builds upon the known Fer expansion, derives a refined bound for the commutator using optimization over a scalar multiple of the identity, and propagates this improved bound through the recursive relation to determine the new, larger convergence radius.

Abstract

The Dyson series expands the propagator of a time-dependent Hamiltonian in powers of the Hamiltonian, but its truncations are in general not unitary. The Fer expansion writes the same propagator as an infinite product of matrix exponentials, each of them unitary, and its remainder decays doubly exponentially with the number of factors. Convergence, however, is guaranteed only within a finite radius: the time integral of the norm of the Hamiltonian must be smaller than $2$. This is the best value known to date. Here we improve it by about $30\%$, raising it to about $2.6058$.

Local time-reversal-invariant topological markers in two dimensions

Authors: Thomas Klein Kvorning, Miguel F. Martínez, Julia D. Hannukainen
Type: theory · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.28213v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5 · quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work introduces the Chern-Simons descendant marker, a novel, local, and state-based tool to characterize the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ topological invariant in 2D time-reversal-invariant systems. It achieves this by deriving the marker from the one-particle density matrix and systematically optimizing an auxiliary operator $S$ to ensure the marker remains well-defined even in the presence of disorder. This provides a powerful, practical diagnostic tool for condensed matter physics.

Why it may be interesting. The development of a local, measurable marker for topological invariants in disordered systems is crucial for understanding real-world materials where perfect crystalline order is absent, bridging abstract topology with measurable physical quantities.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to develop a practical, real-space, and state-based local topological marker for characterizing topological phases in two-dimensional time-reversal-invariant systems.

Main result. A local Chern-Simons descendant marker is derived directly from the one-particle density matrix, providing a robust $\mathbb{Z}_2$ invariant that remains well-defined even when the system is disordered or when simple commutation relations are not met.

Method. The marker is obtained via dimensional reduction from an auxiliary three-dimensional state, requiring the identification and optimization of a local Hermitian involution $S$ that maximizes the spectral gap of an associated operator $\Gamma$.

Model / system. The analysis focuses on two-dimensional time-reversal-invariant near-Gaussian states, utilizing the one-particle density matrix $\rho$ as the primary input. The method is tested in models exhibiting strong Rashba and Dresselhaus spin-orbit coupling.

Key observables. Local Chern-Simons descendant marker, $\mathbb{Z}_2$ invariant, spectral gap of $\Gamma$, one-particle density matrix ($\rho$).

Important parameters / regimes. The optimal angle $\theta$ used to define the involution $S$, which is shown to be robust across different physical regimes.

Assumptions / limitations. The derivation relies on the properties of Gaussian states and the assumption that the spectral gap condition on $\Gamma$ is the key requirement for the marker's definition, rather than exact commutation $[S, \rho]=0$.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the concept of local topological markers, derives the Chern-Simons descendant marker through dimensional reduction, and details the systematic procedure for selecting and optimizing the auxiliary operator $S$ by maximizing the spectral gap of $\Gamma$.

Abstract

Local topological markers provide a real-space characterization of topological phases and are suited to noncrystalline models. We derive a local topological marker, the Chern-Simons descendant marker, for characterizing the topology of two-dimensional time-reversal-invariant near-Gaussian states from the one-particle density matrix \(ρ\). We construct an auxiliary three-dimensional state characterized by the local Chern-Simons marker, and use dimensional reduction to obtain the Chern-Simons descendant marker for the original two-dimensional state. This derivation introduces a local Hermitian involution \(S\) that is odd under time reversal. When \([S,ρ]=0\), the local Chern-Simons descendant marker reduces to half the difference of the local Chern markers in the \(S=\pm1\) sectors. Constructing an \(S\) that commutes exactly with \(ρ\) generally requires fine-tuning, but this is not needed---the local Chern-Simon marker remains well defined whenever \(Γ=\{ρ,S\}-S\) has a spectral gap around zero, even when \([S,ρ]\neq0\). This leaves the practical problem of identifying a suitable \(S\) for a given state, which, importantly, is straightforward even when spin is not a candidate. We show this by considering both strong Rashba and Dresselhaus spin-orbit coupling, for which knowledge of the physical origin of the state reduces the general problem of choosing $S$ to a one-parameter family \(S(θ)\). We evaluate the optimal angle $θ$ maximizing the spectral gap of $Γ$ analytically assuming translation invariance, and find that the gap of $Γ$ remains open for a broad range of angles around the optimum. The same value of \(θ\) remains valid in disordered systems, providing an easy way to obtain choice of \(S\) for evaluating the local Chern-Simons descendant marker of the state.

Summary. This work analyzes Weyl semimetals exhibiting a Matrix Density Wave order, showing that internal flavor degrees of freedom induce complex topological defects. Abstract

A conventional interaction-driven Weyl density wave is a complex scalar and supports vortex lines, but no topologically stable point defects. We show that two Weyl flavors do not merely duplicate this order. Starting from the matrix-valued internode coherence, a local flavor-symmetric repulsion selects a traceless adjoint condensate within the density-wave sector, while the fermionic ground-state energy locks its complex components into the collinear form $\mathbfΔ=Φ_0\mathbf{n} e^{iθ}$. The resulting order-parameter manifold, $(S^2\times S^1)/\mathbb Z_2$, supports both unit hedgehogs and half-quantum Alice strings of the same electronic mass that gaps the Weyl fermions. An elementary hedgehog binds a single normalizable zero mode whose empty and occupied sectors carry charges $-e/2$ and $+e/2$ at neutrality. An elementary Alice string carries a single chiral electronic mode despite its $π$ phase winding, and transporting a hedgehog around it reverses the hedgehog winding, $N\rightarrow-N$. Flavor therefore converts spontaneous translation breaking into a route to point-defect fractionalization and intertwines the point- and line-defect sectors of an interaction-generated electronic mass.

Authors: Vincent Lahoche, Dine Ousmane Samary, Parham Radpay
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.28032v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5

Summary. This paper introduces a relational background field formulation as a new Renormalization Group technique for discrete quantum gravity models, particularly random matrix theories. By utilizing the $O(N)$ model, the authors show how this method preserves gauge symmetry while defining a stable RG flow. The key finding is the existence of an asymptotic Wilson-Fisher fixed point, offering a controlled path to the continuum limit.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on quantum gravity, the rigorous application of RG techniques, fixed-point analysis, and symmetry preservation in the large-N limit provides advanced theoretical tools applicable to critical phenomena in complex many-body systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Developing a reliable Renormalization Group (RG) approach for discrete quantum gravity models while preserving the underlying gauge symmetry and achieving a satisfactory continuum limit.

Main result. The authors demonstrate the existence of an asymptotic Wilson-Fisher-type fixed point using a novel relational background field formulation.

Method. The method employs a relational background field formulation combined with a partial Hubbard-Stratonovich decomposition to define an RG flow based on the BBP phase transition.

Model / system. The study focuses on random matrix models, specifically the $O(N)$ vector model in zero dimensions, which serves as a pedagogical review of the general construction.

Key observables. Asymptotic Wilson-Fisher fixed point, relevant critical exponent, free energy singularity exponent (gamma=1/2).

Important parameters / regimes. Large N limit, $O(N)$ symmetry, BBP phase transition, dimension $D=2k+3$.

Assumptions / limitations. The method relies on the self-averaging property for large random matrices and is presented as a review of the construction.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the relational background field concept, reviews its application to the $O(N)$ model, details the use of the BBP transition to define the RG flow, and analyzes the resulting fixed point structure.

Abstract

In a recent paper arxiv.org/abs/2609.21031, we have introduced the concept of a relational background field as a new approach to the renormalization group (RG) for discrete quantum gravity models, focusing on random matrix models. The proposed method offers the dual advantage of preserving the model's gauge symmetry (typically $U(N)$ or $O(N)$), whose breaking in conventional approaches has remained an open problem, and providing a more satisfactory treatment of the continuum or infrared (IR) limit. In this limit, the theory is described by a non-local field theory in dimension $D = 2k + 3$, where $k$ denotes the number of cuts in the effective spectrum as dictated by random matrix theory. This pedagogical paper reviews this construction for real random vector models in zero dimensions, with $O(N)$ as the symmetry group. Following the general strategy of background-field-type formulations, we show, using the BBP (Baik-Ben Arrous-P'ech'e) phase transition, that a partial Hubbard--Stratonovich decomposition of the quartic interaction, involving a matrix-valued intermediate field, allows a preferred notion of scale to emerge. This scale, in turn, defines an RG flow, whose perturbative structure is analyzed. Our calculation demonstrates the existence of an \textit{asymptotic} Wilson--Fisher-type fixed point, whose sole relevant critical exponent is in qualitative agreement with the double-scaling critical exponent of these models.

Strain Engineering of the Topological Gap and Tunable Chern Numbers in the 2D Kagome Metal-Organic Framework Eu$_2$(C$_6$H$_4$)$_3$

Authors: Jiaxuan Guo, Simin Nie, Fritz B. Prinz
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.27388v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical study investigates the tunable topological properties of the 2D metal-organic framework $ ext{Eu}_2( ext{C}_6 ext{H}_4)_3$. Using DFT calculations, the authors show that the material functions as a controllable Chern insulator. By applying strain, stacking, and electric fields, they demonstrate three distinct methods to manipulate both the size of the topological gap and the quantized Chern number, offering a versatile platform for quantum electronics.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a clear, multi-faceted blueprint for engineering topological electronic states using purely structural and electric field controls, which is highly relevant for designing novel quantum devices.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge addressed is improving the operating temperature and controlling the number of edge channels in the Quantum Anomalous Hall Effect (QAHE).

Main result. The study demonstrates that mechanical strain, stacking, and out-of-plane electric fields can independently tune both the topological gap and the Chern number in the single material $ ext{Eu}_2( ext{C}_6 ext{H}_4)_3$.

Method. The research relies heavily on first-principles density functional theory (DFT) calculations, supplemented by tight-binding models derived from maximally localized Wannier functions to characterize the topological invariants.

Model / system. The physical system is the two-dimensional metal-organic kagome ferromagnet $ ext{Eu}_2( ext{C}_6 ext{H}_4)_3$. The electronic properties are modeled as an intrinsic Chern insulator whose topology is sensitive to external perturbations.

Key observables. Chern number ($\mathcal{C}$), topological gap size, Hall conductance, strain ($\epsilon$), and out-of-plane electric field ($E_{ ext{out-of-plane}}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The initial SOC gap is $72.7 ext{ meV}$, which widens to $124.7 ext{ meV}$ under $-8\%$ biaxial strain. The system exhibits Chern numbers of $\mathcal{C}=-1$ (monolayer) and $\mathcal{C}=-2$ (bilayer).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the topological gap opening is primarily sourced by the hybridization between C-$p$ states and the $ ext{Eu}$ $5d$ states, and that the material remains stable under applied strain and fields.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the atomic structure, phonon dispersion, and AIMD simulations confirming stability. Band structure plots show the evolution of the topological gap under varying strain, while theoretical calculations map out the transitions between different Chern number phases.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the material's stability and intrinsic topological properties (monolayer, $C=-1$). It then systematically explores the tunability by applying mechanical strain (widening the gap) and stacking (achieving $C=-2$). Finally, it introduces an out-of-plane electric field to drive the system through multiple topological transitions.

Abstract

The quantum anomalous Hall effect carries dissipationless chiral edge currents without an external magnetic field, yet raising its operating temperature and controlling the number of edge channels remain difficult. Using first-principles calculations, we identify the two-dimensional metal-organic kagome ferromagnet Eu$_2$(C$_6$H$_4$)$_3$ as an intrinsic Chern insulator whose topological gap can be enlarged by mechanical strain. The monolayer has a spin-orbit-coupling-induced gap of 72.7 meV that widens to 124.7 meV under -8% biaxial strain, realizing a quantum anomalous Hall phase with Chern number $\mathcal{C}=-1$ and a single chiral edge state. The local moments come from the half-filled Eu $4f^{7}$ shell, while the gap-opening spin-orbit coupling is carried by Eu $5d$ states hybridized into the carbon kagome bands. The AB-stacked bilayer couples ferromagnetically and accumulates the per-layer Chern numbers, giving $\mathcal{C}=-2$ with two co-propagating chiral channels. An out-of-plane electric field then drives the bilayer through a sequence of topological transitions among $\mathcal{C}=-2$, $-3$, and $-1$, switching the number of edge channels. Together, strain, stacking, and gating give three distinct handles on the gap and the Chern number within a single stoichiometric material.
