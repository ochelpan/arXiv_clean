Resonantly Enhanced Phonon Transport by Magnon Pumping in a Ferromagnetic/Piezoelectric Bilayer

Type: experiment · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.27101v1

Summary. The researchers experimentally observed that propagating phonon transport in a ferromagnetic/piezoelectric bilayer is significantly enhanced only when the acoustic wave frequency matches the ferromagnetic resonance frequency of the magnetic film. This confirms a direct, resonant energy transfer mechanism from magnons to phonons, establishing a new method for actively controlling acoustic energy flow using magnetism.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a direct experimental realization of energy transfer between magnetic excitations (magnons) and mechanical waves (phonons) in a controlled, resonant manner, which is highly relevant for understanding coupled quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To experimentally demonstrate and investigate the phenomenon of resonantly enhanced propagating phonon transport induced by magnon pumping in a ferromagnetic/piezoelectric bilayer.

Main result. The enhancement of the transmitted acoustic signal occurs exclusively when the SAW frequency matches the ferromagnetic resonance (FMR) condition, confirming resonant magnon-phonon coupling.

Method. The study measures the SAW transmission coefficient ($S_{21}$) spectrum while sweeping the external magnetic field, comparing results on the bilayer versus a bare substrate.

Model / system. The physical system is a Co/LiNbO3 hybrid bilayer. The interaction mechanism relies on magnetoelastic coupling between propagating Surface Acoustic Waves (SAWs) and the magnetization dynamics of the Co film.

Key observables. The enhanced transmitted acoustic signal ($\Delta S_{21}$) measured as a function of the applied magnetic field and SAW harmonic.

Important parameters / regimes. The SAW frequency must satisfy the Kittel dispersion relation for the Co film's FMR condition.

Assumptions / limitations. The observed enhancement is solely due to the dynamic, resonant interaction between propagating phonons and coherent magnetization precession.

Figures summary. Figures show the device schematic, the measured SAW transmission spectrum ($S_{21}$) highlighting the resonance, and comparisons showing that only the SAW harmonic matching the FMR condition exhibits the enhancement.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the physical setup, uses FMR measurements to predict the resonance condition (Kittel fit), and then measures the SAW transmission, demonstrating that the enhancement is localized precisely at the predicted resonance field and is frequency-selective.

Abstract

We report the first experimental observation of resonantly enhanced propagating phonon transport induced by magnon pumping in a ferromagnetic/piezoelectric bilayer. Surface acoustic waves (SAWs) generated in a 128$^{\circ}$ Y-cut LiNbO$_3$ delay-line device resonantly excite magnetization dynamics in an adjacent Co film through magnetoelastic coupling. The enhancement of the transmitted acoustic signal occurs exclusively when the SAW frequency satisfies the ferromagnetic resonance condition predicted by the Kittel dispersion, providing a direct experimental fingerprint of resonant magnon-phonon coupling. A systematic comparison between the Co/LiNbO$_3$ bilayer and the bare LiNbO$_3$ substrate demonstrates that the observed transmission enhancement originates solely from the dynamic interaction between propagating phonons and coherent magnetization precession. Furthermore, measurements performed at different SAW harmonics reveal that only the harmonic satisfying the FMR condition produces a measurable enhancement, confirming the frequency-selective nature of the phenomenon. These findings establish an efficient mechanism for transferring energy from magnons to propagating phonons and provide a new strategy for actively controlling coherent acoustic transport in hybrid spintronic, straintronic, and quantum phononic platforms.

Summary. This paper investigates the speed of the virtual mediator boson in deep inelastic scattering by treating the interaction region as a dispersive medium. It develops a theoretical framework relating group and phase velocities and applies it to HERA data. The key finding is that the derived speed can exceed the speed of light and exhibits strong singularities as the virtual boson's energy approaches zero.

Why it may be interesting. While the core physics is high-energy particle physics, the methodology—using dispersion relations and velocity measurements to probe medium properties—shares conceptual overlap with how collective excitations are studied in condensed matter systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper proposes a novel method to measure the characteristics of a fermion-boson medium formed during deep inelastic electron-proton scattering by analyzing the group and phase velocities of the mediating wave process.

Main result. Analysis of HERA data suggests that the effective mediator boson's speed can exceed the speed of light in free space, and the derived model shows a strong, singular dependence on the virtual boson's energy ($q_0$) as $q_0$ approaches zero.

Method. The authors use the Rayleigh differential condition and derive expressions for group and phase velocities from a relativistic dispersion relation model, comparing these theoretical predictions against indirect measurements from HERA collider data.

Model / system. The physical system is deep inelastic $ep$ scattering, where the interaction is mediated by an effective, non-perturbative boson ($B$). The analysis utilizes a relativistic dispersion relation model ($\omega^2 = \omega_0^2 + c^2 \sum k_i^2$) to define the velocities.

Key observables. Group velocity modulus ($U^*_B$), Phase velocity ($V^*_B$), Normalized speed ($eta^*$), and the absorption mean free path ($L^*$).

Important parameters / regimes. Kinematic variables like $Q^2$, Bjorken variable $x$, and the virtual boson energy $q_0$. The analysis focuses on the limits $q_0 o 0$ and $y o 1$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the applicability of the Rayleigh condition and relies on deriving velocity estimates from modified Heisenberg uncertainty relations and specific kinematic limits.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the kinematic view of the scattering process, the dependence of the normalized speed ($eta^*$) on $q_0$ (showing divergence near $q_0=0$), and the extracted $eta^*$ vs. $Q^2$ from HERA II data.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by establishing the theoretical framework (velocity definitions, Rayleigh condition), applying this framework to the DIS process, deriving model predictions for $eta^*(q_0)$, and finally comparing these model results against actual HERA experimental data across various kinematic limits.

Abstract

We propose a new method for measuring several characteristics of a fermion-boson medium formed during deep inelastic particle scattering. This method involves measuring the group and phase velocities of the accompanying wave process. The phase and group velocities of a wave process are related by the Rayleigh differential condition. The refractive index of a medium is determined by the phase velocity, while the group velocity characterises the medium's dispersion properties. Summary. This work analyzes the Thermodynamic Uncertainty Relations in chaotic Andreev billiards, a system exhibiting particle-hole symmetry. By using RMT, the authors test how quantum interference and barrier transparency affect the TUR across various symmetry classes. They confirm that the fundamental thermodynamic bound remains satisfied, establishing TUR as a powerful symmetry-sensitive diagnostic tool for superconducting quantum transport.

Why it may be interesting. The investigation of TUR in superconducting mesoscopic systems provides a unique, symmetry-sensitive probe of universal transport, linking quantum chaos, superconductivity, and fundamental thermodynamic laws.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how particle-hole symmetry, quantum interference, and tunnel barriers influence the Thermodynamic Uncertainty Relations (TUR) in chaotic Andreev billiards.

Main result. The standard TUR bound is consistently satisfied across all investigated regimes, confirming that Andreev billiards remain compatible with fundamental thermodynamic constraints.

Method. The research employs Random Matrix Theory (RMT) and the Mahaux–Weidenmüller scattering approach to analyze transport properties.

Model / system. The system is an open chaotic Andreev billiard, modeled using the Bogoliubov-de Gennes (BdG) Hamiltonian, which inherently possesses particle-hole symmetry.

Key observables. Charge conductance, shot noise power, and the TUR observable, $Q$, which is compared using two ensemble averages ($Q_R$ and $Q_{MS}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis spans multiple regimes: the extreme quantum limit ($N=1$) to the semiclassical multichannel regime ($N=10$), and varying barrier transparencies ($\Gamma$) from ideal to opaque.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the Random Matrix Theory framework and assumes operation within the universal RMT regime ($ au_\phi, au_D \gg au_E$).

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the dependence of conductance, noise, and the TUR observable on barrier transparency ($\Gamma$) for different symmetry classes and system sizes, showing the relative contributions of quantum interference.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the TUR across different symmetry classes and transport regimes, comparing two distinct definitions of the TUR observable to probe mesoscopic fluctuations and the role of quantum interference corrections.

Abstract

We investigate how particle-hole symmetry, quantum interference, and tunnel barriers shape thermodynamic uncertainty relations in chaotic Andreev billiards. Using random-matrix theory and the Mahaux-Weidenmüller scattering approach, we study charge conductance and shot noise across the four Altland-Zirnbauer symmetry classes, from the single-channel extreme quantum limit to the multichannel semiclassical regime and from ideal to opaque contacts. We characterize thermodynamic precision through two complementary ensemble observables: either by averaging the sample-resolved noise-to-conductance ratio, or from the separately averaging noise and conductance. Their pronounced discrepancy in the extreme quantum regime reveals the non-self-averaging character of mesoscopic transport and persists over a broad range of barrier transparencies. For ideal contacts, the first provides a sensitive fingerprint of the Altland-Zirnbauer symmetry class. In the opaque regime, this hierarchy changes. Despite these strong symmetry- and barrier-dependent effects, the standard thermodynamic uncertainty relation remains satisfied throughout all regimes investigated. Our results establish thermodynamic uncertainty as a symmetry-sensitive probe of universal transport in chaotic normal-superconducting systems.

Summary. This work proposes using correlated quasiperiodic nanostructures to boost thermoelectric energy conversion efficiency. By modeling a 1D chain with an AAH-type potential, the authors show that the resulting non-uniform electronic transmission spectrum is crucial. This engineered disorder allows for high ZT values, surpassing those achievable in simpler or perfectly periodic systems.

Why it may be interesting. The core mechanism relies on engineering the electronic density of states via quasiperiodicity, a concept deeply related to localization phenomena studied in disordered systems, making it relevant to understanding transport in complex, aperiodic materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates methods to enhance thermoelectric energy conversion efficiency in nanoscale systems by engineering the electronic energy landscape.

Main result. Correlated quasiperiodic potentials, specifically weakly varying Aubry-André-Harper (AAH) potentials, can induce highly non-uniform electronic transmission spectra, leading to a figure of merit (ZT) exceeding 2.

Method. The study employs the tight-binding framework combined with the non-equilibrium Green's function (NEGF) formalism and the Landauer approach to calculate transport coefficients.

Model / system. The model is a one-dimensional quantum chain coupled to source and drain reservoirs, featuring a modulated on-site energy potential belonging to the AAH family. The potential is characterized by an incommensurability parameter ($ u$).

Key observables. Figure of Merit (ZT), electrical conductance (G), Seebeck coefficient (S), electronic thermal conductance ($\kappa_e$), and phonon thermal conductance ($\kappa_{ph}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Incommensurability factor ($ u$), disorder amplitude ($W$), Fermi energy ($E_F$), and temperature ($T$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is performed in the linear response regime, assuming small temperature differences ($\Delta T$), and includes detailed analysis of phonon contributions.

Figures summary. Figures compare the electronic transmission spectrum ($ au(E)$) for perfect ($ u=0$) versus correlated ($ u=0.55$) lattices, showing the latter yields necessary asymmetry for high ZT. Other figures plot ZT vs. $E_F$, $ u$, and $T$.

Paper structure. The paper systematically introduces the Hamiltonian and methods (NEGF/Landauer). It compares the results for perfect vs. correlated disorder, demonstrating that the non-uniformity induced by correlated disorder is key to maximizing ZT. Finally, it analyzes the dependence of ZT on coupling asymmetry and temperature.

Abstract

We investigate a route to enhanced thermoelectric energy conversion in nanoscale systems by exploiting a correlated quasiperiodic energy landscape in a one-dimensional chain coupled to source and drain reservoirs. The considered modulation generates a highly non-uniform electronic transmission spectrum, providing favorable conditions for achieving a large thermoelectric figure of merit. By systematically tuning the incommensurability parameter, a variety of quasiperiodic configurations are explored, several of which yield high values of the figure of merit exceeding $2$. Electronic transport properties are evaluated within a tight-binding framework using the non-equilibrium Green's function formalism, while the thermoelectric coefficients, including electrical conductance, Seebeck coefficient, and electronic thermal conductance, are determined through the Landauer approach. The underlying quasiperiodic potential belongs to the Aubry-André-Harper (AAH) family and exhibits a weakly varying spatial profile, leading to transmission characteristics that are favorable for thermoelectric optimization. The influence of phonon thermal conductance on the overall energy-conversion efficiency is also analyzed in detail. For the sake of completeness, we also critically inspect the effect of conductor to electrode coupling and the coupling asymmetry on $ZT$. We also check the thermoelectric response of conventional AAH system and an elaborate comparison is made with our chosen quantum system. Our findings highlight the potential of correlated quasiperiodic nanostructures as promising candidates for efficient thermoelectric applications.

Angular Modulations in Magnetic Torque Induced by Phase Transitions in the Triangular Supersolid $2H$-AgNiO$_2$

Summary. This work uses angle- and field-dependent magnetic torque measurements on $2H$-AgNiO$_2$ to map out its complex magnetic phase diagram. By comparing these experimental results with theoretical simulations, the authors confirm the existence of multiple field-induced transitions, providing strong evidence for a magnetic supersolid phase in this anisotropic material.

Why it may be interesting. The study provides a concrete, measurable platform (magnetic torque) to probe exotic quantum phases like supersolids in a strongly correlated magnetic insulator, linking macroscopic measurements to microscopic spin models.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the complex magnetic phase transitions, particularly the potential supersolid phase, in the triangular lattice antiferromagnet $2H$-AgNiO$_2$.

Main result. The angular and field-dependent magnetic torque measurements reveal characteristic sawtooth signals and multiple anomalies, which are qualitatively explained by crossing different predicted phase boundaries.

Method. The research combines detailed experimental measurements of magnetic torque ($ au$) with theoretical modeling using mean-field and Monte-Carlo simulations on a minimal spin model.

Model / system. The physical system is the delafossite $2H$-AgNiO$_2$, which features localized $S=1$ moments on a triangular lattice interacting with itinerant electrons. The theory uses a minimal easy-axis $J_1-J_2$ spin model.

Key observables. Magnetic torque ($ au$) as a function of angle ($ heta$) and applied field ($H$), and the identification of multiple field-induced anomalies ($H_1$ to $H_4$).

Important parameters / regimes. Critical temperatures ($T_N \approx 19$ K), applied fields up to 45 T, and anisotropy parameters ($D/J_1$).

Assumptions / limitations. The theoretical analysis relies on a minimal spin model and semi-classical approximations, potentially neglecting quadrupolar moments or itinerant electron effects for certain anomalies.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the magnetic ordering (CAF, SS1, SS2, etc.) and show the evolution of the angular dependence of the torque ($ au$) at various temperatures and fields, highlighting kinks and anomalies corresponding to phase boundaries.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by presenting experimental torque measurements across varying fields and angles, correlating the observed anomalies with theoretical phase diagrams constructed via mean-field and Monte-Carlo simulations to map out the complex magnetic phase space.

Abstract

Easy-axis frustrated triangular lattice antiferromagnets provide an important playground for stabilising a wide range of exotic magnetic phases. The delafossite $2H$-AgNiO$_2$ offers a unique model system containing a lattice of localised ($S=1$) moments surrounded by a honeycomb of itinerant electrons. Below $T_{\rm N} =19$ K, the system exhibits collinear stripe antiferromagnetic order, whereas applied magnetic fields induce a cascade of transitions which has been proposed to contain a magnetic supersolid phase. In this study, we perform detailed angular and field-dependent torque measurements in static fields of up to 45~T on single crystal samples. The angular dependence of the torque displays a sawtooth-shaped signal close to the first supersolid phase, while near $T_{\rm N}$, an unexpected additional modulation emerges. On the other hand, the field dependence of the torque indicates the presence of four field-induced anomalies which evolve in distinct ways as a function of field orientation. To understand these complex behaviours and assess the role of local moments, we employ mean-field and Monte-Carlo calculations on a minimal spin model and construct comprehensive polar phase diagrams at different temperatures. We find that the angular dependence of the torque can be understood in terms of crossing different phase boundaries by field rotation, and we identify qualitative similarities between the experimental and theoretical polar phase diagrams. Our study highlights $2H$-AgNiO$_2$ as a rich anisotropic system that provides a robust example of how spin supersolid orders can manifest in angle-dependent magnetic torque.

Summary. This paper presents a novel discrete diffusion model utilizing Variational Autoregressive Networks (VAN) to accurately parameterize normalized probability distributions for spin systems. By applying this framework to the 2D and 3D Ising models, the authors successfully compute key thermodynamic quantities across different phases. This establishes a powerful, scalable neural-network approach for statistical mechanics simulations.

Why it may be interesting. The integration of advanced deep learning (autoregressive networks) with established statistical physics tools (Ising models, MCMC) provides a powerful, generalizable computational framework for analyzing complex many-body systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the need for a discrete diffusion model capable of parameterizing normalized probability distributions, overcoming limitations in both conventional score-based models and low-dimensional tensor-network approaches.

Main result. They introduce a framework using Variational Autoregressive Networks (VAN) that successfully models normalized distributions for spin systems on higher-dimensional lattices, allowing for accurate computation of thermodynamic quantities.

Method. The core method involves training a VAN to approximate the target Boltzmann distribution, governing the forward noising and reverse denoising dynamics via explicit Markov jump operators.

Model / system. The model is applied to spin systems, specifically the two- and three-dimensional Ising model, whose target distribution follows the Boltzmann distribution $P(\sigma) \propto e^{-eta E(\sigma)}$.

Key observables. Free energy, energy, and magnetization per site.

Important parameters / regimes. Inverse temperature $eta$ (spanning ordered, critical, and disordered regimes); lattice dimensions (2D and 3D).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is the use of the variational autoregressive factorization $P_ heta(\sigma) = \prod_{i=1}^D P_ heta(\sigma_i | \sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_{i-1})$ to decompose the joint probability.

Figures summary. Figure 1 contrasts the proposed normalized VAN model against standard unnormalized diffusion models. Figure 2 provides an overview of the framework, detailing training, denoising strategies, and MCMC integration.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the theoretical framework by defining the VAN-based discrete diffusion model, details the dynamics using Markov jump operators, applies it to the Ising model across various regimes, and integrates the method with MCMC sampling techniques.

Abstract

Conventional score-based diffusion models learn scores without representing normalized densities, whereas tractable normalized models support both sampling and direct likelihood evaluation. A recent tensor-network approach provides such a representation but is largely restricted to low-dimensional lattices. Here we introduce a discrete diffusion model that parameterizes normalized probability distributions using variational autoregressive networks. Explicit Markov jump operators govern the forward noising and reverse denoising dynamics, extending discrete diffusion models with normalized distributions to spin systems on higher-dimensional lattices. We apply this framework to the two- and three-dimensional Ising models across ordered, critical, and disordered regimes, accurately computing thermodynamic quantities including free energy, energy, and magnetization. We further integrate the framework with Monte Carlo sampling, using adaptive diffusion steps to maintain high acceptance rates even at low temperatures while enhancing sample diversity. These results establish a neural-network framework for the discrete diffusion model with normalized probability distributions.

Summary. This paper proposes a novel quantization procedure for non-relativistic quantum mechanics by reformulating classical dynamics using a 'state language' based on free semigroups. It successfully derives the Schrödinger equation and the Born rule by analyzing the geometry of phase space states and path integral amplitudes. The work offers a mathematically rigorous, foundational approach to bridging classical and quantum descriptions.

Why it may be interesting. The deep mathematical restructuring of classical dynamics using semigroup theory and the derivation of fundamental quantum rules like the Born rule from first principles make it highly relevant for foundational quantum theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the quantization of non-relativistic phase space states by developing a formalism based on the free semigroup contribution of time, aiming to replace the principle of stationary action.

Main result. The formalism successfully derives the Lagrangian and the Schrödinger equation from a state-vector approach, and it derives the Born rule by comparing path integral amplitudes with density matrix calculations.

Method. The methodology involves introducing a 'state language/Dirac notation' for classical mechanics, dividing quantization into two stages (operator action and Planck scale consideration), and utilizing path integrals with time-slicing.

Model / system. The model is non-relativistic quantum mechanics applied to a general 1D particle system, utilizing phase space concepts, state vectors, and path integrals. The Hamiltonian structure is central to the analysis.

Key observables. The solid angle ($\Omega$) between states, the probability $p$ (for the Born rule), and the state vectors themselves ($|q angle, |p angle$).

Important parameters / regimes. The Planck length ($\hbar$), the free semigroup time contribution, and the scale relative to $\hbar$ (quantum vs. classical limits).

Assumptions / limitations. The formalism assumes the structure of time contribution as a free semigroup and relies on specific conventions for state definitions and operator ordering.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the Legendre transformation structure, the quantum limit of phase space states, and the quasi-classical limit of phase space states.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by establishing a state language for classical mechanics, deriving the Lagrangian, analyzing the solid angle in phase space, deriving the path integral propagator, and finally applying the formalism to derive the Born rule.

Abstract

Quantisation is considered as an action of classical point operators on field states at the Planck scale of the phase space. Emphasis is placed on the sequence of physical variables. A "state language/Dirac notation" for classical mechanics is introduced; in many cases, it replaces the principle of stationary action for conservative systems. Observed classical and quantum dynamics are considered as different vectorisations of the free semigroup contribution of time. There is a dream of describing classical dynamics as complex transformations of state vectors over such a free semigroup, rather than solving differential equations. The transition from the alphabet to states, from states to state functions, and from state functions to measurable quantities is managed by projectors. An attempt is made to provide a derivation of the Legendre transformation. The trivial multiplication of the potential energy by the wave function in the coordinate representation and its non-trivial operator action in the momentum representation are obtained without a Fourier transform nor the Planck scale. The quantisation procedure is divided into two stages: first, the action of point-like classical operators on field functions; and second, accounting for the physically finite scale of the Planck length against the backdrop of the possible infinitesimality in the classical phase space. The concept of an angle between states is introduced, defining the measure of "quantumness". The free semigroup time contribution is studied in the context of the Born rule.

Abstract

Quantum Rabi model (QRM) is a fundamental model in light-matter interactions (LMIs), whereas this finite-component system manifests infinite physics to be explored and a full understanding has not yet been reached after a hundred years since it was proposed. In the present work we create a geometric perspective for the QRM by mapping to a curved nanowire system, with curvature-induced spin-orbit coupling (SOC) manipulatable by LMI strength and frequency ratio, which is capable of revealing and perceiving the property universality and diversity from the ground state to the whole spectrum in a unified picture. Indeed, we unveil a super university in the adiabatic regime which manifests a universal scaling of spin texture ubiquitously valid for different couplings, different frequencies and different energy levels. The curve frame also facilitates our finding that a direction changeover of tangential spin swing emerges in the non-adiabatic spin texture in association with the quantum phase transition (QPT) in the QRM, which endows a geometric non-adiabaticity interpretation and connotation to the QPT. We also clarify that, among the curvature-induced effects, the SOC accounts for the spectral bifurcation and oscillations. Our method and analysis, readily generalized to other light-matter models, opens a dialogue between geometry and physics, thus paving a novel way to gain deeper insight into LMIs.

Abstract

Exciton condensates are long-sought correlated quantum states arising from macroscopic coherence of bound electron-hole pairs. Although equilibrium and transient excitonic states have been reported in several material platforms, direct evidence for a light-induced exciton condensate state has been challenging to achieve as an intrinsic property of a bulk quantum material. Here, we use time- and angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy to investigate long-lived photoexcited carriers in the intrinsic magnetic topological insulator MnBi$_2$Te$_4$ with the chemical potential tuned to the topological surface state by Sb substitution. Following optical excitation, we observe the delayed emergence of a transient state whose formation coincides with depopulation of the bulk conduction band and whose lifetime extends to the microsecond timescale. The quasiparticle dispersion exhibits a pronounced flattening and develops a Mexican-hat-like profile. These spectral signatures are consistent with the formation of a metastable excitonic condensate state. Our results establish magnetic topological insulators as a promising platform for investigating long-lived photoinduced many-body states and their interplay with topology and magnetism.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper evaluates how Hellinger-distance based coherence in two-flavor neutrino oscillations responds to weak decoherence induced by Lindblad damping.

Main result. The Hellinger coherence exhibits a nonanalytic $\sqrt{\epsilon}$ correction in the weak-damping limit, contrasting with other quantum measures that respond linearly in the damping parameter $\epsilon$.

Method. The analysis involves deriving closed-form expressions for various coherence measures and comparing the results obtained from the weak-damping expansion ($\epsilon o 0$) against the exact solutions.

Model / system. The system is two-flavor neutrino oscillations, modeled using a density matrix $ ho$ subjected to Lindblad damping in the mass basis, characterized by a damping factor $\kappa = e^{-\Gamma L}$.

Key observables. Hellinger-distance coherence ($C_H$), flavor-transition probability, $l_1$ coherence, concurrence, entanglement of formation, and local quantum Fisher information.

Important parameters / regimes. The weak-damping parameter $\epsilon = 1 - \kappa$, and oscillation parameters derived from Daya Bay, KamLAND, and MINOS working points.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the two-flavor vacuum treatment with negligible wave-packet separation, and the weak-damping expansion is only valid near $\epsilon = 0$.

Abstract

We evaluate the Hellinger-distance coherence of the two-flavor neutrino state subject to Lindblad damping of the mass-eigenstate interference term, and obtain a closed expression in terms of the flavor-basis density-matrix elements. In the two-flavor vacuum treatment with negligible wave-packet separation, the undamped state is pure, and the smallest eigenvalue of the damped state grows linearly with $ε=1-κ$, where $κ=e^{-ΓL}$. An expansion about the pure state then yields a nonanalytic $\sqrtε$ correction to the Hellinger coherence, with coefficient $K=2\sqrt{2}\,u\sin2θ/\sqrt{1-2u}$, whereas the flavor-transition probability, the $l_1$ coherence, the concurrence, the entanglement of formation and the local quantum Fisher information all respond linearly in $ε$. Using representative Daya Bay, KamLAND and MINOS working points together with a current $90\%$ C.L. bound on energy-independent damping, we find fractional changes in the Hellinger coherence of $0.18\%$, $9.40\%$ and $21.51\%$, against $0.0009\%$, $0.43\%$ and $1.81\%$ for the concurrence. The comparison concerns the response of the quantifiers to the damping parameter and carries no implication about experimental resolution. The same mechanism operates, with a different coefficient, for a correlated dephasing channel acting on the flavor coherence, where the non-Markovian regime produces damped revivals.
