Generalised entanglement wedges in three dimensions

Authors: Abhisek Sahu
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.07176v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical paper explores the origin of entanglement properties in generalized entanglement wedges (GWs) within the context of AdS/CFT. By using integral geometry in AdS$_3$, the authors demonstrate that the strong subadditivity of GWs is mathematically guaranteed by the strong subadditivity of the boundary CFT entropy. This reinforces the idea that bulk geometric structures are dictated by the underlying quantum field theory.

Why it may be interesting. The connection between bulk geometric properties (GWs) and boundary quantum information properties (CFT entropy) is a cornerstone of modern quantum gravity, providing a concrete realization of the holographic principle.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates whether the fundamental properties of Generalized Entanglement Wedges (GWs) in general spacetimes, such as strong subadditivity, originate from the structure of the dual Conformal Field Theory (CFT) in the AdS/CFT correspondence.

Main result. In vacuum AdS$_3$, the strong subadditivity of GWs is shown to be a direct consequence of the strong subadditivity of the boundary CFT von Neumann entropy, suggesting a deep holographic link.

Method. The authors employ integral geometry, specifically the Crofton formula, to relate the perimeter of a bulk region to a measure on the space of geodesics, which is then connected to boundary entanglement entropies.

Model / system. The analysis is performed in vacuum Anti-de Sitter spacetime (AdS$_3$) on a static slice, utilizing the AdS/CFT correspondence where the bulk geometry is dual to a 2D CFT on the boundary.

Key observables. Generalized Entropy ($S_{ ext{gen}}$), von Neumann entropies of CFT intervals, and the perimeter of the geodesic convex regions.

Important parameters / regimes. Leading order in $G$ (Newton's constant), and the geometry of $ ext{AdS}_3$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is restricted to vacuum $ ext{AdS}_3$ and assumes the validity of the Crofton formula and the AdS/CFT dictionary for the leading order terms.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the characterization theorem, showing examples of regions that qualify as GWs based on convexity and perimeter relations, and relating bulk regions to the kinematic space $\Gamma$.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the characterization theorem for GWs in $ ext{AdS}_3$, then uses integral geometry to relate bulk perimeters to boundary entropy measures, and finally proves the strong subadditivity of GWs by leveraging the known strong subadditivity of the boundary CFT entropy.

Abstract

Generalized entanglement wedges (GWs), proposed by Bousso and Penington for gravitating regions in general spacetimes, share many properties of ordinary entanglement wedges, including nesting, monotonicity and strong subadditivity of their generalized entropies. In AdS/CFT, the corresponding properties of entanglement wedges are consequences of the structure of the dual CFT. In this paper we ask whether the properties of GWs have a similar origin. We explore this question in vacuum AdS$_3$ at leading order in $G$. We first show that every GW on a static slice is a union of geodesically convex regions. Each such region is an intersection of ordinary entanglement wedges, so every GW is built from unions and intersections of entanglement wedges. We then use the Crofton formula of integral geometry to write the perimeter of a convex region as the measure of the set of geodesics that intersect it. In AdS$_3$ every geodesic is the Ryu--Takayanagi surface of a boundary interval, so this measure can be written in terms of the von Neumann entropies of CFT intervals. Strong subadditivity of the CFT entropy guarantees that the measure is positive. Monotonicity and strong subadditivity of GWs then follow from simple inequalities between sets of geodesics. The strong subadditivity of GWs is therefore a direct consequence of strong subadditivity on the boundary. Our results further suggest that GWs correspond to subsystems of the CFT that are more general than boundary subregions.

Gradual Localization of Itinerant Cobalt 3d Electrons in the Pyrochlore Ferromagnetic Metal LuInCo4

Summary. This work uses neutron diffraction on LuInCo4 to characterize its magnetic state, confirming a collinear ferromagnetism. Key findings include evidence for a gradual localization of Co-3d electrons upon cooling, which the authors propose enhances the residual ferromagnetic instability. This mechanism is suggested to explain the unusual, anisotropic metamagnetic transition observed at low temperatures.

Why it may be interesting. The concept of electron localization driving a magnetic transition is central to strongly correlated electron physics, providing a concrete experimental platform to test theories of itinerant magnetism beyond simple mean-field models.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the microscopic magnetic state and the origin of the low-temperature metamagnetic transition in the itinerant-electron ferromagnet LuInCo4.

Main result. The magnetic structure is confirmed to be a collinear ferromagnet, and the metamagnetic transition is proposed to arise from a ferromagnetic instability enhanced by the gradual localization of itinerant Co-3d electrons.

Method. The research employed powder and single-crystal neutron diffraction measurements, supplemented by analysis of diffuse magnetic scattering intensity evolution.

Model / system. The physical system is LuInCo4, a cobalt-based metallic pyrochlore compound exhibiting itinerant-electron ferromagnetism. The focus is on the interplay between electron localization and magnetic ordering in this lattice structure.

Key observables. Ordered Co magnetic moment ($\mu_{ ext{Co}}$), diffuse magnetic scattering intensity evolution, and the critical field ($H_m$) dependence on temperature and field direction.

Important parameters / regimes. The ordered moment is found to be around $0.9 \ \mu_{ ext{B}}$ at zero field, and the metamagnetic transition is observed below 100 K.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the observed diffuse scattering broadening is indicative of a gradual localization of the Co-3d electron character, which enhances the magnetic instability.

Figures summary. Figures show PND profiles confirming a $\mathbf{q}=0$ collinear ferromagnetism, while diffuse scattering maps illustrate the temperature-dependent broadening of intensity towards higher q-space regions.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by first establishing the basic magnetic structure using zero-field neutron diffraction, then analyzing the temperature dependence of the magnetic moments and diffuse scattering to pinpoint the mechanism (electron localization), and finally connecting this mechanism to the anomalous metamagnetic transition.

Abstract

We investigated the microscopic magnetic state of the itinerant-electron ferromagnet LuInCo4 with a cobalt pyrochlore lattice by means of powder and single-crystal neutron diffraction measurements. The powder neutron diffraction profiles at zero field are well described by a simple q = 0 collinear ferromagnetic structure with an ordered Co moment of 0.9 uB. Single-crystal neutron diffraction experiments further suggest the absence of both antiferromagnetic order and long-period helical magnetic modulation. In addition, diffuse magnetic scattering was observed around q = 0, with its intensity distribution gradually broadened in reciprocal space upon cooling. Based on these observations, we propose a possible microscopic origin of the low-temperature metamagnetic transition in terms of a ferromagnetic instability enhanced by gradual localization of the itinerant Co-3d electrons. These results suggest that LuInCo4 provides a model system for investigating the interplay between electron localization and itinerant-electron ferromagnetism in cobalt-based metallic pyrochlore compounds.

Neural networks as decision trees: an analytical solution for learning and neural selectivity

Authors: Hugo Tissot, Jonas Ranft, Yves Boubenec
Type: theory · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.08228v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper provides an analytical framework showing that nonlinear neural networks effectively decompose the task they learn into a set of local, piecewise linear regressions defined over distinct activation regions. This structure is mathematically equivalent to a decision tree, suggesting that network selectivity is organized modularly based on these regions. The work establishes activation regions as a unifying concept for understanding network computation.

Why it may be interesting. The explicit mathematical link between optimization principles (least-squares) and the structural organization (decision trees) of high-dimensional representations is relevant for understanding how complex physical systems, including quantum information processing, might naturally organize their computational subspaces.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to understand how the geometry of internal representations in nonlinear neural networks is determined by the specific tasks they are learning.

Main result. The network's computation decomposes into a piecewise least-squares structure across distinct activation regions, which can be interpreted as a decision tree.

Method. An analytical framework is developed for gradient-aligned learning in piecewise-linear networks, deriving stationarity conditions based on regional gradient contributions.

Model / system. The model analyzed is a piecewise-linear feedforward and recurrent neural network, whose activity is partitioned into 'activation regions' ($S_r$) defined by the fixed pattern of active/inactive hidden units.

Key observables. Activation regions ($S_r$), regional deviation ($\Delta_r$), selectivity geometry ($\Sigma$), and the resulting decision-tree structure.

Important parameters / regimes. The per-region training loss ($\ell_r$) and the network's baseline activity level, which regulates representation diversity.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the existence of a stationary point in gradient-aligned learning and that the regional input data ($X_r$) has full row rank for the least-squares decomposition.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the decomposition into a tree structure (Fig. 2) and show that the network's implemented map converges toward the local least-squares solution as training error decreases (Fig. 3).

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by establishing the analytical framework for gradient decomposition, showing the resulting piecewise least-squares approximation, interpreting this structure as a decision tree, and finally validating the prediction of selectivity geometry using task statistics.

Abstract

Nonlinear neural networks develop structured internal representations, yet how their geometry is determined by the tasks being learned remains poorly understood. Here, we develop an analytical framework for piecewise-linear feedforward and recurrent networks that links learning, activation-region structure, and neural selectivity. We show that, at any stationary point of gradient-aligned learning, a nonlinear network decomposes into local linear regressions over its activation regions. Deviations from the corresponding least-squares solutions are jointly constrained by network weights shared across regions and vanish in the low-error regime, yielding an approximate piecewise least-squares decomposition of the task. This structure admits a decision-tree interpretation, which we recover numerically by fitting trees to predict network activation patterns from the input. We further derive how the regions in which neurons are active determine the task statistics they capture, thereby organizing neural selectivity into distinct subpopulations. The resulting predictions closely match the selectivity geometry observed in simulated networks and two empirical neural datasets. Finally, we show that neural baseline regulates activation pattern diversity, placing networks along a continuum between coarse, generalizing representations and fine-grained, expressive representations. Together, these results establish activation regions as a unifying framework for describing how nonlinear networks decompose task structure into local computations and how those computations can be revealed through neural activity.

Pauli Decomposition by Character Theory: A Memory-Bounded Algorithm for Qubits and Qudits

Authors: Mohammadreza Khellat, Mohammad Masoumi, Saman Nasoori, Soroush Nasoori
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.08299v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper presents a novel, memory-efficient algorithm for performing Pauli decomposition on quantum operators acting on qubits and qudits. By leveraging character theory and the Fast Walsh-Hadamard Transform, the authors achieve a fast computational scaling while crucially avoiding the memory bottleneck associated with storing the full exponential operator matrix. This advancement is vital for implementing large-scale quantum simulations and algorithms.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a highly efficient, mathematically rigorous tool for analyzing quantum circuits and Hamiltonians, which is fundamental for simulating complex quantum dynamics in quantum information and computation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of efficiently decomposing a general quantum operator into the Pauli string basis, which is crucial for many quantum algorithms.

Main result. They developed a fast, memory-bounded algorithm using character theory (or DFT for qudits) to compute the Pauli coefficients, achieving a complexity of O(n 4^n) while avoiding the materialization of the full exponential-sized operator matrix.

Method. The core method involves applying Fourier analysis (specifically the Walsh-Hadamard transform for qubits) on an abelian shift-character lattice derived from the Pauli group structure.

Model / system. The theory applies to quantum computation involving n qubits (generalizable to qudits) and focuses on decomposing an arbitrary operator T acting on this system.

Key observables. The Pauli coefficients $d_{x,z}$ that define the decomposition $T = \sum d_{x,z} P(x, z)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The number of qubits/qudits ($n$), the dimension ($p$), and the computational complexity ($O(n 4^n)$).

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes the structure of the Pauli group as a central extension and relies on the mathematical framework of character theory and DFT.

Figures summary. Figure 4 illustrates the performance benchmarking, showing active worker-thread count over time for different numbers of workers, comparing achieved speedup against theoretical limits.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the scientific problem (Pauli decomposition), develops the mathematical framework using character theory and DFT, presents the efficient algorithm for both qubits and qudits, and concludes with performance benchmarking and generalization discussions.

Abstract

Pauli decomposition underpins Trotterization, phase estimation, sparse-Hamiltonian simulation, and variational observables, at exponential naive cost. From the character theory of $(\mathbb{Z}_2)^n$, characters $χ_z(v)=(-1)^{\langle v,z\rangle}$ are the diagonal Pauli strings, so any diagonal operator is already Pauli-decomposed. A general operator still needs the shifts: each $X^{\otimes x}$ couples to a character $χ_z=Z^{\otimes z}$, and the products $X^{\otimes x}Z^{\otimes z}$ -- up to phases in $\{\pm 1,\pm i\}$ -- form the Pauli group, the central extension of $(\mathbb{Z}_2)^n\times(\mathbb{Z}_2)^n$ by that phase group fixed by the symplectic pairing. A section of that extension remains a choice: the bare products $X^{\otimes x}Z^{\otimes z}$, or the genuine operators that insert $Y=iXZ$ at every qubit where $x$ and $z$ overlap. As Fourier analysis on an abelian shift-character lattice (a natural DFT), this formulation is not qubit-bound: $(\mathbb{Z}_p)^n$ recovers Heisenberg-Weyl qudits. The fast algorithm is the FFT for the group at hand; on $(\mathbb{Z}_2)^n$ that FFT is Walsh-Hadamard, at cost $O(n\cdot 4^n)$. paulikit is a memory-bounded realization of that qubit transform: peak usage need not materialize the dense $2^n\times 2^n$ operator, unlike prior codes whose $O(1)$ extra sits on that buffer, checked beyond a billion oscillator terms against Work-Span and bandwidth ceilings.

Pauli Error Composition Determines the Multipartite Advantage in Conference Key Agreement

Authors: Ryder Carlson, Lochlan Fitzgerald, Kidus Peter, Wilson Smith
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.07251v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work analyzes the robustness of GHZ-based quantum key distribution against noise, showing the advantage over pairwise methods depends on the underlying Pauli error structure of the noise, not just its strength. Key findings include that noise should be spread evenly across all parties and that the dealer should be the party with the least noisy link. The study provides crucial theoretical bounds but notes that transmission loss remains the most significant physical omission.

Why it may be interesting. The finding that the advantage depends on the Pauli error composition rather than the channel type provides a powerful, general structural test for quantum communication protocols, applicable across different physical noise sources.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the conditions under which the multipartite advantage of GHZ-based conference key agreement over pairwise BB84 persists when subjected to various types of quantum noise.

Main result. The key advantage is determined by the channel's Pauli error composition, not its specific identity, and the rate is maximized by distributing noise evenly or assigning the dealer role to the cleanest party.

Method. The authors simulate both protocols exactly for small $N$ under different noise channels and derive closed-form rate expressions for arbitrary Pauli noise.

Model / system. The model uses quantum key distribution (QKD) protocols, specifically comparing GHZ-based conference key agreement against pairwise BB84 in a star network topology.

Key observables. Asymptotic conference key rates ($r_{GHZ}$) and pairwise key rates, measured via error rates like $Q_x$ (collective X error) and $Q_{1,i}$ (pairwise Z error).

Important parameters / regimes. Number of parties ($N=3, 4, 5$), noise types (Dephasing, Depolarizing, Amplitude Damping), and the distribution of a fixed total noise budget.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes independent single-qubit channels on each arm and explicitly omits transmission loss, detector efficiency, and finite-key corrections.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the preparation and distribution of an N-qubit GHZ state, while tables compare key rates under different noise distribution strategies.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the theoretical framework by deriving closed-form rates for Pauli noise, compares the GHZ vs. pairwise protocols, analyzes the dependence on noise distribution (even vs. concentrated), and derives structural corollaries regarding the dealer role.

Abstract

GHZ-based conference key agreement distributes a shared key to $N$ parties in one network use, whereas pairwise BB84 needs $N-1$. The survival of that advantage has been studied against party number, loss, and topology, but almost always with the channel fixed at depolarizing. Simulating both protocols exactly for $N = 3, 4, 5$ under dephasing, amplitude damping, and depolarizing noise, we find the crossover is set by the channel's Pauli error composition, not its identity: normalized to average infidelity, amplitude damping and depolarizing agree to within about 1\% at each group size, while dephasing can tolerate roughly $2.4\times$ more at $N = 3$. We derive a closed-form rate for arbitrary per-party Pauli noise and use it to prove that concentrating a fixed noise budget on one arm is strictly worse than spreading it, and that giving the dealer role to the cleanest arm improves the rate by roughly 18\%. One structural fact drives every result: the phase-error term is a product over all $N$ arms, while the bit-error term stays local to a pair.

Pauli Flat Quantum States Mimicking Maximal Magic

Authors: Paweł Cieśliński
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.07172v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical paper explores Pauli flat quantum states, which are defined by having equal absolute expectation values across all Pauli operators. The authors demonstrate that these mixed states can mimic the correlation structure of highly magical pure states, providing a new mathematical lens to understand the necessary resources for fault-tolerant quantum computing.

Why it may be interesting. The connection drawn between Pauli correlation structures (a mathematical tool) and the resource concept of quantum magic is highly relevant for understanding the boundaries of quantum computation and entanglement detection in many-body quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the correlation structure of quantum states that maximize quantum magic, focusing on the concept of Pauli flat states.

Main result. Mixed Pauli flat states can be constructed for arbitrary number of qubits and have been shown to reproduce the correlation structure of highly magical pure states, suggesting a link between mixed states and quantum resource theory.

Method. The authors analyze Pauli expectation values using a witness approach and employ mathematical constructions (including SDP optimization) to determine the existence and properties of these specialized quantum states.

Model / system. The system under study is an N-qubit quantum state, $ ho$, analyzed through its expectation values on all Pauli strings. The core focus is on defining and characterizing 'Pauli flat states' based on these expectation values.

Key observables. Pauli expectation values ($ ext{Tr}[ ho P_i]$), the non-stabiliserness witness value ($ ext{Tr}( ho W)$), and the parameter $\gamma$ defining the state structure.

Important parameters / regimes. The number of qubits ($N$), the constant absolute value of Pauli expectations ($\gamma$), and the threshold value $2N-1$ for detecting magic.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on defining Pauli flat states by equal absolute Pauli expectations and uses the non-stabiliserness witness to prove the presence of quantum magic.

Figures summary. The notes mention figures illustrating sign vectors for $N=4$ and $N=5$, and a table summarizing the best-found values of $\gamma$, Purity, and the non-stabiliserness ratio ($\eta_S$) for small $N$.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining quantum magic and Pauli flat states, analyzing pure state limitations (especially for $N=2$), developing the mixed state framework, proving existence theorems for mixed states, and concluding with explicit, highly technical derivations of the density matrix elements for $N=4$ and $N=5$.

Abstract

Quantum magic is a necessary resource for fault-tolerant quantum computing, capturing the departure of quantum states from the classically simulable stabiliser set. Motivated by its role in quantum information processing, we investigate the correlation structure of states that maximize the Rényi stabiliser entropies, focusing on the T-type and Hoggar states. Inspired by their distinctive properties, we explore the notion of Pauli flat states, defined as N-qubit states whose expectation values on all Pauli strings are equal in absolute value. These states reproduce the correlation structure of highly magical states, which could also be interpreted as a mixed variant of Pauli covariant N-qubit symmetric informationally complete (SIC) Weyl-Heisenberg fiducial states, while not necessarily possessing nontrivial quantum magic. We investigate their existence and possible constructions, including examples both inside and outside of the stabiliser polytope. To identify the latter, we examine a magic witness tailored to the introduced family of states. Finally, we discuss their entanglement and Bell nonlocality, providing a broader characterization of their quantum properties. For completeness, we find pure states with a relaxed condition on the Pauli expectations, which forces unequal elements to zero, and discuss their non-stabiliserness. Our results offer a new perspective on the relation between quantum magic and Pauli correlation structures. They hold relevance to geometrical approaches to quantum mechanics, entanglement and magic detection, as well as estimation tasks.

Quantum Algorithms for Finding Vacua in the ADK Landscape

Authors: Shirabe Endo, Yuta Hamada
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.07439v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops quantum algorithms to efficiently search the vast space of possible vacuum states in the ADK string theory landscape. By reformulating the search as a collision problem, the authors improve the query complexity from $O(2^{N/2})$ to $O(2^{N/3})$. This demonstrates a quantum advantage over classical methods for finding low-energy vacua.

Why it may be interesting. While the physical context is high-energy theory, the core contribution is a rigorous quantum complexity analysis, directly relevant to quantum algorithms for solving hard search problems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the computational challenge of finding a vacuum state with a small cosmological constant within the complex ADK Landscape of string theory.

Main result. The authors propose quantum algorithms that achieve a query complexity of $O(2^{N/3})$, significantly improving upon classical meet-in-the-middle approaches ($O^*(2^{N/2})$) for physically relevant parameters.

Method. The problem is reformulated as a collision problem, allowing the application of quantum search techniques like the sorted-list algorithm and quantum walks.

Model / system. The system is the toy model for the string Landscape (ADK model), where the vacuum energy $\Lambda_m$ is a linear combination of terms determined by $N$ binary choices ($m \in \{0, 1\}^N$).

Key observables. The vacuum energy $\Lambda_m$, and the target window defined by $0 \le \Lambda_m \le \Lambda_{ ext{target}}$.

Important parameters / regimes. The number of scalar fields ($N$), and the ratio $\Lambda_{ ext{target}}/M^4_{ ext{Pl}}$ (e.g., $10^{-120}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The quantum algorithms assume access to Quantum-Accessible Classical Memory (QRACM) and rely on the linearity of the vacuum energy to reduce the search to a collision problem.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the ADK landscape, the state register for the oracle, and the process of the meet-in-the-middle search, comparing complexities for different algorithms.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the physical problem (ADK Landscape search), derives the oracle complexity, compares plain Grover search ($O(2^{N/2})$) to classical methods, and finally develops and analyzes two quantum algorithms achieving $O(2^{N/3})$ complexity.

Abstract

We study quantum algorithms for finding a vacuum with a small cosmological constant in the toy model for string Landscape proposed by Arkani-Hamed, Dimopoulos, and Kachru. First, we construct a function-value oracle and use it for Grover search with query complexity $\mathcal{O}(2^{N/2})$, where $N$ is the number of scalar fields. Then, we point out that, by exploiting the linearity of the vacuum energy, the problem can be reduced to a collision problem between two blocks of the bit string. With this structure, we construct two quantum algorithms with query complexity $\mathcal{O}(2^{N/3})$. Compared with the pseudo-polynomial classical algorithm of Denef and Douglas, the quantum algorithms yield a large reduction in computational cost for the physically motivated parameter values.

Quantum Algorithms for Multivariable Polynomial Transformations: From Efficient Synthesis to Quantum Channel Transformations

Authors: Zheyu Shen, Yusen Wu, Xiao Yuan, Xiao-Ming Zhang, Yukun Zhang
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.08714v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper establishes a comprehensive theory for synthesizing quantum circuits implementing multivariable polynomial transformations on noncommuting matrices. It achieves optimal query complexity bounds and extends the methodology to synthesize complex quantum channel transformations. This work positions multivariable approximation as a fundamental language for designing higher-order quantum information processing protocols.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a rigorous mathematical framework for designing complex quantum circuits by treating multi-variable polynomial operations as a structured, synthesizable resource, which is crucial for advanced quantum algorithm design.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Developing a complete constructive theory for synthesizing multivariable polynomial transformations of noncommuting matrices, and extending this framework to quantum channel transformations.

Main result. The authors provide a synthesis procedure achieving optimal query complexity for these transformations and show how this framework can synthesize any jointly contractive family of noncommutative polynomial maps as completely positive quantum operations.

Method. The core method involves developing a finite algorithmic Schur–Agler theorem to derive a semidefinite certificate for contractivity, which then dictates the structure of the required quantum query circuit.

Model / system. The work focuses on quantum algorithms utilizing polynomial transformations applied to noncommuting matrices and generalized to quantum channels described by Kraus operators and Choi data.

Key observables. Query complexity ($O(D/\sqrt{ au})$ or $D$), contractivity certificate, and the resulting quantum channel operations.

Important parameters / regimes. Degree $D$, contraction parameter $ au$, and the number of joint accesses.

Assumptions / limitations. The synthesis relies on a compact finite-state description of the polynomial and the existence of a joint block access model.

Figures summary. The notes mention figures illustrating the interleaved structure of the complete quantum circuit and the definition of the Gram matrices used in the fixed-query criterion.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the lack of a synthesis theory for multivariable transformations to developing the constructive theory using the Schur–Agler theorem, and finally applying this theory to synthesize quantum channel transformations.

Abstract

Polynomial transformations are basic primitives in quantum algorithms: quantum signal processing and singular value transformation compile univariate polynomials into circuits with query complexity essentially set by degree. Multivariable transformations of noncommuting matrices, however, lack a comparable synthesis theory. We develop a complete constructive theory under joint block access for all polynomials contractive on the prescribed matrix domain. Given a compact finite-state description of a degree-$D$ polynomial $P$ and $0<τ<1$, we synthesize $P$ with $(1+τ)$-optimal normalization using $O(D/\sqrtτ)$ queries, and exactly $D$ under row-block access, matching a degree lower bound. The complete circuit realization is classically computable in polynomial time with polylogarithmic dependence on accuracy. The key to our construction is a finite algorithmic Schur--Agler theorem: the coefficient recurrence defines a polynomial-dimensional continuation space supporting a complete semidefinite certificate for contractivity on the prescribed domain. Factoring this certificate yields the contractive realization underlying the query algorithm. Beyond matrix transformations, our framework lifts multivariable polynomial synthesis to quantum channel transformations. Given coherent Kraus access $K=\{K_a\}_a$, we synthesize any finite jointly contractive family of noncommutative polynomial maps $K\mapsto\{F_b(K)\}_b$ as completely positive operations, allowing coherent interference among Kraus histories. Furthermore, channel-level transformations specified by causal Choi data can be synthesized explicitly as fixed-order quantum combs. Collectively, these results point to a broader program: multivariable approximation as a language for multi-operator quantum algorithms and higher-order quantum information processing.

Replica Fragmentation and Glassy Dynamics in Parity Learning

Authors: Han Ma
Type: theory · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.08503v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work applies concepts from spin-glass theory to analyze how independently trained neural networks learn complex functions, specifically binary string reconstruction. By measuring replica overlaps and defining gaps, the authors distinguish between distinct learning states like 'memorization' and 'retreat.' This provides a theoretical understanding of generalization failure in deep learning models using tools from condensed matter physics.

Why it may be interesting. The application of spin-glass concepts (replica overlaps, glassy dynamics) to the generalization failure of deep learning models provides a powerful theoretical framework for understanding complex, non-equilibrium learning processes.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the dynamics of independently trained Transformer neural networks reconstructing a binary string from local domain walls, characterizing the resulting ensemble of learned functions.

Main result. The self–cross gap ($\chi_{SG}$) successfully distinguishes three distinct learning regimes: memorization, retreat, and recovery, revealing differences in residual correlations between these states.

Method. The analysis uses statistical physics tools, calculating overlaps ($m, q_{ ext{self}}, q_{ ext{cross}}$) and gaps ($\chi_{SG}$) across different learning protocols and data sizes.

Model / system. The model is a Transformer network trained on a binary string reconstruction task defined by parity constraints, treated as an ensemble of 'replicas' whose behavior is analyzed using concepts from spin-glass theory.

Key observables. Truth alignment ($m$), self-confidence ($q_{ ext{self}}$), cross-replica agreement ($q_{ ext{cross}}$), and the self–cross gap ($\chi_{SG}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Training set size (small vs. large), string length ($L$), and the resulting learning regimes (memorization, retreat, recovery).

Assumptions / limitations. The observed regimes are noted to be 'operational and finite-size rather than thermodynamic phases.'

Figures summary. Figures illustrate trajectories of block accuracy for CNN vs. Transformer, the crossover of learning probability with data size, and position-resolved residual gaps showing failure peaks.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by defining the physical system and observables, establishing the three learning regimes using overlaps, and then providing detailed diagnostics (residual correlations, position-resolved gaps) to distinguish between memorization and retreat dynamics.

Abstract

We study how independently trained Transformer neural networks reconstruct a binary string from its local domain walls. Runs sharing the data and training protocol can realize different functions. We treat them as replicas and measure truth alignment $m$, prediction confidence $q_{\mathrm{self}}$, and cross-replica agreement $q_{\mathrm{cross}}$. Confident disagreement defines the finite-size replica fragmentation that we call glass-like. With small training sets, replicas predict all training examples correctly but remain confident in incorrect predictions for unseen inputs, a regime we call memorization. With larger sets, runs can generalize and then retreat. Retreat occurs when outputs start to deviate from truth while confidence remains high. The frontier between learned and unlearned outputs recedes toward shorter strings. Many later recover as the frontier advances again. The self--cross gap $q_{\mathrm{self}}-q_{\mathrm{cross}}$ clearly distinguishes the three learning regimes of memorization, retreat, and recovery. With overall and position-dependent truth alignment subtracted, their residual correlations also differ: memorizing replicas have weakly and uniformly correlated residuals, while retreat has the largest fraction of replica pairs whose residuals are anti-correlated. That is, on the inputs where one replica of such a pair does better than its average, the other tends to do worse. These finite-size observations distinguish persistent memorization from ongoing retreat--recovery dynamics. The thermodynamic and long-time limits, and extensions to other learning tasks, remain open.

Abstract

Controlling valley degrees of freedom with mechanical strain is a promising approach for solid-state information processing. Current theoretical literature routinely predicts strain-tuned valley splitting at the microscopic level but rarely evaluates whether these quantum predictions remain statistically recoverable in realistic macroscopic devices. This manuscript establishes a fully classical, partial differential equation-constrained multiscale inverse framework for quantifying the device-level identifiability of predicted strain-tunable valley effects in two-dimensional magnetic heterostructures, demonstrated here for a molybdenum disulfide and chromium tribromide heterostructure. First-principles structural relaxations confirm a chiral $C_3$ point-group symmetry which mathematically reduces the relevant exchange-strain coupling tensor to a single scalar. A partial differential equation-constrained adjoint-state architecture successfully bridges continuum elastodynamics to valley-resolved anomalous Hall transport. Density functional theory yields a coupling estimate of $η\approx -0.07$ meV whose 95% confidence interval is consistent with zero. Evaluating this specific coupling magnitude against established thermal noise and velocity saturation limits defines a safe operating window bounded between 262.0 and 22,337.6 V/cm. Rather than asserting a confirmed nonzero material property this bounded operational window functions as a precise diagnostic threshold. Deploying this rigorous statistical identifiability framework provides a necessary mathematical filter to determine the true experimental viability of theoretically predicted two-dimensional materials before complex physical fabrication.

Abstract

In colloids, anisotropy in either shape or particle interactions can steer assemblies away from close-packed monolayers and towards more intricate structures. In this study we utilize both shape and interaction anisotropy to stabilize porous surface assemblies of triblock elliptical particles, whose surfaces are decorated with two attractive patches at opposite poles. The resulting structures display a tunable degree of crystalline kagome order, controlled by quenching the temperature and/or changing the chemical potential. In particular, we find evidence that nucleation into the kagome lattice proceeds via a one-step pathway, in contrast to the two-step route widely reported in spherical triblock systems, making triblock ellipses an interesting model system for studying how self-assembly pathways change with particle shape. Beyond ordered crystalline structures, we find disordered porous networks with complex internal geometry. To characterize this range of assemblies, we introduce a particle-patch graph that explicitly captures the localization of bonds at distinct patches. Bonding motifs follow directly from this representation, while the loops, that we identify as the pore boundaries, can be determined from the graph's planar embedding. We detect all loops as the face boundaries in the planar embedding by means of a face-walking algorithm, distinct from shortest-path approaches used in ring detection. Together with a new local and global order parameter measuring the loop network's regularity with respect to a selected loop size, this framework connects microscopic bonding motifs to mesoscopic pore properties and quantifies the tunable order of these porous assemblies.
