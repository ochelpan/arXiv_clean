Exchange self-energy and vertex corrections to static screening in the two-dimensional electron gas

Authors: Sankar Das Sarma
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.08309v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical work calculates advanced correlation corrections (self-energy and vertex diagrams) to the static screening of a 2D electron gas. The key finding is that these exchange corrections provide a significant quantitative and qualitative improvement over standard RPA theory. This improvement is demonstrated by sharpening the Kohn anomaly and enhancing the long-range Friedel oscillations, offering deeper insight into electron-electron interactions in low dimensions.

Why it may be interesting. The detailed study of correlation effects beyond RPA in 2D systems provides crucial benchmarks for understanding strongly correlated electron behavior, which is relevant to high-Tc superconductors and quantum materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper calculates the leading beyond-Random-Phase-Approximation (RPA) corrections to the static irreducible polarizability of the two-dimensional electron gas to accurately determine the static dielectric function and screened interaction.

Main result. Exchange corrections significantly improve screening predictions by converting the Kohn kink into a sharp two-sided peak and enhancing Friedel oscillations in real space compared to RPA. The combined self-energy and vertex corrections are found to be finite and reproduce the Hartree-Fock compressibility sum rule.

Method. The authors analytically compute the two exchange self-energy and one exchange vertex diagram, summing them to obtain the full polarizability correction ($\Pi_1$). They then use this to derive the static dielectric function $\epsilon(q)$ and analyze the small-$q$ limit.

Model / system. The system studied is the two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) interacting via the bare Coulomb potential. The analysis focuses on the static screening properties, which are governed by the polarizability $\Pi(q)$ and the resulting screened interaction $W(q)$.

Key observables. Static dielectric function $\epsilon(q)$, screened interaction $W(q)$, Friedel oscillation amplitude, and the local-field factor $G_x(q)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The dimensionless momentum $x = q/k_F$, the Wigner-Seitz radius parameter $r_s$, and the Fermi wave vector $k_F$.

Assumptions / limitations. The calculation is performed in the static limit and uses first-order perturbation theory for the corrections. The analysis compares the systematic $O(r_s)$ result against approximations like the Hubbard model.

Figures summary. Figures compare the RPA vs. RPA + exchange corrections for the polarizability ($\Pi$), show the asymptotic form of the polarizability, and illustrate the enhanced, growing Friedel oscillations in real space ($\delta n(r)$).

Paper structure. The paper systematically derives the corrections to the polarizability, analyzes the resulting asymptotic behavior in momentum space, compares the exact result against simpler approximations (like Hubbard), and discusses the physical consequences for Friedel oscillations and pairing instabilities.

Abstract

We compute analytically the leading beyond-ring corrections to the static irreducible polarizability of the two-dimensional (2D) electron gas, namely the two exchange self-energy diagrams and the exchange vertex diagram, each of first order in the Coulomb coupling, and insert the resulting polarizability into the RPA geometric series to obtain the static dielectric function and screened interaction. The self-energy and vertex contributions individually diverge; we obtain both divergent pieces in closed form. Their sum is finite and reproduces exactly the Hartree-Fock compressibility, which renormalizes the Thomas-Fermi wave vector. We show that exchange converts the one-sided 2D Kohn kink at 2k_F into a sharp two-sided peak, a qualitative improvement over Thomas-Fermi and RPA screening. In real space this makes the Friedel oscillations around a charged impurity 2-3 times larger than in RPA, with an amplitude that increases with distance over the range studied; implications for Kohn-Luttinger pairing are discussed. Relation to earlier works on the same diagrams is discussed.

Learning Disentangled Representations with Quantum Variational Autoencoders

Authors: Gaoyuan Wang, Jerry Tan, Mark Gerstein

Summary. This paper explores using Quantum Variational Autoencoders (QVAEs) to learn structured and interpretable representations from quantum data. By analyzing the latent space structure and testing on synthetic spectral data, the authors prove that QVAEs can successfully disentangle generative factors, assigning distinct physical meanings to individual qubits. This establishes a foundation for applying quantum ML to complex physical modeling.

Why it may be interesting. This work bridges quantum machine learning with physical interpretation, showing how quantum circuits can enforce structure (disentanglement) on latent variables, which is highly relevant for modeling complex quantum many-body systems where factorized representations are sought.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how Quantum Variational Autoencoders (QVAEs) can learn disentangled and semantically interpretable latent factors from complex data, addressing the challenge of defining individual quantum latent dimensions.

Main result. The authors demonstrate that QVAEs can successfully discover factorized and interpretable latent representations, showing that individual qubits can function as meaningful latent factors for tasks like encoding spectral line parameters.

Method. The approach involves developing theoretical insights into quantum latent dimensions and applying empirical studies on synthetic spectral data and MNIST variants, optimizing a loss function combining reconstruction fidelity and regularization.

Model / system. The core model is the Quantum Variational Autoencoder (QVAE), which maps classical data into a quantum latent space. The system utilizes quantum circuits (e.g., RealAmplitudes ansatz) to encode and decode quantum states, operating within a Hilbert space whose dimensionality is exponentially large relative to the number of qubits.

Key observables. Mean Squared Error (MSE) for predicting specific generative factors (e.g., position, width), eigenvalues of the ensemble density matrix, and the visualization of latent states on the Bloch sphere.

Important parameters / regimes. The regularization strength $\beta$ (controlling the prior match) and the number of qubits ($n$) are critical parameters. The spectral data is controlled by continuous parameters like peak position ($\mu$) and width ($\sigma$).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that the structure of the latent space can be effectively analyzed by decomposing the ensemble density matrix and that regularization can enforce disentanglement, although the notion of an 'individual quantum latent dimension' remains theoretically challenging.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the QVAE architecture, the latent state distributions on the Bloch sphere for different regularization strengths ($\beta$), and the MSE of predictors quantifying the information content of specific latent subsystems (qubits).

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the need for interpretable quantum representations, developing theoretical tools (density matrix analysis), demonstrating the model's capability on synthetic spectral data (position/width factors), and concluding with architectural details and future directions.

Abstract

Variational autoencoders are powerful representation learning models that map complex data into low-dimensional latent spaces, enabling the discovery of interpretable and disentangled factors. Such representations can facilitate the interpretation and controllable generation of data describing complex scientific systems. Understanding how these factors are organized and encoded in latent space is therefore important for developing reliable representation learning models. Recently, quantum variational autoencoders (QVAEs) have been proposed as quantum representation models, demonstrating informative latent representations and improved latent-space occupancy through quantum regularization. However, it remains unclear whether and how QVAEs can learn disentangled and interpretable latent factors. A key challenge in investigating quantum latent factors is that a small number of qubits spans an exponentially large Hilbert space, making the notion of an individual quantum latent dimension nontrivial. Here, we investigate what constitutes an individual quantum latent dimension and whether it can encode a distinct factor. We develop theoretical insights into quantum latent dimensions and support them with empirical studies on representative synthetic problems, including MNIST variants. Across three datasets, we demonstrate that QVAEs can discover factorized and semantically interpretable latent representations, with individual qubits functioning as meaningful latent factors. These results establish a foundation for understanding quantum latent spaces and their potential for structured and interpretable representation learning.

Magnus: neutrino oscillation probabilities for any Hermitian Hamiltonian, any number of flavors, and any matter profile

Authors: Mauricio Bustamante
Type: theory · Category: numerical methods · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.07159v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: methods for driven-dissipative 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper presents Magnus, a novel, open-source computational tool for calculating neutrino flavor oscillation probabilities. It overcomes the computational bottlenecks of traditional solvers by employing the Magnus expansion, which is highly accurate and efficient even when the matter density changes rapidly. This shifts the focus of research from developing complex numerical solvers to simply specifying the underlying particle physics model.

Why it may be interesting. The use of the Magnus expansion to solve differential equations governing quantum evolution is a powerful numerical technique applicable to any system where the Hamiltonian changes slowly or where high accuracy is required over long evolution times, making it relevant for open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Calculating neutrino flavor transition probabilities when the matter density varies along the propagation path, which lacks a simple closed-form solution.

Main result. The authors introduce Magnus, an open-source Python code that computes these probabilities accurately and efficiently, outperforming existing solvers.

Method. It utilizes the Magnus expansion to compute the evolution operator, whose error scales highly with the step width and whose computational cost depends on the rate of density variation rather than the number of oscillations.

Model / system. The system is neutrino flavor oscillation, governed by a time-dependent Hamiltonian $H(l)$ that incorporates neutrino masses, mixing, and various matter interactions (e.g., non-standard interactions).

Key observables. Flavor-transition probability $P_{ u_\alpha o u_eta}(l)$, and phase-averaged probabilities.

Important parameters / regimes. The Hamiltonian $H(l)$ itself, the number of flavors, and the specific density profile along the path (e.g., Earth, Sun).

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes the evolution is governed by a Hermitian Hamiltonian and does not handle non-unitary processes like decay or decoherence.

Figures summary. Figure 1 compares the $ u_e$ survival probability calculated using Magnus against a high-order Runge-Kutta solver across various flavor numbers and density profiles.

Paper structure. The paper details the theory of neutrino oscillation, introduces the Magnus expansion for the evolution operator, describes the implementation in the open-source code, and provides extensive usage examples covering various astrophysical and terrestrial environments.

Abstract

Interpreting neutrino oscillation measurements requires computing the probability that a neutrino born with one flavor is detected with another. In vacuum and in matter of constant density, this probability has exact formulas. Where the density varies along the path, as in the Earth, the Sun, or a supernova, it does not. There, the usual approaches either replace the profile by constant-density steps, whose error falls only as the square of their width, or integrate the evolution equation with a general-purpose solver, which must shorten its steps to meet a tighter tolerance. Both become slow at high accuracy, yet an analysis needs the probability at many energies, directions, and parameter values. Each new experiment or model often requires writing and validating a new solver. We present Magnus, an open-source Python code that avoids these limitations. It uses the Magnus expansion, whose error falls as a high power of its step width and whose cost is set by how fast the density varies, not by how many oscillations the path holds. Magnus accepts any density profile, any number of flavors, and any Hermitian Hamiltonian, such as those of non-standard interactions, sterile neutrinos, Lorentz-invariance violation, and pseudo-Dirac neutrinos. Where a cheaper route exists, it takes it automatically. For example, where the density varies slowly, as in the Sun, it follows the instantaneous eigenstates and applies the expansion only at resonances. Besides oscillating probabilities, Magnus returns phase-averaged ones, which describe solar and astrophysical neutrinos. A typical probability takes a few milliseconds, and batched scans are one to two orders of magnitude cheaper per point. Among the public codes we compared, Magnus is the fastest at high accuracy on a varying profile. Magnus shifts the effort of studying a new experiment or model from writing a solver to specifying its physics.

Nematic order in monomer-dimer models of Heilmann and Lieb with lateral attraction in dimensions $d\ge 2$

Authors: Qidong He
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.08600v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper proves the existence of nematic liquid-crystalline order in generalized monomer-dimer models on the $d$-dimensional lattice. It achieves this by establishing the uniqueness of the Gibbs measure and proving the exponential decay of correlations using advanced techniques like cluster swapping. The results provide a rigorous mathematical characterization of orientational order in these lattice models.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical statistical mechanics, the rigorous mathematical techniques used (e.g., bounding correlations, proving uniqueness of Gibbs measures) are foundational to understanding phase transitions in complex many-body quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to rigorously establish the existence and properties of nematic order in $d$-dimensional monomer-dimer models of liquid crystals.

Main result. Under specific conditions on the chemical potential ($\mu$) and attraction strength ($b$), the model admits a unique Gibbs measure exhibiting anisotropic exponential decay of correlations within the oriented phase.

Method. The proof combines techniques from statistical mechanics, including cluster swapping, strong-percolation frameworks, and bounding probabilities of local configurations using mesoscopic box estimates.

Model / system. The system is a monomer-dimer model on the $d$-dimensional hypercubic lattice ($\mathbb{Z}^d$), describing liquid crystals. The Hamiltonian includes terms for the number of dimers ($\mu$) and a lateral attraction ($b$) between parallel dimers.

Key observables. The primary observables are the probabilities of observing aligned versus misaligned dimers in the ordered phase, and the decay rate of correlations.

Important parameters / regimes. Key parameters include the chemical potential $\mu$, the coupling constant $b$, and the dimension $d$. The analysis requires the conditions $(4d-6)b > \mu$ and $\mu + 2(d-1)b > 0$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the existence of a critical inverse temperature $eta_d$ and the assumption that the relevant clusters are rare enough to be controlled by the cluster swapping technique.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates a typical configuration of Model V on a torus, providing visual evidence of the predicted nematic order.

Paper structure. The paper generalizes Heilmann-Lieb's models to $d \ge 2$. It first establishes the mathematical framework using strong-percolation and then uses cluster swapping to prove the uniqueness of the Gibbs measure and the exponential decay of correlations in the ordered phase.

Abstract

We study the $d$-dimensional generalization of Models II ($d=2$) and V ($d=3$) for liquid crystals introduced by Heilmann--Lieb (1979), which consists of dimers on the hypercubic lattice at chemical potential $μ$, interacting via a hard-core repulsion and a lateral attraction between parallel dimers with coupling constant $b>0$. For $d\in\{2,3\}$, these models were conjectured to exhibit liquid-crystalline order at low temperatures provided that $μ+2(d-1)b>0$. We establish nematic order for all $d\ge 2$ under the additional condition $(4d-6)b>μ$, which corresponds to the physical regime in which misaligned dimers are rare on scales comparable to the correlation length of the one-dimensional reference system. While the treatment of orientational order here follows the approach of our recent work on Model I, the proof of the absence of translational order relies on a custom implementation of cluster swapping in the sense of Sheffield (2005). Consequently, we deduce uniqueness of Gibbs measure and anisotropic exponential decay of correlations within each oriented phase.

Non-invertible SPT phases from fusion rings via Tannaka duality

Authors: Antonio Santaniello, Roberto Valandro, Massimo Zorzenon
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.07165v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical paper develops a powerful technique using Tannaka duality to classify non-invertible symmetry-protected topological (SPT) phases in 1+1D QFTs, using only the fusion ring data. By reconstructing fiber functors, the authors identify multiple distinct phases, providing explicit mathematical structures for their associated anomalies and interface algebras.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical machinery connecting fusion rings to physical phases (SPT phases) is highly relevant for understanding topological order in condensed matter systems, providing tools to classify phases based on symmetry data.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to determine if a symmetry in a (1+1)-dimensional QFT with non-invertible symmetry is anomaly-free, which is crucial for characterizing trivially gapped phases.

Main result. The authors developed a method using Tannaka duality to find multiple fiber functors, thereby identifying several non-invertible SPT phases, particularly for the fusion ring related to extraspecial groups of order 27.

Method. They extract fiber functors directly from the fusion ring by solving for monoidal structure maps and tensor automorphisms, bypassing the direct solution of the pentagon equations.

Model / system. The study focuses on (1+1)-dimensional Quantum Field Theories (QFTs) characterized by non-invertible symmetries, where the symmetry is implemented by topological line defects.

Key observables. Fiber functors, F-symbols, and the anomalous algebra computed on the interface between different SPT phases.

Important parameters / regimes. The structure of the fusion ring (e.g., the rank-11 ring from extraspecial groups) and invariants $(\epsilon, \iota)$ derived from the reconstruction.

Assumptions / limitations. The procedure is tested on the maximal tensor-automorphism case, and the classification for $p>3$ is noted as conjectural.

Figures summary. The notes mention Table 4, which provides a comprehensive list of ring automorphisms used to generate all specific parametric forms associated with $ ext{Rep}( ext{Esp}9)$.

Paper structure. The paper builds theory from fusion categories and Tannaka duality, develops a bootstrap procedure to find fiber functors from the fusion ring, applies this to known examples (Tambara-Yamagami), and concludes with explicit calculations for extraspecial groups.

Abstract

In a (1+1)-dimensional QFT with non-invertible symmetry, the fusion rules of topological lines are often the most accessible data, yet they do not fully determine whether the symmetry is anomaly-free, i.e. whether it admits trivially gapped phases. Such phases are characterized by fiber functors of the corresponding fusion category. Building on Tannaka duality, we extract fiber functors from the fusion ring alone by solving simultaneously for monoidal structure maps and tensor automorphisms. This allows us to find categorifications admitting a fiber functor without solving the pentagon equations directly. We test this procedure on the known Tambara--Yamagami ring of $\mathbb{Z}_2\times\mathbb{Z}_2$. Our main result concerns the rank-11 fusion ring shared by the representation categories of the two extraspecial groups of order 27, which has a multiplicity 3 channel and is relevant to self-triality. In both cases we find more than one fiber functor, i.e. several non-invertible SPT phases. We use the explicit data of these fiber functors to derive the $F$-symbols of the two representation categories in a compact form depending on a non-degenerate alternating bicharacter and an invertible object $ε$. Using the reconstructed data, we compute the anomalous algebra on the interface between two different SPT phases.

Summary. This theoretical work investigates how chiral four-spin exchange generates an odd-parity magnon spin in a honeycomb antiferromagnet. By analyzing the magnon spectrum and calculating the thermal spin accumulation response, the authors show that this interaction leads to a measurable spin density change proportional to the temperature gradient. This work provides insight into spin-momentum locking mechanisms in frustrated magnetic insulators.

Why it may be interesting. The study connects lattice geometry (honeycomb structure) and specific higher-order spin interactions (chiral four-spin exchange) to measurable transport properties (thermal spin current), which is relevant for understanding spin-charge conversion in magnetic materials.

Abstract

Chiral four-spin exchange generates a momentum-dependent magnon spin in a compensated honeycomb antiferromagnet at zero magnetic field. For a Néel vector tilted by an angle $θ_0$ from the layer normal, the interaction contributes to intersublattice magnon pairing already at harmonic order, with an amplitude proportional to $\cosθ_0\sin^2θ_0$. Biaxial anisotropy mixes the two longitudinal-spin sectors, producing nonquantized mode spins that are odd in momentum, while a sublattice-exchange relation keeps the magnon energies reciprocal. A temperature gradient then induces a spin density along the Néel axis through a redistribution of magnon occupations. Reversing the four-spin coupling reverses this spin density and the mode spins without changing the magnon energies. MnPS$_3$ motivates the magnetic geometry; realizing the interaction requires a structural modification compatible with the symmetry assumptions of the model.

Optimal GHZ extraction from MABK violations

Authors: Avraham Kreindel, Aryeh Lev Zabokritskiy
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.08516v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical paper establishes the precise minimum amount of GHZ entanglement that can be extracted from a quantum state given a measured violation of a MABK Bell inequality. It provides uniform analytic proofs for $n \ge 4$ parties and analyzes the stability of this bound using trace-norm distance estimates. The work is crucial for understanding the fundamental limits of quantum correlations in multipartite systems.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical determination of entanglement measures based on Bell inequalities provides fundamental tools for quantifying quantum correlations, which is central to quantum information theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper determines the minimum Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger (GHZ) extractability compatible with a given Mermin-Ardehali-Belinskii-Klyshko (MABK) Bell score for $n>2$ parties.

Main result. The exact minimum extractability is found to be $1 + t^2$, where $t$ parameterizes the deviation from the biseparable bound, and the authors provide uniform analytic proofs for $n \ge 4$ parties.

Method. The analysis uses operator inequalities, Jordan's lemma to reduce verification to qubit sectors, and spectral analysis to establish lower bounds on the target overlap based on the measured Bell score.

Model / system. The study concerns multipartite entangled quantum states, specifically testing entanglement using MABK Bell inequalities on systems of $n$ parties, which can be qubits or qutrits.

Key observables. GHZ extractability ($\Xi_n( ho)$), MABK Bell Score ($eta$), and the normalized score parameter $t$.

Important parameters / regimes. The number of parties ($n \ge 3$), the local dimension of the Hilbert space, and the small error parameter $\epsilon$ used in stability analysis.

Assumptions / limitations. The results hold for normal states on tensor products of arbitrary local dimension and arbitrary binary measurements; stability bounds rely on smallness conditions for the excess error $\epsilon$.

Figures summary. Not specified.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the general affine minimum bound using operator inequalities, then generalizing this proof to higher numbers of parties ($n \ge 4$), and finally analyzing structural questions like the stability of the bound using trace-norm distance estimates.

Abstract

We determine the least Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger (GHZ) extractability compatible with a Mermin-Ardehali-Belinskii-Klyshko (MABK) Bell score for every number of parties greater than two. Extractability is the largest squared overlap with a GHZ state obtainable by separate local quantum channels. Between the biseparable bound and the quantum maximum, the exact minimum is the affine interpolation from one half to one. The bound holds for normal states on tensor products of arbitrary local dimension and arbitrary binary measurements. We settle the previously unresolved range of six or more parties with an analytic proof that is uniform from four parties onward. We also determine the exact minimum when every local system is a qubit, and show that it lies strictly above the unrestricted bound at every interior score. One qutrit and qubits at all remaining parties attain every point of the bound with measurements fixed as the score varies. For at least four parties and strict interior scores, we classify all states attaining the bound with the minimum product of local support dimensions. For at least four parties on one qutrit and qubits elsewhere, we also prove that, when the extractability excess above the affine minimum is small, the trace-norm distance from the attaining mixture, up to local unitaries, is bounded by a constant times the square root of that excess. The exponent $1/2$ is optimal, and the constants are independent of the number of parties on every fixed interior interval of normalized scores.

Perfect state transfer on mixed graphs: complete classes and transfer times

Summary. This theoretical paper classifies the mathematical conditions required for Perfect State Transfer (PST) to occur across all vertices of a mixed graph. It establishes that the set of possible transfer times must satisfy a specific algebraic condition related to rational cosines. The work provides deep structural insights by linking quantum dynamics to number theory and constructing examples of graphs realizing these complex transfer patterns.

Why it may be interesting. The connection between spectral graph theory (PST) and deep number theory (imaginary quadratic fields) provides a powerful, non-standard mathematical toolset for analyzing quantum dynamics in structured physical systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to classify the complete classes and determine the normalized transfer times ($\Lambda$) for Perfect State Transfer (PST) on unweighted mixed graphs.

Main result. A set $\Lambda$ forms a complete PST class if and only if $\cos(2\pi(x - y)) \in \mathbb{Q}$ for all $x, y \in \Lambda$, linking the spectral properties to number theory via imaginary quadratic fields.

Method. The proof combines arithmetic arguments involving imaginary quadratic fields with graph-theoretic constructions, utilizing spectral analysis of the graph's adjacency matrix.

Model / system. The system is modeled by unweighted, finite, simple mixed graphs whose time evolution is governed by $U_H(t) = e^{-itH}$, where $H$ is the Hermitian adjacency matrix.

Key observables. The set of normalized transfer times $\Lambda \subset \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$, the minimum vertex period $T$, and the return phase $\omega$.

Important parameters / regimes. The structure of the graph (connected, oriented), the specific values of the transfer times $\Lambda$, and the resulting bounds on the size of the PST class (at most six or three).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis focuses on the case where the source vertex is nonstationary and periodic, and the graphs are unweighted and finite.

Figures summary. Not specified, but the notes reference arrays (A.1 and A.2) detailing square roots of cell sizes for realizing PST classes of size six and five.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by establishing necessary and sufficient conditions for PST using field theory (Theorem 1.1), deriving constraints on periodicity and transfer times using lemmas (e.g., Lemma 2.2, Lemma 3.1), and finally constructing examples and characterizing the limits of these classes using advanced graph constructions (e.g., equitable-partition method).

Abstract

For perfect state transfer (PST) on unweighted mixed graphs, we classify the normalized transfer times of complete PST classes. A finite set $Λ\subset\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ containing zero occurs at a nonstationary periodic vertex if and only if $\cos(2π(x-y))\in\mathbb Q$ for all $x,y\inΛ$. Every admissible set has a connected oriented realization. We also classify the possible return phases of oriented realizations at the minimum vertex period. Transfers at rational multiples of the common minimum vertex period partition a complete class into sets of size at most six, or at most three in an oriented graph with return phase $-1$; both bounds are sharp. We construct complete classes of every finite size, including classes in which all transfers between distinct vertices occur at irrational multiples of the period and no switching automorphism maps a class vertex to a distinct class vertex. We also characterize simultaneous realization in connected oriented graphs with prescribed relative minimum vertex periods and return phases. The proof combines an imaginary quadratic field restriction with an unweighted construction that selects the complete target set.

Summary. This paper introduces a novel, post-selection-free algorithm for Quantum Automated Learning (QAL) that trains quantum models by encoding the entire learning path into a single coherent quantum circuit. By employing fixed-point amplitude amplification, the method achieves high-probability guarantees on both the learned model's quality and the associated resource costs. This work significantly advances the theoretical feasibility of implementing robust quantum machine learning protocols.

Why it may be interesting. It provides rigorous, resource-aware theoretical guarantees for quantum machine learning protocols, which is crucial for designing fault-tolerant quantum algorithms.

Abstract

Quantum automated learning (QAL) trains quantum models by directly updating quantum states, offering an approach to quantum machine learning that avoids optimizing variational circuit parameters. We provide theoretical guarantees on output error and learning loss, and identify conditions under which additional coherent resources reduce the cost of restarting failed trajectories. The construction gives QAL a coherent implementation with explicit learning guarantees and resource costs, enabling a model with certified learning quality and controlled output error to be obtained with high probability.

Abstract

We derive the local pressure tensor generated by the original Rosenfeld fundamental measure theory (FMT) for hard-sphere fluids from an arbitrary infinitesimal deformation. The variation of a finite-distance FMT kernel is written via the line integrals of the local deformation gradient along the straight segment connecting its endpoints. The resulting symmetric pressure tensor consists of a local term expressed through the standard FMT weighted densities and a finite-range chord term. For a monodisperse hard-sphere fluid, differential relations between the Rosenfeld measures reduce the tensor to a closed one-body form. We demonstrate that no independent pair density or inhomogeneous Ornstein--Zernike equation is required. Abstract

Gaussian steering sets a sharp threshold for creating Wigner negativity at a distance. A measurement by Bob can make Alice's part of a shared Gaussian state Wigner negative only if Alice can steer him with Gaussian measurements. We ask whether multiple copies, ancillas, or catalysts can lift this requirement when Alice is restricted to Gaussian operations. We introduce the steered negativity, a multiplicative monotone whose logarithm equals the Gaussian steerability for Gaussian states. It bounds the negativity that Bob can herald and rules out activation by copies, by ancillas of Bob, and by catalysts without remote negativity of their own. For a strictly unsteerable Gaussian state and a single round of feed-forward to a Gaussian unitary of Alice, we further prove that even a Wigner-negative or steerable catalyst cannot help if it is returned exactly. Gaussian steering thus remains a barrier to remote Wigner negativity beyond the single-copy setting.
