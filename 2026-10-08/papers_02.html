Summary. This research explores the complex momentum and energy exchange between a rapidly moving impurity and a Bose-Einstein condensate. By tuning interactions near Feshbach resonances, the authors observed that the impurity's response deviates significantly from simple two-body predictions. This deviation signals a polaronic enhancement of the impurity's effective mass in the strongly coupled, supersonic regime.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses non-equilibrium dynamics in quantum gases, specifically the crossover between simple collision dynamics and strongly correlated, dressed quasiparticle behavior (polarons) in a supersonic regime.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how a mobile impurity exchanges momentum and energy with a many-body environment, especially when moving rapidly (supersonically) and strongly coupled to the bath.

Main result. Near resonance, the impurity exhibits a marked enhancement of its apparent dynamical inertia, providing evidence for a polaronic response in the strongly coupled, supersonic regime.

Method. The study combines experimental measurements of impurity velocity evolution with theoretical modeling based on two-body collision approximations and Bogoliubov theory.

Model / system. The system is a cold-atom collider consisting of fermionic $^{40} ext{K}$ impurities immersed in a $^{23} ext{Na}$ Bose-Einstein condensate (BEC). The interactions are tuned using Feshbach resonances to explore varying coupling strengths.

Key observables. Impurity velocity $v_I(t_1)$, apparent dynamical inertia ($1/s$), and momentum transfer signatures.

Important parameters / regimes. Supersonic relative speed ($\approx 36$ times the speed of sound), interaction strength ($a_\uparrow$), and proximity to Feshbach resonances.

Assumptions / limitations. The weak coupling regime is modeled by a finite-energy two-body collision model, and the strong coupling regime is analyzed by interpreting deviations from this model.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the experimental sequence, the reconstruction of impurity velocity vs. time, and plots comparing the measured drag response to the theoretical two-body collision model, showing the divergence near resonance.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the physical problem, details the experimental setup using cold atoms, develops theoretical models for drag force in different coupling regimes, presents measurements showing agreement with simple models at weak coupling, and finally highlights the deviation near resonance as evidence for polaronic dressing.

Abstract

How a mobile impurity exchanges momentum and energy with a many-body environment is a central question in nonequilibrium quantum physics. This exchange becomes particularly complex when the impurity moves rapidly and is strongly coupled to its bath. Here, we realize an interaction-tunable cold-atom collider with fermionic $^{40}$K impurities immersed in a $^{23}$Na Bose--Einstein condensate. A species-selective Raman pulse simultaneously launches the condensate and quenches the interspecies scattering length, producing an initial relative speed 36 times the condensate speed of sound. We track the ensuing relative motion as the interspecies interaction is tuned from weak coupling to resonance. At weak and intermediate coupling, the impurity dynamics are well described by a finite-energy two-body collision model. Near resonance, however, the early-time impurity acceleration is markedly suppressed relative to the two-body prediction, corresponding to a strong enhancement of the apparent dynamical inertia. The enhancement is observed near two distinct Feshbach resonances. These observations provide evidence for a polaronic response in the strongly coupled, supersonic regime of a degenerate Bose--Fermi mixture.

Abstract

We derive the density matrix of a nonstationary system in the adiabatic representation and obtain the corresponding Liouville equation. This representation is particularly suitable when the standard interaction representation is inapplicable. We illustrate the approach with two identical, spatially separated spin-1/2 particles coupled by a magnetic dipole-dipole interaction in a slowly varying external magnetic field. Specifically, we analyze the spin polarization of pseudo-qutrit states in regimes where the states become entangled. The application of the adiabatic representation to calculate the dependence of observed physical quantities on time for quantum systems is illustrated. Our results show that entangled states can significantly modify the behavior of these observables and provide additional opportunities for their control.

Abstract

Summary. This paper analyzes the statistical mechanics of systems coupled to multiple heat baths at different temperatures, showing that this coupling generates non-equilibrium steady states. The authors map this complex system onto an effective single-bath description driven by a deterministic curl-force. This framework provides a mechanical understanding of non-equilibrium currents and quantifies the system's departure from equilibrium via the entropy production rate.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful mechanical interpretation of non-equilibrium thermodynamics, showing how multi-temperature baths manifest as deterministic, non-conservative forces, which is highly relevant for understanding energy flow in open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Investigating the statistical mechanics of systems coupled to multiple heat reservoirs at different temperatures, a setting that typically sustains non-trivial circulation and drives the system out of equilibrium.

Main result. The multi-reservoir dynamics can be mapped onto an effective single-bath system driven by a deterministic curl-force, leading to non-equilibrium steady states (NESS) characterized by non-zero entropy production.

Method. The authors employ a canonical transformation to map the complex multi-reservoir coupling onto a simpler, effective Hamiltonian structure featuring an anisotropic kinetic energy and a curl-force.

Model / system. The model is a classical open system (e.g., underdamped Brownian particle) whose dynamics are governed by a generalized Klein-Kramers equation describing coupling to multiple baths at different temperatures ($T_i$).

Key observables. Entropy Production Rate (EPR), steady-state distribution, and the curl of the effective force field ($ abla imes ilde{F}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Multiple reservoir temperatures ($T_i$), damping coefficient ($\xi$), and the mass ($m$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on mapping the system dynamics and assumes the validity of the canonical transformation, though the resulting steady state is generally not the simple Boltzmann distribution.

Figures summary. Not specified by the notes.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the non-equilibrium nature of multi-reservoir coupling, then applies a canonical transformation to derive an effective single-bath description driven by a curl-force. Finally, explicit derivations for the steady-state distribution and EPR are provided for solvable cases.

Abstract

We investigate the statistical mechanics of a system coupled to multiple heat reservoirs at different temperatures, a setting which typically sustains nontrivial circulation. By applying a canonical transformation, we map the system onto that of a particle coupled to a single reservoir yet driven by a deterministic curl-force---a reversible, velocity-independent non-conservative force with non-vanishing curl---that can be described by a Hamiltonian with an anisotropic kinetic energy term. Despite the Hamiltonian structure, the system does not relax to the corresponding Boltzmann distribution, and is genuinely out of equilibrium. The mapping provides a mechanical interpretation of the currents in multi-temperature systems, as we demonstrate by means of two examples. In particular, the direction of circulation is qualitatively predictable from the deterministic force field. For the exactly solvable case of an $N$-dimensional underdamped Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process, we derive explicit expressions for the steady-state distribution and the entropy production rate, highlighting the role of the (generalized) curl of the force field in driving the system out of thermal equilibrium. Finally, we show how a non-conservative force can be used to exactly equilibrate any system coupled to multiple reservoirs, and obtain the corresponding equilibrium distribution.

Summary. This work models diffusion in an open environment subject to particle creation (recruitment) and decay (mortality). It establishes that the system reaches a stable, non-equilibrium state characterized by a saturated Mean Squared Displacement. Crucially, it reveals a 'mortality-assisted search' effect, showing that intermediate decay rates can optimize the speed of reaching a target region.

Why it may be interesting. The framework of open systems with particle creation/destruction and anomalous diffusion is highly relevant to modeling non-equilibrium dynamics in complex physical systems, which shares mathematical structures with open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates heterogeneous diffusion in open systems characterized by continuous particle creation (recruitment) and destruction (mortality). It analyzes how these processes affect transport properties and search times.

Main result. The system reaches a non-equilibrium stationary state where the Mean Squared Displacement saturates to a value independent of the recruitment rate. Furthermore, a moderate mortality rate can counter-intuitively accelerate the arrival of an infection at a new spatial region.

Method. The analysis involves deriving the probability density function and survival probabilities using modified Fokker-Planck equations and the dynamic redundancy framework. Long-time asymptotic behavior is analyzed using Laplace approximations.

Model / system. The model is a Heterogeneous Diffusion Process (HDP) with a position-dependent diffusion coefficient $D(x) \propto |x|^\alpha$. The process is open, incorporating independent recruitment ($\lambda$) and mortality ($\mu$) rates.

Key observables. Mean Squared Displacement ($\langle x^2(t) angle$), survival probability $S(t)$, and mean first-passage time $\langle T angle$.

Important parameters / regimes. Heterogeneity exponent ($\alpha$), recruitment rate ($\lambda$), and mortality rate ($\mu$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes a specific power-law form for the diffusion coefficient $D(x)$ and relies on approximations like the Laplace method for long-time limits.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the saturation of survival probabilities ($S(t)$) and the non-monotonic dependence of the mean first-passage time ($\langle T angle$) on the mortality rate ($\mu$), showing an optimal rate exists.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from deriving the governing equations for the open HDP, calculating the long-time saturation of the MSD, analyzing the first-passage time using survival probabilities, and finally applying the framework to physical examples like epidemiology.

Abstract

We study a heterogeneous diffusion process with a position-dependent diffusion coefficient in the presence of independent recruitment and mortality. This framework generalises heterogeneous diffusion under stochastic resetting, which is recovered as the special case of equal recruitment and mortality rates. At long times, the system approaches a non-equilibrium stationary state with a mean squared displacement that saturates to a value independent of the recruitment rate. Crucially, this saturation limit directly encodes the underlying transport regime via an effective exponent applied to the diffusion coefficient, and we characterise the non-trivial transition to this stationary regime. We further derive the mean first-passage time to a target and show that, for a fixed recruitment-to-mortality ratio, an optimal mortality rate minimises the search time. This "mortality-assisted search" effect is confirmed by numerical evaluation. Reinterpreting recruitment and mortality as infection and removal processes, we apply the model to epidemic spreading and show that a moderate removal rate can counter-intuitively accelerate, rather than delay, the arrival of infection at a new spatial region. Beyond epidemiology, our framework offers broad relevance to systems featuring concurrent particle creation and destruction, such as bacterial growth, tracer diffusion in reactive fluids, or cellular population dynamics in crowded environments.

Summary. This paper explores Entanglement Sudden Death (ESD), demonstrating that the abrupt loss of entanglement in multipartite systems is highly sensitive to the noise model applied. By comparing global versus local depolarization on GHZ states, the authors prove that the entanglement lifetime depends on the joint evolution, not just the local dynamics. This highlights that understanding entanglement requires analyzing the entire composite system state.

Why it may be interesting. This work is highly relevant to AMO physics and open quantum systems as it provides a rigorous, quantitative comparison of how different noise assumptions affect the fundamental persistence of quantum correlations in multi-particle systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates Entanglement Sudden Death (ESD), the phenomenon where entanglement vanishes abruptly at a finite time despite the underlying system dynamics remaining smooth.

Main result. The lifetime of genuine multipartite entanglement depends critically on the nature of the noise (global vs. local), showing that local dynamics alone do not determine the ESD time or its scaling with system size.

Method. The authors analyze entanglement decay using measures like concurrence for both bipartite and multipartite systems, comparing dynamics under different noise models.

Model / system. The study models open quantum systems, specifically using N-qubit GHZ states subjected to different types of environmental noise, such as independent local depolarization versus global depolarization.

Key observables. Concurrence, genuine multipartite entanglement, and the ESD time scale.

Important parameters / regimes. System size N, and the relative strength of local versus global noise coupling.

Assumptions / limitations. The 'suddenness' refers only to the finite time of entanglement vanishing, not a sudden change in the density matrix evolution itself.

Figures summary. The notes reference figures comparing the ESD times for global versus independent local depolarization for N=3 qubits.

Paper structure. The paper revisits the concept of ESD from bipartite systems to genuine multipartite entanglement, first illustrating the mechanism with two qubits, and then comparing the ESD times for N-qubit GHZ states under global versus local depolarization.

Abstract

Entanglement sudden death (ESD), explored through a series of works by Yu and Eberly, supports the notion of entanglement vanishing abruptly and completely at a finite time, even when the underlying open-system dynamics remain smooth. In this article, we revisit the development of ESD from bipartite origins to genuine multipartite entanglement. We first illustrate the canonical mechanism of ESD with two independently damped qubits, for which the concurrence vanishes at a finite time while populations and coherences remain nonzero. We next present our results on multipartite entanglement for generalized GHZ states under depolarization. Global and independent local depolarizing dynamics can be chosen to produce identical reduced evolution for every individual qubit, yet they yield different genuine-multipartite-entanglement sudden death times, whose ordering can reverse with the initial state. For N-qubit GHZ states, this distinction becomes asymptotically stronger since the ESD time scales as 1/N under independent local depolarization, whereas under global depolarization it approaches a finite value in the thermodynamic limit. Echoing a central lesson of Prof. Eberly's work, we report the lifetime of multipartite entanglement is not fixed by the local dynamics, but rather depends on the evolution of the joint quantum state.

Abstract

Dipole-phonon quantum logic (DPQL) offers novel approaches for state preparation, measurement, and control of quantum information in molecular ion qubits. Here we report the first experimental demonstration of DPQL as a spectroscopic tool using the shared motional mode of a CaO$^+$-Ca$^+$ ion chain to measure the frequency splitting of the ground $Ω$-doublet of CaO$^+$. Abstract

Information sharing, threat perception, and collective evasion highlight the advantages of swarm intelligence. Here, we study the behavior of flocks of cognitive agents, based on numerical simulations of the augmented inertial spin model, with an explicit predator. Each agent on the predator-facing side of the flock perceives the threat, triggering collective turns through local interactions. We show that the augmented inertial spin model sustains nearly linear information propagation in the under-damped regime, even with realistic flocking interactions such as local collision avoidance and swarm cohesion in addition to alignment. The turning distance of the flock to a stationary predator scales with the ratio $Δ_p/(v_0\sqrtη)$, identifying an optimal balance between response strength $Δ_p$, flock speed $v_0$, and damping $η$ of information propagation. Fragmentation arises when different parts of the flock turn in different directions, however, fast information propagation can synchronize these responses and maintain flock cohesion while under pursuit. For two predators, a combination of stability analysis and simulations reveals a stark contrast between flock and single-particle reactions, with scattering angle shaped by flock geometry, predator position, and the heterogeneous responses of the detecting front. Together, these results show how information propagation, flock geometry, and dynamics together shapes collective escape, providing general principles relevant to both biological flocks and robotic swarms.

Abstract

In this study, we propose a kinetic trap--diffusion model to describe the emergence of Pareto distributions in money-exchange systems. Using kinetic Monte Carlo simulations, we show that the Pareto exponent depends explicitly on temperature and takes values in the range $0.5 \leq ν(T) \leq 1.5$. In the present framework, the temperature $T$ acts as a control parameter that regulates the exchange dynamics through thermally activated diffusion. Unlike conventional kinetic exchange models, where the Pareto exponent is fixed by microscopic rules, the proposed model generates a range of Pareto exponents as a function of $T$. The temperature-dependent Pareto exponent constitutes the central novelty of the proposed kinetic trap--diffusion framework. This feature provides a natural explanation for the empirically observed variations in Pareto exponents across different countries and economic conditions. In addition, the fraction of zero-wealth agents and the Gini index exhibit a non-monotonic dependence on temperature, revealing distinct dynamical regimes arising from the competition between trapping and money mobility. The persistence of the Pareto-like stationary distribution under strongly nonuniform initial conditions further supports the robustness of the proposed mechanism. These results show that different Pareto-tail and inequality regimes can emerge from the same trap--diffusion dynamics through changes in a single control parameter

Abstract

Motivated by the recent observation of spatially modulated screening in different paramagnetic heavy-fermion materials, we propose a theoretical mechanism for the emergence of such hybridization waves in frustrated Kondo lattices with no magnetic order. By applying a parton mean-field theory and a perturbative calculation to a minimal Kondo-Heisenberg lattice model, we demonstrate that a heavy Fermi liquid can naturally develop hybridization waves when it approaches a quantum phase transition where Kondo screening breaks down and the local magnetic moments form a Dirac spin liquid. We relate the strength of this mechanism to a kinematic condition on the electronic and spinon band structures and show that the spatial periodicity of the hybridization wave is set by a momentum $\vec{Q}$ connecting the nodes in the dispersion of the Dirac spinons to low-energy portions of the electronic band structure.

Summary. This paper proposes using a continuous-variable flag—an auxiliary oscillator—to protect bosonic quantum error correction codes from continuous errors caused by ancillary qubit decay. Main result. The authors introduce a continuous-variable flag mechanism, which successfully transforms continuous noise channels into discrete, correctable error channels, achieving high levels of error suppression.

Abstract

Bosonic quantum error correction represents a route towards significant hardware overhead reduction for fault-tolerant quantum computation. However, controlling quantum harmonic oscillators that encode the quantum information comes with its own challenges. In many platforms such as superconducting circuits and trapped ions, the bosonic degree of freedom is controlled by an ancillary qubit whose decay during a qubit-oscillator gate propagates to the oscillator as a continuous error. Such errors generally lie beyond the scope of errors that the bosonic code is capable of correcting. We introduce the continuous-variable flag: an auxiliary oscillator coupled to the ancilla whose phase-space position encodes the continuous error corrupting the storage mode. After the gate, a heterodyne measurement of the flag estimates this continuous-variable error and a subsequent feedback control undoes it up to a bosonic code stabilizer. The scheme requires no new ingredients, as the flag is driven by the same dispersive coupling, displacements and rotations that implement the gates it is supposed to protect. We construct sequences protecting conditional rotations to all orders in ancilla decay with one flag, and conditional displacements to first order with one flag and to all orders with two. Simulations with realistic circuit-QED parameters predict more than three orders of magnitude fewer logical errors for the parity measurement of a four-component cat code, and a finite-energy GKP logical lifetime near its noiseless-ancilla bound. By discretizing continuous-variable errors, the flags introduced in this work are a useful tool for any hybrid oscillator-qubit processor, and open the route towards fault-tolerant simulation and computation with bosonic modes.
