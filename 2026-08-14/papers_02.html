Summary. This theoretical paper rigorously analyzes the spectral stability of Weyl--Heisenberg measurements relative to the ideal SIC benchmark. By developing explicit constructions in various dimensions (cyclic, finite-field), the authors derive quantitative lower bounds on the minimum eigenvalue ($\lambda$) and show that these measurements approach optimal performance asymptotically without requiring the existence of a true SIC state.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides rigorous, constructive methods to quantify measurement performance metrics like local Fisher efficiency and canonical error bounds, which are crucial for designing optimal quantum state tomography protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to quantify the spectral stability of minimal Weyl--Heisenberg (WH) measurements, determining how closely they can approach the ideal Symmetric Informationally Complete (SIC) benchmark without assuming SIC existence.

Main result. The authors construct a hierarchy of minimal measurements showing that for prime-power dimensions $p \ge 5$, perturbations of cubic Alltop states yield spectra confined to $[L_q, U_q]$ with the ratio $U_q/L_q$ approaching 1, and $\lambda(\phi_q)/\Lambda^\star_q o 1$.

Method. The analysis heavily relies on spectral theory applied to the projector-Gram matrix derived from WH measurements, utilizing Fourier transforms, max-min optimization, and specialized algebraic techniques for cyclic and finite-field correlations.

Model / system. The system under study is quantum measurement theory using minimal rank-one Weyl--Heisenberg (WH) measurements in a $d$-dimensional Hilbert space. The quality of the measurement is diagnosed via the spectrum of the Projector Gram matrix, $ ext{Spec}(G_\Pi^\phi)$.

Key observables. Smallest nonidentity eigenvalue ($\lambda(\phi)$), spectral stability measure ($\eta(\phi)$), and the canonical Mean-Squared Error ratio ($R(\phi)$).

Important parameters / regimes. Dimension $d$, prime power dimension $q=p^k$ with characteristic $p \ge 5$, and the SIC benchmark value.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis often proceeds by assuming specific algebraic structures (e.g., cyclic or finite-field settings) to derive bounds, though it aims to provide results without assuming exact SIC existence.

Figures summary. Figures compare spectral stability ($\eta$) across different dimensions and plot the exact MSE ratio ($R$) for canonical linear inversion against the WH-SIC benchmark ($R=1$).

Paper structure. The paper establishes the relationship between IC, uniform stability, and the SIC endpoint using the projector-Gram matrix spectrum. It then develops specific constructions (cyclic, finite-field) to derive quantitative bounds on $\lambda(\phi)$ and $\eta(\phi)$, culminating in results for prime-power dimensions.

Abstract

Informational completeness (IC) guarantees that an inverse exists, not that it is statistically well conditioned. For minimal rank-one Weyl--Heisenberg (WH) measurements, covariance makes the nonidentity projector-Gram spectrum proportional to the fiducial's ambiguity intensities, with eigenvalues \(d|χ_φ(u)|^2\), turning stability into an explicit worst-direction design problem; write \(λ\) for its smallest nonidentity eigenvalue. Haar fiducials are IC almost surely while \(\mathbb E[λ^{-1}]=\infty\), and an explicit geometric family used to establish balanced informationally complete measurements in every dimension has a normalized spectral floor bounded above by an exponentially decaying envelope. We then construct a hierarchy of minimal measurements. A cyclic family with exactly \(d^2\) outcomes in every integer dimension has floors \(Θ(d^{-3})\) for odd \(d\) and \(Θ(d^{-5})\) for even \(d\); a finite-field family for \(q=2^m\) obeys the uniform bound \(λ\ge4/9\). Our main result treats every prime-power dimension of characteristic \(p\ge5\). A balanced one-coordinate perturbation repairs the zero ambiguity axis of a cubic Alltop state, gives an attained floor uniformly bounded below by a positive constant, and confines the entire nonidentity spectrum to \([L_q,U_q]\) with \(U_q/L_q\to1\). Its SIC-normalized minimum tends to one, and \(λ(φ_q)/Λ_q^\star\to1\) for the global finite-field WH max--min optimum \(Λ_q^\star\), without assuming SIC existence. The complete spectrum determines the exact finite-sample Hilbert--Schmidt error of canonical linear inversion at \(I/d\), while its lower edge controls local Fisher efficiency and canonical-shadow bounds.

Main result. Parity mapping improves both continuous-time Quantum Annealing by increasing spectral gaps and discrete QAOA by achieving constant layer scaling, outperforming conventional methods in complexity metrics.

Model / system. The system is the frustrated Ising ring, modeled by a target Hamiltonian $\hat{H}_{\text{target}}$. Abstract

The frustrated Ising ring is one of the simplest models exhibiting exponential closing spectral gaps, making it a paradigmatic and challenging benchmark for quantum annealing (QA). Ground-state preparation for this model has therefore been studied extensively in both continuous-time QA and digitized protocols such as the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA). Here, we use the frustrated Ising ring to investigate how the parity mapping affects the performance of both QA and QAOA. For QA, finite-size calculations show that the parity mapping increases the minimum spectral gap under the energy normalization used in this work, thereby enabling faster continuous-time ground state preparation protocols. An ideal implementation of Parity-QA, with a single global constraint, shows no evidence of exponential gap closing over the accessible system sizes, whereas a hardware-motivated decomposition into local constraints restores the exponential decrease, albeit with a smaller fitted exponent than conventional QA. For the digitized protocol, we find that the number of Parity-QAOA layers required to prepare the exact ground state remains constant over the simulated sizes, improving upon the quadratic scaling required by conventional QAOA. To investigate the role of constraints in Parity-QAOA, we further consider a modified Ising ring instance in which the constraint term is essential for preparing the target ground state. We then compare the corresponding resource requirements with those of conventional QAOA.

Summary. This theoretical study analyzes quetzalcoatlite, an ideal kagome magnet, to understand its magnetic ground state. Main result. The competition between nearest-neighbor intralayer and next-nearest-neighbor interlayer couplings stabilizes an intrinsic three-dimensional chiral magnetic order characterized by a $60^\circ$ spin rotation between adjacent layers.

Method. The authors employed ab initio calculations (DFT+U) to derive effective Heisenberg exchange interactions, which were then used to minimize the classical magnetic Hamiltonian.

Model / system. The system is modeled as an ideal kagome magnet structure within quetzalcoatlite. The low-energy physics is mapped onto a generalized Heisenberg model incorporating multiple competing exchange couplings ($J_\alpha$) up to the 5th nearest neighbor.

Key observables. Chiral magnetic order, propagation vector Q, static and dynamic spin structure factors, and electronic band gap.

Important parameters / regimes. The relative strengths of the intralayer vs. interlayer exchange couplings ($J_2$ vs $J_3$), which determine the transition between different ordered states.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the crystal structure showing the kagome lattice; one figure shows the opening of the electronic band gap upon including correlation effects (GGA vs GGA+U); others map out the resulting $\sqrt{3} imes \sqrt{3}$ coplanar magnetic order with layer-to-layer $60^\circ$ rotation.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the material as a suitable platform, then uses DFT+U to calculate exchange couplings ($J_\alpha$). Abstract

The natural mineral quetzalcoatlite Zn$_6$Cu$_3$(TeO$_6$)$_2$(OH)$_6$ $\cdot$ (Ag$_x$Pb$_y$Cl$_{x+2y}$) is a structurally ideal kagome magnet, providing a platform for exploring the interplay of geometric frustration, chirality, and tunability in a disorder-free framework. Here, we present the (first) comprehensive ab initio study of its electronic and magnetic properties. The electronic structure is dominated by localized half-filled Cu $d_{x^2-y^2}$ orbitals that become insulating through electronic correlations. Mapping the low-energy physics onto a Heisenberg model reveals that the magnetism is governed primarily by two exchange interactions: a nearest-neighbor intralayer kagome coupling and a next-nearest-neighbor interlayer coupling. Their competition stabilizes an unconventional three-dimensional chiral magnetic state. Each kagome layer hosts a $\sqrt{3}\times\sqrt{3}$ order, while adjacent layers are rotated by $60^\circ$, producing a right-handed spiral along the crystallographic $c$-axis. This intrinsic chiral order emerges naturally from the crystal structure and magnetic interactions, establishing quetzalcoatlite as a distinctive realization of chiral magnetism on a perfect kagome lattice. At the same time, the small energy scale of the exchange interactions places the material close to competing magnetic regimes, suggesting that moderate pressure, chemical substitution, or structural modifications may strongly enhance frustration, suppress long-range order, and potentially drive the system toward a quantum spin-liquid state.

Summary. This paper establishes that reconstructing an operator of rank $S$ in a spin system requires the measurement sampling points to form a spherical $(2J+S)$-design. It provides explicit constructions and protocols, offering a powerful mathematical tool to guide the design of optimal, minimal-sample measurements for quantum systems like magnetometers.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly impacts quantum measurement theory by providing rigorous, mathematically necessary conditions (spherical designs) for achieving high-fidelity state or observable readouts using minimal measurements, which is crucial for experimental quantum sensing.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core problem is determining the minimal discrete set of measurement points (coherent states) required to exactly reconstruct an arbitrary quantum operator within a specific rank sector ($S$) for spin systems.

Main result. Exact reconstruction is mathematically equivalent to the sampling points forming a spherical $(2J+S)$-design, providing a unified framework for reading out physical observables from limited measurements.

Method. The authors use techniques involving multipole expansions and comparing discrete sums over design points against continuous integral representations of quantum operators.

Model / system. The system is based on spin-$J$ angular momentum systems ($ ext{SU}(2)$), whose phase space is modeled by the sphere $S^2$. Operators are analyzed via their decomposition into irreducible tensor sectors ($V_S$).

Key observables. Spin moments ($\langle J_q angle$), second moment contravariant symbol ($I^{ab}$), and the expectation values of operators in specific rank sectors.

Important parameters / regimes. The spin quantum number $J$, the target operator rank $S$, and the required design strength $t = 2J+S$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is specialized for $ ext{SU}(2)$ systems, focusing on reconstructing specific tensor sectors rather than the entire Hilbert space.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the scaling of known spherical designs ($M$ vs $t$) and confirm that exact reconstruction (black cells) occurs when the design strength $t$ meets or exceeds $2J+S$.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the mathematical equivalence between exact operator reconstruction and the existence of a specific type of spherical design, provides explicit constructions (Gauss-Legendre), and concludes with practical protocols for moment estimation.

Abstract

Coherent states bridge the gap between quantum and classical physics, but their overcomplete and nonorthogonal nature makes it difficult to identify the minimal discrete set needed to reconstruct quantum information. Finite spin-coherent tomography and discrete coherent-state operator bases are known, but here we address the more specific rank-resolved problem of preserving the canonical contravariant-symbol representation. We show that the canonical finite coherent-state formula reconstructs every operator in the rank-$S$ sector exactly if and only if the sampling points form a spherical $(2J+S)$-design. We call the associated configurations spin-coherent quantum designs. We further give a fully explicit positive-weight Gauss-Legendre construction that avoids the need for an equal-weight spherical design. Together, these results establish a unified framework for reading out physical observables from a handful of measurement samples, playing for spin systems the role that the so-called von Neumann lattice plays for canonical coherent states. Finally, we derive practical protocols for estimating moments of spin operators from these constructions, with direct applications to polarimetry, magnetometry, and quantum state tomography.

Abstract

Single organic molecules are a well-established platform for high-quality single-photon generation: they can emit lifetime-limited photons with high purity and indistinguishability. However, their emission is accompanied by a pronounced red-shifted phonon sideband and higher-order vibrational peaks, which reduce the fraction of photons emitted into the desired narrowband zero-phonon line. The standard approach to suppressing this unwanted emission is to integrate the emitter into a monolithic photonic nanostructure that provides Purcell enhancement. However, incorporating organic materials using clean-room techniques has proven challenging, and has in fact so far prevented their integration into monolithic microcavities altogether. As a result, efficient, narrowband organic single-photon sources for applications in quantum information processing have remained elusive despite their considerable promise. Here, we propose three monolithic microcavity designs tailored to provide sufficient Purcell enhancement for organic quantum emitters. The Purcell effect induced by these cavities preferentially enhances emission into the 0-0 zero-phonon line, increasing spectral purity, photon extraction, and shortening the excited-state lifetime, which in turn relaxes the requirements for generating indistinguishable photons. The cavities have been optimized using Bayesian optimization and the adaptive Antoulas-Anderson (AAA) algorithm for rational approximation, which offer global optimization and the efficient reconstruction of spectra from scattering simulations, respectively. These structures are compatible with both standard clean-room processing and single-molecule preparation techniques. Therefore, they offer a clear route to realize high-quality, practically monochromatic organic single-photon light sources.

Abstract

The non-equilibrium properties of open quantum systems are determined by the microscopic laws governing energy exchange with their environment. In particular, an enhancement of the performance of quantum heat engines has been predicted by speeding up the dynamics through control of the system--bath interaction. However, direct microscopic control of heat transfer between the machine and the reservoir has remained elusive so far. Here, we experimentally demonstrate such control in a quantum Otto engine realized with ultracold Cs-133 atoms coupled to an atomic reservoir of ultracold Rb-87 atoms. This enables power output optimization at fixed efficiency. Our results establish microscopic control of system-reservoir interactions as a tool for manipulating heat flow at the nanoscale and engineering the finite-time performance of quantum thermal machines.
