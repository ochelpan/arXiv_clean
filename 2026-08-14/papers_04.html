Authors: Nivedita Dey, Mrityunjay Ghosh, Pranav Kaushal, Abhinab Khare, Amlan Chakrabarti

Summary. The paper introduces an adaptive framework that merges multiple classical cryptanalytic techniques (linear, differential, side-channel) to aggressively filter a large key hypothesis space. This reduction allows subsequent quantum verification using amplitude amplification to operate on a much smaller effective space, drastically cutting down the required computational resources for breaking cryptographic systems.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete theoretical blueprint for optimizing quantum algorithms applied to classical security problems, showing how pre-processing steps can drastically reduce resource requirements (qubits, time) for quantum computation in cryptography.

Abstract

Efficient integration of cryptanalytic evidence with quantum verification remains a fundamental challenge in hybrid classical-quantum cryptanalysis. This work presents a capability-adaptive cryptanalytic framework that unifies linear cryptanalysis, differential cryptanalysis, and side-channel leakage analysis within a common candidate-space reduction architecture, followed by reduced-space quantum verification through amplitude amplification. A formal mathematical model is developed for candidate-space construction, adaptive filtering, verification-space reduction, and complexity characterization, supported by the oretical results establishing the relationship between candidate-space contraction and quantum verification effort. A Hamiltonian formulation is further introduced to provide a physically realizable interpretation of the reduced-space verification process. Evaluation using statistically generated cryptanalytic observations demonstrates that the proposed framework reduces an initial candidate-key hypothesis space of 4096 candidates to an effective candidate space of 13 hypotheses, corresponding to an overall reduction of approximately 99.683%. Consequently, the Grover verification requirement decreases from 50 iterations to only 2 iterations, yielding an approximately 25-fold reduction in verification effort, while reduced-space amplitude amplification achieves a target-state success probability of approximately 94.53%. These results demonstrate that adaptive cryptanalytic filtering can substantially reduce quantum verification complexity while preserving cryptanalytic admissibility, providing a practical foundation for capability-aware hybrid cryptanalysis and reduced-space quantum search.

Summary. This work simulates the melting of stripe phases in a 2D system, finding that density dictates the final fluid topology. The authors demonstrate that simple thermodynamic measurements fail to capture this complexity; only advanced topological observables can distinguish between a fragmented cluster state and a continuous network state.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical soft matter physics, the reliance on topological invariants (like Betti numbers) to characterize phase transitions and connectivity changes mirrors concepts used in analyzing complex quantum many-body ground states or entanglement structures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how the melting of stripe-forming systems changes connectivity, specifically determining if the resulting fluid topology is uniquely determined by the initial ordered state or if it is instead selected by the system's density.

Main result. The topological outcome of the melt—whether the filaments fragment into finite clusters or remain in a dynamically fluid, system-spanning network—is strongly dependent on the particle density ($ ho$).

Method. Molecular dynamics simulations are performed along isochoric heating paths using various structural, orientational, and advanced graph-based topological observables.

Model / system. The model is a two-dimensional system of particles interacting via a Lennard-Jones plus Gaussian (LJG) pair potential, which stabilizes one-particle-thick stripe phases at low temperatures.

Key observables. Topological descriptors like the first Betti number ($eta_1$), graph 2-core fraction ($f_{2- ext{core}}$), and connectivity measures are crucial alongside standard thermodynamic quantities like isochoric heat capacity ($c_V$).

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis spans seven densities ($ ho = 0.395$ to $0.455$) across the stripe-forming regime, as well as varying temperatures during heating.

Assumptions / limitations. Conventional scalar order parameters are insufficient because they cannot distinguish between a system-spanning network and finite clusters when local packing is similar; thus, topological diagnostics are necessary.

Figures summary. Figures summarize the density dependence of structural metrics (like nematic order $S_2$) during melting, showing that while global order decays uniformly, the topological structure diverges based on $ ho$.

Paper structure. The paper analyzes the multistage reconstruction upon heating, contrasting the behavior observed using only thermodynamic/orientational data versus the richer information provided by graph-based topology to distinguish between different fluid states.

Abstract

The melting of stripe-forming systems involves changes in connectivity that are not fully captured by conventional structural and orientational descriptors. We investigate a two-dimensional model with competing interactions whose low-temperature phase consists of one-particle-thick stripes. Molecular dynamics simulations along seven heating isochores are analyzed using thermodynamic, orientational, dynamical, and graph-based observables. Heating produces a multistage reconstruction in which the loss of stripe alignment and the reorganization of filament connectivity occur over distinct temperature ranges. Density controls whether the disordered filaments fragment into finite polymer-like clusters or remain joined in a dynamically fluid, system-spanning network. The topological observables distinguish these outcomes, which are not resolved by the thermodynamic and orientational responses alone. Thus, the same ordered stripe microphase can melt into topologically distinct fluids selected by density.

Abstract

A multiscale iteration promotes this local inequality to a global obstruction to bulk connectivity. For parallel strips in AdS$_5$/CFT$_4$, the bound necessitates a quantum resolution of the classical mutual-information transition and fixes the asymptotic growth of geodesic distance.

Summary. This theoretical paper analyzes the spectral properties of quantum Hamiltonians used to encode NP-hard problems like Set Cover. It rigorously distinguishes between the full symmetry-allowed spectrum and the subspace actually reachable by the system's dynamics. The work provides structural guarantees regarding adiabatic evolution times, establishing a fundamental separation between potential symmetries and actual physical accessibility.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous separation of accessible spectral structure is highly relevant to understanding the limitations and guarantees of quantum computation protocols based on Hamiltonian simulation or annealing.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The research addresses which parts of a Hamiltonian spectrum are physically relevant when both the initial state preparation and subsequent time evolution preserve multiple symmetries, specifically for encoding Minimum Set Cover.

Main result. The authors establish a rigorous structural separation between the global symmetry-allowed spectral structure and the dynamically accessible cyclic subspace. They derive bounds on the adiabatic runtime conditional on specific state preparations.

Method. The analysis employs advanced mathematical tools including sector-resolved localization, analyzing algebraic structures generated by Hamiltonians, and deriving gap estimates using techniques related to Dirichlet forms and coupling arguments.

Model / system. The model is a compact multi-register quantum encoding of the Minimum Set Cover problem. The dynamics are governed by complex Hamiltonians involving walk terms, cover constraints, and exclusion terms acting on multiple registers.

Key observables. Joint fixed excitation gap, sector-resolved leakage bounds ($\Gamma_\gamma$), and the dynamically accessible cyclic space ($\mathcal{K}_u$).

Important parameters / regimes. The size of the problem instance ($n$), the number of registers, and parameters governing the Hamiltonian components ($\lambda, \mu$).

Assumptions / limitations. No quantum speedup claim is made; the results are structural separations. The analysis relies on specific conditions for symmetry sectors and state preparation (e.g., Dicke-state preparation).

Paper structure. The paper progresses by defining the physical system and Hamiltonian, establishing the relationship between different spectral subspaces ($H_{ ext{sym}}$ vs $\mathcal{K}_u$), proving localization bounds (Proposition 1), constructing explicit parent paths for specific families, and finally deriving polynomial adiabatic runtime estimates.

Abstract

Which part of a Hamiltonian spectrum is physically relevant when both the initial state and interpolation preserve several symmetries? We address this question for a compact multi-register encoding of Minimum Set Cover. The represented symmetry combines register permutations with the faithful base action of the incidence automorphism group. We distinguish its fixed, symmetry-allowed space from the generally smaller cyclic space generated by the protocol and define gaps relative to isolated bands in that space. A sector-resolved Schur-complement bound certifies cover-measurement probability whenever the sector contains feasible covers and has positive invalid-state separation; the sector kinetic floor is essential. Exact orbit quotients expose inter-sector coincidences invisible to a symmetric protocol, while a stability theorem shows that transverse dark crossings persist under small symmetry-preserving perturbations. On an even-cycle family, the original linear interpolation has an exact global multiplicity closure while the joint-fixed excitation gap remains constant. For the same family we construct a Johnson/Metropolis parent path from a Dicke state to a Gibbs-amplitude state with cover probability $1-O(n^{-5})$. A zero-range comparison and a log-concave three-box coupling give the uniform cyclic-gap certificate $Ω(n^{-13})$. Quantitative derivative bounds then yield a polynomial adiabatic runtime in the abstract Hamiltonian-access model, conditional on Dicke-state preparation and access to the parent Hamiltonian. We make no quantum-speedup claim: the result is a rigorous separation of global, symmetry-allowed, and dynamically accessible spectral structure. Changing the initial state or breaking a preserved symmetry changes that accessible spectrum.

Summary. This paper develops queueing models to analyze the capacity of memory-based quantum repeaters, balancing the trade-off between high entanglement throughput and maintaining a minimum required fidelity. Main problem. The study focuses on optimizing the End-to-End (E2E) entanglement throughput of a memory-based quantum repeater while satisfying a minimum required fidelity constraint.

Main result. Optimal performance requires treating the maximum waiting times in quantum memories as optimization variables, leading to model-derived optimal holding times that significantly boost achievable throughput compared to unconstrained scenarios.

Method. The authors employ queueing models and stochastic process theory (e.g., CTMC) to approximate the system's rate dynamics under decoherence constraints.

Important parameters / regimes. Memory capacities, entanglement attempt rates, classical communication latencies, and the maximum waiting times (MHTs) in quantum memories.

Paper structure. The paper develops queueing models to approximate E2E throughput, derives an expression for E2E fidelity accounting for memory noise and BSM errors, formulates the problem as maximizing rate subject to fidelity constraints, and validates this using simulations.

Abstract

We study the capacity of a memory-based quantum repeater in entanglement swapping between two quantum links with either single or multiple memories, which we refer to as the end-to-end (E2E) entanglement throughput, subject to a constraint on the minimum fidelity. In order to approximate the E2E entanglement throughput, we adopt queueing models, where quantum links can have different characteristics: memory capacities, entanglement attempt rates and success probabilities, as well as classical communication latencies. We develop a model for estimating E2E entanglement fidelity, while taking into account the heterogeneous dephasing and depolarizing dynamics of quantum memories and Bell-state measurements in entanglement swapping as well as classical communication delays and noises. Finally, with the help of our models for approximating the E2E entanglement throughput and fidelity, we use the maximum waiting times of entanglements in quantum memories at the repeater as optimization variables to maximize the E2E entanglement throughput while ensuring required minimum E2E fidelity.

Emergent heavy fermion and superconductivity near Mott transition in twisted bilayer graphene

Authors: Ya-Hui Zhang
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.12319v1

Summary. This work develops a comprehensive theoretical model to describe emergent heavy fermion physics and superconductivity near the Mott transition in twisted bilayer graphene. By treating the system through an emergent Kondo coupling mechanism involving mixed-valence excitations, the authors predict the formation of a heavy semimetal phase and unconventional superconducting domes at charge neutrality. This establishes a powerful, unified framework for correlated materials research.

Why it may be interesting. The development of a unified framework connecting Mott transitions, heavy fermion behavior, and unconventional superconductivity in a tunable solid-state system like TBG is highly relevant for understanding strongly correlated electron physics beyond simple Hubbard models.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The research investigates emergent heavy fermion behavior and superconductivity that arise near a Mott transition in twisted bilayer graphene (TBG).

Main result. The authors establish a unified framework showing how mixed-valence physics leads to an emergent Kondo screening transition, resulting in a heavy semimetal phase and unconventional superconductivity.

Method. The analysis employs an emergent heavy-fermion framework utilizing an 'ancilla' theory involving orthogonal fermions ($\psi$) to capture mixed-valence Mott states. Techniques include Schrieffer-Wolff elimination, mean-field theory for pairing, and self-consistent determination of excitation weights.

Model / system. The system is Twisted Bilayer Graphene (TBG) modeled by an extended Hamiltonian involving itinerant carriers ($c$), local moments ($\psi'$), and charge excitations treated via doublon/holon operators. The physics is analyzed near integer fillings $ u$ relative to the Mott transition.

Key observables. Quasiparticle residue ($Z$), Fermi velocity ($v_F$), superconducting gap structure ($\Delta_{SC}$), Kondo temperature ($T_K$), and momentum-resolved occupations of doublon/holon excitations.

Important parameters / regimes. Hybridization coupling ($\gamma$) vs. on-site repulsion ($U$); twist angle ($ heta$); doping level ($ u$); and the anti-Hund's coupling ($J_A$).

Assumptions / limitations. The theory relies on an emergent heavy-fermion description derived from mixed-valence physics, using approximations like canonical fermions in a mixed state ensemble.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by defining the effective heavy-fermion model using ancilla fermions, deriving the Kondo coupling mechanism from mixed-valence physics, calculating the resulting low-energy semimetal state below $T_K$, and finally incorporating anti-Hund's coupling to predict superconductivity.

Abstract

Near a bandwidth-tuned Mott transition, the Fermi velocity $v_F$ and quasiparticle residue $Z$ of a metal often vanish. Here, we show that analogous phenomena emerge in twisted bilayer graphene (TBG) at integer fillings and can be captured by an emergent heavy-fermion framework within a projective active-band limit. Unlike models incorporating remote bands, our effective heavy-fermion description arises from \textit{mixed-valence Mott} physics via decoupled charge and local-moment sectors. In the charge sector, active bands $c(\mathbf{k})$ hybridize with an emergent \emph{orthogonal fermion} $ψ(\mathbf{k})$ to open a large Mott gap at $|\mathbf{k}| > k_*$ ($k_*$ sets the momentum-patch size) and a quadratic band-touching semimetal near $\mathbf{k}=0$ at neutrality ($ν=0$). The orthogonal fermion is a linear combination of the doublon and holon excitations and may be written as $ψ_i \sim (δn^f_i+\frac{1}{2})^{-1} f_i$. An emergent Kondo coupling $J_K \sim U$ ($U$ is the local Hubbard interaction) between $ψ$ and local moments $ψ'$ frames the Mott transition as a Kondo screening transition, tuned by the twist angle $θ$. Away from the magic angle, a Kondo-screened heavy semimetal develops below $T_K$ (the Kondo temperature) with vanishing $Z$. Introducing anti-Hund's coupling $J_A$ generates an s-wave fully gapped or nematic, nodally gapped superconducting dome near the Mott boundary even at $ν=0$. At other integer fillings $ν= \pm 1, \pm 2$, increasing bandwidth first drives the small-gap Mott state into an intermediate quadratic band-touching semimetal before entering a heavy Fermi liquid with large Fermi surfaces. Our results establish a unified framework for emergent heavy fermion physics with both itinerant carriers and local moments from the $f$ orbital.

Probing Impurity Quantum Criticality with Entanglement Witnesses

Authors: Mateo Cárdenes Wuttig, Andrew J. Millis

Summary. The paper investigates quantum criticality in the two-impurity Kondo model by using entanglement measures as witnesses. By calculating observables like Quantum Fisher Information and various correlation functions across different coupling regimes, the authors demonstrate that these measurable quantities exhibit singular behavior precisely at the predicted quantum critical point. This establishes a robust theoretical framework for detecting exotic quantum phases in solid-state systems.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly connects abstract quantum information concepts (entanglement) to concrete, measurable physical quantities (susceptibilities, fluctuations), providing a powerful diagnostic tool for strongly correlated materials like heavy fermions.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to determine if measurable spin and charge fluctuations can serve as entanglement witnesses to detect quantum critical points (QCPs) in impurity systems.

Main result. The analysis confirms that the Quantum Fisher Information (QFI), derived from measurable correlations, tracks the entanglement changes across the non-Fermi-liquid QCP separating Kondo-screened and inter-impurity singlet phases.

Method. The authors employ advanced numerical techniques like Density-Matrix Renormalization Group (DMRG) and Numerical Renormalization Group (NRG) to calculate entanglement measures and QFI at zero and finite temperatures.

Model / system. The primary model is the two-impurity Kondo model, consisting of two spin-1/2 impurities coupled by an exchange interaction ($K$) and each coupled independently to a bath of conduction electrons via Kondo couplings ($J$).

Key observables. Entanglement entropy ($S(A_{ ext{imp}})$), impurity-impurity correlator ($\langle \mathbf{S}_1 \cdot \mathbf{S}_2 angle$), Quantum Fisher Information (QFI) for spin and charge operators.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the Hamiltonian in different coupling regimes ($J$ vs $K$), calculates ground-state and finite-temperature expectation values using DMRG/NRG, and then maps these results onto entanglement measures and QFI to pinpoint the critical point.

Abstract

Entanglement is a defining feature of quantum mechanics, and its relation to quantum criticality is of considerable current interest. Here we show that measurable spin and charge fluctuations provide an entanglement witness of quantum criticality in the two-impurity Kondo model, which has experimental realizations in terms of coupled quantum dots and magnetic impurities added to surfaces. We use density-matrix renormalization group and numerical renormalization group calculations to resolve the non-Fermi-liquid critical point separating two independently Kondo-screened impurities from an inter-impurity singlet and interpret it as a change in the dominant entanglement partner of each local moment: from entanglement of the local moment with an extended set of conduction-electron degrees of freedom to entanglement with the other impurity. This reorganization is accompanied by a singular response of the impurity-bath entanglement and the inter-impurity susceptibility. We show how the same structure is encoded in the quantum Fisher information of collective spin and charge operators at zero and finite temperatures, connecting the entanglement picture to experimentally accessible dynamical response functions. Our results establish impurity systems as controlled settings in which quantum-critical entanglement can be detected through measurable correlations, and provide further insight into the possibility of understanding heavy-fermion physics in terms of entanglement.

Summary. The paper rigorously analyzes the passage from crystalline topological phases on a lattice to their corresponding continuum field theories. Using advanced algebraic topology, it proves that while every continuum phase can be realized on a lattice, the process loses information, meaning distinct lattice configurations can map to the same continuous description.

Main problem. The central problem is rigorously determining whether the topological properties of gapped bosonic systems defined on a discrete lattice (crystalline phases) survive when taking the continuum limit to form a field theory.

Main result. The map from crystalline to continuum topological phases is surjective, meaning every continuum phase has a lattice realization; however, it is generally not injective, implying that some microscopic lattice information collapses in the continuum description.

Method. The authors employ advanced mathematical tools, including algebraic topology and category theory, to construct a rigorous stratification map relating crystalline classification groups ($\Theta^{d+1}(BG)$) to continuum ones ($\Theta^{d+1}( ext{BO}(d))$).

Model / system. The study focuses on gapped bosonic phases of matter characterized by finite point-group symmetries ($G \subset O(d)$), analyzing the relationship between lattice topological invariants and their effective low-energy continuum field theories.

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically illustrates a surjective 'funnel' mapping the classification of crystalline SPTs onto the continuum SPT classification.

Paper structure. The paper develops the mathematical framework by defining the map between lattice and continuum classifications, proving its surjectivity, analyzing the loss of information (non-injectivity), and applying this general theory to specific physical examples like 2D rotation-symmetric phases and quantum critical points.

Abstract

The continuum limit is a widely used theoretical construct for obtaining continuum field-theories of crystalline systems. We study the formulation of a continuum limit for gapped bosonic phases and ask whether their topological properties survive passage to the continuum. Using methods in algebraic topology and category theory, we give a rigorous formulation of the continuum limit and construct a surjective global map relating crystalline topological phases across all finite point-group symmetries to continuum invertible topological phases. Consequently, we find that some lattice phases admit no continuum limit, while distinct lattice phases that do admit a continuum limit can share the same continuum image, hence implying that some lattice topological data can collapse. Conversely, we find that every continuum invertible phase admits a faithful crystalline realization. In addition to the general framework, we apply it to several examples, including studies of a 2D rotation-symmetric phase, higher-order topological phases, and the mixed spin-lattice anomaly of the deconfined quantum critical point between an antiferromagnet and valence bond solid.

Abstract

We study the power of quantum examples, as compared to classical examples, in the PAC learning framework. Here, we have two learning algorithms, both with access to quantum computation, but one gets quantum examples, whereas the other gets classical examples. It was previously unknown whether there were learning tasks that can be efficiently performed but not by the latter. Our primary result is to show that relative to an oracle, there are distributions that can be efficiently generated by a quantum learner with access to quantum examples, but not by a quantum learner with access to only classical examples, making progress to answering this question in the affirmative.

Abstract

We study the strong-coupling Birman-Schwinger analysis of the three-boson lattice Schroedinger operator at the exceptional quasimomentum K = pi. First, we provide an exact closed-form benchmark for the fiber Fredholm determinant at a distinguished flat momentum, valid for every quasimomentum K, obtained via a uniform elliptic reduction of the two-dimensional lattice integral. Second, we formulate and prove a general order-matching criterion. This criterion decides whether a leading-order Fredholm determinant asymptotic, with relative accuracy of order 1/mu, suffices to determine the constant-order additive correction in the strong-coupling energy, or if the next-order refinement is required. Applying the criterion at K = pi, we derive the complete strong-coupling ground-state asymptotics, including the coefficient of the order 1/mu term. The results are cross-validated against the exact closed-form benchmark and two independent high-precision numerical schemes. Furthermore, we determine the corresponding spectral gap and prove an exact Fredholm-determinant underestimation factor equal to 2. Additionally, we independently confirm that the previously established K = 0 constant is correctly derived and requires no analogous refinement. Finally, we compare the orders of asymptotic precision controlled across recent lattice few-body models and draw a structural parallel with the parity classification of topological band structures.
