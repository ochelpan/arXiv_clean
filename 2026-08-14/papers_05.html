Efficient Compilation for Hamiltonian Simulation via Global Binary Symplectic Form Simplification

Authors: Zhaohui Yang, Yuwei Han, Ruiyun Zhang, Dawei Ding, Jianxin Chen, Yuan Feng, Yuan Xie

Summary. This paper presents Symphony, an advanced compiler that optimizes Hamiltonian simulation circuits by treating the entire Pauli sequence as a global algebraic structure represented by the Binary Symplectic Form (BSF). By applying controlled-Pauli Clifford transformations directly to this BSF tableau, it achieves superior circuit compression compared to existing methods. This results in substantial reductions in required two-qubit gates and overall circuit depth for quantum algorithms.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful algorithmic tool for quantum circuit compilation, directly impacting the feasibility of running complex algorithms like VQE or QAOA on near-term hardware by minimizing gate count and depth.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core challenge is efficiently compiling Hamiltonian simulation workloads, which are expressed as Pauli exponential sequences, by fully exploiting their global algebraic structure.

Main result. The proposed method achieves significant reductions in two-qubit gate count (average 59%) and circuit depth (average 91%) compared to naïve synthesis on standard benchmarks.

Method. It introduces Symphony, a holistic compilation approach utilizing the Binary Symplectic Form (BSF) representation of Pauli strings. This involves applying generalized controlled-Pauli Clifford transformations directly to the global BSF tableau for simultaneous simplification and parallelism extraction.

Model / system. The work focuses on quantum computation simulating time evolution governed by Hamiltonians expressed as weighted sums of Pauli strings ($\sum c_k P_k$). The input is a sequence of Pauli rotations, analyzed using the BSF representation which encodes commutation relations via symplectic geometry.

Key observables. Two-qubit gate count and circuit depth are the primary optimization metrics. Other diagnostics include the BSF weight reduction and the rank of interaction matrices for two-qubit blocks.

Important parameters / regimes. The efficiency is measured by factors like $1.14–1.58 imes$ improvement in CNOT gates and $1.87–5.67 imes$ improvement in circuit depth over prior state-of-the-art compilers.

Assumptions / limitations. Prior methods are limited by constrained paradigms, such as strict adherence to 'first-diagonalize, then-search-CNOT' or computationally expensive long-horizon searches; Symphony overcomes these limitations holistically.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the comparison between naïve synthesis and simultaneous simplification via Clifford conjugation on a BSF tableau. Other figures detail how Clifford transformations act as symplectic updates on the BSF structure.

Paper structure. The paper develops theory around Pauli commutation using the symplectic form, introduces Symphony's global BSF transformation framework, details the causality-preserving block rescheduling heuristic, and validates its performance against state-of-the-art compilers across multiple benchmarks.

Abstract

Hamiltonian simulation is a core quantum workload, underpinning variational quantum algorithms and Trotterized time evolution. Such programs are expressed as Pauli exponential sequences, exhibiting structural patterns that are highly amenable to high-level synthesis and optimization. Existing compilers, however, fail to fully unlock the optimization potential of their global algebraic structure, even when employing advanced graph- or tableau-based methods. We present Symphony, a holistic compilation approach built on the binary symplectic form (BSF) representation of Pauli strings. Unlike prior group-wise BSF simplification and path-based Pauli network synthesis, Symphony applies generalized controlled-Pauli Clifford transformations directly to a global BSF tableau, adaptively reducing active Pauli rows and emitting eligible two-qubit blocks other than single-qubit rotations in a forward Clifford frame. Following algebraic simplification, Symphony performs a causality-preserving block rescheduling heuristic that respects frame-induced dependencies while exposing extensive two-qubit block parallelism opportunities. This streamlined compilation style comprehensively exploits simultaneous simplification and commutativity opportunities, achieving efficient global optimization without relying on computationally expensive heuristics or long-horizon searches. Across the generic Hamiltonian simulation benchmarks in HamLib, Symphony achieves average reductions of 59% in two-qubit gate count and 91% in circuit depth. It strictly Pareto-dominates prior state-of-the-art compilers, requiring 1.14--1.58$\times$ fewer two-qubit gates and especially shrinking two-qubit circuit depth by a substantial factor of 1.87--5.67$\times$ on average.

Abstract

Modular arithmetic is central to quantum algorithms for cryptographic problems, including Shor's algorithm and Grover-based cryptanalysis, with modular reduction contributing substantially to circuit cost. Pseudo-Mersenne moduli $q=2^n-c$ allow classical Crandall reduction to replace division with folding and constant arithmetic, providing a structural opportunity for more efficient quantum modular reduction than Barrett reduction. We translate this advantage into a reversible quantum setting by deriving explicit folding and normalization conditions for $2n$-bit inputs. To the best of our knowledge, this constitutes the first exact reversible quantum circuit formulation of Crandall reduction. Based on this formulation, we develop two variants: Crandall reduction-1 is designed to minimize execution cost through one-step normalization, whereas Crandall reduction-2 uses two-step normalization to support a wider range of $c$ with limited overhead. Logical resource estimates show that both variants require fewer qubits and lower T-count and T-depth than optimized folding Barrett reduction. At $n=10$, Crandall reduction-1 reduces both T-count and T-depth by approximately 46.9% relative to optimized folding Barrett reduction. Surface-code analysis further shows that, at $n=20$ under the Sparse Blossom decoder, the estimated runtimes of the two variants are 30.05 ms and 35.39 ms, respectively, compared with 53.77 ms for optimized folding Barrett reduction. These results demonstrate the practical value of exploiting modulus-specific arithmetic structure in fault-tolerant quantum circuit design.

Summary. This paper rigorously proves that evaluating expectation values for QAOA circuits at depth $p \geq 2$ is computationally intractable, specifically showing it is #P-hard. By constructing specialized graph gadgets, the authors reduce the problem of counting maximum cuts to calculating a specific coefficient in the QAOA's resulting Laurent polynomial. This establishes a significant complexity barrier for variational quantum algorithms.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a strong theoretical barrier, suggesting that simulating or optimizing complex quantum algorithms beyond shallow circuits requires computational resources equivalent to solving hard counting problems.

Main problem. The core problem is determining the computational complexity of evaluating expectation values for variational quantum algorithms, specifically QAOA applied to MaxCut.

Main result. The authors prove that for fixed depth $p \geq 2$, evaluating these expectation values (and related quantities like gradients/Hessians) is #P-hard, meaning it is as hard as counting optimal solutions.

Method. The hardness proof refines previous work by constructing specialized 'counting gadgets' within the QAOA framework. This allows mapping the problem of counting maximum cuts ($\# ext{MC}(G)$) to the coefficient extraction from a Laurent polynomial derived from the expectation value.

Model / system. The study focuses on the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) applied to finding the MaxCut of a graph $G$. The system is described by cost and mixer Hamiltonians, leading to quantum states $|\psi_p(\gamma, eta) angle$ at depth $p$.

Key observables. The primary observable is the cost expectation value $F_p(G; \gamma, eta) = \langle\psi_p|C_G|\psi_p angle$, and also single pairwise correlators $\langle Z_r Z_s angle$.

Important parameters / regimes. The depth parameter $p$ is critical: $p=1$ is tractable ($\in ext{FP}$), while fixed $p \geq 2$ exhibits #P-hardness.

Assumptions / limitations. The hardness results apply to worst-case families of graphs and require evaluating the expectation value with exponential precision ($\epsilon = 2^{-cN}$).

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates a balanced counting gadget for $K_3$, demonstrating how the number of maximum cuts in the constructed graph relates directly to the original graph's count.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes tractability for $p=1$ (Chunk 4). It then builds upon this by introducing specialized gadgets and mathematical tools (Laurent polynomials) to prove that exact evaluation becomes #P-hard for fixed $p \geq 2$ (Chunks 1 & 2). Finally, it extends the hardness result to computing derivatives like gradients and Hessians (Chunk 3).

Abstract

Evaluating expectation values is a critical task for variational quantum eigensolvers, and for parameterized quantum circuits and other quantum algorithms more generally. We consider the well-studied case of the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) for the MaxCut problem. Recent work of Wang et al. [arXiv:2511.20212] showed this task to be NP-hard in general for any QAOA depth $p\geq 2$, complementing past results showing efficiently computable formulas for $p=1$ with arbitrary problem graphs. We sharpen this dichotomy showing that for $p\geq 2$ exact or exponentially precise cost expectation value evaluation is #P-hard under deterministic polynomial-time Turing reductions. Hardness at $p\geq 2$ is shown to remain even for evaluating single pairwise correlators $\langle Z\otimes Z\rangle $, as well as for highly restricted sets of algorithm parameters. Our proof refines the NP-hardness construction of Wang et al. that recovers the maximum cut value from the largest exponent of a QAOA Laurent polynomial, utilizing a distinct and simpler construction that extracts a value proportional to the total number of maximum cuts, in addition to the optimal cut value. Thus we show that the QAOA expectation value hardness transition from $p=1$ to $p=2$ is not only from tractability to optimization hardness, but to that of counting optimal solutions. As an application we show our results imply analogous hardness results for computing gradients and Hessians of QAOA circuits.

Abstract

Given a particular heat engine cycle, what is the optimal working medium that results in the highest efficiency? While one might jump to the conclusion that it must surely be the ideal gas, the situation is actually more intricate. Starting with a general Hemlholtz potential that depends polynomially on molar volume and temperature we derive exact expressions for the ideal Stirling, Otto, and Brayton cycles. We find that quite universally the maximal efficiency is achieved for thermodynamics systems, whose working mediums are linear in temperature. This includes the ideal gas, but also classical harmonic oscillators and pheonmenological models of the rubber band.

Abstract

A spin nematic order, analogous to the nematic liquid crystal, characterizes the spontaneous breaking of spin-space rotational symmetry while preserving time-reversal ($T$) symmetry. In contrast, scalar spin chirality (SSC), a composite three-spin order, breaks $T$ symmetry and is known to induce an anomalous Hall effect (AHE). Although a spin nematic phase has been suggested in frustrated magnets and the square-lattice iridate, how it might affect magnetotransport properties is unknown. Here we use polarized neutron scattering to show that tetragonal $A$MnBi$_2$ ($A$ = Ca, Yb) is a strictly $c$-axis-aligned collinear antiferromagnet (C-type), with $T_N \approx 270$ K and 290 K, respectively. On cooling from 450 K to $T_N$, low-energy spin excitations in YbMnBi$_2$ spontaneously change from isotropic to anisotropic in spin space within the tetragonal plane, forming a dynamic spin nematic phase around 400 K due to heavy Yb-induced spin-orbit coupling, before gapping out below $T_N$. Similar measurements on CaMnBi$_2$ reveal isotropic paramagnetic scattering without a spin nematic phase above $T_N$. Under an in-plane magnetic field, the Yb$^{3+}$ moments may interact with the dynamic spin nematic phase to induce nonzero SSC, giving rise to AHE and an anomalous Nernst effect (ANE) in YbMnBi$_2$ that are absent in CaMnBi$_2$ above $T_N$. A symmetry-based Ginzburg-Landau analysis shows that coupling terms between the nematic order and SSC are allowed under an external magnetic field, which could explain the rapid increase of AHE with field in YbMnBi$_2$. Our results provide compelling evidence for dynamic SSC-induced AHE and ANE in the paramagnetic phase of a compensated collinear antiferromagnet, opening a new avenue for the physics of composite spin orders and room-temperature spintronics without magnetic order.

Abstract

Understanding how coherently driven quantum many-body systems redistribute energy prior to thermal equilibrium remains a central challenge in many-body physics. Here, we utilize nitrogen-vacancy (NV) magnetometry to perform micron-scale spatial imaging of room-temperature magnon dynamics in a yttrium iron garnet (YIG) thin film. By revealing the multi-stage dynamical process through which monochromatic injected magnons evolve toward equilibrium, our work establishes spatially resolved magnonics as a powerful platform for visualizing non-equilibrium many-body kinetics.
