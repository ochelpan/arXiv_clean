Certified decoding of quantum LDPC codes

Authors: Ragavi Krishnamoorthy, Florian Gerhardt, Johannes Knaute, Thomas Klir, Stefan Raimund Maschek, Erik Schulze, Tomislav Maras, Alexander Dotterweich, Loong Kuan Lee, Christian Bauckhage, Nico Piatkowski
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25545v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work tackles the difficult problem of decoding quantum LDPC codes by reformulating it as estimating partition functions within a Markov Random Field model. The authors introduce novel, certified decoders that surpass existing heuristic methods by providing mathematical guarantees of optimality for most decisions. This advances the practical feasibility of fault-tolerant quantum computation by improving error correction decoding.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical framework for handling error degeneracy in quantum codes, especially the development of certified decoding methods, provides tools applicable to understanding the limits of fault-tolerant quantum computation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is decoding quantum Low-Density Parity-Check (qLDPC) codes, which is significantly harder than classical decoding because errors are equivalent up to stabilizers, requiring the comparison of entire error class probabilities (partition functions).

Main result. The authors developed a certified sampling decoder that matches or exceeds existing decoders while providing formal certificates of optimality for the majority of syndrome decisions.

Method. The problem is reframed as probabilistic inference in an undirected graphical model (Markov Random Field) over the code's check variables, allowing for the estimation of class partition functions using techniques like Annealed Importance Sampling (AIS) and Bethe free energy approximations.

Model / system. The system is quantum error correction using qLDPC codes, which are treated as an MRF. The framework generalizes concepts from the surface code to arbitrary CSS codes and spacetime decoding.

Key observables. Logical error rates, certified fractions of correct decisions, and the comparison of decoding performance against heuristic decoders like BP+OSD.

Important parameters / regimes. Code structure (e.g., [[72, 12, 6]]), noise models (depolarizing, circuit-level), and the computational cost (e.g., millisecond decoding time).

Assumptions / limitations. The core assumption is that the problem can be mapped to an MRF partition function estimation, and the Bethe decoder's bias cancels out when comparing different error classes.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the difficulty of degenerate quantum decoding, proposes the MRF framework for partition function estimation, details two main decoders (certified sampling and region-based), and validates them across various codes and noise models, concluding with hardware implementation results.

Abstract

Quantum low-density parity-check (qLDPC) codes reduce the qubit overhead of fault-tolerant quantum computation by an order of magnitude, but their decoding is harder than its classical counterpart: because many physical errors are equivalent up to stabilizers, the degenerate maximum-likelihood (ML) decoder must compare the probabilities of entire equivalence classes of errors, that is, partition functions, rather than single errors. The workhorse decoder BP+OSD sidesteps degeneracy heuristically and offers no guarantees. We treat degenerate decoding as probabilistic inference in an undirected graphical model: the probability of each logical class is the partition function of an unconstrained, strictly positive Markov random field over the code's check variables, a construction that generalizes the random-bond Ising mapping of the surface code to arbitrary CSS codes and to spacetime decoding with measurement errors and circuit-level noise. On this model we build two decoders. The first estimates all class partition functions by annealed importance sampling with common random numbers and attaches to every decision a certificate of optimality: a paired bootstrap test, or, composed with constant-factor estimators such as WISH, an exact optimality proof. The second is region-based: the Bethe free energy, whose bias cancels between classes, reproduces exact ML decoding on every tested surface-code instance at millisecond cost, and enlarging the regions to elimination clusters makes exact degenerate ML decoding of the [[72,12,6]] bivariate bicycle code feasible. Across surface codes and the bivariate bicycle codes [[72,12,6]] and [[144,12,12]], under code-capacity, phenomenological, and circuit-level noise, the sampling decoder matches or exceeds BP+OSD while certifying the bulk of its decisions, and the certificate flags exactly the syndromes on which any fast decoder should be distrusted.

Summary. This paper presents a generalized, efficient quantum circuit framework for simulating discrete-time quantum walks on arbitrary Cayley graphs. By systematically decomposing the complex shift operator using a multi-stage factorization technique, the authors achieve substantial reductions in required CNOT gates compared to standard methods. This provides a crucial, hardware-conscious tool for realizing quantum walk simulations on near-term quantum hardware.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly tackles the resource estimation for implementing quantum walks, which are fundamental models for quantum search and simulation, making it highly relevant for quantum algorithm development.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of developing a generalized and efficient quantum circuit framework for implementing discrete-time quantum walks (DTQWs) on Cayley graphs of arbitrary dimension.

Main result. The proposed multi-stage decomposition of the shift operator significantly reduces the upper-bound CNOT cost compared to naive implementations, providing a scalable pathway for near-term quantum devices.

Method. The authors build upon the Boundary QFT scheme by introducing a systematic, hierarchical multi-stage decomposition of the shift operator, which factorizes the QFT-diagonalized operator into structured block components.

Model / system. The model is the Discrete-Time Quantum Walk (DTQW) evolving on a Cayley graph $\Gamma(G, S)$. The evolution is governed by $U = S(C \otimes I_p)$, where $S$ is the shift operator, which is the primary computational bottleneck.

Key observables. Upper-bound CNOT cost, gate complexity, and the reduction factor compared to naive implementations.

Important parameters / regimes. The degree of the generating set ($k$) is identified as the dominant resource parameter, with efficiency gains shown for $k \le 64$ (inverse-closed) and $k \le 16$ (non-inverse-closed).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the linear CNOT scaling established by Rosa et al. and assumes the efficiency gain is largely insensitive to the system size $N$.

Figures summary. The figures illustrate examples of different Cayley graphs and provide a graphical representation of the DTQW setup, including specific optimized quantum circuits for 2D torus graphs.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the DTQW model and the role of the shift operator. It then details the systematic multi-stage decomposition, generalizing the Boundary QFT scheme. The core of the work involves applying this decomposition to specific graph examples (1D and 2D torus graphs) and performing gate complexity analysis to demonstrate the efficiency gains.

Abstract

We present a generalized and efficient quantum circuit framework for implementing discrete-time quantum walks (DTQWs) on Cayley graphs of arbitrary dimension. Building on the Boundary QFT scheme of Razzoli et al., we introduce a systematic multi-stage decomposition of the shift operator for 1D Cayley graphs across three classes of generating sets: inverse-closed without involutions, inverse-closed with an involution, and non-inverse-closed. The decomposition hierarchically factorizes the QFT-diagonalized shift operator into structured block components, progressively reducing the control degree of the required rotation gates and replacing high-degree multi-qubit controlled operations with collections of lower-degree equivalents. We extend this construction to $d$-dimensional torus graphs and provide explicit circuit implementations for an 8-Cayley graph and a $\mathbb{Z}_{16} \times \mathbb{Z}_8$ torus graph as concrete illustrations. Gate complexity analysis using the linear CNOT scaling of Rosa et al. demonstrates that the decomposed implementation achieves a substantial reduction in upper-bound CNOT cost relative to the naive implementation within the regime $k \leq 64$ for inverse-closed graphs and $k \leq 16$ for non-inverse-closed graphs, where $k$ denotes the degree of the generating set. Benchmarking further reveals that this efficiency gain is largely insensitive to the system size $N$, identifying $k$ as the dominant resource parameter for the shift operator. These results provide a scalable and hardware-conscious pathway toward practical DTQW implementations on near-term quantum devices.

Abstract

Strained germanium ($\varepsilon$-Ge) quantum wells on metamorphic SiGe buffers have enabled advanced hole-based spin qubit devices. Alternatively, unstrained Ge with lattice-matched strained silicon-germanium ($\varepsilon$-SiGe) barriers eliminates the need for metamorphic buffers altogether. The ground state character of both these platforms is predominantly heavy-hole (HH) with a largely anisotropic spin response. We propose and study an alternative heterostructure, lattice-matched to Ge, in which both the SiGe quantum well and barriers are tensile strained, with their composition contrast providing the band offset for confinement and the tensile strain stabilizing a light-hole (LH) ground state. We show large spin-orbit coupling (SOC), both linear and cubic, along with a significantly more isotropic spin response compared to strained HH qubits. We also study the decoherence properties of the proposed device, showing an appreciable gain in the quality factor compared to their HH counterparts. Finally, we propose a bilayer heterostructure that allows for electrical switching between HH and LH ground state character.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the magnetic ordering in Ca2CuWO6 via NPD, determining the collinear structure and moment size. Abstract

We present the antiferromagnetic ground state of the triclinic double perovskite Ca$_2$CuWO$_6$solved by neutron powder diffraction, and analyze it in direct comparison with tetragonal Sr$_2$CuWO$_6$. Below $T_\mathrm{N} \simeq 32$ K, the magnetic Bragg reflections of Ca$_2$CuWO$_6$ are indexed by the commensurate propagation vector $\mathbf{k}=(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2},0)$ in its native $P\bar{1}$ cell. Rietveld refinement yields a collinear structure with equal-magnitude, antiparallel moments on the crystallographically inequivalent Cu1 and Cu2 sites and an ordered moment of $0.66(3)~μ_{\mathrm B}$ per Cu at 1.5 K. A direct comparison with Sr$_2$CuWO$_6$ is obscured by different crystallographic settings and orientations of the cooperative Jahn-Teller elongation axes. Hence, we introduce a common crystallographic supercell through which effects of symmetry lowering from tetragonal to triclinic in the double perovskite are explored: Apparently different magnetic propagation vectors map onto the same supercell wave vector, revealing a common magnetic structure stabilized by tungsten-mediated, second-neighbor interactions. Mean field calculations further show that tetragonal symmetry preserves the degeneracy of four magnetic $\mathbf{k}$-domains in Sr$_2$CuWO$_6$, whereas the triclinic splitting of symmetry-related exchange pathways in Ca$_2$CuWO$_6$, most strongly within the Cu2 network, selects a single $\mathbf{k}$-domain. These results establish the magnetic ground state of Ca$_2$CuWO$_6$ and show how symmetry breaking selects a given ordered state without changing the underlying magnetic motif of the Sr analogue.

Summary. This paper tackles the fundamental cryptographic question of whether quantum indistinguishability obfuscation (qIO) can be secured using standard, falsifiable computational assumptions. By proving an impossibility result under specific reduction constraints, the authors show that qIO security for QMA cannot be based on these assumptions. Model / system. The work operates within the framework of quantum computation, focusing on quantum circuits, the complexity class QMA, and cryptographic primitives like qIO and WE.

Abstract

Quantum indistinguishability obfuscation (qIO) aims to make a quantum circuit unintelligible while preserving its functionality. It serves as a foundational primitive for advanced applications, such as witness encryption (WE) for QMA, non-interactive zero-knowledge arguments for QMA, and attribute-based encryption for BQP. Despite its importance, constructing qIO from standard assumptions remains a major open problem. In this work, we prove that the security of WE for QMA cannot be based on any falsifiable cryptographic assumption via a restricted class of quantum black-box reductions. Because qIO for null quantum circuits implies WE for QMA, this also separates null-qIO from falsifiable assumptions. Since almost all standard cryptographic assumptions are falsifiable, our result presents a barrier to basing qIO on standard cryptographic assumptions. The reductions we rule out are restricted: the reduction must query the adversary classically, non-adaptively, at the same security parameter, and only on honestly generated ciphertexts. Moreover, our impossibility applies only to WE with classical ciphertexts, and therefore does not rule out qIO with obfuscators whose output is a quantum state. Ruling out more general reductions, as well as more general forms of WE and qIO, remains open. Our impossibility relies on the existence of a QMA-QCIP[2] gap problem, an average-case assumption postulating a QMA language that cannot be verified with two messages of classical communication.

Superadditivity of classical communication over quantum channels via random and deterministic permutations

Summary. This paper tackles the superadditivity of classical communication over quantum channels by replacing continuous random unitaries with discrete random permutations. This transformation converts the problem into a combinatorial one, allowing for asymptotic derandomization. While the theory provides powerful structural tools, the required system sizes for explicit counterexamples remain computationally infeasible.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides deep structural insights into fundamental capacity theorems in quantum information theory, showing how continuous random matrix theory results can be translated into discrete combinatorial statements, which is a major theoretical advance.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the structural explanation for the superadditivity of classical communication over quantum channels, which was first established using concentration of measure for Haar random unitaries.

Main result. The authors show that the continuous problem over unitaries can be replaced by a discrete combinatorial problem over random permutations, leading to asymptotic derandomization of the nonadditivity proof.

Method. The approach involves replacing Haar randomness with random permutations, utilizing theorems like Bordenave and Collins, and employing asymptotic constructions derived from strong convergence in free probability theory.

Model / system. The study concerns finite-dimensional quantum channels $\Phi: D(\mathcal{H}_A) o D(\mathcal{H}_B)$, analyzed using the random-subspace model. The core comparison is between the minimum output von Neumann entropy of the tensor product channel versus the sum of individual minimum entropies.

Key observables. Minimum output von Neumann entropy ($H_{\min}$), Holevo information ($\chi$), and the comparison $H_{\min}(\Phi_1 \otimes \Phi_2)$ vs $H_{\min}(\Phi_1) + H_{\min}(\Phi_2)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The required system size $N$ for a finite counterexample is astronomically large ($N \le 5.422 imes 10^{116216}$), indicating the difficulty of practical construction.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that the limiting geometry responsible for nonadditivity is preserved when moving from continuous Haar randomness to discrete random permutations.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the connection between Haar randomness and random permutations, then providing asymptotic constructions for derandomization, and finally presenting explicit, albeit numerically enormous, finite-dimensional counterexamples to superadditivity.

Abstract

Since Hastings' proof of superadditivity of classical communication over quantum channels, considerable effort has been devoted to finding a structural explanation of this phenomenon that was originally established by concentration of measure for Haar random unitaries. The main observation of this work is that Haar randomness can be replaced by random permutations without changing the limiting geometry responsible for nonadditivity. This replacement turns a continuous problem over unitary matrices into a discrete combinatorial problem over zero--one permutation matrices, and thereby opens a path toward derandomization. The theorem of Bordenave and Collins shows that random permutations have the required limiting behavior and the algorithm of O'Donnell and Wu then provides a deterministic asymptotic construction, running in polynomial time in the size when the channel parameters and accuracy are fixed. Thus the random construction can be derandomized in an asymptotic algorithmic sense, although finding a simple closed-form or practically computable counterexample remains open. Finally, a quantitative random permutation estimate by Chen, Garza-Vargas, Tropp and van Handel gives a fully numerical estimate: there exists a tuple of 57,836,025 permutations acting on a set of size \[ N \le 5.422\times 10^{116216}\] such that the associated finite dimensional channel exhibits nonadditivity. This enormous value remains an obstacle to a practical construction.

Abstract

Compressively strained Ge quantum wells in planar SiGe/Ge heterostructures are the state-of-the-art platform for hole spin qubits. While they exhibit robust coherence times, they possess weak intrinsic spin-orbit interaction (SOI) due to the heavy-hole (HH) character of the wavefunction. Recently, light-hole (LH) qubits were proposed in GeSn/Ge heterostructures, offering strong, intrinsic, linear-in-momentum SOI. In this work, we propose a switchable HH-LH spin qubit in a bilayer Ge heterostructure with SiGeSn barriers, combining the advantages of HH and LH devices. The character of the qubit can be changed by shuttling from an LH well to an HH well, which also enables fast, hopping-based single-qubit rotations. Additionally, we observe an HH-LH resonance introduced by the in-plane confinement, resulting in $g$-factor peaks and first-order charge noise sweet spots. Our calculations reveal a sweet spot with Rabi frequencies on the order of 100 MHz, comparable to the LH regime, but with a more than tenfold increase in coherence time, on the order of 100 $μ$s.

Summary. This work demonstrates that attosecond transient absorption spectroscopy can serve as a quantitative, carrier-resolved polarimeter for measuring valley polarization in monolayer MoS2. By analyzing the circular dichroism at the Mo 4p core edge, the authors can track the creation and evolution of valley polarization in real time, providing a powerful tool for ultrafast valleytronics research.

Why it may be interesting. The methodology of using core-level transitions to create a highly sensitive, background-free, and carrier-resolved probe for non-equilibrium quantum states (valley polarization) is highly relevant to open quantum systems and quantum optics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core challenge is developing a method to read out valley polarization (VP) in 2D semiconductors on the same ultrafast timescale (attosecond) that it can be written or switched.

Main result. Attosecond transient absorption spectroscopy (ATA) successfully functions as a carrier-sensitive, quantitative polarimeter, allowing the real-time tracking of valley polarization buildup and dynamics.

Method. The study combines theoretical simulations using semiconductor Bloch equations with the concept of attosecond core-level dichroism measurements.

Model / system. The system is monolayer MoS2, a 2D semiconductor whose valley pseudospin is probed using the Mo 4p semicore edge. The theory employs a tight-binding model solved via semiconductor Bloch equations.

Key observables. Circular Dichroism (CD) of the transient absorption spectrum ($\Delta\mu_{\pm}$), which is shown to be linearly proportional to the valley polarization (VP).

Important parameters / regimes. Attosecond probe delay (time resolution), pump helicity ($\sigma^\pm$), and the energy separation between hole ($\approx 35.0 ext{ eV}$) and electron ($\approx 38.5 ext{ eV}$) transitions.

Assumptions / limitations. The simulation assumes the core semicore level is localized on Mo atoms and neglects population relaxation, though the symmetry rules are robust.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the readout scheme, showing how $\sigma^+$ and $\sigma^-$ probes yield different signals, and demonstrate the time evolution of the hole-line CD tracking the valley population buildup.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the need for ultrafast readout, details the use of ATA and core-level transitions to separate electron/hole signals, presents the quantitative relationship between CD and VP, and finally demonstrates time-resolved tracking of the valley dynamics.

Abstract

In recent years, all-optical writing and switching of valley polarization in two-dimensional semiconductors has been demonstrated on femtosecond timescales. Reading this polarization out on the same timescale, carrier by carrier, has so far remained out of reach. Employing semiconductor Bloch equation simulations of monolayer MoS$_2$, we show that attosecond transient absorption closes this gap by turning the Mo $4p$ semicore edge into a quantitative valley polarimeter. We find that pump-enabled core-to-valence absorption probes the holes while core-to-conduction bleaching probes the electrons, so that a single spectrum identifies each carrier by its photoabsorption energy. The hole channel, Pauli-blocked in equilibrium, emerges background-free. Its circular dichroism reverses sign with the pump helicity, and its normalized magnitude is proportional to the valley polarization. Finally, we show that scanning the probe delay clocks the few-femtosecond write in real time. Thus, attosecond core-level dichroism is a carrier-sensitive, quantitative probe of the creation and evolution of valley polarization.

Abstract

We study mesoscopic configurations of a neutron superfluid coupled to a proton superconductor in the outer core of a neutron star. The condensates are described by a two-component Ginzburg-Landau free energy with local couplings, neglecting genuine phase-gradient entrainment. In two spatial dimensions, we minimize the free energy using quasi-periodic boundary conditions and constrained phase-imprinting calculations to study vortex-fluxtube and fluxtube-fluxtube interactions. We find that, for locally attractive couplings in the free energy, vortex-fluxtube overlap is energetically favoured and several pre-existing proton fluxtubes can bind around a neutron vortex, forming finite vortex-centred aggregates that we call fluxtube bouquets. These bouquet configurations may become so dense that a vortex can effectively accommodate several quanta of magnetic flux. We also confirm the possible presence of a type-1.5-like regime and find that it survives in the zero-entrainment regime considered here. In this type-1.5 regime, the fluxtube-fluxtube interaction is repulsive at short distances and attractive at intermediate distances, leading to self-assembled clusters while the individual fluxtubes remain topologically distinct. Possible implications for dissipative coupling and transport in neutron stars are discussed.

Summary. This work predicts a 'viscochiral' transport regime in electronic fluids where the spatial gradient of the Hall viscosity controls the flow structure. By modifying the Navier-Stokes equations, the authors show that this gradient selectively amplifies one hydrodynamic vortex while suppressing the other. This mechanism is theoretically derived and proposed to be experimentally accessible in valley-polarized bilayer graphene.

Why it may be interesting. The concept of chirality selection driven by geometric/topological properties (Berry curvature) in fluid dynamics is a rich area connecting condensed matter topology to non-equilibrium statistical mechanics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates a 'viscochiral regime' of electronic transport where the spatial variation of Hall viscosity dictates the selection and pattern of hydrodynamic vortices.

Main result. A non-uniform Hall viscosity gradient acts as a chiral selector, causing the mean-square vorticity to amplify in one chamber while suppressing or extinguishing the vortex in the other.

Method. The analysis uses modified Navier-Stokes equations incorporating an odd viscosity term derived from Berry curvature, solving for the vorticity evolution.

Model / system. The system is modeled as an incompressible charged electronic fluid flowing in a two-chamber geometry, with the physics being demonstrated using bilayer graphene.

Abstract

We predict a new \textit{viscochiral regime} of electronic transport in which spatially varying Hall viscosity selects vortical flow patterns. Although uniform Hall viscosity cannot alter incompressible bulk flow, its spatial gradient redistributes vorticity, amplifying vortices in one chamber while suppressing the vortex in the other. We further determine the underlying mechanism to be generic to recirculating flows and insensitive to the details of the device geometry. The paper maps the resulting phase diagram and shows that the regime is experimentally accessible in valley-polarized bilayer graphene.
