The Effects of Inter-Valley Coupling of Dirac Fermions near Four Dimensions

Authors: S. Thiagarajan, F. Krüger
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25132v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 3/5 · Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical work investigates the quantum critical behavior of Dirac fermions in a multi-valley system using a renormalization group approach. It finds that inter-valley interactions stabilize a new critical fixed point, which breaks Lorentz invariance. The results are particularly valuable for understanding quantum criticality in materials with multiple degenerate electronic bands.

Why it may be interesting. The study of critical points in interacting fermion systems, especially those exhibiting topological features like Dirac valleys, is central to understanding strongly correlated materials and quantum phase transitions relevant to condensed matter physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper analyzes the quantum criticality of an Ising Gross-Neveu-Yukawa (GNY) theory describing Dirac fermions with both intra- and inter-valley interactions.

Main result. A new critical fixed point is found where inter-valley coupling is finite, leading to broken Lorentz invariance, although this symmetry is asymptotically restored in the large $N_\psi$ limit.

Method. The analysis employs a renormalization-group (RG) technique using an $\epsilon$ expansion in $4-\epsilon$ dimensions, calculated to one-loop order.

Model / system. The model involves $N_\psi$ Dirac fermion valleys, motivated by topological phase transitions (like the Kitaev model), coupled to Ising order parameter fields ($\phi$ and $\varphi_{i, i+1}$).

Key observables. Critical exponents, anomalous dimensions ($\eta_\psi, \eta_\phi, \eta_\varphi$), dynamical critical exponent ($z$), and correlation-length exponents ($ u_1, u_2$).

Important parameters / regimes. The number of valleys ($N_\psi$), the dimension parameter ($\epsilon$), and the coupling constants ($g_\phi, g_\varphi, \lambda$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes short-ranged interactions and is performed to one-loop order in the continuum field theory approximation.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the Brillouin zone with Dirac valleys, Feynman diagrams for self-energy and vertex corrections, and plots showing the RG flow of couplings and critical exponents as a function of $N_\psi$.

Paper structure. The paper builds from defining the full action and the RG methodology (integrating out UV modes) to deriving the coupled RG flow equations for couplings and velocities, culminating in the determination of the stable critical fixed point $P_c$ and its large-$N_\psi$ limit.

Abstract

We analyze the criticality of an Ising Gross-Neveu-Yukawa (GNY) theory of $N_ψ$ Dirac fermion valleys with intra- and inter-valley interactions, using a renormalization-group analysis in $4-ε$ space-time dimensions to one-loop order. The conventional GNY fixed point of decoupled valleys is unstable against inter-valley fluctuations which are naturally present if the symmetry breaking is driven by short-ranged interactions. At the new fixed point, the critical exponents differ from the conventional GNY universality. Most importantly, Lorentz invariance is broken due to interference effects resulting from the relative rotation between valley coordinate frames. In the limit of large $N_ψ$ the critical fixed point with finite inter-valley coupling remains stable but Lorentz invariance is asymptotically restored.

A Unified Framework for Operator Backpropagation and Observable Measurement in Quantum Computing

Authors: Kevin Dougherty, Ji Liu
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25042v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 3/5 · analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper presents a unified framework to optimize the process of estimating quantum expectation values by combining Operator Backpropagation (OBP) with advanced measurement protocols. It systematically determines the most efficient measurement strategy—choosing between QWC grouping or derandomization—by analyzing accumulated errors and resource scaling. This guides the development of more resource-efficient quantum algorithms for NISQ devices.

Why it may be interesting. It provides rigorous, resource-aware guidelines for running variational quantum algorithms, which is crucial for designing practical quantum circuits that minimize measurement overhead.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To develop a unified framework that co-optimizes operator backpropagation and observable measurement protocols to achieve a desired accuracy with the minimum number of measurements.

Main result. The optimal measurement strategy depends on a trade-off between Qubit-wise Commuting (QWC) grouping and derandomization, leading to a combined OBP-Derandomization protocol that significantly improves efficiency over individual methods.

Method. The authors propose a framework that analyzes error sources (backpropagation, truncation, shot noise) and selects the measurement protocol by comparing the cost scaling of QWC grouping versus derandomization bounds.

Model / system. The work is applied to quantum computing circuits, tested on models like the XYZ Heisenberg Hamiltonian, QAOA, Ising, and QFT circuits, relevant for near-term quantum devices.

Key observables. Expectation values of observables ($\langle O angle$), backpropagated observables (linear combinations of Pauli observables), and the measurement cost (number of required measurements).

Important parameters / regimes. Desired accuracy ($\epsilon$), number of QWC groups ($N_{QWC}$), and the scaling factor related to Pauli weights ($\log(L) \cdot \max_{\ell} 3^{w(O_\ell)}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The number of backpropagated observables can grow exponentially, necessitating truncation strategies (coefficient-based or Pauli-weight-based).

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the comprehensive framework for protocol selection, analyze the distribution of Pauli weights, and show performance comparisons (including noisy results) across various circuit families (QAOA, Ising, QFT) using the OBP-Derandomization protocol.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the unified framework, analyzes error sources, compares measurement protocols (QWC vs. Derandomization), and provides case studies on various Hamiltonians to determine the optimal measurement strategy.

Abstract

Decoherence imposes severe limits on quantum circuit depth, motivating methods to reduce circuit depth. Operator backpropagation has recently been proposed as a way to lower circuit depth by classically backpropagating observables through subcircuits. While it lowers the circuit depth, it also significantly increases the number of backpropagated observables that must be measured. This calls for an efficient measurement protocol tailored to these backpropagated observables. Moreover, errors arise both during the backpropagation procedure and during measurement, and they accumulate in the final computation. No prior work has examined how to integrate operator backpropagation with advanced measurement protocols, and how to jointly optimize the combined process. In this paper, we introduce a comprehensive framework to determine the appropriate measurement protocol to achieve a desired level of accuracy with the fewest number of measurements based on the number of qubit-wise commuting (QWC) groups formed from operator backpropagation. We analyze and categorize the primary sources of error that arise through our workflow, including error incurred from the backpropagation algorithm, truncation error, and shot noise variation. We also examine truncation strategies to reduce the number of backpropagated observables by characterizing the structure of the set of backpropagated observables.

Electric excitation of spin resonance in altermagnetic and antiferromagnetic conductors

Summary. This theoretical work predicts that altermagnetic conductors exhibit a unique Electric-Dipole Spin Resonance (EDSR) peak when subjected to an AC electric field perpendicular to the magnetization. This resonance occurs above a threshold frequency determined by the transverse magnetic field, offering a powerful method to experimentally distinguish altermagnetism from conventional antiferromagnetism.

Why it may be interesting. The theoretical framework for EDSR provides a concrete, measurable spectroscopic signature related to magnetic order, which is highly relevant for understanding exotic electronic states in correlated materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to predict and provide a diagnostic signature for Electric-Dipole Spin Resonance (EDSR) in altermagnetic and antiferromagnetic conductors, allowing differentiation from other magnetic orders.

Main result. EDSR manifests as a distinct spin resonance peak above a lower threshold frequency $\omega_- = 2H_\perp/\hbar$, which can serve as a unique diagnostic tool for altermagnetism.

Method. The analysis uses a combination of kinetic equation treatments and diagrammatic calculations within the Matsubara formalism to derive the spin polarization and absorption rate.

Model / system. The system involves altermagnetic and antiferromagnetic conductors, modeled using a low-energy Hamiltonian incorporating spontaneous spin splitting ($\Delta_p$) and an external transverse magnetic field ($\mathbf{H}_\perp$).

Key observables. The primary observable is the EDSR absorption rate ($P_{s\omega}$), which is compared against the background ohmic absorption ($P_{e\omega}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Key parameters include the transverse magnetic field $H_\perp$, the ratio $m/M$ characterizing spin splitting, and the clean limit condition $k_F l \approx 2\mu au \gtrsim 300$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the relaxation-time approximation for the spin polarization and assumes that vertex corrections vanish for $\delta$-correlated disorder.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the theoretical EDSR peak shape (with and without finite scattering time $ au$) compared to the ohmic background. Figure 2 shows the evolution of the total absorption $P(\omega)$ with increasing magnetic field, and Figure 3 details the diagrammatic calculation framework.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the physical model and Hamiltonian, derives the EDSR signal using kinetic equations and diagrammatic methods, predicts the characteristic frequency thresholds ($\omega_-$), and discusses the experimental conditions required for clear observation.

Abstract

We predict electric-dipole spin resonance (EDSR) in altermagnetic conductors: in a magnetic field ${\mathbf H}_\perp$, perpendicular to the magnetization axis, an AC electric field will induce a \textit{spin} resonance peak above the lower threshold frequency $ω_- = 2H_\perp$. In sufficiently clean samples, this peak shall be clearly visible on the background of ohmic absorption. EDSR can thus serve as a diagnostic of altermagnetism in conducting materials and as a means to distinguish it from higher-symmetry antiferromagnetic order.

Position- and Momentum-Space Quantum Information Measures of the Double-Morse Oscillator

Authors: Firoz Chogle, Ernesto Damiani, Berihu Teklu

Summary. This theoretical study analyzes the quantum information properties of a particle in the double-Morse potential by calculating metrics like entropy and Fisher information. The research tracks how these measures change as the potential transitions from two separated wells to a single merged well. It demonstrates that the complexity of the position distribution and the uncertainty in the momentum distribution exhibit complementary behaviors across this physical crossover.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a quantitative link between the geometry of a confining potential (double-well structure) and the resulting quantum uncertainty and complexity, which is highly relevant for understanding quantum state preparation and measurement in cold atom or superconducting circuit platforms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how quantum information measures characterize the particle confinement within the double-Morse potential in both position and momentum spaces.

Main result. The position distribution shows increased structural complexity when the double-well character is strong, while the momentum distribution exhibits a complementary trend. The Fisher-Shannon product approaches the Gaussian reference value as the wells merge.

Method. The analysis employs various information-theoretic measures—including Shannon entropy, Onicescu energy, and Fisher information—calculated for the first two bound states using quasi-exactly solvable analytical expressions.

Model / system. The system is the double-Morse oscillator, governed by a potential $V_{DM} = D(A \cosh(\alpha x) - 1)^2$. This model is relevant for systems like ultracold atoms or semiconductor quantum wells.

Key observables. Shannon entropy ($S_ ho, S_\gamma$), Onicescu energy ($O_ ho, O_\gamma$), Fisher information ($I_ ho, I_\gamma$), statistical complexity ($CGL$), and the Fisher-Shannon product ($P$).

Important parameters / regimes. The dimensionless shape parameter $A$ controls the crossover from a double-well to a single-well profile, with the analysis focusing on $A \in (0, 1)$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is limited to the ground and first excited states due to the quasi-exactly solvable sector used, and the results are interpreted diagnostically rather than as a full device-specific estimation protocol.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the potential $V(y)$ vs. $A$, wavefunctions ($\psi_0, \psi_1$) vs. $A$, energy levels ($\epsilon_0, \epsilon_1$) vs. $A$, and the evolution of various information measures ($S_ ho, S_\gamma, O, P, CGL$) as $A$ changes.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the double-Morse potential and its quasi-exact solvability. It then systematically calculates and analyzes several quantum information measures (Shannon, Onicescu, Fisher, etc.) as a function of the parameter $A$, detailing the physical interpretation of the trends observed in the position and momentum domains.

Abstract

We investigate the quantum-information properties of a particle confined by the double Morse potential in position and momentum spaces. The quasi-exact solvability of the model gives analytical expressions for the first two bound states, allowing the corresponding probability densities to be analyzed directly. Shannon entropy, Onicescu energy, Fisher information, statistical complexity, and Fisher-Shannon products are evaluated as functions of the parameter $A$, which controls the transition from a well-separated double well to a merged single-well profile. The position distribution is more delocalized and structurally complex when the double-well character is pronounced, whereas the momentum distribution exhibits the complementary trend. As the wells merge, the ground-state Fisher--Shannon product approaches its Gaussian reference value, whereas the excited state retains stronger non-Gaussian structure.

Real-time decoder for a MegaQuOp quantum computer using a single CPU

Authors: Min Ye, Andrii Maksymov, Nicolas Delfosse

Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: QC/QI experiment 3/5 · quantum measurements 1/5

Summary. This paper presents a complete, real-time decoding stack for fault-tolerant quantum computation targeting MegaQuOp scale devices. By implementing a dual-decoder system on a single CPU, the authors successfully demonstrated that the decoding overhead remains negligible even for circuits involving millions of gates and hundreds of qubits. This work is crucial for realizing large-scale, practical quantum computation.

Why it may be interesting. The work directly addresses the engineering bottleneck of fault-tolerant quantum computation by providing a concrete, high-performance decoding pipeline runnable on classical hardware.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Developing a decoding system capable of real-time error correction for 'MegaQuOp' quantum computers, which execute millions of gates on hundreds of logical qubits.

Main result. The authors demonstrated real-time decoding at MegaQuOp scale on a single conventional CPU, showing decoding delays of less than 0.3% at $p_{\mathrm{CNOT}}=10^{-4}$.

Method. They implemented an end-to-end real-time decoding stack utilizing a dual sliding-window decoder (error and outcome decoders) running on a single CPU.

Model / system. The system is designed for a universal fault-tolerant trapped-ion quantum computer architecture, benchmarked on circuits simulating Heisenberg models and Measurement-Induced Phase Transitions (MIPT).

Key observables. Decoding delay (stretch) relative to computation time, total number of T gates, and total logical measurements.

Important parameters / regimes. Logical qubit count (up to 408), number of T gates (up to $10^6$), and physical error rates ($p_{\mathrm{CNOT}}=10^{-4}$ to $5 imes 10^{-4}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The architecture relies on the 'walking-cat architecture' (WCA), which simplifies decoding by keeping logical computation structure regular and avoiding block merging.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the circuit mapping onto the WCA, the dual-decoder structure (continuous error vs. measurement outcome decoder), and plots showing the decoding stretch versus $p_{\mathrm{CNOT}}$ for various benchmarks.

Paper structure. The paper details the necessity of real-time decoding for large-scale quantum computation, presents the dual-decoder methodology optimized for the WCA, benchmarks this stack on complex circuits (MIPT, Heisenberg), and quantifies the resulting low decoding overhead.

Abstract

As quantum computers advance toward the regime of MegaQuOp machines executing millions of gates, a decoding system capable of real-time error correction in such a device will be crucial. Recent efforts have been focused on decoding an error-corrected memory or a small number of logical operations. Here we demonstrate an end-to-end real-time decoding stack for a universal fault-tolerant trapped-ion quantum computer architecture capable of decoding real workloads with millions of logical gates over hundreds of logical qubits. The complete pipeline, including on the fly detector error model generation, decoding of all logical qubits, logical operations, and magic-state factories, runs on a single CPU. We benchmark the decoder on practically relevant quantum applications spanning up to 408 logical qubits, and up to one million $T$ gates. Assuming a trapped-ion architecture with 1 to 5 ms cycle time, the decoding delay stretches the computation by less than $0.3\%$ at $p_{\mathrm{CNOT}}=10^{-4}$ and less than $12\%$ at $p_{\mathrm{CNOT}}=5\times 10^{-4}$ for all workloads studied. These results demonstrate real-time decoding at MegaQuOp scale on a single conventional CPU.

Distributed Trotterization with optimal time-scaling entanglement cost

Authors: Tianfeng Feng, Jinzhao Sun, Yunlong Xiao, Qi Zhao
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25896v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 3/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces an adaptive Repeat-Until-Success (RUS) protocol to simulate quantum dynamics across multiple processors. It demonstrates that this protocol allows the total entanglement cost to scale only linearly with simulation time, independent of the required accuracy. This linear scaling is proven to be optimal, establishing a resource-efficient foundation for high-fidelity quantum simulation on distributed hardware.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly tackles the resource scaling bottleneck in large-scale quantum simulation, providing a theoretically optimal resource estimate for networked quantum processors.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the resource-efficient simulation of many-body dynamics on distributed quantum processors, specifically minimizing the entanglement cost associated with nonlocal operations.

Main result. By using a Repeat-Until-Success (RUS) protocol, the total entanglement cost for high-accuracy simulation scales only linearly with the evolution time ($O(t)$), which is proven to be optimal.

Method. The authors compare the entanglement cost scaling of the proposed adaptive RUS protocol against conventional teleportation methods within the framework of product formula Trotterization.

Model / system. The model is the distributed quantum simulation of a many-body system governed by a Hamiltonian $H = H_A + H_B + H_{AB}$, where interactions occur between spatially separated subsystems A and B.

Key observables. Entanglement Cost (measured in ebits), Total Entanglement Cost scaling ($O(t)$ vs. superlinear/divergent), and Simulation Error ($\epsilon$).

Important parameters / regimes. Evolution Time ($t$), Target Error ($\epsilon$), Trotterization Order ($q$), and Interaction Strengths ($J_x$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the applicability of the RUS protocol and relies on matching lower bounds from quantum communication complexity to establish optimality.

Figures summary. Figures schematically illustrate the distributed simulation setup, the RUS protocol mechanism, and the comparative scaling of entanglement costs showing $C_{ ext{RUS}}$ remaining linear while $C_{ ext{TEL}}$ diverges as accuracy increases.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the problem of resource-intensive nonlocal gates in distributed simulation. It then introduces and analyzes the RUS protocol, deriving its expected entanglement cost. Finally, it compares this cost scaling against standard teleportation methods, proving the linear scaling is optimal.

Abstract

Distributed architectures extend quantum simulation of many-body dynamics beyond the reach of any single processor, with shared entanglement mediating interactions between spatially separated devices. Conventional implementations rely on quantum teleportation, which provides a universal realization of nonlocal operations but incurs a fixed entanglement cost per gate, irrespective of its strength. This becomes increasingly inefficient in product formula simulation, where higher accuracy requires ever more numerous, yet progressively weaker, nonlocal rotations, causing the entanglement cost to diverge in the high-accuracy limit. Here we introduce a simple repeat-until-success protocol that makes entanglement consumption adaptive to interaction strength. Incorporating this primitive into distributed product formulas yields a total entanglement cost that scales linearly with evolution time and remains independent of Trotter error. A matching lower bound from quantum communication complexity proves this time scaling to be optimal, establishing a resource-efficient foundation for high-accuracy quantum simulation across networked processors.

Electrical manipulation of oxygen stoichiometry in multiterminal YBa$_2$Cu$_3$O$_{7-δ}$ junctions

Summary. This work demonstrates a novel, post-fabrication method to locally tune the superconducting properties of YBCO junctions by electrically driving oxygen vacancy migration. By applying controlled currents, researchers can direct oxygen depletion fronts, which is confirmed by changes in resistance, $T_c$, and crystal structure. This establishes a route for engineering superconducting weak links.

Why it may be interesting. The ability to induce controlled, non-equilibrium changes in the electronic structure of a high-Tc superconductor via ionic motion provides a powerful, tunable handle on correlated electron behavior, relevant to understanding charge/orbital ordering.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study addresses the challenge of locally controlling the electronic properties of complex oxide superconductors by selectively manipulating oxygen stoichiometry in multi-terminal junctions.

Main result. The authors successfully demonstrated that oxygen vacancy migration can be electrically controlled in a Y-shaped YBCO junction, leading to measurable changes in resistance, superconducting transition temperature, and lattice structure.

Method. The research combines electromigration (EM) experiments with advanced characterization techniques like KPFM, SLM, and nanoprobe XRD, complemented by finite-element modeling.

Model / system. The physical system is a YBa2Cu3O7-delta superconductor patterned into a three-terminal junction. The underlying physics involves oxygen vacancy diffusion driven by applied current, modeled by coupled diffusion and electromigration equations.

Key observables. Resistance ($R$), superconducting critical temperature ($T_c$) contrast, work-function changes (KPFM), $c$-axis lattice parameter (XRD), and optical reflectivity changes.

Important parameters / regimes. Current polarity (determining front direction), applied current density, and relaxation time constants ($ au$).

Assumptions / limitations. The relaxation process is assumed to be a thermally activated diffusion process, and the correlation between $T_c$ contrast and oxygen content is assumed to be quantitative.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the electrical response during EM cycles, visualization of oxygen-vacancy fronts via optical contrast, and quantitative structural changes via XRD lattice parameter modulation.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the problem of local stoichiometry control, details the multi-modal experimental setup (electrical, optical, structural), presents results showing current-direction dependent vacancy movement, and validates these findings using continuum modeling.

Abstract

Local manipulation of oxygen stoichiometry offers a route to control the electronic properties of complex oxides, yet the selective modification of individual current-carrying branches through oxygen redistribution remains unexplored in multiterminal high-temperature superconducting junctions. In a YBa$_2$Cu$_3$O$_{7-δ}$ Y-shaped three-terminal device, we demonstrate the possibility to electrically control oxygen vacancy migration on a hand-picked terminal while largely preserving the other two. Oxygen-depleted propagating fronts are directly visualized by the resulting change in optical reflectivity and they are linked to the evolution of the electrical response. The process is highly directional and determined by the polarity of the applied current, allowing for the creation of either a converging or a diverging propagating front from the central node of the Y-shaped device. The associated changes in resistance exhibit relaxation on a timescale of minutes, driven by the vacancy concentration gradient. Effects of oxygen migration are also mapped by Kelvin Probe Force Microscopy and Scanning Laser Microscopy, which probe work-function changes and spatially resolved variations in the superconducting transition, respectively. Notably, the Tc contrast revealed by the latter provides a quantitative handle on the underlying oxygen content, enabling direct visualization of oxygen redistribution. Finite-element modeling and nanoprobe X-ray diffraction qualitatively reproduce the observed vacancy redistribution. These results establish a post-fabrication route to locally tune properties of superconducting multiterminal devices such as nanocryotrons, yTron, and tunable weak links.

The authors develop protocols to use ENM for classical key-controlled activation of non-Clifford operations and for magic secret sharing. This establishes a new paradigm for controlling quantum computational power in distributed quantum systems.

Main problem. The paper investigates Entirely Nonlocal Magic (ENM), a quantum resource where nonstabilizerness exists only in global correlations and not locally, and demonstrates that this magic can be controlled without relying on entanglement.

Main result. They show that magic can be classically activated, localized, and secret-shared using separable ENM states, providing new resource-control primitives for distributed quantum computation.

Abstract

Nonstabilizerness, or magic, is an archetypal \emph{quantum} resource that is necessary for quantum computational advantage. Here we uncover a phenomenon seemingly at odds with the quantum nature of magic: entirely nonlocal magic (ENM)---magic present only in correlations and absent from each party's marginal---can live without entanglement. We systematically study this separation and show it is universal and operationally reversible: every magical state or channel can be encoded into and recovered from a separable ENM realization using only local stabilizer processing and classical communication. We leverage this mechanism to devise an activation key protocol in which a classical key controls access to non-Clifford operations. We further formulate magic secret sharing, in which computational power inaccessible to any party alone becomes accessible through cooperation. On a superconducting quantum processor, we experimentally demonstrate activation key and network computing primitives, together with separable ENM state preparation and extraction protocols. Together, our results establish that magic can be classically activated, localized, and secret-shared without entanglement, providing new resource-control primitives for distributed quantum computation.

Exact analytical spectrum, eigenstates, and quantum geometry of the quarter-flux Harper-Hofstadter model

Summary. This paper provides an exact analytical solution for the spectrum and quantum geometry of the quarter-flux Harper-Hofstadter model. By exploiting sublattice symmetry, the authors derive closed-form expressions for the energy bands and the full Quantum Geometric Tensor. The results confirm the model's topological nature, showing the lowest band is nearly an ideal Chern insulator, which is highly relevant for experimental realization using ultracold atoms.

Why it may be interesting. The explicit analytical solution for the QGT and the quantification of topological stability defects provide powerful, exact benchmarks for understanding topological phases in lattice models relevant to AMO experiments.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To derive exact analytical expressions for the spectrum, eigenstates, and quantum geometry of the quarter-flux Harper-Hofstadter model.

Main result. The model is solved analytically by exploiting sublattice symmetry, revealing that the lowest band is nearly an ideal Chern band with a calculated Chern number of -1.

Method. The analysis uses the sublattice symmetry to block-diagonalize the Bloch Hamiltonian, allowing for the analytical derivation of the spectrum via singular value decomposition and the calculation of the full Quantum Geometric Tensor (QGT).

Model / system. The system is the Harper-Hofstadter model, describing charged particles on a 2D square lattice subjected to a uniform magnetic field at quarter flux ($\alpha=1/4$). The model is realized in platforms like ultracold atoms and superconducting circuits.

Key observables. Full Quantum Geometric Tensor (QGT), Berry curvature, quantum metric, Chern numbers ($C_n$), and various integrated stability defects (e.g., $ ext{THH}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Quarter flux ($\alpha=1/4$), lattice geometry (2D square lattice), and the magnetic field strength.

Assumptions / limitations. The analytical solution hinges on the preservation of sublattice symmetry in the symmetric $2 imes 2$ Magnetic Unit Cell (MUC) in quasimomentum space.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the energy spectrum along high-symmetry paths and across the full Brillouin zone, confirming the analytical results for the four bands.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the model and the symmetry enabling the analytical solution. It then derives the spectrum and eigenstates, followed by the calculation of the QGT. Finally, it evaluates global stability criteria for hosting Fractional Chern Insulators (FCIs).

Abstract

Quantum geometry has emerged as a guiding principle across atomic and condensed-matter physics, shaping the topological responses of Bloch bands and the stability of the correlated phases they host. Beyond two-band models, however, closed-form expressions for both the spectrum and the quantum geometry are rare. Here we provide such expressions for a paradigmatic four-band model that has recently been realized experimentally with ultracold atoms, photons and in superconducting circuits: the Harper-Hofstadter model at quarter flux, describing charged particles on a two-dimensional square lattice subjected to a uniform magnetic field. We achieve this by first showing that the model possesses a sublattice symmetry, which renders its Bloch Hamiltonian anti-block-diagonal allowing us to derive the spectrum and the eigenstates analytically. From that we also obtain closed-form expressions for the full quantum geometric tensor (QGT), including both the Berry curvature and the quantum metric, for all the bands of the model. For this purpose we first derive a general expression for the QGT for sublattice-symmetric systems in terms of contributions from the individual sublattice sectors. Finally, we evaluate fractional-Chern-insulator stability criteria analytically and quantify the lowest band of the quarter-flux Harper-Hofstadter model to be a nearly ideal Chern band.

Abstract

We present an experimental and numerical study of the spin fluctuations in 1D Kondo metal CeCo$_2$Ga$_8$. Using inelastic neutron spectroscopy, we measure highly one-dimensional magnetism with low-energy incommensurate short-ranged magnetic fluctuations. ARPES similarly shows a highly one-dimensional electronic band structure, confirming the one-dimensional nature of the system. We use density matrix renormalization group (DMRG) simulations of the 1D Kondo lattice model to interpret the measured spectrum, which successfully reproduce the neutron scattering features. We are thus able to place CeCo$_2$Ga$_8$ within the emergent incommensurate phase of the 1D Kondo lattice phase diagram, and demonstrate that the Kondo lattice simulated non-perturbatively is an accurate microscopic model for heavy fermion physics. This shows CeCo$_2$Ga$_8$ to be one-dimensional despite its complexities, and reveals a coexistence of low-energy Kondo and magnetic features in its inelastic spectrum.

Abstract

Three dimensional compact U(1) gauge theory supports a finite density of monopoles at a fixed lattice gauge coupling. After showing evidence for the expected dependence on the lattice coupling of the density of monopoles, we study the low lying spectrum of a two flavors of two-component Wilson fermions as a function of the bare Wilson mass. The Wilson-Dirac operator that realizes the two flavors are hermitian conjugate of each other to restore parity invariance and the commutativity of the two operators expected in the typical continuum limit is not the case in the presence of monopoles for all values of the lattice gauge coupling. This results in an anomalous behavior where we see clear evidence for the presence of very small eigenvalues for a wide range of a particular sign of the bare Wilson mass. These eigenmodes are typically in one-to-one correspondence with the number of monopoles as defined by the gauge field and the spectrum associated with these modes are separated by a gap from the bulk in the asymptotic regime of the lattice gauge coupling. The eigenvalues come in pairs around zero and the eigenvalues of one sign couple to the monopoles while the modes of the opposite sign couple to the anti-monopole implying flavor symmetry breaking.
