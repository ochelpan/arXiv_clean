Phase calibration of quantum oscillations in the magnetostrictive coefficient using the topological antiferromagnet YbMnBi$_2$

Authors: Qin Deng, Long Zhang, Zeyu Li, Ying Zhu, Shuai Wu, Yan Liu, Aifeng Wang, Yu Pan, Yisheng Chai
Type: experiment · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25656v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5 · analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work calibrates the phase offset for quantum oscillations measured via the ac magnetostrictive coefficient in the topological antiferromagnet $ ext{YbMnBi}_2$. By directly comparing the oscillation phases from magnetization and magnetostriction, the authors confirm a $\pi/2$ phase shift predicted by thermodynamics. This calibration is crucial for accurately extracting Berry phase information from magnetostrictive measurements.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on condensed matter magnetism, the rigorous phase comparison technique is a powerful diagnostic tool for probing fundamental symmetries and topological properties of electronic band structures, which has parallels in understanding quantum phases in other strongly correlated systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is to experimentally calibrate the phase offset associated with quantum oscillations when measured using the ac magnetostrictive coefficient.

Main result. The study observed a relative phase shift of approximately $\pi/2$ between the quantum oscillations in magnetization and the ac magnetostrictive coefficient, consistent with the Maxwell relation.

Method. The authors measured quantum oscillations in both magnetization and the ac magnetostrictive coefficient on a single crystal of $ ext{YbMnBi}_2$ and compared the resulting phases.

Model / system. The system studied is the topological antiferromagnet $ ext{YbMnBi}_2$. Its electronic structure is analyzed using DFT calculations, revealing Dirac-like bands near the Fermi level, which are sensitive to the magnetic ordering.

Key observables. Oscillation frequencies (around 160 T), relative phase shift ($\approx \pi/2$), and the temperature dependence of oscillation amplitudes.

Important parameters / regimes. Magnetic field up to 14 T, fundamental frequency $F \approx 160$ T, and the relative phase shift $\Delta\varphi \approx \pi/2$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the validity of the Maxwell relation linking the magnetostrictive coefficient to the stress derivative of magnetization, and the use of the leading-phase approximation for theoretical derivations.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the composite magnetoelectric setup, the crystal structure, and the calculated electronic band structure/Fermi surface of $ ext{YbMnBi}_2$.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the theoretical framework using the Lifshitz-Kosevich theory and Maxwell relations, details the experimental setup using the composite ME method, presents comparative measurements of oscillations in M and $\epsilon$, and concludes by using the phase comparison to calibrate the required phase offset.

Abstract

The Berry phase accumulated along a cyclotron orbit encodes important information about electronic band topology and is commonly inferred from the phase of quantum oscillations. Measurements of the ac magnetostrictive coefficient have recently emerged as a sensitive thermodynamic probe of quantum oscillations, but the phase offset has not been experimentally calibrated. Here, using the topological antiferromagnet YbMnBi$_2$, we calibrate this offset by directly comparing quantum oscillations in magnetization with those in the ac magnetostrictive coefficient. Measurements of both responses on the same single crystal reveal a single fundamental frequency of approximately 160 T in fields up to 14 T, enabling a direct phase comparison free from ambiguities associated with multiple frequencies. We observe an approximately $π/2$ relative phase shift between the two oscillatory responses, consistent with the Maxwell relation linking the magnetostrictive coefficient to the stress derivative of magnetization. Our results establish the appropriate phase needed to extract cyclotron-orbit phase information from quantum oscillations in the ac magnetostrictive coefficient.

Asymptotically optimal purification of noisy unitary channels in any dimension

Authors: Ryotaro Niwa, Satoshi Yoshida, Mio Murao
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.26061v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical paper determines the fundamental limits for purifying unknown unitary quantum channels corrupted by noise. It establishes that the optimal strategy achieves a query complexity scaling of $\Theta(d^2 p / \epsilon)$, which is an improvement over prior methods. The work rigorously proves these bounds using advanced mathematical tools from quantum information theory and representation theory.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous derivation of fundamental bounds and the explicit construction of optimal protocols for channel estimation and purification are central to quantum information theory, providing concrete benchmarks for quantum hardware.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the fundamental limits on purifying an unknown unitary quantum channel corrupted by depolarizing noise, and analyzes the dual problem of noisy unitary conjugation.

Main result. The optimal query complexity for purification is found to be $\Theta(d^2 p / \epsilon)$, achieving a scaling better than naive methods, and the optimal fidelity for purification matches that of conjugation in the low-noise limit.

Method. The analysis employs advanced techniques including the Choi matrix formalism, semidefinite programming (SDP) to establish lower bounds, and constructing explicit $\mathrm{SU}(d)$-covariant parallel protocols to saturate these bounds.

Model / system. The system involves unknown $d$-dimensional unitary channels ($U \in SU(d)$) subjected to depolarizing noise ($D_p$). The goal is to design a superchannel that reconstructs $U$ from noisy measurements.

Key observables. Channel fidelity ($f_{ ext{Choi}}$), first-order infidelity coefficient ($C_{n,d}$), and the query complexity ($\Theta(d^2 p / \epsilon)$).

Important parameters / regimes. Noise strength ($p$), dimension ($d$), and number of uses/queries ($n$). The analysis focuses on the low-noise ($p o 0$) and large-query ($n o \infty$) limits.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on assuming the use of the 'twirled protocol' for universality and utilizes complex algebraic machinery involving Schur transforms and Young's orthogonal representation for deriving bounds.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the structure of the quantum superchannel for purification, the relationship between Young diagrams, and the Stinespring circuit diagram used in the fidelity calculation.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the problem formulation, derives fundamental lower bounds using SDP (Theorem 1), constructs an explicit $\mathrm{SU}(d)$-covariant protocol to achieve an upper bound (Theorem 2), and then analyzes the dual problem of noisy unitary conjugation, showing the results coincide.

Abstract

We consider the problem of noisy unitary purification. Given access to an unknown $d$-dimensional unitary channel followed by depolarizing noise of strength $p$, we aim to construct a superchannel that universally purifies the noisy unitary back to the original unknown unitary. We optimize over arbitrary adaptive sequential strategies and analytically derive the optimal fidelity to the leading order in the noise strength and number of channel uses, while also providing a concrete $\mathrm{SU}(d)$-covariant parallel strategy that attains the optimum. Our result implies the query complexity $Θ(d^2p/ε)$ for achieving leading-order infidelity $ε$ in the low-noise regime, which scales better than the naive approach combining optimal state purification and storage-and-retrieval of quantum channels. We also consider the dual problem of noisy unitary conjugation, where the goal is to obtain the best approximation of the complex conjugate of the original unknown unitary from access to noisy queries. We show that the optimal fidelity for this task coincides with that of noisy unitary purification to the leading-order in the low-noise and large-query limit.

Kochen-Specker Configurations from Grids on Dual Quadrics

Authors: Giuseppe Favacchio
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25008v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces a novel, unified projective-geometric method using grids on dual quadrics to systematically construct Kochen-Specker configurations. It shows that this single mechanism can generate numerous known examples of quantum contextuality, including those related to $F_4$ and $H_4$. The work provides deep mathematical insight into the structure of quantum contextuality.

Why it may be interesting. While highly mathematical, the core subject—quantum contextuality—is fundamental to quantum foundations and has implications for understanding measurement limitations in quantum information theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops a systematic geometric framework to construct and organize Kochen-Specker (KS) configurations, which are mathematical realizations of quantum contextuality.

Main result. It demonstrates that several prominent contextual configurations (like those related to $F_4$ and $H_4$) arise from a single, unified projective-geometric mechanism involving grids on dual quadrics in $\mathbb{P}^3(\mathbb{R})$.

Method. The construction uses finite grids on pairs of smooth quadrics related by Euclidean polarity, employing a 'polar-secant principle' to generate orthogonal measurement contexts.

Model / system. The model is quantum contextuality, mathematically represented by sets of rays (vectors) in a Hilbert space that must satisfy specific constraints (contextuality) regardless of the measurement context chosen.

Key observables. Orthogonal contexts (sets of four pairwise orthogonal rays), the existence of infinite families of KS configurations, and the recovery of known exceptional root configurations ($F_4, H_4$).

Important parameters / regimes. Odd integers $p, q \geq 3$ used to define the grid parameters $Z_{p,q}$.

Assumptions / limitations. The construction relies on the properties of Euclidean polarity and the parity criterion for KS configurations; later sections test assumptions about complete intersections in projective space.

Figures summary. Not specified in the notes, but the structure involves geometric constructions on quadrics and verification of algebraic relationships.

Paper structure. The paper builds from the general geometric framework (quadrics and polarity) to construct an infinite family of configurations ($Z_{p,q}$), then analyzes specific cases (Cabello, $F_4, H_4$), and finally verifies advanced structural properties using computational algebra (e.g., checking for complete intersections).

Abstract

We develop a geometric framework for constructing and organizing Kochen--Specker configurations in real four-dimensional space. The construction uses finite grids on pairs of smooth quadrics related by Euclidean polarity. Their incidence geometry directly produces orthogonal measurement contexts and parity proofs of quantum contextuality, yielding infinite families of configurations and a geometric interpretation and extension of a previously known cyclic construction. For a distinguished subfamily, the same geometry admits a canonical completion determined by secant lines. The first two instances of this completion recover the exceptional root configurations of types $F_4$ and $H_4$, while the smallest case also recovers the Cabello configuration and its embedding in the Peres configuration. Thus several prominent four-dimensional contextual configurations, previously obtained from different constructions, arise from a single projective-geometric mechanism.

$(k,n)$-core percolation on hypergraphs with anchor nodes

Authors: Hoseung Jang, Byungjoon Min, Ginestra Bianconi
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25560v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5 · driven-dissipative phase transition 1/5 · non-equilibrium universality 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops a theoretical framework for analyzing percolation on hypergraphs where nodes have distinct functional roles (anchors vs. non-anchors). By deriving self-consistency equations, the authors map out the phase diagram governing the existence of a giant core, showing that node heterogeneity can fundamentally change the transition from gradual to catastrophic collapse.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical network theory, the study of critical transitions, phase diagrams, and structural robustness in complex systems shares mathematical parallels with phase transitions studied in quantum many-body systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To develop a comprehensive theoretical framework for $(k,n)$-core percolation models on hypergraphs that accounts for functional heterogeneity in node roles.

Main result. The study derives self-consistency equations for first- and second-neighbor pruning processes, revealing how node heterogeneity and extended interactions affect network robustness, leading to phase diagrams with continuous and discontinuous transitions.

Method. The authors use the cavity approach to derive self-consistency equations for the percolation process, comparing analytical predictions with numerical simulations on random hypergraphs.

Model / system. The model is based on hypergraphs $H = (V, E)$, where nodes can be designated as 'anchor nodes' with probability $ heta$. The core structure is defined by simultaneous constraints: a node must belong to at least $k$ active hyperedges, and a hyperedge must retain at least $n$ active nodes.

Key observables. The size of the giant $(k, n)$-core, and the nature of the phase transition (continuous vs. discontinuous).

Important parameters / regimes. Anchor probability ($ heta$), minimum node degree ($k$), minimum hyperedge size ($n$), and the connectivity range (first-neighbor vs. second-neighbor).

Assumptions / limitations. Nodes independently become anchor nodes with probability $ heta$. The analysis covers first-neighbor and second-neighbor pruning processes.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate phase diagrams in the $\langle q angle-\langle m angle$ plane, showing phase boundaries separating percolating/non-percolating regimes for second-neighbor pruning.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the model, derives self-consistency equations for first-neighbor and second-neighbor pruning, analyzes limiting cases ($ heta=0, heta=1$), and concludes by discussing the implications of functional heterogeneity on network robustness.

Abstract

Hypergraphs describe higher-order interactions that involve more than a pair of nodes. A characteristic feature of hypergraphs is that their robustness can be strongly affected by the different roles of the nodes. Indeed, some nodes might be essential for a hyperedge's function, while others might not be. The loss of a single essential node completely destroys the hyperedge it belongs to, while the loss of a non-essential node has a buffering effect, inducing the hyperedge to simply reduce its size. In order to capture this phenomenology, we formulate a comprehensive theoretical framework for $(k,n)$-core percolation models on hypergraphs, where each node of a hyperedge is an anchor with probability $θ$, and a hyperedge fails if an anchor node fails. Hypergraph $(k,n)$-core percolation problems can be classified as first-neighbor and second-neighbor problems, indicating that in the pruning process the connectivity is ensured only by the state of the first neighbors or the second neighbors, respectively. We derive self-consistency equations for first-neighbor and second-neighbor (node- and hyperedge-based) pruning processes, and obtain the size of the giant $(k,n)$-core. We obtain the phase diagram, including continuous and discontinuous transitions, and confirm our theory on random hypergraphs using numerical simulations. The results show how the heterogeneity of the nodes' functional roles and the extended range of the interactions affect the robustness of higher-order networks.

Summary. This theoretical work explores novel magnetic domain walls in rhombohedral graphene, which possess both spin and valley degrees of freedom. By modeling the system using Hartree-Fock theory, the authors identify two distinct wall types whose existence depends on the competition between Hund coupling and SOC. Crucially, they show that the dynamics of these textures can couple to superconducting transport, suggesting potential signatures for quantum electronic devices.

Why it may be interesting. The coupling of magnetic texture dynamics to superconducting transport (generating a voltage) provides a mechanism for reading out complex quantum states using superconducting junctions, which is highly relevant for quantum device physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the emergence of qualitatively new spatial magnetic textures, specifically domain walls, in rhombohedral graphene due to the interplay of spin and valley degrees of freedom.

Main result. The research uncovers two distinct classes of domain walls—a two-component and an intrinsically multicomponent (four-component) wall—whose existence is controlled by the competition between intervalley Hund's coupling and spin-orbit coupling.

Method. The study employs a microscopic approach using the self-consistent Hartree–Fock (HF) Hamiltonian, supplemented by continuum $CP^3$ theory for analysis.

Model / system. The physical system is rhombohedral multilayer graphene, analyzed in the valley-imbalanced quarter-metal phase. The model incorporates spin-valley-momentum (SWMc) band structure, intrinsic SOC, and Hund's exchange interactions.

Key observables. Local charge density, spin-valley order parameters ($\langle s_i \omega_j angle$), and the superconducting phase difference coupling to the texture's internal phase.

Important parameters / regimes. The ratio between intervalley Hund's coupling ($g_{\perp}|n_e|$) and spin-orbit coupling ($\lambda_{soc}$), which dictates the transition between the two wall types.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the HF approximation and, when using continuum theory, assumes a uniform local carrier density and a rank-one projector for the order parameter.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the spatial profiles of order parameters for both two-component and four-component walls, demonstrating the continuous development of transverse spin and valley coherence above a critical coupling ratio.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the physical problem in rhombohedral graphene, detailing the microscopic HF calculations, characterizing the two distinct domain wall regimes based on coupling strengths, and finally exploring the coupling of these textures to superconducting transport.

Abstract

Spatial textures of magnetic order, such as domain walls and skyrmions, are fundamental objects in magnetism. In rhombohedral multilayer graphene, magnetic order involves spin and valley degrees of freedom, opening the possibility of qualitatively new spatial textures. Here, we explore this possibility through a microscopic study of a one-dimensional domain wall in the valley-imbalanced quarter-metal phase of rhombohedral graphene. We uncover two different classes of domain walls. One resembles a conventional magnetic domain wall, locally rotating between the two bulk states, whereas the other is intrinsically multicomponent and explores states that are not occupied in either bulk domain. Which texture is realized is controlled by the competition between intervalley Hund's coupling and spin-orbit coupling, and we identify experimental signatures to distinguish them. We further show that, in a superconducting junction formed across the wall, the superconducting phase difference couples directly to the intervalley-coherent phase of the texture. Precession of this internal phase can therefore generate a voltage across the junction. Our theory shows that rhombohedral graphene indeed has magnetic textures beyond conventional magnet and that their dynamics can couple to superconducting transport.

Parity Anomaly as Modular Commutator with Massless Dirac Fermion

Authors: Meng Zeng
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.26078v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5 · quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work extends the concept of the modular commutator, originally used to measure the chiral central charge in gapped topological phases, to gapless systems. The key finding is that at a Dirac node, the commutator yields a robust half-quantized value, which the authors interpret as the entanglement signature of the parity anomaly. This provides a novel, information-theoretic measurement tool for topological properties.

Why it may be interesting. The connection drawn between entanglement measures (modular commutator) and fundamental topological invariants (parity anomaly, Chern number) provides a powerful, information-theoretic tool for characterizing topological phases, which is highly relevant to modern condensed matter theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates what the modular commutator measures when the 2D bulk state transitions from a gapped phase to a gapless phase, generalizing its utility beyond gapped systems.

Main result. For a single Dirac node, the modular commutator exhibits a robust half-quantized value, which is proposed to be the entanglement manifestation of the parity anomaly. This quantization is lost in other types of gaplessness like Fermi surfaces.

Method. The analysis uses the modular commutator $J(A,B,C)$ formalism, comparing its behavior in gapped versus gapless regimes, and employing symmetry arguments and correlation function analysis.

Model / system. The primary model is the free-fermion Haldane honeycomb model, studied at its critical point hosting an isolated Dirac node. The analysis also considers systems with chiral edge modes and compares results to quadratic nodes and Fermi surfaces.

Key observables. Modular Commutator ($J$), Chiral Central Charge ($c^-$), Half-quantized value (1/2), Hall conductance ($\sigma_{xy}$), and Conditional Mutual Information ($ ext{CMI}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Dirac velocity, Dirac cone anisotropy, system size (for finite-size corrections), and energy cutoff ($\Lambda$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the free-fermion approximation and assumes that the half-quantization is robust against tripartition deformation for the Dirac node case.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the setup using the Haldane model, showing the MC converging to 0.5 at the critical point, and comparing entanglement scaling for different gapless systems.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from the gapped case where $J$ measures $c^-$, to the gapless Dirac node case where $J$ is half-quantized, and finally generalizes the analysis by comparing this behavior to other gapless scenarios and relating it to the parity anomaly.

Abstract

The modular commutator $J(A,B,C) = i\langle[K_{AB},K_{BC}]\rangle$ extracts the chiral central charge $c_-$ from a single bulk wavefunction of a \emph{gapped} 2d state, where $3J/π=c_-$. Inspired by the recent developments in the field of gapless symmetry-protected topological phases, we ask: what does the modular commutator measure, if it is well-defined at all, when the 2d bulk becomes \textit{gapless}? Several interesting new insights can already be obtained using the simple Haldane honeycomb model. For the critical point hosting an isolated Dirac node we find that $J$ remains sharp: it converges to a \textit{half-quantized} value, with corrections that decay as a power law in the subsystem size rather than exponentially, mirroring the power-law correlations in gapless systems. We prove the half-quantization using an emergent reflection symmetry of the massless Dirac cone, and show that the half-quantized contribution comes from the other gapped cone (the massive partner of the massless one). This massive partner can be interpreted as the physical incarnation of the Pauli-Villars regulator, which is the origin of the parity-breaking level-$\frac{1}{2}$ Chern-Simons term (with half-quantized Hall conductance) and the parity anomaly. When protected chiral edge modes coexist with a bulk Dirac node we obtain $3J/π= c_-+\frac{1}{2}$. The half-quantization is also shown to be robust against tripartition deformation, tuning Dirac velocity and Dirac cone anisotropy. We further investigate other types of gaplessness---quadratic nodes (in contrast to linear Dirac) and the case with Fermi surface---and show that the robust half-quantization of $J$ is lost in such non-Dirac cases. These results generalize the modular commutator beyond gapped phases, and at the same time provide an information-theoretic measurement of the parity anomaly.

Surpassing Gaussian optimality in multiparameter estimation with indefinite causal order

Authors: Sudipta Das, Rivu Gupta, Aditi Sen De, Himadri Shekhar Dhar

Summary. This work investigates enhancing quantum parameter estimation in continuous-variable systems by analyzing optimal probe states for estimating displacement and squeezing. The authors demonstrate that while Gaussian probes are optimal under standard assumptions, incorporating indefinite causal order (ICO) allows for surpassing the Gaussian precision limit. This highlights ICO as a novel, independent resource for quantum metrology.

Why it may be interesting. It directly tackles fundamental limits in quantum sensing, showing how non-standard resources like ICO can enhance metrological performance beyond what is achievable with simple Gaussian states.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to improve the precision of simultaneous multiparameter estimation (displacement and squeezing) in continuous-variable quantum systems beyond the standard Gaussian optimality limits.

Main result. Introducing indefinite causal order (ICO) in the protocol can surpass the Gaussian precision bound, and this advantage is shown to be independent of non-Gaussianity.

Method. The analysis uses quantum metrology frameworks, calculating bounds via the Quantum Fisher Information Matrix (QFIM) and comparing the standard SLD-CRB against the Holevo-Cramér-Rao bound (HCRB).

Model / system. The system involves single-mode Gaussian probes generated by displacement and squeezing operations on the vacuum state, analyzed in the context of continuous-variable quantum systems.

Key observables. Estimation precision (quantified by the CRB) for displacement ($\alpha$) and squeezing ($r$) parameters.

Important parameters / regimes. Probe energy ($E_P$), squeezing strength ($\zeta$), and the parameters defining the unknown transformations ($\alpha, r$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis compares the SLD-CRB to the HCRB, and the optimal Gaussian probe state is found to be agnostic to the operation ordering.

Figures summary. Figure 1 compares the SLD-CRB versus encoding squeezing strength ($r$) for different probe energies, showing the optimal Gaussian bound ($C_{G,opt}$) and the displaced-squeezed bound ($C_{DS}$).

Paper structure. The paper analyzes Gaussian probe optimality first, followed by dedicated sections studying the introduction of ICO in probe preparation and ICO in parameter encoding.

Abstract

We identify single-mode Gaussian probes, generated by displacement and squeezing operations on the vacuum state, which are optimal for the simultaneous estimation of displacement and squeezing operations in continuous-variable quantum systems. Importantly, our results reveal that the best precision at a fixed energy is achieved not by an experimentally costly squeezing resource, but rather by redirecting some of the energy towards displacement, thus allowing for more resource-effective operations. Furthermore, introducing indefinite causal order (ICO) in either the probe preparation or parameter encoding step can surpass the Gaussian precision bound, even though the optimal Gaussian probe state is agnostic to the ordering of the operations. Specifically, we observe that odd-parity superpositions of the two definite orders can enhance precision over optimal Gaussian probes in specific parameter regimes. Further, the observed advantage cannot be attributed solely to non-Gaussianity, as quantified by the relative entropy of non-Gaussianity, highlighting ICO as an independent resource for enhancing multiparameter estimation.

Summary. This work provides a comprehensive mathematical toolbox for calculating key rates in one-sided device-independent Quantum Key Distribution (DIQKD). By extending the NPA hierarchy and using advanced algebraic techniques, the authors solve the key rate problem as a complex optimization over C*-algebras. The results highlight that the security performance is critically dependent on the asymmetric placement of trust between the two communicating parties.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical framework developed—linking key rate computation to C*-algebraic optimization—provides powerful, general tools applicable to analyzing security in various quantum communication protocols beyond simple qubit systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper focuses on developing general tools and methods to calculate the achievable secret key rates for one-sided device-independent quantum key distribution (DIQKD).

Main result. The key rate calculation is shown to be highly dependent on which party's device is characterized, demonstrating no universal ordering for optimal trust placement.

Method. The authors develop extensions of the NPA hierarchy and utilize the Generalized Entropy Accumulation Theorem (GEAT) to formulate the key rate problem as a non-commutative polynomial optimization over C*-algebras.

Model / system. The model is the one-sided DIQKD setting, characterized by an asymmetric trust model where one party's device is fully characterized while the other is treated as a black box.

Key observables. Achievable secret key rate ($r_\infty$), conditional von Neumann entropy ($H(S|IQE)_ ho - H(S|IB)_ ho$).

Important parameters / regimes. The choice of which party (Alice or Bob) is characterized; the size of the key ($n$) for finite-size bounds.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining the one-sided DIQKD setting and the key rate optimization problem (using entropy bounds) to developing advanced mathematical tools. These tools involve extending the NPA hierarchy and establishing rigorous algebraic isomorphisms between the measurement constraints and tensor products of algebras.

Abstract

The defining feature of one-sided device-independent quantum key distribution is its asymmetric trust model in which only one party is characterized. This scenario sets an interesting middle ground between high key rates achievable by characterizing devices and the security of full device-independence. Here, we provide new tools, methods, and benchmarks for calculating key rates in this setting. To achieve this, we develop and compare two extensions of the NPA hierarchy and derive finite-size security bounds against general attacks with arbitrary device memory. The latter is based on the Generalized Entropy Accumulation Theorem. We then investigate the performance of various protocols: the BB84 protocol both with and without losses, a qutrit mutually unbiased bases protocol, and protocols based on Bell inequalties such as CHSH and $I_{3322}$. We find that the choice of which party is characterized can strongly affects the key rate, surprisingly without a universal ordering. Our work thus offers a general toolbox for calculating key rates for one-sided device-independent QKD protocols.

Convergence of the conformal Ward identity in the derivative expansion approximation

Authors: Jorge Ibañez, Matthieu Tissier, Gonzalo De Polsi

Summary. This theoretical work examines how well the conformal symmetry is preserved when using the derivative expansion approximation within the Functional Renormalization Group for critical systems like the 3-d Ising model. By systematically increasing the order of the approximation up to $O(\partial^4)$, the authors demonstrate that the violation of conformal constraints decreases, strongly suggesting the convergence of the approximation scheme.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on critical phenomena, the rigorous study of symmetry restoration and approximation convergence techniques (like PMS/PMC) is highly relevant to understanding effective field theories in complex quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the convergence of the conformal Ward identity when using the derivative expansion approximation within the functional renormalization group framework.

Main result. The analysis shows that increasing the order of the derivative expansion systematically reduces the breaking of conformal invariance, providing evidence for the convergence of this approximation scheme.

Method. The study employs the Functional Renormalization Group (FRG) coupled with a Derivative Expansion (DE) approximation, analyzing constraints derived from conformal Ward identities at orders up to $O(\partial^4)$.

Model / system. The focus is on the 3-d Ising universality class, modeled via $\phi^4$ theory near its critical point, where conformal invariance is expected.

Key observables. Universal critical exponents ($ u, \omega$), the degree of conformal symmetry breaking, and the function $g( ho, ar{\alpha})$ measuring constraint improvement.

Important parameters / regimes. The regulator parameter $\alpha$, the order of the derivative expansion (up to $O(\partial^4)$), and the scale-dependent cutoff $k$.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that the DE is a systematically improvable approximation, and the analysis relies on specific regulator choices (e.g., exponential or Wetterich regulators).

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the improvement of constraints ($g( ho, ar{\alpha})$) and the reduction of symmetry violations as higher orders of the derivative expansion are included, comparing results to conformal bootstrap values.

Paper structure. The paper builds by first establishing the FRG framework and the DE ansatz, then analyzing the conformal constraints at increasing orders, culminating in quantitative comparisons showing the convergence trend.

Abstract

Conformal invariance is expected to be an emergent property of many systems in their critical regime. However, approximation schemes generically spoil this property. This is in particular the case of the derivative expansion, a widely used approximation scheme in the framework of the functional renormalization group. In this article, we consider Ward identities associated with conformal invariance in the 3-d Ising universality class with truncations at order 4 (next-to-next-to-leading order) in the derivative expansion, with $Z_2$ invariant composite operators. Our results confirm that the regulating functions which yield a small breaking of conformal invariance also present a small sensitivity of the universal critical exponents with the choice of this regulating function. We also show that, in the vicinity of regulator-parameter values for which the conformal constraints are best satisfied, the breaking of conformal invariance reduces as the order of the derivative expansion is increased, providing a new indication of the convergence of this approximation scheme.

Abstract

Programmable quantum devices nominally act on a Hilbert space whose dimension grows exponentially with the number of constituents, but the presence of noise makes it unlikely that they remain coherent across all of this immense Hilbert space. Then, what is the effective coherent quantum dimension that should be associated with such imperfect devices? To answer this question we here introduce an operational basis-independent framework which imposes a dimension bottleneck on the programmable transformations. Concretely we ask how strongly the quantum information they process can be compressed. Formalizing this idea we identify three inequivalent notions, termed $d$-compressibility, $d$-simulability and $d$-embeddability, which differ in the causal structure used to impose the bottleneck and form a strict hierarchy. The framework unifies several existing notions: joint measurability and simulability of quantum measurements, and the absolute dimensionality of state ensembles, are recovered as special cases. We illustrate the hierarchy with noisy qubit measurements in complementary bases, and we determine the white-noise thresholds at which the set of all noisy unitary channels in dimension $n$, a noisy universal quantum processor, becomes $d$-compressible, $d$-simulable and $d$-embeddable. The thresholds confirm the expectation -- maintaining coherence across the full Hilbert space becomes increasingly demanding as the nominal dimension $n$ increases.

Abstract

Codeword-stabilized quantum codes give a unified graph-state description of stabilizer and nonadditive quantum error-correcting codes. Although each individual CWS word state is stabilizer, coherent superpositions of different word states can be nonstabilizer. We develop a CWS-adapted magic-witness framework that isolates this codeword coherence and converts it into certified lower bounds on robustness of magic. The main result is an exact reduction of the stabilizer threshold of a natural CWS coherence witness to a finite-geometric problem over the classical CWS word set. For general weighted superpositions, the threshold is computed by enumerating affine intersections and affine-quadratic phases. For equal-weight superpositions, the phase optimization collapses, and the threshold is determined entirely by how many CWS words can lie in affine flats of each dimension. Thus a quantum optimization over stabilizer states becomes a classical affine-incidence problem. This reduction yields a fixed-parameter algorithm, an analytic lower bound for an infinite union-stabilizer family, and exact rational certificates for several standard nonadditive CWS examples. The framework also clarifies why exact enumeration fails for large structured families and identifies the remaining task as an affine-intersection problem. The result provides a geometric mechanism by which nonlinear CWS word sets generate certifiable magic.
