Unextendible stabiliser bases

Authors: Markus Frembs
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.10202v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical paper studies Unextendible Stabiliser Bases (USBs), which are incomplete sets of orthogonal stabiliser states. Using symplectic geometry, it proves their existence for general qubit and odd-prime qudit systems. The work is significant for showing that while USB unextendibility has deep mathematical consequences (like defining magic witnesses), it does not imply a universal quantitative barrier to distinguishing these states using stabilizer measurements.

Why it may be interesting. It provides deep mathematical insights into the structure of quantum state spaces defined by stabilizer groups, which is fundamental to understanding quantum error correction codes and entanglement theory in qudit systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates Unextendible Stabiliser Bases (USBs), which are incomplete sets of orthogonal pure stabiliser states analogous to unextendible product bases (UPBs). It aims to characterize their properties, especially concerning resource theories like magic states.

Main result. USBs exist for general qubit and qudit systems under certain dimensional constraints. Crucially, the paper shows that USB unextendibility alone does not impose a uniform quantitative obstruction to state discrimination by stabiliser operations.

Method. The study employs tools from symplectic geometry applied to Pauli groups in finite fields ($\mathbb{F}_q$). Key techniques include constructing magic witnesses and analyzing the properties of the orthogonal complement subspace relative to UPBs/USBs.

Model / system. The model is based on $n$-qudit stabiliser states defined within the framework of the Pauli group structure, analyzed using symplectic geometry. Comparisons are drawn between these stabilizer systems and product bases in quantum information theory.

Key observables. Orthogonality of states ($\langle \cdot | \cdot angle$), existence/non-existence of extensions, magic monotone $M( ho)$, and the success probability of stabiliser discrimination protocols ($p_{ ext{SO}}^{ ext{succ}}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Local dimension $q$ (especially odd primes vs. $q=2$ for qubits), number of qudits/qubits ($n$), and ancillary system size ($m$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies heavily on the mathematical structure provided by the symplectic form $\omega$ underlying the Pauli group, and comparisons are made assuming specific operational definitions (e.g., distillability).

Figures summary. Not specified in the notes.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining USBs as analogues of UPBs, providing explicit constructions for small systems (4 qubits, 3 qudits), establishing general existence theorems, and finally comparing resource-theoretic aspects like bound magic properties and discrimination capabilities against known results for UPBs.

Abstract

We study incomplete sets of orthogonal stabiliser states that cannot be extended by a further stabiliser state orthogonal to all of its members. Such sets are the stabiliser analogue of unextendible product bases (UPBs), and we thus call them unextendible stabiliser bases (USBs). Leveraging the symplectic geometry underlying the $n$-qudit Pauli group in prime local dimension, we explicitly construct USBs for systems of four qubits and three qudits of odd prime local dimension. Moreover, we show that these are the respective minimal qubit, respectively qudit numbers for which such bases exist, and that USBs exist for all $n\geq4$ qubit and for all $n\geq3$ odd-prime-dimensional qudit systems. Finally, we compare the resource-theoretic aspects of USBs with those of UPBs. We establish that, analogous to the case of UPBs, the orthogonal complement of every unextendible stabiliser set is a stabiliser-free subspace, and its normalised projector is necessarily magic; moreover, it is bound magic in odd prime dimension, yet need not be for qubits. We also show that USB unextendibility alone imposes no uniform quantitative obstruction to discrimination by stabiliser operations.

Coupled-Layer Codes: Beyond Quantum Product Constructions

Authors: Shuyu Zhang, Tzu-Chieh Wei, Nathanan Tantivasadakarn
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.10069v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces 'coupled-layer codes' as a generalization of standard product constructions for building quantum error-correcting codes. By formalizing the coupling using excitation algebras and relating it to homological algebra concepts like mapping cones, the authors demonstrate how to generate non-CSS stabilizer codes. This advances the theoretical understanding of topological quantum computation by providing new methods to construct robust quantum codes.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a powerful mathematical machinery (mapping cones, excitation algebras) to construct novel topological quantum codes that go beyond simple product constructions, which is crucial for realizing fault-tolerant quantum computation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To generalize quantum product constructions for building quantum error-correcting codes beyond standard CSS settings by introducing 'coupled-layer codes.'

Main result. The coupled-layer construction provides a general framework to derive non-CSS stabilizer codes, reproducing models like the fermionic toric code and generalizing tensor products via mapping cone structures.

Method. The method involves defining an excitation algebra and using an algebra-preserving map to condense Pauli excitations across multiple layers, relating the construction to homological concepts like the mapping cone.

Model / system. The work focuses on Quantum Error-Correcting Codes (QECCs), specifically building upon product codes by coupling layers of stabilizer codes. The underlying mathematical structure involves chain complexes and gauging symmetries.

Key observables. The resulting quantum error-correcting codes; the ability to produce non-CSS codes; commutation relations between various Pauli operators ($ ext{A}_p^{ ext{q}1}, ilde{f} ext{q}$, etc.).

Important parameters / regimes. The structure of the underlying group $G$ and the specific coupling terms derived from the input CSS codes.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on algebraic manipulations concerning overlaps between supports of Pauli operators, assuming certain commutation relations hold for simplification.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the tensor product complex structure (Fig. 1), the general coupled-layer construction via gauging (Fig. 2 & 3), and the mathematical formalism using mapping cones to represent code products and surgeries (Fig. 4, 5, 6).

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining product codes to introducing the generalized coupled-layer concept. It then details the connection to gauging symmetries and homological algebra via mapping cones, culminating in detailed algebraic proofs of commutation relations for the resulting operators.

Abstract

Product codes are an important class of quantum error-correcting codes constructed from multiple input codes, which can give rise to asymptotically good quantum low-density parity check codes. In previous work, we showed how the product between two codes can be physically implemented by coupling layers of the first code using checks of the second code. In this work, we further unify product code constructions with coupled-layer constructions of phases of matter by introducing coupled-layer codes. The essential strategy is to condense general excitations created by Pauli operators among multiple decoupled layers of the first code. The condensation is specified by an excitation algebra, which encodes the excitations, along with an algebra-preserving map. This coupling between layers generalizes the notion of gauging in physics as well as the mapping cone in homological algebra, and can be used to produce non-CSS codes. As examples, we show how coupled-layer codes reproduce the X-cube and Chamon models. We further generalize the balanced product code by allowing a unitary transformation in addition to a group action by free permutation and describe its corresponding coupled-layer construction. In particular, we show how balancing by a ZX-duality can reproduce non-CSS codes such as the fermionic toric code and the 3-fermion Walker-Wang model in 3D.

Summary. This paper develops the theory and explicit constructions for Quantum Locally Recoverable Codes (qLRCs) characterized by parameters $(r, t, x)$. It shows that these quantum codes can be derived from classical LRCs via the CSS construction. The main contribution is deriving tight upper bounds on the code rate and dimension, alongside providing concrete families of such codes.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides rigorous mathematical tools and concrete constructions for advanced quantum error correcting codes that go beyond standard stabilizer or distance-based requirements by incorporating locality constraints.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The work focuses on constructing and deriving bounds for quantum locally recoverable codes (qLRCs) with specific parameters $(r, t, x)$. It establishes the necessary and sufficient conditions for a CSS code to achieve this structure.

Main result. Explicit families of binary dual-containing classical LRCs are constructed using subset-inclusion matrices, which yield corresponding quantum qLRCs. Upper bounds on the dimension and rate for these codes are derived.

Method. The authors utilize the CSS construction to translate known results from classical locally recoverable codes (cLRCs) into the quantum domain. They derive bounds by analyzing puncturing techniques applied to the underlying classical code structure.

Model / system. The model is Quantum Error Correction, specifically focusing on $(r, t, x)$-qLRCs built via the CSS construction from binary linear codes $C$. The parameters involve locality ($r$), availability ($t$), and intersection parameter ($x$).

Key observables. Code dimension ($\kappa$), code rate (R), minimum distance ($\delta$), and the specific parameters $(r, t, x)$.

Important parameters / regimes. Locality $r$, availability $t$, intersection parameter $x$, and the underlying classical code structure defined by parameters $(m, s, \alpha)$ in the construction.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption for the necessary and sufficient condition is that the underlying classical codes have a dual minimum distance of at least two. The constructions also rely on the resulting classical code being dual-containing.

Figures summary. TABLE I lists representative parameters $(m, s, \alpha)$ satisfying the required conditions and provides the corresponding quantum code parameters $[[n, \kappa, \delta]]_q$.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the equivalence between CSS qLRCs and cLRCs (Theorem 1). It then details a construction method using subset-inclusion matrices to generate explicit cLRCs. Finally, it derives general upper bounds and specific Singleton-like dimension bounds for the resulting quantum codes.

Abstract

In this work, we study $(r,t,x)$ quantum locally recoverable codes (qLRCs) with locality $r$, $t$ recovery sets per qudit, and intersection parameter $x$. We first show that, assuming the underlying classical codes have dual minimum distance at least two, a CSS code is an $(r,t,x)$-qLRC if and only if the underlying classical codes are $(r,t,x)$ classical LRCs (cLRCs) with common recovery sets. We then use subset-inclusion matrices to construct families of binary dual-containing $(r,t,x)$-cLRCs, which yield binary $(r,t,x)$-qLRCs via the CSS construction. For CSS $(r,t,x)$-qLRCs, we derive upper bounds on the dimension and rate, minimum-distance bounds in the pure case, and a Singleton-like dimension bound in the exact case. Finally, we show that these families attain high rates and nontrivial minimum distances.

Exact Expressions of Entropy for Classical Non-interacting Many-body Systems

Authors: Yuheng Wu, Henrik Heelweg, Rigel Galgana
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.11104v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work develops a universal statistical mechanics theory to calculate the exact entropy for finite classical many-body systems across all relevant ensembles. By applying maximum entropy principles based on two core physical postulates, the authors show that while ensemble properties differ at small sizes, they converge correctly to established thermodynamic limits. This provides a rigorous framework for understanding finite-size effects in statistical physics.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical systems, establishing rigorous, non-asymptotic ensemble equivalence is foundational. The methodology of deriving fundamental quantities from minimal postulates (stationarity/unbiasedness) has parallels in defining quantum equilibrium states.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To develop a universal ensemble theory that provides exact expressions for entropy for classical non-interacting many-body systems, resolving discrepancies in thermodynamic properties among different ensembles when the system is finite.

Main result. The derived entropy expressions across various statistical ensembles are shown to converge asymptotically to a common limit in the thermodynamic limit, confirming ensemble equivalence while rigorously quantifying finite-size corrections.

Method. The theory uses maximum entropy optimization based on two postulates: stationarity and unbiasedness, applied to four representative thermodynamic ensembles.

Model / system. The system is modeled as classical non-interacting many-body particles governed by a simple quadratic Hamiltonian (L = sum(p_i^2/2m)).

Key observables. Entropy (S), Temperature (T), Pressure (P), Chemical Potential (mu), and the ensemble-dependent intensive variables.

Important parameters / regimes. System size (N, V), Energy (E), and the thermodynamic limit ($N o \infty$).

Assumptions / limitations. The system is assumed to be a regular Hamiltonian system, and the analysis relies on extending beyond the standard thermodynamic limit approximation.

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematics illustrate the state space constraints for four representative ensembles. Figure 2 illustrates the ensemble dependence of intensive variables like chemical potential ($\partial S / \partial N$).

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the need for a universal theory to address finite-size effects, then derives exact entropy expressions for four key ensembles using maximum entropy principles, and finally proves the asymptotic equivalence of these results in the thermodynamic limit.

Abstract

In the thermodynamic limit, the equilibrium state of a many-body system can be characterized by three pairs of conjugate thermodynamic variables: $E/T,V/P,N/μ$. In this limit, the thermodynamic properties in all ensembles are equivalent up to the leading order of $E,V,N$. However, for systems of finite size, this ensemble equivalence is no longer exact, and the thermodynamic properties may differ substantially among ensembles. To quantify these finite-size effects rigorously, it is desirable to develop a universal ensemble theory applicable to systems of arbitrary size, providing exact expressions for entropy and, thereby, giving rise to the precise value of all equilibrium thermodynamic quantities. In this work, we propose a theory that determines the exact entropy expressions for classical non-interacting many-body systems of arbitrary size across all statistical ensembles, based on only two postulates: \textbf{stationarity}, requiring that the physical laws be invariant under time translation, and \textbf{unbiasedness}, requiring that the equilibrium mixed state maximize the entropy subject to the prescribed constraints. Moreover, we show that the entropy expressions obtained in different ensembles converge to the common asymptotic form $S \asymp \ln\!\left( \left( \frac{4πm e E}{3N} \right)^{3N/2} \cdot \left(\frac{V}{N}\right)^N \right)+N$, consistent with the predictions of the large deviation theory.

From Pattern Detection to Composition Analysis in Quantum Software

Authors: Neilson Carlos Leite Ramalho, Erico Augusto da Silva, Anthony Accioly, Higor Amario de Souza, Marcos Lordello Chaim
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.10882v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper analyzes how reusable design patterns are actually implemented in open-source quantum software by building advanced mining tools. It extends pattern detection to composition analysis using graph databases to map component call structures. This work provides a critical resource (qpa pipeline and knowledge base) for understanding the practical adoption and architectural evolution of quantum algorithms.

Why it may be interesting. While not directly about physical Hamiltonians, understanding the software architecture and limitations of quantum programming tools is crucial for developing scalable algorithms that can be physically realized or simulated efficiently.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The research moves beyond simple detection of quantum software patterns to analyze their composition, understanding how high-level abstractions are assembled in practice.

Main result. The authors successfully built a pipeline that analyzes pattern usage across 80 open-source projects and constructed composition graphs showing the internal assembly logic of these patterns within quantum frameworks.

Method. They developed an enhanced mining tool (qpa) that uses multiple matching channels and vocabulary expansion, coupled with building graph databases to map component call relationships.

Model / system. The focus is on quantum software development using existing frameworks (like Qiskit), treating the framework components as the system being analyzed rather than a physical Hamiltonian or state.

Key observables. Pattern occurrence frequency, detection accuracy (micro-F1 score), and call relationships within composition graphs (direct vs. internal calls).

Important parameters / regimes. The catalog size (61 patterns) and the number of analyzed projects (80).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that framework components can be reliably mapped to abstract quantum software patterns, and that LLMs can semi-automatically classify new components.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the need for composition analysis over simple detection, detailing the enhancement of the mining tool (qpa), presenting quantitative accuracy results, and finally using composition graphs to analyze pattern assembly logic.

Abstract

Quantum software patterns provide high-level abstractions for building quantum programs, but there is still little empirical evidence on how they are adopted in practice. In prior work, we extended an existing quantum-pattern atlas into a 61-pattern catalog, created a knowledge base that links framework components to those patterns, and built a tool that mines pattern implementations from open-source code. We applied this tool on 80 projects and find that all 23 patterns occur in practice. In this work, we extend the tool with two additional matching channels and a vocabulary expansion step, and execute a quantitative evaluation of its accuracy on Qrisp, a framework not present in the knowledge base, reaching a micro-F1 of 0.712 against 0.449 without the expansion step. We then construct composition graphs that record calls among the high-level framework components associated with patterns and store them in a graph database. We use these graphs to examine how pattern implementations are assembled inside each framework, why patterns co-occur, and how much of a pattern's detection count comes from components called directly by developers rather than introduced through internal framework calls. We release qpa, an open-source mining pipeline, together with the knowledge base, which maps 286 framework components across five sources to the pattern catalog, maintained with the support of an LLM ensemble that classifies newly extracted components, and the resulting pattern usage dataset, to support reproducible studies on the adoption and evolution of quantum patterns.

Quantum Computing for Industrial Electromagnetics: Applicability and Case Studies in Solving Maxwell's Equations

Authors: Francesco Turro, Marco Maronese, Daniele Dragoni

Summary. This paper investigates using quantum computing algorithms, specifically QSVT and HHL, to solve the massive linear systems generated by simulating Maxwell's equations via FDTD. The research validates these approaches on industrial electromagnetics problems, showing that QSVT is superior and that resource scaling remains manageable even for very large physical domains.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly applies advanced quantum linear algebra techniques (QSVT/HHL) to a canonical problem in computational physics (electromagnetics), providing concrete benchmarks for near-term quantum advantage claims in applied science.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is accelerating computationally intensive simulations of electromagnetics, specifically solving large linear systems derived from Maxwell's equations.

Main result. The study demonstrates that the Quantum Singular Value Transformation (QSVT) algorithm consistently achieves higher accuracy than HHL for these tasks. Crucially, it shows that the system matrix condition number saturates with increasing spatial grid size, allowing scaling to realistic industrial dimensions without prohibitive resource growth.

Method. The authors map the time-stepping evolution of the Finite-Difference Time-Domain (FDTD) method into a linear system ($ ilde{A}\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$) and then apply quantum algorithms like HHL and QSVT to solve this system efficiently.

Model / system. The physical model is electromagnetics governed by Maxwell's equations, simulated using the FDTD method on lattices. The mathematical core involves solving the resulting large, sparse linear system matrix $ ilde{A}$.

Key observables. Quantum-state fidelity ($\epsilon_F$), algorithmic success probability ($P_s$), and the condition number ($\kappa$) of the system matrix.

Important parameters / regimes. The required qubits scale logarithmically with spatial size and time steps; performance is critically dependent on $\kappa$ and the approximation degree $d$.

Assumptions / limitations. Simplifications include assuming lossless materials, frequency-independent $\epsilon$ and $\mu$, and using Perfectly Matched Layers (PML) boundary conditions.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate schematics of field formulations (Yee lattice), circuit diagrams for HHL/QSVT, and plots showing the condition number's independence from large lattice sizes ($L_x$), as well as performance metrics vs. lattice size.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the physical problem (Maxwell's equations via FDTD) and reformulates it into a linear system. It then details the application of quantum algorithms (HHL, QSVT) to solve this system, benchmarking them across industrial use cases like radar propagation.

Abstract

Computational electromagnetics plays a central role in many industrial applications but often requires substantial computational resources, particularly when fine spatial discretizations are needed. While classical approaches remain the standard, quantum computing offers the potential to accelerate large-scale simulations by encoding them with a limited number of qubits. Here, we investigate the performance and resource scaling of the Harrow-Hassidim-Lloyd (HHL) and Quantum Singular Value Transformation (QSVT) algorithms for solving linear systems generated by the finite-difference time-domain (FDTD) method, a widely adopted numerical scheme for discretizing Maxwell's equations. We benchmark their performance across representative industrial use cases, including radar propagation, lens simulations, and beamforming processes. Our results demonstrate the validity of the approaches, achieving state infidelities smaller than $2\cdot 10^{-3}$ with success probabilities greater than $10^{-3}$, compatible with practical quantum state sampling. Overall, we observe that the QSVT method consistently delivers higher accuracy. We further observe that the condition number of the linear matrix, a key factor governing the performance of quantum solvers, saturates as the number of spatial lattice points increases. This implies that the spatial grid can be scaled to realistic industrial dimensions without increasing the HHL or QSVT circuit depth due to ill-conditioned matrices.

Verifying full quantum network nonlocality in arbitrary configurations by nonlinear Bell-like inequalities

Authors: Shuyuan Yang, Jinchuan Hou, Kan He, Mingxing Luo
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.10972v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

Summary. This paper introduces a novel method to verify Full Quantum Network Nonlocality (FQNN) across any network topology using just one nonlinear Bell-like inequality. The technique involves augmenting the original network with auxiliary local sources and testing the resulting structure against a derived inequality. A violation of this single test proves that all original quantum sources exhibit genuine nonlocality.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, general tool for certifying fundamental quantum properties (nonlocality) in complex, realistic network architectures, which is highly relevant to quantum communication protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is to develop a method to verify Full Quantum Network Nonlocality (FQNN) in quantum networks with arbitrary topologies.

Main result. The authors propose an efficient technique that certifies FQNN using only a single nonlinear Bell-like inequality, overcoming limitations of previous topology-specific tests.

Method. They enlarge the original network by adding carefully chosen auxiliary local sources and parties. The resulting augmented network is then tested with a single derived $l$-QNL inequality.

Model / system. The system involves quantum networks distributing entangled states among spatially separated parties ($S_1, S_2, \dots$). Specific examples analyzed include triangular, chain, and cyclic network topologies ($\Xi(n, m)$).

Key observables. Violation of the derived nonlinear Bell-like inequality (e.g., $| ext{Inew}|_1/4 + | ext{Jnew}|_1/4 > ext{Bound}$), which implies that all original sources must be nonlocal.

Important parameters / regimes. The maximum independent number ($h$) and the topological parameters defining the network structure are crucial for constructing the inequality.

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes that violating the derived inequality provides a conclusive proof of FQNN, and concrete calculations sometimes assume sources are in Werner states.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the concept using triangular networks (showing augmentation), chain networks, and cyclic networks, detailing how auxiliary local sources expand the original structure for testing.

Paper structure. The paper develops a general inequality based on network topology parameters ($h$) and then applies this method to specific, complex topologies like $\Xi(3, 3)$, chain networks ($\Xi(n, n-1)$), and cyclic networks ($\Xi(n, n)$) through augmentation.

Abstract

Full quantum network nonlocality (FQNN) describes a scenario where all sources in a network are nonlocal. Existing criteria of FQNN can only be verified in star networks by violating a single Bell-like inequality. Here we propose a method that certifies FQNN in general quantum networks using only a single Bell-like inequality. We show that the topological obstacle to one-shot detection can be overcome by expanding the original network with a carefully chosen number of auxiliary local sources and parties. The correlations of the enlarged network are then tested with a single inequality; a violation implies that all original sources must be nonlocal. Our approach provides an efficient, experimentally friendly way to verify FQNN in any network topology.

Summary. The research develops a method to extract a purely geometric component of energy pumping from an exciton manifold by analyzing its energy transfer statistics via Full Counting Statistics. By implementing optimized, non-uniform driving protocols, the authors demonstrate a significant enhancement in signal detectability while proving the robustness of this geometric signature against realistic environmental noise.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete, measurable signature—the geometric contribution to energy transfer statistics—in a solid-state platform. The methodology for separating this topological/geometric feature from thermal noise using advanced counting statistics is highly relevant to open quantum system dynamics in condensed matter.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The work aims to generate and detect a geometric contribution to energy transfer statistics (geometric phonon pumping) from slow cyclic modulation in an open quantum system, separating it from dynamic noise.

Main result. A Non-Uniform Field Modulation (NUFM) driving protocol significantly enhances the signal-to-noise ratio by suppressing dynamical fluctuations while robustly retaining the geometric pumping signal across realistic environmental variations.

Method. The analysis employs Full Counting Statistics (FCS) applied to a master equation framework, separating energy cumulants into dynamic and geometric parts based on loop traversal direction reversal.

Model / system. The system is modeled as a gate-tunable WSe2/WS2 moiré exciton manifold, treated as an open quantum system governed by a Liouvillian approach. The dynamics are analyzed using concepts derived from the tilted master equation and adiabatic approximations.

Key observables. Geometric first cumulant ($C_{ ext{geo},1}$), dynamic mean ($ ext{Cdyn}_1$), cycle-to-cycle fluctuation ($C_2$), and the fixed-time intrinsic Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR).

Important parameters / regimes. Gate voltage modulation rate, pump intensity modulation rate, environmental relaxation rates (phonon/radiative loss), and the cycle period ($ au$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the adiabatic approximation for slow driving and models coupling to the environment via phenomenological rate terms rather than fully coherent Hamiltonian terms.

Figures summary. Figures compare performance metrics (like SNR) between uniform vs. NUFM traversals, show period dependence of signal retention, and illustrate the local mechanism involving relaxation gaps and control parameter rates.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the theoretical framework using FCS for open systems, develops the geometric separation technique via loop reversal, optimizes the driving protocol by comparing uniform versus non-uniform modulation (NUFM), and finally validates the robustness of the enhanced signal against varying physical parameters.

Abstract

Slow cyclic modulation of an open quantum system can generate a geometric contribution to its energy transfer statistics, separable from the dynamic background. In this work we construct an experimentally anchored four state open system model for a gate-tunable WSe2/WS2 moiré exciton manifold within a population level full counting statistics framework. We first drive the system uniformly through a closed gate and pump loop and separate the geometric contribution from the dynamic background by reversing the loop direction. We then examine how this response changes with environmental rates, exciton coupling, control conditions, and static spectral broadening. A shorter cycle period followed by nonuniform traversal of the driving loop substantially improves detectability while preserving the geometric response. Combined with enhanced phonon relaxation and reduced radiative loss, this gives about a fourteenfold fixed time signal to noise gain relative to the uniform loop. Our results show that the geometric phonon response remains robust against realistic variations in the system and its environment, and that we can improve its detectability more effectively by suppressing dynamical noise through frequency modulated driving.

Summary. The authors studied graphene's optical response in ultrahigh magnetic fields, finding that the behavior deviates significantly from simple models. They demonstrated that at extreme fields, the system forms a fully compensated electron-hole plasma whose collective excitations are accurately described by an Alfvén wave model. This work establishes graphene as a platform for simulating astrophysical relativistic plasmas.

Why it may be interesting. This work bridges condensed matter physics (graphene) with high-energy astrophysics concepts (relativistic plasma), using advanced magneto-optics to probe fundamental quantum electrodynamic effects in a solid-state system.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To investigate the infrared magneto-optical response of n-doped epitaxial graphene under ultrahigh magnetic fields, specifically determining if the system deviates from ideal Dirac cone behavior.

Main result. The extreme-field absorption spectra are excellently reproduced by a collective Alfvén wave model, demonstrating that the 2D graphene system can realize a tabletop analog to relativistic electron-positron plasmas.

Method. A combination of experimental magneto-optical spectroscopy (using single-turn coils and flux compression) and theoretical modeling based on generalized bilayer graphene Hamiltonians was employed.

Model / system. The physical system is n-doped epitaxial graphene on 4H-SiC, modeled using effective Hamiltonians that account for band warping and electron-hole plasma dynamics in extreme magnetic fields. Abstract

We investigate the infrared magneto-optical response of n-doped epitaxial graphene on 4H-SiC in ultrahigh magnetic fields up to 560~T, utilizing single-turn coil and electromagnetic flux compression techniques. The measured absorption spectra are anomalously broad, strongly deviating from conventional cyclotron resonance. Angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES) reveals a distorted Dirac dispersion featuring a ``camel-back" structure with an energy gap of $E_g \sim 0.2$~eV. Abstract

We study how Floquet driving with linearly polarized light controls the topology and magneto-optical response of a two-dimensional (2D) $d$-wave altermagnet. In the absence of linearly polarized optical field and under spin conservation, we find that the system hosts a spin-Chern (a quantum-spin-Hall analog) phase with Chern numbers of opposite sign in the two spin sectors. The irradiated optical field breaks the $C_{4z}\mathcal{T}$ crystalline antiunitary symmetry between the spin sectors. Symmetry breaking originates from polarization-dependent Peierls phases, which renormalize hopping anisotropically along the two axes. The resulting spin-selective gap closures produce intermediate Chern-insulating phases with $C=\pm1$. The drive amplitude $A_0$ determines the inversion thresholds, while rotating the polarization by $π/2$ swaps the spin sectors and reverses the Chern number. Using the Kubo formalism, we compute the frequency-dependent longitudinal and Hall conductivities and derive the corresponding Faraday and Kerr rotations for a free-standing conducting sheet. The longitudinal response tracks the Floquet-renormalized interband thresholds, whereas the optical Hall response, together with the sign of the magneto-optical rotations, distinguishes the two opposite Berry-curvature chiralities. Sizable Kerr angles occur only within narrow resonant windows and should be interpreted together with the reflected intensity and Kerr ellipticity. These results identify linearly polarized light as a symmetry-selective handle for spin-resolved band inversion, Chern-number switching, and contact-free optical detection in $d$-wave altermagnets.
