Physics-Constrained Compressed Sensing for Quantum Sensing in the Data-Starved Regime

Authors: Amir Kalev
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.11092v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces a framework to enhance quantum sensor performance by treating signal reconstruction as a convex optimization problem constrained by known physical properties of correlation functions. By enforcing Positive Semidefiniteness and Toeplitz structure, the method robustly recovers parameters from sparse or noisy data where standard techniques fail. This offers a practical, calibration-free way to improve measurements in challenging quantum regimes.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a concrete, data-driven methodology for extracting maximum information from limited experimental measurements in quantum systems by embedding fundamental physical symmetries into the reconstruction algorithm.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Improving parameter estimation in quantum sensors when performance is degraded by noise, finite sampling, and implementation imperfections, especially in the data-starved regime.

Main result. Enforcing intrinsic physical constraints (Positive Semidefiniteness, Toeplitz structure, low-rank prior) during signal reconstruction significantly improves frequency estimation accuracy over standard spectral methods when data is sparse or noisy.

Method. The problem is formulated as a convex optimization program that reconstructs the two-time correlation function Gram matrix by minimizing an error functional subject to physical constraints.

Model / system. The work analyzes quantum sensors aiming for Heisenberg Limit precision, using the two-time correlation function of Hermitian observables (e.g., in GHZ-based magnetometry) as the diagnostic tool.

Key observables. Two-time correlation functions $G(t)$, frequency estimation error ($\delta\alpha$), and the reconstructed Gram matrix $\hat{G}$.

Important parameters / regimes. Data-starved regime (few time samples available), noise level ($\epsilon$), and the rank ($r$) of the underlying signal.

Assumptions / limitations. The core assumption is that physically valid correlation functions must be Positive Semidefinite, Toeplitz, and low-rank; the method assumes noise is bounded in $\ell_2$ norm over observed samples.

Figures summary. Figures demonstrate that PSD-constrained reconstruction significantly reduces estimation error variance compared to unconstrained methods (like matrix pencil) when data sampling is limited or imperfections are present.

Paper structure. The paper builds from the general problem of constrained signal recovery, applies it specifically to quantum metrology using GHZ states, and then rigorously compares its performance against established spectral estimation techniques across varying noise/sampling regimes.

Abstract

Quantum sensors promise measurement sensitivities that can scale at the Heisenberg limit, but in practice their performance is often degraded by noise, finite sampling, and implementation imperfections. In this work we present a general framework for improving parameter estimation in such settings by exploiting intrinsic structural constraints of time-domain correlation functions. Our approach builds on the observation of Kemper et al. [PRL 132, 160403 (2024)] that two-time correlation functions of Hermitian observables generate Gram matrices that are positive semidefinite, a property that can be violated in experimentally acquired data. We formulate signal reconstruction as a convex optimization problem that enforces positive semidefiniteness, Toeplitz structure, and low-rank priors motivated by the underlying dynamics. We show analytically that, under suitable conditions, the ground-truth signal can be uniquely identified in the noiseless case and recovered stably in the presence of noise. We further demonstrate numerically, in a GHZ-based magnetometry protocol, that enforcing these physical constraints can significantly improve frequency estimation from sparse and noisy data. In particular, we observe a clear advantage in the data-starved regime, where only a small number of time samples are available and standard spectral estimation methods, including matrix pencil techniques, provide limited or unstable improvement over direct fitting. While the reconstructed signals do not in general reach the shot-noise-limited performance, the proposed approach consistently reduces estimation error and recovers much of the underlying structure of the signal. These results indicate that incorporating universal physical constraints into data analysis can enhance the practical performance of quantum sensing protocols without requiring additional hardware resources or calibration.

Private Correlations Certify Sensing Capability

Authors: Yunkai Wang, Peixue Wu, Graeme Smith, Sisi Zhou
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.10377v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5

Summary. The paper establishes that 'private correlations' within a bipartite quantum state act as a quantifiable resource for enhancing distributed quantum sensing. It proves this by showing that positive private information guarantees a lower bound on the achievable local Fisher information, an advantage not shared by classical correlations alone.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides rigorous theoretical tools for quantifying quantum resources beyond standard measures like entanglement or mutual information, directly linking fundamental correlations (private information) to practical sensing limits in noisy channels.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The research aims to determine if an operational, information-theoretic quantity of a noisy quantum state can certify a non-trivial lower bound on its sensing capability in distributed scenarios.

Main result. Positive private correlations in the encoding basis are proven to constitute a metrological resource that certifies a quantitative lower bound on the locally accessible sensing Fisher information when assisted by an auxiliary channel.

Method. The authors use theoretical quantum information measures, specifically comparing private information ($P_X$) against entanglement and classical correlation bounds, applied within a framework of parameter estimation using LOCC Fisher information.

Model / system. The study analyzes bipartite or tripartite quantum states ($\sigma_{XBC}^{ heta}$) shared among parties (Alice, Bob) undergoing parameter estimation ($ heta$). The model incorporates the concept of an assisting channel ($C$) to enhance sensing performance.

Key observables. Private correlations (positive private information $P_X$), Locally accessible Fisher information ($F_{ ext{LOCC}}$), and Global Quantum Fisher Information ($F_Q$).

Important parameters / regimes. Noise parameters ($\lambda, p$), system dimension ($d$), encoding strength ($N$), and the existence/value of positive private information.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes a non-degenerate encoding Hamiltonian ($H_X$) and relies on specific channel formulations (e.g., erasure or depolarizing channels) for assistance.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate different state formulations, including data-hiding examples and general state structures ($\sigma_{XBC}^{ heta}$), used to demonstrate the resource quantification.

Paper structure. The paper develops from an initial example showing private correlations improving sensing over mere distillable key capacity. It then proves a general theorem linking positive private information to a lower bound on $F_{ ext{LOCC}}$ after assistance, contrasting this with classical correlation bounds and analyzing the reduction to entanglement when no assisting channel is present.

Abstract

We show that private correlations in a bipartite quantum state constitute a metrological resource for distributed sensing assisted by a possibly noisy channel from one party to the other. We begin with an example with distillable secret key but poor locally accessible sensing performance and show that an assisting channel substantially improves its performance. We then prove a general theorem showing that positive private information in the encoding basis certifies a quantitative lower bound on the locally accessible sensing capability after assistance. We further show that classical correlations alone provide no analogous guarantee, whereas, in the absence of the assisting subsystem, the privacy-based guarantee reduces to an entanglement-based one. Finally, in a channel formulation, we show that a channel with positive private information allows nonzero locally accessible sensitivity when paired with a suitable assisting channel.

Statistically-Secure Bit Commitment and Coin Flipping Protocols Based on Quantum Hardware Assumptions

Authors: Roo Dunnill, Mina Doosti
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.11187v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops constructive protocols for statistically secure bit commitment and coin flipping using Hybrid Locked Physical Unclonable Functions (HLPUFs). By integrating classical hardware tokens with quantum communication, the authors overcome previous impossibility results. This work suggests a new paradigm for building practical, hardware-anchored cryptographic primitives in quantum networks.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on cryptography, the reliance on physical hardware assumptions (HLPUFs) provides a tangible link between abstract quantum information theory and potential near-term quantum device implementations, which is relevant for understanding resource constraints in quantum networks.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is to construct statistically secure bit commitment and coin flipping protocols that overcome theoretical impossibility results in quantum cryptography.

Main result. The authors successfully achieve statistical security for both bit commitment and coin flipping by basing the protocol on Hybrid Locked Physical Unclonable Functions (HLPUFs).

Method. The construction uses a novel, non-trivial combination of classical hardware tokens and quantum communication within the HLPUF structure to enforce security guarantees.

Model / system. The core physical model is the Hybrid Locked Physical Unclonable Function (HLPUF), which combines classical PUFs with quantum encoding into BB84 states. The security analysis involves bounding operator norms and trace distances between candidate commitment states.

Key observables. Statistical hiding ($ ext{dtr}( ho_0, ho_1) \le \eta$) and binding probability bounds ($p_0 + p_1 \le 1 + 2 / 2^{s-\ell_{\min}/2}}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The security depends on the parameters of the HLPUF, including $\ell_{\min}$ (qubit register size) and $s$ (related to encoding length), as well as the bias $\delta_r$.

Assumptions / limitations. Security relies on natural assumptions about the underlying HLPUF hardware primitive and its resistance to tampering once locked.

Paper structure. The paper develops the protocol by first establishing the necessary security bounds (hiding/binding) using the HLPUF structure, followed by deriving the concrete probability bounds through mathematical analysis of projector operators and finally applying these results to derive the coin-flipping bias.

Abstract

Bit commitment is impossible to achieve with unconditional security, even in quantum cryptogra- phy. We show that statistically secure bit commitment, satisfying both hiding and binding, can be constructed from hybrid locked physical unclonable functions (HLPUFs), a hardware primitive that combines classical hardware tokens and quantum communication. Our protocol uses these hardware assumptions in a novel and non-trivial way to achieve the first mistrustful two-party cryptographic protocol based on hybrid hardware modules. We prove statistical hiding and binding under natu- ral assumptions on the HLPUF and using a carefully designed challenge generation algorithm as a subroutine of our bit-commitment protocol. The construction also yields the first hardware-based coin-flipping protocol. Our results suggest a new paradigm for secure two-party cryptography in quantum networks, combining rigorous security guarantees with a concrete route toward practical implementation.

Anisotropic magnon spin transport in CrPS$_4$

Authors: Krishnaraajan Sundararajan, Muhammad Zohaib, Yulia Kreminska, Sytze H. Tirion, Bart J. van Wees
Type: experiment · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.10802v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5 · correlated / nonlocal dissipation 1/5 · quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work experimentally demonstrates that crystal anisotropy strongly controls magnon spin transport in $ ext{CrPS}_4$. By measuring nonlocal magnetotransport signals, the authors quantify direction-dependent properties like conductivity and diffusion length. The findings establish intrinsic crystalline structure as a powerful tool for designing next-generation magnonic devices.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on condensed matter magnetism, the study of spin transport dynamics and engineered directional responses in crystalline materials provides a solid experimental platform for understanding energy/momentum channeling effects relevant to quantum devices.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how crystal anisotropy dictates the direction-dependent transport properties of magnon spin currents in van der Waals magnets.

Main result. Pronounced anisotropic magnon spin conductivity and diffusion lengths were observed when using electrically generated magnons, confirming that intrinsic crystalline anisotropy is a key control parameter for magnonic device engineering.

Method. The research employs nonlocal magnetotransport measurements (both first- and second-harmonic resistance) on patterned Platinum strips deposited on the $ ext{CrPS}_4$ antiferromagnet.

Model / system. The physical system is the monoclinic van der Waals antiferromagnet $ ext{CrPS}_4$. The analysis utilizes magnon spin diffusion models incorporating anisotropic exchange couplings ($J_1, J_2, J_3$) and measures transport along crystallographic $a$ and $b$ axes.

Key observables. Magnon spin conductivity ($\sigma_m$), magnon spin diffusion length ($\lambda_m$), nonlocal first-harmonic resistance ($R_{NL}^{1\omega}$), and nonlocal second-harmonic resistance ($R_{NL}^{2\omega}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature (e.g., 25 K, 15 K), magnetic field strength (up to 8 T), and crystallographic direction ($\hat{a}$ vs $\hat{b}$).

Assumptions / limitations. Electrically generated magnon transport is preferred over thermal excitation for isolating intrinsic anisotropy effects; extracting $\lambda_m$ from thermal measurements is complicated by temperature gradients.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the crystal structure and device geometry, showing how Pt electrodes are patterned along $a$ and $b$ axes. Results plot nonlocal resistances versus magnetic field or separation distance to quantify directional differences.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the theoretical framework for anisotropic magnon diffusion, details the experimental setup using nonlocal magnetotransport measurements on $ ext{CrPS}_4$, presents quantitative comparisons between electrical and thermal excitation regimes, and concludes by emphasizing anisotropy control.

Abstract

Crystal anisotropy provides a powerful route for realizing direction-dependent transport in solid-state systems. While its influence on electronic transport is well established, the role of anisotropy in magnon spin transport in van der Waals magnets is largely unexplored. Here, in a nonlocal geometry, utilizing the monoclinic van der Waals antiferromagnet CrPS$_4$, we observe pronounced anisotropy in both electrically and in thermally excited magnon spin transport. Electrically generated magnons exhibit a magnon spin conductivity at least 2.2 times larger and a spin diffusion length at least 2.7 times longer for transport along the crystallographic-b axis compared to the crystallographic-a axis, where $λ_m^{a} \sim$ 211 nm and $λ_m^{b} \geq$ 575 nm. In comparison, at 8T, we find the nonlocal second-harmonic resistance associated with thermally excited magnons to be $\sim$7 times larger along the crystallographic-b axis at 25K. We further show that a magnon spin diffusion length cannot be reliably extracted from the nonlocal second-harmonic resistance, owing to the extended temperature profile within CrPS$_4$. Likewise, we show that the anisotropy in the spin Seebeck coefficients cannot be reliably estimated from the thermally excited magnon spin transport alone, as it is intertwined with the anisotropic heat conductivity of CrPS$_4$. Utilizing the electrically generated magnon spin transport, we demonstrate that intrinsic crystalline anisotropy serves as an effective control parameter for tuning magnon spin transport, opening new avenues for magnonic device engineering.

Summary. This paper develops a toy model using 'overlapping qubits' to address the black hole information paradox. By allowing operators corresponding to interior and exterior regions to only approximately commute, the authors circumvent no-cloning violations and demonstrate that entropy calculations correctly recover the Page curve. This provides an intriguing quantum information perspective on reconciling unitarity with gravitational collapse.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on gravity, the use of overlapping operators and state compression maps ($\mathcal{N}_C$) provides a concrete, mathematically rigorous framework for handling approximate symmetries or near-factorizations common in complex many-body systems where exact tensor product descriptions fail.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses reconciling quantum unitarity with black hole evaporation paradoxes, specifically by modeling how information is preserved across an event horizon while respecting principles like complementarity.

Main result. By constructing a toy model using 'overlapping qubits,' the authors show that accounting for operator overlaps allows the entropy calculation to recover the expected Page curve, suggesting a mechanism for unitary evolution.

Method. The core method involves defining operators (qubits) that only approximately commute ($\sim O(\epsilon)$), which circumvents strict Hilbert space factorization and avoids apparent violations of no-cloning theorems.

Model / system. The system is a toy model of an evaporating black hole, mathematically realized using overlapping qubits derived from overlapping Majorana fermions. This framework allows for quasi-local degrees of freedom that mimic the structure expected in quantum gravity regimes.

Key observables. Page curve recovery (entropy calculation), fidelity between compressed states ($F$), and the overlap parameter ($\epsilon$).

Important parameters / regimes. The small overlap parameter $\epsilon$, the number of fundamental degrees of freedom $n$, and the number of overlapping qubits $N$.

Assumptions / limitations. The model is explicitly a 'toy model'; it relies on weak interdependencies ($\epsilon$) leading to approximate factorizations, and later sections assume Gaussian states for fidelity analysis.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the analogy between fitting overlapping vectors in $\mathbb{R}^n$ and constructing overlapping qubits. Figure 7 shows the exponential decay of state fidelity with Hamming distance and the scaling relationship between this decay rate and the ratio $N/n$.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the physical problem (black hole complementarity) to introducing the mathematical tool (overlapping qubits via fermions). It then develops techniques for analyzing overlaps in quantum states, culminating in fidelity calculations that confirm the expected scaling behavior.

Abstract

We construct a toy model of an evaporating black hole using approximately local degrees of freedom acting on overlapping" qubits in which a version of black hole complementarity arises naturally. The operators corresponding to the radiation and the interior are identified as two distinct representations of the same fundamental algebra, thereby preventing the exact factorization of the Hilbert space into interior and exterior and avoiding the conventional no-cloning violations. We show how this toy model captures several qualitative and quantitative features of black hole evaporation and how the ability to account for thisoverlap" in the entropy calculation leads to the recovery of a Page curve.

Recursion Coefficients and Krylov Dynamics in Polynomial Random Matrix Models

Authors: Juan F. Pedraza, Le-Chen Qu
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.10072v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5 · driven-dissipative phase transition 1/5 · non-equilibrium universality 1/5

Summary. This paper develops a powerful method using moment recursion to analyze the behavior of orthogonal polynomial coefficients in random matrix models with asymmetric potentials. By studying the large-$N$ asymptotics, they link mathematical features like 'gradient catastrophes' to physical concepts such as chaotic transition regions in models like DSSYK. This provides deep insights into how spectral properties encode complex quantum dynamics.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on RMT, the connection between spectral properties (recursion coefficients) and dynamical indicators like complexity/chaos makes it relevant to understanding quantum dynamics in complex systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study focuses on analyzing the recursion coefficients of orthogonal polynomials and their associated Krylov dynamics within random matrix models featuring high-degree or asymmetric polynomial potentials.

Main result. The authors derived large-$N$ asymptotic behaviors for these coefficients, showing that gradient catastrophes in the associated recursion functions correspond to 'chaotic' transition regions in the physical models studied.

Method. They employ a moment recursion method combined with recursive algorithms to efficiently construct and analyze the coefficients, connecting them to continuum string equations.

Model / system. The work applies to general random matrix ensembles defined by potentials $V(\lambda)$, specifically examining an asymmetric quartic potential and the double-scaled Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev (DSSYK) model.

Key observables. Recursion coefficients ($R_n, S_n$), the recursion function, gradient catastrophes, and spread complexity $C(t)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The degree of the potential, asymmetry parameters ($w_3, w_2, w_1$), and the scaling variable $x=n/N$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on treating the discrete string equation as an initial value problem or using moment calculations for high degrees.

Figures summary. Numerical results confirm that the discrete coefficients approach the derived continuum functions ($R(x), S(x)$) as $N$ increases, illustrating the large-$n/N$ behavior.

Paper structure. The paper develops the moment recursion method for high-degree potentials, derives the continuum string equations for the quartic potential, analyzes the asymptotic limits of the coefficients, and applies this framework to both the quartic model and the DSSYK model.

Abstract

We study the recursion coefficients of orthogonal polynomials and their associated Krylov dynamics in random matrix models with high-degree and possibly asymmetric polynomial potentials. We develop a moment recursion method that, when combined with the recursive algorithm, provides an efficient construction of the recursion coefficients. We also obtain their large-$n$ asymptotic behavior for general asymmetric potentials; for $Nw_d=1$, the leading asymptotic form of $R_n$ reproduces Freud's conjecture. We apply this framework to an asymmetric quartic potential and to the double-scaled Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev (DSSYK) model. In both models, the recursion functions capture the overall qualitative behavior of the recursion coefficients, and the gradient catastrophes of the recursion functions are associated with ``chaotic'' transition regions in the recursion coefficients. For the quartic potential, such regions can occur in both $R_n$ and $S_n$, whereas the DSSYK model can exhibit multiple transition regions in $R_n$, with the recursion function remaining accurate in the smooth intervals between them. Finally, we compute the corresponding spread complexity and find that transition regions do not qualitatively modify its behavior, while a two branch structure produces early time oscillations followed by monotonic growth.

Carrier-tunable RKKY magnetism in a crystalline magnet

Authors: Xiang S. W. Huang, Bruno K. Saika, Satoshi Hamao, Yuki Majima, Yuki Settai, Hideki Matsuoka, Yuki M. Itahashi, Masato Sakano, Taro Nakajima, Shinichiro Seki, Yoshihiro Iwasa, Kyoko Ishizaka, Masaki Nakano

Summary. The research reports on creating an unprecedented material system ($ ext{Cr-NbSe}_2$) where the magnetic exchange interaction (RKKY) can be tuned by independently controlling both the atomic spacing of magnetic moments ($r$) and the carrier concentration ($k_F$). This is achieved using a combination of MBE growth for structural control and post-growth annealing for electronic doping, allowing precise study of carrier-sensitive magnetism.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a highly controllable platform for studying fundamental many-body interactions like the RKKY coupling by decoupling its key parameters ($r$ and $k_F$) through epitaxial strain engineering and doping, which is crucial for understanding correlated electron physics in artificial heterostructures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To independently tune the moment-moment distance (r) and the Fermi wavevector (kF) in itinerant magnets governed by the RKKY interaction, which are typically coupled in bulk synthesis.

Main result. The authors successfully demonstrated carrier-sensitive RKKY magnetism in $ ext{Cr-NbSe}_2$ thin films by using MBE growth to fix the moment geometry and post-growth annealing to independently tune the carrier density.

Method. The study employs epitaxial thin film growth via Molecular-Beam Epitaxy (MBE) followed by controlled post-growth annealing, coupled with transport measurements like Hall effect and magnetization studies.

Model / system. The physical system is $ ext{Cr}_{1/x} ext{NbSe}_2$ in the form of epitaxial thin films grown on a single crystalline host ($ ext{NbSe}_2$). The RKKY interaction governs magnetism, depending critically on both the moment separation ($r$) and the carrier density (related to $k_F$).

Key observables. Hall response evolution, magnetic transition temperatures ($T_C$, $T_N$), resistivity ($ ho(T)$), and structural superstructures ($2 imes2R0^\circ$ vs $\sqrt{3} imes\sqrt{3}R30^\circ$).

Important parameters / regimes. Annealing temperature (tuning carrier density) and growth window selection (setting moment geometry $r$).

Assumptions / limitations. The ability to decouple the structural control ($r$) from the electronic doping/carrier tuning ($k_F$) via sequential MBE growth and annealing.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate RHEED patterns confirming superstructures, transport measurements showing systematic evolution of Hall response with annealing, and magnetization curves revealing magnetic transitions dependent on processing history.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the challenge (coupled $r$ and $k_F$), details the MBE/annealing methodology to create distinct phases, presents structural confirmation via RHEED/LEED, and finally uses transport/magnetic measurements across an annealing series to prove independent tuning of carrier-sensitive RKKY magnetism.

Abstract

In itinerant magnets governed by the Ruderman-Kittel-Kasuya-Yosida (RKKY) interaction, the exchange coupling depends on both the moment-moment distance $r$ and the Fermi wavevector $k_{\mathrm{F}}$, yet in bulk synthesis the two are tightly coupled: a change in composition typically alters both. Here, we use a thin-film approach to tune these two variables independently in a single crystalline host. Using molecular-beam epitaxy (MBE), we stabilize either $2\times2\mathrm{R}0^\circ$ Cr$_{1/4}$NbSe$_2$ or $\sqrt{3}\times\sqrt{3}\mathrm{R}30^\circ$ Cr$_{1/3}$NbSe$_2$ within the same NbSe$_2$ host through separate growth windows. Controlled post-growth annealing performed across a series of temperatures then modifies the carrier density while leaving the Cr superstructure intact below a structural-transition threshold. The two as-grown phases are distinct in electronic structure, magnetic ground state, and transport. Along this annealing series, the Hall response evolves systematically while the magnetic response changes in a structurally insensitive manner, with ferromagnetic order emerging within the same $\sqrt{3}\times\sqrt{3}\mathrm{R}30^\circ$ structural class only above a critical annealing temperature, experimentally disentangling carrier density and moment geometry. The Hall magnitude and sign evolution point to a low-carrier-density system in which $k_{\mathrm{F}}$ is susceptible to modest external tuning. Cr-NbSe$_2$ thus realizes carrier-sensitive RKKY magnetism in a single crystalline host, within an MBE-plus-annealing approach extensible across the intercalated transition-metal dichalcogenide family.

Abstract

In systems with infinitely many degrees of freedom, fundamental results from quantum information theory can fail. An important example is the uniqueness of purifications: Even when two subsystems, described by commuting von Neumann algebras $A$ and $B$, are tomographically complete, purifications of a state on $A$ need not be related by unitaries in $B$. It was recently shown that this occurs precisely when Haag duality fails, i.e., when the commutant $B'$ is strictly larger than $A$. This raises the question of which fundamental entanglement properties survive in such a setting. We show that, for a pure global state, the ability to steer any ensemble decomposition of the marginal state on $A$ by measurements on $B$ is equivalent to the existence of a state-preserving conditional expectation from $B'$ onto $A$. This establishes a direct connection between quantum steering and subfactor theory. The key observation is that steering is equivalent to the existence of extensions of ensemble decompositions from $A$ to $B'$. Working with general Jordan algebras, we prove that unital positive maps have state-preserving left inverses if and only if ensemble decompositions can be lifted. For the inclusion $A\hookrightarrow B'$, a left inverse is precisely a conditional expectation, yielding the characterization above.
