Summary. This paper develops and validates a composite-boson ansatz to microscopically describe the low-energy states of lattice bosons exhibiting fractional quantum Hall physics at fillings less than 1/2. By calculating key topological invariants like Chern numbers and analyzing quasihole braiding, the authors establish a robust theoretical framework that successfully maps complex FQH behavior onto a manageable bosonic Hilbert space.

Why it may be interesting. This work bridges concepts from strongly correlated electron physics (FQH states) with lattice models using bosonic descriptions. The explicit construction of a composite-boson basis provides a powerful theoretical tool for understanding topological order in condensed matter systems, which is relevant to AMO platforms simulating these interactions.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The research aims to develop a microscopic composite-boson framework to describe the low-energy manifolds of fractional quantum Hall (FQH) systems realized on lattices at generic fillings $\nu < 1/2$. This moves beyond simple counting rules by providing a structured wave function ansatz.

Main result. The proposed composite-boson ansatz successfully organizes the low-energy space, reproducing the expected manifold degeneracy and exhibiting characteristic fractional topological responses like quasihole density depletion and specific braiding phases.

Method. The study employs constructing a many-body trial basis using a composite-boson ansatz derived from reduced-flux orbitals. Calculations involve variational Monte Carlo (VMC) for large systems and direct diagonalization for small ones to probe the spectrum and topological invariants.

Model / system. The system is modeled by interacting bosons on a square lattice governed by the Hofstadter–Bose–Hubbard (HBH) model, studied on a torus geometry. The physics mimics FQH states at sub-half fillings ($\nu < 1/2$).

Key observables. Many-body Chern number ($C_{ ext{MB}}$), quasihole charge diagnostic $Q(R)$, Aharonov–Bohm braiding phase $\theta_B$, and the low-energy manifold dimension (rank).

Important parameters / regimes. Generic fillings $\nu < 1/2$; lattice parameters related to the Hofstadter model; reduced flux quantum numbers.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the system's low-energy physics can be captured by a composite-boson wave function ansatz built from reduced-flux orbitals. VMC is used as a controlled numerical summation method for large systems.

Figures summary. Figures compare exact and ansatz spectra, show density depletion plateaus matching $\nu_{ ext{eff}}$, and illustrate the mean Berry phase ($\theta_B$) matching $2\nu_{ ext{eff}}$ for quasihole braiding.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem by noting the limitations of simple descriptions for FQH manifolds on lattices. It then details the composite-boson ansatz construction, validates it by reproducing known degeneracies and topological invariants (Chern numbers, braiding phases), and finally applies advanced numerical techniques like VMC to confirm results for larger systems.

Abstract

Hofstadter systems provide a lattice route to fractional quantum Hall physics, but their low-energy manifolds often lack simple wave-function descriptions. Here we present a composite-boson ansatz for a broad family of finite-size lattice-split Laughlin-quasihole manifolds in the low-flux Hofstadter--Bose--Hubbard model on a torus at generic fillings \(ν<1/2\). Attaching two vortices to each boson yields reduced-flux orbitals from which we construct a many-body trial basis. Its translation-resolved rank reproduces the expected low-energy-manifold dimension and provides a composite-boson interpretation of the established quasihole counting previously inferred from generalized exclusion rules and thin-torus arguments. For smaller systems, diagonalization within the lowest-band-projected ansatz span closely reproduces the exact projected spectrum, while variational Monte Carlo extends the rank and energetic analysis to larger systems for which explicit subspace construction becomes costly. To probe the fractional quantum Hall character of these manifolds, we calculate their many-body Chern-numbers and show that localized added-flux excitations exhibit quasihole-like behavior, with fractional density depletion and an Aharonov--Bohm-free braiding phase that both track the effective filling. Together, these results establish a microscopic composite-boson framework for organizing a broad family of sub-half-filled fractional quantum Hall manifolds of lattice bosons.

Abstract

These lecture notes provide an introduction to Bose-Einstein condensates with Raman-induced spin-orbit coupling. Owing to the interplay between the peculiar single-particle dispersion, featuring a double-minimum structure, and the two-body interaction, these systems possess a complex phase diagram. Three different quantum phases can be observed, i.e., a stripe, a plane-wave, and a single-minimum phase, each characterized by different broken symmetries. The condensate dynamics is also significantly affected by the spin-orbit coupling, as revealed by the behavior of the Bogoliubov spectrum in infinite systems and the behavior of the discretized collective mode frequencies in trapped configurations, especially close to the phase transitions. In turn, the superfluid and rotational properties are also deeply modified by the coupling between the motional and spin degree of freedom. Finally, a special attention is devoted to the stripe phase and its supersolid character, which can be clearly revealed by the study of its dynamic features.

Summary. This theoretical study proposes a novel mechanism for realizing the spin splitter effect in altermagnetic materials by exploiting symmetry constraints rather than relying on traditional band splitting. By analyzing a frustrated honeycomb magnet, the authors show that hole doping can induce this pure spin current response while maintaining zero charge Hall conductivity. This work is significant because it provides a robust, symmetry-protected route to engineered topological transport phenomena.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a deep symmetry-based understanding of topological magnetotransport phenomena, showing how fundamental crystalline symmetries can enforce exotic quantum responses (like zero charge Hall response) even when spectral features are suppressed. This links condensed matter theory to programmable physical platforms like photonics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The research aims to demonstrate a spin-splitter effect—converting an electric field into a pure transverse spin current without charge response—in altermagnetic materials without relying on conventional crystal-fixed spin-split bands.

Main result. Hole doping in the model realizes this spin splitter using an odd-parity residual, achieving a finite spin Hall conductivity ($\sigma_{sy}^H=0.082\,e^2/h$) while maintaining zero charge Hall response ($\sigma_{ch}^{xy}=0$).

Method. The analysis uses symmetry constraints derived from specific Spin-Space-Group elements (an antitranslation $\Theta$ and a helicity mirror $g$) applied to an $s$-$d$ Hamiltonian describing itinerant electrons coupled to a classical spin texture.

Model / system. The system is modeled as a frustrated honeycomb magnet exhibiting a noncoplanar counter-spiral ground state. The physics is analyzed by examining how the symmetry elements $\Theta$ and $g$ constrain band splitting and transport responses.

Key observables. Spin-splitter conductivity ($\sigma_{sy}^H$), Charge Hall response ($\sigma_{ch}^{xy}$), Band splitting ($\Delta(k)$), and polarization axis rotation angle ($120^\circ$).

Important parameters / regimes. The residual hopping term (odd-parity) below $2 imes 10^{-7} t$ at the Fermi level, and the specific value $\sigma_{sy}^H = 0.082\,e^2/h$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes coupling itinerant electrons to a preformed classical spin texture via an $s$-$d$ Hamiltonian, and that the symmetry constraints ($\Theta$ and $g$) are robustly applicable.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the counter-spiral magnetization texture, the calculated signed band splitting $\Delta(k)$ showing small spectral separation, and plots demonstrating the finite spin Hall conductivity at optimal doping while keeping the charge Hall zero.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the theoretical framework by identifying symmetry elements ($\Theta$ and $g$) that govern altermagnetism. It then develops the core argument that these symmetries dictate transport properties, culminating in the crucial finding about hole doping realizing the spin splitter without band splitting, followed by discussions on reconfigurability.

Abstract

The altermagnetic spin-splitter effect converts an electric field into a transverse pure spin current, with no net magnetization and no charge-Hall counterpart. In established materials this function is tied to crystal-fixed spin-split bands that lock the polarization axis to the lattice. We show that the noncoplanar counter-spiral ground state of a frustrated honeycomb magnet instead carries the altermagnetic operation through a $\mathbf Q$-locked helicity mirror $g$. The mirror selects the spin-current polarization and forbids the perpendicular one, while an antitranslation $Θ$ forbids even-parity spin splitting. Band splitting and spin-splitter response therefore rest on different symmetry elements. Either element alone enforces the charge-Hall zero---a redundancy absent from other spin--orbit-free noncollinear routes---and a charge Hall appears only when both elements are removed. Hole doping then realizes a \emph{spin splitter without spin-split bands}---the symmetry-allowed odd-parity residual below $2\times10^{-7}$ of the hopping $t$ at the Fermi level---with $σ_H^{(s_y)}=0.082\,e^2/h$ without spin--orbit coupling and with zero charge Hall response. Selecting among the three degenerate $\mathbf{Q}$ orientations rotates the polarization axis in exact $120^\circ$ steps at fixed magnitude and charge-Hall zero; the selection rules persist in a $32$-site cell accessible to programmable photonic and circuit lattices.

Abstract

Obtaining equilibrium distributions of nonlinear systems is essential for accurately computing macroscopic observables. Conventional theoretical corrections are typically limited to weak nonlinearities, where interaction terms can be treated as effectively uncorrelated perturbations and the random phase approximation applies. Our approach recovers existing corrections in the weakly nonlinear regime and, crucially, remains valid for strong nonlinearities, where perturbative contributions become correlated and conventional approaches break down. Numerical simulations of the nonlinear Schrödinger equation, the Majda-McLaughlin-Tabak model, and the Fermi-Pasta-Ulam-Tsingou model demonstrate accurate corrections for nonlinearities more than an order of magnitude stronger than those accessible to conventional theories.

Abstract

This paper develops a resource-bounded framework for evaluating practical controllability in open quantum systems using a trained graybox response model. Rather than treating controllability as a binary property of an idealised Hamiltonian model, the proposed approach evaluates the best-achievable process fidelity over a finite, hardware-realisable pulse family under explicit control constraints. The graybox model retains the known coherent dynamics while learning control-dependent open-system distortions from pulse-response data. The resulting surrogate predictions are used to reconstruct the implemented processes and compare them with Haar-random target gates. Practical controllability is then characterised through the distribution of best-achievable infidelities and an area-based summary metric. The results show how finite control resources and open-system noise jointly constrain the gate performance attainable by the chosen pulse family.

Summary. This work identifies 'instability-selection-induced branching' as the dynamical source of irreversibility in amorphous solids when they yield under cyclic stress. Detailed structure

Main problem. The study addresses the fundamental dynamical mechanism responsible for irreversibility when amorphous solids transition from reversible periodic dynamics to irreversible diffusive dynamics upon yielding under cyclic shear.

Main result. Irreversibility is shown to arise not from chaotic sensitivity to initial conditions, but from 'instability-selection-induced branching' events occurring during plastic activity.

Method. The authors employed both particle-based simulations (Kob-Andersen model) and a coarse-grained mean-field soft-spot model to analyze trajectory separation dynamics.

Model / system. The system is amorphous solids subjected to athermal quasistatic oscillatory shear. The theoretical framework utilizes a soft-spot model where plasticity is governed by the competition between nearly-degenerate local instabilities (soft spots).

Paper structure. The paper first establishes that standard chaotic diagnostics fail to explain irreversibility. Abstract

Amorphous solids subjected to athermal quasistatic oscillatory shear undergo a transition from periodic reversible dynamics to irreversible diffusive dynamics at yielding. How irreversibility arises in such deterministic, dissipative dynamics remains unclear. Here we show that trajectories remain locally stable, with perturbations decaying rather than growing even in the irreversible regime, ruling out the sustained exponential sensitivity to initial conditions associated with chaotic dynamics. Rather than diverging continuously, nearby trajectories initially remain close before eventually separating through rare branching events, after which their separation grows diffusively. A mean-field soft-spot model reproduces the same branching statistics and reveals their microscopic origin. We find that branching originates from competition between nearly-degenerate plastic instabilities, in which a small perturbation changes which instability activates first and thereby alters the subsequent sequence of plastic events. These results identify instability-selection-induced branching as a dynamical mechanism for irreversibility in cyclically driven amorphous solids.

Abstract

Projected ensembles---the collections of conditional pure states induced on a system by measuring an entangled environment---have become central objects in quantum science, underlying deep thermalization, quantum state designs, the emergence of classicality, and exhibiting interesting phase transitions. Here we develop a general and exact theory of their dynamics, yielding a systematic approach to their dynamics and equilibrium and non-equilibrium stationary states. We derive an exact continuity equation on quantum state space for the projected ensemble, together with microscopic kinetic equations and analytical expressions for the probability flux and source terms generated by the system-environment interaction. The resulting dynamics admits a classical representation: isolated systems obey Hamiltonian transport and Liouville's theorem, while open systems are described by a kinetic theory of probability transport. The stationary continuity equation provides a general characterization of equilibrium and non-equilibrium stationary projected ensembles, which can be analyzed through the method of characteristics. The theory provides an analytical framework for studying the emergence, structure, and timescales of equilibrium and non-equilibrium stationary projected ensembles, with applications ranging from deep thermalization and the emergence of classicality to random quantum-state generation and quantum device benchmarking.

Abstract

We consider the 1D Su-Schrieffer-Heeger (SSH) model. It was recently shown that, for the noninteracting model in its Dirac semimetal phase, the linear response of the axial charge density to an external electric field is proportional to the electrical conductivity in the presence of finite dissipation, with the proportionality factor determined by the coupling constants. This relation may be viewed as a manifestation of the chiral effect, which is a dimensional reduction of the 3D chiral magnetic effect. In the present work, we investigate the same model in the presence of two versions of local Hubbard-type interactions using numerical Quantum Monte Carlo simulations. We find, within the numerical resolution and for the parameters studied, that, even in the regime where sufficiently strong interactions drive the system into a Mott insulating phase, the proportionality between the induced axial charge density and the electrical conductivity remains unchanged. This result indicates that the chiral effect is not renormalized by local Hubbard interactions.

Abstract

Determining microscopic interactions from spectroscopic and scattering measurements is central to understanding quantum materials, yet it often remains unclear which interactions can be reliably revealed by the available experimental data and how additional experimental modalities should be designed to resolve the remaining ambiguities. Here we present an artificial intelligence-enabled framework for uncertainty-aware Hamiltonian inference and adaptive experimental design. By combining Hamiltonian-conditioned neural surrogates with Bayesian inference and observation geometry, the framework characterizes how measurements constrain Hamiltonian parameter space, quantifies the identifiability of microscopic interactions, and propagates posterior uncertainty directly in the physical Hamiltonian parameter space rather than an abstract learned representation. Using multimodal powder and single-crystal inelastic neutron scattering measurements of the quantum magnet NiPS$_{3}$, we demonstrate physically interpretable Hamiltonian inference, modality-aware uncertainty quantification, and adaptive experimental design. The framework provides a general strategy for uncertainty-aware microscopic characterization and multimodal experimental design across quantum materials.

Abstract
