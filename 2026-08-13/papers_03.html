Williamson majorization theory of fermionic non-Gaussianity

Authors: Xhek Turkeshi, Piotr Sierant, Poetri Sonya Tarabunga
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.10140v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 3/5 · analog quantum simulation 1/5 · correlated / nonlocal dissipation 1/5 · quantum measurements 1/5

Summary. This paper develops the Williamson majorization theory for fermionic non-Gaussianity, creating a resource measure analogous to entanglement measures in quantum information. It establishes that this spectral law governs state conversion under Gaussian protocols, providing strong monotonicity theorems and deriving irreversible bounds on how much non-Gaussian correlation can be converted between states.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, quantifiable resource theory framework for fermionic quantum computation that directly links spectral properties (Williamson spectrum) to fundamental physical processes like state transformation and irreversible dynamics. The connection between entanglement monotones and non-Gaussianity is highly relevant.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper establishes a majorization law for fermionic non-Gaussianity, drawing an analogy to how majorization governs entanglement under local operations.

Main result. It proves that the Williamson spectrum obeys a fundamental majorization law, turning convex deficits into strong monotones and establishing irreversible conversion bounds for pure states.

Method. The analysis uses spectral theory applied to Majorana covariance matrices, defining non-Gaussianity via the deviation of the Williamson spectrum from its Gaussian limit.

Model / system. The system is fermionic, described by $N$ Majorana operators. The diagnostics rely on the covariance matrix $\Gamma_ ho$, which characterizes the state's correlations.

Key observables. Williamson Spectrum ($r_j$), Fermionic Non-Gaussianity ($\Phi_f$), Gaussian Nullity ($ u_G$), and conversion rates $R(\psi o \phi)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The function $f$ defining the monotone must be convex on $[0, 1]$ and satisfy $f(r) < f(1)$ for $r < 1$. Parity conservation is a key constraint.

Assumptions / limitations. The theory primarily analyzes Gaussian protocols with pure state outcomes; rate bounds rely on asymptotic limits ($\epsilon_n o 0$) and definite fermionic parity.

Paper structure. The paper builds from establishing the Williamson majorization law for pure states (analogous to entanglement) to characterizing non-Gaussianity monotones via convex functions $f$. It then extends this to single-shot and asymptotic conversion rates, proving irreversibility when parity is conserved.

Abstract

Pure-state entanglement rests on a single algebraic backbone: majorization of the Schmidt spectrum governs state conversion under local operations and classical communication, and constrains entanglement monotones. Here we establish a corresponding majorization law for fermionic non-Gaussianity, the resource that elevates free fermions to universal quantum computation. Under any fermionic Gaussian protocol with pure state outcomes, the Williamson spectrum of a pure state's Majorana covariance matrix is weakly majorized by its ensemble average. This spectral law mirrors that of entanglement theory. It turns computable non-Gaussianity quantifiers such as fermionic antiflatness and occupation entropies into strong monotones for fermionic non-Gaussianity, and delivers necessary conditions and converse bounds on state conversion under Gaussian protocols. When fermion parity is conserved, no catalyst can remove a majorization obstruction---unless it carries parity coherence---and asymptotic interconversion is irreversible already for pure states. All relevant quantities are accessible from two-point Majorana correlators, turning the theory developed here into experimentally observable properties of quantum matter, testable on present-day quantum devices.

Summary. This paper develops a full quantum-mechanical theory for two-electron Hanbury Brown-Twiss interference. It calculates the correlation function by treating both fermionic exchange statistics and Coulomb repulsion simultaneously. The results establish clear, measurable signatures that allow experimentalists to disentangle these two fundamental quantum effects.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous theoretical tool to distinguish between purely quantum mechanical effects (like fermionic antibunching) and classical/semiclassical interactions (like Coulomb repulsion) in mesoscopic interference experiments.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to identify genuine quantum signatures arising from the interplay between fermionic exchange symmetry and Coulomb repulsion in two-electron interference experiments.

Main result. The theory predicts characteristic features, including a pronounced Coulomb-dominated suppression region and phase offsets, which are absent in semiclassical descriptions. The spatial oscillation frequency outside this region is governed by fermionic exchange symmetry.

Method. The analysis employs a fully quantum-mechanical framework, calculating the second-order spatial correlation function using both wavefunction and field operator approaches.

Model / system. The system models two electrons emitted from two independent needle-tip emitters in free space, treated as a two-body scattering problem governed by a Hamiltonian including kinetic energy and Coulomb interaction. The wave function is separated into center-of-mass and relative coordinates.

Key observables. The second-order spatial correlation function, G^(2)(r1, r2), which measures the probability of detecting two electrons at specific positions.

Important parameters / regimes. Tip separation ($r_0$), detector distance ($D$), electron energy ($\kappa$ or $k$), and bias voltage ($U_{ ext{bias}}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The model assumes point-like sources, monochromatic emission, and instantaneous acceleration to the detectors in the far field.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the experimental setup with two needle tips. Figure 2 depicts the quantum paths corresponding to parallel and crossed paths for the relative wavefunction.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theory by first establishing the general framework using a fully quantum-mechanical description, then deriving the correlation function via both wavefunction and field operator methods, and finally analyzing the distinct signatures arising from Coulomb interaction versus exchange symmetry.

Abstract

Understanding how fermionic exchange and Coulomb repulsion jointly shape two-electron correlations is essential for identifying genuine quantum signatures in multi-electron interference experiments. To address this interplay, we investigate Hanbury Brown and Twiss interference of two electrons generated by two independent needle-tip emitters within a full quantum-mechanical framework. In the absence of Coulomb interaction, the approach reproduces the results previously obtained within a quantum path formalism. For Coulomb-interacting electrons, we predict characteristic features absent in a semiclassical description: a pronounced Coulomb-dominated suppression region as well as Coulomb-induced phase offsets and fringe shifts. At the same time, outside of the Coulomb-dominated region, the spatial oscillation frequency is essentially governed by fermionic exchange symmetry. Our results establish quantitative parameter regimes for disentangling Coulomb interaction from fermionic exchange symmetry in such experiments.

A Simple Algorithm for Best Separable State

Authors: Prashanti Anderson, Samuel B. Hopkins, Amit Rajaraman
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.10147v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 3/5 · Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper presents a new, simpler algorithm to solve the Best Separable State (BSS) problem, which quantifies how well a quantum measurement can distinguish between entangled and separable states. By improving upon existing Sum-of-Squares relaxation techniques, the authors achieve significantly faster running times for finding near-optimal solutions in this fundamental problem of quantum resource theory.

Why it may be interesting. The techniques developed—advanced algebraic methods like refined pinning lemmas and efficient polynomial optimization solvers—are highly relevant for analyzing resource constraints in quantum information protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the Best Separable State (BSS) problem, which seeks to find the maximum acceptance probability of a quantum measurement restricted to unentangled (separable) states.

Main result. The authors develop an algorithm that significantly improves the time complexity for solving BSS in both the $\epsilon$-approximation regime ($1-\epsilon$) and the $q/n$ approximation regime, beating previous state-of-the-art bounds.

Method. The core method involves generalizing global correlation rounding techniques applied to Sum-of-Squares (SoS) relaxations of polynomial optimization problems over the unit sphere.

Model / system. The problem is formulated using a Hermitian matrix $M$ describing a quantum measurement device, studied in the context of finding optimal separable states $\langle x \otimes y, M(x \otimes y) angle$.

Key observables. BSS(M), approximation error ($\epsilon$), and the ratio $\|\Pi(x \otimes y)\|_2^2 / \|x \otimes y\|_2^2$.

Important parameters / regimes. The dimension $n$, the desired accuracy $\epsilon$, and the parameter $q$ defining the target separation.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is simplified by working over $\mathbb{R}$, though the result generalizes from a symmetric case to the non-symmetric one. The proof relies on proving a new variant of the 'pinning lemma'.

Paper structure. The paper introduces BSS, relates it to polynomial optimization and SoS relaxations, develops an improved rounding technique based on global correlation rounding, proves a novel pinning lemma, and applies this machinery to achieve the final time complexity bounds for distinguishing between different values of BSS(M).

Abstract

We study the best separable state problem (BSS), which asks for the maximum acceptance probability of a quantum measurement over unentangled states. In classical terms, the goal is to maximize $\langle(x \otimes y), M (x \otimes y)\rangle$ over unit vectors $x,y$ where $0 \preceq M \preceq I$; we call this value $\mathrm{BSS}(M)$. We study $\mathrm{BSS}$ in the "perfect completeness" regime, where given $M$ such that $\mathrm{BSS}(M) = 1$ the goal is to find the best possible solution $x,y$ -- this generalizes the problem of finding a rank-one matrix as close as possible to a given subspace of $\mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ guaranteed to contain a rank-one matrix. The strongest known algorithmic guarantees for this problem are: (1) an algorithm which finds a solution with value $1-\varepsilon$ in time $\exp(\sqrt{n} (\log n)^{O(1)} / \varepsilon^2)$, due to Barak, Kothari, and Steurer, and (2) an algorithm which finds a solution with value $q/n$ in time roughly $n^{O(q)}$, due to Bhattiprolu, Ghosh, Guruswami, Lee, and Tulsiani. We give a much simpler approach to rounding the SoS relaxation, generalizing the canonical "global correlation rounding" technique, and obtain a better running time. Given $M$ with $\mathrm{BSS}(M) = 1$, our algorithm finds a solution with value $1-ε$ in time $n^{O(\sqrt{n/\varepsilon})}$, and a solution of value $q/n$ in time $n^{O(\sqrt q)}$. Using the same techniques, we prove a new variant of the "pinning lemma", a measure-decomposition theorem widely used in LP/SDP rounding, high-dimensional probability, and statistical physics, which we believe is of independent interest.

Helimagnetism from competing intra- and interchain interactions in CrBr$_2$ and CrI$_2$

Authors: John A. Schneeloch, Matthew B. Stone, Zachary Morgan, Despina Louca
Type: experiment · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.10263v1

Summary. This work uses inelastic neutron scattering on $ ext{CrBr}_2$ and $ ext{CrI}_2$ to determine that their helimagnetic order stems from the competition between intrachain and interchain antiferromagnetic interactions. The study quantifies this by fitting spin wave data, finding that single-ion anisotropy modulates the helix, while also revealing evidence of structural disorder in the crystal stacking.

Why it may be interesting. The detailed analysis of spin wave dispersions and the extraction of multiple competing exchange couplings provide a quantitative understanding of magnetic frustration in low-dimensional quantum magnets, which is highly relevant for designing novel quantum materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to determine the unknown magnetic interactions responsible for the helimagnetic order observed in the layered compounds $ ext{CrBr}_2$ and $ ext{CrI}_2$, while also investigating how dimensionality affects this ordering.

Main result. Helimagnetism primarily arises from the competition between nearest-neighbor antiferromagnetic exchange interactions within chains ($ ext{J}_1$) and between chains ($ ext{J}'_1$). Furthermore, single-ion anisotropy significantly modulates the helical spin structure.

Method. The research heavily relies on inelastic neutron scattering (INS) measurements on single crystals to map out spin wave dispersions and extract magnetic exchange constants. Elastic neutron scattering is also used to probe structural disorder.

Model / system. The physical system consists of van der Waals layered magnets, $ ext{CrBr}_2$ and $ ext{CrI}_2$, which exhibit quasi-2D magnetism. The analysis uses spin wave theory applied to Heisenberg Hamiltonians incorporating competing exchange couplings ($J_1, J'_1$) and single-ion anisotropy (SIA).

Key observables. Spin wave dispersion ($\omega(Q)$), magnetic Bragg peak intensities, incommensurability ($\delta$), and the temperature dependence of spin correlations.

Important parameters / regimes. The dominant exchange interactions are $ ext{J}_1$ and $ ext{J}'_1$; SIA values are significant (e.g., $-0.16 ext{ meV}$ for $ ext{CrBr}_2$); interlayer coupling ($J_c$) is found to be weak.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the observed magnetic behavior can be modeled by nearest-neighbor exchange interactions and incorporates corrections from single-ion anisotropy and higher-order harmonic terms in INS fitting.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate lattice structures, elastic intensity measurements showing disordered stacking, and INS data revealing spin wave dispersions with avoided crossings attributed to SIA and $3k_M$ harmonics.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the magnetic order via INS, then fitting exchange parameters using simplified models ($J_1-J'_1$), refining the model by incorporating SIA effects visible in avoided crossings, and finally analyzing structural disorder and higher-order harmonic peaks to constrain the full Hamiltonian.

Abstract

CrBr$_2$ and CrI$_2$ are promising platforms for studying the effect of dimensionality on magnetic order, having been isolated in a 3-, 2-, and 1-dimensional form as bulk crystals, monolayers, and individual chains encapsulated in carbon nanotubes. However, the interactions that give rise to their helimagnetic order are unknown. Via inelastic neutron scattering on single crystals, we have determined the exchange interactions of these compounds from the spin wave dispersions, finding that the helimagnetic order arises primarily from competing antiferromagnetic interactions between intrachain and interchain nearest-neighbors. Single-ion anisotropy is substantial, modulating the helical spin rotation and gapping the inelastic intensity at points of branch crossings. As temperature increases, long-range order vanishes but intralayer correlations remain detectable up to at least 50 K.

Summary. This paper addresses the fundamental challenge of performing high-precision measurements involving multiple unknown parameters simultaneously. By introducing the concept of Indefinite Evolution (IE), the authors demonstrate a novel resource that allows quantum systems to overcome inherent encoding and measurement incompatibilities. This breakthrough opens a pathway toward realizing true Heisenberg-limited sensing in complex, multi-parameter physical platforms.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete theoretical mechanism (IE) for achieving optimal sensing bounds in complex multi-parameter settings, which is highly relevant for designing next-generation quantum sensors based on interferometry or atomic clocks.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Extending quantum metrology precision from single-parameter estimation (Heisenberg limit) to multiparameter estimation is fundamentally constrained by parameter-encoding and measurement incompatibility.

Main result. The introduction of Indefinite Evolution (IE) overcomes these incompatibilities, enabling the simultaneous achievement of Heisenberg-limited scaling for multiple parameters in quantum sensing.

Method. The authors propose a framework utilizing IE, where control operations are placed in a coherent superposition to allow compatible parameter encoding and optimal measurement strategies.

Model / system. The model involves quantum probing using unitary evolution $U_h = e^{-iH(h)t}$ with unknown parameters $h=(h_1, \dots, h_n)$ encoded by noncommuting generators. The analysis is generalized to noisy, many-body, and high-dimensional probes.

Key observables. Quantum Fisher Information Matrix (QFIM), Classical Fisher Information Matrix (CFIM), Simultaneous Heisenberg Limit (SHL) scaling ($F \propto t^2$).

Important parameters / regimes. The relationship between signal generators (orthogonal vs. parallel); the presence or absence of Signal Reversal (SR).

Assumptions / limitations. The core assumption is that IE acts as an operational resource capable of circumventing fundamental limitations imposed by definite evolution protocols.

Figures summary. Figure 2 compares precision estimates for different strategies (FE, QEC, IE) showing IE achieves SHL where others fail; other figures illustrate the general protocol structure and scaling in multi-qubit/high-dimensional settings.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the limitations of standard metrology protocols due to encoding/measurement incompatibility. It then introduces and details the IE framework, demonstrating its success across various generator types (orthogonal, non-parallel, parallel) and generalizing the results to noisy and high-dimensional systems.

Abstract

Quantum metrology achieves Heisenberg-limited precision in single-parameter estimation, but its multiparameter extension is fundamentally constrained by both parameter-encoding and measurement incompatibility. Noncommuting signal generators may cause incompatible parameter-encoding, preventing the quantum Fisher information matrix from simultaneously achieving the Heisenberg scale for all parameters. Due to incompatible optimal measurements, the classical Fisher information matrix represents the practical attainable precision. Here, we introduce a multiparameter metrology framework based on indefinite evolution (IE), in which different control operations and signal reversal are placed in a coherent superposition. For a single-qubit probe with mutually orthogonal signal generators, IE enables compatible parameter encoding and optimal measurement without the signal reversal. For parallel generators, where only signal reversal realized by its generator is available, IE can achieve the same performance. We further extend this mechanism to noisy, many-body, and high-dimensional probes, and establish general conditions for achieving the simultaneous Heisenberg-limit. In contrast, definite evolution cannot achieve the same performance under compatible optimal measurements, even when signal reversal is available. Our results identify IE as an operational resource for overcoming multiparameter incompatibility and open a route toward attainable Heisenberg-limited sensing in interferometric platforms.

Summary. This paper constructs a detailed quantum circuit realization for the attention mechanism used in Transformers, specifically when inputs are probability distributions. It shows that core components like softmax and temperature can be mapped exactly to sequences of quantum measurements and rotations. This work bridges advanced deep learning architectures with fundamental principles of quantum information processing.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on AI, the rigorous mapping of continuous mathematical functions (like softmax) to discrete quantum operations (Born measurements, rotations) provides deep insights into how complex classical computations can be fundamentally constrained or realized within quantum information theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to develop an exact, component-by-component quantum realization for the softmax attention mechanism, particularly when inputs are constrained to the probability simplex.

Main result. It establishes that softmax attention admits such a quantum analog in the infinite-shot limit, mapping classical components like temperature and residual connections to specific quantum gates and measurement protocols.

Method. The realization uses quantum primitives including amplitude encoding, block-encoded projections, Hadamard tests for inner products, and controlled rotations ($ ext{c}R_y$) within a circuit framework.

Model / system. The model is the single-head gated transformer attention layer operating on inputs restricted to the probability simplex. The computation is mapped onto a quantum circuit utilizing qubits and unitary transformations.

Key observables. Attention scores, which are realized as Hadamard-test statistics on block-encoded projections of amplitude-encoded inputs.

Important parameters / regimes. The softmax temperature ($eta$) is physically realized by the number ($L$) of post-selected measurement rounds; learnable parameters correspond to rotation-gate angles.

Assumptions / limitations. The equivalence is exact only in the infinite-shot limit. The construction assumes inputs are row-normalized probability distributions (the probability simplex domain).

Paper structure. The paper builds an equivalence dictionary by realizing core attention components (scores, softmax, temperature, residual) sequentially. It details the necessary quantum primitives for each classical function, culminating in a full layer equivalence theorem machine-checked in Lean 4.

Abstract

The attention mechanism forms the foundation of many modern AI models such as the Transformer. In one subclass of problems where attention is used, inputs and outputs are bound to the probability simplex so that all outputs sum to one. In this setting, softmax attention admits an exact, component-by-component quantum realization. Attention scores are Hadamard-test statistics on block-encoded projections of amplitude-encoded inputs. The exponential softmax is the interior of a cosine-squared family generated by Born-rule measurement under an exact bijection, whose boundary expresses sparse attention with exact zeros at finite parameter values. The softmax temperature is a repetition count where post-selected measurement rounds realize discretized inverse temperature exactly. Value aggregation is a deterministic column-loading channel that dilates the column-stochastic value matrix. The gated residual is the preparation angle of a single ancilla, with the additive identity at a mixing angle of π/2. Every learnable parameter is a rotation-gate angle. The composed layer is exact in the infinite-shot limit with one measure-and-reload step per attention score; a fully-coherent variant is ε-approximate via quantum singular value transformation in the infinite depth limit. The algebraic core is machine-checked in Lean 4.

Robust CHSH Self-Testing with Finite-Energy GKP States

Authors: Farzin Salek, Masahito Hayashi
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.11122v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 3/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops a theoretical framework to enhance Bell-pair self-testing using finite-energy GKP states. By incorporating measurements based on the photon number modulo four, the authors demonstrate that the achievable CHSH violation and extractability bounds exceed previous limits. This work is significant for quantum optics as it provides a pathway toward device-independent verification of entanglement in realistic physical systems.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a concrete, resource-efficient method for achieving device-independent quantum guarantees (Bell testing) in continuous variable systems by engineering measurement bases using discrete information (photon number modulo 4).

Detailed structure

Main problem. The research aims to establish robust self-testing protocols for Bell pairs using finite-energy Gaussian states (GKP) in continuous variable systems.

Main result. The analysis provides quantitative thresholds showing that the CHSH value and extractability bound can be significantly improved by incorporating deterministic phase-bit coarse-graining derived from modular photon-number information.

Method. The authors employ a full-oscillator analysis, combining measurements on position/momentum with fixed binary coarse-grainings of photon number modulo four to calculate physical correlations without logical post-corrections.

Model / system. The system is based on bosonic modes encoded using the Gottesman–Kitaev–Preskill (GKP) code. The analysis focuses on finite-energy filtered versions of these states, allowing for measurements that utilize modular photon-number information.

Key observables. CHSH value ($S$), extractability bound ($\Xi( ho o |\psi_B angle)$), and model thresholds (e.g., 4.56 dB).

Important parameters / regimes. The canonical displacement amplitude $d$, the filter parameter $eta$, and the finite-energy cutoff/approximation level ($n$).

Assumptions / limitations. The results are 'honest-model predictions' that do not account for experimental imperfections like loss or detection efficiency, though a device-independent guarantee is discussed.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theoretical framework by analyzing the extractability bound using finite-rank projections and then applies this to the GKP state structure, showing how modular photon-number measurements enhance the self-testing capability beyond standard Pauli settings.

Abstract

We present a full-oscillator analysis of a finite-energy GKP CHSH test whose observed score yields robust Bell-pair self-testing. Periodically binned position and momentum give the Pauli settings, while a fixed binary coarse-graining of photon number modulo four and its displaced conjugate realize the tilted settings. For a number-filtered GKP source, we retain the finite codeword overlap, define the measurements on all photon-number sectors, and compute the physical correlations without logical post-corrections. With the canonical ideal-logical displacement \(d=\sqrtπ\), the CHSH value exceeds the local bound above \(4.56\) dB of per-peak squeezing, and Kaniewski's extractability bound becomes nontrivial above \(5.02\) dB. Calibrating only \(d\) using an independently characterized finite-energy parameter lowers these model thresholds to \(4.21\) dB and \(4.58\) dB, respectively; at \(12\) dB, it raises the score from \(2.69486\) to \(2.78858\) and the corresponding target-state overlap bound from \(0.90758\) to \(0.97243\). This calibration is fixed before Bell-test data are collected. The displacement activates the odd modulo-four sectors, so their fixed a priori assignments are a genuine finite-energy component. The large gain is specific to the deterministic phase-bit coarse-graining; independently calibrating the one-bit POVM with randomized odd-sector outcomes gives only a much smaller improvement. These are honest-model predictions, not loss, detection-efficiency, or finite-sample thresholds. In an experiment, a device-independent guarantee for an extracted Bell pair follows by inserting a confidence lower bound on the observed CHSH score into the self-testing theorem.

Abstract

We study the sample complexity of estimating the von Neumann entropy of an unknown $d$-dimensional quantum state. All previously known estimators require $Ω(d^2)$ samples, and plug-in estimators are known to face a quadratic barrier. We give the first subquadratic-sample estimator: for additive error $\varepsilon$, our estimator uses \[ O\!\left(\frac{d^2 \log^2(\log(d)) \log(1/\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon^2 \log^2(d)} + \frac{\log^2(d/\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon^2}\right) \] samples. In particular, for constant $\varepsilon$, the complexity is $O_\varepsilon(d^2\log^2(\log(d))/\log^2(d))=o(d^2)$. Our analysis introduces a new pinching inequality that bounds the entropy loss under a space direct-sum decomposition, together with a bias-corrected estimator for large eigenvalues and a new bounded-coefficient polynomial estimator for small eigenvalues.

Abstract

The Uhlmann fidelity ${\rm F}(ρ_0,ρ_1) = {\rm tr}|\sqrt{ρ_0}\sqrt{ρ_1}|$ is one of the most fundamental quantities in quantum information theory for quantifying the closeness between two quantum states. Estimating the Uhlmann fidelity to within additive error $\varepsilon$ requires a number of copies of the states, or queries to their state-preparation circuits, that depends at least linearly on the smaller of the ranks of $ρ_0$ and $ρ_1$. Consequently, this rank dependence disappears when either state is pure, in which case the query and sample complexities depend only polynomially on $1/\varepsilon$. However, the known optimal estimator for ${\rm F}(ρ,|ψ\rangle\!\langleψ|)$ due to Fang and Wang (ESA 2025) requires prior knowledge of which state is pure. In this work, we remove this mathematically unnecessary prior-knowledge requirement and establish an optimal estimator for ${\rm F}(ρ, |ψ\rangle\!\langleψ|)$ under the sole promise that one of the two states is pure, without knowing which one. Our estimator is obtained by specializing the refined algorithmic Uhlmann transform of Utsumi, Nakata, Wang, and Takagi (2025) to the case where one state is pure. In this setting, the Uhlmann fidelity can be recovered as follows: apply a unitary dilation of ${\rm tr}_{\sf A}(|ψ_0\rangle\!\langleψ_1|)$ (or its inverse) to the reference register $\sf R$ of the purification $|ψ_1\rangle$ (or $|ψ_0\rangle$) on the registers $\sf A$ and $\sf R$, estimate the corresponding square-root amplitude in each case, and take the maximum of the resulting two estimates.
