Fermi surface change and $d$-wave superconductivity in the square lattice Kondo-Heisenberg model

Authors: Alexander Nikolaenko, Riccardo Rende, Luciano Loris Viteritti, Subir Sachdev, Ya-Hui Zhang
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23799v1

Summary. The paper theoretically investigates the Kondo-Heisenberg model to understand how magnetism and superconductivity compete. Using advanced quantum simulation methods like NQS, it demonstrates that the system undergoes a fundamental change in its electronic Fermi surface structure. This transition region hosts robust $d_{x^2-y^2}$ superconductivity, suggesting pairing is mediated by residual antiferromagnetic correlations.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses fundamental questions in strongly correlated electron physics, particularly the mechanism of unconventional superconductivity arising from competing magnetic orders, which is a major focus area for theoretical condensed matter physicists.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the interplay between Fermi surface changes, antiferromagnetism, and unconventional $d_{x^2-y^2}$ superconductivity in a two-dimensional Kondo-Heisenberg model.

Main result. The model exhibits a transition from a small to a large electronic Fermi surface as the Kondo coupling increases, with $d$-wave superconductivity appearing robustly in the crossover region between magnetic and heavy Fermi liquid states.

Method. The research employs advanced quantum many-body techniques, primarily Neural Network Quantum States (NQS) variational optimization, supplemented by mean-field calculations for comparison.

Model / system. The system is a two-dimensional Kondo-Heisenberg model on a square lattice, describing conduction electrons interacting with localized spin moments via exchange and Kondo couplings. The Hamiltonian includes terms for hopping, Heisenberg exchange ($J$), and Kondo coupling ($JK$).

Key observables. Fermi surface enclosed area (small vs. large), $d_{x^2-y^2}$ pairing correlator ($\Delta$), staggered magnetization ($m_s^2$), and conduction electron momentum distribution ($n(\mathbf{k})$).

Important parameters / regimes. Kondo coupling ($JK$) and Heisenberg coupling ($J$); the analysis is performed at fixed doping (e.g., $\delta=1/4$).

Assumptions / limitations. The use of NQS methods allows for calculations away from half-filling, avoiding the sign problem encountered by auxiliary-field QMC; mean-field approaches are used for comparison but are noted to overestimate magnetic order.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the evolution of order parameters ($m_s^2$ vs. $\Delta$) across different coupling regimes, showing a superconducting dome peaking near the quantum critical point where the Fermi surface changes character.

Paper structure. The paper systematically maps the ground-state phase diagram by analyzing how electronic and magnetic order parameters evolve as tuning parameters (Kondo/Heisenberg couplings) are varied. It contrasts results from NQS with mean-field theory to establish physical regimes.

Abstract

We study the two-dimensional Kondo-Heisenberg model on a square lattice, with the conduction electrons away from half-filling, using neural network quantum states. Mapping the ground-state phase diagram as a function of the Kondo and Heisenberg couplings, we identify (i) at weak Kondo coupling, antiferromagnetic Néel order with a Fermi surface whose enclosed area counts only the conduction electrons and is insensitive to the Néel order, and (ii) at strong coupling, a heavy Fermi liquid with a Fermi surface whose enclosed area counts both the conduction electrons and the spins. In the crossover between these regimes, we find $d_{x^2-y^2}$ superconductivity, evidenced by off-diagonal long-range order in the pair-pair correlations and a pairing-amplitude dome that coexists with the underlying magnetic phase. Our results establish Fermi volume change and unconventional superconductivity as intrinsic features of the two-dimensional Kondo-Heisenberg model.

Fluctuation exchange study on the electron-hole asymmetry of the superconductivity across 1/3 filling in the trilayer Hubbard model

Summary. This theoretical study analyzes superconductivity in a trilayer Hubbard model near $1/3$ filling using the FLEX approximation. It finds that while weak interactions yield symmetric superconducting behavior, strong correlations induce a significant electron-hole asymmetry favoring hole doping. This suggests that spin fluctuations play a crucial role in determining the pairing symmetry and stability of the superconducting state.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on correlated electrons, the detailed analysis of pairing mechanisms ($\Delta$) and susceptibility ($\chi_s$) provides insights into how collective excitations (spin fluctuations) mediate pairing in strongly interacting quantum systems, which has parallels to dynamics studied in AMO physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the electron-hole asymmetry of superconductivity near $1/3$ filling in the trilayer Hubbard model.

Main result. Strong correlations (large U) induce a pronounced electron-hole asymmetry, favoring superconductivity when the system is hole-doped relative to $1/3$ filling.

Method. The research employs the Fluctuation Exchange Approximation (FLEX) combined with solving the linearized Eliashberg equation to calculate superconducting parameters.

Model / system. The model is the trilayer Hubbard model, featuring three coupled layers and an onsite interaction U. The analysis focuses on band fillings near $1/3$ where bonding and nonbonding bands are nearly half-filled.

Key observables. Superconducting coupling constant ($\lambda$), gap function ($\Delta$), spectral function ($A(\mathbf{k}, \omega)$), and dynamical spin susceptibility ($ ext{Im } \chi_s$).

Important parameters / regimes. Interaction strength ratio ($U/t_{\perp}$), doping level (hole-doped vs. electron-doped relative to $1/3$ filling).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary approximation used is the Fluctuation Exchange Approximation (FLEX) theory, and calculations involve solving the linearized Eliashberg equation.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate $\lambda$ dependence on doping showing asymmetry for large $U/t_{\perp}$, and gap functions ($\Delta$) confirming $s_{\pm}$-wave structure in the hole-doped case versus local pairing at strong coupling.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the model and methodology (FLEX, Eliashberg), then comparing weak vs. strong coupling limits to identify the emergence of electron-hole asymmetry, and finally analyzing gap functions and spin susceptibilities across different doping regimes.

Abstract

We study within the fluctuation exchange approximation the trilayer Hubbard model where three layers of the Hubbard model are coupled by large interlayer hoppings so that the overlap of the bonding, nonbonding, and antibonding bands is relatively small. We pay special attention to the band fillings close to 1/3, for which the bonding and nonbonding bands as a whole are close to half filling. For relatively small values of the onsite $U$, superconductivity roughly exhibits electron-hole symmetric behavior, as expected for a nearly half-filled two-band system. By contrast, an asymmetry appears when $U$ becomes large, where superconductivity is more favored in the hole-doped regime, i.e., in the regime where electrons are removed from 1/3 filling. We attribute this asymmetry to the asymmetric renormalization of the bonding, nonbonding, and antibonding bands when $U$ is large.

Auxiliary Schmidt Rank as a Resource for Photonic Bell Measurements

Authors: Pradip Laha, Peter van Loock
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.24591v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: entanglement & information structure 3/5 · quantum measurements 3/5 · measurement-induced transitions 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. The paper establishes a rigorous resource threshold theorem showing that achieving deterministic Bell measurements for qudits requires the auxiliary entangled state's Schmidt rank ($r_\Phi$) to match the dimension of the system ($d$). This proves that entanglement is a necessary and quantifiable resource beyond simple passive optical elements.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides fundamental resource theorems quantifying how entanglement (via Schmidt rank) must be supplied to overcome inherent limitations of passive linear optics when performing high-dimensional quantum state measurements.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The research determines the minimum auxiliary Schmidt rank required for photonic systems to achieve deterministic Bell measurements when passive linear optics are used.

Main result. Deterministic discrimination of all $d^2$ Bell-state labels requires the auxiliary Schmidt rank ($r_\Phi$) to be at least equal to the qudit dimension, i.e., $r_\Phi \ge d$.

Method. The analysis uses rank constraints derived from analyzing effective system-level detection matrices ($\Gamma_\Phi(P)$) formed by contracting over the auxiliary state.

Model / system. The model involves two photons carrying unknown qudits ($S_A, S_B$) interacting with a fixed auxiliary entangled state $|\Phi angle_R$ via passive linear optics and Photon-Number-Resolving (PNR) detection.

Key observables. Bell-state labels $(p, q)$, the success probability of Bell measurement ($p_{ ext{BM}}$), and the rank of the effective detection matrix $\Gamma_\Phi(P)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The qudit dimension $d$ and the auxiliary Schmidt rank $r_\Phi$. Thresholds include $r_\Phi \ge \lceil d/2 ceil$ for single outcomes and $r_\Phi \ge d$ for full determinism.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes ideal PNR detection, a fixed auxiliary state $|\Phi angle$, and the use of only passive linear optics without active operations.

Figures summary. Figures summarize resource hierarchies showing thresholds for bare algebra ($R_0(d)$), single functional ($\lceil d/2 ceil$), and deterministic decoding ($d$).

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the limitations of passive optics, then deriving rank bounds on detection matrices using auxiliary state entanglement, culminating in the proof that $r_\Phi=d$ is necessary and sufficient for full Bell-label distinguishability.

Abstract

In quantum communication and fusion-based quantum computation, photonic Bell measurements are fundamentally limited when only passive linear optics is employed. While for qubits, some Bell states can be unambiguously identified with static beam splitters and no extra photons or entanglement, additional auxiliary photons or at least additional auxiliary degrees of freedom with a certain level of additional entanglement are needed to approach or attain a complete, deterministic Bell measurement. Here, we prove an exact resource threshold when the same two photons carry system qudits of dimension $d$ and a fixed auxiliary entangled state $Φ$, possibly distributed over several additional degrees of freedom, with total Schmidt rank $r_Φ$. We show that a single conclusive Bell-label functional can occur for $r_Φ\geqslant\lceil d/2\rceil$, but deterministic discrimination of all $d^2$ Bell-state labels requires $r_Φ\geqslant d$. A maximally entangled rank-$d$ auxiliary state achieves the bound by local Bell-basis sorting between each photon's system and auxiliary degrees of freedom. Thus, the auxiliary Schmidt rank is a certified resource for ancilla-photon-free, embedded photonic Bell measurements.

Efficient Graph State Purification with Factorized Graph-Preserving Operations across Local Clifford Orbits

Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23809v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: entanglement & information structure 3/5 · analog quantum simulation 1/5 · correlated / nonlocal dissipation 1/5 · correlated cavity matter 1/5 · driven-dissipative phase transition 1/5

Summary. This work develops a systematic framework to efficiently design entanglement distillation circuits for graph states by restricting gates to those that preserve the graph structure. By classifying these 'factorized graph-preserving' operations using concepts like Local Complementation orbits and Minimum Edge Representatives, the authors provide a scalable method superior to standard recurrence protocols under realistic noise conditions.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on quantum computation, the rigorous topological classification of allowed operations (H/B groups) provides a powerful mathematical tool for understanding constraints in highly entangled many-body systems, which can inform theoretical models in condensed matter physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Systematic entanglement distillation for arbitrary graph states is challenging due to the rapid growth of the circuit design space.

Main result. The authors introduce factorized, graph-preserving Clifford operations and use Minimum Edge Representatives (MERs) over Local Complementation (LC) orbits to design scalable, topology-aware purification circuits that outperform standard protocols under noise.

Method. They classify admissible gates into homogeneous (H) and bilocal (B) groups based on the underlying graph structure (bipartiteness or leaf vertices), allowing for a compact, factorized description of the gate set.

Model / system. The system involves multipartite entangled graph states $| ext{G} angle$, which are fundamental to measurement-based quantum computation and stabilizer codes. The analysis focuses on operations that preserve this graph-state basis across local Clifford transformations.

Key observables. Output fidelity ($F_{out}$), success probability, and the minimum number of edges in a Local Complementation (LC) orbit representative.

Important parameters / regimes. Graph structure (bipartite vs. non-bipartite), presence of leaf vertices, input entanglement fidelity ($F$), and noise model parameters.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the ability to use MERs to provide a canonical representation for LC orbits, and that the gate set can be factorized into H and B components.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the action of CNOT gates on graph states (Fig. 1), the structure of homogeneous gates on bipartite graphs (Fig. 2), and how bilocal XCX operations at a leaf vertex can be compensated by a CZ gate to preserve the state basis (Fig. 4).

Paper structure. The paper systematically introduces the problem, defines graph-preserving gates via H and B groups based on graph topology, establishes the framework using LC orbits and MERs for canonical representation, and finally applies this structure to optimize noisy distillation circuits.

Abstract

Graph states form a broad class of multipartite entangled states underlying measurement-based quantum computation, quantum networks, and stabilizer codes. However, systematic entanglement distillation for arbitrary graph states remains challenging because the circuit design space grows rapidly with the number of parties. We introduce a group of Clifford operations that we call "factorized graph-preserving". It enables us to efficiently enumerate and optimize graph-state purification circuits at finite size for realistic noisy hardware. These operations map products of graph-basis states to products of graph-basis states, so their action can be represented as permutations of graph-basis labels. Moreover, this useful gate set admits a compact factorized description determined by simple graph-theoretic features. This structure also allows, after some initial cached precomputation, drastically lower computational complexity for simulating a gate. We further organize these operations over local-complementation (LC) orbits using minimum-edge representatives (MERs), which let us design purification circuits that apply to all locally equivalent graph states (up to a basis change). Using this framework, we optimize noisy finite-size multipartite distillation circuits for several graph-state families. Numerical results show that the resulting graph-preserving circuits can outperform standard recurrence-based purification protocols under realistic gate and measurement noise. Our results establish LC-orbit structure and factorized graph-preserving operations as practical tools for scalable, topology-aware and hardware-constrained graph-state distillation protocol design. Our work can also be interpreted as a graph-based heuristic for finding transversal gates.

Summary. This work rigorously analyzes the probability distribution of particle displacements in Continuous-Time Random Walks (CTRWs), showing that while typical fluctuations follow Gaussian statistics, rare events exhibit pronounced Laplace tails. By developing a rate function framework valid at finite times, the authors provide a powerful theoretical tool to characterize anomalous diffusion in complex media.

Why it may be interesting. The robust derivation of non-Gaussian tails (Laplace tails) in a fundamental transport model like CTRW provides insights into anomalous diffusion regimes relevant to complex biological or glassy systems, which is crucial for understanding energy/particle transport dynamics beyond simple Brownian motion.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the deviation of particle displacement distributions from the expected Gaussian behavior (Brownian motion) in complex media, focusing on the emergence of pronounced exponential decay known as Laplace tails.

Main result. The probability density function $P(x, t)$ for displacements in Continuous-Time Random Walks exhibits Laplace tails in the large spatial separation limit ($|x| o \infty$), which is quantified by a rate function $\chi_{ARF}(q)$.

Method. The analysis employs advanced techniques from Large Deviation Theory (LDT), utilizing saddle point approximations and developing rate function-like frameworks for both the positional PDF $P(x, t)$ and the renewal probability $Q_t(n)$.

Model / system. The model is the Continuous-Time Random Walk (CTRW), which describes particle diffusion in complex environments like glasses or living cells. The process is governed by random waiting times $\psi( au)$ and subsequent jumps.

Key observables. $P(x, t)$ (positional PDF), $Q_t(n)$ (probability of exactly $n$ renewals), and the associated rate functions ($I(y)$ or $\chi(q)$).

Important parameters / regimes. Time $t$, spatial separation $|x|$, ratio $q=x/t$, and parameters defining the waiting time distribution $\psi( au)$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on developing rate function frameworks valid for finite times, extending beyond standard asymptotic LDT results. Approximations are made using saddle point methods and Taylor expansions of $\psi( au)$ near $ au=0$.

Figures summary. Figures compare $Q_t(n)$ across different waiting time PDFs (Erlang, sum of two exponentials) against CLT predictions and the derived Asymptotic Rate Function (ARF), demonstrating matching tail behavior. Other figures plot $P(x, t)$ showing convergence to Laplace tails.

Paper structure. The paper develops a rate function framework for $Q_t(n)$, analyzes the large deviation regime ($y=n/t$), and then extends this formalism to derive the propagator $P(x, t)$. The core is comparing theoretical predictions (ARF) against known limits (CLT) and numerical simulations across various time scales.

Abstract

During Brownian motion, the displacement is normally distributed, a classical fact aligned with the central limit theorem. However, single particle tracking in complex media such as glasses, living cells, and colloidal suspensions often reveals pronounced exponential decay of the displacement distribution, known as Laplace tails. In a short letter, two of us presented the emergence of Laplace tails in the continuous time random walk (CTRW) framework. Here, a detailed complementary study is presented. By exploring the behavior of $Q_t(n)$, the probability that exactly $n$ renewals occur during time $t$, we develop a rate function-like framework for this quantity, valid for finite $t$. We show that $Q_t(n)$ exhibits exponential tails, which in turn give rise to exponential tails of the positional probability density function $P(x,t)$. Favorable comparison to finite-time numerical simulations and asymptotic large deviation rate functions establishes the validity of our results over a wide temporal range.

Connecting Quantum Tomography and Quantum Retrodiction

Authors: Sebastian Murk, Ian Tan, Fabian Müller, Dominik Šafránek
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23777v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 3/5 · Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5 · entanglement & information structure 1/5 · measurement-induced transitions 1/5

Summary. This work unifies quantum tomography and retrodiction by demonstrating that they are governed by the same underlying principle: maximizing the log-likelihood of measurement outcomes. The authors derive a generalized Kubo–Mori update, showing it relates directly to the Petz recovery map, thereby providing a robust, gradient-based framework for state reconstruction in quantum systems.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a rigorous mathematical link between fundamental concepts in quantum estimation theory (tomography/retrodiction) using modern tools from information geometry and optimization.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper unifies quantum tomography (state reconstruction) and quantum retrodiction (inferring past states) by showing they stem from a single underlying principle.

Main result. It proves that the Petz recovery map is mathematically equivalent to the gradient update used in maximum-likelihood quantum tomography, establishing a general framework for state estimation.

Method. The authors employ techniques from information geometry and optimization theory, deriving iterative updates (KM update) based on maximizing the log-likelihood functional of measurement data.

Model / system. The analysis is performed on general quantum channels $E$ acting on density matrices $ ho$, applicable to various measurement scenarios including quantum-to-classical measurements.

Key observables. Log-likelihood functional $L_X(\sigma)$, Petz recovery map ($\mathcal{R}_{\sigma, E}(X)$), and the Kubo–Mori (KM) update ($\mathcal{R}_{ ext{KM}}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The degree of non-commutativity between measured observables ($[E(\sigma), X]$) dictates the difference between the Petz map and the KM update.

Assumptions / limitations. Assumes conditions ensuring positive evidence ($X \ge 0$) and that the output state is invertible or well-behaved for gradient calculations.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the schematic process of the KM update, showing how evidence leads to a corrected estimate, and demonstrate convergence towards the Maximum Likelihood Estimator (MLE) in various setups.

Paper structure. The paper builds from defining the MLE problem, deriving the gradient-based KM update, establishing its relationship to the Petz map via non-commutativity terms, and finally proving monotonicity properties for both updates.

Abstract

Quantum tomography and quantum retrodiction are traditionally viewed as separate inference tasks: tomography reconstructs quantum states from measurement data, whereas retrodiction infers past quantum states from observed outcomes. We show that the two are manifestations of the same underlying principle. We prove that the Petz recovery map associated with a measurement channel is precisely the gradient update of the log-likelihood used in maximum-likelihood tomography. Consequently, repeated applications of the Petz map monotonically increase the likelihood. Extending beyond measurement channels, we derive a noncommutative generalization of the Petz map from the gradient of a generalized likelihood for arbitrary quantum channels. The resulting iterative procedure maximizes the likelihood and provides a general framework for quantum tomography, establishing a direct bridge between retrodiction, recovery maps, and statistical inference.

Wavelet Matrix Product States for Quantum Fields

Type: theory · Category: numerical methods · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23823v1

Summary. The paper introduces Wavelet Matrix Product States (wMPS) as a novel, highly accurate variational method for solving continuous Quantum Field Theories. By leveraging the multi-resolution properties of Daubechies wavelets, it allows systematic refinement of approximations without needing explicit lattice discretization. This technique is shown to accurately compute key observables like energy density and correlation functions for models such as the Lieb-Liniger system.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, systematic numerical tool for studying continuum quantum systems that avoids the pitfalls of standard lattice discretization, offering variational bounds on ground state energies relevant to many-body physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is developing a variational method to solve continuum quantum field theories (QFT) by mapping them onto discrete tensor network representations.

Main result. The authors demonstrate that using Wavelet Matrix Product States (wMPS) with Daubechies wavelets allows for systematic, highly accurate approximation of QFT ground states, showing convergence towards Continuous MPS results.

Method. They employ a variational approach utilizing wMPS, which are built on wavelet bases and optimized iteratively through multi-resolution analysis to approximate the continuum Fock space.

Model / system. The method is applied to continuum quantum models like the Lieb-Liniger model of non-relativistic bosons. The Hamiltonian involves kinetic and four-point interaction terms derived from the wavelets.

Key observables. Energy density, two-point correlation functions, and density-density correlators are computed to track convergence.

Important parameters / regimes. The resolution scale ($r$) of the wavelets, the bond dimension ($\chi$), and the Daubechies function order ($N \geq 6$).

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes the Hamiltonian is local and that the state can be accurately approximated by a tensor product structure derived from the wavelet basis; it relies on the inclusion property of the chosen wavelets.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the reduction sequence of the Hilbert space ($\mathcal{H} o \mathcal{H}_r o ext{MPS manifold}$), show energy density convergence vs. resolution $r$, and compare optimization routines with and without iterative refinement.

Paper structure. The paper builds mathematically by establishing the necessary properties for a suitable wavelet basis (Daubechies functions) to ensure variationality and inclusion. It then details the construction of the Hamiltonian in terms of local coefficients ($K, \Gamma_4$) and presents an iterative algorithm involving Inverse Wavelet Transforms ($ ext{IWT}$) followed by $ ext{ITEBD}$ contraction and projection.

Abstract

We introduce a variational method to solve continuum quantum models with discrete tensor network techniques. The method leverages wavelet matrix product states (wMPS): matrix product states built on top of sufficiently regular ($N\geq 6$) Daubechies scaling functions. These states live in the continuum field theory Fock space, have finite energy density, and can be optimized with standard algorithms, without restriction to free theories. Further, exploiting the multi-resolution analysis built into wavelets, and its quantum circuit description, we can iteratively refine wMPS to obtain accurate approximations at arbitrarily fine length-scales. We showcase the efficiency of the method on the Lieb-Liniger model, computing energy density and correlation functions.

Summary. The paper presents a novel design framework to drastically reduce the physical space required for generating essential magic states like CCZ and T states in surface code quantum computation. Why it may be interesting. This work is highly relevant as it directly addresses hardware constraints (physical qubit count) in scaling quantum computers. The techniques developed—especially the Pauli rotation conversion and compact measurement implementation—provide concrete engineering blueprints for building fault-tolerant modules.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is to reduce the physical resource cost, specifically the spatial footprint, required for implementing magic state distillation protocols within a surface code architecture.

Main result. The authors propose a framework that reduces the spatial cost of generating CCZ and T magic states by converting gate-based circuits into compact joint measurement architectures using four-qubit twisted measurements. This results in significant resource savings compared to previous designs.

Method. They convert complex, gate-based distillation protocols (like [[8, 3, 2]] CCZ) into sequences of Pauli rotation joint measurements ($\pi/8$ rotations). Redundant auxiliary qubits are eliminated by exploiting the trivial action of Z-type operators on $|0 angle$ states.

Model / system. The work is situated in the context of fault-tolerant quantum computation using the surface code. Abstract

We propose a design framework for reconstructing gate-based magic state distillation protocols as compact joint-measurement architectures implementable with the surface code. The goal is to reduce the surface-code resource cost of a magic state factory while preserving the logical function and error-detection structure of the distillation protocol. We construct a reduced architecture for implementing an eight-to-three CCZ distillation protocol using smaller surface-code patches. The proposed factory preserves the single-fault-detection property and the leading-order error suppression of the protocol, while producing CCZ magic states with lower spatial cost than the design of Gidney and Fowler. The proposed design perspective can also be applied to T-state factories and other multiqubit non-Clifford resource-state factories. Our approach provides a framework for extending the design space of surface-code magic state factories beyond a single CCZ layout optimization.

Probing the pairing symmetry of moiré graphene superconductors

Summary. This work theoretically distinguishes between singlet and triplet superconducting pairing symmetries in moiré graphene by predicting distinct signatures. The authors leverage symmetry constraints and model both bulk quasiparticle excitations and interface-bound Andreev states. The pairing symmetry of magic-angle moiré graphene is a fundamental question that remains unresolved. Abstract

Two-electron processes can generate high harmonics beyond the conventional single-active-electron cutoff. Motivated by recent experimental evidence of an extended secondary plateau in the helium high-harmonic spectrum [S. Wang et al, Optica, (2023); S. Wang et al, In Print in Nature Photon., (2026)], we present a two-electron generalisation of the strong-field approximation. We analyse the resulting expressions using the saddle-point method and determine the extended cutoff. We find good agreement with classical predictions of cutoff scalings of $4.7$ and $5.5$ times the ponderomotive energy, which significantly exceed the established single-electron scaling of 3.17. We calculate high-harmonic spectra generated via a two-electron process in helium atoms driven by an intense few-cycle infrared laser pulse. Our results demonstrate that the harmonic spectrum extends far beyond the water window, reaching photon energies up to $\approx 1.2\,\mathrm{keV}$ in the soft x-ray region. The large spectral bandwidth can support the generation of sub-attosecond soft x-ray pulses, which are of particular interest for probing ultrafast dynamics across matter, including applications in core-level spectroscopy and biological imaging.

Summary. The paper proves that the fundamental rate of communication capacity in interactive quantum protocols is determined equally by two measures: the Quantum Information Cost and the Amortized Quantum Communication Complexity. This equivalence is achieved using a novel type-constrained de Finetti reduction, which simplifies complex worst-case analysis into manageable i.i.d. bounds.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides deep theoretical tools—the type-constrained de Finetti reduction—that bridge classical probability theory with quantum information bounds, offering a powerful new technique for analyzing resource theories.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to establish an equivalence between two fundamental measures of communication capacity: the prior-free quantum information cost (QIC) and the prior-free amortized quantum communication complexity (AQCC). This addresses a long-standing open question in quantum information theory.

Main result. It proves that for interactive quantum communication protocols with classical inputs, the Quantum Information Cost equals the Amortized Quantum Communication Complexity ($ ext{QIC}(\Pi) = ext{AQCC}(\Pi)$).

Method. The core methodology involves developing and applying a novel type-constrained de Finetti reduction to simplify complex worst-case input analyses into tractable i.i.d. settings.

Model / system. The analysis focuses on quantum interactive communication protocols involving multiple rounds of communication, where the inputs are classical sequences drawn from fixed alphabets. The mathematical framework utilizes density operators and tensor products over Hilbert spaces.

Key observables. Quantum Information Cost ($ ext{QIC}$), Amortized Quantum Communication Complexity ($ ext{AQCC}$), total quantum communication cost ($QCC$), and various conditional mutual information terms.

Important parameters / regimes. The asymptotic limit ($n o \infty$) is crucial, as are the approximation parameters $\delta$ (controlling typicality) and the structure of the classical inputs/types.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the protocols operate with classical inputs drawn from fixed alphabets and relies heavily on bounding errors using $\delta$-typical sets derived from de Finetti's theorem.

It then applies this to bound channel norms and state approximations (Chunks 2 & 7), culminating in simulating the full interactive protocol using these bounds to prove the final equivalence $ ext{QIC}(\Pi) = ext{AQCC}(\Pi)$ (Chunks 3, 5, & 6).

Abstract

For many information processing tasks, de Finetti-style theorems can often simplify the analysis in worst-case input scenarios for which the task exhibits some permutation-invariance symmetry, as they can allow for a reduction from an analysis on worst-case inputs to that of i.i.d. inputs. If further information is available on the inputs, it might be advantageous to reflect this information in the de Finetti reduction. In our work, we focus on a form of such constraint, based on the type of the input. This allows us to obtain a conceptually simple proof of a new de Finetti reduction for classical probability distributions, derived from elementary properties from the method of types. We apply our constrained de Finetti reduction to the compression of quantum interactive communication protocols with classical inputs, and prove that the prior-free quantum information cost equals the worst-case input amortized quantum communication cost.
