$K$-Theoretic Obstructions to Linearizing QCA Representations

Authors: Mattie Ji, Bowen Yang
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.19657v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops an advanced obstruction theory using K-theory to determine when Quantum Cellular Automata (QCA) representations can be linearized into standard linear group actions. By analyzing universal obstruction classes derived from the homotopy type of QCA spaces, it establishes precise mathematical criteria for physical systems modeled by QCA to possess simple, linear dynamics.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a deep mathematical framework connecting quantum computation models (QCA) to fundamental algebraic topology invariants. The explicit calculation of obstruction classes over number fields suggests powerful, non-trivial constraints on physically realizable quantum dynamics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The central problem is developing an obstruction theory to determine when a Quantum Cellular Automata (QCA) representation, which models quantum spin systems with locality constraints, can be linearized into a standard linear group representation.

Main result. The authors establish that the linearizability of QCA representations is governed by the vanishing of specific universal obstruction classes derived from algebraic K-theory spectra. They provide explicit conditions for this vanishing in various settings (e.g., finite groups, rational coefficients).

Method. The methodology heavily employs advanced algebraic topology, specifically utilizing stable homotopy theory, K-theory spectra, and cohomology theories to construct and analyze obstruction cocycles.

Model / system. The model is the QCA representation $\varphi: G o Q(X, q)$, where $G$ is a group acting on quantum spin systems defined over a metric space $X$. The locality constraints are encoded in the structure of the algebra of local observables $A(X, q)$.

Key observables. Universal obstruction classes ($u_i(\varphi)$), stabilization maps ($\varphi_{st}$), and specific cohomology/homotopy groups (e.g., $ ext{H}^k(X; \mathbb{F}_p)$, $\pi_i( ext{K}(C(X)))$).

Important parameters / regimes. The field of coefficients ($\mathbb{F}$, $\mathbb{Q}$), the structure of the metric space $X$ (e.g., point, line, plane, lattice $\mathbb{Z}^d$), and the group $G$.

Assumptions / limitations. The theory relies on establishing equivalences between different notions of linearizability (linearizable vs. stably linearizable) and assuming certain algebraic properties for the underlying spaces $X$ and groups $G$ (e.g., $G$ being rationally acyclic).

Figures summary. Figures illustrate specific examples of QCA ($\alpha$) associated with central simple algebras or annular shifts on $\mathbb{Z}^2$, used to demonstrate non-circuit, non-linearizable cases.

Paper structure. The paper builds from the single-particle case (Theorem 1.1) to generalizing the obstruction theory for QCA representations over arbitrary fields. It systematically defines various notions of linearizability and derives necessary and sufficient conditions using K-theoretic obstructions across different coefficient rings ($\mathbb{C}$, $\mathbb{R}$, finite fields).

Abstract

Projective representations arise naturally in physics and representation theory, and determining whether they can be linearized has been a fundamental problem. In this work, we study the analogous problem for quantum cellular automata (QCA) representations, which incorporate locality constraints imposed by a metric space $X$. Over an arbitrary field $\mathbb{F}$, we develop an obstruction theory for the linearization of QCA representations, using the algebraic $K$-theory spectrum of QCA constructed in previous work of the authors. The resulting obstructions are governed by the homotopy type of the QCA spaces, from which we extract universal obstruction classes to linearization. In the complex algebraic and unitary case, we also fully compute the homotopy types of the QCA spaces over a point, a line, and a plane.

Applications of quantum annealing to magnetic dipole hyperfine structure constants: First results beyond energies for atoms

Authors: Boni Paul, Subimal Deb, Per Jönsson, Jörgen Ekman, Bhanu Pratap Das
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.20166v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. The authors successfully applied a modified Quantum Annealer Eigensolver (QAE) algorithm on D-Wave hardware to calculate magnetic dipole hyperfine structure constants for several light atoms. By benchmarking these results against high-level classical calculations (GRASP), they demonstrated that quantum annealing can accurately determine complex atomic properties, marking an extension of the technique beyond ground-state energy determination.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly tests the capability of quantum annealers to solve complex, physically relevant many-body problems beyond simple energy minimization, providing a crucial benchmark for quantum simulation in atomic physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is to calculate magnetic dipole hyperfine structure (HFS) constants for several light atoms using quantum annealing techniques, extending the application of these methods beyond simple ground-state energy calculations.

Main result. Quantum hardware demonstrations yielded HFS constant results completely consistent with established classical relativistic atomic structure calculations (GRASP) at the specified precision.

Method. The problem is mapped onto a QUBO formulation suitable for quantum annealing, utilizing a modified Quantum Annealer Eigensolver (QAE) approach combined with a zooming-and-sigma-annealing technique.

Model / system. The calculations focus on neutral and alkali-like atoms ($ ext{Li}, ext{Be}, ext{Na}, ext{Mg}$) using the relativistic Dirac-Coulomb Hamiltonian. The methodology involves constructing matrix elements from Configuration State Functions (CSFs).

Key observables. Magnetic dipole HFS constants ($A$) and ground-state eigenvalues/eigenvectors of the $ ext{HDC}$ matrix.

Important parameters / regimes. The QAE precision is limited to three decimal places, corresponding to up to 10 qubits; hardware constraints limit the number of CSFs used (e.g., $B=11$).

Assumptions / limitations. A CSF truncation scheme is employed for larger basis sets, estimating contributions based on approximations derived from the independent electron pair model.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the iterative 'zooming' process used to improve eigenvalue precision during quantum annealing simulations.

Paper structure. The paper systematically presents comparisons of calculated CSF coefficients and HFS constants across multiple atomic species ($ ext{Li}, ext{Be}^+, ext{Na}, ext{Mg}^+$) using three methods: GRASP (reference), Simulated Annealing (SA), and Quantum Annealing (QA).

Abstract

We report the first results of the magnetic dipole hyperfine structure (HFS) constants of neutral $\mathrm{Li}$, Li-like $\mathrm{Be}$, neutral $\mathrm{Na}$, and Na-like $\mathrm{Mg}$ using a modified version of the Quantum Annealer Eigensolver (QAE) algorithm on D-Wave's quantum hardware. The results are benchmarked against relativistic configuration interaction with multiconfiguration Dirac Hartree-Fock (MCDHF) calculations using the General-purpose Relativistic Atomic Structure Package (GRASP), and simulated annealing. In our modified QAE, a zooming-and-sigma-annealing approach with a floating-point encoding scheme is adopted to estimate the ground-state eigenvalue and eigenvector of the relativistic Dirac-Coulomb Hamiltonian matrices ($H_{\mathrm{DC}}$) constructed from 11 or fewer configuration state functions (CSFs). For calculations with extended correlation orbital sets, we applied a CSF truncation scheme, retaining only CSFs (up to 12) that make significant contributions to the ground-state wavefunction. Our modified QAE precision is kept limited to three decimal places (up to 10 qubits). Hardware demonstrations on the D-Wave quantum processing unit (QPU) yielded results that were completely consistent with GRASP (at the chosen precision) in determining the magnetic dipole HFS constants, with accuracy varying across systems and $H_{\mathrm{DC}}$ matrix dimensions.

Exploiting More Than Symmetry in Variational Quantum Machine Learning

Authors: Markus Baumann, Claudia Linnhoff-Popien
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.20316v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. The paper investigates how to optimally constrain Variational Quantum Circuits by leveraging problem symmetries in VQML. By testing on Tic-Tac-Toe, the authors find that while symmetry defines a necessary admissible space, achieving peak performance requires supplementing this constraint with trainable interactions directly targeting known, critical features of the task.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete framework for incorporating domain knowledge (symmetry) into quantum circuit design, which is crucial for building practical, resource-efficient quantum algorithms beyond simple pattern matching.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core challenge is designing Variational Quantum Machine Learning (VQML) models that are not just expressive, but optimally constrained by the underlying symmetries of the problem to improve generalization.

Main result. While enforcing symmetry provides a significant benefit over unconstrained models, the largest performance gains come from adding trainable interactions specifically tailored to known, decisive task motifs, suggesting task knowledge guides effective ansatz design within the admissible symmetric space.

Method. The authors compare different parameter sharing strategies (varying subgroup constraints) and test the impact of adding specific, symmetry-respecting gates (winning-line gates) on a fully enumerable quantum game model.

Model / system. The study uses Variational Quantum Circuits (VQC) applied to the game Tic-Tac-Toe. The input data is encoded using $RX$ rotations, and symmetries are enforced by restricting trainable parameters via parameter sharing based on subgroups of the dihedral group $D_4$.

Key observables. Test accuracy (the primary metric), generalization gap (difference between training and test error), and the number of trainable parameters.

Important parameters / regimes. The imposed symmetry subgroup ($H \subseteq D_4$), the specific interactions added (e.g., ZZZ or CCRZ gates on winning triples), and optimization hyperparameters like learning rate and epochs.

Assumptions / limitations. The study relies on Tic-Tac-Toe being a fully enumerable and structurally transparent test case, allowing for precise identification of task-relevant motifs.

Figures summary. Figure 2 demonstrates that enforcing $D_4$ symmetry improves accuracy over no constraint, and further shows that adding trainable gates corresponding to winning lines provides the largest boost in performance. Figure 3 compares random vs. orbit-based sharing and confirms that parameter count alone does not explain all gains.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the need for structure-aware VQML models by first showing how symmetry constrains the design space. It then systematically investigates two axes of freedom: varying the strength of symmetry enforcement, and adding task-specific interactions on top of the base symmetric ansatz.

Abstract

The success of variational quantum learning models crucially depends on choosing parametrizations that reflect the structure of the problem at hand. Symmetries provide one of the clearest such structures: whenever transformations of the input leave the desired outcome unchanged, this invariance should be built into the model rather than discovered during training. However, imposing a symmetry does not by itself determine a useful ansatz. Even within the symmetry-preserving space, one must decide where the trainable degrees of freedom should be placed. In this work, we study this remaining design freedom in equivariant variational quantum circuits. Building on symmetry-based parameter sharing, we disentangle two architectural choices: how much symmetry should be enforced, and which symmetry-respecting interactions should be trainable. Using Tic-Tac-Toe as a fully enumerable and structurally transparent test case, we find that suitable subgroups preserve most of the generalization benefit. By contrast, the dominant gains arise from gates acting directly on decisive task motifs. Thus, symmetry defines the admissible design space, while effective ansatze require an additional task-informed choice of trainable interactions.

Impossibility of superluminal signalling rules out causal loops in conical spacetimes

Authors: Maarten Grothus, V. Vilasini
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.20476v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical paper investigates the relationship between superluminal signaling constraints (NSS) and causal loops in spacetime. By introducing the concept of 'conicality,' the authors prove that this geometric property forces any compatible causal structure to be non-looping, thereby establishing a deep link between local causality principles and global spacetime geometry.

Why it may be interesting. While not directly related to quantum many-body physics, the rigorous mathematical framework for causality and information flow across curved spacetimes is foundational for theories involving quantum gravity or relativistic quantum field theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The central problem is determining whether the principle of No Superluminal Signalling (NSS) inherently rules out all operationally detectable causal loops in spacetimes that possess a 'conical' geometric structure.

Main result. The authors prove a general no-go result: in any conical spacetime, NSS rules out all operationally verifiable causal loops, establishing that this relationship depends fundamentally on the geometry of spacetime.

Method. The analysis uses an advanced framework combining causal inference (affects relations) with geometric constraints derived from poset theory, specifically leveraging the concept of 'conicality' in the spacetime embedding.

Model / system. The model involves describing causality using directed graphs and affects relations, which are then embedded into a partially ordered set (poset) representing the spacetime light cone structure. The focus is on 'conical' spacetimes, including $d>1$ Minkowski space.

Key observables. Operationally detectable causal loops (ACLs), compatibility conditions derived from affects relations ($ ext{X} \models ext{Y} | ext{do}( ext{Z})$), and the geometric property of conicality.

Important parameters / regimes. The spatial dimension $d$ (comparing $d=1$ vs. $d>1$), and the irreducibility conditions ($ ext{Irred1}, ext{Irred3}$) applied to affects relations.

Assumptions / limitations. The entire proof structure relies heavily on assuming a conical embedding of the causal model into spacetime, as this geometric property is key to deriving contradictions that rule out loops.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates complex examples of ACLs in $(1+1)$-Minkowski space. Figure 2 compares light cone representations for $d=1$ (non-conical) versus $d>1$ (conical), showing how joint futures differ.

Paper structure. The paper develops the framework by first defining affects relations and conical embeddings, then proving necessary lemmas that simplify higher-order conditional affects relations ($ ext{Lemma A.7}$). Finally, it applies these results to prove that any compatible embedding of ACLs in a conical spacetime must be degenerate (Theorem A.9).

Abstract

In PRL 129, 110401 it was shown that it is theoretically possible to have operationally detectable causal loops without violating the principle of no superluminal signalling (NSS) in (1+1)-Minkowski spacetime. Whether or not such causal loops are also possible in $d > 1$ spatial dimensions, has remained a key open question. We resolve this question by showing that in a wide class of "conical" spacetimes, including Minkowski with d > 1, NSS does rule out all operationally detectable causal loops, in classical, quantum and post-quantum theories. This establishes that the relationship between the relativistic principles of NSS and no causal loops depends inherently on the geometry of spacetime.

Norms, overlaps and Yangian descendants for the Haldane--Shastry spin chain

Authors: Yunfeng Jiang, Jules Lamers, Yuan Miao
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.20168v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. The paper tackles the complex problem of characterizing all eigenstates, not just the highest-weight states, within the Yangian symmetry framework of the Haldane–Shastry spin chain. By developing systematic methods using algebraic Bethe ansatz and determinant formulas, it provides explicit formulae for the norms and overlaps of these descendant states. This advances the theoretical understanding of integrable quantum many-body systems with long-range interactions.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides deep spectral information about an integrable quantum many-body system whose structure is governed by infinite symmetries (Yangian), which is crucial for understanding non-equilibrium dynamics in 1D quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is to systematically construct an orthogonal eigenbasis for the Yangian descendant states of the Haldane–Shastry (HS) spin chain and compute their norms and overlaps.

Main result. Product and determinant formulae are derived for the norms and overlaps of these descendant states, establishing a complete understanding of the spectral structure beyond the highest-weight states.

Method. The analysis employs advanced techniques from the Algebraic Bethe Ansatz (ABA), leveraging connections to spin-Calogero–Sutherland systems and utilizing Jack polynomials and determinant formulas (Gaudin/Slavnov).

Model / system. The system is the Haldane–Shastry (HS) spin chain, an integrable model with long-range interactions, which serves as a discrete analogue of a conformal field theory. Its integrability is governed by full Yangian symmetry.

Key observables. Norms and overlaps of descendant states ($\langle u|u angle$, $\langle v|u angle$), energy spectrum $E(\mu)$, and the structure of Yangian multiplets labeled by motifs $\mu$.

Important parameters / regimes. The twist parameter $\kappa$ (analyzed in limits like $\kappa=1$ for $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ symmetry, and $\kappa o \infty$ for the Gelfand–Tsetlin basis).

Assumptions / limitations. The derivation relies on the completeness of the ABA framework and specific mathematical identities related to Jack polynomials.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the structure of Yangian multiplets, showing descendant towers in both the extreme twist (GT) limit and the periodic ($\kappa=1$) limit, analogous to known spin chain structures.

Paper structure. The paper reviews existing knowledge on HS symmetry, details the connection to spin-CS systems via freezing limits, constructs the descendant states using ABA techniques, and concludes by deriving explicit formulas for norms and overlaps.

Abstract

The Haldane-Shastry spin chain is a prototypical integrable model with long-range interactions, notable for hosting quasiparticles with fractional statistics and serving as a discrete analogue of a conformal field theory. Its remarkable simplicity is closely tied to a full Yangian spin symmetry. While the highest-weight states for this symmetry are known explicitly, a systematic treatment of the descendant states, needed for the computation of various physical quantities, has remained incomplete. In this work, we provide a detailed construction of these descendants in terms of the algebraic Bethe ansatz following recent work of Ferrando et al. In the limit of extreme twist, it includes the Gelfand-Tsetlin basis. As an application, we derive product and determinant formulae for norms and overlaps of these states.

Operator Learning for efficient Quantum Computation

Authors: Paul Over, Sergio Bengoechea, Leonardo Borello Busilacchi, Martin Kiffner, Thomas Rung, Alexios A. Michailidis
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.20184v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work proposes a comprehensive variational framework to compile arbitrary classical operators—from those governing quantum dynamics to those from fluid mechanics—into compact quantum circuits. By using backpropagation and block encoding, it overcomes the limitations of standard Trotterization and tensor network methods. This universal approach establishes a powerful programming layer for realizing complex physical simulations on near-term quantum hardware.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a universal, hardware-agnostic compilation layer that bridges continuous mathematical models (PDEs, flow) directly into discrete quantum gate sequences, which is crucial for near-term quantum advantage demonstrations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Efficient quantum algorithm implementation is limited by the lack of efficient primitives for arbitrary operators and state preparation.

Main result. The framework successfully learns compact quantum circuits to approximate complex, general-purpose operators with improved resource scaling over standard Trotter expansions.

Method. A full-stack variational approach uses backpropagation and a cost function to transform arbitrary operators (unitary or non-unitary) into trainable quantum circuits via block encoding.

Model / system. The framework is tested across diverse domains: simulating time evolution in quantum chemistry, solving PDEs using the Laplace operator, and modeling potential flow around an airfoil.

Key observables. Relative error ($\epsilon$), success probability ($p$), objective function value ($J$), and resource scaling (circuit depth/qubits).

Important parameters / regimes. Time step ($\delta t$), system size ($n$ or $n_s$), regularization strength ($ ho$), and ancilla qubit count ($n_a$).

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes the target operator can be approximated by a quantum circuit ansatz, and it utilizes techniques like SVD projection to enforce constraints.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the overall pipeline (discretization $ ightarrow$ encoding $ ightarrow$ hardware), compare error scaling against ST methods for time evolution, and show optimization strategy comparisons for PDEs and fluid dynamics.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem, details the block-encoding methodology for non-unitary operators, presents the hierarchical optimization protocol using automatic differentiation and SVD projection, and validates this approach across quantum simulation, PDE solving, and classical fluid mechanics problems.

Abstract

An efficient implementation of quantum algorithms is often hindered by the lack of efficient primitives for operators and state preparation. This limits both the ability of near-term quantum hardware to simulate complex problems and the potential of fault-tolerant algorithms to achieve practical quantum advantage. To address this, we propose a full-stack variational framework that transforms arbitrary operators to compact quantum circuits. The resulting variational circuits can be tailored to the connectivity and long-range interaction of the target hardware. The learning process employs backpropagation together with a cost function that efficiently optimizes unitary operators and non-unitary -- dense or sparse -- operators using only a single ancilla qubit for block encoding. Additionally, we introduce a regularization term that reduces the approximation error. The approach is validated for both quantum mechanical and engineering applications. In the former case, we learn propagators that arise in native quantum problems -- such as quantum simulation and quantum chemistry -- and achieve improved resource scaling in comparison to standard Suzuki-Trotter expansions. In the latter case, we demonstrate the approach's ability to implement the second-order central finite difference approximation of the Laplace operator -- relevant for solving partial differential equations -- while improving upon current error metrics. The final example deals with learning a dense, non-unitary operator that arises in the analysis of inviscid potential flow around an airfoil. This universality of the framework opens the door for solving general problems beyond prototypical engineering and quantum applications.

Quantum-Accelerated Self-Consistent Field: A Hybrid Algorithm

Authors: Alexis Ralli, Tim Weaving, Thomas M. Bickley, Peter V. Coveney, Peter J. Love
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.20176v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. The paper proposes the Grover Adaptive Search Self-Consistent Field (GAS-SCF) algorithm to solve electronic structure problems by treating the SCF minimization as a QUBO optimization problem. It leverages quantum arithmetic and amplitude amplification to achieve a theoretical quadratic speedup over classical search methods. While demonstrating potential on small systems, its full utility is contingent upon fault-tolerant, large-scale quantum hardware.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly tackles a fundamental problem in quantum chemistry—the SCF calculation—by mapping its optimization structure onto a known quantum speedup primitive (Grover's search), providing a concrete roadmap for near-term quantum simulation algorithms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops a quantum algorithm, GAS-SCF, to accelerate the Self-Consistent Field (SCF) calculation, which is an optimization problem in quantum chemistry.

Main result. GAS-SCF theoretically offers a quadratic speedup for the SCF optimization by using Grover's search mechanism within a hybrid classical/quantum framework. The authors demonstrate its potential on small molecular systems, finding energy improvements over classical benchmarks.

Method. The method combines quantum arithmetic (using QFT) to construct an oracle that marks target Fock states satisfying energy criteria, followed by amplitude amplification via Grover's algorithm. This is wrapped in a classical outer loop that iteratively updates the Molecular Orbital basis set.

Model / system. The model is electronic structure calculation using the Hartree-Fock approximation, formulated as minimizing an expectation value over a Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) problem defined by molecular orbital occupancies. Systems tested include H3-, LiH, and O2/O3.

Key observables. Minimum energy expectation value ($\langle\vec{x}| H_{ ext{QUBO}} |\vec{x} angle$), overlap between true wavefunctions and reference determinants, and the probability of measuring the optimal bitstring as a function of search repetitions ($L$).

Important parameters / regimes. The required number of qubits scales with the size of the system (up to 330 qubits mentioned), and the complexity depends on the structure of the QUBO coefficients.

Assumptions / limitations. Achieving chemically relevant results requires large-scale, fault-tolerant quantum hardware. The analysis relies on classical simulation of the quantum steps and assumes specific circuit constructions for arithmetic operations (e.g., QFT-based addition).

Figures summary. Figures detail the full GAS-SCF algorithm flow, the quantum circuits for implementing necessary components like quantum addition in the Fourier basis, state marking based on energy thresholds, and reflection operators.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the SCF problem mapped to QUBO. It then details the GAS-SCF hybrid algorithm, showing circuit implementations for arithmetic (QFT/Fourier Basis) and constraint enforcement (particle number). Finally, it presents results comparing GAS-SCF performance against classical benchmarks on various molecules.

Abstract

We present the Grover adaptive search self-consistent field (GAS-SCF) algorithm. GAS-SCF leverages quantum arithmetic to construct an efficient oracle that marks target states (Fock states) which improve upon some initial classical energy estimate. Amplitude amplification then increases the probability of measuring these states. This approach offers a theoretical quadratic speed-up for the optimization problem encountered in SCF quantum chemistry and establishes a baseline against which structured optimization algorithms, such as QAOA and DQI may be compared. In this work, we classically simulate three examples as proofs of concept of the algorithm, the largest consisting of 26 qubits. We then extend our analysis to two larger systems, with O3 representing the largest case at 330 qubits. These examples are chosen to probe classically challenging SCF regimes. Achieving chemically relevant applications of GAS-SCF will require large-scale, fault-tolerant quantum hardware.

Sparse Configuration Interaction for the Electronic Schrödinger Equation Revisited: Complete Basis Set Limit Complexity and Quantum-Encoding Impact

Authors: Michael Griebel, Jan Hamaekers
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.20385v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. The paper develops Sparse Configuration Interaction (SCI) methods to solve the electronic Schrödinger equation by rigorously analyzing function spaces using Meyer wavelets. It proves that SCI mitigates the curse of dimensionality in basis set expansions, leading to convergence rates independent of electron number $N$. This breakthrough offers a path toward simulating strongly correlated systems with drastically reduced computational scaling for both classical and quantum computing platforms.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous theoretical framework showing how advanced functional analysis techniques can overcome fundamental exponential scaling barriers in many-body physics simulations, directly impacting the feasibility of simulating complex chemical systems on both classical and quantum hardware.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To develop rigorous, scalable approximation methods for solving the electronic Schrödinger equation by mitigating the curse of dimensionality inherent in full configuration interaction (FCI) expansions.

Main result. The proposed Sparse Configuration Interaction (SCI) method achieves convergence rates to the complete basis set limit that are independent of the number of electrons ($N$), significantly reducing computational scaling compared to conventional methods.

Method. The work combines advanced mathematical tools, including Meyer wavelet analysis on weighted Sobolev spaces defined over the $N$-particle domain, with quantum chemistry techniques like SCI and sparse grid approximations.

Model / system. The system is governed by the electronic Schrödinger equation for interacting electrons in a molecular or atomic environment. The Hamiltonian involves electron-electron repulsion, electron-nucleus attraction, and nuclear repulsion terms.

Key observables. Convergence rate to the Complete Basis Set (CBS) limit; approximation error ($\|\Psi_{ ext{s}} - \Psi_{ ext{BFCI}}\|_{H^1}$); required number of qubits for quantum encoding ($n_{SCI}^q$).

Important parameters / regimes. Number of electrons ($N$); size of the one-particle basis set ($K$ or $L$); smoothness indices ($t, r$); and the degree of approximation (related to grid level $L$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on establishing norm equivalences using Meyer wavelets in mixed-regularity weighted Sobolev spaces; it assumes that the underlying eigenfunctions possess sufficient regularity properties.

Figures summary. Notes reference comparisons showing SCI dramatically reduces determinant space dimensions and required qubits compared to FCI across various molecules (e.g., H2, FeMoco).

Paper structure. The paper builds mathematically by first characterizing function spaces using Meyer wavelets on the $N$-particle domain (Chunks 3 & 4), then applies these tools to derive error bounds for sparse grid approximations (Chunk 7), and finally translates these mathematical findings into computational scaling improvements for both classical solvers and quantum algorithms via SCI (Chunks 1, 2, 5, 6).

Abstract

In this article we revisit regularity results for eigenfunctions in the discrete spectrum of the electronic Schrödinger equation and study their consequences for approximation complexity. In particular, for the convergence to the complete basis set limit, it can be shown that the curse of dimensionality in the leading algebraic exponent can be mitigated. That is, for general sparse grid constructions, the main term of the convergence rate with respect to the number of degrees of freedom is independent of the number of electrons. These insights indicate potential benefits for classical numerical solvers of the electronic Schrödinger equation and also for quantum-computing approaches through new qubit-efficient wavefunction encodings.

Stalls and Spequlation: Pipelined Execution for Fault Tolerant Quantum Computation

Authors: Aditi Awasthi, Gokul Subramanian Ravi, Jonathan Mark Baker
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.19593v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper proposes a pipelined execution framework for fault-tolerant quantum computation by decomposing operations into Control, Execute, and Decode stages. By introducing speculative scheduling strategies, the authors demonstrate that overlapping these sequential steps significantly reduces overall computational time compared to treating operations as atomic units. This work provides crucial architectural insights for building practical, high-throughput quantum processors.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on quantum computation architecture rather than AMO physics directly, the underlying concept of managing sequential dependencies across heterogeneous subsystems—especially when dealing with error correction overheads—is highly relevant to designing complex quantum control systems built from physical platforms like trapped ions or superconducting circuits.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Current scheduling models for fault-tolerant quantum computation treat logical operations as atomic, failing to account for the sequential idle time across classical control, quantum hardware, and error decoding subsystems.

Main result. Implementing a pipelined execution framework with speculation reduces total pipeline steps by 20-40% compared to non-speculative baselines, significantly improving throughput.

Method. The authors decompose logical operations into sequential stages (Control $ ightarrow$ Execute $ ightarrow$ Decode) and apply speculative scheduling techniques analogous to classical pipelining.

Model / system. The model simulates fault-tolerant quantum computation using surface codes/lattice surgery, treating the process as a three-stage pipeline. The system is analyzed by comparing different speculation strategies (Aggressive, Commute-Aware, T-Cautious).

Key observables. Reduction in total pipeline steps (20-40% saving), stage utilization smoothing, and the performance hierarchy among scheduling strategies.

Important parameters / regimes. The ratio of stage durations (assumed 1:1:1 for simplicity) and the frequency/cost associated with speculative rollbacks are key parameters.

Assumptions / limitations. Key assumptions include exploiting algebraic properties (Clifford group commutation) to enable speculation, and initially assuming balanced hardware timing across stages.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the three-stage pipeline structure; Figure 4 quantifies percentage savings for different strategies; Figure 5 shows how speculation smooths out bursty utilization across subsystems.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem of atomic operation modeling, proposes the pipelined framework (Control/Execute/Decode), details speculative scheduling mechanisms, evaluates performance against baselines using various benchmarks, and concludes by discussing load balancing benefits.

Abstract

Fault-tolerant quantum computation requires the coordinated action of three distinct systems: classical control logic, quantum hardware, and classical error decoders. Current scheduling models treat logical operations as atomic, hiding the fact that these subsystems operate sequentially and spend significant time idle. We present a pipelined execution framework that decomposes each logical operation into its component stages i.e. Control, Execute, and Decode. Building on this, we discuss some speculation strategies that allow successor operations to begin processing before their predecessors have completed decoding. We evaluate our framework on several common benchmarks and show that pipelining with speculation reduces total pipeline steps by 20-40% compared to a no-speculation baseline. The most aggressive strategy consistently outperforms conservative alternatives, even though partial rollback is needed at times, because the per-rollback penalty is small relative to the parallelism gained. We further show that speculation facilitates load balancing by distributing work more evenly across the heterogeneous subsystems of a fault-tolerant quantum computer, converting idle time into useful computation while also saving on execution time.

Boltzmann-constrained extraction of spin splitting and momentum relaxation in d-wave altermagnets

Abstract

The scanning tunneling microscope (STM) is a powerful tool for investigating and manipulating molecules on surfaces. We demonstrate with a low-temperature STM operated at 6.2 K the controlled manipulation of ternary clusters of persistent molecular $π$ radicals as a whole. Abstract

We theoretically investigate the optical spin-injection response in different stoichiometric configurations of graphane and fluorographene using density functional theory. Our goal is to determine which configuration yields the strongest degree of spin polarization. The results show that the fluorographene zigzag configuration yields the best degree of spin polarization response (${\cal DSP}^{\mathrm{z}}$), with 98\% spin polarized electrons at the band edge and over a wide range of excitation photon energies. In contrast, other graphane and fluorographene configurations achieve a ${\cal DSP}^{\mathrm{z}}$ of roughly 83--100\%, but only within a limited photon-excitation energy range. Abstract

Recent advances in silicon nitride nanomechanical resonators have pushed mechanical quality factors to ultra-high values by combining stress-induced dissipation dilution with mode-shape engineering. Neither mechanism alters the intrinsic quality factor $Q_{\mathrm{intr}}$. Targeting the intrinsic loss itself therefore remains an untapped route to even higher $Q$. Doing so first requires reliable quantification of $Q_{\mathrm{intr}}$, which has proven challenging. Here we present a robust methodology that quantifies $Q_{\mathrm{intr}}$ by combining automated mode identification with systematic ringdown measurements over a large number of mechanical modes. Applied to high-stress silicon nitride membranes, it reveals a systematic dependence of $Q_{\mathrm{intr}}$ on thickness that cannot be described using established models, particularly in the ultra-thin limit. We account for this trend with a phenomenological model that incorporates a thickness-dependent loss channel. Together, our method and model open a route toward a microscopic understanding of intrinsic dissipation and toward directly mitigating its loss channels.
