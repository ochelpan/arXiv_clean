Binary Optimization of Measurement Groupings for Quantum Energy Estimation

Authors: Isaac L. Huidobro-Meezs, Rodrigo A. Vargas-Hernández
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.10339v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 3/5 · quantum measurements 3/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper tackles the resource bottleneck in quantum energy estimation by optimizing how Pauli observables are grouped for measurement. It formulates this as a binary optimization problem, using MILP for non-overlapping groups and introducing an 'O-clique' method for overlapping supports. The results demonstrate significant, transferable reductions in required measurement repetitions across various quantum Hamiltonians.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses resource optimization in variational quantum algorithms, providing concrete, mathematically rigorous methods (MILP) to reduce the required sampling overhead for estimating ground state energies in quantum chemistry and condensed matter simulations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core problem is minimizing the required number of repeated measurements for accurate quantum energy estimation, as this resource requirement can dominate computational costs.

Main result. Variance-informed optimization techniques, particularly using Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (MILP) for non-overlapping groups, substantially reduce the measurement requirement compared to standard methods.

Method. The authors formulate the measurement grouping problem as a classical binary optimization problem, utilizing MILP for non-overlapping sets and introducing 'O-clique' for optimizing overlapping supports.

Model / system. The methods are benchmarked on molecular and lattice Hamiltonians, including Fermi-Hubbard, Kitaev-Heisenberg-$\Gamma$, and XYZ lattice Hamiltonians, which are represented by Pauli observables.

Key observables. The primary metric is $\epsilon_{2M}(G)$, the required measurement repetitions, derived from minimizing the variance $S(G)$ associated with the Pauli observables.

Important parameters / regimes. The optimization depends on node features ($v_i$, variance) and edge features ($w_{ij}$, covariance) derived from the Pauli terms.

Assumptions / limitations. The MILP formulation is used to linearize the selection process, and the comparison separates gains from measurement-support selection versus coefficient refinement.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the pipeline, detailing the MILP approach for non-overlapping grouping and the O-Clique method for overlapping grouping, showing how weights are adjusted.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theoretical framework by defining the measurement cost function, proposing MILP for non-overlapping groups, extending to O-clique for overlapping groups, and finally benchmarking these optimized groupings across various lattice and molecular Hamiltonians.

Abstract

Repeated measurements can dominate the resources required for quantum energy estimation, making the choice of which Pauli observables to measure together a central optimization problem for variational quantum algorithms. We formulate fully commuting measurement grouping as a classical binary optimization problem based on clique selection and construct non-overlapping groups using mixed-integer linear programming (MILP). For a benchmark of molecular Hamiltonians, groupings optimized with approximate covariances reduce the non-overlapping measurement requirement $\varepsilon^2M$ by $51.8\%$ on average relative to sorted insertion (SI). Using the MILP groups to initialize iterative coefficient splitting (ICS), denoted MILP-ICS, yields an average $24.3\%$ reduction relative to ICS initialized from SI (SI-ICS). The optimized groups also transfer across nearby molecular geometries while preserving substantial measurement savings. We further introduce O-clique, which directly optimizes overlapping commuting supports through candidate-clique selection and coefficient profiles. Although O-clique provides only modest additional reductions beyond MILP-ICS, it reduces the measurement requirement by $27.3\%$ on average relative to SI-ICS with 100 iterations, despite using only five final coefficient refinement iterations, indicating improved support quality. Finally, we extend the comparison to Fermi--Hubbard, Kitaev--Heisenberg--$Γ$, and XYZ lattice Hamiltonians, where MILP-based groupings substantially outperform the corresponding SI-based strategies. Together, these results show that variance-informed optimization of measurement-group structure can substantially reduce sampling costs across molecular and lattice Hamiltonians, with optimized non-overlapping groups providing strong, transferable initializations and direct overlapping optimization offering further gains.

From continuum neural quantum states to effective lattice models: Fluctuating Wigner molecules in moiré superlattices

Authors: Ahmed Khalifa, Shubhayu Chatterjee
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.08955v1

Summary. This work pioneers a method using Neural Quantum States to solve the electronic structure of moiré superlattices, revealing that the true ground state is highly fluctuating and low-spin, contrary to mean-field predictions. By developing a wavefunction readout, the authors successfully bridge the gap between the complex continuum solution and a manageable effective model, specifically a two-orbital Hubbard model.

Why it may be interesting. The methodology provides a powerful, general framework for deriving effective lattice Hamiltonians directly from complex, continuum quantum wavefunctions, which is highly relevant for understanding strongly correlated systems where simple band theory fails.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of developing accurate effective low-energy descriptions for quantum materials when competing energy scales break down the standard energy hierarchy.

Main result. The continuum Neural Quantum State (NQS) approach successfully reveals that the ground state of moiré superlattices is a low-spin state characterized by significant charge fluctuations, which necessitates an effective description via a spinful two-orbital Hubbard model.

Method. The authors use Continuum Neural Quantum States (NQS) to solve the microscopic many-body problem directly, followed by a novel wavefunction readout technique to map the continuum state onto site-resolved charge and spin statistics.

Model / system. The study focuses on moiré superlattices formed by semiconducting transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDs) at a specific electron filling ($ u=3$). The system is governed by a continuum Hamiltonian incorporating kinetic, lattice potential, and interaction terms.

Key observables. Site charges ($Q_A$), site spins ($\langle S_{z_A} angle$), charge fluctuation variance ($ ext{Var}(Q)$), and spin correlation functions ($\langle S_A \cdot S_B angle$).

Important parameters / regimes. Electron filling $ u=3$, moiré potential strength, and the comparison between mean-field ferromagnetic states and the true low-spin ground state.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that the effective low-energy physics can be extracted by analyzing the local statistics (charge/spin) derived from the highly accurate continuum NQS solution.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the coarse-graining process of the NQS wavefunction onto moiré sites, comparing charge and spin distributions between different total spin sectors (e.g., $S_{z_{tot}}=0$ vs. $S_{z_{tot}}=N_e/2$).

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the problem of scale separation breakdown, introduces the NQS method with a readout, applies it to moiré TMDs, and systematically analyzes the resulting charge and spin correlations to derive the effective Hubbard model.

Abstract

Effective descriptions of quantum materials rely on a separation of energy scales that identifies their low-energy degrees of freedom. When kinetic energy, lattice potential, and interactions compete, this hierarchy breaks down, and both the microscopic state and its effective description are unknown. Continuum neural quantum states (NQS) can find the ground state directly without projecting onto prescribed bands, orbitals, or spins, but their microscopic wavefunctions are difficult to interpret in terms of effective variables. Here, we introduce a wavefunction readout that maps the continuum NQS onto site-resolved charge and spin statistics, allowing effective degrees of freedom to emerge from the microscopic solution. Focusing on moiré transition metal dichalcogenides at filling $ν= 3$, the NQS reveals a low-spin ground state below the ferromagnetic crystal of spin-polarized Wigner molecules. Although the charge density retains triangular molecular motifs, our readout reveals significant charge fluctuations through bound nearest-neighbor doublon--hole pairs. Each molecular charge state has an inert spin-singlet core and rim electrons that carry the spin. Charge and spin fluctuations are therefore intertwined, with antiferromagnetic spin-correlations between sites, motivating an effective description by a spinful two-orbital Hubbard model. More generally, our results demonstrate a route from continuum wavefunctions to effective lattice models when competing scales preclude a controlled reduction.

Design-Time Conformance Checking for Pulse-Level Quantum Control

Authors: Rylan Malarchick
Type: experiment · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.10427v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: QC/QI experiment 3/5 · quantum measurements 2/5

Summary. This paper introduces qconform, a formal checker designed to verify if a quantum pulse control program is physically realizable on a quantum device. It achieves this by comparing the program against a versioned capability descriptor, using exact arithmetic to avoid floating-point errors. The evaluation demonstrates that the checker successfully catches discrepancies between different versions of vendor toolchains.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous, formal verification layer for quantum control sequences, which is crucial for moving quantum algorithms from simulation to reliable hardware implementation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core problem is ensuring that a pulse-level quantum control program is physically realizable on a target quantum device, as vendor documentation often fails to capture all operational constraints.

Main result. The proposed checker, qconform, successfully accepted no program that the vendor toolchains refused during extensive differential testing, identifying discrepancies between toolchain versions.

Method. The authors developed qconform, a design-time, offline checker that verifies a pulse program against a versioned capability descriptor using exact rational arithmetic.

Model / system. The work focuses on pulse-level quantum control for superconducting qubits, using vendor toolchains like QICK and Qblox to define the operational constraints of the physical hardware.

Key observables. The checker reports a verdict, the specific rules applied, and a coverage manifest detailing what was not checked.

Important parameters / regimes. Device constraints include minimum pulse length, timing grid, frequency band, and amplitude resolution, all defined using exact rational numbers.

Assumptions / limitations. The checker does not decide the converse (it might reject a program the toolchain accepts), nor does it cover run-time behavior or data-dependent control flow.

Figures summary. The notes mention a differential loop diagram illustrating the flow from program generation to vendor toolchain testing, and tables summarizing differential results and defects found.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem of hardware constraint verification, details the qconform checker and its descriptor format, presents the methodology using differential testing against QICK and Qblox, and concludes with evaluation results and identified limitations.

Abstract

A pulse-level quantum control program is written against a device whose limits are recorded, if at all, in vendor documentation and source code. A program that exceeds them can be refused at compile time. It can also be accepted and silently altered, or compile and then fail at the board. In the last two cases the experiment runs, and the data does not correspond to the program that was written. We present qconform, a checker that decides whether a pulse program is realizable on a device, given a versioned capability descriptor for that device. Every constraint in a descriptor cites the toolchain observation that established it. The checker is design-time, offline, and deterministic, and it uses no floating point. It reports a verdict, the rules it applied, and a coverage manifest that names what it did not check. We evaluate qconform by differential testing against the QICK and Qblox toolchains, on three QICK board configurations and one Qblox cluster. Over 1263 program runs of 969 distinct programs, the checker accepted no program that a toolchain refuses. On both vendors, a descriptor pinned to one toolchain release accepts programs that older releases refuse. The evaluation also found ten defects in the checker and its descriptors, all fixed.

Vertex crossings in a symmetric Markov multinomial model

Authors: Arjun Pemmasani
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.09258v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: non-equilibrium universality 3/5 · Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical paper analyzes the probability distribution of counts in a Markov multinomial model, simulating a ball on a generalized Galton board. It determines the precise conditions under which the most probable outcome shifts from being concentrated at the corners (vertices) of the simplex to the center. The work uses advanced asymptotic analysis to show this transition occurs predictably as the system size increases.

Why it may be interesting. The mathematical machinery used—analyzing phase transitions in high-dimensional probability distributions—is highly relevant to understanding critical phenomena and equilibrium states in complex many-body systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the crossover point in a symmetric Markov multinomial model where the most likely outcome (bin) shifts from being near the vertices (corners) of the simplex to the center.

Main result. For large systems, the most likely bin transitions from the vertices directly to the center as the expected number of turns grows to a fixed multiple of the logarithm of the steps.

Method. The analysis employs advanced asymptotic techniques, including second-order analysis, generating functions, and saddle-point approximations to compare the probability of interior points against those at the vertices.

Model / system. The model is a ball bouncing down a generalized Galton board, modeled as a Markov chain. The observable is the count vector $\mathbf{n}$ of directions used over $N$ steps, which resides on a $d$-dimensional simplex.

Key observables. The probability distribution of the count vector $\mathbf{n}$, the ratio of the probability of an interior bin to the probability of a vertex ($P_{\mu}^N(n)/V_N$), and the crossing point $p_{N,k}$.

Important parameters / regimes. The number of steps $N$, the dimension $d$, the expected number of turns $T$, and the logarithmic scale $L = \log N$.

Assumptions / limitations. The results are asymptotic, valid for large $N$, and the analysis often relies on specific scaling regimes for the parameters (e.g., $T$ growing proportionally to $L$).

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the crossover behavior on a triangle ($d=3$) at $N=24$, showing the ratio $P_N(n)/V_N$ crossing a threshold.

Paper structure. The paper builds from first-order analysis (suggesting simultaneous crossing) to a second-order analysis to break ties, establishing explicit asymptotic formulas for the crossing time $T_{N,k}$ and proving the general transition from vertices to the center.

Abstract

We observe a ball bouncing down a Galton board with any number of directions. At each peg it either keeps its direction with some fixed probability or randomly turns to one of the other directions. Its bin measures how often it went each way, a point of a simplex. When the ball rarely turns, the most likely bins are the corners, which only a ball that never turns can reach. We ask when the best bin of each face of the simplex becomes as likely as a corner. To first order every face catches up at the same moment. We break this tie at second order, with an explicit constant for each face. Hence on a long board, as the expected number of turns grows to any fixed multiple of the length's logarithm, the most likely bin jumps once, from the corners straight to the center. A nonuniform start or a weak external field changes the constants, potentially allowing an intermediate face to win.

Exact solutions for kinematics across interaction-deformed Fermi surfaces

Authors: Alexander Kruchkov, F. Duncan M. Haldane
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.08921v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 3/5 · methods for driven-dissipative 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical work derives exact rate equations governing the redistribution of quasiparticle weight across an interaction-deformed Fermi surface. By tracking the change in particle deficit and surplus populations as interactions are turned on, the authors establish fundamental kinematic identities. These results provide a robust, general framework for studying spectral function evolution in strongly correlated electron systems.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous derivation of these kinematic identities provides a powerful, general framework for understanding how spectral weight and particle distribution evolve in strongly correlated systems, independent of specific approximations like the Luttinger theorem itself.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper derives exact kinematic identities describing how the occupation deficit inside and surplus outside the Fermi surface change when interactions are switched on at fixed particle density.

Main result. It establishes rate equations for these populations, showing that when Luttinger's theorem holds, the rates of change for the deficit and surplus are equal, balancing surface flux with bulk redistribution.

Method. The analysis uses a continuum of interaction parameters ($\lambda$) and applies transport theorems (like the Reynolds theorem) to derive continuity equations for the occupation numbers and the Luttinger integrand.

Model / system. The system is spinless fermions on a lattice in $d$ dimensions at zero temperature, modeled by tracking the evolution of the interacting Green's function and self-energy as an interaction parameter $\lambda$ is varied.

Key observables. Quasiparticle weight ($Z_{\lambda k}$), occupation deficit ($N_1$), quasiparticle surplus ($N_2$), and the Luttinger integrand ($L_{\lambda k}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Interaction switch parameter ($\lambda$), Fermi momentum ($k_F$), and chemical potential ($\mu$).

Assumptions / limitations. The derivations rely on the existence of a quasiparticle pole and the application of transport theorems across moving discontinuities; some results require assumptions related to functional invariance.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the momentum distribution $n_{\lambda k}$ along a line through the Fermi sea, comparing free and interacting fermion cases.

Paper structure. The paper develops the formalism by defining the populations ($N_1, N_2$), deriving the rate equations using transport theorems, establishing conservation laws, and finally relating these rates to the Luttinger integrand to confirm the kinematic identities.

Abstract

Luttinger's theorem fixes the volume of the Fermi sea of a Fermi liquid, but not how interactions redistribute occupation around its surface. For an interaction switched on and tuned at fixed density, we derive the exact rates of change of the occupation deficit inside the interacting Fermi surface and of the surplus outside it. Each is the weight swept by the deforming surface plus a change of Luttinger's integrand at fixed momentum; the difference of the surface terms is flux of incoherent weight $1 - Z_{\vec k}$. These identities hold with or without the Luttinger theorem.

Summary. This paper introduces a Measurement-Efficient Differentiable Quantum Architecture Search (ME-DQAS) framework for optimizing quantum circuits used in combinatorial problems. By exploiting the structural properties of the cost Hamiltonian and circuit, the authors derive a method that significantly reduces the number of required quantum measurements for gradient estimation. This lowers the practical overhead, making DQAS more feasible for current quantum hardware.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the practical hardware limitations of quantum machine learning algorithms by providing a concrete, theoretically derived method to reduce the required quantum measurement budget for gradient estimation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge addressed is the high measurement cost associated with Differentiable Quantum Architecture Search (DQAS) when applying variational quantum algorithms to combinatorial optimization.

Main result. The authors propose a measurement-efficient scheme that reduces the required gradient measurement cost by 39% to 41% compared to standard two-shift estimates on benchmark problems like 3-SAT and MaxCut.

Method. They derive and implement a measurement-saving gradient estimator that exploits the diagonal structure of the cost Hamiltonian and the structure of the quantum circuit suffix to replace standard two-shift rules with one-shift or zero-measurement estimates.

Abstract

Differentiable quantum architecture search (DQAS) is a promising framework for the automated design of quantum circuits, particularly for variational quantum optimization algorithms. However, its practical deployment on quantum hardware is limited by the large number of circuit measurements required during optimization, making hardware execution costly. In this work, we show that for a broad class of combinatorial optimization problems and commonly used rotational gate parameterizations, the measurement cost of DQAS can be significantly reduced without changing the optimization objective. We derive the proposed measurement reduction scheme theoretically and validate it experimentally on 3-SAT and MaxCut benchmark problems. Our approach reduces the requested gradient measurement cost by about 39 to 41% while introducing only negligible classical post-processing overhead, lowering the practical cost of executing DQAS on quantum hardware.

Quantum-vortex excitons beyond band topology

Authors: Jin-Hyung Choi, Sang-Hoon Han, Young-Kwon Han, Jun-Won Rhim, Sun-Woo Kim, Joshua J. P. Thompson

Summary. This theoretical study investigates exciton binding in flat Chern bands using a kagome lattice model. It demonstrates that the Coulomb interaction's phase matching stabilizes quantum-vortex excitons, causing a topological transition as spin-orbit coupling increases. This reordering is predicted to manifest as a distinct change in the material's optical absorption spectrum.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a microscopic link between exciton topology, Coulomb binding energy, and optical response, showing that phase effects beyond simple band Chern numbers dictate the ground state, which is highly relevant for designing novel quantum emitters.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to explain how Coulomb phase matching stabilizes a quantum-vortex exciton as the lowest bound state in flat Chern bands, extending understanding beyond the underlying electronic band Chern numbers.

Main result. Increasing spin-orbit coupling drives a transition of the lowest exciton from a quantum-vortex state to a zero-winding state, which is observable as a transition from a dark vortex exciton to a bright zero-winding exciton in the optical spectrum.

Method. The analysis uses a projected electron-hole formulation, calculating total energy by minimizing the sum of kinetic and Coulomb interaction energies, while isolating the phase contribution to the binding energy.

Model / system. The study uses a prototypical Yin–Yang kagome lattice to model excitons (bound electron-hole pairs) in a setting exhibiting flat Chern bands and spin-orbit coupling.

Key observables. Exciton topology (classified by winding number relative to Chern-number difference), total energy $E(\eta)$, and circularly polarized optical absorption spectra $\epsilon_{\sigma}(E_\gamma)$.

Important parameters / regimes. Spin-orbit coupling ($\lambda_{soc}$), the lattice structure (Yin–Yang kagome), and the exciton wavefunction phase winding.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the Bloch-overlap geometry is relevant for binding, and later sections test the failure of local approximations to capture the full nonlocal momentum-pair contributions to the energy ordering.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the kagome lattice realization, the exciton energy spectrum $E(\eta)$ vs. $\lambda_{soc}$ showing winding sectors, and comparisons between native eigenstates and amplitude-controlled trials showing phase vortex structures.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the physical model and the role of Coulomb phase matching in stabilizing vortex excitons. It then uses topological indices to track competing states and demonstrates the reordering via energy minimization. Finally, it connects this reordering to measurable optical selection rules.

Abstract

Optically exciting a typical low-dimensional semiconductor produces a nodeless \(s\)-type exciton as its lowest-energy bound state. Here, we explain how Coulomb phase matching stabilises a quantum-vortex exciton as the lowest bound state in flat Chern bands. Using a prototypical Yin--Yang kagome lattice, we demonstrate that increasing the spin--orbit coupling drives a transition of the lowest exciton from a quantum-vortex state to a zero-winding state, while leaving the band Chern numbers unchanged. We trace this behaviour to a gauge-invariant combination of exciton phase differences and Bloch-overlap phases that can reduce the Coulomb energy. By comparing states with identical wavefunction amplitudes, we isolate this phase contribution and show that it is sufficient to drive the reversal in exciton ordering. We classify exciton topology by the momentum-space wavefunction winding relative to the conduction--valence Chern-number difference,connecting the competing states to circular-polarisation selection rules. We then show that the resulting reordering can be tracked directly in the optical absorption spectrum, through the transition from a dark vortex exciton to a bright zero-winding exciton. These findings establish a microscopic link between exciton topology, Coulomb binding, and optical response that extends beyond the information contained in the underlying electronic band Chern numbers.

Causal confusion in quantum systems: distinguishing direct cause and common cause

Summary. This paper tackles the problem of quantum causality by defining 'causal confusion'—when measurement data cannot distinguish between a shared common cause and a sequential direct cause. The authors prove that this confusion occurs if and only if the underlying quantum channel possesses the PPT property, providing a new operational meaning for PPT channels.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous operational definition for PPT channels by linking them to a fundamental concept in quantum causality, which is crucial for understanding the limits of quantum information processing and measurement theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates 'causal confusion,' which is the situation where joint expectation values measured in a spatial (common-cause) quantum scenario coincide with those from a temporal (direct-cause) quantum scenario.

Main result. It establishes that for general systems, a quantum channel exhibits causal confusion for some input state if and only if it is a Positive-Partial-Transpose (PPT) channel, and for qubits, this condition is equivalent to the channel being entanglement-breaking.

Method. The authors compare joint expectation values calculated under two distinct causal models—a shared bipartite state (spatial) versus sequential measurement through a quantum channel (temporal)—using Pauli observables.

Model / system. The study uses general finite-dimensional quantum systems and focuses on local measurements performed by two parties (Alice and Bob) on quantum states, analyzing the structure of quantum channels connecting these measurements.

Key observables. Joint expectation values of Pauli observables ($\sigma_j \otimes \sigma_k$), which are used to distinguish between the spatial and temporal correlation structures.

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis depends on the properties of the quantum channel $E$ (e.g., PPT, entanglement-breaking) and the choice of input state $ ho$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is restricted to 'light-touch observables' and uses the Lüders' update rule for the temporal scenario in qubit examples.

Figures summary. The notes mention a Table I summarizing key results relating bipartite states, PPT/CPTP processes, and EB channels to causal confusion, and a $4 imes 4$ table structure for qubits comparing spatial and temporal expectation values.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by defining the spatial and temporal measurement scenarios, demonstrating the conditions for causal confusion using qubit examples, and culminating in general structural theorems linking confusion to PPT and entanglement-breaking properties.

Abstract

The joint expectation values of quantum observables measured by two parties typically depend on the causal structure of the measurement protocol. The two causal structures we investigate here are the direct-cause scenario, in which a single quantum state is measured twice in series with a general quantum channel between measurements, and the common-cause scenario, in which a shared bipartite quantum state is measured in parallel. Through a variety of examples of qubit channels with tunable parameters, we identify the full set of input states where the direct-cause joint expectation values of Pauli observables coincide with those obtained from a common cause, a situation that we call causal confusion. More generally, for arbitrary finite-dimensional quantum systems and light-touch observables, we prove that a channel exhibits causal confusion for some input state if and only if it is a positive-partial-transpose (PPT) channel, thereby endowing PPT channels with a new operational meaning in terms of causal confusion. Using this result, we characterize the set of qubit channels for which there exists an input state leading to causal confusion as the set of entanglement-breaking channels.

Our results suggest a design principle for quantum machine learning algorithms across a wide range of applications, which can be more feasibly tested on state-of-the-art quantum processors or within modular quantum architectures being developed for future hybrid infrastructures interconnected via high-speed communication links.

Abstract

Energy landscapes of non-convex optimization problems are high-dimensional surfaces that are difficult to reveal, visualize, or analyze. Message-passing algorithms, or equivalently cavity approaches in statistical physics, may fail to identify local minima as messages do not converge owing to the ruggedness of the energy landscapes. Here we aim to reveal the characteristics of these complex energy landscapes by limiting the amount of information passed in local messages, improving the convergence of messages for local minima. By studying a routing optimization problem with its convexity governed by a single parameter, we introduce a cavity message-passing algorithm with a trust region, and sample ensembles of converged local minima of rugged energy landscapes across many independent realizations of the same problem instances. We observe three regimes with different characteristics of the energy landscapes: (1) a maximally rugged regime with a peak in the diversity of low-energy minima; (2) an intermediate regime with a hierarchical multi-cluster organization of low-energy solutions; and (3) a smooth regime dominated by a single minimum.
