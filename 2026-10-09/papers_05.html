Trend formation with sparse global sampling

Authors: Sarath Sankar, Abhijit Sinha, Shankar Ghosh, Vikram Tripathi, Amitava Bhattacharya
Type: both · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.10521v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: driven-dissipative phase transition 1/5 · non-equilibrium universality 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work investigates how self-propelled swarms can achieve global order using only sparse, random local information sampling. The authors demonstrate that this sparse sampling is highly efficient, showing that even reducing the sampled subset size can accelerate alignment. The findings establish a theoretical and experimental framework for information-efficient collective coordination.

Why it may be interesting. The concept of using inherent estimation noise (fluctuations) as the driving force for spontaneous symmetry breaking and collective order is a general principle applicable to non-equilibrium statistical physics and complex systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core challenge is achieving global coordination among self-propelled agents without relying on a central controller or dense global communication.

Main result. Sparse, random sampling is shown to be an information-efficient mechanism that reliably drives disordered populations into a globally aligned, nematic state, with noise playing a constructive role in symmetry breaking.

Method. The authors combine analytical derivations of predictive criteria with extensive numerical simulations and physical experiments using differential-drive robots.

Model / system. The model consists of N point particles (or robots) in 2D, where each agent periodically reorients its motion toward the centroid of a small, randomly sampled subset of its peers.

Key observables. Nematic Order Parameter (S(t)), Circular Variance, and Trend Formation Time (t_f).

Important parameters / regimes. Sampling size (n), sampling frequency, and the reorientation threshold ($R_T = \sqrt{N}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The dynamics are modeled as collisionless point-particle motion, and the experimental setup maps this idealized model onto physical robots using non-overlapping offsets.

Figures summary. Figures compare the evolution of alignment (nematic order) and the time required to reach alignment ($t_f$) when using different sampling sizes ($n=2$ vs. $n=[\sqrt{N}]$), demonstrating that smaller samples accelerate ordering.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining the minimal model dynamics, to analyzing the role of sampling size on ordering metrics, deriving analytical criteria, and finally validating these findings through physical robot experiments.

Abstract

Achieving global coordination without a central controller or dense global communication is a defining challenge for both biological collectives and engineered swarms. We introduce and analyze a minimal model in which self-propelled agents in a bounded domain periodically reorient their motions toward the centroid of a small, randomly chosen subset of their peers, with no direct sensing of any individual neighbor's position or heading. We show that this sparse, non-local sampling rule reliably drives an initially disordered population to a globally aligned, nematic state, and that shrinking the sampled subset -- down to the minimum of two agents -- accelerates ordering rather than impeding it: the resulting estimation noise, filtered through a geometric turning rule, is itself the engine of symmetry breaking. We derive an analytical criterion, in quantitative agreement with simulation, that predicts when this ordering succeeds as a function of the sampling size and sampling frequency. We confirm the mechanism experimentally in a swarm of up to fifteen differential-drive robots. These results identify sparse random sampling as an information-efficient route to collective coordination, with implications for understanding animal collectives and for designing communication-limited robotic swarms.

Ambipolar metal-insulator transition in bilayer MoSe2

Authors: Ha-Leem Kim, Hyungbin Lim, Terry Wu, Amogh Y. Waghmare, Garima Gupta, Takashi Taniguchi, Kenji Watanabe, Archana Raja, Eric Y. Ma, Feng Wang
Type: experiment · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.09036v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work reports on the ambipolar metal-insulator transition in bilayer MoSe2 using advanced contact-free radio-frequency reflectometry. By measuring both electron and hole transport, the authors provide strong evidence that the MIT is governed by strong electron-electron interactions, leading to Wigner crystallization. This opens a quantitative transport window for studying correlated states in high-quality 2D semiconductors.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides quantitative transport measurements in a clean, tunable 2D system, offering experimental validation for theoretical models describing strongly correlated phases like Wigner crystals.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the metal-insulator transition (MIT) in two dimensions, specifically resolving the long-standing debate over whether Coulomb interactions or disorder primarily drive this transition.

Main result. The study demonstrates interaction-driven MITs in bilayer MoSe2 for both electrons and holes, evidenced by comparable Wigner-Seitz radii at transition, and maps the transition using contact-free RF reflectometry.

Method. The authors employed contact-free radio-frequency (RF) reflectometry to measure sheet resistance and magnetotransport properties in the bilayer semiconductor.

Model / system. The physical system is bilayer MoSe2, a semiconductor used to study ambipolar transport. The measurements probe the transition between a correlated liquid and a Wigner solid state.

Key observables. Sheet resistance (R) vs. carrier density, Magnetoresistance (MR) peaks, and resonance frequency ($f_0$) shifts.

Important parameters / regimes. Wigner-Seitz radii ($r_s$), critical densities ($n_c$), and the ratio of Coulomb interaction energy to kinetic energy.

Assumptions / limitations. The RF readout is non-perturbative, and the analysis compares results to theoretical benchmarks derived from disorder-free, symmetric gate geometries.

Figures summary. Figures show RF spectra, extracted sheet resistance vs. density (for both electron and hole doping), and magnetotransport ratios mapping the MIT.

Paper structure. The paper systematically presents the experimental setup, characterizes the ambipolar MIT using RF measurements, analyzes the temperature and magnetic field dependence of the transition, and compares findings to theoretical models of correlated electron systems.

Abstract

When Coulomb interactions dominate kinetic energy, dilute two-dimensional carriers form a Wigner solid. Both its melting and disorder-driven localization can drive density-tuned metal-insulator transitions, but their relative roles and interplay have been debated for decades. Atomically thin semiconductors offer a new test because transport and scanning tunneling microscopy can probe the same material, yet poor contacts impede transport where correlations are strongest. Here we introduce contact-free radio-frequency reflectometry that senses sheet resistance through capacitive gate coupling. It resolves the electron and previously inaccessible hole transitions in the same bilayer MoSe2 device, whose carriers share disorder but not mass. Their critical densities differ fourfold yet correspond to comparable Coulomb-to-kinetic-energy ratios, where tunneling microscopy finds solid-liquid coexistence. Magnetoresistance peaks sharply at each transition, reflecting a Pomeranchuk-like, field-driven expansion of the Wigner solid against the correlated liquid. Our results demonstrate interaction-driven transitions in high-quality two-dimensional semiconductors, opening quantitative transport in contact-limited systems.

Efficient Estimation of Logical Sensitivities Through Fault-Counting

Authors: Winston Fu, J. Wilson Staples, Jeff D. Thompson
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.10531v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: methods for driven-dissipative 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces a differentiable estimator to overcome the shot-budget limitations of traditional finite difference methods when calculating logical sensitivities in quantum error correction. By estimating the full gradient from a single simulation run, it drastically reduces the required computational resources. This efficiency allows researchers to accurately map out critical performance boundaries, such as noise thresholds, in complex, multi-parameter noise models.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, statistically efficient tool for characterizing the robustness of quantum codes, which is crucial for designing fault-tolerant quantum computers by precisely mapping out the boundaries of reliable operation in complex noise landscapes.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the need for an efficient method to estimate logical sensitivities ($ u_i = \partial p_L / \partial p_i$), which quantify how the logical error rate ($p_L$) responds to individual physical noise parameters ($p_i$) in quantum error correction.

Main result. A novel differentiable estimator can calculate all components of the gradient $ abla_{\mathbf p}p_L$ simultaneously from a single Monte Carlo data set, achieving a significant reduction (one to two orders of magnitude) in required simulation shots compared to standard finite difference methods.

Method. The core method is a differentiable estimator that leverages information about underlying fault configurations to estimate partial derivatives of the logical error rate. This is combined with Newton root finding to trace threshold contours in multi-dimensional noise models.

Model / system. The primary model is quantum error-correcting circuits, specifically using the surface code subjected to circuit-level noise. The analysis is performed on the logical error rate $p_L$ as a function of various physical noise parameters $p_i$.

Key observables. Logical Error Rate ($p_L$), Logical Sensitivities ($ u_i = \partial p_L / \partial p_i$), Threshold Contours ($\Delta(p) = 0$).

Important parameters / regimes. Physical noise parameters ($p_i$), circuit distance ($d$), shot budget ($N$), and the step size ($h$) used in finite difference methods.

Assumptions / limitations. The differentiable estimator relies on accessing underlying fault configurations in stabilizer tableau simulations. The analysis often uses linear approximations for small error rates and assumes the ability to use local sensitivity information to guide root finding.

Figures summary. Figures compare the variance and estimates of sensitivities ($ u_i$) between the proposed differentiable method and standard finite differences. Other figures illustrate the tracing of threshold contours in multi-parameter noise spaces.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the problem of sensitivity estimation, contrasts the standard finite difference approach with the proposed differentiable estimator, demonstrates the superior variance scaling of the new method, and finally applies this technique to locate and trace threshold contours in complex noise models using Newton root finding.

Abstract

Quantum error-correcting circuits are affected by multiple physical noise mechanisms, whose contributions to logical failure must be understood to evaluate code performance and guide improvements in hardware. The individual error budget contributions for an error type $i$ can be characterized by its logical sensitivity $ν_i = \frac{\partial p_L}{\partial p_i}$, which measures the response of the logical error rate $p_L$ to each physical noise parameter $p_i$. Normally, $ν_i$ is measured using linear fits such as finite differences, where $p_L$ is measured at two or more values of $p_i$ to calculate partial derivatives. In this paper, we develop a differentiable estimator to obtain all components of $\nabla_{\mathbf p}p_L$ simultaneously from a single Monte Carlo data set at one noise configuration, using information about the underlying fault configurations. In surface code simulations with circuit-level noise, the estimator agrees with conventional finite differences while requiring one to two orders of magnitude fewer shots to achieve the same variance. We apply this technique to quantify error budgets, resolve sensitivities at the individual qubit level, and infer effective code distance. Finally, we incorporate the sensitivities into Newton root finding to locate and trace threshold contours in multidimensional noise models. These results provide an efficient method for extracting and applying logical sensitivity information from standard quantum error correction simulations.

Efficiently computable bounds on the energy-constrained quantum reading capacity

Authors: Vishal Singh, Zixin Huang, Mark M. Wilde
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.08945v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work establishes theoretical upper and lower bounds for the energy-constrained quantum reading capacity. It tackles the problem by utilizing advanced quantum information measures like relative entropies within semidefinite programming frameworks. The results provide a quantitative understanding of how energy limitations restrict the information rate achievable in quantum communication channels.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous derivation of capacity bounds under physical constraints like energy is crucial for designing realistic quantum communication protocols, bridging abstract theory with physical implementability.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper determines the energy-constrained quantum reading capacity of a finite family of quantum channels.

Main result. Upper and lower bounds for this capacity are derived using advanced information-theoretic tools, showing how the capacity relates to channel relative entropies under an energy constraint.

Method. The authors use semidefinite programming (SDP) techniques, involving approximations of relative entropies (Belavkin-Staszewski and Umegaki) and alternating optimization to establish rigorous bounds.

Model / system. The model is quantum reading, where classical messages are encoded into sequences of quantum channels, and the reader probes these channels while respecting an average energy constraint on the probes.

Key observables. Quantum reading capacity ($R_{H,E}(N)$), Holevo information, Belavkin–Staszewski relative entropy, and Umegaki relative entropy.

Important parameters / regimes. The average probing energy constraint ($E$), the set of quantum channels ($\{N_x\}$), and the number of uses ($n$).

Assumptions / limitations. The upper bound relies on SDP approximations using Gauss-Legendre quadrature, and the lower bound is derived via bilinear programming, both of which involve approximations.

Figures summary. Figure 1 compares upper and lower bounds for unconstrained capacity for unitary channels, showing overlap. Figure 2 plots upper and lower bounds for loss-dephasing channels against the average photon number.

Paper structure. The paper develops the converse bound using relative entropy techniques, establishes the upper bound via SDP (Appendix A), and derives the lower bound using bilinear programming (Appendix B), culminating in comparisons and numerical examples.

Abstract

In quantum reading, classical messages are encoded in sequences of quantum channels and recovered by probing the channels and processing their outputs. We study the reading capacity of a finite family of finite-dimensional channels under an average constraint on the probing energy, allowing arbitrary adaptive operations between channel uses. Using an input-dependent chain rule for the Belavkin-Staszewski relative entropy, we derive a converse bound expressed as an optimization involving channel Choi operators and the operator relative entropy. This formulation admits semidefinite approximations and reduces, without an energy constraint, to a channel information radius. To obtain achievable rates, we construct bilinear semidefinite lower approximations to a standard non-adaptive reading bound. Alternating optimization produces feasible probe states and encoding distributions, whose Holevo information gives a directly evaluable achievable rate. For jointly classical-quantum channels, we derive a separate converse based on the Umegaki relative entropy. Without an energy constraint, this converse matches a non-adaptive achievable rate, recovering a recent result of Pascual Abraldes and Winter: non-adaptive protocols suffice to achieve the unconstrained reading capacity of jointly classical-quantum channels.

Electronic Properties of Ladder-Type Phenylenes Studied by Thermally-Assisted-Occupation Density Functional Theory

Authors: Tsu-Hsuan Wang, Jeng-Da Chai

Summary. This work applies TAO-DFT to study ladder-type phenylenes, revealing that their electronic properties are not monotonic but exhibit a distinct damped periodic oscillation with a period of three. This periodicity dictates a transition between closed-shell and polyradical states, offering fundamental guidelines for the rational design of these carbon-based nanomaterials.

Why it may be interesting. The study provides a detailed, quantitative understanding of how structural periodicity dictates electronic correlation in extended $\pi$-systems, which is crucial for designing next-generation organic electronic materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Accurately predicting the electronic properties of extended pi-conjugated systems containing radical character, which is a major theoretical challenge due to strong static correlation effects.

Main result. The electronic properties of ladder-type phenylenes exhibit a unique damped periodic oscillation, with highly correlated hotspots appearing at intervals $n = 3k - 1$. This periodicity governs the transition from nonradical to polyradical character.

Method. The study employs Thermally-Assisted-Occupation Density Functional Theory (TAO-DFT) to efficiently capture static correlation effects in these large systems.

Model / system. The physical system is ladder-type phenylenes ($n$-LPs), which are extended $\pi$-conjugated systems formed by $n$ fused four-membered carbon rings, studied for $n=1$ to $40$.

Key observables. Singlet-Triplet Energy Gap (EST), radical character, Symmetrized von Neumann Entropy (SvN), Vertical Ionization Potential ($I_v$), Electron Affinity ($E_a$), and Fundamental Gap ($E_g$).

Important parameters / regimes. The periodicity of 3 in the electronic structure, and the specific structural localization of active orbitals along the central ladder framework.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary approximation is the use of TAO-DFT, which is assumed to accurately model static correlation effects, overcoming limitations of standard KS-DFT.

Figures summary. Figures systematically present calculated properties (gap, IP, EA, etc.) and real-space orbital distributions for small $n$ (up to $n=5$), demonstrating the evolution of electronic structure.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the electronic structure evolution of $n$-LPs using TAO-DFT, focusing on how key observables like the S-T gap and radical character oscillate with chain length $n$, contrasting this periodicity with monotonic trends seen in other nanographenes.

Abstract

Accurate prediction of the electronic properties of extended $π$-conjugated systems with radical character remains a major theoretical challenge. To overcome this challenge, thermally-assisted-occupation density functional theory (TAO-DFT) has been developed in recent years. In this study, we employ TAO-DFT to systematically explore the electronic properties of ladder-type phenylenes ($n$-LPs), containing $n$ fused four-membered carbon rings. Our calculations reveal that all investigated $n$-LPs (with $n$ = 1 ~ 40) possess singlet ground states. With increasing $n$-LP size, we observe a transition from the nonradical character of smaller $n$-LPs to the polyradical character of larger $n$-LPs. Notably, we identify a distinct damped periodic oscillation in the radical character of $n$-LPs, featuring highly correlated hotspots at specific intervals ($n = 3k - 1$, where $k$ are positive integers). The real-space representation of active orbitals confirms this global electronic behavior is governed by the structural localization of active orbitals along the central ladder framework. These findings provide fundamental insights into the size-dependent electronic properties of $n$-LPs, guiding their rational design for future nanomaterial applications.

Finite-size scaling analysis of three dimensional Z(2) and O(2) spin models with non-vanishing symmetry breaking parameter

Authors: Jishnu Goswami, Frithjof Karsch, Sabarnya Mitra
Type: both · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.10350v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

Summary. This work performs a detailed finite-size scaling analysis on 3D Z(2) and O(2) spin models in an external field. By determining the leading finite-size correction term, the authors provide a robust method to eliminate systematic errors in calculating critical parameters. This methodology is particularly valuable for studying quantum phase transitions, such as those found in QCD.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical statistical mechanics models, the rigorous application of finite-size scaling techniques and the connection to QCD phase transitions provide valuable methodological insights applicable to quantum critical points in many-body quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to determine the leading finite-size scaling corrections to universal functions for 3D Z(2) and O(2) spin models when an external symmetry breaking field is present.

Main result. The leading finite-size correction is found to be proportional to $e_{z_L}^2 \sim 1/(H L^{1/ u_c})^2$, allowing for the precise elimination of systematic errors in critical temperature determinations, such as in (2+1)-flavor QCD.

Method. The study employs detailed finite-size scaling (FSS) analysis using high statistics Monte Carlo simulations, fitting scaled observables to scaling ansatze derived from the Renormalization Group approach.

Model / system. The physical system is the 3-dimensional Z(2) and O(2) spin models, studied under the influence of a non-vanishing external magnetic field H. The analysis uses concepts from lattice field theory and critical phenomena.

Key observables. The primary observables are the singular parts of thermodynamic quantities, including the order parameter (M) and susceptibility ($\chi_M$), analyzed via scaling functions $f_{\chi}(z, z_L)$ and $f_G(z, z_L)$.

Important parameters / regimes. Key parameters include the external field H, lattice size L, and the scaling variable $e_{z_L} = 1/(H L^{1/ u_c})$. Critical exponents ($\delta, eta, u_c$) are also determined.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the leading order finite-size correction is controlled by the parameter $e_{z_L}^2$, and it compares results across different scaling variables to estimate systematic uncertainties.

Figures summary. Figure 1 shows scaled observables ($\chi_M/M$, etc.) plotted against $e_{z_L}$ and $e_{z_L}^2$ for both Z(2) and O(2) models. Figure 6 compares the free energy density $f_G$ for both models at zero and non-zero fields. Figure 7 shows the ratio $H\chi M/M$ versus the coupling $z$ for the Z(2) model.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the scaling functions for the spin models, first establishing the appropriate scaling variable ($e_{z_L}^2$), then fitting data to extract leading and next-to-leading order coefficients, and finally applying these results to fix systematic errors in physical observables like the chiral transition temperature.

Abstract

We present results from a detailed finite-size scaling analysis of the $3$-$d$, $Z(2)$ and $O(2)$ spin models in an external field $H$. Using high statistics Monte Carlo data, we obtain the leading finite-size scaling correction to the infinite volume scaling functions. We show that these corrections are proportional to $\widetilde{z}_L^2=1/(H L^{3/(1+1/δ))})^{2}$. This provides the parametric form for finite-size corrections to bulk thermodynamic observables as well as the pseudo-critical temperatures determined at non-vanishing $H$. In particular, it allows to eliminate systematic errors in the analysis of the chiral phase transition temperature in (2+1)-flavor QCD, that arose from the previously not well-controlled ansatz for infinite volume extrapolations. We also establish the validity range of this leading order correction and point out that there are significant differences between the $3$-$d$, $O(2)$ and $Z(2)$ universality classes.

Inequivalent Quantum Resources from Multipartite State Discrimination

Summary. This work analyzes the resource cost associated with distinguishing orthogonal multipartite quantum states using LOCC. It proves that the required quantum resource is highly dependent on the geometric separation (Hamming distance) between the states. The results establish a clear operational asymmetry, showing that non-zero distance allows for entanglement-preserving discrimination using minimal auxiliary resources.

Why it may be interesting. The findings provide a fine-grained understanding of multipartite nonlocality by quantifying how geometric properties (Hamming distance) dictate the minimum quantum resources needed for state discrimination, which is crucial for quantum communication protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the operational resource cost required for locally distinguishing between orthogonal multipartite quantum states, specifically addressing whether this cost is state-dependent.

Main result. The resource cost of LOCC discrimination is state-dependent, showing that two orthogonal GHZ states with nonzero relative Hamming distance can be distinguished using only a single ancillary Bell pair, while zero-distance pairs require no net quantum resource extraction.

Method. The authors employ a resource theory framework based on Local Operations and Classical Communication (LOCC), utilizing entanglement monotones and the Hamming distance to characterize distinguishability.

Model / system. The focus is on multipartite quantum systems, particularly $n$-qubit Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger (GHZ) states, and the analysis extends to larger ensembles of such states.

Key observables. Quantum resources (e.g., entanglement), operational cost of LOCC discrimination, and the relative Hamming distance ($d_H$) between the states.

Important parameters / regimes. The Hamming distance ($d_H$) between the states dictates the required auxiliary resource; the number of qubits ($n$) and the size of the ensemble ($k$) are key parameters.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis often assumes the ability to perform non-destructive discrimination, which requires the preservation of the target state's intrinsic multipartite entanglement.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the quantum circuit used for nondestructive GHZ-state discrimination, involving an ancillary Bell state shared between two parties and subsequent measurements.

Paper structure. The paper builds from the bipartite case to the general multipartite case, establishing theorems regarding distinguishability based on the Hamming distance. It contrasts resource consumption for zero vs. non-zero distance pairs, culminating in protocols for entanglement-preserving discrimination.

Abstract

Any two orthogonal quantum states can be perfectly distinguished via local operations and classical communication (LOCC), yet whether all orthogonal multipartite pairs incur an identical operational cost remains an open question. Here, we address the resource consumption required for local state discrimination across multipartite pair of states. We demonstrate that distinct pairs of orthogonal multipartite maximally entangled states require unequal amounts of quantum resources to achieve local distinguishability. Extending our framework beyond individual pairs, we analyze larger sets of both locally distinguishable and indistinguishable multipartite orthogonal states, revealing a nontrivial hierarchy in their entanglement consumption. These results show that the resource cost of LOCC discrimination is state-dependent, providing insight into the fine-grained structure of multipartite nonlocality.

Mathematical statistics of wild mammal biomass

Authors: Klaus M. Frahm, Dima L. Shepelyansky, José Lages
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.09744v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: non-equilibrium universality 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper applies advanced statistical mechanics tools, particularly the Wealth Thermalization Hypothesis, to analyze the biomass distribution of wild mammals. It finds the distribution exhibits extreme inequality, which is accurately modeled by an extended Rayleigh-Jeans spectral distribution. This suggests that the concentration of biomass among large species follows a universal statistical law, analogous to wealth concentration in human societies.

Why it may be interesting. While the subject is biology/economics, the methodology relies heavily on applying concepts from statistical mechanics (RJ distribution, thermalization hypotheses) to model complex, non-equilibrium distributions, which is a general theoretical tool.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper statistically analyzes the global biomass distribution of wild terrestrial mammals, drawing parallels with tools used to characterize wealth inequality.

Main result. The biomass distribution exhibits extreme inequality, which is successfully modeled by an extended Rayleigh-Jeans (RJ) spectral model, suggesting a universal thermalization principle.

Method. The analysis employs economic tools like the Lorenz curve and Gini coefficient, applying concepts from statistical mechanics, specifically the Wealth Thermalization Hypothesis (WTH) using RJ distributions.

Model / system. The system is the biomass distribution of 4795 wild mammal species. The model uses the RJ steady-state distribution, comparing the data to theoretical curves derived from both RJS and RJE spectral models.

Key observables. Gini coefficient (G=0.944), Lorenz curve, and Pareto curve describing biomass concentration.

Important parameters / regimes. The model parameters include the temperature T and chemical potential $\mu$, leading to optimized parameters like $\epsilon_{ ext{RJE}} = 7.57 imes 10^{-4}$.

Assumptions / limitations. The core assumption is the analogy between biomass/energy and wealth/energy, suggesting that the observed distribution is a robust, mechanism-independent thermalization outcome.

Figures summary. Figures compare the real Lorenz curve against theoretical curves derived from RJS and RJE models, demonstrating the fit across multiple orders of magnitude of biomass variation.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the statistical analysis using economic tools, then details the WTH framework and the RJ modeling, compares the theoretical models to the real mammal data, and finally discusses the implications for ecological mechanisms.

Abstract

Using the recently published global census of the biomass of wild terrestrial mammals, we perform a detailed mathematical statistical analysis of its distribution over $N_s=4795$ species, drawing on tools developed in economics to characterize wealth inequality. We show that the Lorenz curve of the mammal biomass distribution is characterized by a large Gini coefficient $G=0.944$ exceeding the inequality reported for wealth distribution among world countries. This distribution is compared to the predictions of the Wealth Thermalization Hypothesis (WTH), in which species biomass values are treated as energy levels populated according to a Rayleigh-Jeans (RJ) steady-state distribution. We show that an RJ extended spectral model reproduces the real Lorenz and Pareto curves over four orders of magnitude of biomass variation, capturing the strong condensation of biomass among the rare, heavy-bodied species and its near-absence among the vast majority of light-bodied ones. These results extend the WTH framework, previously validated on distributions of human economic origin, to a biological distribution shaped by ecological and evolutionary constraints, and place the extreme rarity of large-bodied mammal species within the same statistical mathematical description as the oligarchic concentration of wealth in human societies. We finally discuss possible ecological mechanisms that may contribute to the observed distribution, including interspecific interactions, food availability, and competition.

Abstract

In this paper, we investigate the use of mixed quantum states in Rabin oblivious transfer, where a sender holds one bit and a receiver learns its value with a certain probability while the sender remains ignorant of whether the receiver learned it. Applying this framework, we recover the $\mathbb{Z}_{2m}$ parafermion zero mode structure of fermionic Laughlin FQH-SCs at filling $ν=1/m$, obtain a $\mathbb{Z}_2^{(n)}\times\mathbb{Z}_N$ structure for generalized Moore-Read states at filling $ν=1/m$, with $N=2m$ for fermionic states and $N=m$ for bosonic states, and find a $\mathrm{Fib}\times\mathbb{Z}_N$ structure for the $k=3$ Read-Rezayi state at filling $ν=3/(3M+2)$, with $N=2(3M+2)$ for fermionic states and $N=(3M+2)/2$ for bosonic states. In the Read-Rezayi case, zero mode operator tunneling and direct zero mode anyon transfer yield distinct universal amplitude ratios between the neutral fusion channels, providing a characteristic signature of the underlying Fibonacci defect structure.

Abstract

Standard quantum mechanics takes the complex numbers as its scalar algebra. We ask whether this complex structure is algebraically fundamental or may instead form a distinguished embedded sector of a larger commutative and associative scalar algebra. Requiring the ordinary complex sector to remain intact while admitting an additional stable complex-like analytic sector turns the latter into a proper ideal, makes zero divisors unavoidable in finite dimension, and implies a lower bound of four real dimensions. The bicomplex algebra realizes this minimum, and under an additional compatibility assumption the four-dimensional realization is unique up to isomorphism. The resulting algebra exhibits two distinct decompositions: the canonical decomposition into orthogonal idempotent ideals and a decomposition adapted to the embedded complex sector and the additional ideal-valued sector. The ordinary complex scalars therefore lie diagonally across the canonical idempotent decomposition. The ideal sector supports Taylor series, oscillatory exponentials, Fourier-type kernels, and Schrödinger-type expressions, and carries a formal ideal-valued eigenvalue structure. Restricting the Born rule to the embedded complex sector recovers its standard form. Whether this dual scalar architecture has physical significance beyond the algebraic framework remains an open question.

Summary. This paper introduces an automated technique to simplify fault-tolerant quantum circuits by performing fault-equivalent rewrites. Abstract

We present an automated method for reducing fault-tolerant circuits through fault-equivalent rewrites. Starting from a known fault-tolerant circuit, the search applies non-reducing enabling rules to expose Bell-pair reductions, each of which removes an ancilla preparation and a CNOT gate. Because every search transition preserves fault equivalence, the resulting circuits inherit the fault-tolerance properties of the input circuit. Candidate circuits are evaluated using circuit-level Monte Carlo simulation. For Shor-style syndrome extraction with the $[[7,1,3]]$ code, our method reduces one syndrome-measurement round from 30 to 18 ancilla preparations and from 54 to 42 CNOT gates. At a physical two-qubit error rate of $p = 10^{-3}$, the optimized circuit lowers the logical error rate by approximately 21% for both logical basis states. The reduction ranges from 13% to 23% over a range of $p$ spanning two orders of magnitude. We also apply the method to Steane-based dynamic syndrome extraction constructed from Goto's verified logical-$\lvert 0 \rangle$ preparation, in which the verification qubit serves as a flag. The search selects a circuit using four ancillas and 14 CNOT gates, matching the resource counts of the previous design but with an improved CNOT depth. Under depolarizing idle noise at $3p/10$ the selected circuit lowers the logical error rate by approximately 15% for both logical basis states, and by approximately $10%$ at $p/10$. These results demonstrate that automated fault-equivalent rewriting can identify circuits with lower resource costs or improved logical performance without separately verifying the fault tolerance of every candidate.
