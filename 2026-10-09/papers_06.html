Benchmarking Modular Optimization Strategies for Parameterized Quantum Circuits

Authors: Carla Cotea, Stefan Balauca, Andreea Arusoaie
Type: both · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.10254v1

Summary. This paper introduces a modular benchmarking framework to systematically test optimization strategies for parameterized quantum circuits. By decoupling gradient estimation from parameter updates, the authors evaluate performance across diverse tasks like chemistry and machine learning. The work is vital for understanding how resource constraints and algorithmic choices affect practical quantum computation.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a rigorous, resource-aware framework for comparing optimization algorithms in the NISQ era, which is crucial for developing practical quantum machine learning and simulation protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Training parameterized quantum circuits (PQCs) is challenging due to the need to balance optimization progress against limited evaluation budgets and the inherent statistical uncertainty of near-term quantum hardware.

Main result. The study benchmarks various optimization strategies, showing that performance is highly dependent on the specific workload, resource constraints, and the modular separation of gradient estimation from parameter updates.

Method. The authors developed a modular benchmarking framework that independently evaluates quantum search-direction estimators (e.g., OCP, finite-difference) and classical parameter-update rules (e.g., Adam, SGD).

Model / system. The benchmarking is applied across four distinct quantum workloads: QAOA for MaxCut, QCNN for Iris/MNIST classification, and VQE for molecular hydrogen ($ ext{H}_2$).

Key observables. Solution quality (e.g., Test Accuracy, Energy Error), and resource costs (Objective Evaluations, Logical Circuits, Shots).

Important parameters / regimes. Finite-shot simulation (256 shots/circuit), 156-qubit processor, and various hyperparameter settings for optimizers.

Assumptions / limitations. The hardware runs are presented as targeted case studies and do not establish a universal optimizer ranking or isolate the causal effect of device noise.

Figures summary. Figures provide an overview of the modular optimization loop across the four workloads, comparing solution quality against quantum-evaluation cost.

Paper structure. The paper reviews background, defines component-level comparisons, details the specific workloads/protocols, presents results comparing various optimizers, and concludes with a discussion of limitations.

Abstract

Training parameterized quantum circuits requires balancing optimization progress with limited evaluation budgets and the statistical uncertainty of near-term quantum hardware. We present a modular benchmarking framework that separates quantum search-direction estimation from classical parameter-update rules, allowing their interactions and sensitivities to be examined independently. We evaluate a suite of gradient-based, stochastic, and derivative-free optimizers across combinatorial optimization using the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA), supervised quantum machine learning using Iris classification and a quantum convolutional neural network (QCNN) for binary MNIST classification, and quantum chemistry using the variational quantum eigensolver (VQE) for molecular hydrogen. By separating objective evaluations from sample-circuit costs under finite-shot simulation and physical execution on a 156-qubit processor, we compare optimizer behavior across workloads with different parameter counts and measurement requirements. Four selected simulator and hardware case studies report terminal and best observed objectives separately, alongside post-training classifier accuracies. The comparisons include selected MaxCut and hydrogen trajectories and illustrate workload-dependent objective changes and evaluation costs, while the simulator benchmark compares both peak and mean performance across three seeds. The hardware runs do not establish an optimizer ranking or isolate a causal effect of device noise.

Building codes with transversal CCZ using projective geometry and SAT solvers

Summary. This paper develops a method to construct quantum error-correcting codes capable of implementing a non-Clifford CCZ gate using physical T/T⁽ gates. By combining projective geometry with SAT solving, the authors build codes of increasing length while proving a lower bound on the minimum required code length. This advances the theoretical understanding of fault-tolerant quantum computation.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the feasibility of implementing universal quantum gates (like CCZ) in fault-tolerant quantum computation using specific hardware constraints (transversal gates), which is central to quantum computing theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper focuses on constructing Calderbank-Shor-Steane (CSS) quantum error-correcting codes that can implement a non-Clifford logical CCZ gate using transversal physical T/T⁽ gates.

Main result. The authors successfully construct such codes for various block lengths (e.g., $n=48$ to $n=496$) and prove that no such code exists for lengths below $n=39$.

Method. The construction combines projective geometry to define the stabilizer structure, uses the constraints imposed by the target CCZ gate (leading to Campbell-Howard conditions), and solves the resulting compatibility search as a Boolean Satisfiability (SAT) problem.

Model / system. The model is quantum error correction using CSS codes, where the logical operations are realized via transversal physical T/T⁽ gates. The underlying mathematical structure is defined using binary projective geometry $ ext{PG}(s-1, 2)$.

Key observables. Code distance parameters ($d_X, d_Z$), the weight of codewords, and the existence of a compatible logical operator.

Important parameters / regimes. Block length $n$ (tested from 48 to 496, with a gap $39 \le n \le 46$ remaining open), and the required distance $d_Z \ge 3$.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that the code structure can be derived from projective geometry, and the search for compatible operators is encoded as a SAT problem.

Figures summary. The notes do not provide a summary of figures, but mention references to equations (6), (10), and a general proof structure for Theorem 4.5.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by first defining the problem using projective geometry to establish $d_Z \ge 3$. It then translates the physical constraints into algebraic conditions (CH conditions), which are solved using SAT solvers to find compatible codes. Finally, it systematically proves non-existence for small lengths ($n < 39$) through exhaustive algebraic exclusion.

Abstract

We construct CSS codes with three logical qubits whose logical CCZ gate is implemented by transversal physical $T/T^{\dagger}$ gates and whose distance is at least $3$. These codes start with $8$-divisible $X$-stabilizers defined by projective geometry, which guarantees $d_Z\geq 3$. A SAT solver then finds three $X$-logical operators compatible with the transversal CCZ. We build codes at thirteen block lengths from $n=48$ to $n=496$, with $d_Z=3$ or $4$, and illustrate the method with the $[[48,3,3]]$ code $Q_{48}$. Additionally, we prove that no CSS code with three logical qubits, $Z$-distance at least $3$, and a quasi-transversal CCZ gate (transversal $T$ with a diagonal Clifford correction) exists below $n=39$. The $[[47,3,3]]$ code of Jacinto et al. serves as an upper bound, leaving $39\leq n\leq46$ open.

Cost of Delay for Post-Quantum Migration: Putting Classical and Harvest-Now-Decrypt-Later Risk on One Ordered List

Authors: Animesh Shaw
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.09029v1

Summary. This paper creates a novel, unified metric to help organizations prioritize post-quantum cryptographic migration by combining classical data breach risks with future quantum computing threats. It mathematically proves how these two distinct risks interact using a competing-risks model. The resulting 'Cost of Delay' score provides an optimal, actionable ordering for security investments.

Why it may be interesting. While not physics-focused, the rigorous mathematical modeling of competing failure processes and the use of survival analysis techniques are highly relevant to open quantum systems modeling where multiple decoherence or failure channels compete.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the lack of a common metric for prioritizing data migration to post-quantum cryptography, balancing immediate classical security risks against future quantum threats.

Main result. It develops a unified 'Cost of Delay' metric that successfully couples classical and quantum risks, demonstrating that the quantum threat can significantly reorder asset prioritization compared to classical-only assessments.

Method. The authors model both risks as a 'cost of delay' in currency per year, using a competing-risks framework that combines an annualized classical loss expectancy with a quantum loss rate derived from an arrival law.

Model / system. The model is an abstract risk assessment framework applied to digital assets, using parameters like data value, turnover, classical hazard rates, and a Weibull-fitted arrival law for a Cryptographically Relevant Quantum Computer (CRQC).

Key observables. The primary observable is the total Cost of Delay (CoD), which dictates the optimal sequence for security upgrades.

Important parameters / regimes. Key parameters include the classical hazard rate ($\lambda$), data value ($V$), data lifetime ($L$), and the quantum arrival rate derived from expert anchors.

Assumptions / limitations. Major assumptions include that parameters are assumed from public documentation rather than elicited from practitioners, and that the model only covers confidentiality risk.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the race between classical and quantum attackers over time, show the dominance threshold ($\lambda^*$) where quantum risk takes over, and demonstrate the quantum term's ability to reorder assets beyond input uncertainty.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the unified CoD metric, proves mathematical propositions regarding its structure, validates the construction using simulations, and applies the model to several public system case studies.

Abstract

Organisations deciding which assets to migrate to post-quantum cryptography first, and how that work competes with a backlog of classical findings, lack a common unit: post-quantum scores are dimensionless and quantum-only, while classical risk is annualised loss. We express both threats as a cost of delay in currency per year on the same asset. The quantum term is the rate at which deferring migration commits irreversible harvest-now-decrypt-later loss, computed from required confidentiality duration, recordable traffic share and data turnover, using a quantum-arrival law fitted in closed form to published expert anchors. A competing-risks factor couples the two terms so the same loss is not counted twice. We prove that the construction is not a weighted sum of a classical and a quantum score, give a dominance threshold on the classical hazard that is independent of asset value, and show that the induced order is optimal for sequencing work under constant loss rates. We verify the implementation by simulation, adaptive quadrature and a second implementation. On four public systems with parameters assumed from public documentation, the quantum term reorders the asset list beyond input uncertainty for one system only (signal-to-noise 1.36 against 0.14-0.46), moves specific long-lived, quiet, recordable assets decisively, and otherwise changes magnitudes. Asset value and turnover explain 73-91% of the uncertainty in the quantum term; the arrival date explains 3-17%. The case studies are illustrative and are not validated against outcomes.

Summary. This paper compares two open-source quantum network simulators, QuISP and SeQUeNCe, by cross-validating basic tasks like entanglement generation and purification. The authors find that while the simulators disagree on the time taken for these tasks, they correctly predict the final resource fidelity under the same noise conditions. This work establishes a vital benchmark for improving the reliability of quantum network simulations.

Main result. The simulators show quantitative differences in task completion time due to connection models, but they agree on the final fidelity of distributed resources under identical error parameters.

Method. A comparative analysis (cross-validation) was performed by simulating fundamental quantum networking primitives like entanglement generation, swapping, and purification using two distinct simulators.

Model / system. The study models quantum networks, which utilize photonic qubits and quantum memories to distribute entanglement over long distances, simulating first-generation (1G) repeater protocols.

Key observables. Latency of resource distribution (time required for tasks) and the fidelity of the distributed entangled resources.

Important parameters / regimes. Error parameters (e.g., Pauli noise, qubit loss), connection model assumptions, and classical communication timing.

Assumptions / limitations. The comparison assumes that the core physics of resource fidelity is correctly modeled, even if the timing mechanics differ between simulators.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the three core processes: link-level entanglement generation (MIM), entanglement swapping at a repeater node, and entanglement purification.

Paper structure. The paper establishes a comparative framework by detailing the simulation of basic quantum networking tasks, comparing the results from QuISP and SeQUeNCe, and concluding with a methodology for future cross-validation.

Abstract

We present a comparative analysis of two open-source quantum network simulators, QuISP and SeQUeNCe, focusing on cross-validation of basic networking tasks to ensure consistency and accuracy in simulation outputs. Although both simulators are designed to study quantum network management policies and their dynamics under complex noise models, their differing underlying assumptions can lead to variations in simulation results. We highlight the discrepancies in how the two simulators handle connections, internal network node processing time, and classical communication, resulting in significant quantitative and qualitative differences in the time required to perform basic network tasks such as entanglement generation, purification, and swapping. We simulate common scenarios between the simulators to compare both the latency of resource distribution and the fidelity of the distributed resources. Our findings indicate that while the simulators differ in the time required to complete network tasks---a constant factor difference attributable to their respective connection models---they agree on the fidelity of the distributed resources under identical error parameters. This work demonstrates a crucial first step towards enhancing the reliability and reproducibility of quantum network simulations, as well as leading to full protocol development. Furthermore, our benchmarking methodology establishes a foundational set of tasks for the cross-validation of simulators to study future quantum networks.

Effective-Geometry Rescaling and Universal Critical Behavior in the Anisotropic Three-State Potts Model on the Square Lattice

Summary. This paper studies the anisotropic three-state Potts model on a square lattice to determine how spatial anisotropy affects its critical behavior. Using Monte Carlo simulations and finite-size scaling, the authors show that while anisotropy alters the system's effective geometry, the underlying bulk critical universality class remains robustly that of the isotropic three-state Potts model.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a detailed theoretical understanding of how lattice geometry and anisotropy manifest at criticality, offering a quantitative framework (effective-geometry rescaling) to separate universal physics from non-universal boundary effects.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To investigate whether spatial anisotropy in the two-dimensional three-state Potts ferromagnet modifies the bulk critical universality class or primarily affects the finite-size critical geometry.

Main result. The study concludes that spatial anisotropy changes the effective critical geometry (e.g., directional correlation ratios) but does not alter the bulk critical universality class, which remains consistent with the 2D three-state Potts model.

Method. The research employs Wolff single-cluster Monte Carlo simulations and finite-size scaling analysis, supplemented by the analysis of Fisher zeros and directional correlation ratios.

Model / system. The system is an anisotropic three-state Potts ferromagnet defined on a square lattice with Hamiltonian $H = -\sum_{\langle ij angle_x} J_x \delta_{\sigma_i, \sigma_j} - \sum_{\langle ij angle_y} J_y \delta_{\sigma_i, \sigma_j}$. The anisotropy is controlled by the ratio $\lambda = J_y/J_x$.

Key observables. Overall correlation ratio ($R$), directional correlation ratios ($R_x, R_y$), correlation-length exponent ($ u$), specific-heat exponent ($\alpha$), and the effective aspect ratio ($ ho_e$).

Important parameters / regimes. The anisotropy ratio $\lambda$ is varied, with specific studies conducted for $\lambda = 0.5, 0.75, ext{ and } 1$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the effective geometry rescaling can account for directional splitting in finite-size observables, and that the effective aspect ratio can be determined from critical FK wrapping probabilities.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the phase diagram, finite-size scaling results for correlation ratios, and the comparison of directional ratios ($R_x, R_y$) showing how geometric compensation restores directional equivalence.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the critical behavior using multiple diagnostic tools (FSS, Fisher zeros, directional ratios) to distinguish between universal bulk properties and non-universal geometric effects induced by anisotropy.

Abstract

We study the two-dimensional anisotropic three-state Potts ferromagnet on the square lattice using Wolff single-cluster Monte Carlo simulations and finite-size scaling. For coupling ratios $λ=J_y/J_x=0.5$, $0.75$, and $1$, finite-size scaling of the correlation ratio yields critical behavior consistent with the two-dimensional three-state Potts universality class. The scaling of the leading Fisher zeros gives a correlation-length exponent consistent with $ν=5/6$, while their cumulative density is consistent with the expected specific-heat exponent $α=1/3$. We further characterize the anisotropy at criticality using directional correlation ratios $R_x$ and $R_y$ together with directional FK wrapping probabilities. For $λ=0.5$ on a physically square lattice, $R_x$ and $R_y$ approach distinct critical values while yielding a common correlation-length exponent. From the wrapping probabilities, we independently determine an effective aspect ratio $ρ_e^\square=0.6413(5)$, in close agreement with the theoretical value $ρ_e^{\square,\mathrm{th}}\simeq0.64150030$ obtained from the isoradial representation. Using the theoretical value to set the physical aspect ratio restores directional equivalence, $R_x\simeq R_y$, and brings the overall correlation ratio toward the isotropic-square reference. The results show that spatial anisotropy changes the effective critical geometry without altering the bulk three-state Potts universality class.

This allows for detailed predictions of magnetic observables across various crystal symmetries.

Important parameters / regimes. Zero-wavevector (k=0) magnetic orders; tetragonal, trigonal/hexagonal, and cubic symmetries.

Abstract

In this work we study phenomenological Landau theories of non-relativistic magnetic systems, generalizing work on the collinear case to coplanar and non-coplanar orders. Focusing on zero-wavevector ($\boldsymbol{k}=0$) magnetic orders described by a single irreducible representation of the crystallographic point group, we construct the symmetry-allowed free energies to quartic order for all possible multi-dimensional irreducible ordering channels. We find distinct Landau theories for tetragonal, trigonal/hexagonal, and cubic symmetry. Similar to the case of spin-nematic fluids, we find two natural outcomes: a collapse to a lower-symmetry collinear phase or a genuinely coplanar or non-coplanar order where the order parameters form a mutually orthogonal set in spin space -- reminiscent of the $A$ and $B$ phases in ${}^3$He. We show in general that this symmetry-broken phase realizes the spin space group associated with the spatial representation of the order parameter sharing the same parent space group as the paramagnetic phase. This provides a direct link between the Landau and spin space group approaches. We calculate key observables using this Landau approach, including net magnetization, spin conductivity, multipolar moments, and piezomagnetism, connecting our results to constraints from the underlying spin space group. Examples of coplanar and non-coplanar magnetic orders for each case are worked out in detail. Finally, we discuss the relationship of the lower-symmetry collinear phases to the usual collinear spin groups, as well as connections to atomic altermagnetism, superfluid ${}^3$He and electronic nematic phases.

On the Expressive Power and Capacity of Quantum Data Reuploaders

Authors: Lord Sen, Shyamapada Mukherjee
Type: both · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.09947v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper provides a deep theoretical and experimental analysis of Quantum Data Reuploaders (QDRs), characterizing their achievable frequency spectrum using Fourier analysis. It derives capacity bounds based on this spectrum size and compares QDR performance against classical MLPs on various function approximation and classification tasks. The findings highlight that while QDRs have a theoretically structured capacity, their practical performance is highly sensitive to input scaling and dimensionality.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides rigorous mathematical tools (Fourier analysis, sumsets) to quantify the limitations of quantum circuit depth in approximating classical functions, which is crucial for understanding the practical utility of variational quantum algorithms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the expressive power and capacity of Quantum Data Reuploaders (QDRs), a specific architecture for Quantum Neural Networks (QNNs). It aims to characterize how the structure of the quantum circuit limits the functions it can approximate.

Main result. The accessible frequency spectrum of QDRs grows polynomially with the reuploading depth $R$, leading to a capacity bound proportional to $\sqrt{|KR|/n}$. Experimentally, QDRs perform well on certain tasks but are limited in multivariate regression compared to MLPs.

Method. The analysis relies heavily on Fourier theory, mapping the quantum circuit output to a finite Fourier series whose frequency set $KR$ is characterized using sumsets and word length metrics on an integer lattice.

Model / system. The model is the QDR architecture, $U( heta, x) = W_{R+1}V(x)W_R( heta) \cdots W_1$, where $V(x)$ encodes classical data $x$. The analysis is performed on the resulting output function $f_ heta(x)$, which is a finite Fourier series.

Key observables. Test Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) for regression tasks (e.g., $ anh(x^3/3)$) and classification accuracy for tasks like XOR and spirals.

Important parameters / regimes. The reuploading depth $R$, the input scale $\sigma$, the number of qubits $n$, and the number of trainable parameters $P$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the encoding $V(x)$ has a finite phase decomposition, and the theoretical bounds rely on the structure of the frequency set $KR$.

Figures summary. Figures compare the test RMSE of QDRs versus matched MLPs across various targets (ReLU, sigmoid, $ anh(x^3/3)$) and show decision boundaries for classification tasks.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the theoretical characterization of the frequency spectrum $KR$ using Fourier analysis. It then derives capacity bounds based on the size of this spectrum. Finally, it presents extensive experiments comparing QDR performance against MLPs on regression and classification tasks.

Abstract

Data reuploading---repeatedly encoding classical inputs at multiple layers of a parameterized quantum circuit---is a central mechanism for enhancing the expressivity of quantum neural networks (QNNs). Building on the Fourier-theoretic framework of Schuld et al.~\cite{schuld2021fourier}, we study the frequency spectrum of the general multivariate, $R$-reupload architecture (QDR). (i) We make explicit that the accessible frequencies form the set $K_R=S^{(R)}-S^{(R)}$, which equals the $R$-fold sumset $T^{(R)}$ of the difference set $T=S-S$ of the encoding spectrum, and we characterise it exactly: $K_R=\{k\in\mathcal L:\ \ell_T(k)\le R\}$, where $\mathcal L=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb Z}(T)$ is an integer lattice and $\ell_T$ is word length in $T$. (ii) We show that the \emph{directions} of the spectrum stabilise already at $R=1$ (the real span of $K_R$ equals that of $\mathcal L$), whereas the spectrum itself never saturates: the maximal frequency grows linearly in $R$ and the number of frequencies grows polynomially, as $Θ(R^{m})$ with $m=\mathrm{rank}\,\mathcal L$. (iii) Using the spectrum size, we give a Rademacher-complexity bound on the capacity of QDRs of order $\sqrt{|K_R|/n}$. (iv) We report experiments on function approximation and classification with train/test splits, several seeds and parameter-matched classical baselines. The simulated spectra match the theory exactly. A single-qubit QDR beats a matched MLP on several smooth or periodic univariate targets and on the spiral task, but it is clearly worse on multivariate regression ($d\ge4$), where it is highly sensitive to the input-encoding scale; it beats the MLP only on equations with $d\le3$ inputs.

Abstract

We present the deterministic generation and all-optical switching of ternary topologically distinct vortex states in a triad of trapped exciton-polariton condensates with direct state readout from momentum-space emission distribution. Tuning the non-resonant pump power controls the sign of the dissipative coupling, selecting between a ferromagnetic in-phase state and two frustrated states with global phase winding. The zero-winding ferromagnetic state has unstructured far-field emission at low momenta and forms vortex-antivortex arrays at the Dirac points, reflecting the C_3v global symmetry. The two frustrated states of +-2pi winding can be activated on-demand by a control beam and produce different triangular emission patterns selectively localized at the K or K' points. Although all three states share identical real-space emission distributions, their momentum-space photoluminescence profiles distinguish them unambiguously, revealing the chirality without the need for interferometry. This enables on-demand orbital angular momentum control using a pump independent of resonant phase or OAM imprinting, establishing condensate triads as a compact platform for reconfigurable chiral light generation operating on a ternary logic basis.

Summary. This research demonstrates how to achieve programmable, field-free, and asymmetric magnetization switching in magnetic multilayers using Spin-Orbit Torque. The asymmetry is fundamentally linked to an internal effective magnetic field ($H_y$) that breaks system symmetry. By controlling this asymmetry based on the initial magnetization state, the authors successfully realize a reconfigurable spin logic device.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on spintronics, the concept of symmetry breaking via an internal effective field ($H_y$) to control non-linear switching behavior is analogous to controlling non-equilibrium dynamics in open quantum systems or engineered quantum potentials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The work aims to demonstrate and understand programmable, field-free, asymmetric spin-orbit torque (SOT)-induced magnetization switching for realizing reconfigurable spin logic devices.

Main result. Asymmetric switching is shown to originate from an in-plane effective field ($H_y$) that breaks system symmetry, enabling the realization of programmable spin logic by controlling the initial magnetization state.

Method. The study combines experimental measurements (SQUID, magnetotransport, pulsed current switching) with theoretical modeling using the Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert (LLG) equation.

Model / system. The physical system is an Fe/Mn2CoAl(MCA)/Pt multilayer heterostructure grown on GaAs(001). The dynamics are modeled using the LLG equation, incorporating SOT torques and effective in-plane anisotropy fields ($H_x, H_y$).

Key observables. Critical switching currents (different for opposite polarities), magnetization hysteresis loops, and the resulting Boolean logic output states ($R_{xy}$ vs. pulse number).

Important parameters / regimes. The asymmetry is governed by the internal effective field $H_y$, while the switching polarity is controlled by the initial magnetization direction of the IMA layer.

Assumptions / limitations. The asymmetry is attributed to intrinsic symmetry breaking by the magnetic field, ruling out Joule heating effects. The logic inputs are encoded via current pulses.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the multilayer stack, M-H curves showing switching, symmetry schematics detailing the role of $H_y$, and finally, a truth table demonstrating the realization of multiple Boolean logic gates.

Paper structure. The paper first characterizes the material's in-plane magnetic anisotropy using PHE measurements. It then investigates SOT switching, establishing that the asymmetry is due to $H_y$. Finally, it leverages this tunable asymmetry across four initial magnetization states to implement a full set of programmable spin logic operations.

Abstract

Spin logic devices provide a promising route toward ultralow-power and nonvolatile information processing. In this work, we demonstrate field-free spin-orbit torque-induced asymmetric magnetization switching in in-plane anisotropy (IMA) and perpendicular magnetic anisotropy heterostructures, characterized by different critical switching currents under opposite current polarities. Combined experiments and macrospin simulations reveal that the asymmetric switching originates from an in-plane effective field Hy , which breaks the Myz mirror symmetry of the system. By controlling the magnetization direction of the IMA layer, both the switching polarity and bias direction can be tuned. Building on four distinct types of asymmetric switching behaviors, we realize a reconfigurable spin logic operation within a single Hall-bar device. These results clarify the physical origin of asymmetric magnetization switching and demonstrate an approach for realizing reconfigurable spin logic devices.

Summary. This theoretical work establishes that thermal conductance alone cannot distinguish between topological and kinetic origins of fractional conductance in edge systems. The authors prove that the separation requires analyzing the charge sector, specifically using the Lorenz ratio and shot noise measurements, which are sensitive to the underlying charge dynamics at the superconducting boundary.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a rigorous theoretical framework for diagnosing the microscopic origin of quantized transport phenomena in topological materials, suggesting that electrical measurements (charge sector) are necessary when thermal measurements (energy sector) fail to distinguish mechanisms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the fundamental challenge of distinguishing between fractional thermal conductance arising from non-Abelian topology (like Majorana modes) versus that arising from full thermal equilibration of co-propagating edge modes.

Main result. A theorem proves that under full thermal equilibration, the thermal conductance ($\kappa_{th}$) depends only on the net chiral central charges, making it impossible to distinguish the two origins using $\kappa_{th}$ alone. Separation requires analyzing the charge sector via the Lorenz ratio and shot noise.

Method. The analysis uses boundary Conformal Field Theory (CFT) to derive universal formulas for energy currents and thermal conductance, followed by applying energy conservation principles to composite quantum systems.

Model / system. The primary theoretical model is a network of chiral edge segments joined at ideal floating contacts, applied specifically to the topological insulator–superconductor (TI–SC) interface involving Majorana and Integer Quantum Hall (IQH) edge modes.

Key observables. Thermal conductance ($\kappa_{th}$), Lorenz ratio ($\mathcal{L}$), and excess shot noise (Fano factor).

Important parameters / regimes. Net chiral central charges ($c_i$), temperature ($T$), and magnetic field ($B$).

Assumptions / limitations. The theorem relies on the assumption of full thermal equilibration and ideal floating contacts; the separation mechanism requires the IQH edge not to abut the superconductor.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the general setup for the anomaly-pinned theorem using chiral edge segments; Figure 2 (mentioned) shows the geometry for explicit three-segment calculations.

Paper structure. The paper first proves the general Anomaly Pinning Theorem for thermal conductance in edge networks (Section II), then applies this to the TI–SC interface (Section III/IV), concluding that charge-sector diagnostics (Lorenz ratio, noise) are necessary for resolution (Section V).

Abstract

A recent experiment by Roy et al. (Nat. Commun. 17, 2853 (2026)) demonstrated a robust half-integer thermal conductance plateau, κ_0 T/2, at a bipolar (ν,ν')=(2,-1) junction in bilayer graphene, produced not by non-Abelian topology but by full equilibration of co-propagating electron and hole edge modes. We prove a theorem that fixes when the two origins can be told apart: under full thermal equilibration, the two-terminal thermal conductance of a network of chiral edge segments joined at ideal floating contacts is a rational function of the net chiral central charges of the segments alone, quantities pinned by the gravitational anomaly and invariant under arbitrary local boundary kinetics. The corollary is an impossibility statement: whenever two realizations present the same anomaly data to the same network, no thermal-conductance measurement can distinguish them. That the equilibrated (2,-1) value equals the central charge of a chiral Majorana mode is an arithmetic fact about one filling combination, but wherever such a coincidence occurs it is beyond the reach of thermometry, and the separation must come from the charge sector, which is not anomaly-pinned at a superconducting boundary. We develop the topological insulator-superconductor interface as the application: a vortex carries fractional charge e/4 from the θ= πmagnetoelectric coupling, the boundary hosts a chiral Majorana mode of central charge 1/2, and a laterally adjacent integer quantum Hall (IQH) region supplies the kinetic realization. The Lorenz ratio (anomalous for the isolated Majorana boundary, L_0 / (1 + 4|ν_{IQH}|) in the composite device) and the excess shot noise (growing with B from e/4 vortex tunneling versus locked to IQH plateaus) together resolve the mechanism, provided the quantum Hall edge does not abut the superconductor.
