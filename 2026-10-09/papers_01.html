Numerically exact simulation of open quantum networks with strong system-bath couplings using tensor network path integrals

Authors: Quentin W. Richter, Moritz Cygorek

Summary. This paper introduces CTEMPO, a novel tensor network path integral method designed for numerically exact simulation of open quantum systems with strong coupling. It overcomes the limitations of previous techniques by maintaining small bond dimensions, enabling the study of complex models like photosynthetic complexes and large quantum dot arrays. This provides a turnkey solution for non-Markovian quantum dynamics.

Why it may be interesting. This work is highly relevant to AMO physics and quantum optics as it provides a powerful, scalable numerical tool to simulate open quantum dynamics in complex, strongly coupled environments where standard methods fail due to memory or computational cost.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of numerically simulating non-Markovian open quantum networks, especially those involving strong system-bath couplings, which typically lead to exponentially scaling state spaces.

Main result. The proposed CTEMPO scheme provides a significant computational speed-up (orders of magnitude) over established methods like TEMPO and PT-MPO, allowing for the simulation of complex systems like the 7-site FMO complex and large QD arrays.

Method. The core method is CTEMPO, a novel tensor network path integral scheme that combines insights from TEMPO and causal contraction order to maintain small bond dimensions when simulating the time evolution of the system density matrix.

Model / system. The work applies to general open quantum networks, including the spin-boson model, the 7-site Fenna-Matthews-Olson (FMO) photosynthetic complex, and arrays of quantum dots (QDs) coupled to structured phonon baths.

Key observables. Time evolution of expectation values (e.g., $\langle \sigma_z angle$), superradiant emission intensity, and computational metrics like bond dimensions ($\chi$) and run time.

Important parameters / regimes. Strong coupling regimes ($\alpha=1$), spectral density structure (super-Ohmic), and system size ($N$ up to 20 QDs).

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes the environment coupling can be effectively mapped onto a tensor network structure, and the simulation is limited by available computer memory for very large systems.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the tensor network structure comparison (CTEMPO vs. others), the time evolution of $\langle \sigma_z angle$ showing performance gains, and the superradiant emission intensity decay curves.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem, details the CTEMPO algorithm and its multi-site extension (comb tensor network), validates it by benchmarking against existing methods on the spin-boson model, and then applies it to complex physical systems like FMO and QDs.

Abstract

Non-Markovian open quantum networks are among the most challenging problems in quantum theory, as they suffer from both, the exponentially scaling many-body state space as well as long memory times when the sites are strongly coupled to structured environments. Recent efforts to address them by combining tensor networks with real-time path integrals, such as TEMPO and process tensors, were however limited to systems with rather weak system-environment couplings because strong couplings lead to large bond dimensions. Here, we devise a tensor network scheme (CTEMPO) resulting in particularly small bond dimensions for single-site problems. We then extend this to a practical scheme to simulate non-Markovian quantum networks by time-evolving a corresponding comb tensor network. Benchmarking the single-site algorithm on the spin-boson model we find orders-of-magnitude improvement over established methods. The multi-site algorithm is shown to solve the notoriously difficult problem of the 7-site Fenna-Matthews-Olson photosynthetic complex, whose spectral density contains 62 peaks. We then apply our approach to explore the scaling behavior of the superradiance of up to $N=20$ quantum dots individually coupled to super-Ohmic phonon baths. Our approach provides a turnkey solution to non-Markovian quantum networks and is compatible with more general tensor network topologies.

Summary. This paper demonstrates that the symmetry of dissipation in a tripartite Mollow system can actively control the quantum emission pathway. By tuning the dissipation symmetry, the system can be forced to emit either highly correlated photon-phonon pairs or distinct photon bundles. This establishes dissipation symmetry as a powerful resource for programmable hybrid quantum emitters.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the frontier of open quantum systems, demonstrating that engineered dissipation can be a constructive tool for quantum state engineering, moving beyond its traditional role as a source of loss.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates whether dissipation can actively determine or select the type of quantum resource (e.g., correlated pairs vs. photon bundles) emitted from an open hybrid quantum system, rather than simply causing decoherence.

Main result. Dissipation symmetry acts as a controllable resource, allowing the system to steer quantum emission between correlated photon-phonon pairs (under symmetric dissipation) and purely photonic nonclassical states (under asymmetric dissipation).

Method. The analysis uses the Lindblad master equation to model the open system dynamics and characterizes emission using time-resolved correlation functions, Fock-state distributions, and Wigner quasiprobability distributions.

Model / system. The system is a tripartite Mollow setup involving a strongly driven two-level emitter coupled simultaneously to cavity photons and motional phonons. The dynamics are governed by an effective Hamiltonian derived in the Mollow regime.

Key observables. Time-delayed second-order correlation functions ($g^{(2)}$), Fock-state distributions, and Wigner quasiprobability distributions.

Important parameters / regimes. The dissipation ratio ($\kappa_a/\kappa_b$) is the key control parameter, determining the emission regime (symmetric vs. asymmetric dissipation).

Assumptions / limitations. The system dynamics are modeled using effective Hamiltonians derived under the rotating-wave approximation, and the selection mechanism is attributed to the symmetry of the bosonic dissipation channels.

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically illustrates the switching between pair and bundle emission by tuning the dissipation ratio ($\kappa_a/\kappa_b$), and another figure shows the dressed-state structure in the Mollow regime.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the tripartite Mollow model, deriving effective Hamiltonians for $N$-pair processes, and then analyzing the resulting emission regimes—symmetric (pair emission) versus asymmetric (bundle emission)—by controlling the dissipation symmetry.

Abstract

Dissipation is generally regarded as detrimental to nonclassical-state generation, yet whether it can determine which quantum resource is emitted from an open hybrid quantum system remains largely unexplored. Here, we show that the symmetry of bosonic dissipation channels can select and switch quantum-emission pathways in a tripartite Mollow system, where a strongly driven two-level emitter simultaneously couples to cavity photons and motional phonons. In the Mollow regime, higher-order tripartite interactions generate resonant photon-phonon pair excitations, whose emitted quantum states are subsequently selected by dissipation. Under symmetric dissipation, the system produces strongly correlated photon-phonon pairs, enabling both single-pair emission and correlated multipair bundles with pronounced nonclassical correlations. Breaking the dissipation symmetry progressively suppresses the hybrid correlations and dynamically decouples the photonic and phononic degrees of freedom. In the strongly asymmetric regime, long-lived phonons accumulate in highly excited Fock states, while rapidly leaking cavity photons are converted into high-purity single-photon and two-photon bundles. The crossover between these distinct emission regimes is characterized through time-resolved correlation functions, Fock-state distributions, and Wigner quasiprobability distributions. Our results establish dissipation symmetry as a resource for steering quantum emission between hybrid and purely photonic nonclassical states, providing a route toward programmable hybrid quantum emitters and dissipative quantum-state engineering.

Summary. This paper proposes that path complementarity, arising from the interplay between cavity backscattering and Zeeman splitting in a ring-cavity QED system, is the key mechanism for generating directional quantum light. The authors demonstrate that this mechanism allows for the simultaneous observation of directional photon blockade and strong single-photon path entanglement. This establishes a general design rule for controlling quantum light sources for applications in quantum communication.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete, general design principle—path complementarity—for engineering nonclassical light sources, linking fundamental concepts of coherence preservation with directional quantum control, which is highly relevant to quantum optics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The challenge is achieving directional quantum light that exhibits asymmetric photon statistics while maintaining high coherence between counterpropagating paths.

Main result. The interplay between cavity backscattering and Zeeman splitting establishes 'path complementarity' as a general design principle, allowing directional photon blockade and strong path entanglement to coexist.

Method. Theoretical analysis using the Hamiltonian and master equation in the driven-dissipative regime to derive intrinsic polariton properties and steady-state quantum correlations.

Model / system. The system is a ring-cavity Quantum Electrodynamics (QED) setup hybridized with a three-level emitter, modeled by a Hamiltonian incorporating cavity detuning, coupling, backscattering, and Zeeman splitting.

Key observables. Directional photon blockade (antibunching in one direction, bunching in the other), single-photon path entanglement, and path concurrence ($C_n$).

Important parameters / regimes. Weak driving regime, Zeeman splitting ($\delta_f$), cavity backscattering ($J$), and the resonance branch selection.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis focuses on the weak-drive regime, allowing the intrinsic polariton structure to dictate the directional response.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the physical setup, the dressed spectrum, and the intrinsic photonic path concurrence; later figures establish the coexistence of directional blockade and path entanglement across parameter regimes.

Paper structure. The paper first identifies the physical ingredients (backscattering and Zeeman splitting) leading to the problem. It then analyzes the intrinsic polariton structure to derive path predictability and concurrence. Finally, it extends this analysis to the driven-dissipative steady state to demonstrate the coexistence of directional photon statistics and path entanglement.

Abstract

Directional quantum light requires asymmetric photon statistics without sacrificing coherence between propagation paths, a combination that remains difficult to achieve within a single quantum-optical platform. Here we identify a microscopic path-complementarity mechanism in hybridized cavity polaritons that reconciles these two requirements. In a Zeeman-split ring-cavity QED system, cavity backscattering hybridizes the counterpropagating modes, whereas the Zeeman splitting creates spectrally asymmetric excitation channels. Their interplay gives rise, under weak driving, to directional photon blockade coexisting with strong single-photon path entanglement: one input direction exhibits antibunching while the opposite direction is bunched. Remarkably, switching between the two one-photon polariton resonances reverses both the photon-statistical and intensity asymmetries while nearly preserving the path concurrence over a broad parameter regime. Our results establish path complementarity as a general design principle for directional nonclassical light with retained quantum coherence.

Summary. This paper demonstrates geometric heat pumping on a superconducting quantum processor by cyclically driving a qubit coupled to two thermal reservoirs. The authors show that the measurable, non-zero heat pumped is a geometric quantity determined by the path taken in parameter space. This bridges fundamental concepts from quantum thermodynamics and geometric phases to near-term quantum hardware implementations.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete, experimentally relevant platform (superconducting qubits) to test fundamental non-equilibrium thermodynamic principles, specifically linking quantum work extraction to geometric phases.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates geometric heat pumping—the transfer of energy between reservoirs at equal temperature—by cyclically driving a quantum system.

Main result. The pumped heat is shown to be a purely geometric quantity, depending only on the area enclosed by the driving contour in parameter space, and this is confirmed by comparing collision models to the adiabatic limit.

Method. The authors employ a collision model implemented on a quantum processor, analyzing the system dynamics using both discrete time-stepping (collision model) and continuous-time adiabatic expansions.

Model / system. The system is a working qubit coupled to two thermal reservoirs (L and R), modeled via a collision process involving ancilla qubits. The dynamics are described by a time-dependent Lindblad master equation.

Key observables. Stochastic heat exchanged per collision ($Q_{\alpha,j,k}$), ensemble average heat ($Q_c^\alpha$), and the geometric heat contribution ($Q_{geo}^\alpha$).

Important parameters / regimes. The slow-driving regime, the area enclosed by the driving contour, and the ratio of pumped heat to noise-induced heating.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the adiabatic approximation in the slow-driving limit to extract the geometric contribution, and the collision model assumes ancillas are reset after each interaction.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the quantum circuit for a single time cell, detailing the sequence of collisions and the structure of energy measurements ($E_{in}$ and $E_{out}$) used to calculate heat.

Paper structure. The paper moves from defining the physical setup and the collision model to deriving the continuous-time Lindblad dynamics. It then analyzes the slow-driving limit using adiabatic expansions to isolate the geometric heat term, benchmarking these results against direct simulations of the collision model.

Abstract

Geometric heat pumping is the transfer of energy between reservoirs at equal temperature where the heat exchanged in an adiabatic cycle depends only on the driving contour in parameter space. We implement this mechanism on a superconducting quantum processor using a collision model, where a driven qubit interacts repeatedly with two ancilla qubits that are prepared in thermal states and emulate the reservoirs. Energy measurements before and after each interaction provide a calorimetric record of the heat exchanged with each reservoir. By reversing the driving contour, we extract a heat contribution associated with pumping. Its dependence on the period and on the contour follows exact simulations of the collision model, without adjustable parameters, and approaches in the slow-driving regime the geometric limit of the corresponding Lindblad dynamics.

Correspondence between Asymptotic Quantum Many-Body Scars in Closed Systems and Diffusive Nambu-Goldstone Modes in Open Systems

Summary. This paper reveals a fundamental duality connecting slow, non-thermalizing states (Quantum Many-Body Scars) in closed quantum systems to diffusive Nambu-Goldstone modes in open systems. By establishing a spectral correspondence between the parent Hamiltonian and the effective Lindbladian, the authors unify the understanding of slow relaxation across different physical regimes. This has broad implications for understanding quantum dynamics in both isolated and dissipative environments.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, unifying mathematical framework connecting two distinct physical phenomena—non-thermal scars in isolated systems and diffusive relaxation in open ones—using spectral theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper establishes a deep theoretical correspondence between slow relaxation mechanisms in closed quantum systems (Quantum Many-Body Scars) and diffusive dynamics in open quantum systems (Nambu-Goldstone modes).

Main result. It shows that a common underlying structure links weak ergodicity breaking in closed systems to diffusive hydrodynamics in open systems, specifically mapping an exact quadratic parent-Hamiltonian branch to a diffusive NG mode.

Method. The authors use spectral analysis, variational constructions, and the comparison between the parent Hamiltonian of the scar states and the effective Lindbladian of the open system.

Model / system. The study considers general spin, fermionic, and bosonic systems, exemplified by models like the XXZ Hamiltonian on a two-leg ladder. The dynamics are governed by a closed system Hamiltonian and an open system Lindblad master equation.

Key observables. The key observables are the spectral properties: the gap scaling ($O(k^2)$) in the parent Hamiltonian for scars, and the decay rate $\lambda(k) \simeq Dk^2$ for the diffusive NG modes.

Important parameters / regimes. The correspondence holds under the assumption of strong U(1) symmetry and local charge-dephasing Lindblad operators, and for uniformly bounded positive transition rates.

Assumptions / limitations. The correspondence is derived assuming the existence of asymptotic scar states and the applicability of the Lindblad formalism for the open system dynamics.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by first defining the scar states in closed systems, then analyzing the dynamics in open systems via the Lindbladian, and finally establishing the spectral correspondence between the two frameworks.

Abstract

Hydrodynamic relaxation in open quantum many-body systems can be understood in terms of diffusive Nambu--Goldstone (NG) modes associated with strong-to-weak spontaneous symmetry breaking (SWSSB). Here, we find a common structure underlying weak ergodicity breaking in closed quantum systems and diffusive hydrodynamics in open quantum systems. We establish a spectral correspondence between the parent Hamiltonian of rainbow quantum many-body scar (RQMBS) states in closed systems and the effective Lindbladian of strongly dissipative open systems with strong $U(1)$ symmetry and local charge-dephasing Lindblad operators. For broad classes of spin, fermionic, and bosonic systems, we construct asymptotic RQMBS (ARQMBS) states. Within the enlarged scar subspace, the parent Hamiltonian admits a local Rokhsar--Kivelson-type frustration-free decomposition. A single-mode variational construction gives an $O(k^2)$ upper bound on its lowest excitation energy and yields states satisfying the defining ARQMBS criteria: orthogonality, vanishing energy variance, and characteristic entanglement scaling. Under vectorization, RQMBS states are mapped to maximally mixed infinite-temperature states in fixed-charge sectors, which exhibit long-range SWSSB order under finite-density conditions. Assuming uniformly bounded positive transition rates, the effective Lindbladian has the same local projectors as the parent Hamiltonian but with positive transition-dependent weights, yielding two-sided spectral bounds and the same system-size scaling of their gaps. For uniform rates, the two operators are proportional to each other and their eigenmodes coincide. Therefore, an exact quadratic parent-Hamiltonian branch maps directly to a diffusive NG mode. Our results connect different mechanisms of slow relaxation in closed and open quantum many-body systems.

Summary. This paper theoretically analyzes the superradiant phase in a quantum Rabi ring, showing that the system possesses extensive degeneracy in the mean-field limit. Crucially, it demonstrates that quantum fluctuations introduce a non-classical Ising coupling that selects between ferro- and antiferro-superradiant states. The methodology combines mean-field theory with advanced quantum perturbation techniques.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a detailed theoretical understanding of how quantum fluctuations can stabilize and select specific ordered phases (FSR/AFSR) in cavity QED systems, demonstrating a quantum order-by-disorder effect absent classically.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the superradiant phase transition and the extensive degeneracy of superradiant configurations in an N-site quantum Rabi ring.

Main result. The system exhibits extensive degeneracy in the mean-field limit ($\eta o \infty$), which is stabilized by quantum fluctuations, and finite-frequency corrections select specific ferro- or antiferro-superradiant states via a quantum order-by-disorder effect.

Method. The analysis employs mean-field theory, followed by a Schrieffer–Wolff transformation and second-order perturbation theory to derive an effective Ising Hamiltonian describing quantum corrections.

Model / system. The model is an N-site quantum Rabi ring with two-level atoms, where each atom couples to two cavities two sites apart. The system is governed by a Hamiltonian involving cavity fields and atomic spins.

Key observables. Superradiant excitation gap ($\varepsilon_{\min}$), effective Ising coupling constants ($J_r$), and the degeneracy of superradiant configurations.

Important parameters / regimes. The atomic-to-cavity frequency ratio $\eta = \Delta/\omega$, and the coupling ratio $eta$ that determines the phase (FSR, AFSR, CSR).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the mean-field approximation and subsequent quantum corrections derived via perturbation theory, assuming the photon band remains dispersive.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the model setup, momentum-dependent critical coupling, phase diagrams, and show the evolution of correlation lengths and sign structure factors distinguishing different superradiant phases.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the mean-field degeneracy in the infinite limit, to deriving the effective low-energy Hamiltonian using quantum corrections, and finally analyzing the resulting quantum order-by-disorder selection mechanism.

Abstract

We show that an $N$-site quantum Rabi ring with matched giant-atom couplings has $2^N$ degenerate superradiant configurations as the atomic-to-cavity frequency ratio $η=Δ/ω$ tends to infinity. Each atom couples to two cavities two sites apart, and the photon hopping carries a phase $π/2$. Tuning the ratio of contact strengths to the photon hopping reduces the mean-field energy to a sum of single-site Rabi energies, with a common critical coupling for all momentum-pair sectors. The degeneracy survives quadratic quantum fluctuations despite a dispersive excitation spectrum. At finite $η$, second-order processes involving the local cubic anharmonicity generate a same-sublattice Ising coupling of order $ω/η$, selecting ferro- and antiferro-superradiant configurations. This quantum order-by-disorder effect has no classical counterpart. Four-site calculations retaining all spin states quantitatively support the predicted energy coefficients. The separation between the induced coupling and the excitation gap allows a broad temperature interval with macroscopic photon occupation and short-range correlations between displacement signs.

Abstract

Quantum emitters in hexagonal boron nitride (hBN) are promising building blocks for solid-state quantum photonics because of their bright room-temperature emission. These emitters strongly interact with lattice vibrations, which partially underpins their excitation efficiency. Here, using complementary cathodoluminescence (CL) and photoluminescence (PL) spectroscopy, we demonstrate cavity-mediated interactions between hBN quantum emitters coupled to the same plasmonic Au void cavity at the room temperature. Cathodoluminescence measurements reveal that a vibronic transition of the 443 nm emitter hybridizes with the cavity mode, giving rise to a spectrally resolved splitting, while the neighboring 537 nm emitter remains in the weak-coupling regime despite spectral overlap. Excitation-dependent PL further shows that the hybridized state can be accessed through phonon-assisted excitations below the zero-phonon transition. These results demonstrate that vibronic transitions provide an effective pathway for controlling interactions between multiple quantum emitters in hybrid plasmonic nanophotonic systems.

Abstract

We investigate the dynamics of quantum ergotropy in a multipartite system of two-level atoms interacting with a quantized field. The role of multipartite quantum mutual information and quantum relative entropy is analyzed to understand how correlations and the distinguishability between the passive state and the product of local states affect the extractable work. Numerical results for systems containing two to five atoms show that the maximum ergotropy does not increase linearly with the system size, while the intervals of zero ergotropy are gradually modified and suppressed for larger systems. We also study the effect of the average thermal photon number and different initial atomic states. Increasing the thermal photon number mainly reduces the relative-entropy contribution, whereas the initial state strongly influences both the magnitude and persistence of ergotropy. Among the considered states, the partially entangled state provides the largest and more persistent extractable work, while the mixed GHZ state gives the smallest ergotropy. These results highlight the importance of multipartite correlations, thermal effects, and state preparation in controlling quantum work extraction and may be useful for future studies of quantum batteries and quantum power.

Abstract

Can an agent's predictions reveal whether its reasoning is based on a classical or a quantum model? Using known results on matrix factorizations, here we show that quantum Bayesian retrodiction can produce one-step-ahead forecasts incompatible with every classical Bayesian explanation based on learning about a fixed but unknown sampling law from conditionally independent observations. The separation has a sharp threshold in the number of outcomes: for any measurement with at most four outcomes, the quantum predictions admit a classical realization, irrespective of the prior state and the finite Hilbert-space dimension, whereas a five-outcome measurement on a single qubit already violates an explicit classical bound. Thus, in this setting, quantum Bayesian retrodiction is strictly more expressive than classical Bayesian learning, and this difference can be witnessed without observing or specifying the agent's internal description.

Abstract

Abstract

This work is devoted to a theoretical study of the interaction of a quantum three-level ladder system with a continuous electromagnetic field in a one-dimensional open waveguide. We consider a situation closely resembling stimulated emission - the scattering of a single-photon exponential pulse by an excited three-level emitter. Using the real-space formalism, we obtain an analytical expression for the wavefunction of our system. We show that, for the low anharmonicity of a three-level system inherent to a transmon (the most common type of artificial atom), the influence of the third level can have a significant effect on the system's behavior. Namely, the presence of the third level largely suppresses the stimulated emission effect if the relative anharmonicity is lower than $| α_r | < 4\%$. However, for a three-level system, it is possible to obtain correlation effects such as photon bunching in the reflected field, which can also be 'switched off' by tuning the incident photon parameters.
