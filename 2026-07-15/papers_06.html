Efficient Circuit Transpilation of Commuting Gates on 2D Grids

Authors: Sabina Drăgoi, Daniel J. Egger
Type: both · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.10274v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: QC/QI experiment 2/5 · analog quantum simulation 1/5 · methods for driven-dissipative 1/5 · non-equilibrium dynamics 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper presents a novel transpilation scheme for mapping quantum circuits involving commuting gates onto 2D lattice hardware. By iteratively optimizing both the required SWAP layers and the qubit layout, it achieves substantial reductions in circuit depth compared to existing methods. This efficiency boost allows for testing larger-scale QAOA applications on hard combinatorial problems.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on optimization algorithms, the underlying need to map abstract Hamiltonians onto physical hardware connectivity maps directly to resource constraints in simulating complex quantum many-body systems or designing quantum network protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core challenge is developing efficient transpilation schemes for quantum circuits, particularly those involving blocks of commuting two-qubit gates, to run on hardware with limited connectivity.

Main result. The proposed greedy transpilation scheme significantly reduces circuit depth and gate count (by a factor of two) compared to standard methods, enabling larger qubit counts and improving approximation ratios for optimization problems like Max Cut and MIS.

Method. The authors propose an iterative method that alternates between constructing problem-dependent SWAP-layer sequences and optimizing the initial qubit layout by adapting routing directly to required interactions.

Model / system. The work focuses on quantum computation implemented on a two-dimensional lattice grid. The circuits are used for Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) applied to combinatorial optimization problems like Maximum Cut (MC) and Maximum Independent Set (MIS).

Key observables. Circuit depth, gate count, mean approximation ratio, and kept shot ratio.

Important parameters / regimes. The number of qubits ($n$), the circuit depth parameter ($p$) in QAOA, and the graph structure (Random Regular or Erdős-Rényi graphs).

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes the underlying hardware supports two-dimensional grid connectivity; standard optimal qubit routing is noted to be NP-complete.

Figures summary. Figures compare circuit complexity (SWAP layers, CZ gates) across various transpilation strategies (linear, hybrid, greedy) on different graph types. Other figures illustrate the search process and performance metrics like approximation ratios vs. qubits.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem of efficient transpilation for QAOA circuits on 2D grids. It details the proposed greedy strategy by optimizing both SWAP layer sequences and initial mappings, benchmarking it against established line-based and grid-based reference strategies across MC and MIS instances.

Abstract

Combinatorial optimization problems are central to many applications but can be challenging to solve. Quantum approaches such as the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) offer new tools with which to tackle such problems. However, QAOA circuits inherit the interaction structure of the target Hamiltonian, often resulting in deep circuits when compiled onto hardware with limited connectivity. Efficient transpilation is therefore critical to their practical performance. In this work, we propose a transpilation scheme for circuits consisting of blocks of commuting two-qubit gates on two-dimensional lattices. Unlike standard approaches based on random initial mappings and fixed routing, our method alternates between constructing problem-dependent SWAP-layer sequences and updating the qubit layout. By adapting the routing to the required interactions, this yields significantly shorter circuits for graphs with few edges. We benchmark our approach on QAOA instances for Maximum Cut (MC) on Random Regular graphs and Maximum Independent Set (MIS) on Erdős-Rényi graphs. Compared to standard methods, we reduce circuit depth and gate count by about a factor of two, enabling experiments with up to $80$ qubits and improving approximation ratios by up to $6.6\%$ for MC and $9.3\%$ for MIS.

Gauge-invariant thermodynamics of a finite-time quantum Otto engine

Authors: Midana Baial Sambú, Thiago R. de Oliveira, Lucas C. Céleri
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.09857v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: non-equilibrium dynamics 2/5 · driven-dissipative phase transition 1/5 · methods for driven-dissipative 1/5 · non-equilibrium universality 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical paper develops a gauge-invariant framework to analyze thermodynamics in finite-time quantum Otto engines using the LMG model. It successfully decomposes total work and entropy production into components associated with population changes and quantum coherences. This clarifies the role of coherence in irreversible processes, showing how the invariant sector captures the essential extracted work.

Why it may be interesting. The systematic separation of coherence effects from classical thermodynamic contributions in non-equilibrium quantum engines provides a powerful, general tool for understanding work extraction beyond standard energy conservation laws.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses how to formulate quantum thermodynamics for finite-time processes by defining thermodynamic quantities that are invariant under transformations preserving the mean energy (gauge invariance). It aims to separate work extraction into contributions from population changes and quantum coherences.

Main result. A gauge-invariant decomposition of work and entropy production is derived, showing that the extracted work can be split into an 'invariant contribution' related to energy spectrum changes and a 'coherent contribution.'

Method. The authors employ a gauge-invariant thermodynamic framework by defining observables on equivalence classes of states, analyzing the Otto cycle using the Lipkin-Meshkov-Glick model.

Model / system. The working medium is modeled by the Lipkin-Meshkov-Glick (LMG) Hamiltonian, which describes collective angular momentum operators for spin-1/2 particles. The analysis focuses on a finite-time quantum Otto engine cycle.

Key observables. Gauge-invariant work ($W_{ ext{inv}}$), heat, entropy production ($\Sigma$), and the ratio $\kappa$ comparing usual vs. gauge-invariant work.

Important parameters / regimes. Driving speed (related to time dependence of fields), system size ($N=2j$), and critical parameters associated with the LMG model ($\delta_c = \gamma_x$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that thermodynamic quantities can be robustly defined via gauge invariance, particularly when only energy measurements are accessible.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the gauge-invariant formalism, applies it to the LMG model describing an Otto engine cycle, derives explicit decompositions for work and entropy production ($\Sigma = \Sigma_{ ext{pop}} + \Sigma_{ ext{coh}}$), and analyzes how these quantities behave near critical points.

Abstract

We investigate a finite-time quantum Otto engine within the framework of gauge-invariant quantum thermodynamics, using the Lipkin-Meshkov-Glick model as the working medium. In this formulation, thermodynamic quantities are defined on equivalence classes of thermodynamically indistinguishable states, leading naturally to gauge-invariant notions of work, heat, entropy, and efficiency. We derive explicit expressions for these quantities and show that the usual work performed during the unitary strokes decomposes into an invariant contribution, associated with changes in the instantaneous energy spectrum and populations, and a coherent contribution arising from finite-time quantum coherences. This decomposition induces the corresponding splittings of engine efficiency and entropy production, providing a geometric interpretation of finite-time irreversibility and clarifying the role of coherence in work extraction. By analyzing the cycle's dependence on driving speed and system size, we identify the engine's operating region and investigate the influence of criticality. We show that crossing the critical region substantially reduces the parameter space in which the cycle operates as a heat engine. At the same time, whenever the engine condition remains satisfied, the discrepancy between the conventional and gauge-invariant descriptions becomes strongly suppressed, indicating that the gauge-invariant sector predominantly carries the extracted work. These results establish a direct connection between gauge-invariant thermodynamics, finite-time irreversibility, and work extraction in many-body quantum thermal machines.

Multiparameter Quantum Metrology in Molecular Dimers

Summary. This paper analyzes multiparameter quantum estimation in a model of interacting molecular dimers. By calculating the Quantum Fisher Information Matrix, the authors show that simultaneous estimation is superior when the system is near resonance and at low temperatures. The results establish temperature as a key control parameter for achieving high-precision quantum sensing.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses quantum sensing in realistic molecular platforms, providing quantitative metrics (QFIM) to guide experimental design for enhancing measurement precision beyond classical limits.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the fundamental limits of simultaneously estimating multiple parameters, specifically temperature (T) and detuning ($\Lambda$), in a molecular dimer system.

Main result. Simultaneous estimation outperforms individual estimations when quantum coherence is high (low T and near resonance), demonstrating that temperature acts as a critical control parameter for metrology precision.

Method. The analysis employs the Quantum Fisher Information Matrix (QFIM) derived via a vectorization approach to calculate the ultimate precision bounds (Quantum Cramér–Rao bound).

Model / system. The physical system is a molecular dimer modeled by two dipole-dipole interacting two-level systems. The dynamics are governed by a Hamiltonian incorporating detuning and interaction strength.

Key observables. Quantum Fisher Information Matrix elements ($K_{\mu u}$), estimation variances ($\Delta T, \Delta \Lambda$), and the performance ratio $\Gamma(T, \Lambda)$.

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature (T), Detuning ($\Lambda$), Dipole-dipole interaction strength ($V_{AB}$), and the near-resonant/low-temperature regime.

Assumptions / limitations. The system is assumed to reach global thermal equilibrium ($\varrho_{th}$ state) and utilizes an approximation for coherent excitonic interactions.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the comparison of simultaneous vs. individual estimation variances across T and $\Lambda$, showing the advantage ratio $\Gamma < 1$ near resonance and low temperature.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the physical model, derives the QFIM using vectorization, compares the bounds for simultaneous versus individual parameter estimation, and concludes by mapping out the optimal operating regime governed by T and $\Lambda$.

Abstract

We investigate multiparameter quantum estimation in a molecular dimer composed of two dipole--dipole interacting two-level systems, focusing on the simultaneous estimation of temperature $T$ and detuning $Λ$. By employing a vectorization approach to derive the quantum Fisher information matrix, we analyze the precision limits of both simultaneous and individual estimation strategies. We show that simultaneous estimation outperforms the individual one in the near-resonant and low-temperature regime, where quantum coherence is enhanced, while its advantage is progressively reduced under detuned conditions and increasing temperature. Our results demonstrate that temperature acts as a key control parameter governing both estimation precision providing a unified perspective on quantum metrology. These findings highlight the potential of molecular quantum systems as realistic platforms for multiparameter quantum sensing.

Code-space recovery for sample-based quantum diagonalization beyond native symmetry constraints

Authors: Byeongyong Park, Sanha Kang, Doyeol Ahn, Keunhong Jeong
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.10227v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 2/5 · methods for driven-dissipative 1/5 · quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. The paper presents code-space recovery as an advanced technique to enhance Sample-based Quantum Diagonalization (SQD) for finding ground states of general quantum Hamiltonians. By employing a dual-rail encoding, the method engineers robustness against sampling errors, allowing accurate diagonalization even when natural symmetries are absent. This significantly extends the applicability and reliability of quantum simulation algorithms.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses limitations in applying quantum simulation techniques to complex Hamiltonians by providing a robust error mitigation strategy that moves beyond relying solely on inherent physical symmetries.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To improve Sample-based Quantum Diagonalization (SQD) for finding ground states of Hamiltonians when native symmetries are not guaranteed.

Main result. Code-space recovery, which engineers structure via dual-rail encoding and self-consistent repair, yields lower projected Ritz energies than unencoded methods even with increased circuit overhead.

Method. The authors introduce code-space recovery using a dual-rail representation to encode logical qubits into physical pairs. This allows for the detection and iterative repair of sampling errors (code-space violations) during the diagonalization process.

Model / system. The method is benchmarked on Ising-type spin Hamiltonians, specifically Transverse-Field and Mixed-Field Ising Models, up to 36 spin sites. The target operator $O$ is set to the Hamiltonian itself.

Key observables. Projected Ritz energies (estimating ground state energy), detection/repair of code-space violations, and comparison against classical reference energies from DMRG calculations.

Important parameters / regimes. The encoding doubles the qubit count; recovery involves iterative steps ($N_{I,max}$); benchmarks test varying projected basis dimensions ($D_{ ext{tar}}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes that engineered recoverable structure can compensate for the increased circuit overhead associated with the dual-rail encoding.

Figures summary. Figure 1 summarizes the workflow: combining dual-rail encoding with self-consistent code-space recovery to estimate the lowest Ritz pair of $O$.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem, details the dual-rail encoding and the iterative, self-consistent code-space recovery algorithm (including clustering and stochastic repair), benchmarks this against unencoded methods on various Ising models, and validates results using classical reference energies.

Abstract

Sample-based quantum diagonalization (SQD) diagonalizes a Hamiltonian in a compact subspace built from quantum samples, and its performance often relies on recovery procedures that exploit native constraints such as particle-number symmetry. For a broad class of eigenvalue problems, however, no analogous constraint is guaranteed, limiting the applicability of SQD-type recovery. Here, we introduce code-space recovery, which engineers recoverable structure through encoding rather than assuming it in the target problem. Using a dual-rail representation, each logical qubit is mapped to a physical pair, $|0\rangle \to |01\rangle$ and $|1\rangle \to |10\rangle$, making code-space violations in noisy samples detectable and repairable. We combine this encoding with self-consistent recovery and benchmark it on transverse- and mixed-field Ising models with up to 36 spin sites. Despite increased circuit overhead, code-space recovery yields lower projected Ritz energies than unencoded sample-support diagonalization even at smaller projected-basis dimensions, suggesting that engineered recoverable structure can extend SQD beyond native constraints.

Summary. This research investigates the phase transitions in Ta2Ni(S_xSe_{1-x})_5 by linking anomalous phonon transport to electronic instabilities. The authors demonstrate that the soft-phonon anomaly is not isolated but arises from a complex interplay involving excitonic, hybridization, and lattice fluctuations. This suggests that multiple correlated mechanisms must act together to stabilize the material's high critical temperature.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides an excellent case study for understanding how lattice dynamics (phonons) are intimately coupled to electronic correlations and gap formation, a central theme in strongly correlated electron physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To clarify the microscopic origin and significance of a 'soft-phonon transport anomaly' observed at a phase transition in Ta2Ni(S_xSe_{1-x})_5, and to establish the interplay between electronic instabilities and lattice dynamics.

Main result. The soft-phonon anomaly is strongly coupled to underlying electronic instabilities, suggesting that three types of interactions—excitonic, hybridization-gap, and intrinsic lattice instability—must coexist to explain the high transition temperature ($T_c$).

Method. The study combines experimental measurements (thermal/electrical transport, heat capacity) with theoretical analysis comparing multiple competing instability mechanisms.

Model / system. The physical system is the quasi-one-dimensional chalcogenide solid solution Ta2Ni(S_xSe_{1-x})_5. The investigation probes how tuning stoichiometry ($x$) modifies the interplay between electronic gaps and lattice softening.

Key observables. Soft-phonon transport anomaly (anisotropic phonon scattering), electrical conductivity ($\sigma$), thermal conductivity ($\kappa$), and the logarithmic derivative of conductivity ($\epsilon_{\parallel}$).

Important parameters / regimes. $T_c$ (ranging from $\sim 130 ext{ K}$ to $326 ext{ K}$), stoichiometry $x$, and electronic gap energies.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the observed anomalies are due to strong electron-phonon coupling, requiring consideration of excitonic, single-particle hybridization, and lattice instabilities simultaneously.

Figures summary. Figures show signatures of electronic gaps in $\epsilon_{\parallel}$ and magnetic susceptibility ($\chi$), alongside V-shaped suppression anomalies in perpendicular phonon diffusivity ($D^{( ext{ph})}_{\perp}$) centered at $T_c$.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes transport measurements across the composition gradient, correlating the vanishing of the soft-phonon anomaly with the approach to a band insulator state. It culminates by proposing that only the simultaneous action of multiple instabilities can explain the high $T_c$ observed in $ ext{Ta}_2 ext{NiSe}_5$.

Abstract

Ta$_2$NiSe$_5$ continues to be investigated for its phase transition at $T_\textrm{c}$ = 326 K, where it develops both an electronic gap and a distortion of its Ta/Ni chains. One intriguing feature at $T_\textrm{c}$ seen in thermal transport is the giant anisotropic scattering of phonons moving perpendicular to the chains, which is apparently associated with the softening of a transverse acoustic phonon, but whose microscopic origin and significance demand clarification. By tuning the normal-state band overlap/gap with S substitution, we uncover a close connection between this soft-phonon transport anomaly and underlying electronic instabilities: When Ta$_2$Ni(S$_x$Se$_{1-x}$)$_5$ approaches a band insulator at high $x$, and signatures of the electronic transition are suppressed, the soft-phonon transport anomaly concomitantly vanishes. Our results establish the following picture for the Ta$_2$Ni(S$_x$Se$_{1-x}$)$_5$ family: Near the S end, a sole lattice instability gives rise to a weak structural transition with $T_\textrm{c}$ approaching 130 K. Near the Se end, additional electronic instabilities boost $T_\textrm{c}$ up to 326 K and amplify experimental signatures of the transition. The strong interaction between electrons, holes, and the lattice is manifested as a soft-phonon transport anomaly accompanied by electronic fluctuations, which include excitonic and hybridization-gap fluctuations.

Geometric Universality and Thermodynamic Microstructure of Real Fluids in a Unified Entropic Framework

Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.10076v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: non-equilibrium universality 2/5 · driven-dissipative phase transition 1/5 · non-equilibrium dynamics 1/5

Summary. This paper unifies several equations of state for real fluids within a novel geometric framework called Geometrothermodynamics (GTD). By analyzing the curvature of the thermodynamic manifold, the authors show that macroscopic phenomena like critical points are encoded geometrically. This provides a powerful, universal tool for classifying and understanding fluid behavior based on fundamental mathematical invariants.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical fluids, the use of advanced geometric tools like GTD to classify universal scaling behaviors is highly relevant for understanding phase transitions in quantum many-body systems where geometry often dictates critical points.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To develop a unified entropic framework for real fluids that encompasses multiple existing Equations of State (EoS) within a common thermodynamic description.

Main result. The authors show that the geometric structure, quantified by scalar curvature ($\mathcal{R}$), successfully encodes macroscopic critical phenomena and universal scaling features across different fluid models.

Method. The study employs Geometrothermodynamics (GTD), mapping the thermodynamic equilibrium space onto a Riemannian manifold whose geometry is analyzed using concepts like scalar curvature and invariant ratios.

Model / system. The system under study is real fluids, modeled by various EoS such as van der Waals, Berthelot, Redlich-Kwong, and Peng-Robinson. The analysis extends the framework to examine general thermodynamic systems.

Key observables. Scalar curvature ($\mathcal{R}$), dimensionless critical-amplitude ratios ($Q^i_{j}$), zero-curvature curves, and critical parameters ($V_c, T_c, P_c$).

Important parameters / regimes. Intermolecular attraction/repulsion strengths, system size (for scaling analysis), and the generalized thermodynamic potential.

Assumptions / limitations. The model relies on a mean-field approximation for intermolecular potentials and assumes thermodynamic consistency can be captured by geometric invariants.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates P–V isotherms for the van der Waals model, demonstrating the Maxwell equal-area construction for first-order phase transitions.

Paper structure. The paper systematically derives generalized EoS forms, maps them onto a GTD manifold structure, calculates critical parameters using geometric invariants (curvature), and concludes by statistically reconstructing zero-curvature curves using MCMC methods.

Abstract

We introduce a unified entropic framework for real fluids that encompasses the van der Waals, Berthelot, Redlich Kwong, and Peng Robinson equations of state within a common thermodynamic description. The corresponding microscopic interactions are then explored using Geometrothermodynamics, GTD, through the scalar curvature $mathcal{R}$ of the equilibrium manifold. We show that curvature singularities accurately reproduce macroscopic critical phenomena, while vanishing curvature $\mathcal{R}=0$ identifies specific thermodynamic states where attractive and repulsive intermolecular forces effectively balance. Furthermore, we introduce a set of dimensionless critical-amplitude ratios $Q^i_{j}$, which reveal universal geometric features of the critical regime. Although individual critical amplitudes exhibit a logarithmic dependence on the system size, these invariant ratios organize different molecular species according to the strength of criticality and encode universal scaling features, suggesting their potential as robust classification parameters. Finally, employing Bayesian inference and Markov Chain Monte Carlo, MCMC methods, we statistically reconstruct the zero-curvature curves. The posterior distributions support the consistency of the geometric scaling behavior, demonstrating that the GTD manifold encodes non-trivial information about the underlying thermodynamical models.

MDQEC-QAS: Meta-Decoding for Quantum Error Correction with Hardware-Aware VQC Search and Confidence-Gated Recovery

Summary. This work introduces a meta-decoding framework to create universal decoders for Quantum Error Correction, capable of adapting across various codes and noise types. By integrating hardware-aware VQCs and implementing a confidence-gated fallback mechanism, the authors show that selective recovery based on model certainty is vital for achieving robust logical performance.

Why it may be interesting. It directly addresses the gap between machine learning performance metrics (accuracy) and physical system reliability (logical error rate), which is crucial for building fault-tolerant quantum computers.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Developing a robust quantum error correction (QEC) decoder that can adapt across diverse physical conditions, including multiple stabilizer codes, noise families, and varying error rates.

Main result. A confidence-gated hybrid recovery strategy significantly improves logical performance by selectively using learned decoders only when they are highly confident, outperforming methods relying solely on high classification accuracy.

Method. The authors propose a unified meta-decoding framework that trains a single model to map syndromes to recoveries across various codes and noise settings. This is augmented with hardware-aware Variational Quantum Circuits (VQC) and a confidence-gated fallback mechanism.

Model / system. The study analyzes QEC performance using several stabilizer codes (FiveQubit, Steane, Planar3x3, Planar5x5). The core model involves training meta-decoders on syndrome measurements derived from noisy physical qubits.

Key observables. Logical failure probability ($p_L(p)$) and raw logical-failure ratios relative to the teacher decoder are key diagnostics, rather than just classification accuracy.

Important parameters / regimes. The benchmark tests five evaluation regimes: interpolation, unseen-$p$ transfer, unseen-noise transfer, few-shot unseen-code adaptation, and few-shot held-out-size adaptation.

Assumptions / limitations. High teacher-label accuracy alone is insufficient for assessing true logical performance in complex topological codes; therefore, a confidence threshold ($ au$) is necessary to gate the learned recovery.

Figures summary. Figure 12 specifically shows Planar $5 imes 5$ logical-failure curves comparing different protocols under the confidence-gated hybrid recovery protocol across five adaptation scenarios.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the meta-decoding framework, details the comparison between Meta-MLP and hardware-aware VQC decoders, rigorously tests generalization across multiple transfer regimes, and culminates in a detailed analysis of logical failure rates using confidence gating.

Abstract

We propose a unified meta-decoding framework for quantum error correction that learns syndrome-to-recovery mappings across multiple stabilizer codes and noise settings, without requiring separate decoders for each configuration. The benchmark includes FiveQubit, Steane, Planar3x3, and Planar5x5 codes, four noise families, and five evaluation regimes: interpolation, unseen-p transfer, unseen-noise transfer, few-shot unseen-code adaptation, and few-shot held-out-size adaptation. We compare a classical Meta-MLP teacher-trained baseline with variational quantum circuit (VQC) meta-decoders selected through hardware-aware quantum architecture search over qubit count, circuit depth, and entangling topology. The Meta-MLP achieves teacher-label accuracies of 0.9993, 0.9118, 0.9342, 0.6304, and 0.7548 across the five regimes, while the hardware-aware VQC achieves 0.9400, 0.8495, 0.8415, 0.5678, and 0.7143. However, logical-level evaluation shows that high teacher-label accuracy alone is insufficient in the most challenging Planar5x5 setting. During interpolation, the raw logical-failure ratios relative to the teacher are 12.08 and 25.91 for the Meta-MLP and VQC, respectively, whereas confidence-gated fallback reduces them to 1.71 and 1.11. These results support confidence-aware selective recovery rather than unconditional teacher replacement.

Optimal tomography of bosonic and fermionic Gaussian states

Authors: Senrui Chen, Marco Fanizza, Filippo Girardi, Ludovico Lami, Francesco Anna Mele, Michael Walter, Freek Witteveen
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.11847v1

Summary. This paper solves an open problem by determining the optimal sample complexity required to reconstruct unknown bosonic and fermionic Gaussian quantum states. The key finding is that this complexity scales quadratically with the number of modes, $m^2$, which is highly efficient for characterizing these important physical states. It achieves this using advanced mathematical tools like generalized random purification channels.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides fundamental resource estimates (sample complexity) for characterizing complex quantum states relevant in quantum information processing and quantum simulation using continuous variable systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is determining the optimal sample complexity—the minimum number of copies required for quantum state tomography—for bosonic and fermionic Gaussian states.

Main result. The authors prove that the sample complexity scales quadratically with the number of modes ($m$), specifically $n = \Theta(m^2 / \epsilon^2)$, regardless of whether the state is pure or mixed.

Method. The analysis employs advanced techniques including representation theory of Gaussian unitaries and generalizing the random purification channel to handle bosonic symmetries.

Model / system. The focus is on bosonic and fermionic Gaussian quantum states, which are ground or Gibbs states associated with quadratic Hamiltonians. These systems are fundamental in quantum optics and many-body physics.

Key observables. Sample complexity ($n$), purified distance, trace distance, covariance matrices.

Important parameters / regimes. Number of modes ($m$), accuracy ($\epsilon$), state purity (pure vs. mixed).

Assumptions / limitations. For bosonic states in infinite dimensions, the derivation relies on assuming that irreducible representation spaces are finite-dimensional to define a random purification channel.

Figures summary. A table compares previous best sample complexities with the new results for various Gaussian state types, showing improvements from exponential/cubic scaling to quadratic scaling.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the general problem of sample complexity, then developing generalized tools like random purification channels, and finally applying these tools to derive the optimal $m^2$ scaling law for both bosonic and fermionic Gaussian states.

Abstract

The sample complexity is the minimum number of copies required to learn an accurate classical description of a quantum state. Bosonic and fermionic Gaussian quantum states are families of quantum states that play a key role in quantum science and technology, from quantum optics and many-body physics to quantum chemistry, quantum computing, and quantum information theory. Despite their importance, their sample complexity had not been fully determined. We settle this open problem and show that both bosonic and fermionic Gaussian states can be learned using a number of copies that scales quadratically in the number of modes, regardless of whether the state is pure or mixed, and independently of any energy bound on the state. We derive these results by using the representation theory of Gaussian unitaries and by putting forth a generalization of the random purification channel to this setting and beyond.

Stripe-Ordered Altermagnetism Emerging from Correlation-Driven Spin-Density-Wave Instability

Authors: Zenghui Fan, Jingyao Meng, Tianxing Ma
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.11532v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 2/5 · Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5 · non-equilibrium dynamics 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work explores a mechanism for Altermagnetism arising from a Spin-Density Wave instability rather than conventional antiferromagnetism. By modeling iron pnictides with an extended Hubbard model, the authors demonstrate that correlation effects drive a stripe-ordered insulating phase (SOAM). The application of an electric potential breaks symmetry in a way that unlocks measurable spin splitting, confirming the theoretical viability of this novel magnetic state.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on condensed matter magnetism, the use of advanced quantum simulation techniques (DQMC) to study emergent magnetic orders in strongly correlated systems provides methodological insights relevant to open quantum system dynamics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Investigating whether Altermagnetism can emerge from a Spin-Density Wave (SDW) instability, moving beyond the conventional Antiferromagnetic paradigm.

Main result. The study reveals a novel stripe-ordered altermagnetic (SOAM) insulating phase driven by correlation-induced $(\pi, 0)$ SDW instability. This phase is stabilized and characterized by unconventional $d_{xy}$-wave spin splitting when symmetry is broken by an electric potential.

Method. A combination of Hartree-Fock mean-field theory for initial analysis and unbiased Determinant Quantum Monte Carlo (DQMC) simulations to confirm finite-temperature stability.

Model / system. The system uses a minimal Hubbard model relevant to iron pnictides, studied on a square lattice with a four-sublattice basis. The Hamiltonian includes hopping terms, chemical potential, and an interaction term U, modified by a uniaxial staggered electric potential ($\epsilon$).

Key observables. Spin-stripe order parameter ($\delta m_S$), spin structure factor $S(\mathbf{k})$, and the nonrelativistic spin splitting energy difference ($\Delta E$).

Important parameters / regimes. Half-filling ($n=1$), hopping parameters ($t_1, t_2, t'_2$), interaction strength ($U$), and the staggered potential magnitude ($\epsilon$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on mapping the problem to a minimal Hubbard model and employing mean-field approximations alongside DQMC simulations.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate real-space spin configurations, band structures showing symmetry protection/breaking, momentum-space spin structure factors confirming $(\pi, 0)$ instability, and phase diagrams mapping SOAM stability based on $U$ and $\epsilon$.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the SDW instability via mean-field calculations, then using DQMC to confirm the finite-temperature existence of the stripe order, and finally detailing how symmetry breaking ($\epsilon$) unlocks the characteristic altermagnetic spin splitting.

Abstract

Altermagnetism is conventionally identified within the paradigm of collinear antiferromagnets. Its potential realization within other spin instabilities, such as a spin-density wave (SDW), remains a fundamentally compelling open question. Here, we combine Hartree-Fock mean-field and unbiased determinant quantum Monte Carlo methods to investigate a minimal Hubbard model relevant to iron pnictides. We reveal a novel $d_{xy}$-wave stripe-ordered altermagnetic (SOAM) insulating phase driven fundamentally by the correlation-induced $(π,0)$ SDW instability. Abstract

We study a Wigner-type random matrix in which the off-diagonal correlation between entries is generated by a random factor shared among all entries in a given row and column, with the coupling strength held fixed as the matrix size grows. Although the bulk spectral moments remain those of the pure semicircle law, we show that the underlying correlation matrix decomposes into a vanishing bulk together with a countable family of outlier eigenvalues that, at fixed rank $k$, converge to the singular values of a compact Volterra (cumulative-sum) integral operator -- obtained in closed form via the classical Karhunen--Loève expansion of Brownian motion and confirmed numerically to better than one percent across the top twenty such values. Each singular value drives an independent Baik--Ben Arous--Péché (BBP) transition as the coupling strength increases, producing an evenly spaced, discrete hierarchy of critical points -- rather than a single transition -- at each of which one further eigenvalue detaches from the semicircle edge, in close agreement with direct diagonalization. We show that this mechanism generalizes to a broader family of correlation structures, with the critical hierarchy in every case set by the spectrum of an associated compact integral operator.

Abstract

Since its discovery in 2008, iron-based superconductors (FeSCs) have become a central platform for exploring high-temperature superconductivity in multiband, electron-correlated materials. This review focuses on major developments over the past decade or so, emphasizing experimental advances, pairing mechanisms, and emerging applications. Structural tuning through chemical substitution, pressure, and epitaxial growth enables precise control of the electronic, magnetic, and superconducting ground states, thereby revealing their interplay. In particular, the electronic nematic phase and stripe-type antiferromagnetic order-often coexisting or competing-are central to understanding the phase diagrams. Spin waves in magnetically ordered parent compounds and spin excitations (fluctuations) in doped superconductors are extensively characterized by inelastic neutron scattering. While high-energy spin excitations in doped superconductors retain substantial spectral weight across a wide energy range reminiscent of spin waves in their undoped parents, the low-energy response reveals a collective spin excitation termed ``resonance'' coupled to superconductivity. The momentum structure of superconductivity-induced resonance provides strong evidence for sign-changing pairing in many FeSCs, while disorder effects, orbital-fluctuation scenarios, quasiparticle damping, and compound-dependent gap structures indicate that $s_{\pm}$, $s_{++}$, nodal $s$, $d$-wave, and multicomponent states must be discussed in a material-specific framework. Advances in thin-film growth, intercalation chemistry, and interface engineering-particularly in FeSe-based systems-have enabled enhanced $T_{c}$ and novel device geometries. With high upper critical fields, moderate anisotropy, and improving current densities, FeSCs continue to drive both fundamental insight and technological applications in superconductivity.
