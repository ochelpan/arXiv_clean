Authors: Shih-Lung Yu, Ming-Kang Ho, Tai-Yue Li, Sheng Yun Wu

Summary. This paper proposes using Matrix Product States (MPS) as a scalable computational backend for simulating Variational Quantum Circuits designed for image super-resolution. It demonstrates that MPS overcomes the memory and computational bottlenecks faced by statevector or exact tensor network methods when scaling up input sizes. The results confirm MPS's utility, showing that shallow circuits can achieve optimal performance metrics for SR tasks.

Why it may be interesting. The comparison between different tensor network simulation techniques (Statevector, Exact TN, MPS) is highly relevant to understanding resource scaling in quantum machine learning algorithms applied to physical problems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The work addresses the challenge of simulating quantum feature extraction for image super-resolution (SR) using Variational Quantum Circuits (VQCs) when input sizes exceed the practical limits of statevector or exact tensor network simulations.

Main result. Matrix Product States (MPS) provide a scalable simulation backend, successfully performing forward feature extraction on up to 16x16 inputs where other methods fail; furthermore, shallow circuits ($L=1$) achieved the best reconstruction quality metrics for SR tasks.

Method. The authors developed and benchmarked an MPS simulation framework to approximate the expectation values of Adaptive Non-Local Observable VQCs (ANO-VQCs) used in image super-resolution.

Model / system. The model involves mapping low-resolution pixels to qubits, encoding them via RY rotations/CNOTs, and extracting quantum features using trainable Hermitian observables ($\langle H_q angle$). The simulation is performed on the MPS representation of the quantum state $|\psi angle$.

Key observables. Loss (MSE + LPIPS), PSNR, SSIM, Mean Absolute Error (MAE) comparing MPS to Exact TN.

Important parameters / regimes. Input size ($2 imes2$ up to $16 imes16$), Circuit Depth ($L=1$ to $4$), and MPS Bond Dimension ($\chi$).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary approximation is using the MPS ansatz, which requires increasing bond dimension $\chi$ with circuit depth $L$ to maintain accuracy relative to Exact TN contraction.

Figures summary. Figures compare simulation runtimes (Statevector vs. Exact TN vs. MPS) across varying input sizes; another figure illustrates how required bond dimension ($\chi$) increases with circuit depth ($L$) for fixed inputs.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the ANO-VQC framework for SR, then compares the computational scaling of Statevector, Exact TN, and MPS methods. It analyzes the accuracy vs. cost trade-off by sweeping $\chi$ for a fixed input size, concluding with performance metrics on a full SR task.

Abstract

This work presents a matrix product state (MPS) simulation framework for adaptive non-local observable variational quantum circuits (ANO-VQCs) in image super-resolution (SR) beyond the practical limits of statevector simulation. Runtime benchmarks on a single NVIDIA RTX 4070 GPU show that, under the tested shallow-circuit setting, MPS completes ANO-VQC forward feature extraction for individual inputs up to 16 x 16 pixels (256 qubits), whereas statevector simulation encounters a memory bottleneck at 6 x 6 inputs (36 qubits) and exact tensor-network (Exact TN) contraction becomes computationally impractical beyond 12 x 12 inputs (144 qubits). For a fixed 7 x 7 input (49 qubits), a bond-dimension sweep over depths L = 1 to L = 4 shows that the required MPS bond dimension increases with circuit depth. Using Exact TN contraction as the reference, the bond dimension required for near-exact agreement increases from chi = 2 at L = 1 to chi = 16 at L = 4. Finally, 7 x 7 to 28 x 28 Fashion-MNIST SR training with chi = 16 shows that the shallow L = 1 model achieves the lowest loss and best LPIPS, PSNR, and SSIM among the tested depths. These results highlight MPS as a scalable and controllable simulation backend for ANO-VQC image SR and as a practical tool for studying large-scale quantum algorithms.

Summary. This theoretical paper investigates the mathematical constraints on anyon models in 2+1 dimensions, focusing on the associativity data encoded in F-symbols. The authors prove that these symbols can be 'inherently complex'—meaning their non-real phases are unavoidable—by analyzing specific group representation categories lacking charge-conjugation symmetry. This deepens our understanding of topological order beyond simple braiding statistics.

Why it may be interesting. This work pushes the boundaries of topological quantum field theory by showing that simple invariants (like modular data) are insufficient, requiring deeper analysis of fusion associativity structures for a complete physical description.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The central question is whether the associativity data (F-symbols) governing anyon fusion rules must be real-valued, or if they can possess inherent complex phases.

Main result. The authors demonstrate that inherently complex F-symbols do exist in unitary premodular categories, exemplified by $ ext{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_7 times\mathbb{Z}_3)$ and $ ext{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_5 times\mathbb{Z}_4)$.

Method. The analysis involves calculating gauge-invariant quantities built from F-symbols using representation theory techniques applied to specific group extensions, comparing them against known symmetries like charge-conjugation.

Model / system. The study concerns anyons in $2+1$ dimensions, mathematically modeled by unitary braided spherical fusion categories (UMTCs), specifically analyzing the representations of semidirect products of cyclic groups ($ ext{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_n times \mathbb{Z}_m)$).

Key observables. F-symbols ($\mathcal{F}$), R-symbols, and derived gauge-invariant quantities whose non-real nature proves inherent complexity.

Important parameters / regimes. The structure of the underlying group extensions (e.g., $\mathbb{Z}_7 times\mathbb{Z}_3$), which dictates the fusion rules and symmetry properties.

Assumptions / limitations. The results are consistent with the converse of a statement linking real F-symbols to the existence of a charge-conjugation symmetry, and the examples studied lack this symmetry.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the definitions of F- and R-symbols, showing how they govern associativity (trivalent vertices) and braiding in the topological model.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by establishing that modular data is insufficient for full classification, then develops methods to test for inherent complexity using tailored invariants, culminating in explicit calculations for specific group representations ($ ext{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_7 times\mathbb{Z}_3)$ and $ ext{Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_5 times\mathbb{Z}_4)$) that yield complex results.

Abstract

Anyons in $2+1$ dimensions are not only characterized by exotic braiding statistics but also by intricate fusion properties. Two anyons may fuse into multiple topological charge sectors, and associativity of fusing three anyons to produce a fixed charge sector is governed by $F$-symbols. While braiding invariants, such as the modular data, are typically complex valued, a complete description of general anyon models requires understanding the arithmetic properties of its fusion associativity data as well. The $F$-symbols for many of the most common $2+1$d topological orders, including all Abelian anyon models as well as Fibonacci and Ising anyons, can be made real valued. We show this phenomenon is not universal by exhibiting braided fusion categories whose $F$-symbols cannot be made real. We call such $F$-symbols \lq\lq inherently complex." The examples we study lack a charge-conjugation symmetry and our results are therefore consistent with the converse of a statement proved in a companion work linking real $F$-symbols in braided fusion categories with the existence of a suitable charge-conjugation symmetry. We analyse the smallest-rank braided fusion categories we know of with inherently complex $F$-symbols: ${\rm Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_7\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_3)$ and ${\rm Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_5\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_4)$. Consequently, the corresponding $\mathcal Z({\rm Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_7\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_3))$ and $\mathcal Z({\rm Rep}(\mathbb{Z}_5\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_4))$ anyon models also have inherently complex $F$-symbols. Our presentation connects these examples with recent results on classifying anyons beyond modular data.

Abstract

Conformal field theory (CFT) offers a modern viewpoint for understanding phase transitions. However, directly accessing the conformal algebra and microscopically uncovering the emergent conformal symmetry, especially in higher dimensions, remains a significant challenge. Motivated by recent advances in revealing CFT features via the fuzzy two-sphere, here we generalize this approach to higher dimensions and aim to expose the conformality at the (3+1)-D quantum critical point. Together with prior advances, this work establishes a new pathway for the microscopic study of emergent conformality in higher-dimensional phase transitions.

Summary. The paper proposes using Quantum Topological Data Analysis (qTDA) via Pauli Correlation Encoding (PCE) to detect financial stress by estimating topological features ($eta_k$) from stock market returns. It demonstrates that PCE offers a shallow and robust quantum approach compared to other methods for this task. While promising, the authors note that achieving full generalization across different economic crises remains an open challenge.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core problem is developing a depth-efficient quantum algorithm to estimate Betti numbers ($eta_k$) from complex financial time series data, which classically requires computationally expensive diagonalization of large combinatorial Laplacians.

Main result. The authors successfully adapted Pauli Correlation Encoding (PCE) for this task, showing that while the method achieves shallow circuits and polynomial gradient decay, its current implementation relies on a classical warm-start to recover topological invariants exactly.

Method. They reformulate Betti number estimation as a continuous-PCE Rayleigh-quotient minimization. This variational quantum circuit approach encodes necessary combinatorial data into qubits using Pauli correlators, bypassing the deep circuits required by standard Quantum Phase Estimation (QPE).

Model / system. The system is derived from S&P 500 returns time series, processed via Takens embedding and Vietoris–Rips filtration to construct a point cloud whose topology is characterized by Betti numbers ($eta_k$). Important parameters / regimes. Number of qubits ($n$), correlation order ($\kappa$), noise parameter ($p$), and time-series window size (e.g., 2007-2009).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary limitation is that the quantum recovery of $eta_1$ requires warm-starting from a classical null-space surrogate, suggesting the bottleneck lies in the optimization landscape rather than the encoding itself.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the need for quantum TDA due to classical computational bottlenecks in calculating Betti numbers from market data. It then details the novel adaptation of PCE for this purpose, comparing it favorably against QPE methods based on circuit depth and noise resilience. Abstract

We present, to our knowledge, the first adaptation of Pauli Correlation Encoding (PCE) to quantum topological data analysis, reformulating Betti number estimation as a depth-efficient variational optimization over a compressed qubit register. From a Takens embedding and Vietoris--Rips filtration of S&P~500 returns, we extract combinatorial Laplacians and recast null-space counting as a continuous-PCE Rayleigh-quotient minimization with variational deflation, encoding $n_k$ simplex indices into $O(n_k^{1/κ})$ qubits with shallow, ancilla-free circuits. Because the resulting loss is rational rather than bilinear in the correlators, the barren-plateau bound of~\cite{Sciorilli25} does not transfer; empirically the gradient variance decays only polynomially, with no exponential barren plateau, over $n=4$--$12$ qubits. On the real market Laplacians ($β_1=1$--$22$), warm-starting from a classical null-space surrogate allows PCE-VQE to recover $β_1$ exactly at every scale, placing the obstacle in the optimisation landscape rather than the encoding. Chronologically split classification gives in-regime ROC AUC $0.818$, but out-of-distribution evaluation on the 2020 COVID shock and 2022 rate cycle (AUC $0.009$, $0.515$) shows the calibration does not generalize across crisis regimes.

Summary. This paper presents the Clustered Synthesis Tree (CST), a novel framework designed to build deep-circuit quantum oracles for solving SAT problems efficiently. By grouping related clauses into parallel clusters, CST significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods across multiple benchmarks. The results demonstrate massive reductions in circuit depth, proving its utility for resource-constrained quantum computation.

Main problem. The core challenge is synthesizing quantum oracles for SAT (CNF) formulas with minimal circuit depth while operating under strict constraints on available ancilla qubits.

Main result. The proposed Clustered Synthesis Tree (CST) framework achieves dramatic reductions in oracle circuit depth, showing speedups of up to 43.2x over state-of-the-art methods on standard benchmarks.

Method. It introduces CST, a depth-oriented synthesis tree that groups individual clause leaves into clusters to expose instance-dependent parallelism, which is then compiled using the ClausePack oracle and SeedGrow heuristic.

Model / system. The work models quantum oracles for SAT problems, utilizing the Hierarchical Recursive Synthesis-Evaluation (HRSE) framework as a structural backbone. The system involves mapping the CNF formula onto a computation tree structure constrained by qubit availability.

Key observables. Circuit Depth ($D(\cdot)$), Ancilla Qubit Budget ($a_q$), and the resulting speedup ratio compared to baseline methods.

Important parameters / regimes. The number of variables ($n$), clauses ($m$), clause width ($k$, e.g., 3-CNF, 4-CNF), and the available ancilla qubits.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that depth reduction can be achieved by exploiting clause-level parallelism through intelligent clustering, which leads to an NP-complete scheduling problem (ACCS-D).

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the limitations of existing methods, then introduces the CST framework which refines the HRSE/ASDT backbone. Abstract

Quantum oracles are a common building block of many quantum algorithms, where circuit depth is a primary cost that directly affects overall performance. Synthesizing oracles for SAT (CNF) formulas under a limited ancilla budget, however, tends to yield deep circuits, as existing methods underexploit clause-level parallelism. In this work, we present the Clustered Synthesis Tree (CST), a depth-oriented framework whose core idea is to group the individual clause leaves of a hierarchical synthesis tree into clusters, exposing instance-dependent clause-level parallelism under ancilla constraints. CST comprises three parts: the clause-grouping problem it induces, which we formulate as an ancilla-constrained scheduling problem and prove NP-complete in general, is addressed by SeedGrow, a polynomial-time $O(m^2 k)$ heuristic; ClausePack, a reversible oracle that evaluates a cluster's clauses in parallel at only a logarithmic-depth overhead; and CST-Map, which compiles the clustered tree into an executable SAT-oracle. On random $4$-CNF under the same ancilla budgets, CST reduces the oracle's circuit depth over the state-of-the-art (SOTA) baseline by $68\%$--$94\%$. On the standard SATLIB benchmarks, CST achieves about a $2.6\times$--$43.2\times$ reduction over the SOTA baseline, with the largest gains under dense variable sharing, and matches the baseline's maximum-budget depth using only $3.7\%$--$20\%$ of its ancilla qubits. A Grover-search resource estimate shows the advantage carries over to the full algorithm, reducing total circuit depth by $70\%$--$89\%$.

Summary. The paper introduces HSF-S, a novel compiler and accelerator framework designed to efficiently emulate quantum circuits using the Hybrid Schrödinger-Feynman (HSF) method. It minimizes runtime bottlenecks by optimizing circuit structure via path-cost modeling and reordering, and further accelerates execution using a custom FPGA-based processor. This significantly improves the practical tractability of exact HSF simulation.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the computational bottlenecks in simulating complex quantum circuits by developing specialized compiler optimizations and custom hardware accelerators tailored to the path-centric nature of HSF simulation, which is crucial for scaling quantum algorithm testing.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The practical runtime of Hybrid Schrödinger-Feynman (HSF) quantum circuit emulation is limited by exponential path growth from cross-boundary two-qubit gates, which existing simulators do not handle efficiently.

Main result. The proposed HSF-S framework reduces the effective path cost by up to 90.0% and provides a dedicated hardware accelerator yielding up to 4.34x speedup on compiled workloads.

Method. HSF-S is a compiler-accelerator co-designed framework that lowers circuits to an HSF basis, applies dependency-preserving reordering, and uses discounted-gain SWAP insertion based on a rank-aware effective path-cost model.

Model / system. The paper focuses on exact quantum circuit emulation using the Hybrid Schrödinger-Feynman (HSF) model, which partitions computation into local (Schrödinger) and cross-boundary (Feynman) evaluations. The implementation is prototyped on a stand-alone RISC-V core integrated onto an AMD VCU118 FPGA.

Key observables. Effective path cost ($C_{eff}$), amplitude matching to reference values, and hardware speedup factor.

Important parameters / regimes. Cross-boundary gate types (e.g., controlled gates $ ightarrow$ rank 2, SWAP $ ightarrow$ rank 4); the fixed bipartition of qubits into slices A or B.

Abstract

Hybrid Schrodinger-Feynman (HSF) simulation offers an attractive memory-path tradeoff for exact quantum-circuit emulation, but its practical runtime is often dominated by exponential path growth from cross-boundary two-qubit gates. Existing GPU and FPGA quantum simulators are largely optimized for full-state Schrodinger execution and therefore do not align well with HSF's path-centric workflow. This paper presents HSF-S, a compiler-accelerator co-designed framework for exact HSF-based quantum circuit emulation. HSF-S lowers input circuits to an HSF-compatible basis, formulates a rank-aware effective path-cost model, and applies dependency-preserving reordering together with discounted-gain SWAP insertion to suppress recurring cross-boundary interactions while preserving exact circuit semantics. A regression-free selector guarantees that the compiled circuit never increases effective path cost relative to the naive lowered baseline. We further design a dedicated HSF-S accelerator and execution flow, and integrate them into a stand-alone processor for efficient per-path dual-slice evaluation and final accumulation without materializing the full state vector. Across 56 benchmark circuits, HSF-S matches reference amplitudes to within floating-point precision, reduces effective path cost by up to 90.0%, and substantially improves practical tractability, including representative timeout-to-sub-second reductions under a 1-hour budget. On the resulting compiled workloads, the HSF-S processor prototype delivers up to 4.34x additional speedup.

Input-Aware Dynamic Backdoor Attack Against Quantum Neural Networks

Summary. This paper introduces Q-DIBA, the first input-aware dynamic backdoor attack specifically designed for Quantum Neural Networks (QNNs). It overcomes major theoretical hurdles in quantum machine learning—namely measurement collapse and state fluctuation—by using an ensemble density contrastive loss. The resulting attack is highly successful, stealthy, and resilient against several known defense mechanisms.

Why it may be interesting. The work directly addresses security vulnerabilities in quantum machine learning architectures by proposing a sophisticated, input-specific attack that circumvents common detection mechanisms based on fixed patterns or simple spectral analysis.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Quantum Neural Networks (QNNs) are vulnerable to backdoor attacks, and existing quantum backdoors use fixed triggers which are easily detectable by modern defenses.

Main result. The proposed Q-DIBA achieves high attack success rates while remaining stealthy against visual inspection, spectral signature detection, and fine-tuning, establishing a new threat model for secure QNN deployment.

Method. Q-DIBA jointly trains the QNN and an input-aware trigger generator using a three-mode mini-batch strategy and introduces an ensemble density contrastive loss operating on post-ansatz quantum states before measurement.

Model / system. The system is a Quantum Neural Network (QNN), which is a hybrid quantum-classical model composed of an encoding circuit, parameterized quantum circuits (PQCs), and a final measurement layer. The attack targets the parameters ($ heta$) governing these trainable unitary operations.

Key observables. Clean Model Accuracy (CA), Attack Success Rate (ASR), Cross-Trigger Accuracy (CTA), and performance after fine-tuning epochs.

Important parameters / regimes. The three training modes: clean samples, attack activation samples, and cross-trigger samples; the contrastive loss hinge distance ($d_{ ext{HS}}$).

Assumptions / limitations. It is assumed that averaging the density matrices ($ar{ ho}$) across samples within a mode provides a stable gradient for training the joint optimization.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the QNN structure (Encoding $ ightarrow$ PQC $ ightarrow$ Measurement) and show examples of fixed-trigger attacks. Tables summarize performance metrics like ASR, CTA, and resilience against fine-tuning across different epochs.

Paper structure. The paper first reviews QNNs and backdoor vulnerabilities, identifies the limitations of current quantum attack models (measurement collapse), proposes Q-DIBA with its novel loss function, validates it on MNIST/Fashion-MNIST, and finally demonstrates its robustness against state-of-the-art defenses.

Abstract

Quantum Neural Networks (QNNs) are a promising framework for quantum machine learning on near-term quantum devices, but their security risks remain insufficiently understood. Studies have shown that QNNs are vulnerable to backdoor attacks, yet existing quantum backdoors mostly rely on a fixed trigger shared by all poisoned inputs. This fixed-trigger design is a major weakness because many defenses detect or weaken the repeated patterns such triggers leave in data representations. Although input-aware dynamic backdoors have been studied in classical neural networks, transferring them to QNNs is difficult because quantum learning introduces new obstacles. In particular, measurement compresses the post-ansatz quantum state into a limited classical output, weakening supervision for a trigger generator, while individual density matrices fluctuate with the input and make per-sample contrastive learning unstable. Our work demonstrates a high tunability of superconductivity by uniaxial pressure.
