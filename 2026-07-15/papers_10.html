Summary. This research details the development of $ ext{NaBiF}_4: ext{Er}^{3+}, ext{Yb}^{3+}$ upconversion particles as advanced bio-markers. Detailed structure

Main problem. The challenge is achieving high-contrast, sub-diffraction bioimaging using upconversion particles without requiring high-power lasers in noisy biological media.

Main result. Bismuth-doped UCPs ($ ext{NaBiF}_4: ext{Er}^{3+}, ext{Yb}^{3+}$) are shown to be superior biomarkers due to enhanced quantum yield and the ability to achieve controllable PL quenching/enhancement via dual-wavelength excitation.

Method. The study combines optical characterization (PL spectroscopy, time-resolved measurements), physical modification (sol-gel coating, APTES functionalization), and modeling of energy transfer dynamics.

Model / system. The system is based on lanthanide-doped upconversion particles ($ ext{NaBiF}_4$) excited by near-infrared lasers ($975 ext{ nm}$ and $1064 ext{ nm}$). The PL dynamics are modeled using a simplified stimulated emission depletion process involving three energy levels.

Key observables. Quantum yield (3x overall, 4x red), systematic quenching/enhancement of PL by adjusting laser power, optical trapping stiffness ($\kappa$), and characteristic fluorescence lifetimes ($ au_1, au_2$).

Important parameters / regimes. Near-infrared excitation wavelengths ($975 ext{ nm}, 1064 ext{ nm}$), sub-diffraction particle thickness ($\sim 100 ext{ nm}$), and the relative powers of the two exciting lasers.

Abstract

Lanthanide-doped upconversion particles (UCPs) have revolutionized optical bioimaging platforms because of their excellent photostability, non-toxicity, and utilization of near-infrared excitation, which facilitates deep tissue penetration with negligible autofluorescence. However, it remains a challenge to achieve high-contrast and sub-diffraction imaging in noisy biological media, without using a high-power laser. Here, we report various protocols applied to bismuth-doped UCPs address some of these challenges. Compared to the photoluminescence (PL) emission of the regular Yttrium doped UCPs, we observe a three-fold increment in the quantum yield of the overall emission of bismuth-UCPs, and a four-fold increment, specifically, in red emission. Leveraging this advantage, we devise a protocol employing two infrared wavelengths, 975 nm and 1064 nm, to selectively control the PL emission. Interestingly, our results reveal two distinct regimes in which PL can be systematically quenched or enhanced, by adjusting the 975 nm laser power. We model the overall dynamics as a simplified stimulated emission depletion process involving three energy levels. In addition, the particle has a thickness under sub-diffraction, shows optical trapping ability, and potential of surface functionalization to enable specific conjugation with diverse biospecimens. These studies establish bismuth doped UCPs as an excellent candidate in accomplishing advanced biomarker operating with enhanced signal-to-noise ratio and sub-diffraction imaging capabilities.

Summary. This paper theoretically analyzes how fluctuations above the critical temperature ($T_c$) induce nonreciprocal electrical responses like dichroism and gyrotropy in 2D noncentrosymmetric superconductors. Using generalized Ginzburg-Landau theory, the authors show these effects critically depend on particle-hole asymmetry and require broken inversion/time-reversal symmetry. The results offer a pathway to probe superconducting dynamics using techniques like quantum noise spectroscopy.

Why it may be interesting. The explicit derivation of nonreciprocal responses from fluctuation effects provides a concrete theoretical link between broken symmetries (inversion/time-reversal) and measurable optical properties in superconductors, relevant to understanding exotic quantum states.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how superconducting fluctuations induce nonreciprocal dichroism and gyrotropy in two-dimensional, noncentrosymmetric superconductors.

Main result. These nonreciprocal effects require the simultaneous breaking of inversion and time-reversal symmetries and are dependent on particle-hole asymmetry. The reactive component (birefringence) shows a critical enhancement as temperature approaches $T_c$.

Method. The authors utilize time-dependent Ginzburg-Landau theory, calculating the Aslamazov-Larkin contribution to the gyrotropic conductivity in closed form.

Model / system. The model is a two-dimensional noncentrosymmetric superconductor, exemplified by gated transition metal dichalcogenides like $ ext{MoS}_2$. The theory incorporates particle-hole asymmetry and the cubic Lifshitz invariant of trigonal superconductors.

Key observables. Nonreciprocal conductivity (dichroism and gyrotropy), which are analyzed via even-in-$k$ ($\sigma_{ ext{even}}$) and odd-in-$k$ ($\sigma_{ ext{odd}}$) components.

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature relative to $T_c$, particle-hole asymmetry parameter ($\gamma_2$), and the frequency $\omega$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that nonreciprocal terms do not alter the leading order results for gyrotropy near $T_c$ (Appendix B).

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the frequency dependence of the derived fluctuation conductivity, showing distinct behaviors for the dissipative dichroism ($W(\varpi)$) and reactive birefringence ($V(\varpi)$).

Paper structure. The paper builds by first establishing the theoretical framework using TDGL theory, then calculating the $ ext{AL}$ contribution to gyrotropy, analyzing the symmetry dependence on particle-hole asymmetry ($\gamma_2$), and finally discussing experimental implications for probing superconducting dynamics.

Abstract

We study the spatially dispersive conductivity of a two-dimensional noncentrosymmetric superconductor, demonstrating that it acquires a nonreciprocal, odd-in-wavevector component from fluctuation-induced Cooper pairs above the critical temperature $T_c$. Utilizing time-dependent Ginzburg-Landau theory generalized to include particle-hole asymmetry and the cubic Lifshitz invariant of trigonal superconductors, we compute the Aslamazov-Larkin contribution to the gyrotropic conductivity in closed form, including its complete frequency dependence. The dissipative part describes nonreciprocal directional dichroism: it is odd in frequency and displays a nonmonotonic dependence, peaking at frequencies comparable to the decay rate of fluctuating Cooper pairs. Its Kramers-Kronig dual component describes gyrotropic birefringence, which remains finite in the static limit and is strongly enhanced as the temperature approaches $T_c$. Both effects require simultaneously broken inversion and time-reversal symmetries, are dependent on particle-hole asymmetry in close analogy to the fluctuation Hall effect, and trace to the same asymmetric Cooper-pair dispersion responsible for the superconducting diode effect and the giant magnetochiral anisotropy observed near $T_c$. This critical enhancement dominates over the smooth normal-state gyrotropy, which we evaluate for the same band model as a baseline. Finally, we frame our analysis within the context of gated transition metal dichalcogenides like MoS$_2$, discussing the implications for probing superconducting dynamics through nitrogen-vacancy-center quantum noise spectroscopy.

Summary. This paper develops a powerful theoretical framework to analyze how axion topological insulators respond to external electric and magnetic fields. By reformulating the problem using 'surface variables' instead of bulk descriptions, the authors derive solvable integral equations for induced surface charges and currents. This method allows for detailed numerical simulations on complex geometries like spheres and tori.

Why it may be interesting. While not directly focused on quantum information, the rigorous mathematical treatment of topological responses using reduced-dimensionality surface variable methods is highly relevant to modern condensed matter theory dealing with emergent electromagnetic properties in novel materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study focuses on determining the macroscopic quasi-static magnetoelectric response of axion topological materials, specifically topological insulators, when subjected to external static electric and magnetic fields.

Main result. The authors derive a set of integral equations governing effective surface charge and current densities, providing exact analytic solutions for axial-symmetric cases and developing a boundary finite element method for complex geometries.

Method. The core methodology involves reformulating the bulk field equations using 'surface variables' (effective surface charge/current), leading to linear integral equations that are solved numerically via a boundary finite element approach.

Model / system. The system is an axion topological material characterized by a sharp discontinuity in the axion field ($ heta$). The physics is governed by modified Maxwell's equations incorporating induced sources derived from $ abla heta$, leading to integral equations for surface variables.

Key observables. Effective surface charge density ($\omega_s$) and surface current density ($s_s$), as well as the resulting electric and magnetic fields.

Important parameters / regimes. Quasi-static regime, external static E and B fields, geometry (spheres, tori).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes a quasi-static limit and treats $ abla heta$ as localized at material interfaces, allowing the problem to be reduced to surface integral equations.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the calculated field distributions (E and B) and induced charges/currents for various geometries like tori and hollow spheres under external fields or point charges.

Paper structure. The paper follows a logical progression: establishing the Hamiltonian formulation, introducing surface variables to derive integral equations, presenting analytic solutions for simple cases, detailing the numerical boundary finite element method, and finally applying this framework to complex geometries.

Abstract

We study the response of axion topological materials under the presence of external static electric and magnetic fields. We focus on the macroscopic quasi-static magnetoelectric response of topological insulators. We use techniques based on surface variables that have been previously employed in soft condensed matter problems for the description of heterogeneous systems composed of multiple homogeneous materials. A complete description of the whole system is written in terms of surface degrees of freedom, which in this case correspond to effective surface charge and surface current densities. We obtain a set of integral equations satisfied by these variables. We present exact analytic solutions for axial-symmetric cases. We develop a numerical method for the solution of the integral equations by means of a boundary finite element method. We apply the numerical method to topological insulators with different geometries such as spheres, hollow spheres and toroidal surfaces. We show that that a variational principle can be used to recover the surface variables equations.

A minimal Hamiltonian was constructed that successfully reproduces the topology, captures the spin-orbit coupling (SOC)-driven splitting, and models the subsequent annihilation of Weyl nodes in momentum space. Model / system. The system is trigonal $ ext{PtBi}2$, modeled using a tight-binding approach incorporating orbital degrees of freedom (${p_x, p_y}$) and SOC terms. The Hamiltonian is built sector-by-sector to enforce crystal symmetries ($C$) across multiple coupled blocks.

Abstract

Weyl semimetals host topologically protected Fermi arcs on their surfaces, originating from the Chern number of the bulk Weyl nodes. In trigonal PtBi$_2$, superconducting signatures have been associated with the Fermi arcs. Theoretical descriptions of this surface superconductivity have so far relied on effective models that are not directly tied to the microscopic electronic structure of the material. In this work we develop a minimal description of the low-energy bands guided by density functional theory calculations and fully constrained by the relevant crystalline and time-reversal symmetries. The model captures the evolution of the Weyl nodes with spin-orbit coupling, including node annihilations, and is able to describe the spin-momentum locking of the surface Fermi arcs. It reproduces the orbital content and the band topology of PtBi$_2$ and provides a starting point for further studies.

Summary. This paper analyzes Akhmediev breathers in the Manakov system, challenging the assumption that breather spectra must be governed by linearly unstable modes. Through numerical simulation and detailed analysis of nonlinear forcing, the authors prove that a spectrally stable harmonic can spontaneously become dominant at the breather's peak. This mechanism is driven purely by four-wave mixing, offering new insights into nonlinear wave dynamics across various physical platforms.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete example of how strong nonlinearity can fundamentally alter the spectral dynamics of wave packets, suggesting that energy transfer mechanisms (like four-wave mixing) can overcome linear stability constraints in physical systems relevant to quantum optics and Bose-Einstein condensates.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates whether an Akhmediev breather (AB) can be spectrally dominated by a frequency component that is linearly stable, contrary to the usual expectation derived from modulation instability theory.

Main result. The authors demonstrate that in the Manakov system, a stable harmonic component ($n=\pm 2$) can spontaneously emerge and dominate the spectrum of an AB peak, driven entirely by four-wave mixing from initially unstable modes.

Method. The analysis combines linear stability theory (calculating MI gain) with numerical simulations using the Manakov equation to track spectral energy distribution over time.

Model / system. The system is modeled by the Manakov nonlinear Schrödinger equation, governing two coupled wave components ($\psi^{(1)}, \psi^{(2)}$). The analysis focuses on constructing and simulating a third-order Akhmediev breather solution.

Key observables. Dominance measure $D(j)$ (quantifying stable harmonic contribution), Fourier amplitudes $|A_n|$, and the nonlinear forcing term derived from four-wave mixing.

Important parameters / regimes. The wavenumber offset $eta$ is critical, with a specific window ($1 \le eta < 1.5$) identified where this spectral dominance occurs.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the validity of the Manakov system description and relies on isolating the nonlinear forcing term to quantify its source (four-wave mixing).

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the transition of the dominance measure $D(j)$ towards unity for specific parameters, show the time evolution of Fourier amplitudes peaking at stable harmonics, and confirm that four-wave mixing accounts for most of the nonlinear driving.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the theoretical conflict (stable vs. unstable spectral control) to constructing the AB solution, performing linear stability analysis, running numerical simulations on the Manakov system, and finally quantifying the mechanism via nonlinear forcing analysis.

Abstract

In the scalar nonlinear Schrodinger equation, an Akhmediev breather (AB) is dominated by a frequency that lies inside the modulation instability gain band. This exactly correspondence between instability and breathers is challenged in vector systems such as the Manakov system, where the gain spectrum splits into disconnected lobes separated by stable gaps. We analytically and numerically construct an AB that is generated by unstable modes but is spectrally dominated by a stable harmonic at its peak. Numerical simulations starting from a simple continuous wave background perturbed only by the unstable harmonics confirm that the stable component emerges spontaneously and becomes dominant without any initial seed. We identify the precise parameter window in which this phenomenon occurs and show that this passive amplification of the linearly stable component is driven by four-wave mixing, which accounts for 96% of the nonlinear forcing. Given the universality of the Manakov system across nonlinear physics, from nonlinear optics to ultracold quantum gases, these results open an experimentally accessible new perspective on breather dynamics, one in which linearly stable frequencies can dominate.

Lifshitz-Kosevich Theory of Anomalous Landau Levels in Topological Flat Bands

Summary. This work develops an extension of Lifshitz-Kosevich theory to describe quantum oscillations arising from anomalous Landau levels found in topological flat bands. By analyzing the temperature dependence of these oscillations, the authors demonstrate that the thermal damping factor yields a measurable effective mass directly proportional to the system's underlying quantum geometric structure.

Why it may be interesting. The direct link established between measurable transport properties (thermal damping of oscillations) and fundamental geometric invariants (the quantum metric) provides a powerful new experimental probe for characterizing topological band structures in condensed matter systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To determine how quantum oscillations persist in topological flat bands, which naively suggest thermal suppression due to zero group velocity, and whether the thermal damping factor can reveal information about the underlying quantum geometry.

Main result. The paper derives an LK theory for anomalous Landau levels (ALLs), showing that their persistent oscillations yield a finite, field-dependent effective mass whose thermal damping directly measures the quantum metric of the flat band.

Method. The authors adapt the Lifshitz-Kosevich formalism to analyze the grand potential and magnetization oscillations in the presence of an anomalous LL spacing. They fit temperature dependence using standard LK forms to extract effective masses.

Model / system. The study uses a minimal model Hamiltonian describing topological flat bands, relevant to moiré materials, which possess Time-Reversal and inversion symmetries. The analysis is performed under a perpendicular magnetic field at fixed carrier density.

Key observables. Magnetization ($M$) oscillations as a function of $1/B$, the extracted effective mass ($m^*_{ ext{eff}}$), and the thermal damping factor derived from the fundamental harmonic amplitude ($A_1(T)$).

Important parameters / regimes. Magnetic field ($B$), temperature ($T$), carrier density ($ ho$), and parameters related to the quantum metric tensor.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on a minimal model with perfectly flat topological bands, and it treats the local LK fit as a diagnostic tool for measuring the effective mass derived from thermal damping.

Figures summary. Figures show band dispersion, Berry curvature distribution, LL spectra, and magnetization oscillations ($M$ vs $1/B$) for both normal and anomalous LLs. Subsequent figures compare extracted effective masses against analytical expectations.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the problem by contrasting conventional Landau quantization with flat bands. It then adapts the LK formalism to derive oscillatory terms ($\delta\Omega$), analyzes the thermal damping factor ($R_T(p)$), and finally extracts the field-dependent effective mass, linking it theoretically to the quantum metric.

Abstract

In conventional metals, quantum oscillations arise from Landau quantization of Fermi-surface cyclotron orbits, whose dynamics are governed by the Fermi velocity and cyclotron effective mass within Lifshitz-Kosevich (LK) theory. A perfectly flat band, by contrast, has vanishing group velocity, which would naively imply an infinite cyclotron mass and complete thermal suppression of quantum oscillations. Yet topological flat bands can support anomalous Landau levels (LLs) whose finite-field spacing is generated by quantum geometry rather than band curvature, allowing quantum oscillations to persist. This work addresses how such anomalous flat-band LLs behave within the LK framework and whether their thermal damping can reveal quantum geometric information. Using a minimal model with exactly flat topological bands, we derive an LK theory for these anomalous LLs and analyze fixed-density magnetization oscillations. The resulting oscillations exhibit a finite LK effective mass that is substantially larger than the normal-band value and possesses a strong magnetic-field dependence. In the weak-field limit, this anomalous mass reflects the quantum geometric origin of the LL spacing and scales inversely with both the magnetic field and the trace of the quantum metric. Thus, thermal damping of flat-band quantum oscillations directly measures the quantum metric, establishing quantum oscillations as a probe to flat-band quantum geometry.

Abstract

Understanding friction at single-asperity contacts is essential for bridging the gap between nanoscale structural superlubricity and realistic tribological systems dominated by Hertzian contact geometry. In this work, we combine atomistic simulations and a modified continuum model to investigate the onset of sliding at crystalline SiO$_2$/SiO$_2$ interfaces. Interfacial sliding potential energy surfaces (ISPES) are computed to determine the load-dependent shear strength and minimal-scale sliding (MSS) friction. Both quantities exhibit linear dependence on normal pressure below 3 GPa, and have non-zero values at zero pressure. Incorporating these parameters, we extend the classical Mindlin model by including adhesion and nanoscale load effects, allowing us to describe the stick to slip transition under realistic Hertzian stress distributions. The model shows that nonuniform pressure distributions substantially lower the effective static friction, and oscillatory-shear experiments on graphene-passivated contacts reproduce both the predicted stiffness-collapse signature and, in the passivated limit, the adhesion-limited shear strength obtained from simulation, supporting the model's relevance to real micro-asperity tribology.

Summary. This paper introduces a novel dual spectral-domain neural network that predicts the optical scattering T-matrix for complex 3D particles. Model / system. The system involves electromagnetic scattering from compact irregular particles. The paper introduces the dual spectral model, details the training process using a spectral-token Transformer on ground-truth T-matrices derived from boundary element methods, demonstrates prediction accuracy for various angles/polarizations, and tests generalization to out-of-distribution shapes.

Abstract

Rapid prediction of optical scattering from arbitrarily shaped three-dimensional particles is important for particle optics and photonic characterization, but remains challenging because of the large variability of complex morphologies and the strong angular dependence of their scattering responses. To address both issues, a dual spectral-domain neural scattering model is introduced in which morphology and scattering are represented in physically ordered bases: particle geometry is compressed into only 256 spherical-harmonic coefficients, and the optical response is encoded by the complex T-matrix in a spherical-vector-wave basis. The morphology spectrum replaces high-dimensional Euclidean geometry representations, such as voxel grids, point clouds, or meshes, with a compact ordered descriptor, while the T-matrix represents a geometry-determined scattering operator that can be queried for different incidence directions, polarizations, and observation angles. A spectral-token Transformer trained on 50{,}000 irregular particles at 1064~nm maps the morphology spectrum directly to the T-matrix. The predicted operators recover modal structure and reproduce full-angle differential scattering maps and incidence-angle scans. Generalization to out-of-distribution synthetic shapes and natural sand-particle morphologies shows that the dual spectral architecture learns an intrinsic relation from the geometry spectrum to multipolar scattering. This establishes spectral-domain operator learning as a compact route for reusable, angle- and polarization-resolved optical scattering prediction of complex 3D particles.

Abstract

We report a transverse-field muon-spin rotation/relaxation ($μ$SR) study of the internal-field distribution in the mixed state of LaFeAsO$_{0.89}$F$_{0.11}$ and LaFeAsO$_{0.75}$H$_{0.25}$, representative of the first (SC1) and second (SC2) superconducting domes of the LaFeAsO$_{1-x}M_x$ ($M={\rm F,H}$) family, respectively. Below the superconducting transition temperature $T_{\rm c}$, the linewidth of the internal-field distribution increases in both samples, indicating the formation of a vortex lattice. Above $T_{\rm c}$, the linewidth remains field dependent and increases approximately linearly with field, consistent with broadening of the powder spectrum caused by an anisotropic Knight shift. After subtraction of this normal-state contribution, the superconducting linewidth $σ_{\rm sc}$ exhibits qualitatively different field dependences in the two samples. At 4K, the SC1 ($x_{\rm F}=0.11$) sample shows the expected monotonic decrease with increasing field, whereas the SC2 ($x_{\rm H}=0.25$) sample develops a pronounced local maximum near 3T. A contour representation of $σ_{\rm sc}(T,H)$ further reveals a ridge of local maxima whose field position, $H_{σ,\max}(T)$, shifts to lower fields upon warming and disappears near $T_{\rm c}$. The anomalous field evolution observed in the SC2 sample is consistent with an additional field-induced contribution associated with enhanced Pauli-paramagnetic effects, highlighting the distinct electronic character of the two superconducting domes.

Abstract

Superconductivity was recently observed in twisted WSe2 and MoTe2, raising a central question: is the pairing driven by electronic correlations, by phonons, or by both? Answering it requires determining the electron-phonon coupling (EPC) in these moiré semiconductors, whose calculation in realistic supercells of thousands of atoms lies beyond the reach of direct first-principles methods. Here we combine filling-dependent Raman spectroscopy with machine-learning first-principles calculations to obtain the EPC mode by mode in supercells of up to tens of thousands of atoms. Raman reveals only a few moiré phonons whose frequencies shift strongly with filling; we trace this to an interference selection rule: a phonon couples strongly only when its displacement texture matches the static lattice-reconstruction pattern, and is otherwise suppressed by destructive interference. The rule selects the low- and high-frequency breathing modes seen in Raman and makes the coupling peak at large twist angles, near those at which superconductivity appears. Lattice-reconstruction interference thus emerges as an organizing principle for moiré EPC, pointing to a substantial, potentially dominant, phonon contribution to large-angle pairing.

Abstract

We report on the synthesis of infinite-layer Nd1-xEuxNiO2 (0<x<0.7) thin films using pulsed laser deposition (PLD) followed by topotactic reduction with CaH2. Resistivity measurements on these films reveal a superconducting dome within the doping range 0.2<x<0.5, which is wider than that achieved by molecular beam epitaxy and comparable to that obtained by chemical synthesis. The x=0.3 PLD film exhibits the optimal superconducting transition temperature Tc~31 K, much higher than those grown by other vacuum epitaxial techniques. This result indicates that PLD is an ideal approach for fabricating high-quality, high-Tc Nd1-xEuxNiO2 superconducting films. Magneto-transport measurements reveal robust field-enhanced and re-entrant superconductivity in both underdoped and overdoped regimes. At low temperatures just above the onset Tc, the Hall resistance exhibits nonlinear behavior, which may originate from magnetic impurity scattering. These results highlight the crucial role of magnetic rare-earth Eu2+ ions in producing the exotic physical properties of the infinite-layer nickelates.
