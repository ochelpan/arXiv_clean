Abstract

Unified theory of classical and quantum semiparametric efficiency

Summary. This paper introduces a unified geometric theory to calculate statistical efficiency bounds for estimating parameters when the underlying model is high-dimensional (semiparametric). By generalizing classical and quantum concepts like the Cramér-Rao and Helstrom bounds, it offers a powerful tool to determine fundamental limits on measurement precision in fields ranging from general sensing to advanced optical imaging.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a powerful mathematical toolkit for quantifying ultimate measurement precision limits in scenarios where many parameters are unknown, which is crucial for advanced quantum sensing and metrology beyond simple parameter estimation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to develop a unified statistical theory for efficiency bounds applicable to both classical and quantum semiparametric models.

Main result. It provides a general framework that unifies the Cramér-Rao and Helstrom bounds, allowing computation of classical and quantum limits for complex estimation tasks like optical imaging.

Method. The approach is highly abstract and geometric, defining concepts like the parameter tangent space and information semimetric to generalize existing statistical bounds beyond finite dimensions.

Model / system. The theory is applied broadly, covering general classical and quantum models (Gaussian, Poisson fields) with specific applications in coherent and incoherent optical imaging problems.

Key observables. Statistical efficiency bounds, the output efficient influence ($ abla_{ ext{out}}eta$), and classical/quantum limits to imaging resolution.

Important parameters / regimes. High-dimensional unknown parameters ($ heta$) summarized by a finite-dimensional parameter of interest ($eta( heta)$).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is the need for minimal assumptions about high-dimensional parameters, and mathematically, it relies on differentiability and the existence/properties of certain operators like $F^{ ext{push}}$.

Paper structure. The paper builds from fundamental abstract concepts (parameter tangent space, information semimetric) to specific applications in classical statistics (e.g., Gaussian fields), quantum estimation theory (Helstrom bounds), and finally applies the unified formalism to complex physical systems like optical imaging.

Abstract

In classical and quantum statistics, high-dimensional unknown parameters are abundant and it is often prudent to make minimal assumptions about them using so-called semiparametric models. To attack a wide range of semiparametric problems in one broad stroke, we present a unified treatment of statistical efficiency for classical and quantum semiparametric models, generalizing the Cramér-Rao and Helstrom bounds beyond finite-dimensional parameters. We introduce the fundamental concepts in abstract and geometric terms before applying them to many examples, covering general classical and quantum models as well as the paradigmatic special cases of Gaussian and Poisson fields. We give an in-depth treatment of channels in the semiparametric efficiency theory and advocate the use of the singular value decomposition to elucidate the statistical effects of channels. To demonstrate the utility of the formalism, we apply it to coherent and incoherent optical imaging problems, assuming an arbitrary field or intensity on the object plane without parametric assumptions. Our formalism enables us to compute classical and quantum limits to coherent and incoherent imaging resolution in statistical terms. For subdiffraction incoherent imaging, we demonstrate that spatial-mode demultiplexing can be far superior to direct imaging in estimating generalized Fourier coefficients and come closer to the quantum limits. We envision our theory becoming an essential tool for both classical and quantum statistics with useful applications to sensing and imaging, whenever minimal assumptions about a high-dimensional parameter should be made.

Ideal Bands in Tight-Binding Models

Authors: Lexu Zhao, Yang Ge, Shinsei Ryu, Jiabin Yu
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.20624v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper rigorously analyzes the concept of 'ideal bands' by examining when their associated topological functional saturates a theoretical lower bound. The authors provide an explicit construction for such bands under conditions of exponentially decaying hopping but prove that these ideal states are impossible to realize in models with only finite-range interactions. This establishes fundamental limitations on the topology achievable in lattice Hamiltonians.

Why it may be interesting. The mathematical constraints derived from topological invariants like the Chern number place severe restrictions on the realizability of specific band structures in physical models, which is fundamental knowledge for designing topological materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates 'ideal bands,' specifically Chern-ideal and Wilson-loop-ideal bands, defined by the saturation of a topological lower bound on their Dirichlet functional.

Main result. The authors prove that isolated Chern-ideal bands with non-zero Chern number cannot exist in finite-band tight-binding models utilizing only finite-range hopping. This conclusion extends to critical and Wilson-loop-ideal bands under similar conditions.

Method. The proof heavily relies on complex analysis, specifically using the identity theorem applied to functions derived from Bloch Hamiltonians defined over the Brillouin zone.

Model / system. The system is a finite-band tight-binding model defined on a 2D Bravais lattice with conventional translation symmetries. The Hamiltonian structure is analyzed via its momentum-space representation, $h(\mathbf{k})$.

Key observables. Chern number (Ch), Quantum metric ($\mathbf{g}_{\mathbf{k}}$), Berry curvature ($F_{\mathbf{k}}$), and the Dirichlet functional.

Important parameters / regimes. The nature of hopping decay (exponentially decaying vs. finite-range) and the relative positions of embedded orbitals ($\Delta\xi$).

Assumptions / limitations. Conventional lattice translation symmetry is assumed; the proof for non-existence relies on assuming $ ext{Ch} > 0$ or that the Berry curvature does not diverge.

Paper structure. The paper first provides an explicit construction of Chern-ideal bands with Ch=1 using exponentially decaying hopping. It then develops a rigorous proof, utilizing complex analysis techniques (like extending variables to $\mathbb{C}^2$), demonstrating that the requirement for non-zero Chern number leads to contradictions when restricted to finite-range hoppings.

Abstract

A band is called ideal when its Dirichlet functional saturates the topological lower bound. We study ideal bands in finite-band tight-binding models with conventional two-dimensional lattice translation symmetries, allowing the bands to have non-flat dispersion. We first provide an analytic construction of isolated Chern-ideal bands with Chern number $|\mathrm{Ch}|=1$ in finite-band models with exponentially decaying hopping. This construction applies only when at least two orbitals have different embedded positions (modulo lattice vectors), complementing the previously known construction for $|\mathrm{Ch}|>1$. We then show that isolated Chern-ideal bands with any nonzero Chern number cannot exist in finite-band models with finite-range hopping, regardless of the embedded orbital positions. The conclusion holds even if there are isolated band touching points, as long as the Berry curvature does not diverge anywhere in the Brillouin zone. We finally generalize the conclusions to Wilson-loop-ideal bands with zero total Chern number, such as Kane-Mele $\mathbb{Z}_2$-ideal bands.

Long-wavelength density response and momentum resolution in strange-metal charge spectroscopy

Authors: Ilmo Sung

Summary. This work theoretically connects momentum-resolved EELS data with optical conductivity in strange metals by analyzing the underlying charge density response function. It proves that this bulk response must obey a universal $q^2$ scaling law as momentum transfer approaches zero. This constraint defines a crossover surface, offering a fundamental test for theories describing charge dynamics in high-temperature superconductors.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical constraint derived from fundamental symmetries (Ward identity) provides a powerful tool to test theoretical models of strongly correlated electron systems against disparate experimental measurements.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to reconcile discrepancies between charge dynamics measured by momentum-resolved EELS (finite momentum) and those from optical spectroscopy (local limit) in strange metals.

Main result. It proves that the proper bulk density response function must exhibit a universal $q^2$ asymptotic suppression at fixed frequency, which constrains how finite-$q$ measurements relate to the local optical conductivity.

Method. The analysis uses the Ward identity connecting the charge-density polarization ($\chi''_{ ho ho}$) to the longitudinal conductivity ($\sigma_L$), and then proves this $q^2$ scaling holds for any non-negative, self-similar momentum-resolution kernel.

Model / system. The study focuses on strange-metal cuprates like Bi-2212, treating the charge dynamics via the proper homogeneous bulk charge-density polarization function ($\chi''_{ ho ho}$).

Key observables. Proper longitudinal charge-density polarization $\chi''_{ ho ho}(q, \omega)$, local conductivity $\sigma_L(\omega)$, and the resulting crossover surface $q^*(\omega, T)$.

Important parameters / regimes. Momentum transfer ($q$), frequency ($\omega$), temperature ($T$), and the ratio of momentum-resolution kernel width to nominal momentum.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the system is a homogeneous, U(1)-conserving metal, and that the measured quantity can be related back to the proper bulk response via electrodynamic conversion.

Figures summary. Not specified in the notes; the focus is on deriving functional relationships (Eq. 6, Eq. 8) and proving asymptotic theorems.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the Ward identity linking $\chi''_{ ho ho}$ to $\sigma_L$, derives the required $q^2$ asymptotic behavior for the bulk response, proves this scaling is robust against realistic momentum-resolution kernels, and concludes by defining the crossover surface connecting different measurement regimes.

Abstract

Momentum-resolved electron energy-loss spectroscopy (EELS) on strange-metal Bi$_2$Sr$_2$CaCu$_2$O$_{8+x}$ (Bi-2212) observes a broad charge continuum that is nearly momentum independent over an extended finite-momentum regime, whereas optical spectroscopy determines a metallic local conductivity. We analyze the long-wavelength response function that connects these regimes: the proper homogeneous bulk charge-density polarization. For any homogeneous $U(1)$-conserving metal whose longitudinal matter conductivity remains finite in the $q\to0$ limit at fixed nonzero frequency, the Ward identity gives $χ''_{ρρ}({\bf q},ω)=q^2\operatorname{Re}σ_L({\bf q},ω)/ω$. We then prove that any nonnegative normalized momentum-resolution kernel with a self-similar $q$-scaled profile, uniformly bounded second moment, and tight second-moment tails preserves this $q^2$ asymptotic behavior, changing only the prefactor. Consequently, a nearly local finite-$q$ continuum in Bi-2212, if continuously connected to a regular metallic optical limit of the same proper bulk response, is naturally described by a crossover surface $q_\ast(ω,T)$. The theorem is a constraint on the proper bulk density response, or on a positive $q$-scaled convolution of it; screened loss functions and surface EELS observables require the corresponding electrodynamic conversion before the constraint is applied.

Summary. This paper introduces QSTAR, a selective framework for Quantum Transfer Learning that routes samples to a quantum circuit head only if the initial classical prediction is low-confidence. The results demonstrate that this targeted approach significantly boosts accuracy by leveraging quantum computation where it is most needed, suggesting quantum heads are best used as specialized fallback tools rather than general replacements.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on machine learning, the concept of selectively engaging complex physical models (like quantum circuits) only when classical methods fail or are uncertain mirrors concepts in open quantum systems where specific environmental coupling or measurement protocols are only applied under certain conditions.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The evaluation of Quantum Transfer Learning (QTL) often incorrectly assumes the quantum head replaces the classical classifier, obscuring when the quantum component is genuinely useful.

Main result. Selective routing, which only passes low-confidence samples to a quantum fallback branch, significantly improves performance over standard QTL methods and outperforms adaptive classical baselines.

Method. The authors propose QSTAR, a selective framework that uses sample confidence scores ($ au$) to route inputs: high-confidence data stays with the classical prediction, while uncertain data is passed to a parameterized quantum circuit head.

Model / system. The model utilizes a frozen ResNet18 backbone for feature extraction on Fashion-MNIST. The classification involves comparing linear classical heads, MLP fallbacks, and various architecture-searched quantum circuits (KetGPT-designed heads).

Key observables. Accuracy ($ ext{Acc.}$), F1-score, and the performance improvement of the quantum branch specifically on low-confidence samples.

Important parameters / regimes. The confidence threshold $ au$ used for adaptive routing; circuit parameters (e.g., 10 parameters, 9 gates) in ablation studies.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that the utility of quantum heads is conditional on input uncertainty rather than being a universal replacement for classical methods.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the QSTAR methodology. Table I compares fixed-head transfer learning results, and Table II shows adaptive routing performance across different confidence thresholds ($ au$).

Paper structure. The paper introduces the limitation of standard QTL evaluation, proposes the selective QSTAR framework using adaptive routing based on sample confidence, presents quantitative comparisons against various baselines (classical/quantum heads), and concludes by arguing for the role of quantum circuits as targeted fallback mechanisms.

Abstract

Quantum transfer learning (QTL) is often evaluated by replacing a classical classifier with a fixed variational quantum head, but this hides a key question: when is the quantum branch actually useful? We propose QSTAR: Quantum Selective Transfer with Adaptive Routing, a selective QTL framework that keeps high-confidence classical predictions and routes only low-confidence samples to a fallback branch. Using a frozen ResNet18 backbone on Fashion-MNIST, we compare manually designed QTL heads, KetGPT-designed quantum heads, and parameter-matched classical baselines under a common data split and optimization schedule. Standard QTL heads reach at most 57.0% accuracy, while the strongest KetGPT head in the main filtered sweep reaches 78.5% accuracy and 0.785 F1-score. Although the strongest fixed classical head remains higher at 81.6%, selective routing gives the quantum branch a clearer role. On low-confidence samples, KetGPT #180 improves accuracy over a parameter-matched MLP fallback by 6.82, 4.31, and 3.03 percentage points at thresholds of 0.70, 0.80, and 0.90. At the full-system level, Adaptive KetGPT-QTL reaches 80.9% accuracy and 0.807 F1-score, outperforming the adaptive classical baseline. A separate compact-circuit ablation identifies KetGPT #160 as a stronger fixed-head candidate, reaching 81.9% accuracy with only 10 quantum parameters and 9 gates. These results suggest that architecture-searched quantum heads are most useful as targeted fallback branches for uncertain inputs rather than uniform replacements for classical classifiers.

Relational path integral, effective actions and quantum frame covariance

Summary. This paper proposes a novel, gauge-invariant path integral formulation for quantum gravity by introducing Quantum Reference Frames (QRFs). By formulating physics using 'relational observables,' it achieves manifest gauge invariance without explicit gauge fixing. The key finding is that physical measurements become frame-dependent—localizing an event in one reference frame makes it appear 'fuzzy' when viewed from another.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on quantum gravity, the core concept of describing physical quantities relative to internal, dynamical reference frames (QRFs) shares conceptual parallels with developing gauge-invariant descriptions in open quantum systems or time evolution when the background geometry itself is fluctuating or ill-defined.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is formulating a manifestly gauge-invariant path integral for quantum gravity that resolves issues arising from diffeomorphism invariance.

Main result. The authors propose a relational path integral formulation using Quantum Reference Frames (QRFs) which yields an anomaly-free, gauge-invariant description of gravity.

Method. They develop the formalism by constructing the path integral in terms of 'relational' or frame-dressed observables, bypassing explicit gauge fixing procedures.

Model / system. The system is Quantum Gravity, specifically addressing the gravitational path integral. The approach centers on defining physics relative to internal, dynamical reference frames (QRFs) rather than fixed background coordinates.

Key observables. Relational observables ($\hat{O}^A_R$), the relational effective action ($\Gamma_R$), and frame-change kernels ($K_{R o R'}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The formulation is characterized by its covariance under arbitrary QRF changes, leading to 'fuzziness' in local correlators when changing perspectives.

Assumptions / limitations. The construction proceeds at a non-perturbative level of quantum gravity, and while gauge invariance is achieved, the relationship between dynamics across different frames requires specialized kernels.

Figures summary. Figure 1 conceptually illustrates the difference between sharply localized events in one foliation versus 'fuzzy' (field-dependent) events when viewed from another QRF perspective.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem of gauge invariance in gravity, proposes the relational bundle-geometric formulation using QRFs and relational observables, derives the path integral structure, discusses the implications for vacuum states and correlators (fuzziness), and concludes by defining frame-dependent effective actions for renormalization.

Abstract

We propose a relational bundle-geometric formulation of the gravitational path integral by invoking the new tool of quantum reference frames (QRFs), which in gravity are gauge-covariant coordinate systems constructed from the available field content. Formulated in terms of relational (frame-dressed) observables, this yields a manifestly gauge-invariant path integral without ghosts and anomalies, and in which observables and their correlators are local to a frame. While eliminating the need for gauge fixing, it is equivalent to Faddeev-Popov versions in which the QRF is gauge-fixed, recovering certain previous proposals. A key feature is its covariance under QRF changes: it is a perspective-neutral path integral which encodes all internal QRF perspectives and the transformations between them. This leads to several qualitative predictions: local correlators and time evolution of relational observables in one QRF perspective become fuzzy in another, and a new spectrum of relational vacua arises. Comprised of frame-dependent no-boundary and asymptotic ground states, a vacuum from one perspective appears generally excited in another. Finally, we construct gauge-invariant, yet frame-dependent effective actions by coupling sources exclusively to relational observables, setting the stage for a relational definition of renormalization.

Summary. This paper develops a mathematically rigorous framework to define particle localization for the Dirac electron that strictly adheres to principles of relativistic causality. It constructs a covariant causal-logic representation by analyzing conserved probability currents and establishing connections between different mathematical definitions of localization. The work is significant for providing a consistent, projection-valued observable in relativistic quantum mechanics.

Why it may be interesting. While highly technical, the rigorous treatment of relativistic causality constraints on position measurement is fundamental to understanding quantum field theory observables beyond standard QFT formalism.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The central problem is constructing a covariant, causality-respecting localization observable for relativistic quantum particles like the Dirac electron.

Main result. The authors construct a Representation of the Causal Logic (RCL) that provides a projection-valued measure satisfying microscopic causality and correctly reproduces known results in specific limits.

Method. The methodology involves defining achronal localization via conserved probability currents, utilizing non-stationary phase arguments, and establishing mathematical correspondences between different types of localization operators (AL $\iff$ RCL).

Abstract

We construct a covariant representation of the causal logic for the Dirac system and the electron, based on the conserved Dirac probability current and a general current-to-localization procedure for achronal regions. A polynomial decay estimate, derived by a non-stationary phase argument, verifies the conditions required to define covariant achronal localization for the full Dirac system. Extending this localization to complete spacetime regions yields the corresponding causal-logic representation; restriction to the positive-energy invariant subspace gives the analogous construction for the electron. We establish several structural properties. On Euclidean space, Dirac localization agrees with canonical projection-valued localization, while its position operator differs from the Newton--Wigner operator by an explicitly determined bounded self-adjoint correction. Full Dirac achronal localization is projection valued and represents achronal separateness by orthogonality, thus satisfying microscopic causality. After compression to the electron subspace, it becomes positive-operator valued while preserving causality. We further extend known results from spatial to general achronal regions, prove separation and norm-one criteria, and analyze the high-boost limit, obtaining Lorentz contraction in a precise probabilistic sense. Finally, we discuss the electron--positron decomposition and the state transformation induced by position measurements, showing that measurement-induced positron production is universal and independent of the specific measuring device.

Summary. This paper introduces Uni-XAS, a unified machine learning framework designed to solve the challenging bidirectional problem in X-ray Absorption Spectroscopy (XAS). It overcomes limitations of prior methods by treating spectral and structural inference as a single cross-modal alignment task. The resulting framework achieves state-of-the-art performance across retrieval, forward prediction, and inverse structure generation on a massive dataset.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on materials science spectroscopy, the methodology heavily relies on advanced deep learning techniques—such as generative flow matching, cross-modal alignment, and manifold optimization—which are highly relevant to developing ML models for complex physical systems beyond standard quantum computing paradigms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core challenge is developing a unified machine learning framework for X-ray Absorption Spectroscopy (XAS) that can consistently bridge the gap between heterogeneous 1D spectral data and 3D local atomic structures.

Main result. Uni-XAS establishes a scalable, reproducible benchmark demonstrating strong performance in cross-modal retrieval, accurate absolute-spectrum prediction, and composition-conditional 3D structure generation.

Method. The authors propose reframing the bidirectional problem as a cross-modal alignment and conditional generation task using specialized components like XASLip for alignment and Permutation-Rectified Flow Matching (PR-Flow) for inverse inference.

Model / system. The system is X-ray Absorption Spectroscopy (XAS), which probes local atomic environments by mapping between 3D local coordination geometries ($\mathbf{G}$) and continuous spectra ($\mathbf{Y}$).

Key observables. XAS spectra (1D continuous sequences) and explicit 3D coordinates defining local coordination geometry.

Important parameters / regimes. The framework utilizes a large-scale benchmark of 328,839 structure-spectrum pairs; key metrics include MAE in physical eV for energy anchors and OT-RMSD for structural fidelity.

Assumptions / limitations. Existing methods are limited by decoupling forward/inverse tasks or failing to resolve permutation ambiguity among identical atoms. The framework assumes a shared latent space can unify these modalities.

Paper structure. The paper introduces Uni-XAS as a framework, detailing XASLip for alignment, followed by formulations for anchored absolute-spectrum generation (forward) and Permutation-Rectified Flow Matching (inverse). It concludes with performance evaluation on the large benchmark.

Abstract

X-ray absorption spectroscopy (XAS) is a key technique for probing local atomic environments, yet learning based modeling must bridge two heterogeneous modalities: 1D continuous spectra and 3D atomic structures. Existing approaches typically decouple forward spectrum prediction and inverse structure inference into separate regression tasks, hindering shared representation learning. Moreover, severe permutation ambiguity among identical atoms often limits inverse modeling to coarse structure descriptors rather than explicit 3D structure generation. In this work, we present Uni-XAS, a unified benchmark and learning framework that reframes bidirectional XAS modeling as a cross-modal alignment and conditional generation problem. We first propose XASLip, an alignment recipe coupling a physics-aware spectral encoder with an absorberaware manifold optimization strategy to resolve fine-grained intra-element coordination variations. Building upon this shared latent space, we formulate forward prediction as anchored absolute-spectrum generation via retrieval-augmented decoding, effectively preventing physical scale collapse and energy drift. For the inherently ill-posed inverse problem, we introduce Permutation-Rectified Flow Matching, which integrates type-wise optimal transport into a continuous generative flow to provide a principled solution to ligand permutation ambiguity without relying on heavy high-order equivariant architectures. Evaluated on a largescale standardized benchmark of 328,839 structure-spectrum pairs, Uni-XAS demonstrates strong performance in cross-modal retrieval, accurate absolute-spectrum prediction, and composition-conditional 3D structure generation, establishing a scalable, reproducible, and protocol-consistent foundation for multimodal learning and standardized evaluation in scientific spectroscopy.

Abstract

There have been a number of recent proposals to use analog logical rotations in early fault-tolerant quantum algorithms. Existing proposals implement a logical rotation by angle $φ$, with error $O(pφ)$, where $p$ is the physical error rate. While this is not fault-tolerant, if $φ$ is sufficiently small the logical error rate can be suppressed. In this work, we introduce and analyze the `balanced fusion' technique for improving the error scaling of analog logical rotations to $O(p φ^{1.5})$, without having to resort to fallback synthesis. Balanced fusion is enabled by an improved analysis of the accumulation of coherent error terms in analog logical rotations. Our techniques improve the viability of analog logical rotations for small rotation angles as an alternative to cultivation-powered rotation synthesis.

Abstract

Quantum geometric quantities have featured heavily in the discussion of the properties of quantum systems in recent years. Among quantities most commonly discussed is the variance of Berry curvature and the integral of the trace of the quantum metric tensor. Despite their usefulness, it is known that they suffer from one significant complication: for a tight-binding model, the quantum geometric quantities depend not only on the parameters of the tight-binding model itself, but also the real space geometry of the tight-binding model, the so-called "orbital embedding". One explicitly geometry-independent quantity is therefore the minimal value of the quantum geometric quantity out of all possible real space geometries. In this work, we demonstrate that, if the tight-binding model is compatible with certain spatial symmetries, then the real space geometry that minimizes the variance of the Berry curvature or the integral of the trace of the quantum metric tensor must obey all those spatial symmetries. We further show that the statement is applicable to systems with magnetic translation symmetries and other composite symmetries, with implications for the quantum geometry of the Hofstadter model.
