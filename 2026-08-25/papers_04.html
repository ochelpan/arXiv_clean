Summary. This work investigates the unconventional insulating transition in Rb2Ni3S4 by focusing on electron-phonon coupling in the regime of localized phonons. By analyzing complex Raman spectra, the authors demonstrate that the transition is driven by vibronic self-trapping, suggesting that localized lattice vibrations play a crucial role in the material's correlated electronic state.

Why it may be interesting. The strong coupling between electronic localization and lattice vibrations, particularly when mediated by localized phonons, provides a concrete example of how lattice dynamics can drive correlated electronic phases, relevant to open quantum systems modeling.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Investigating electron-phonon coupling in the regime where the relevant phonon mode is localized or weakly dispersive, contrasting with conventional models involving dispersive lattice vibrations.

Main result. The insulating transition in Rb2Ni3S4 is attributed to vibronic self-trapping involving a localized lattice coordinate, rather than conventional structural or magnetic ordering.

Method. The study combines multiple experimental techniques, including polarization-resolved Raman spectroscopy, temperature-dependent X-ray diffraction, STM, and transport measurements.

Model / system. The physical system is the kagome compound Rb2Ni3S4, which undergoes an unconventional insulating transition near 260-280 K. The analysis focuses on the strong coupling between electronic excitations and localized lattice vibrations.

Key observables. Giant Franck-Condon progression associated with a nearly dispersionless phonon, and a characteristic comb-like fine structure with a spacing of 40.6 cm-1.

Important parameters / regimes. The transition temperature T* (260-280 K); the phonon frequency (333.7 cm-1); the fine structure spacing (40.6 cm-1); the Huang-Rhys factor (S approx 2.9).

Assumptions / limitations. The nested coexistence of the progression and the comb structure is interpreted as evidence for a strongly coupled electron–vibrational manifold.

Figures summary. Figures show temperature dependence of resistivity, Raman spectra (revealing progressions and fine structure), and phonon frequency/linewidth changes across the transition temperature.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the unconventional nature of the insulating transition using multiple probes (transport, structural, electronic). It then uses Raman spectroscopy to analyze the phonon response, fitting the overtones to the Huang-Rhys model, and finally interprets the combined spectral features as evidence for vibronic self-trapping.

Abstract

Electron-phonon coupling is usually understood in terms of electronic quasiparticles interacting with dispersive lattice vibrations. Much less is known about the complementary limit in which the relevant phonon mode is itself localized or weakly dispersive. Here we investigate this regime in the kagome compound Rb$_{2}$Ni$_{3}$S$_{4}$, which undergoes an unconventional insulating transition near $T^{*} \approx$ 260-280~K. Combining polarization-resolved Raman spectroscopy with temperature-dependent x-ray diffraction, scanning tunneling microscopy, and electrical, thermal, and magnetic measurements, we show that the transition involves electronic localization without a conventional structural or magnetic order parameter. Raman spectra reveal a giant Franck-Condon progression associated with a nearly dispersionless 333.7~cm$^{-1}$ phonon, decorated by an equally spaced comb-like fine structure with a characteristic spacing of 40.6~cm$^{-1}$. The comb spacing is insensitive to magnetic field, whereas its spectral weight is strongly field tunable. Rather than treating either hierarchy alone as pure phonon effect, we interpret their nested coexistence as evidence for a strongly coupled electron-vibrational manifold involving a localized lattice coordinate. These results identify dispersionless phonons as an active route to vibronic correlations in solids and suggest that such electron-vibrational self-trapping is closely associated with the insulating phase of Rb$_{2}$Ni$_{3}$S$_{4}$.

Abstract

Quantum electrodynamics (QED) predicts that the vacuum should behave as a non-linear medium. For many schemes aimed at verifying this fundamental prediction, the signal consists of one or more photons in a certain mode (determined by polarization $σ$ and wave-number $\boldsymbol{k}$) which would be empty in the absence of a QED vacuum non-linearity. Here we consider modifying these schemes by sending in a classical or non-classical (e.g., squeezed) photon state instead of the initial vacuum state in that mode. We find that the detectability can be improved significantly.

Prediction of a layer nonlinear Hall effect in bilayer nonmagnetic or antiferromagnetic systems

Summary. This theoretical work predicts the existence of a switchable layer nonlinear Hall effect (LNHE) in bilayer quantum materials lacking linear Hall response. The effect is intrinsically hidden by layer symmetry but can be unmasked by an out-of-plane electric field ($E_z$). The study uses symmetry analysis and k.p models to show that this nonlinear response is a universal probe of layer-resolved quantum geometry.

Why it may be interesting. While the core physics is condensed matter transport, the reliance on symmetry analysis and the concept of 'hidden' responses activated by external fields (like $E_z$) shares conceptual overlap with controlling quantum states in engineered quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to predict and understand the layer nonlinear Hall effect (LNHE) in bilayer nonmagnetic or antiferromagnetic systems, especially when the linear Hall conductivity vanishes.

Main result. The LNHE is predicted to be a switchable, macroscopic nonlinear response activated by an out-of-plane electric field ($E_z$), which breaks layer-exchange symmetry.

Method. The study employs systematic symmetry analysis of layer groups, combined with minimal k.p models and tight-binding Hamiltonians to derive the necessary symmetry constraints.

Model / system. The system is a stacked bilayer of nonmagnetic or antiferromagnetic materials (e.g., $1T'- ext{WTe}_2$ or $1T'- ext{ReS}_2$). The physics is modeled using a Hamiltonian incorporating interlayer coupling and an out-of-plane electric field ($E_z$) that introduces a potential difference ($\Delta$).

Key observables. The key observable is the nonlinear Hall conductivity ($\sigma_{xy}^{ ext{NL}}$), which is predicted to exhibit an opposite sign upon reversal of the applied $E_z$.

Important parameters / regimes. The out-of-plane electric field ($E_z$) is the critical parameter that breaks symmetry and activates the response. The vanishing linear Hall conductivity is the prerequisite condition.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the low-energy physics can be captured by the minimal k.p model, and that the effect originates from geometric dipole mechanisms (Berry curvature dipole, etc.).

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the switchable nature of the response using band structures for $\Delta=0$, $\Delta>0$, and $\Delta<0$, confirming the sign reversal of the BCD components with field reversal.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the theoretical framework by analyzing layer-exchange symmetry leading to cancellation at $\Delta=0$. It then details how $E_z$ breaks this symmetry, enabling the LNHE via mechanisms like the Berry curvature dipole. Finally, it confirms these predictions using symmetry analysis and first-principles calculations on specific material examples.

Abstract

Nonlinear Hall effects provide a powerful probe of quantum geometry in solids and enable rectification phenomena beyond the constraints of linear response. In this Letter, we predict a \emph{layer nonlinear Hall effect} (LNHE) in stacked bilayer systems composed of nonmagnetic or antiferromagnetic materials with a vanishing linear Hall conductivity. In such systems, the second- or third-order nonlinear Hall responses are intrinsically layer odd: the contributions from the two constituent layers have equal magnitude but opposite sign, resulting in exact cancellation under layer-exchange symmetry. An out-of-plane electric field $E_z$ can break this symmetry, thereby unveiling the hidden response and converting it into a switchable macroscopic nonlinear Hall signal. Using a minimal $k\!\cdot\!p$ model, we demonstrate that the LNHE can originate from the Berry curvature dipole mechanism. A systematic symmetry analysis of all 80 layer groups further yields a complete classification of the symmetry constraints and stacking configurations that allow for this type of LNHE. Beyond this mechanism, additional symmetry analysis reveals that LNHE may also arise from quantum metric dipole or inversed mass dipole. Remarkably, even in cases where second-order nonlinear Hall responses are symmetry forbidden, a third-order LNHE can still survive in certain stacked bilayer configurations. First-principles calculations on representative bilayers---nonmagnetic 1T$'$-WTe$_2$ and 1T$'$-ReS$_2$---explicitly demonstrate electrically reversible second-order LNHE, in full agreement with our symmetry-based predictions. Overall, our results establish LNHE as a universal phenomenon in a wide range of layered quantum materials and provide a robust route toward electrically tunable nonlinear transport.

Abstract

We theoretically investigate a one-dimensional trimerized Su--Schrieffer--Heeger chain with three sublattices per unit cell subjected to a nonreciprocal Rashba spin-orbit coupling. Invoking a spin-flip symmetry, the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian decomposes into two independent spin sectors, enabling a spin-resolved analysis of non-Hermitian skin effects and system symmetries. We identify a rich phase diagram consisting of four bulk phases and two edge-state phases. The bulk phases include fully $\mathcal{PT}$-unbroken (real-spectrum) and fully anti-$\mathcal{PT}$-unbroken (imaginary-spectrum) regimes, as well as two mixed phases where one band remains on the real or imaginary axis while the other two form complex-conjugate pairs. The two edge-state phases correspond to topological edge modes with either $\mathcal{PT}$-unbroken (real) or anti-$\mathcal{PT}$-unbroken (imaginary) energies. Using non-Bloch band theory and Cardano's method, we derive closed-form expressions for phase boundaries and establish the bulk-edge correspondence for each spin sector. Calculations of Berry phase and directional inverse participation ratios confirm our analytical predictions. Our results provide a minimal platform for realizing spin-resolved non-Hermitian topology and edge-selective symmetry preservation, in which the bulk and edge states belong to distinct symmetry classes.

Summary. This paper analyzes the 4D Chamon code, a complex quantum error-correcting code, confirming its structure and restricted-mobility excitations. This new decoder significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods in numerical simulations, advancing the practical application of topological quantum memory.

Model / system. The system is the 4D Chamon code, a type of fracton model constructed via the 4D XYZ product of two 2D toric codes, defined on a 4D periodic lattice.

Key observables. Ground-State Degeneracy (GSD), restricted-mobility excitations (monopoles and dipoles), and the logical error rate during decoding.

Important parameters / regimes. The dimensionality (4D), the type of noise (Pauli Z errors), and the comparison metric (decoding accuracy vs. BP-OSD).

Assumptions / limitations. The core decoding strategy relies on separating the error components, particularly the mixed-face error ($\mathbf{e}_{ ext{mix}}$), which allows the problem to decompose into independent 2D subproblems.

It then develops the decoding theory by identifying structural symmetries, leading to the proposal of a two-layer decoding algorithm that solves the projected equation first, followed by parallel decoding on the remaining components.

Abstract

Fracton models have attracted considerable interest as candidates for quantum memories because of their unconventional ground-state degeneracy (GSD) and restricted-mobility excitations. The four-dimensional (4D) Chamon code introduced in our previous work is constructed via the 4D XYZ product of two two-dimensional (2D) toric codes. Its GSD grows exponentially with the system size, similar to that of the three-dimensional (3D) Chamon code, suggesting that it may be regarded as a 4D generalization of the 3D Chamon code. However, the excitation properties of the 4D Chamon code have not been studied in depth, and a high-performance decoding strategy is still lacking. In this work, we first establish the correspondence between the algebraic structure of the 4D Chamon code and the 4D lattice, thereby characterizing the geometric distributions of qubits and stabilizers. Second, we show that the 4D Chamon code supports three types of restricted-mobility excitations analogous to those of the 3D Chamon code, further supporting its interpretation as a 4D generalization of the 3D Chamon code. Finally, we uncover two structural properties relevant to decoding: a hyperplane symmetry and a projection-induced 2D toric-code structure. By exploiting these properties, we develop a two-layer decoding strategy that decomposes the original decoding problem into multiple independent and parallelizable subproblems. Numerical simulations show that the proposed decoder substantially outperforms BP-OSD in decoding accuracy, demonstrating the benefit of incorporating the intrinsic geometric and algebraic structures of the code into decoder design.

Summary. This theoretical work distinguishes between Mott and strain-induced semimetallic phases in twisted bilayer graphene by analyzing their response to an out-of-plane magnetic field. The key diagnostic tool is the gap size hierarchy observed in the Landau levels. The distinct, robust hierarchy predicted for the strain-induced state, compared to the field-sensitive hierarchy of the Mott state, offers a pathway for experimental verification.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a concrete, measurable spectroscopic signature (the gap hierarchy) that can differentiate between two complex, correlated quantum phases in a highly tunable solid-state system, which is relevant for understanding emergent topological phases.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to provide a sharp, spectroscopic criterion to distinguish between two candidate electronic states in magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene (MATBG): a 'Mott semimetal' governed by strong correlations, and a 'strain-induced anisotropic semimetal' characterized by weak Hartree-Fock effects.

Main result. The out-of-plane magnetic field serves as a definitive probe: the strain-induced semimetal exhibits a robust gap hierarchy ($\Delta_{\pm 4} > \Delta_{\pm 8} > \Delta_{\pm 12}$), while the Mott semimetal shows a different, field-dependent hierarchy for its higher-order gaps.

Method. The analysis involves calculating the expected Landau level (LL) gap size hierarchy using the Topological Heavy Fermion (THF) framework, comparing results derived from the Hubbard-I approximation (Mott) versus single-particle strained Hamiltonians.

Model / system. The physical system is MATBG, modeled using the THF framework. The study compares the behavior of the Mott state (using Hubbard-I) against the strain-induced state (using $ ext{H}_{ ext{strain}}$) under the influence of an out-of-plane magnetic field.

Key observables. Landau level gap size hierarchy ($\Delta_C$), specifically comparing $\Delta_{\pm 4}, \Delta_{\pm 8}, \Delta_{\pm 12}$ gaps.

Important parameters / regimes. Out-of-plane magnetic field ($B$), strain ($\epsilon$), and interaction strength ($U_1$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis often relies on the flat-chiral limit ($v'_* = M = 0$) for the Mott semimetal to achieve solvable spectra, though extensions include lattice relaxation and Zeeman coupling.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate QTM paths and expected $d^2I/dV^2$ maps for both candidates, and show the evolution of Chern gaps ($\Delta_C$) as a function of magnetic flux and various physical parameters.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theoretical framework by first establishing the diagnostic tool (LL spectroscopy) and then performing detailed calculations comparing the gap evolution for the Mott state versus the strain-induced state under varying external fields and internal symmetries.

Abstract

Recent quantum twisting microscopy experiments [1] have provided unprecedented momentum- and energy-resolved imaging of magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene, revealing a stark dichotomy between the heavy and light electronic characters of its narrow bands. In this work, we provide a sharp distinguishing criterion between the two most likely candidate states that exhibit spectroscopic features consistent with the experimental results. Focusing on charge neutrality, these states are 1) the 'Mott semimetal', governed by strong dynamical correlations, and 2) the 'strain-induced anisotropic semimetal', characterized by weak Hartree-Fock effects. We demonstrate that an out-of- plane magnetic field serves as a sharp discriminating probe based on the expected Landau level gap size hierarchy. We find that the strain-induced anisotropic semimetal has a robust gap hierarchy where the Chern C gaps, Δ_C , decrease in order Δ_{\pm 4} > Δ_{\pm 8} > Δ_{\pm 12}. In contrast, for the Mott semimetal, Δ_{\pm 4} remains the largest gap, while the relative magnitude of the C = \pm 8, \pm 12 gaps depends sensitively on the applied magnetic field. We test the robustness of these results against strain orientation and lattice relaxation finding consistency between the gap hierarchy of the strained semimetal and previous incompressibility measurements at finite magnetic field [2].

Summary. This work develops a general Landau theory for Electronic Toroidal Metals (ETM), showing how toroidal order can spontaneously arise in itinerant electron systems. The theory uses advanced projection techniques to isolate the physical instability, predicting unique signatures in the magnetoelectric response and nonlinear charge transport. It establishes ETM as a distinct metallic phase governed by Fermi-liquid instabilities.

Why it may be interesting. The concept of a higher-rank multipolar order (toroidal) emerging from itinerant electrons provides a rich theoretical framework for understanding exotic metallic phases beyond standard charge or spin ordering.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To develop a generic theory for the electronic toroidal metal (ETM), where toroidal order spontaneously emerges from electronic degrees of freedom near the Fermi surfaces in itinerant-electron systems.

Main result. ETM is established as a distinct metallic Landau phase, characterized by a soft collective mode and a unique enhancement of nonlinear charge transport linked to pseudospin texture topology.

Method. The theory employs a projected instability criterion (Schur projection) to handle the overlap between toroidal candidates and the uniform charge current, leading to a Fermi-liquid instability analysis.

Model / system. The study focuses on itinerant electron systems, using a minimal two-band Hamiltonian on a square lattice as a prototype for spin-orbit-coupled systems.

Key observables. Antisymmetric magnetoelectric response tensor, nonlinear charge transport ($\sigma_{interQMD}$), and the continuous evolution of the toroidal moment ($T$).

Important parameters / regimes. The instability is governed by the soft mode appearing in the P-odd and T-odd particle-hole channel.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary technical hurdle addressed is the overlap of toroidal candidates with the uniform charge current, necessitating the Schur projection to select the physical instability.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the temperature dependence of various nonlinear conductivity contributions (NLD, interQMD, intraQMD) and show the momentum-space distribution of the interband quantum metric near pseudospin vortices.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theory by first formulating the instability criterion, then using a Schur projection formalism on a minimal model to derive the effective mean-field Hamiltonian, and finally analyzing the resulting nonlinear transport signatures.

Abstract

Toroidal order is a higher-rank multipolar order whose intrinsic realization in itinerant-electron systems remains unexplored. Here, we develop a generic theory of the electronic toroidal metal (ETM), in which toroidal order emerges spontaneously from electronic degrees of freedom near the Fermi surfaces. Because candidate toroidal bilinears can overlap by symmetry with the uniform charge current, we formulate a projected instability criterion that removes the noncondensable current component. Within this current-orthogonal sector, we identify a soft collective mode in the $\mathcal{P}$-odd and $\mathcal{T}$-odd particle-hole channel and demonstrate that ETM arises as a Fermi-liquid instability. We then define the toroidal moment in an itinerant system through the antisymmetric magnetoelectric response tensor. We further establish an intimate connection between this response and the topology of pseudospin texture: the rearrangement of pseudospin vortices changes the winding structure of the Fermi surfaces and markedly enhances the magnetoelectric response. ETM also exhibits characteristic nonlinear charge transport, including a pronounced enhancement of its interband quantum-geometric contribution. Our results establish ETM as a distinct metallic Landau phase and provide a general framework for understanding toroidal order generated by itinerant electrons.

Summary. This theoretical paper constructs solutions to the non-braided scalene Yang--Baxter equation using algebraic methods based on noncommutative algebras. The key finding is the derivation of multiparameter quantum affine spaces that satisfy this relation, providing a general framework for studying quantum integrability. The results are realized explicitly using weighted and multiplicative shift operators, connecting abstract algebra to concrete physical models.

Why it may be interesting. The connection between the scalene YBE and quantum affine algebras provides a powerful, representation-independent tool for studying quantum integrability, which is crucial for understanding many-body dynamics in condensed matter systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper focuses on constructing representation-independent families of solutions to the non-braided scalene Yang--Baxter equation (YBE). This equation governs the algebraic structure underlying quantum integrability.

Main result. The construction yields purely scalene solutions associated with multiparameter quantum affine spaces, providing an algebraic framework for investigating quantum integrability.

Method. The authors use algebraic methods, starting from quadratic noncommutative algebras and generalizing the construction from anticommuting generators to $q$-deformed algebras.

Model / system. The study concerns abstract algebraic structures, specifically quantum affine spaces and the operators satisfying the scalene YBE. Representations are realized using weighted shifts and multiplicative-shift operators acting on $\mathbb{Z}$-indexed spaces.

Key observables. The satisfaction of the scalene Yang--Baxter relation ($A_{12}B_{13}C_{23} = C_{23}B_{13}A_{12}$) and the derived cross-commutativity relation $[t_B, t_C] = 0$.

Important parameters / regimes. Deformation parameters ($q_{\mu u}^{\alpha}$ or $\lambda_\mu / \lambda_ u$), which dictate the specific quantum affine space realized.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the existence of a basis (PBW property) for the algebra and that the initial construction can be generalized from anticommuting generators to $q$-deformed algebras.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the triangular organization of the anticommuting two-generator construction, while other figures likely detail the structure of the shift operators and the algebraic relations.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from the initial anticommuting construction of the scalene YBE solution (Theorem 2.1) to generalizing this to $q$-deformed algebras (Theorem 4.1), and finally providing explicit infinite-dimensional realizations using weighted and multiplicative shift operators (Section 6).

Abstract

We construct representation-independent families of solutions of the non-braided scalene Yang--Baxter equation from quadratic noncommutative algebras. Beginning with two anticommuting generators, we obtain a continuous deformation in terms of quantum-plane algebras and extend the construction to an arbitrary number of generators. In the latter case the scalene Yang--Baxter relation fixes the multiparameter exchange matrix to an exact multiplicative form, thereby selecting a distinguished subclass of multiparameter quantum affine spaces. We construct finite-dimensional realizations using singular matrices and finite Heisenberg--Weyl operators, as well as infinite-dimensional realizations in terms of bilateral weighted shifts and multiplicative-shift operators. The explicit realizations are generically \emph{purely scalene}: although the ordered triple satisfies the scalene Yang--Baxter equation, its individual constituent operators do not satisfy the ordinary non-braided Yang--Baxter equation. These results provide a representation-independent algebraic framework for constructing Yang--Baxter-irreducible scalene triples and a starting point for investigating their possible applications to quantum integrability.

Abstract

We represent sandwiched and $α$-$z$ Rényi divergences as averages of ordinary relative entropy. The $α$-$z$ Rényi divergence is shown to be an integral over the relative entropy of a canonical family of fixed-ray escort states along the ray $z=cα$. We prove this representation for normal states on an arbitrary von Neumann algebra, using Haagerup non-commutative $L^p$ spaces and interpolation. The formula holds for every $z>0$: for $0<α<1$ it holds when the support of the first state is contained in that of the reference state, and for $α>1$ it holds whenever the divergence is finite. When the lower-order support condition fails, we identify the exact fixed-ray support-boundary term. The representation yields a monotone escort profile and a convex order potential. We use these to reformulate one-shot testing converses, exact sandwiched strong-converse exponents, and work-extraction reliability as signed-area or level-crossing statements, and discuss a restricted two-parameter pair-conversion rate.

Abstract

The trace of the average mixing matrix of a quantum walk measures the "laziness" of the walk: the higher the trace, the more likely that the walker returns home in the long run. In this paper, we develop tools to study this graph invariant arising from Laplacian quantum walks. It is known that the complete graph $K_n$ is the laziest connected graph on $n$ vertices. Using our machinery, we show that the star $S_n$ is the second laziest connected graph on $n$ vertices (and hence the laziest tree on $n$ vertices), the complete multipartite graph $K_{n-2,1,1}$ is the third laziest connected graph on $n$ vertices, and the double star $DS(n-3,1)$ is the second laziest tree on $n$ vertices. We also show that on the same number of vertices, more unbalanced double stars are lazier.
