Summary. This paper reports the first observation of ultralow-field Bose-Einstein condensation of triplons in the spin-ladder magnet Henmilite. By combining advanced measurements with QMC simulations, the authors establish that the material resides in a quantum-disordered parent state, extending the BEC concept beyond simpler dimer magnets. Abstract

We realise the first ultralow-field Bose-Einstein condensation of triplons in a spin-ladder magnet, uncovering a quantum critical point at only $μ_0 H_{c1}=0.17$ T in Henmilite ($\mathrm{Ca_2Cu(OH)_4[B(OH)_4]_2}$). Unlike dimer magnets, a ladder retains extended one-dimensional correlations in its gapped parent state, making this limit strongly fluctuation dominated. Thermodynamic, magnetoelastic, $μ$SR, and neutron-diffraction measurements overturn the previous assignment of zero-field antiferromagnetic order, establishing a quantum-disordered coupled-ladder parent state with persistent low-energy dynamics. The weak low-temperature anomaly instead marks a gap-controlled crossover from the correlated ladder regime into the activated quantum-disordered state. These measurements further reveal an exceptionally asymmetric ordered dome extending to $μ_0 H_{c2}\simeq 8.2$ T. Quantum Monte Carlo simulations for the relevant spin Hamiltonian place Henmilite just on the gapped side of the zero-field ladder-ordering instability, naturally accounting for the strong separation between the exchange and residual-gap scales and the tiny critical field. Our findings extend ultralow-field triplon condensation beyond the dimer paradigm and establish Henmilite as a platform for controlled tuning across quantum criticality in a fluctuation-dominated spin ladder.

Summary. This thesis provides a deep mathematical study of quantum channels (CPTP maps), treating them as objects within abstract mathematical structures. It characterizes the extreme points of these sets and explores their topological and categorical relationships. The work is fundamental for understanding the mathematical limits and structure of quantum operations.

Why it may be interesting. This is highly relevant as it provides the rigorous mathematical backbone for understanding noise and dissipation in open quantum systems, which is central to quantum information theory and quantum optics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper mathematically analyzes quantum channels (CPTP maps), focusing on characterizing their extreme points, topological structure, and categorical properties.

Main result. It establishes that the sets of CPTP and UCPTP maps are compact convex sets, constructing extreme maps for various ranks and proving specific properties regarding the tensor product and existence of products/coproducts.

Method. The work employs advanced mathematical tools, including topological techniques (CW structures), category theory (monoidal categories), and functional analysis applied to quantum operations.

Model / system. The model is the quantum channel, mathematically represented by Completely Positive Trace-Preserving (CPTP) maps acting on density matrices in finite-dimensional complex Hilbert spaces.

Key observables. The primary observables are the quantum channels themselves, characterized by their extreme points, Choi-rank, and structural properties.

Important parameters / regimes. The dimensionality of the Hilbert space ($d$) and the rank of the maps are key parameters explored in the construction of extreme examples.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the mathematical framework of Hilbert spaces and the assumption that the sets of quantum channels form compact convex sets.

Figures summary. The notes indicate the presentation of extensive tables detailing parameters for numerous concrete examples of extreme UCPTP maps derived from various mathematical constructions.

Paper structure. The paper systematically reviews CPTP/UCPTP maps, details the structure of the category of CPTP maps, presents constructions and properties of extreme CPTP maps, and concludes with topological investigations into the closure of the extreme point set.

Abstract

In this thesis, quantum channels are studied from the point of view of Mathematics. We studied the CPTP (completely positive trace preserving) and UCPTP channels, the latter being the unital channels. In both cases, the sets are compact convex, so they are the closure of their extreme points. We constructed extreme CPTP maps for every possible rank. We also constructed extreme UCPTP maps with rank 2 for each possible dimension. For ranks of at least 3, we constructed extreme UCPTP maps for many dimensions and ranks. We also proved that the tensor product of extreme CPTP maps is an extreme CPTP map. For UCPTP maps, we proved that the same is not always true, with many counterexamples. It is known that the category of CPTP maps is semicartesian. We add to this a classification of binary products. We proved that products only exist in trivial cases. For binary coproducts, we obtained the dimensions they should have, if they exist. In both cases, topological techniques were used. For this reason, we also investigated the topology of the closure of the set of extreme points of the CPTP maps. Topological invariants can be computed from decompositions of a space such as CW structures. These are a type of decomposition in which the space is expressed as a gluing of balls. In the search for a CW structure for the closure of the set of extreme CPTP maps, we obtained a partial decomposition that resembles a CW structure of the complex projective space.

Summary. This paper reinterprets MCMC sampling by modeling the acceptance step as a Maxwell's Demon, quantifying the flow of information. It derives an entropy balance equation that separates useful 'cooling' information from 'housekeeping' circulation. The work provides powerful diagnostics to analyze and accelerate sampling algorithms beyond simple relaxation time measurements.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous application of information thermodynamics to MCMC provides novel diagnostics for sampling efficiency, which has parallels in understanding energy dissipation and information flow in complex physical systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to mathematically formalize the analogy between Maxwell's Demon and the acceptance module in Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithms, linking it to information thermodynamics principles.

Main result. It establishes the information balance equation $v(t) = \dot{I}_{cool}(t) + \dot{S}(t)$, showing that acceleration requires maximizing the conversion of decision information into cooling current while minimizing housekeeping circulation.

Method. The analysis uses information-theoretic tools, defining mutual information, cooling information flow, and entropy production rates within the framework of MCMC dynamics.

Model / system. The model is the MCMC sampling process, viewed as a feedback device operating on a state space governed by a master equation. The analysis compares different MCMC kernels (local MH, learned proposal, irreversible drive) on a 50-state rugged ring.

Key observables. Decision Mutual Information ($M_{dec}$), Cooling Information Flow ($\dot{I}_{cool}$), Housekeeping Entropy Production ($\sigma_{hk}$), and the relative-entropy relaxation rate ($v(t)$).

Important parameters / regimes. The target distribution ($\pi(x)$), the acceptance/rejection mechanism, and the relaxation time ($ au_{1/2}$) are key parameters.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on viewing the MCMC process through the lens of information flow, particularly distinguishing between useful cooling information and housekeeping circulation.

Figures summary. Figure 1 conceptually summarizes the Maxwell-demon interpretation, detailing the flow from Proposal to Measurement to Memory and Feedback to the probability current.

Paper structure. The paper builds from identifying the Maxwell demon analogy in MCMC, deriving the core information balance equation, and then applying this framework to analyze and compare different MCMC kernels (local MH vs. learned/irreversible drives) using quantitative diagnostics.

Abstract

Markov chain Monte Carlo algorithms can be viewed as feedback devices that compare a proposed move with the target distribution and then accept or reject it. In this paper the Maxwell demon is identified with the acceptance module: it measures a proposed edge, stores the outcome in the accept/reject bit, and uses that bit to shape the probability current. The decision bit carries a genuine Shannon mutual information about the proposal, whereas only its directional part is converted into a cooling information flow. The relative-entropy relaxation rate obeys $v(t)=\dot{\mathcal I}_{\rm cool}(t)+\dot S(t)$, which separates useful cooling from housekeeping circulation in nonreversible chains.

Summary. This theoretical paper constructs local observables for the Bell-CHSH inequality in a free massive scalar field using finite polynomials of Weyl operators. By employing Fejér approximants, the authors show that the resulting Bell-CHSH value can approach Tsirelson's bound of $2\sqrt{2}$. This method is significant because it surpasses limitations found in simpler, one-quadrature settings.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a concrete, field-theoretic realization of approaching fundamental quantum limits (like Tsirelson's bound) using structured, finite polynomial approximations, which has implications for understanding entanglement bounds in relativistic quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to construct bounded local operators for the Bell-CHSH inequality in a free massive scalar field, specifically to approach Tsirelson's bound ($2\sqrt{2}$).

Main result. The finite-Weyl polynomial construction demonstrates that the Bell-CHSH correlator can approach $2\sqrt{2}$ from below, achieving values like $2.80027$ with a degree-511 polynomial.

Method. The authors construct the operators as finite polynomials of unitary Weyl operators, utilizing techniques involving odd Weyl harmonics and normalized Fejér approximants of trigonometric functions.

Model / system. The physical system is a free massive scalar field in $1+1$ dimensions, analyzed using the framework of Weyl operators and spectral representations in rapidity space.

Key observables. The Bell-CHSH correlator $\langle 0|C|0 angle$ is the primary diagnostic, alongside the construction of four bounded Hermitian operators.

Important parameters / regimes. The degree of the polynomial (e.g., 511) and the bandwidth of the spectral packets are key parameters controlling the approximation quality.

Assumptions / limitations. The construction circumvents the restriction that limits Bell-CHSH tests to $\le 2$ by ensuring Bob's final settings mix two noncommuting axis observables.

Figures summary. The notes detail the calculation of the Bell-CHSH expectation value using specific finite polynomial approximations (e.g., $B_{ ext{spec}}^{511}$ and $B_{511}$), comparing them to the theoretical limit.

Paper structure. The paper builds from the general problem of local operators in QFT, details the construction using Weyl polynomials and spectral localization, and culminates in numerical calculations using Fejér approximants to approach the theoretical bound.

Abstract

We construct four bounded Hermitian operators, each one a finite polynomial in the unitary Weyl operators, for the Bell-CHSH inequality in a free massive scalar field in $1+1$ dimensions. The operators are localized in complementary wedges. Odd Weyl harmonics provide two exactly anticommuting axis observables in each wedge, while normalizable packets with compact support in the spectrum of the boost generator give exact modular inner products at nonvanishing bandwidth. The resulting Bell-CHSH correlator is a finite double sum. An operator with six Weyl terms per axis already gives $2.14885$. Using normalized Fejér approximants of $\operatorname{sgn}(\cos(x))$, we show that the supremum over the finite-polynomial family equals $2\sqrt{2}$, although no finite member attains it; a degree-$511$ example gives $2.80027$. We also point out that, in a centered quasifree state, a Bell-CHSH test whose four final settings are bounded functions of individual quadratures admits one common Gaussian representation and therefore stays below $2$. The finite-Weyl construction avoids this restriction because Bob's final settings mix two noncommuting axis observables.

Quantum thermodynamics and semidefinite optimization: Boltzmann, Fermi-Dirac, and Bose-Einstein frameworks

Summary. This work unifies the mathematical structure of quantum information problems (SDPs) by interpreting them through the lens of quantum thermodynamics. It demonstrates that Boltzmann, Fermi-Dirac, and Bose-Einstein statistics each provide a natural, solvable regularization for different types of quantum constraints. This yields hybrid quantum-classical algorithms for solving complex quantum optimization problems.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a powerful, unifying mathematical tool that connects the abstract optimization problems of quantum information theory directly to fundamental concepts of statistical mechanics (thermal expectation values, chemical potentials).

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to develop a unified thermodynamic framework to interpret various Semidefinite Programs (SDPs) that arise in quantum information theory.

Main result. It shows that three distinct statistical ensembles—Boltzmann, Fermi-Dirac, and Bose-Einstein—each provide a natural thermodynamic regularization for different types of SDP constraints, leading to a common mathematical structure.

Method. The approach involves using free-energy regularization and Lagrangian duality to transform constrained SDPs into unconstrained, concave dual optimization problems parameterized by chemical potentials.

Model / system. The analysis covers quantum systems governed by three statistical frameworks: Boltzmann (for state variables), Fermi-Dirac (for measurement operators), and Bose-Einstein (for general PSD operators). The optimization is governed by a grand canonical Hamiltonian.

Key observables. The dual gradient and Hessian are shown to be closed-form as thermal expectation values, which are the key quantities for algorithm implementation.

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature (T) acts as a continuous control parameter, allowing the method to smoothly approximate the original, hard-constrained SDPs.

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes the system can be treated at a finite temperature T>0 to obtain a smooth approximation of the original SDP. For the BE case, it assumes the Hamiltonian is gapped.

Figures summary. Table I summarizes the three frameworks, detailing the feasible set, entropy, relative entropy, and the form of the primal optimum for Boltzmann, FD, and BE statistics.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the general thermodynamic regularization for SDPs, then details the specific mathematical and algorithmic implementations for the Boltzmann, Fermi-Dirac, and Bose-Einstein cases, culminating in a discussion of hybrid quantum-classical solvers.

Abstract

Here we argue how quantum thermodynamics offers a unifying interpretation for a wide class of semidefinite programs (SDPs) that arise in quantum information. Three SDP variable constraints, namely trace-one density operators, operator-bounded measurements, and the unbounded positive semidefinite cone, admit thermodynamic regularizations associated with Boltzmann, Fermi-Dirac, and Bose-Einstein statistics, respectively. In each case the entropy-regularized dual is unconstrained and concave in a chemical-potential vector, and the primal optimum is a thermal operator of the matched statistics. The dual gradient and Hessian are thermal expectation values, enabling hybrid quantum-classical algorithms for solving a wide variety of SDPs.

Summary. This paper performs extensive Monte Carlo simulations to map out the critical behavior of 2D bond percolation with long-range, algebraically decaying interactions. Main result. The study establishes three distinct universality regimes (LR-WF-A, LR-WF-B, LR-MF) and finds that global observables can exhibit coexistence between Complete-Graph asymptotics and Long-Range Gaussian-Fixed-Point scaling.

Method. The research employs comprehensive Monte Carlo simulations using the event-based ensemble method, supplemented by conventional ensemble simulations and finite-size scaling analysis.

Model / system. The model is two-dimensional bond percolation on a lattice where the connection probability decays algebraically with distance $r$ according to $p(r) \propto 1/r^{2+\sigma}$.

Important parameters / regimes. The interaction decay exponent $\sigma$ is the primary tuning parameter, defining regimes such as $1 < \sigma \leq 2$ (LR-WF-A) and $0 < \sigma \leq 2/3$ (LR-MF).

Figures summary. Figures are expected to show the universality class diagram in the $(d, \sigma)$ plane and plots comparing calculated critical exponents ($\eta$ vs $\sigma$) against theoretical predictions.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the critical behavior across different $\sigma$ regimes, presenting quantitative results in tables detailing fits for various susceptibilities ($\Delta, S_2, \chi_k$) across different $\sigma$ values.

Abstract

We present a comprehensive Monte Carlo study of two-dimensional bond percolation with algebraically decaying connection probabilities $p(r)\propto 1/r^{2+σ}$, establishing the universality diagram in the long-range (LR) regime for $σ\le2$. Using the event-based ensemble method, we simulate systems with linear sizes up to $L=16384$ and investigate three universality regimes: LR Wilson--Fisher (WF) A ($1<σ\le2$), LR Wilson--Fisher B ($2/3<σ\le1$), and LR mean-field (MF) ($0<σ\le2/3$). In the LR-WF-B regime, the anomalous dimension is consistent with $η=2-σ$, in agreement with mathematical results for $2/3<σ<1$, while the correlation-length exponent $ν(σ)$ exhibits nontrivial, non-Gaussian variation. In the LR-WF-A regime, although $η$ remains close to $2-σ$ for smaller $σ$, statistically resolvable deviations $δη(σ)=η-(2-σ)>0$ start to appear near $σ\simeq3/2$ and grow toward the short-range crossover at $σ=2$. Finally, by complementing the event-based simulations with conventional ensemble simulations, we reveal the coexistence of complete-graph asymptotics and LR Gaussian-fixed-point scaling in the LR-MF regime. These results further clarify the critical properties in long-range percolation and provide crucial benchmarks for long-range statistical systems.

Abstract

Quantum state ensembles are important in quantum information processing. For example, quantum $t$-designs model highly entangled states in complex systems, while projected ensembles appear in generative quantum machine learning and studies of thermalization. With their sample state accompanied by a classical label, these ensembles contain operational information beyond their average density operators. Yet an ensemble differs from a classical-quantum state because it is invariant under permutations of labels. We formulate binary hypothesis testing between finite quantum ensembles and derive fundamental limits on error probability. Given an observed label pattern, we show that the joint sampled state can be described by power-weighted ensemble moments. This yields the Bayes-optimal measurement and exact finite-sample error, revealing that discrimination is governed by the full moment hierarchy up to the number of samples. In the many-sample limit, we derive Chernoff bounds and obtain exact error exponents for finite uniform pure-state ensembles. We apply these results to optical communication and $t$-designs. For finite uniform pure-state $t$-designs with large $t$, the maximal discrimination exponent scales sharply as $\sim t^{-2}$, while equal-prior fixed-error testing requires $\sim t^2$ samples.

Abstract

Modular integration has emerged as the main pathway for scaling superconducting quantum processing units (QPUs) beyond the constraints of fabrication yield and physical footprint. Currently, two primary strategies lead this effort. Mirroring the "Scaling Up" and "Scaling Out" approaches in GPU architectures and AI infrastructures, these are: chiplet-based scaling, which preserves dense connectivity and high gate fidelity at the expense of engineering complexity, and distributed architectures, which decouple system scaling from monolithic QPU advancements at the expense of sparser connectivity and lower interconnect quality. To evaluate these approaches, we introduce a quantitative stress test measuring the execution cost of a dense workload of random logical entangling operations using a surface code scheme. Using a dedicated compiler, we compute the space-time cost as the number of network nodes increases, analysing this scaling behaviour across various surface code distances, Bell-state fidelities, and Bell-pair generation times. We find that distributed architectures incur an up to exponential space-time performance penalty compared to an effectively monolithic architecture across all simulations. Our results also show that as the network grows, this penalty manifests in two distinct scaling regimes: a noise-dominated regime constrained by insufficient Bell-state fidelity and generation rates, and a connectivity-dominated regime bottlenecked by lattice-surgery routing congestion.
