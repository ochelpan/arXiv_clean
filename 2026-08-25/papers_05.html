Metabolic Network Properties: Comprehensive Analysis Across Domains

Authors: José Antônio Pellizzaro, Daniel Gamermann, Julian Triana Dopico
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.21168v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper conducts a large-scale topological analysis of metabolic networks from diverse life forms. It introduces a refined method for building these networks based on direct chemical transformations and uses advanced community detection to quantify their structure. The findings reveal that the organization of these biological networks possesses specific topological signatures that cannot be explained by random chance, pointing to underlying evolutionary mechanisms.

Why it may be interesting. While the subject is biochemistry, the rigorous application of graph theory, community detection (Surprise function), and statistical comparison against null models mirrors techniques used in analyzing complex systems in statistical physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To comprehensively analyze the topological properties and community structure of metabolic networks across diverse life domains, identifying features driven by evolutionary pressures beyond simple degree distribution.

Main result. Metabolic networks exhibit distinct topological signatures, such as being disassortative and having a higher local clustering coefficient compared to random models, suggesting underlying evolutionary constraints.

Method. The study employs novel network construction methods focusing on direct chemical transformations and uses advanced community detection algorithms like Surprise to compare real networks against randomized null models.

Model / system. The physical system is a metabolic network, modeled as a graph where nodes are metabolites and edges represent direct chemical transformations. The analysis spans 10912 organisms across Bacteria, Archaea, and Eukarya.

Key observables. Community structure metrics (Surprise function, $N_c$, $ar{n}_c$, $PI$), topological properties ($ar{k}$, $ar{C}$, $C$, $A$, $ar{d}$), and comparisons against randomized network metrics.

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis compares real vs. randomized networks using statistical tests (e.g., t-test) and focuses on identifying structural deviations from random expectations.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that topological features not explained by degree distribution reflect genuine, non-random evolutionary pressures. The method also assumes the ability to resolve currency metabolites based on elemental balance.

Figures summary. The paper compares traditional vs. novel network constructions (Fig 1), presents statistical summaries of topological properties (Table 2), details community structure analysis (Table 5), and compares these findings against randomized network models (Tables 6 and 7).

Paper structure. The paper progresses from introducing the problem and novel network construction (focusing on chemical pathways) to detailing the statistical analysis of global and community metrics, culminating in a rigorous comparison between real biological networks and randomized null models.

Abstract

Metabolic networks play pivotal roles in understanding the evolution of organisms, microbiome dynamics and disease prevention and treatment. This study presents a comprehensive analysis of metabolic network properties across 10912 organisms spanning Bacteria, Archaea, and Eukarya domains. A novel method for the network construction is introduced, emphasizing the chemical transformations of metabolites. Unlike conventional approaches that link every substrate to every product in all chemical reactions, this method aligns more closely with metabolic pathways, linking products only to their generating substrates. Emphasis is placed on investigating the community structure within metabolic networks, employing the Surprise quality function that better addresses some limitations of previous approaches. Through comparisons between real and randomized versions of the networks, we identify characteristics that cannot be explained solely by the network degree distribution, thus identifying topological properties and community structures that arise from additional evolutionary pressures. This highlights the need for evolutionary models to incorporate additional mechanisms beyond the replication of the degree distribution.

Predicting Resource Efficient Hamiltonian Decomposition for Continuous-Time Quantum Walk Simulations

Authors: Mostafa Atallah, Rebekah Herrman, Zain H. Saleem
Type: both · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.20660v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This research tackles the challenge of efficiently simulating Continuous-Time Quantum Walks by comparing two ways to decompose the system's Hamiltonian. By training machine learning models on graph properties, the authors developed a predictor that can advise quantum compilers on which decomposition minimizes costly CX gates. This offers a practical tool for optimizing quantum circuit compilation for quantum simulation.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the resource optimization bottleneck in simulating quantum dynamics on near-term quantum hardware by providing a data-driven tool to select the best circuit decomposition.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to predict which of two Hamiltonian decompositions (Pauli vs. matching) will result in fewer CX gates when simulating a Continuous-Time Quantum Walk (CTQW) via Trotterization.

Main result. The choice of the resource-efficient decomposition is primarily governed by the decomposition property $n_{ ext{Pauli}}$, rather than intrinsic graph topology. The trained ML model shows strong transferability, achieving high performance on larger, unseen graphs.

Method. A supervised machine learning approach is used to classify the optimal decomposition by training models on a large set of graphs, using graph topology and decomposition term counts as features.

Model / system. The system is a CTQW evolving on a graph $G$, whose time evolution $e^{-iHt}$ must be approximated using Trotterization. The comparison is between the Pauli and matching decompositions of the Hamiltonian $H$.

Key observables. The number of CX (two-qubit) gates required by each decomposition, and the classification accuracy (MCC) of the prediction model.

Important parameters / regimes. The number of vertices ($N$), the graph structure (e.g., 8-vertex graphs, Erdos-Renyi), and the feature counts ($n_{ ext{Pauli}}$, $n_{ ext{match}}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The simulation relies on Trotterization, and the selection criterion is solely based on minimizing the CX gate count, ignoring circuit depth or connectivity.

Figures summary. The notes mention tables comparing performance metrics (precision, recall, MCC) across various ML models, feature subsets, and increasing graph sizes ($N=8$ to $N=256$).

Paper structure. The paper establishes the problem (Hamiltonian decomposition for CTQW), develops the methodology (feature engineering using graph and decomposition properties), trains ML models on a known population, and validates the model's predictive power on larger, held-out test sets.

Abstract

Simulating a continuous-time quantum walk (CTQW) on a graph in the circuit model of quantum computing requires decomposing its Hamiltonian into terms that can be Trotterized into hardware-native gates. We consider two such decompositions: the standard Pauli decomposition and the recently introduced matching decomposition. Prior work suggests that the matching decomposition uses fewer CX gates on sparse graphs, while the Pauli decomposition uses fewer on denser graphs. Since CX gates dominate error and runtime on current hardware, we train machine learning models to predict, for a given graph, which of the two decompositions produces the smaller CX gate count. We train and evaluate on the complete population of all 11,117 connected eight-vertex graphs from Brendan McKay's database, so the class balance and overlap are measured directly rather than estimated. We use twelve features: ten topological properties of the graph and two that count the terms the Pauli and matching decompositions produce (n_Pauli and n_match), both computable without transpiling the simulation circuit. Standard topological properties alone provide little predictive power. Instead, the dominant signal comes from n_Pauli, a property of the Hamiltonian decomposition rather than an intrinsic property of the graph; degree variance is the only other feature that carries signal. Across a range of models the Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC) falls in a narrow band, from 0.569 untuned to 0.593 after tuning, so no single architecture stands out. We adopt a single-hidden-layer neural network at MCC 0.593. Applied frozen to a held-out, class-balanced test set of larger graphs (up to 256 vertices) from structured and Erdos-Renyi families, the model transfers, with MCC rising from 0.785 at N=8 to 1 at N>=64.

Quantifying Entangling Power of Controlled Unitary Gates

Authors: Ankur Haldar, Pankaj Agrawal, Prasenjit Deb
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.21105v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces a novel, computable quantity, $\zeta$, to quantify the entanglement generated by controlled unitary gates in quantum computation. It establishes theoretical upper bounds for this entangling power, showing that the controlled unitary architecture can achieve optimal entanglement for specific dimension pairings. This provides a powerful, state-independent tool for analyzing quantum circuit performance.

Why it may be interesting. The development of a simple, computable metric ($\zeta$) that bypasses full state simulation is highly valuable for analyzing complex quantum circuits, providing theoretical benchmarks for quantum hardware performance.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to develop a computable quantity to quantify the entangling power generated by controlled unitary gates, avoiding the need to simulate the full output state.

Main result. A new computable quantity, $\zeta$, is introduced, which successfully quantifies entanglement and establishes a universal upper bound $\zeta_{ ext{max}} = (2d-1)/(2d+1)$ for the generalized controlled unitary architecture.

Method. The authors derive $\zeta$ using properties of reduced density matrices and trace overlaps, relating it to established measures like purity and linear entropy.

Model / system. The study focuses on two-qubit and multi-qubit systems governed by Controlled Unitary (CU) and Generalized Controlled Unitary (GCU) gates acting on bipartite control and target registers.

Key observables. The primary observable is $\zeta$, the computable entangling power. Other key diagnostics include purity ($\gamma$), normalized linear entropy ($\zeta_r$), and von Neumann entropy ($S( ho)$).

Important parameters / regimes. The dimension of the control ($m$) and target ($n$) registers, and the probability distribution $\{p_i\}$ over the control basis states.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes ideal quantum operations and derives bounds based on specific controlled unitary architectures, noting limitations when extending to arbitrary dimensions.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the entanglement ratio $\zeta_r$ for the Toffoli gate, showing regions of separability and maximal entanglement, and compare theoretical entropy calculations against series approximations.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first defining $\zeta$ for two-qubit gates, then generalizing it to GCU gates, deriving the universal upper bound $\zeta_{ ext{max}}$, and finally establishing functional relations between $\zeta$ and other entropy measures.

Abstract

Applying controlled unitary gates to generate entanglement between qubits is a routine task in both quantum communication and computation. The existing tools for predicting how much entanglement a given gate can generate require either simulation of the entangling circuit or averaging over a distribution of inputs for a given controlled unitary gate. Here, we introduce a computable quantity $ζ$ that not only determines whether a controlled unitary gate generates entanglement, but also quantifies the entanglement for any specific input without requiring the construction of the output state. For two-qubit controlled unitary gates, we establish the physical conditions corresponding to the extremum values of the proposed quantity. Extending the dimension of control and target registers to arbitrary size through a generalized controlled unitary architecture, we derive a universal upper bound on $ζ$ and identify the conditions for its saturation. Later we establish functional relation between the quantity and other known quantities, such as purity, normalized linear entropy, and von Neumann entropy. Finally, we compare our results with previous research work and show that the quantity proposed in this work achieves the previously known optimal values of entangling power of controlled unitary gates for some specific dimensions of target and control registers.

Scaling Behavior of Parameterized Quantum Circuits from a Lie-Algebraic Perspective

Authors: Hiroshi Ohno
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.20603v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper analyzes how the performance of parameterized quantum circuits scales by comparing several metrics of model size. The authors find that while simple counts like parameter number ($P$) or Lie algebra dimensions ($ ext{DLA}$, $ ext{OOD}$) are insufficient, the Jacobian effective dimension ($ ext{Deff}$) provides a robust, geometry-aware measure of the circuit's accessible degrees of freedom. This suggests $ ext{Deff}$ should be used instead of $P$ when characterizing model capacity.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a rigorous, mathematically grounded metric ($ ext{Deff}$) to quantify the true complexity or capacity of variational quantum circuits, which is crucial for designing scalable quantum machine learning algorithms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to characterize how the performance (loss) of parameterized quantum circuits scales with available resources, such as data size, model size, and computational effort.

Main result. The Jacobian effective dimension ($ ext{Deff}$) was shown to be a geometry-aware measure that scales comparably to the nominal parameter size ($P$) and is superior to other Lie-algebraic measures ($ ext{DLA}$, $ ext{OOD}$) which rapidly saturate.

Method. The authors numerically investigated scaling laws by training the circuits on a linear regression task using Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) minimization via SPSA.

Model / system. The model is a parameterized quantum circuit (PQC) defined by a unitary $U( heta) = \prod e^{-i heta_j G_j}$, where generators $G_j$ are sampled from Pauli strings.

Key observables. Loss (RMSE), Dimension of dynamical Lie algebra ($ ext{DLA}$), Observable orbit dimension ($ ext{OOD}$), and Jacobian effective dimension ($ ext{Deff}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Training sample size ($M$), parameter size ($P$), and number of optimization iterations ($T$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is restricted to a regression task and small qubit numbers (four and eight), and the scaling laws are observed over finite ranges.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the decrease in loss with increasing $M$, $P$, and $T$. Figure 3 compares $ ext{DLA}$, $ ext{OOD}$, and $ ext{Deff}$ vs. $P$, showing saturation for the first two and strong correlation for $ ext{Deff}$.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the need to measure resource scaling, analyzes data/model/compute scaling using various metrics ($ ext{DLA}$, $ ext{OOD}$, $ ext{Deff}$), presents results showing $ ext{Deff}$'s superior scaling behavior, and concludes by proposing $ ext{Deff}$ as a robust geometric measure.

Abstract

Understanding how the performance of parameterized quantum circuits scales with available resources is important for characterizing their trainability and effective model capacity. In this study, we numerically investigate data scaling, model scaling, and compute scaling in parameterized quantum circuits and examine Lie-algebraic quantities as alternative measures of model size. In addition to the number of circuit parameters, we consider the dimension of the dynamical Lie algebra, the observable-orbit dimension, and a Jacobian effective dimension defined as the rank of the Jacobian of the parameterized observable orbit. Using a regression task with randomly generated Pauli-string generators, we observe decreasing loss with increasing training dataset size, parameter size, and number of optimization iterations over the ranges investigated. For model scaling, the dynamical Lie algebra and observable orbit dimensions rapidly saturate as the parameter size increases, whereas the Jacobian effective dimension remains strongly correlated with the parameter size and exhibits comparable scaling behavior. These results suggest that the Jacobian effective dimension provides a geometry-aware measure of the locally accessible observable degrees of freedom of finite-depth parameterized quantum circuits and may serve as a useful scaling parameter beyond the nominal parameter size.

Tools for Reducing Service Time in Near-Term Quantum Networks

Authors: Jake Smith, Thomas R. Beauchamp, Scarlett Gauthier, Oumayma Bouchmal, Stephanie Wehner
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.20954v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work introduces a novel method to optimize resource utilization in near-term quantum networks by intelligently shortening the mandatory idle time scheduled between entanglement generation attempts. By analytically modeling the scheduling process, the authors demonstrate significant reductions in total service time, especially when multiple applications share the quantum hardware. This is crucial for making quantum network services more efficient and practical.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a concrete, resource-management optimization technique applicable to the scheduling overhead inherent in building and operating quantum communication infrastructure.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the inefficiency in near-term quantum networks caused by mandatory idle time scheduled between entanglement generation attempts.

Main result. The proposed optimization method can reduce the total network service time by up to 30% when multiple applications are co-scheduled.

Method. It uses an analytical execution model, treating the scheduling problem as an optimization to shorten the scheduled separation time while maintaining required service probabilities.

Model / system. The system is a multi-user quantum network utilizing the Arqon architecture, which generates shared entanglement for quantum information applications.

Key observables. Network service time reduction (up to 30% for co-scheduled apps), session execution speedup, and the optimized scheduled separation time.

Important parameters / regimes. Minimum separation time ($t_{minsep}$), service probability, and the degree of co-scheduling.

Assumptions / limitations. The optimization assumes a balance between minimizing time and ensuring the target number of instances is completed with a specified service probability.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the comparison between full minimum separation and reduced separation in the scheduling process.

Paper structure. The paper identifies the scheduling limitation, proposes the analytical optimization method, applies it to the Arqon architecture, and presents quantitative results comparing reduced vs. baseline service times.

Abstract

Architectures have been proposed to control entanglement generation in multi-user quantum networks. To allow time for local operations and classical communication at end nodes, these architectures insert fixed separations between consecutive batches of entanglement generation attempts. This reduces network utilization when attempts fail, leaving the network idle during the scheduled separation. To address this limitation, we propose a novel method to reclaim this idle time by shortening the scheduled separation between attempts while respecting hardware constraints. The method uses an analytical execution model to optimize the separation and reduce the total network service time of an application. Evaluations within the Arqon architecture show network service time reductions of up to 42 minutes (7.6%) for single applications and 16-29 minutes (26-30%) per application when co-scheduled. The approach applies broadly to quantum network architectures that share hardware between entanglement generation and local operations, and the method can be used online by network schedulers.

Summary. This work demonstrates phase control over magnon-phonon coupling in magnetic thin films by varying an external magnetic field. By tuning the coupling constant's phase from real to complex, the system can couple to overdamped magnons. This mechanism is experimentally observable as a distinct minimum in the SAW transmission spectrum near zero magnetic field.

Why it may be interesting. The ability to tune the coupling phase via magnetic field provides a clean platform to explore non-Hermitian physics and dissipative coupling mechanisms in solid-state magnonics, relevant to open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how the phase of the magnon-phonon coupling constant can be controlled by tuning an external magnetic field in magnetic thin films.

Main result. The phase control allows for coupling to overdamped magnons via an imaginary coupling component ($\Phi = -\pi/2$), which manifests as a pronounced minimum in the SAW transmittance spectrum near zero magnetic field.

Method. The research combines theoretical modeling using coupled equations of motion (LLG and elastic wave equations) with analysis of measurable quantities like SAW transmittance.

Model / system. The system is a magnetic thin film, specifically a Ni/Ru/Ni synthetic antiferromagnet, where magnons interact with surface acoustic waves (SAW) phonons.

Key observables. SAW transmittance ($\lvert S_{21} vert^2$), the phase ($\Phi$) of the coupling constant, and the magnon eigenfrequency ($\omega_m$).

Important parameters / regimes. External magnetic field ($\mu_0H$), magnetic damping ($\alpha$), and the transition field ($H_{tp}$).

Assumptions / limitations. Initial analyses neglect magnetic anisotropy and magnetic dipole interactions, though later work incorporates these effects.

Figures summary. Figures show the dependence of SAW transmittance on magnetic field, illustrating dips associated with both $\Phi=0$ and $\Phi=-\pi/2$ coupling regimes.

Paper structure. The paper develops the coupled equations of motion, analyzes the complex nature of the coupling constant, and then calculates the resulting SAW transmittance spectrum under varying magnetic field conditions.

Abstract

We study the phase of the coupling between magnons and surface acoustic wave (SAW) phonons in magnetic thin films. The coupling constant changes from a real to a complex number as the external magnetic field is reduced. Below a transition field, the imaginary coupling constant allows SAW phonons to couple to overdamped magnons whose resonance frequency is close to zero and far from the SAW resonance. The strength of the imaginary coupling constant and the magnitude of the transition field both scale with magnetic damping. We find the coupling produces a broad, pronounced minimum in the SAW transmittance spectrum near zero magnetic field in a Ni/Ru/Ni synthetic antiferromagnet with large magnetic damping. These results demonstrate that the phase of the complex magnon-phonon coupling constant can be tuned via magnetic field in strongly damped magnets, offering a platform to explore novel regimes of magnon-phonon interactions.

Summary. This work details the controlled fabrication of highly-ordered plasmonic nanostructures on thin gold films using a double-pulse femtosecond laser approach. By managing the transient fluid dynamics through optimized pulse timing, the researchers suppress chaotic melting and achieve structures exhibiting narrow Surface Lattice Resonances. These highly ordered plasmonic arrays are presented as a robust platform for next-generation, ultrasensitive biosensing.

Why it may be interesting. The detailed coupling between electromagnetic excitation (plasmons) and complex non-equilibrium thermofluid dynamics (Marangoni flow) to achieve a desired quantum-optical feature (narrow resonance) is highly relevant to open quantum systems and light-matter interaction in structured media.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is to fabricate highly-ordered Laser-Induced Periodic Surface Structures (LIPSS) on thin metallic films for use in high-sensitivity plasmonic biosensing.

Main result. The use of a double-pulse femtosecond laser scheme, optimized with a specific interpulse delay, successfully controls the fluid dynamics, leading to highly-uniform LIPSS that exhibit narrow Surface Lattice Resonances (SLRs).

Method. The study combines experimental techniques (femtosecond laser processing, SEM, ellipsometry) with theoretical modeling (3D-FDTD, Two-Temperature Model, Navier-Stokes equations).

Model / system. The system involves thin gold films where the process is governed by a synergy of coupled Surface Plasmon Polaritons (SPPs) excitation, electron-lattice thermalization, and subsequent Marangoni fluid flow dynamics.

Important parameters / regimes. Interpulse delay ($\Delta au \approx 1.2$ ns), film thickness (32 nm), and laser fluence ($\Phi$).

Abstract

The fabrication of highly ordered laser-induced periodic surface structures (LIPSS) on thin metallic films is dictated, predomaninatly, by a synergy of periodic electromagnetic energy deposition and complex fluid dynamics. In this work, we present a combined experimental and theoretical study on the formation of ultra-regular LIPSS on 32-nm-thick Au films using a double-pulse femtosecond laser scheme. We demonstrate that for thin films, the excitation of coupled Surface Plasmon Polaritons (SPPs) at both interfaces dictates the initial energy distribution. On the othe hand, the final morphology is greatly influenced by hydrodynamical processes. Interestingly, due to the low electron-phonon coupling of Au and the high thermal confinement of the thin film, single-pulse irradiation leads to uncontrolled hydrodynamic instabilities a non uniform topographies. Thus, we demonstrate that a double-pulse approach with an optimized interpulse delay (Δτ=1.2 ns) effectively controls the melt duration and viscosity, suppressing complex fluid motion and promoting the growth of highly ordered arrays. These structures support narrow surface lattice resonances (SLRs) suitable for high-sensitivity plasmonic biosensing.

Summary. This work reports on the experimental observation and theoretical modeling of spectral anisotropy in transition radiation emitted from biaxial van der Waals crystals. By rotating the sample and measuring polarization-resolved spectra, the authors prove that TR is highly sensitive to the material's orientation-dependent dielectric response. This opens up optical anisotropy as a new diagnostic tool for free electron-driven spectroscopy.

Why it may be interesting. While the core physics is in condensed matter optics, the technique itself—using electron-beam induced radiation to map anisotropic dielectric tensors—is a powerful, non-destructive diagnostic tool applicable to characterizing complex quantum materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how the spectral anisotropy of transition radiation (TR) is influenced by the orientation-dependent dielectric response of biaxial optical media.

Main result. The authors experimentally demonstrate and theoretically model that TR spectra exhibit pronounced, measurable anisotropy when measured from biaxial crystals, confirming that TR is a sensitive probe of the material's optical axis dependence.

Method. The research combines experimental cathodoluminescence (CL) spectroscopy, involving sample rotation and polarization-selective detection, with the development of a thin-film theoretical model.

Model / system. The physical system involves biaxial van der Waals crystals (like GeS and MoOCl2) acting as the source. The model adapts the standard TR intensity formula to incorporate the two principal in-plane dielectric responses ($\varepsilon_{xx}$ and $\varepsilon_{yy}$) to predict orientation-dependent spectra.

Key observables. The primary observables are the polarization-resolved transition radiation spectra, measured as a function of the crystal's rotation angle ($\phi$) and the electron beam energy ($E$).

Important parameters / regimes. Key parameters include the crystal's principal dielectric axes, the electron beam energy (10–30 keV), and the orientation angle ($\phi$).

Assumptions / limitations. The theoretical model uses an effective projection model to combine principal-axis intensities, which is noted as an approximation compared to a full vectorial treatment of the dielectric tensor.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the polarization-selective CL geometry, show angle-resolved spectra for isotropic materials (WS2), and demonstrate the periodic spectral changes and energy tunability for anisotropic materials (MoOCl2) along their principal axes.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the physical problem of anisotropy in TR, details the experimental setup using CL spectroscopy on biaxial crystals, develops an anisotropic thin-film model, and presents results showing the successful reproduction of angle-dependent spectra and energy shifts.

Abstract

In anisotropic optical media, the electromagnetic response depends on the orientation of the optical field relative to the material's principal dielectric axes. While this direction dependence is well understood in conventional optics, it should also influence light-generation processes driven by free electrons. Here, we experimentally observe spectrally anisotropic transition radiation from biaxial van der Waals crystals. Using cathodoluminescence spectroscopy on germanium sulphide (GeS) and molybdenum oxydichloride (MoOCl$_2$) crystals, we show that the transition-radiation spectra differ along the principal in-plane optical axes. To describe this spectral anisotropy, we develop a thin-film transition-radiation model that reproduces the experimental observations. Our results demonstrate that transition radiation is a sensitive probe of the axis-dependent dielectric response of biaxial optical media and suggest that optical anisotropy can provide an additional degree of freedom for free electron-driven spectroscopy, radiation sources, and transition-radiation-based diagnostics.

Summary. This work demonstrates that emergent geometric symmetries dictate the formation of Weyl nodes in interacting multi-terminal Josephson junctions. By confining these topological singularities to a specific manifold ($\chi$-manifold), the authors provide a robust, gate-tunable design principle for creating quantum devices exhibiting non-trivial topological properties.

Why it may be interesting. The explicit connection between geometric symmetries, topological invariants (Weyl nodes), and tunable superconducting junctions provides a powerful, symmetry-protected design principle for realizing exotic quantum states in solid-state devices.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to characterize the mechanism by which an emergent geometric symmetry generates non-trivial band topology, specifically Weyl nodes, within interacting multi-terminal Josephson junctions (MTJJs).

Main result. The symmetry confines the topological singularities (Weyl nodes) to an analytically defined manifold ($\chi$-manifold), providing a general design principle for fabricating devices with predictable topological signatures.

Method. The analysis combines analytical derivations in the Atomic Limit (AL) approximation with numerical simulations using Density Matrix Renormalization Group (DMRG) on the full interacting model.

Model / system. The system is a quantum-dot-based multi-terminal Josephson Junction (MTJJ) modeled by a Hamiltonian involving dot energies, interdot couplings, and superconducting self-energies. The physics is analyzed by examining spectral symmetries.

Key observables. Weyl nodes (with charges $\pm 1$ in the singlet sector and $\pm 2$ in the doublet sector), Chern numbers, and the gap opening between same-spin Andreev bound states (ABS).

Abstract

We show that an emergent geometric symmetry generates non-trivial topology in quantum-dot-based multi-terminal Josephson junctions. It confines same-spin Andreev bound state crossings to an analytic one-dimensional manifold of the synthetic Brillouin zone, where interdot coupling selects Weyl nodes of charge $\pm1$ in the singlet sector and doubly degenerate cones of charge $\pm2$ in the doublet sector, at gate-tunable locations. The mechanism yields a spectroscopic detection protocol and a design principle for fabricating devices with non-trivial topological signatures.

Abstract

We assess whether a non-local thin-film model with a bounded, constant-exponent power law can reproduce high-speed magneto-optical measurements of transient flux penetration in a $3\times3$~mm$^2$ YBa$_2$Cu$_3$O$_{7-δ}$ film. The model is solved forward in physical units and used as a controlled constitutive baseline. The critical sheet current is constrained by the final penetration geometry, while the criterion field and creep exponent are constrained by the turnover and relaxation times. The calibration reveals an intrinsic degeneracy: the turnover time depends primarily on the product of the criterion field and saturation parameter, equivalently on the saturated sheet resistivity, so these quantities cannot be identified independently. After parameterizing this degeneracy, the model captures a guarded-current turnover near 22~ms, a relaxation constant of 41.2~ms compared with the measured 37.5 and 43.7~ms, and a late maximum approximately 0.92~mm from the film edge. The amplitude remains discrepant: the calculated transient maximum is $1.90J_{c0}$ and the peak-to-225-ms ratio is 2.13, compared with the reported 1.20. This excess persists under changes in the creep exponent, the degenerate parameter direction, the Kim field dependence, and mesh resolution. Matching the measured ratio through the criterion field would require a value about three orders of magnitude below the conventional scale, while spatial exclusion sufficient to match it would remove the edge peak itself. We also decompose the electric field into irrotational and solenoidal parts and quantify the boundary layer where spectral and finite-difference forms of Faraday's law disagree. The results establish a reproducible non-local power-law baseline and identify limitations of the bounded, constant-exponent constitutive family rather than of non-local thin-film electrodynamics.

Abstract

We investigate the topological phases, joint density of states (JDOS), optical conductivities, and magneto-optical responses of a two-dimensional $d$-wave altermagnet with spin-orbit coupling and Zeeman splitting. The system hosts Dirac gaps at the high-symmetry points $Γ$, $\textrm{M}$, $\textrm{X}$, and $\textrm{Y}$. We show that the JDOS exhibits kinks at the corresponding Dirac gap frequencies and pronounced peaks at Van Hove singularities, whose positions can be tuned by the altermagnetic order. These features are reflected in the optical conductivities, with complementary signatures in their real and imaginary parts. Particularly, the Van Hove signatures in the transverse optical conductivity disappear in the absence of $d$-wave altermagnetism, revealing an altermagnet-induced optical signature of the Van Hove singularity. Finally, the Faraday and Kerr rotations inherit characteristic features of the optical conductivity. Our results establish optical and magneto-optical spectroscopy as sensitive probes of Dirac gaps and Van Hove singularities in altermagnetic topological systems.
