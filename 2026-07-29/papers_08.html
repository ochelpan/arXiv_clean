Symmetry Criterion for Van Hove Criticality at Non-Time-Reversal-Invariant Momenta

Authors: Min-Quan Kuang, Hua-Yu Li
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.23985v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5 · analog quantum simulation 1/5 · driven-dissipative phase transition 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops a powerful group-theoretical framework to predict when Van Hove Singularities (VHSs) are guaranteed to occur at non-time-reversal-invariant momenta in electronic band structures. By analyzing the little group symmetry, it provides a parameter-independent criterion that dictates whether the band gradient must vanish due to fundamental symmetries of the crystal lattice.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on solid-state band structure, the rigorous application of representation theory to determine fundamental physical constraints ($ abla E=0$) is highly relevant to understanding symmetry protection in quantum materials, which underpins many advanced topics in AMO physics and topological systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper establishes a systematic, symmetry-based criterion to determine whether the band gradient ($ abla E$) vanishes at non-time-reversal-invariant momenta (non-TRIMs) in electronic band structures.

Main result. The vanishing of $ abla E$ is dictated by the representation theory of the little group ($G_{\mathbf{k}_0}$); specifically, for nondegenerate bands, criticality is forced if and only if the vector representation ($\Gamma_{ ext{vec}}$) does not contain the trivial representation ($\Gamma_1$).

Method. The analysis uses group theory (representation theory of the little group) applied to the band dispersion $E(\mathbf{k})$ near high-symmetry points, utilizing Wigner-Eckart theorem and Clebsch–Gordan coefficients for classification.

Model / system. The study analyzes single-particle electronic band dispersions in three dimensions, modeled using a tight-binding framework applied to specific space groups (e.g., 225) under the single-group limit (no SOC).

Key observables. Vanishing or non-vanishing of the band gradient ($ abla E$), which determines if Van Hove Singularities (VHSs) are symmetry-enforced critical points.

Important parameters / regimes. The crystal space group, the specific high-symmetry momentum $\mathbf{k}_0$, and the structure of the little group's vector representation ($\Gamma_{ ext{vec}}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is strictly limited to single-particle band theory in weakly correlated, paramagnetic systems within the single-group (no SOC) limit.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate Brillouin zones marking TRIMs and non-TRIMs; tables summarize criticality ($ abla E=0?$) based on group symmetry for various points and bands.

Paper structure. The paper develops a general criterion using little group theory, applies it systematically to specific space groups (like 225) to classify VHS types at non-TRIMs, and concludes by providing a parameter-independent framework for engineering these singularities.

Abstract

At non-time-reversal-invariant momenta (non-TRIMs), time-reversal symmetry does not constrain the linear term of the band dispersion. Whether $\nabla E$ vanishes is therefore determined entirely by the representation theory of the little group. For nondegenerate bands, $\nabla E$ is forced to zero if and only if the vector representation $Γ_{\mathrm{vec}}$ of the little group does not contain the trivial representation $Γ_1$. When $Γ_{\mathrm{vec}}$ does contain $Γ_1$, $\nabla E$ is not forced to vanish for any nondegenerate band; the classification instead depends on the multiplicity of $Γ_1$ in $Γ_{\mathrm{vec}}$. For degenerate bands, the Wigner-Eckart theorem and Clebsch--Gordan coefficients determine whether linear couplings vanish, with classification performed at the subband level. Applied to space group 225, the criterion explains why the $W$ point is critical for all nondegenerate bands, the degenerate $E$ bands are generically noncritical, and the $K$ and $U$ points host parameter-dependent criticality. Supporting phase diagrams reveal a two-tier hierarchy: symmetry enforces $\nabla E=0$, while band parameters determine higher-order character. We extend this classification to all space groups hosting non-TRIMs in the single-group limit, providing a symmetry-dictated, parameter-independent framework for engineering Van Hove singularities in three-dimensional quantum materials.

Amortized Posteriors for Estimation of Material Constitutive Parameters from Multimodal Measurements on Small Punch Tests

Authors: Mohammad Ali Seyed Mahmoud, Aditya Venkatraman, Raj Mahat, Samantha Mitra, Surya R. Kalidindi
Type: both · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.24534v1

Summary. This paper introduces a novel, scalable framework using Gaussian Processes and Conditional Flow Matching to estimate material properties from combined mechanical test data (Force vs. Displacement and DIC fields). By learning conditional posteriors directly, it overcomes the mathematical difficulties of defining joint likelihoods across different measurement types, providing robust parameter inference for complex materials testing like Small Punch Tests.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical materials science, the core methodology—using advanced generative ML models (Flow Matching) for likelihood-free inference from complex, multi-source experimental data—is highly relevant for analyzing noisy measurements in quantum experiments (e.g., characterizing decoherence rates or interaction strengths).

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is performing Bayesian calibration of material constitutive parameters from multimodal mechanical test data when specifying a joint likelihood across different measurement types is difficult.

Main result. The proposed framework successfully uses DIC measurements to contract broad posteriors derived from force-displacement curves alone, leading to accurate estimation of Young's modulus and yield strength.

Method. A novel amortized, likelihood-free framework combining Gaussian Process (GP) surrogates with Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) is used to learn conditional posteriors directly from synthetic data.

Model / system. The study uses the Small Punch Test (SPT) on aluminum alloys, modeling the material response using an elastic–perfectly plastic (EPP) constitutive model. Measurements include global force-displacement curves and spatially resolved displacement fields from Digital Image Correlation (DIC).

Key observables. Young's modulus ($E$), yield strength ($\sigma_y$), Force–Displacement ($F–D$) curve, and out-of-plane displacement field $w(x, y)$.

Important parameters / regimes. Constitutive parameters ($E$, $\sigma_y$); reference values from independent tensile tests.

Assumptions / limitations. The framework assumes that conditional posteriors can be learned robustly without explicitly constructing a joint likelihood function across modalities.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the experimental setup (DIC), show parameter coupling in $F–D$ data alone, and demonstrate how adding DIC displacement fields contracts the posterior distributions towards known reference values.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem of multimodal Bayesian inference for material parameters. It proposes the GP-CFM framework trained on synthetic data. The utility is demonstrated by applying this to SPT/DIC data, showing improved parameter estimation over using force data alone.

Abstract

Bayesian calibration of material constitutive parameters from multimodal mechanical test data is often limited by the need to specify a joint likelihood across measurement modalities that differ in dimensionality, noise structure, and physical units. The resulting posteriors are often broad or strongly correlated, causing standard Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) samplers to mix poorly. Here, we present an amortized, likelihood-free framework that combines Gaussian process (GP) surrogates with Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) to learn conditional posteriors over constitutive parameters directly from synthetic multimodal parameter--observation pairs, avoiding hand-crafted likelihoods and repeated MCMC sampling. Once trained, the GP--CFM model generates posterior samples for each new specimen at negligible cost. The utility of this novel approach is demonstrated in this paper by estimating the values of Young's modulus and yield strength from the early portion of the force--displacement ($F$--$D$) curve and a Digital Image Correlation (DIC)-based displacement field measured in a Small Punch Test (SPT). It is observed that the $F$--$D$ data alone produce broad posteriors, consistent with limited parameter discrimination in the global response. Adding the DIC-measured displacement field was seen to contract the posteriors and shift them towards the independently measured tensile reference values. This work establishes a robust likelihood-free framework for the inference of material constitutive parameters from multimodal data, demonstrated through SPT--DIC integration.

Efficient LLM-Generated Shuttling Compilers for Complex Trapped-Ion Architectures

Authors: Fabian Kreppel, Reza Salkhordeh, Ferdinand Schmidt-Kaler, André Brinkmann
Type: both · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.24714v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: QC/QI experiment 1/5 · methods for driven-dissipative 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper demonstrates that frontier Large Language Models can autonomously generate highly efficient shuttling compilers for complex trapped-ion quantum architectures. By treating the trap as a graph, the LLMs minimize the required ion movement time steps compared to existing methods. This capability significantly accelerates the development cycle for new quantum hardware designs.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the crucial engineering bottleneck in trapped-ion quantum computing—the translation layer between abstract circuits and physical hardware movements—using advanced AI tools.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core problem is developing efficient software compilers that translate abstract quantum algorithms into physical sequences of ion-qubit movements (shuttling) required by trapped-ion hardware.

Main result. Frontier LLMs can generate working, competitive shuttling compilers for complex architectures without manual algorithmic engineering, drastically reducing development time from months to days.

Method. The authors used state-of-the-art LLMs (Claude Opus 4.7 and Fable 5) to iteratively generate and refine Python code that maps quantum circuits onto physical trap graphs representing ion connectivity.

Model / system. The system is a trapped-ion quantum computer, modeled as a graph where vertices are trap segments and edges represent shuttling paths. The compiler must schedule sequential movements of ions (qubits) across this graph structure.

Key observables. The primary observable is the number of shuttling timesteps required to execute a circuit; reductions up to 76% were reported compared to hand-crafted methods.

Important parameters / regimes. Architectural complexity, measured by connectivity (linear vs. junction-rich graphs), and qubit count (up to 50 qubits tested).

Assumptions / limitations. Shuttling operations are assumed to be executed sequentially; the current focus deliberately excludes scheduling simultaneous gates across multiple segments.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the systematic, chained generation process from simple linear traps to complex general graph architectures. Figure 2 shows the structure of the resulting compiler code, highlighting LLM-written functions within a fixed framework.

Paper structure. The paper systematically develops the compiler by testing increasing architectural complexity: first on linear segments, then on junctions, and finally on broad classes of connected trap graphs using comprehensive test suites (T1-T14).

Abstract

Trapped-ion quantum computers rely on shuttling compilers, which cast an input algorithm into a sequence of ion-qubit movements within a given architecture. We present the first study in which a single frontier large language model (LLM), Claude Opus 4.7, generates and iteratively refines the full Python code of shuttling compilers from written specifications. We start with a compiler for (i) a linear segmented trap, extend it to (ii) a trap with junctions, and finally achieve efficient compilation for (iii) a broad class of connected trap graphs. The compilers for the more general cases are seeded with code from the previous ones. We benchmark the LLM-generated compilers against state-of-the-art hand-crafted ones using a common suite of quantum circuits. The number of shuttling timesteps is reduced by up to 76% for (i) and up to 39% for (ii). For the broad case (iii) of freely connected architectures, we find large variations in the required number of shuttling timesteps, depending on the connectivity. A densely connected, junction-rich architecture yields an order-of-magnitude reduction in shuttling timesteps compared to a corridor-like one. Repeating the complete generation and evaluation with a second frontier LLM, Claude Fable 5, reproduces these findings, with the Fable 5 compilers surpassing the hand-crafted ones more often on the largest circuits. Our results show that an unmodified frontier LLM can produce working, correct, and competitive shuttling compilers without additional manual algorithmic engineering, thus reducing the development time for new architectures from several months to a few days.

Recoverable Quantum Computation: An Information-Centric Paradigm for Quantum Computing with Errors

Authors: Shengwang Du
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.23996v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: entanglement & information structure 1/5 · quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces Recoverable Quantum Computation (RQC), a new paradigm that evaluates quantum computation based on whether task-specific information can be extracted from noisy outputs. Instead of demanding full fault tolerance, RQC focuses on preserving only the necessary computational data. This offers a practical framework for assessing useful quantum advantage in today's imperfect hardware.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a crucial theoretical lens for assessing near-term quantum hardware capabilities beyond simple fidelity measures, directly impacting the feasibility assessment for complex quantum simulations or algorithms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The realization of fault-tolerant quantum computers is hindered by massive error correction overhead, raising the question of whether useful computation must wait for perfect hardware.

Main result. The paper proposes Recoverable Quantum Computation (RQC), an information-centric paradigm showing that useful computational information can be extracted from noisy outputs even without full state fidelity.

Method. It introduces recoverability as an operational principle, defining it by the ability to extract task-specific information with overhead preserving quantum advantage over classical methods.

Model / system. The framework applies to general quantum computation operating in the intermediate regime between current Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) devices and future Fault-Tolerant Quantum Computers (FTQC).

Key observables. Computational information required for a task (e.g., frequency estimate, decision label), recovery overhead, and recoverability efficiency.

Important parameters / regimes. The degree of noise/error; the quantum advantage threshold relative to classical methods.

Assumptions / limitations. It assumes that many practical applications only require extracting specific pieces of information rather than preserving the entire quantum state vector.

Figures summary. Figure 1 conceptually maps the relationship between Classical Computing, RQC (intermediate regime), and FTQC, illustrating useful outcome extraction from noise.

Paper structure. The paper introduces RQC as a paradigm shift, defines recoverability operationally with metrics, illustrates it using examples like QFT period estimation, and concludes by proposing a classification of applications and a research roadmap.

Abstract

Quantum computing promises transformative advances in computation, communication, sensing, and machine learning. Yet the realization of large-scale fault-tolerant quantum computers remains hindered by the enormous overhead required for quantum error correction. This challenge raises a fundamental question: Must useful quantum computing wait until fully fault-tolerant quantum hardware becomes available? In this Perspective, we propose Recoverable Quantum Computation (RQC), an information-centric paradigm for quantum computing with errors. Rather than requiring faithful preservation of the complete quantum state, RQC focuses on preserving the computational information required to accomplish a given task. A quantum computation is considered recoverable if the desired computational information can be extracted from noisy quantum outputs with an overhead that preserves quantum advantage relative to the best known classical method. We introduce recoverability as an operational principle for evaluating noisy quantum computations and propose practical metrics based on recovery overhead and recoverability efficiency. We illustrate the framework using quantum Fourier transform period estimation on IBM quantum hardware and a conceptual example from quantum machine learning, demonstrating that useful computational information may remain recoverable despite significant physical errors. Building on these examples, we propose a preliminary classification of quantum applications according to their expected recoverability and outline a research roadmap toward a predictive theory of recoverability. RQC is intended not as an alternative to fault-tolerant quantum computing, but as a complementary paradigm for understanding and evaluating useful quantum computation in the broad intermediate regime between today's noisy quantum processors and tomorrow's fault-tolerant quantum computers.

The Ruskai-Audenaert conjecture & equipartitions of positive operators

Authors: Niranjan Kumar, Michael M. Wolf

Summary. This paper tackles major open problems in quantum information, such as the Ruskai-Audenaert conjecture and MUB existence, by framing them as equipartition problems for positive operators. The authors develop a sophisticated mathematical framework using equivariant cohomology to prove these conjectures for wide classes of quantum channels and related structures.

Why it may be interesting. The reliance on deep mathematical tools like equivariant cohomology to solve fundamental questions about quantum resource theory (like channel decomposition or POVM existence) provides powerful new theoretical machinery applicable across quantum physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses open problems in quantum information theory, specifically formulating them as equipartition problems for positive operators.

Main result. They prove the strong Ruskai-Audenaert conjecture for a large set of quantum channels and establish necessary and sufficient conditions relating MUB existence to operator decompositions using topological methods.

Method. The authors primarily use advanced mathematical tools, including equivariant cohomology (Borsuk-Ulam theorem) and functional analysis on convex sets of operators, alongside direct algebraic proofs.

Model / system. The core models are quantum channels ($\mathcal{T}$), represented by Choi matrices, and positive operators acting on finite-dimensional complex Hilbert spaces. The context involves decomposing these operators into rank-bounded parts.

Key observables. Equipartition existence (i.e., finding decompositions $P = \sum P_i$ with uniform constraints $f(P_1) = \cdots = f(P_n)$), and the structure of projectors related to MUBs ($R_U$).

Important parameters / regimes. The dimension of the Hilbert space ($d$), the number of components in the decomposition ($n$), and the rank constraints on the parts ($ ext{rank}(P_i) \le r$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis often relies on establishing topological obstructions (non-existence of equivariant maps) or analyzing stationary points of functionals defined over the space of quantum channels.

Figures summary. Not specified in the notes, but the structure suggests figures illustrating geometric constraints on operator spaces and cohomology diagrams.

Paper structure. The paper is structured by first defining the general equipartition problem template. It then applies topological methods (equivariant cohomology) to prove existence theorems for specific physical problems like MUBs and the Ruskai-Audenaert conjecture, culminating in concrete proofs for certain classes of quantum channels.

Abstract

Several open problems in quantum information theory can be formulated as equipartition problems for positive operators, asking for a decomposition into bounded-rank positive parts under uniform constraints. The existence problems for SIC-POVMs and MUBs are of this type, as is the Ruskai-Audenaert conjecture. We first show that certain problems of this form can be attacked using equivariant cohomology, and then present new results on the Ruskai-Audenaert conjecture. In its weak form, this conjecture asserts that every quantum channel admits a convex decomposition into a minimal number of generalized extreme points; in its strong form, one with equal weights. We prove the strong conjecture in all dimensions for a set of channels of nonzero measure, including all cq- and qc-channels, as well as for all channels with qubit inputs. We also prove the weak conjecture for all qutrit channels, along with further results on convex decompositions of quantum channels.

Summary. This paper develops advanced phase-space tools to measure quantumness in systems possessing SU(1,1) symmetry. It establishes that Wigner negativity is a reliable indicator of nonclassicality for these systems, contrasting with other symmetries. By combining this with generalized multipole expansions, the authors offer a comprehensive mathematical toolkit for characterizing quantum states.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous mathematical framework for quantifying quantum resources in systems with hyperbolic symmetries, offering an alternative diagnostic tool beyond standard quadrature measurements relevant to quantum optics and field theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The goal is to systematically characterize and quantify quantum resources (quantumness) for systems governed by SU(1,1) dynamical symmetry using phase-space methods.

Main result. For SU(1,1) systems, Wigner negativity serves as an unambiguous sufficient witness of nonclassicality because the Wigner function remains strictly positive for all coherent states. The analysis provides a comprehensive toolkit combining Wigner negativity and generalized multipoles.

Method. The authors construct covariant quasiprobability distributions on the two-sheeted hyperboloid and analyze quantumness using spectral methods, harmonic expansions, and comparisons between different state representations (e.g., cat states vs. coherent states).

Model / system. The study focuses on quantum systems exhibiting SU(1,1) dynamical symmetry. The phase space is modeled by the two-sheeted hyperboloid or Poincaré unit disk, utilizing Perelomov SU(1,1) coherent states.

Key observables. Wigner negativity (negative volume), Stellar Rank (zeros of the Husimi Q-function), and generalized multipole strengths ($q_{\varrho}(k, \lambda)$).

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis involves parameters related to the excitation number ($n$), hyperbolic coordinates ($\zeta$), and spectral indices ($\lambda$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the applicability of harmonic function theory on the hyperboloid and relies on comparing state properties against those of coherent states.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate density plots of harmonic functions, Wigner functions for various states (coherent, basis, cat), and plots showing multipole norms ($q$) versus spectral index ($\lambda$).

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by establishing the covariant quasiprobability framework on the hyperboloid. It then analyzes key diagnostics like Wigner negativity and stellar rank, introduces generalized multipoles via harmonic expansion, and finally applies these tools to specific quantum states (e.g., cat states) to quantify nonclassicality.

Abstract

Quasiprobability distributions for systems endowed with SU(1,1) dynamical symmetry have received surprisingly little attention, despite the central role of this symmetry in two-photon physics, squeezed states, and nonlinear interferometry. Here, we fill this gap by constructing a full covariant family of $s$-ordered quasiprobability distributions defined on the two-sheeted hyperboloid, or equivalently, on the Poincaré unit disk via stereographic projection. A key result is that the Wigner function is strictly positive for all Perelomov SU(1,1) coherent states, in sharp contrast to the SU(2) case. This positivity endows Wigner negativity with an unambiguous operational meaning: any negative volume is a direct signature of genuinely quantum behavior. We further examine the stellar rank of SU(1,1) states, defined through the zeros of the Husimi $Q$-function, and show how it compares with Wigner negativity as a geometry-adapted witness of nonclassicality in this setting. We further introduce a hierarchy of generalized multipoles through a harmonic expansion of the density operator on the hyperboloid, providing a complementary framework for probing quantumness. This offers a comprehensive toolkit for characterizing and quantifying quantum resources in SU(1,1) systems.

DeComp2: Description Complexity aware Decomposition

Authors: Aritra Sarkar
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.23990v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: entanglement & information structure 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper proposes DeComp2, a new metric for quantum compilation that combines the standard circuit complexity with a Kolmogorov-style description complexity. By minimizing this total cost, it regularizes the optimization landscape to favor structured decompositions over merely shortest ones. This suggests incorporating structural information into compiler objectives for better resource management.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a mathematically rigorous framework for improving quantum compiler design by incorporating information-theoretic measures of program structure alongside physical execution costs.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Quantum compilers currently optimize only for execution resources (like gate count or depth), ignoring the structural complexity of the program that generates the circuit.

Main result. The proposed total cost function, $C_{ ext{tot}} = \alpha C_{ ext{circ}} + eta C_{ ext{Kol}}$, selects compilation candidates that are superior to those chosen by minimizing either execution cost or description length alone.

Method. The authors introduce a regularization term based on Kolmogorov complexity ($C_{ ext{Kol}}$) and minimize the weighted sum of this term with the standard circuit complexity ($C_{ ext{circ}}$).

Model / system. The problem is instantiated for decomposing an arbitrary target unitary $U$ within the $SU(2)$ group, treating the compilation process as finding a sequence of elementary gates.

Key observables. Total Complexity ($\mathcal{C}_{ ext{tot}}$), Circuit Complexity ($\mathcal{C}_{ ext{circ}}$), and Kolmogorov Description Complexity ($\mathcal{C}_{ ext{Kol}}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Weights $\alpha$ and $eta$; the tolerance $\epsilon$ for unitary approximation.

Assumptions / limitations. The approach assumes that total complexity can be modeled as an additive combination of positional (circuit) and kinetic (description) entropy, borrowing structure from physical laws rather than deriving it from them.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the limitation of execution-only optimization, proposes the $\mathcal{C}_{ ext{tot}}$ objective function by analogy to second law thermodynamics, and validates this joint cost on $SU(2)$ decompositions, showing its unique selection capability.

Abstract

Quantum compilers optimize execution-only proxies such as gate count, depth, and fidelity, treating the compiled circuit as the unit of cost. This conflates two distinct resources, how much the substrate has to do at run time, and how much has to be said to describe what to do. Unrolling a looped program leaves run-time cost unchanged while erasing the hierarchical structure on which downstream optimization and reuse rely. We propose lifting the compiler objective from execution to life cycle complexity by pairing the circuit complexity $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{circ}}$ with a Kolmogorov-style description complexity $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Kol}}$ of the emitted circuit, and minimizing the additive total $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{tot}} = α\,\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{circ}} + β\,\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Kol}}$. The mirrors the additive positional and kinetic entropy of the second law of quantum complexity, and reads as a minimum-description-length regularizer over the otherwise degenerate set of $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{circ}}$-minimizers. We instantiate the objective on $SU(2)$ through exhaustive HT-enumeration with compression-based surrogates upper-bounding $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Kol}}$. Across a Haar-representative target grid the two surrogates correlate positively with $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{circ}}$ yet break its rank order on a non-trivial fraction of points, ruling out a tight functional dependence. With calibrated weights the joint cost selects, for a small but operationally meaningful fraction of targets, a candidate that is neither the shortest nor the most compressible HT-string in the $\varepsilon$-ball, exhibiting the compilation choices a $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{circ}}$-only optimizer discards and motivating $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Kol}}$ as an active optimization signal for hierarchical intermediate representation for compiler.

Summary. This paper calculates the vacuum energy induced by a magnetic topological defect on a scalar field in flat spacetime, incorporating terms dependent on spacetime curvature. The key finding is that using general boundary conditions introduces an explicit dependence on the curvature coupling constant ($\xi$), highlighting the crucial role of boundary physics in quantum vacuum effects.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on QFT in curved spacetime, the rigorous treatment of boundary conditions (Robin vs. Dirichlet/Neumann) and the explicit inclusion of curvature coupling ($\xi$) provide general theoretical tools applicable to effective field theories describing quantum systems near boundaries or defects.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how a magnetic topological defect influences the vacuum polarization, quantified by the induced vacuum energy, of a charged massive scalar field in flat (3+1)-dimensional spacetime.

Main result. The induced vacuum energy explicitly depends on the curvature coupling constant ($\xi$) when general Robin boundary conditions are applied, unlike special cases like Dirichlet or Neumann boundaries. This dependence is significant even in flat space-time.

Method. The analysis involves solving the stationary Klein-Gordon equation using Bessel functions and calculating the renormalized expectation value of the energy-momentum tensor ($T_{00}$).

Model / system. The system models a charged massive scalar field interacting with spacetime curvature ($\xi R$) near an impenetrable, finite-thickness cylindrical tube carrying a magnetic flux. The analysis employs the most general Robin boundary condition.

Key observables. Induced vacuum energy density ($\epsilon$ or $E^{(3+1)}_\xi$).

Important parameters / regimes. Curvature coupling constant ($\xi$), magnetic flux ($F$), tube radius ($r_0$), and boundary condition parameter ($ heta$).

Figures summary. Figures compare dimensionless vacuum energy dependence on $ heta$ and tube thickness ($m r_0$), showing crossover points between (3+1)D and (2+1)D results.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the Lagrangian involving curvature coupling, derives the governing Klein-Gordon equation with general Robin boundary conditions, calculates the vacuum energy using $T_{00}$, and compares the resulting energy in (3+1)D to known limits and lower dimensions.

Abstract

We investigated how a magnetic topological defect affects the vacuum polarization of a charged massive scalar field in a flat $(3+1)$-dimensional space-time. The defect was modeled as an impenetrable to matter field finite-thickness tube with magnetic flux inside. We implemented the most general form of the Robin boundary condition on the surface of the magnetic tube, which enables a fully general analysis of the problem. We have found that in flat spacetime, the total vacuum energy generated by a magnetic topological defect depends on the curvature $ξ$, except for special cases corresponding to the Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions. By contrast, when Robin's general boundary conditions are imposed, the induced vacuum energy acquires an explicit dependence on the curvature coupling $ξ$, which is significant even in flat space-time. A detailed study of the dependence of the effect on the boundary condition parameter has been carried out. The obtained results highlight the nontrivial role played by boundary conditions in vacuum polarization phenomena.

Summary. This paper introduces MPStab, a novel hybrid simulator that combines the exact simulation power of stabilizer formalism for Clifford gates with the efficiency of tensor networks for non-Clifford rotations. It allows for polynomial-time simulation of complex quantum circuits by treating different gate types optimally. This is crucial for benchmarking and understanding the computational boundaries between classical and quantum computation.

Main problem. Efficiently simulating quantum circuits on classical computers, which is challenging due to exponential state space growth from entanglement.

Main result. The proposed hybrid simulator (MPStab) achieves polynomial-time simulation for circuits mixing Clifford and non-Clifford gates by combining exact stabilizer tracking with tensor network evolution.

Method. It decomposes the unitary circuit into alternating Clifford blocks (simulated exactly via stabilizers) and single-qubit rotations (encoded as bond-2 Matrix Product Operators, MPOs).

Model / system. The method targets general quantum circuits composed of Pauli strings and arbitrary unitaries, which are decomposed into Clifford ($C$) and rotation ($R$) components.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the simulator's modular structure, the operational workflow combining stabilizer backpropagation and TN forward evolution, and benchmark results comparing time cost and fidelity against pure tensor network methods across varying magic content.

Paper structure. Abstract

Pair-density-wave (PDW) superconductivity is a state in which the superconducting order parameter modulates at a finite wavevector. Using large-scale density matrix renormalization group, we study the doped altermagnetic Mott insulator in the checkerboard $t$-$J$ model, where altermagnetic exchange anisotropy is encoded microscopically through anisotropic ferromagnetic next-nearest-neighbor exchange. Abstract

We introduce the pair second-order correlation function $g_{\textrm{pairs}}^{\left(2\right)}=\left\langle \left(P^{\dagger}\right)^{2}P^{2}\right\rangle /\left\langle P^{\dagger}P\right\rangle ^{2}$, defined through the pair operator $P^{\dagger}=a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}$, to characterize second-order correlations and pair bunching and antibunching in photon-pair creation processes. This quantity directly probes correlations between pair-generation events within a single two-mode quantum state, providing access to the intrinsic pair-generation process beyond conventional single-mode or heralded second-order correlations, which do not directly capture correlations between pair events. Values of $g_{\textrm{pairs}}^{\left(2\right)}$ greater than, equal to, or less than unity correspond respectively to pair bunching, Poissonian pair statistics, and pair antibunching. Using the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, we further show that all classical two-mode fields described by a positive Glauber--Sudarshan \ensuremath{P}-function satisfy $g_{\textrm{pairs}}^{\left(2\right)}\geq1$, so that pair antibunching is classically forbidden and constitutes an unambiguous signature of nonclassicality. We evaluate $g_{\textrm{pairs}}^{\left(2\right)}$ for several representative quantum states and show, in particular, that even arbitrarily weak two-mode squeezed vacuum states exhibit pair bunching. Comparison with heralded second-order correlations highlights the complementary information provided by these observables. The proposed correlation function is experimentally accessible via standard coincidence measurements, requires no phase reference or state reconstruction, and remains invariant under uniform loss.

Abstract

Neural quantum kernels (NQKs) construct quantum kernels by pretraining a quantum neural network (QNN) and subsequently reusing the trained circuit as a task-adapted embedding. Extending this framework to qudits, with local unitaries in $\mathrm{SU}(d)$, provides a natural route to richer data embeddings through the increased local degrees of freedom and a direct interface for multiclass classification via intrinsically multi-level quantum systems. In this work, focusing on qutrits ($d=3$), we extend NQKs to the qudit setting and perform a systematic study of key design choices, including the number of encoded features, the number of qutrits, the kernel construction (1-to-$n$ and $n$-to-$n$), and the parameterization of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ unitaries. Across binary and three-class tasks on four benchmark datasets, qutrit NQKs improve over the corresponding QNN baselines in nearly all settings considered and can benefit from scaling both the feature budget and the system size, although the magnitude of these gains may saturate, is dataset-dependent, and depends on the chosen parameterization. In particular, an ablation over $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ parameterizations shows that the unitary representation can substantially impact both optimization behaviour and classifier performance. These findings highlight the potential of qudit-based quantum models not only as a straightforward generalization of qubit-based architectures, but also as a promising means to better exploit complex data structures in quantum machine learning.
