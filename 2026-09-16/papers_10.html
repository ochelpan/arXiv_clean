Exact computation of quantum wave functions for nonlinear potentials

Authors: Winfried Loghmiller, Jean-Jacques Slotine
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.14480v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper details a novel, exact theoretical method to compute quantum wave functions for systems with nonlinear potentials. It achieves this by transforming the complex nonlinear Schrödinger equation into an equivalent, solvable linear harmonic oscillator problem using harmonic coordinate transformations and time scaling. This breakthrough allows for non-perturbative solutions for systems beyond the standard textbook examples.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, non-perturbative theoretical framework for finding exact solutions to quantum problems that are typically intractable, offering a rigorous alternative to semi-classical approximations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the exact computation of quantum wave functions for systems governed by nonlinear potentials, aiming to surpass the limitations of standard approximate methods like quantum perturbation theory.

Main result. The authors present a method that constructs the exact quantum wave function by transforming the nonlinear Schrödinger equation into a time-scaled, linear harmonic oscillator form via harmonic coordinate transformations.

Method. The core method involves solving the Hamilton-Jacobi equation for the classical action, followed by applying a harmonic coordinate transformation and time scaling to map the problem onto a known solvable linear system.

Model / system. The theory applies to quantum systems described by the Schrödinger equation, particularly those with nonlinear potentials (e.g., quartic potentials, nonlinear pendulums) or position-dependent inertia tensors.

Key observables. Action ($\varphi$), propagated classical density ($ ho$), and the resulting quantum wave function ($\psi$).

Important parameters / regimes. The method is effective for 1 to 3-dimensional systems, where the transformation simplifies the potential to that of a linear oscillator.

Assumptions / limitations. The method fundamentally requires that the propagated classical square root density remains harmonic throughout the transformation process.

Figures summary. The notes do not provide a summary of figures, but mention the derivation of the kernel $K(t', q')$ and the resulting eigenwaves $\psi_{kx,ky}$.

Paper structure. The paper builds from solving the Hamilton-Jacobi equation using least action paths, introduces the harmonic coordinate transformation and time scaling, proves the equivalence to the linear harmonic oscillator, and then applies this framework to solve specific nonlinear potentials.

Abstract

Recent work shows that the Schroedinger equation can be solved exactly based only on classical least action rspa.2025.0413. The computation is based on first solving a Hamilton-Jacobi equation for the action, computing the classical propagated density along all stationary action paths, and finally constructing the exact kernel and wave function based on these classical quantities alone. The method requires that the propagated classical square root density along each stationary action path is harmonic (where a sufficient condition is that the Laplacian of the propagated action is purely time-varying), as is the case for basic examples such as the double-slit, quantum tunneling, the Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen experiment, or also as discussed here the relativistic propagator of a Higgs boson. This density is fundamentally different from using the Madelung density, which is the norm of an existing overall quantum wave. In the case of arbitrary nonlinear potentials, this condition can still be verified without loss of generality, by using a harmonic coordinate transformation and an associated time scaling for the propagated classical density and quantum wave, as this paper details. The resulting quantum wave of the time-scaled Schroedinger equation is equivalent to the quantum wave of the original Schroedinger equation, extending a standard result of Duru and Kleinert. Hence, in principle it can replace the approximations of quantum perturbation theory. For 1 to 3-dimensional systems, this harmonic coordinate change transforms a nonlinear potential into the quadratic potential of a linear oscillator, for which an exact analytic action and wave solution are already known. This makes the construction of the quantum wave beyond the hydrogen wave straightforward for basic cases where no exact solution has been derived, such as the quartic potential or the nonlinear pendulum.

Failure of the Asymptotic Equipartition Property for Quantum Channels

Authors: Gilad Gour
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.15004v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper rigorously analyzes the Asymptotic Equipartition Property for quantum channels, finding that the standard formulation using trace-preserving maps fails. It establishes that the correct formulation relies on subchannel smoothing, proving its equivalence to several key quantum information theorems. The work highlights a fundamental separation between the constraints imposed by exact trace preservation versus those imposed by subchannel approximations.

Why it may be interesting. It provides deep theoretical insights into the fundamental limits of quantum communication channels, distinguishing between physical constraints (trace preservation) and mathematical approximations (subchannel smoothing).

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the Asymptotic Equipartition Property (AEP) for quantum channels, specifically comparing the AEP formulated using smoothing over completely positive trace-preserving maps (CPTP) versus smoothing over subchannels.

Main result. The AEP formulated using CPTP maps fails in general, while the AEP using subchannels is shown to be equivalent to several other strong theoretical statements regarding quantum channel divergences.

Method. The analysis involves comparing various quantum channel divergences (like max-relative entropy and channel Lorenz divergence) and constructing explicit counterexamples using qutrit channels to demonstrate the asymptotic gap between the two smoothing types.

Model / system. The study focuses on abstract quantum channels, represented by pairs of channels ($N$ and $M$), and their behavior under different types of approximation or 'smoothing' procedures.

Key observables. Channel divergences such as max-relative entropy ($D_{\max}$), regularized relative entropy ($D_{ ext{reg}}$), and channel Lorenz divergence ($D_L$).

Important parameters / regimes. The asymptotic limit ($n o \infty$), the error threshold ($\epsilon$), and the smoothing parameter ($\delta$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on establishing equivalences between four conjectured formulations of the AEP for subchannels, though the validity for general channel pairs remains open.

Figures summary. Not specified (The notes describe theoretical results and comparisons of rates rather than figure contents).

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the failure of the CPTP-smoothed AEP, then proves the equivalence of four different mathematical formulations of the AEP under subchannel smoothing, and finally constructs a counterexample demonstrating the unbounded asymptotic gap between the two smoothing types.

Abstract

We establish two results on the asymptotic equipartition property (AEP) for quantum channels. First, the AEP formulated using smoothing over completely positive trace-preserving maps fails in general. Second, the AEP with smoothing over subchannels (completely positive trace-nonincreasing maps) is equivalent to the strong converse for parallel quantum channel Stein's lemma, to a sharp threshold for the channel hockey-stick divergence, and to an AEP for the channel Lorenz divergence. The counterexample reveals a surprising separation between channel and subchannel smoothing. Although states and classical channels admit a sharp, dimension-independent completion bound, which we establish, the corresponding single-shot gap for quantum channels is unbounded already for qubits. This separation persists asymptotically: we construct qutrit channel pairs with finite max-relative entropy whose CPTP-smoothed rate strictly exceeds the regularized channel relative entropy. The asymptotic gap between channel and subchannel smoothing is unbounded across the family, even when the subchannel approximation error decays exponentially. To establish the equivalences, we develop a uniform filter that converts Lorenz smoothing into a single nearby subchannel. The four conjectured formulations thus share a common asymptotic threshold, whose validity for general channel pairs remains open. Together, these results separate this threshold question from the obstruction imposed by exact trace preservation.

Abstract

Hybrid quantum-classical neural networks provide a promising approach for incorporating quantum circuits into machine learning in the noisy intermediate-scale quantum regime. However, existing hybrid models often rely on fixed or globally shared fusion strategies, which may limit their ability to exploit complementary information carried by quantum branches, especially under distribution shifts. In this work, we propose a Feature-Adaptive Fusion Hybrid Quantum-Classical Neural Network (FAF-HQNN) for biomedical image classification. The model combines a classical deep feature encoder with a variational quantum circuit (VQC) and introduces a feature-adaptive fusion mechanism to dynamically weight classical and quantum predictions. We evaluate FAF-HQNN on two MedMNIST benchmarks, PathMNIST and BloodMNIST, under clean and corrupted test conditions. FAF-HQNN achieves the strongest overall performance on clean data among the compared methods and shows improved robustness under Gaussian, salt-and-pepper, and Poisson corruptions. Further analysis of circuit layout, measurement basis, and depth shows that even shallow variational quantum circuits can provide useful complementary information. These results demonstrate that feature-adaptive fusion is an effective strategy for improving accuracy and robustness in hybrid quantum-classical models for biomedical image classification.

Summary. This paper tackles the challenging problem of optimizing 2D cutting stock layouts by reformulating it into a QUBO model. By using techniques like unbalanced penalization, they create a quantum-compatible objective function that can be solved on quantum annealers. This work bridges industrial optimization problems with the practical constraints of near-term quantum hardware.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a concrete, complex combinatorial optimization problem (CSP) that is successfully mapped onto the QUBO framework, directly motivating and testing the applicability of quantum annealing hardware.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper focuses on formulating the complex Multistage 2D Cutting Stock Problem (CSP) into a Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) format suitable for quantum computation.

Main result. A non-exact QUBO reformulation is derived for the restricted version of the CSP, allowing the incorporation of usable leftover material into the objective function.

Method. The approach involves translating the problem from a linear programming formulation into a QUBO form using unbalanced penalization to handle inequality constraints.

Model / system. The theoretical framework is applied to the 2D CSP, which is then mapped onto the Hamiltonian structure of quantum annealers, referencing trapped-ion architectures like MAGIC.

Key observables. The objective function to be minimized, which relates to minimizing cuts and maximizing usable leftover area ($A_{ ext{rest}}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The coupling strengths ($J_{i,j}$) and the penalty coefficients ($\lambda$) used in the QUBO formulation.

Assumptions / limitations. The derived QUBO formulation is non-exact, and the work primarily focuses on the restricted formulation of the CSP.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates different 2D CSP types, contrasting general cuts with simpler Guillotine cuts.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the relevance of CSPs, presents the linear programming model for the restricted case, derives the non-exact QUBO reformulation using penalization, and discusses extensions to the unrestricted problem and potential quantum hardware implementations.

Abstract

Cutting stock problems are of large relevance to a variety of industry branches. Here, we present a linear programming formulation for a restricted version of the multistage 2D cutting stock problem with Guillotine cuts and test it on published benchmark instances. After this, we derive a non-exact reformulation in quadratic unconstrained binary optimization (QUBO) form via unbalanced penalization, which we show to have possible benefits in modeling the problem with usable leftovers. This work mainly considers the restricted formulation, in which the dimensions of each cut are determined by those of a single required piece. We further discuss how the approach can be extended to the unrestricted problem, which allows more general cutting patterns involving cuts with dimensions of multiple required pieces and naturally gives rise to quadratic terms in the inequalities of the problem formulation. We showcase solutions via a Simulated Annealing solver for the restricted problem class, where we mainly study the performance of the iterative augmented Lagrangian method. We also discuss possible applications of Quantum Annealing in this setting, as a strong motivation to research QUBO formulations.

Glassiness and dynamic arrest in magnetic and non-magnetic colloids

Summary. This work uses molecular dynamics simulations to study glassiness in magnetic and non-magnetic colloidal fluids. By analyzing the van Hove function and employing the CTRW model, the authors quantify how magnetic dipole interactions promote specific structural arrangements and modify the underlying slow dynamics compared to simple LJ systems. The findings advance the understanding of relaxation mechanisms in magnetically responsive soft matter.

Why it may be interesting. The detailed analysis of dynamic heterogeneity using advanced statistical mechanics tools like the van Hove function and CTRW formalism provides a quantitative framework for understanding how long-range, anisotropic interactions (like dipoles) fundamentally alter the relaxation pathways in glassy materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates and compares various indicators for glassiness and dynamic arrest in both magnetic and non-magnetic soft-sphere fluids, specifically quantifying the influence of magnetic moments.

Main result. The inclusion of dipolar interactions leads to branched, open morphologies and a heavier tail in the van Hove function compared to non-magnetic systems, indicating strongly correlated motion and modified relaxation mechanisms.

Method. The research employs extensive Molecular Dynamics (MD) simulations, analyzing dynamics through the self-part of the van Hove function and fitting the data using the Continuous Time Random Walk (CTRW) formalism.

Model / system. The system consists of monodisperse soft-sphere fluids simulated using either a pure Lennard-Jones (LJ) interaction (non-magnetic) or the Stockmayer model (SM) which includes anisotropic dipolar interactions. The simulations involve rapid quenching deep below freezing temperatures.

Key observables. Self-part of the van Hove function, Non-Gaussianity Parameter ($\alpha(t)$), Edwards-Anderson (EA) parameter ($q_{EA}$), Softness Order Parameter ($S_i$), and the Magnetization ($M$).

Important parameters / regimes. Low temperatures, deep quenches, dipole strength ($\mu$), and the exponents $t_1$ and $t_2$ derived from the CTRW fit.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on separating the total van Hove function into contributions from vibrational motion and particle jumps within the M-CTRM formalism, though the authors note this separation may be imperfect.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the fitting parameters ($l, d, t_1, t_2$) and the resulting van Hove function for both LJ and SM fluids, comparing the quantitative results derived from the CTRW model.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the physical models (LJ vs. SM), then characterizing static properties and dynamics using multiple observables ($g(r), q_{EA}, S_i$), and finally applying the advanced M-CTRM formalism to fit the time-dependent van Hove function.

Abstract

We investigate and compare a range of indicators for glassiness in monodisperse magnetic and non-magnetic soft-sphere fluids at low temperatures with a view to exploring the effect of the magnetic moment. We perform extensive molecular dynamics simulations using the Stockmayer model for magnetic fluids and a pure Lennard-Jones interaction for the non-magnetic case. Our investigations involve quenching experiments, in which both systems are rapidly cooled deep below their freezing temperatures. Although the Lennard-Jones fluid forms compact aggregates, the inclusion of dipolar interactions promotes the development of branched and open morphologies. After characterizing the static properties of the frozen structures, we focus on their dynamics. A key observable is the self-part of the van Hove function, which measures the probability that a particle is displaced by a distance $Δ$ over time $t$. In both fluids, this function exhibits non-Gaussian behavior --- thereby providing a signature of dynamic heterogeneity and glassiness. This behavior stems from a separation of time scales between two distinct processes: mobile particles that escape their environments and immobile particles that vibrate within cages. In particular, we find a heavier tail in the van Hove function for the Stockmayer fluid, which is a consequence of the strongly correlated motion in chain-like structures found there. These findings shed light on core relaxation mechanisms in magnetic fluids, advancing our understanding of magnetically responsive colloidal systems.

By applying critical scaling analysis, the researchers determined that the material's critical exponents match those of the 3D Heisenberg model. This suggests the dominant magnetic interactions are short-range, while also proposing theoretical significance regarding potential 2D magnetic ordering.

Why it may be interesting. The study bridges the gap between dimensionality effects (2D fluctuations vs. 3D critical exponents) in magnetic materials, which is a core topic in critical phenomena and statistical mechanics.

Abstract

LaCrSb3 is a material exhibiting both quasi-two-dimensional spin fluctuations and three-dimensional magnetic interaction characteristics. By measuring the isothermal magnetization of single-crystals and conducting a systematic critical behavior analysis, we clarify the critical properties of its ferromagnetic phase transition and the intrinsic magnetic interaction mechanism. Based on high-precision isothermal magnetization data measured in the vicinity of the critical point, the Curie temperature for the ferromagnetic-paramagnetic phase transition is determined to be TC = 126 K, with the critical exponents obtained as $β$ = 0.376, $γ$ = 1.417 and $δ$ = 4.76 via the self-consistent iterative method based on the Arrott-Noakes equation. The reliability of these critical exponents is verified by the Widom scaling law, the magnetic state scaling equation and other analyses. A comparison with theoretical models demonstrates that the critical behavior of the magnetic phase transition in this system basically belongs to the universality class of the three-dimensional Heisenberg model. This conclusion is further confirmed by the distance-dependent decay behavior of the exchange interaction J(r), revealing the dominant role of isotropic direct exchange interactions in this system. Finally, drawing on research findings of other quasi-two-dimensional magnetic materials, this work proposes that LaCrSb3 may exhibit finite-temperature magnetic order in the two-dimensional limit, thereby possessing important theoretical research significance and promising practical application prospects.

Abstract

Quantum Amplitude Amplification (QAA), the generalization of Grover's algorithm, is well-positioned for combinatorial optimization and is particularly promising for Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) problems. QAA is appealing due to its ability to drive the quantum system to a target state, yielding the globally optimal solution with probability over $90$+%. However, realizing QAA for application-scale optimization currently exceeds quantum hardware capacity, which is further compounded by unresolved algorithmic challenges regarding the choice of free parameters. In this study, we address these issues by utilizing a realistic traffic flow QUBO problem to investigate the implementation of QAA as a heuristic solver. Specifically, allowing the diffusion operator parameter to take non-$π$ values expands the capabilities of QAA. This unlocks a new multiple-shot, low-iteration strategy that aims for a high cumulative probability rather than maximizing the probability of a single basis state. Using $25$-qubit simulations, our results demonstrate that heuristic QAA addresses two of the main challenges cited in previous studies. Firstly, heuristic QAA works even at a single iteration, reducing circuit depth by orders of magnitude compared to standard QAA. And secondly, we show that the problem-dependent parameters necessary for reaching optimal algorithmic performance can be reliably approximated with minimal upfront classical computing overhead.

Abstract

Ce$_2$Te$_5$ is a layered $f$-electron van der Waals magnet in which reduced dimensionality and inequivalent Ce sites give rise to competing magnetic interactions. We investigate its magnetic ground state using muon spin relaxation ($μ$SR), neutron powder diffraction (NPD), and inelastic neutron scattering (INS). Zero-field $μ$SR reveals an onset of static magnetism below $T_{\mathrm{C}} = 5.0(1)$~K, followed by an additional anomaly in the internal field at $T_{\mathrm{2}} = 2.3(2)$~K, consistent with features observed in bulk thermodynamic and transport measurements. Abstract

Classical-to-quantum I/O is a critical bottleneck for data-intensive quantum workloads. Quantum Oracle Sketching (QOS) has been proposed to address this problem. We identify its practical requirements, including the representation of data as a probability distribution over oracle addresses, the relation between sample count, approximation error, and update cost, and the coherence and runtime support required for online execution. To study QOS on current quantum computing devices, we develop an offline realization that compiles sample blocks into empirical phase oracles and evaluate it using DNA fingerprinting. The application converges with relatively few samples, while exact phase fusion combines repeated updates to the same address. However, the compiled circuits remain too deep for current quantum computing devices. Practical QOS therefore requires lower depth oracle synthesis and interactive quantum runtimes.

Summary. This work addresses the discrepancy between classical and quantum predictions for the vibrational spectra of LiF at low temperatures. Abstract

Recent studies have shown that classical LiF simulations can reproduce low-temperature experimental spectra when performed at a simulation temperature $T^*$ higher than the physical temperature $T$. We show that the characteristic scale of this rescaling follows naturally from quantum vibrational statistics. An effective temperature $T_{\rm eff}(T)$ is obtained by matching classical and quantum harmonic energies and is evaluated using an analytic acoustic-optical model and a first-principles LiF vibrational density of states $D(\varepsilon)$. The resulting temperature reproduces the low-temperature scale of the reported spectral temperature and approaches the physical temperature in the classical regime. Our results identify zero-point motion and Planck-Bose-Einstein statistics as the origin of the reported temperature scale.
