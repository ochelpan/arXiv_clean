Quantum Codes for Generalized Amplitude-damping Noise

Authors: Sourav Dutta, Anubhab Rudra, Manav Seksaria, Anil Prabhakar, Prabha Mandayam
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.15924v1

Summary. This work develops the Probabilistic Approximate Quantum Error Correction (PAQEC) framework to combat Generalized Amplitude-damping (GAD) noise. By optimizing recovery maps using semidefinite programming, the authors construct a five-qubit code that achieves superior, quadratic scaling of fidelity loss compared to existing methods. This offers a resource-efficient path toward building reliable quantum computers for near-term devices.

Why it may be interesting. The focus on GAD noise, which is highly relevant to superconducting and trapped-ion quantum hardware, provides a concrete, realistic noise model for developing fault-tolerant quantum computation protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of protecting quantum information from Generalized Amplitude-damping (GAD) noise, a realistic source of decoherence, using Quantum Error Correcting (QEC) codes.

Main result. The authors introduce Probabilistic Approximate Quantum Error Correction (PAQEC) and demonstrate a five-qubit code that achieves a fidelity loss quadratic in the damping strength, significantly outperforming conventional codes.

Method. The framework combines approximate QEC with post-selection recovery, formulated and optimized using numerical techniques based on Charnes–Cooper and semidefinite programming.

Model / system. The system involves qubits subjected to GAD noise, modeled by specific Kraus operators involving damping strength ($\gamma$) and temperature-dependent parameters ($p$). The core structure is a five-qubit permutation-invariant code.

Key observables. Entanglement fidelity ($F_{ ext{ent}}$), probability of successful recovery, and the scaling of fidelity loss with noise strength.

Important parameters / regimes. Damping strength ($\gamma$), temperature-related parameter ($p$), and the noise parameters $(\gamma', p')$ used during optimization.

Assumptions / limitations. Conventional deterministic QEC codes fail to correct for first-order GAD errors; the PAQEC framework is proposed to overcome these limitations.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the action of GAD errors on codewords, the syndrome-based recovery protocol using ancilla qubits, and comparisons showing the numerical code outperforming the standard PI code in fidelity.

Paper structure. The paper surveys existing AQEC/PQEC methods and GAD noise, introduces the five-qubit PI code, formally defines PAQEC conditions, and finally uses semidefinite programming to find optimal recovery maps and codes.

Abstract

Quantum error correcting (QEC) plays a crucial role in protecting quantum information against decoherence and enabling scalable, reliable quantum computing. One of the most realistic and ubiquitous sources of noise affecting quantum hardware today is generalized amplitude-damping (GAD) noise. Conventional, deterministic QEC codes struggle to correct for GAD noise because of their inherent structure, leading to fidelity losses that scale linearly with the damping strength. In this work, we introduce the framework of probabilistic approximate quantum error correction (PAQEC), that combines the flexibility of approximate QEC with the potential of post-selected recovery, enabling high-fidelity, resource-efficient error correction. We construct a five-qubit permutation-invariant code that, under probabilistic recovery, achieves a fidelity loss quadratic in the damping strength, thus outperforming existing QEC codes. Formulating PAQEC as an optimization problem, we present a numerical technique based on Charnes-Cooper and semidefinite programming to identify the optimal recovery map for any PAQEC code. Our results establish PAQEC as a powerful tool for developing resource-efficient, high-fidelity quantum codes tailored to realistic noise, with promising implications for near-term quantum devices and future fault-tolerant architectures.

Summary. This work develops an effective string model for doped antiferromagnets to explain the pseudogap phase. By treating fluctuating Néel order as domain walls with vortex ends, the authors map the pseudogap transition onto a topological BKT-type crossover. The findings suggest that the pseudogap is governed by the confinement of these string ends rather than simple symmetry breaking.

Why it may be interesting. The use of topological concepts (vortices, confinement/deconfinement) to describe a phase transition in a strongly correlated material is highly relevant to understanding emergent order in quantum many-body systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the long-standing puzzle of the microscopic origin of the pseudogap phase in cuprate superconductors, linking it to magnetic fluctuations and doping.

Main result. The pseudogap boundary ($T^*$) is identified as a topological crossover associated with the confinement/deconfinement of vortex ends in the effective string model, rather than the onset of a local order parameter.

Method. The authors developed an effective model based on fluctuating Néel domain walls (stripes) and analyzed its phase diagram using classical Monte Carlo simulations.

Model / system. The system is a doped antiferromagnet modeled by an effective string theory where domain walls are treated as 1D strings. The Hamiltonian incorporates terms for the spin background and vortex interactions, leading to a lattice gauge theory description.

Key observables. Percolation-based geometric order parameters (POPs), the $U(1)$ correlation function decay (algebraic vs. exponential), and the compressibility (showing a Widom line).

Important parameters / regimes. Doping ($\delta$), temperature ($T$), and the ratio of correlation lengths ($\xi^* \gg d$).

Assumptions / limitations. The effective model assumes the correlation length of the spin background ($\xi^*$) is much larger than the separation of the open string ends ($d$).

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically illustrates the phase diagram, distinguishing between the spin-pseudogap regime and the hidden-order pseudogap phase via fluctuating stripes and vortex ends.

Paper structure. The paper develops the effective string model, analyzes the phase diagram using Monte Carlo simulations to detect percolation and BKT crossovers, and interprets these transitions in the context of the pseudogap physics.

Abstract

The microscopic origin of the pseudogap phase constitutes a longstanding puzzle related to the emergence of high-temperature superconductivity in cuprate materials. In this work, we develop an effective model for doped antiferromagnets in terms of fluctuating stripes, or string-like domain walls, which obscure the antiferromagnetic order of the spin background. The open ends of such domain walls of the N'eel order are treated as vortices in the resulting lattice gauge theory. We numerically evaluate the phase diagram by classical Monte Carlo simulations, using percolation-based geometric order parameters to diagnose hidden N'eel order. At high temperatures, we identify a BKT-type crossover in which the domain wall ends become deconfined. We interpret this as the $T^*$ crossover from the hidden order regime to the paramagnetic metal above. At low temperatures, we identify stripe instabilities. Predictions of our effective model can be tested in ultracold fermion quantum simulators.

Fermionic quantum error correction is never free

Authors: Yifan Tang, Ingo Roth, Philippe Faist, Zi-Wen Liu, Jens Eisert, Zhenhuan Liu

Summary. This paper proves that fermionic quantum error correction cannot be achieved using only free-fermion (Gaussian) dynamics, unlike the standard qubit model. It establishes a fundamental resource overhead, showing that the required non-Gaussian gates scale linearly with the desired error protection strength and logical capacity. This highlights a major physical hurdle for building scalable quantum computers based on fermionic systems.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a rigorous theoretical constraint on the physical implementation of quantum computation using fermionic systems, suggesting that achieving high error correction thresholds requires going beyond simple quadratic Hamiltonians.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To determine the fundamental physical resources required for implementing quantum error correction (QEC) on fermionic platforms.

Main result. Exact fermionic QEC necessarily requires non-Gaussian operations, and the required overhead scales at least linearly with both the code distance and the number of encoded modes.

Method. The analysis uses fundamental incompatibility arguments between fermionic error correction and Wick's theorem, alongside bounding the fidelity achievable using Gaussian operations.

Model / system. The study focuses on fermionic quantum codes, which can be realized on platforms like Majorana-based qubits or fermionic cold atoms. The dynamics are contrasted between free-fermion (Gaussian) regimes and general non-Gaussian operations.

Key observables. Code distance ($d_F$), logical capacity ($k$), Gaussian fidelity ($F^{(g,q)}_{g,q}(C)$), and the required number of non-Gaussian gates.

Important parameters / regimes. Code distance ($d_F \geq 3$), number of encoded modes ($n$), and the resource scaling $\Omega(d_F + k)$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis contrasts the fermionic setting with the qubit setting (where stabilizer operations are Gaussian) and examines the limitations of Gaussian operations in both QEC and entanglement distillation.

Figures summary. No specific figure summaries were extracted, but the notes detail mathematical proofs involving projectors, isometries, and fidelity bounds.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the necessity of non-Gaussian operations for QEC (Theorem 1), then quantifying the resource cost (Theorem 2), and finally contrasting this with the capabilities of Gaussian operations in entanglement distillation (Theorem 3).

Abstract

Fermionic platforms offer compelling architectures for quantum computing, ranging from topologically protected Majorana-based qubits to fermionic cold atoms. To achieve scalability, however, they require quantum error correction. In this work, we prove that any exact and sufficiently accurate approximate fermionic quantum error correction necessarily requires non-Gaussian operations, beyond the free-fermion regime of quadratic dynamics. This is in sharp contrast to the qubit setting, where the efficiently classically simulable stabilizer operations form the standard framework for quantum error correction. Specifically, we show that the logical space of any non-trivial fermionic error-correcting code contains no pure fermionic Gaussian state, utilizing a fundamental incompatibility between fermionic error correction and Wick's theorem. We further show that the required number of bounded-weight non-Gaussian gates for unitary codeword preparation grows at least linearly with both the code distance and the number of encoded modes, revealing an intrinsic resource overhead that increases simultaneously with error-protection strength and logical capacity. Furthermore, we analyze the performance of fermionic Gaussian operations in entanglement distillation, revealing a distinction from their bosonic counterparts. Our results reveal fundamental difficulties for fermionic error correction from the perspectives of both physical implementation and classical simulation, suggesting connections to fermionic phases of matter and state preparation complexity.

Summary. This paper constructs a specific 3D probabilistic cellular automaton, called the 'coincube,' to model fermionic quantum field theories. It successfully demonstrates that the automaton supports a single Weyl fermion with exact relativistic kinematics. The work is significant for establishing a deep, non-trivial connection between classical computation models and fundamental quantum field theory.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete, non-standard lattice realization of relativistic fermionic dynamics using classical probabilistic rules, offering a novel computational framework for studying QFTs.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper tackles the open problem of constructing an exact equivalence between fermionic Quantum Field Theories (QFTs) in three dimensions and probabilistic cellular automata (PCA).

Main result. The authors construct a 'coincube' layered automaton that realizes the free single-fermion sector with exact relativistic kinematics, demonstrating an isotropic Weyl cone.

Method. The construction uses a layered automaton operating on a 3D cubic lattice, where environment bits stream outrunning the carrier's causal cone to ensure independence of updates.

Model / system. The model is a 'coincube' layered automaton on $\mathbb{Z}^3$, utilizing a carrier species whose state is governed by the real quaternion representation of $\text{Cl}(0, 3)$, alongside autonomous binary environment fields.

Key observables. The single-carrier excitation is confirmed as a Weyl fermion with an exactly isotropic leading cone; the step operator is shown to be unitary for an explicit complex structure.

Important parameters / regimes. The environment field density $q$ is a key parameter, with the vacuum speed $v(q) = 2 |1 -2q|$ being a critical diagnostic.

Assumptions / limitations. The construction relies on the 'fresh-tape' property, ensuring that environment streaming outruns the carrier's causal cone; a dichotomy exists between isotropic cones and in-state unitarity.

Figures summary. The notes do not detail specific figures, but mention Table II mapping principal claims to verification scripts and Table III collecting exact expressions for free and dressed spectra.

Paper structure. The paper follows a structure of establishing the physical problem, detailing the 'coincube' automaton construction (layers, streaming, quaternion coin), verifying the equivalence via the single-particle propagator, and concluding with a discussion of constraints and open questions regarding unitarity and cone existence.

Abstract

Wetterich showed that certain 1+1D probabilistic cellular automata are equivalent to fermionic QFTs and posed the 3D construction as an open problem. We construct one: a layered automaton ("coincube") whose conversion blocks form the real quaternion representation of Cl(0,3), controlled by binary environment fields whose streaming outruns the carrier ("fresh tape") -- no bit is consulted twice, and the single-particle ensemble propagator equals the annealed Bloch operator at every time. Exact results: the step operator is unitary for an explicit complex structure; the local Grassmann action is extracted mechanically; the single-carrier excitation is a Weyl fermion with an isotropic leading cone (slope ratios 1 within errors, factorized transport excluded by >=10 error bars); at finite momentum the node carries a parity-odd anisotropy c(q)kx ky kz/|k|, cancelled on the Dirac branch whose gap is 2 arctan[qm/(1-qm)], while off-resonant spectators stay massless; media-mediated interaction obeys Ug = (1-2gq^2) U. Obstructions: ballistic transport admits only finite velocity sets; Abelian sign gauges leave the factorized surface; no protected 2-component node survives an exhaustive search. Finally, a dichotomy for the quaternionic event class: an isotropic cone forces overdamping -- Q = w_0/Gamma <= pi/(2 ln 2) at all densities, 0.66 at working density -- while in-state unitarity forces factorized transport. Postselected two-boundary amplitudes evade this, exactly unitary at all times under the fresh tape, with induced weight positivity open: the relativistic object here is an amplitude whose probabilistic reading is unsettled.

Handling Imperfections in Subcircuit Compilation for Linear Optical Quantum Computing

Authors: Tobias Forster, Yannick Stade, Andreas Fyrillas, Jean Senellart, Lukas Burgholzer, Robert Wille
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13379v1

Summary. This paper presents the first automated compiler for subcircuits in Linear Optical Quantum Computing (LOQC) that explicitly models hardware imperfections. By optimizing photon routing and placement within a localized 'computation zone,' the method maximizes the coincidence rate. The results show significant performance gains, demonstrating a path toward more robust and scalable photonic quantum processors.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly tackles the engineering bottleneck of scaling photonic quantum computation by providing a rigorous, optimized compilation framework that accounts for realistic hardware losses, which is crucial for any physical realization of quantum computation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of compiling quantum subcircuits for Linear Optical Quantum Computing (LOQC) while accurately accounting for hardware imperfections like photon loss and phase noise.

Main result. The proposed compilation method significantly improves the coincidence rate—the probability of successfully measuring all desired photons—by up to 33.2% compared to existing methods.

Method. It introduces a novel compilation strategy that localizes the computation to a 'computation zone' and uses dynamic programming on a layered Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) to optimize photon routing while balancing input/output port losses against internal beam splitter losses.

Model / system. The system is LOQC implemented using linear optical circuits, which are modeled using interferometers (like Mach-Zehnder Interferometers) and are described by unitary transformations on Fock states.

Key observables. Coincidence Rate (probability of successful photon measurement), Fidelity (Total Variation Distance), and overall circuit robustness against phase noise.

Important parameters / regimes. Beam splitter reflectivities, input/output port transmission rates, and the degree of phase noise (e.g., 15 or 30 mrad).

Assumptions / limitations. The problem is mapped onto a layered DAG structure, allowing for efficient dynamic programming; current evaluations are based on simulations.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the building blocks of LOQC circuits, the embedding of a subcircuit onto a larger architecture (Bell-Walmsley), and the 'Problem Graph' used for the optimization algorithm.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the physical problem (LOQC imperfections), then details the limitations of current compilers, proposes the localized subcircuit compilation method using graph theory and dynamic programming, and finally validates the approach by comparing coincidence rates against baseline methods under varying noise conditions.

Abstract

Linear Optical Quantum Computing (LOQC) is emerging as a promising technology in large-scale quantum computing. Corresponding devices, including linear optical circuits, are rapidly growing in size and fabrication precision. However, even with improved quality, the circuits still exhibit imperfections leading to photon loss or phase noise that vary across the circuit. Already small deficiencies can have a vast impact on both accuracy and efficiency. For computations that do not require the entire linear optical circuit, so-called subcircuits, one can choose where to perform the computation, opening optimization opportunities that are addressed by subcircuit compilation. Current compilers spread the subcircuit's computation across the entire circuit, connecting the same input and output ports, thereby failing to fully exploit the optimization opportunities. However, given the subcircuit's reduced size compared to the entire circuit, one can keep its implementation localized and intentionally route photons to connect good input and output ports. This, however, is a highly non-trivial task, as numerous options must be weighed against one another, and the quality of the solution is influenced by a wide range of hardware parameters. In particular, the reflectivities of imperfect beam splitters can lead to photon losses during routing, which must be traded off against losses at the input and output ports due to imperfect transmission rates. Since current compilers do not account for these effects, we propose the first efficient subcircuit compilation method that optimizes for the coincidence rate, the probability of successfully measuring all desired photons, by handling hardware imperfections. Our evaluations demonstrate significant improvement over existing methods in robustness against phase noise and significantly increased coincidence rates across all benchmarks of up to plus 33.2%.

Summary. This research resolves the ambiguity of orbital ordering in MnV2O4 by combining high-precision synchrotron X-ray diffraction with advanced theoretical calculations. Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, direct experimental route (VED mapping) to probe subtle orbital degrees of freedom in strongly correlated materials, which is crucial for understanding magnetism and electronic structure in frustrated systems.

Main problem. The paper resolves the long-standing controversy regarding the orbital-ordered ground state in the Mott-insulating vanadium spinel oxide, MnV2O4.

Abstract

Orbital ordering in vanadium spinel oxides with a geometrically frustrated pyrochlore structure has been a subject of controversy, owing to competing theoretical models and the absence of direct experimental evidence. Here we combine high-precision single-crystal synchrotron x-ray diffraction with core differential fourier synthesis to visualize the valence electron density (VED) of the orbital-ordered ground state in real space. By carefully investigating multiple-scattering artifacts, we identify the low-temperature structure as belonging to the I41/amd space group. The reconstructed VED around the V sites reveals an orbital-ordered state distinct from both previously proposed real- and complex-orbital models. Our results resolve the long-standing controversy in MnV2O4 and establish a route to identifying orbital states in frustrated spin-orbital systems.

Summary. This paper analyzes a modified, asymmetric Hopfield-type memory model designed to exhibit sequential pattern retrieval. Using advanced techniques like Generating Functional Analysis, the authors derive the dynamics of the system's order parameters. They establish that the memory's capacity scales differently with interaction order compared to standard symmetric models, providing theoretical bounds for pattern storage.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study focuses on the retrieval dynamics and storage capacity of an $n$-body asymmetric dense associative memory model, specifically investigating sequential pattern retrieval.

Main result. The asymmetric coupling induces sequential retrieval characterized by a stable limit-cycle attractor, and the normalized storage capacity decreases monotonically with increasing interaction order $n$ for $n \ge 3$.

Method. The analysis employs Generating Functional Analysis (GFA) to derive the dynamical equations, followed by saddle-point approximations to characterize the order parameters in the thermodynamic limit.

Abstract

We study the retrieval dynamics of an $n$-body asymmetric dense associative memory model, in which sequential retrieval is implemented by shifting one pattern index in the higher-order Hebbian interaction. The model exhibits sequential retrieval of the stored patterns, and the retrieval state corresponds to a stable limit-cycle attractor. The storage capacity is defined as the maximum loading rate for which this limit cycle remains stable. Using generating functional analysis, we derive the dynamical equations describing the sequential retrieval state. The generating functional analysis reveals that the retarded self-interaction term vanishes in the asymmetric model, and the resulting overlap dynamics is reproduced by an Amari-Maginu-type signal-to-noise analysis. Also, we find that for the interaction order $n\ge 3$, the normalized storage capacity decreases monotonically with increasing interaction order, in contrast to symmetric Modern Hopfield models.

Abstract

A core component of this task is the ability to determine whether the actual evolution of the system coincides with the target evolution. Abstract

Randomizing the readout time averages the mode interference in a quantum walk. We ask when this makes the site populations of a uniformly coupled $XY$ chain exactly uniform for every initial site. For $N\ge2$ spins, this is possible when $N+1$ is a power of two, a prime, or twice a prime. It is impossible when $15\mid(N+1)$, $21\mid(N+1)$, or $6\mid(N+1)$ with $N\ge11$. Uniformity uniquely fixes the averaged cosine coherences according to the mirror parities of the modes. Equal Bohr frequencies can impose incompatible coherence values. We classify these parity collisions using vanishing sums of roots of unity and prove existence in the positive cases through a phase distribution on a torus.

Abstract

Relativistic $5d$ Mott insulators host a rich variety of exotic hidden multipolar order, whose microscopic nature remains heavily debated. A broken local point-symmetry (BLPS) phase followed by antiferromagnetism was previously observed in the doped compound Ba$_2$Na$_{0.1}$Ca$_{0.9}$OsO$_6$, where mixed Os valence places the system toward the $5d^2$ end of the $5d^1$-$5d^2$ regime. In contrast, pristine $5d^2$ Ba$_2$CaOsO$_6$ has shown a single octupolar transition with average cubic symmetry, raising the question of whether BLPS arises from chemical disorder in the doped systems. Here, we discover the BLPS phase in pristine Ba$_2$CaOsO$_6$ emerging at $T^* \approx 50$ K, precisely coincident with the octupolar transition. Beyond standard ${}^{43}$Ca nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectral analysis, our spin-echo nutation measurements detect a finite electric field gradient, demonstrating spontaneous symmetry breaking despite the cubic average structure. While our NMR data support the previously identified octupolar order, the BLPS phase reveals a subdominant quadrupolar contribution, establishing that the hidden order in Ba$_2$CaOsO$_6$ is intrinsically multicomponent. Such mixed multipolar states lie beyond conventional theoretical descriptions of the $5d^2$ ground state. Our results establish local symmetry breaking as an intrinsic property of the clean $5d^2$ limit and provide a new constraint on microscopic theories of strongly spin-orbit-coupled Mott insulators.
