Modulated structures in discrete approximations to a three-dimensional cholesteric model on a recursive lattice

Authors: William de Castilho
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.15891v1

Summary. This paper analyzes the modulated structures in a 3D cholesteric liquid crystal model by mapping its statistical mechanics onto a nonlinear recursion on a Cayley tree. By examining different discrete approximations, the authors predict the emergence of various stable periodic attractors, demonstrating how chirality dictates the selection of modulated critical modes. The work highlights the difference between mathematical attractor stability and true thermodynamic equilibrium.

Why it may be interesting. The use of recursive lattice structures and stability analysis of nonlinear maps to characterize phase behavior in soft matter systems provides a powerful, non-equilibrium theoretical tool applicable to complex many-body systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the formation and stability of modulated structures within discrete approximations of a three-dimensional cholesteric liquid crystal model.

Main result. The analysis reveals that the system supports various modulated attractors (e.g., period-two, period-three) whose stability depends critically on the chosen lattice discretization and the chirality parameter $\Delta$.

Method. The statistical problem is mapped onto a nonlinear recursion relation on a rooted Cayley tree, which is analyzed in the infinite-coordination limit as a softmax map for state probabilities.

Model / system. The system is a chiral nematic liquid crystal modeled using a rotated quadrupolar interaction on a lattice. Approximations are made by restricting local directors to finite state sets (e.g., Cartesian three-state or planar four-state).

Key observables. Attractor stability (fixed points and cycles), periodicity (e.g., period-two, period-three), and the selection of a modulated critical mode.

Important parameters / regimes. Chirality parameter ($\Delta$), reduced temperature ($t$), and the lattice discretization type (three-state, planar four-state, non-planar four-state).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis interprets results as attractor/stability diagrams, not guaranteed equilibrium phase diagrams, due to the nature of the recursive map analysis.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the attractor/stability diagrams for the three-state and planar four-state discretizations, showing regions corresponding to different stable periodic states.

Paper structure. The paper analyzes the model on a Cayley tree, deriving a nonlinear recursion. It then systematically analyzes three different state discretizations (three-state, planar four-state, non-planar four-state), determining the stability of the resulting map's fixed points and periodic attractors as a function of temperature and chirality.

Abstract

We study finite-state approximations to a chiral nematic lattice model whose local directors are embedded in three-dimensional orientational space. The model analyzed here is formulated in terms of a rotated quadrupolar interaction, which provides a direct angular chirality parameter $Δ$ and reduces to the usual planar chiral-nematic form for directors confined to the xy plane. On a rooted Cayley tree, the statistical problem is written as a nonlinear recursion for branch partition functions. In the infinite-coordination limit, with $rJ_Q$ fixed, the recursion becomes a softmax map for the state probabilities. We analyze Cartesian three-state, planar four-state, and non-planar four-state discretizations. The first supports ordered and period-two attractors, whereas the planar four-state discretization displays a sequence of commensurate and longer-period modulated attractors. Its disordered fixed point loses stability at $t=9/16$ through a complex-conjugate pair with phase $q=\pm 2Δ$, so a modulated critical mode is selected for every nonzero chirality. The non-planar four-state set is intrinsically anisotropic and, when the stated model is reconstructed directly, supports prominent period-three as well as period-two and higher-period cycles. Because attractor stability does not by itself establish global thermodynamic stability, the diagrams reported here are interpreted as attractor/stability diagrams rather than equilibrium phase diagrams.

Summary. This work analyzes the critical phase transition in Borromean Supercounterfluids (BSCF) in 2D, a system characterized by more topological excitations than Goldstone modes. Using Renormalization Group theory, the authors derive scaling laws that predict a BKT-type transition, providing quantitative predictions for superfluid stiffnesses near the critical point.

Why it may be interesting. The study provides a deep theoretical understanding of topological phase transitions in multicomponent superfluids, connecting concepts like Goldstone modes, vortex physics, and critical exponents using advanced RG techniques.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless (BKT)-type phase transition in Borromean Supercounterfluids (BSCF) in two dimensions, especially when symmetry is slightly broken.

Main result. The critical behavior is predicted to be BKT-type, and the analysis yields explicit scaling laws for superfluid stiffnesses and phase boundaries, showing how topological excitations govern the transition.

Method. The primary method involves developing and solving Renormalization Group (RG) equations for the system's effective action, analyzing flow equations in different proximity regimes to critical points.

Model / system. The system is a counterflow superfluid (SCF) with $N \ge 3$ components, modeled using the J-current model or a two-component representation of a BSCF. The key feature is the existence of $N$ elementary topological excitations despite $N-1$ Goldstone modes.

Key observables. Superfluid stiffnesses ($\Lambda_s, \Lambda_d$), vortex fugacities ($g_s, g_d$), and correlation lengths ($\lambda_c$).

Important parameters / regimes. The asymmetry parameter ($\Gamma$ or $\xi$), the golden ratio constant ($\varphi$), and the critical coupling constants ($\xi^{(c)}_s, \xi^{(c)}_d$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on linearizing the RG equations near the symmetric point and making approximations regarding the smallness of asymmetry parameters.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate scaling functions for superfluid stiffnesses, the phase diagram showing boundaries dependent on the golden ratio, and the flow of correlation lengths with system size.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by establishing the RG framework for the multicomponent superfluid, analyzing the symmetric limit, then introducing asymmetry via coupled RG equations, and finally deriving explicit scaling solutions for different proximity regimes to the critical point.

Abstract

The characteristic feature of counterflow superfluids consisting of $N\geq 3$ components -- the so-called Borromean supercounterfluids (BSCF) -- is the presence of $N$ distinct elementary topological excitations (vortices) despite having only $N-1$ independent Goldstone modes. We show that this remarkable property clearly manifests itself at the Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless-type transition from the BSCF to the normal state, under the conditions of slight to moderate deviations from the case of exact intercomponent symmetry. More generally, our analysis also applies to any multicomponent superfluid with intercomponent drag fine-tuned to the value when certain composite vortices compete energetically with elementary ones.

Abstract

Summary. This paper presents a Floquet-engineered framework to generate multimode NOON states in bosonic networks. By coupling $d$ bosonic modes to a $d$-level quantum controller, the authors achieve controlled chiral routing, which realizes an exact cyclic permutation. This provides a systematic protocol for quantum-enhanced sensing applications.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, generalizable theoretical tool (Floquet engineering) to implement complex quantum operations (cyclic permutations) necessary for generating highly entangled states like NOON states, which are cornerstones of quantum metrology.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is to develop a systematic method for preparing multimode NOON states, which are crucial for achieving Heisenberg-limited phase sensitivity in quantum metrology.

Main result. The authors establish a general Floquet construction that enables the preparation of odd-$d$-mode NOON states by controlling chiral excitation flow using a $d$-level quantum controller.

Method. The method employs a Floquet-engineered conditional-routing framework, deriving an effective Hamiltonian that couples the bosonic modes' dynamics to the state of a multilevel controller.

Model / system. The system is a hybrid model consisting of $d$ bosonic modes coupled to a $d$-level quantum controller. The dynamics are controlled by periodically modulated interactions, analyzed via an effective Hamiltonian $\hat{H}_{eff} = \hat{S}_z \otimes \hat{H}_{cyc}$.

Key observables. The final state is the $d$-mode NOON state $|NOON(l)\rangle_d$; key diagnostics include the controlled cyclic permutation of bosonic excitations and the resulting phase sensitivity.

Important parameters / regimes. The dimension $d$ must be odd ($d=2s+1$). The protocol requires tuning the controller state to control the direction of chiral excitation flow.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the high-frequency regime approximation (van Vleck expansion) and assumes specific energy level spacings for the controller and bosonic modes.

Figures summary. FIG. 1 schematically illustrates the hybrid system coupling a $d$-level system to $d$ bosonic modes, mediated by the controller.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the physical model and the need for controlled routing. It derives the effective Hamiltonian and analyzes the controlled cyclic permutation achievable at specific time intervals. Finally, it outlines the multi-step protocol using this controlled routing to generate the target NOON states.

Abstract

The NOON states possess Heisenberg-limited phase sensitivity and are crucial for quantum-enhanced metrology. In our work, we develop a Floquet-engineered conditional-routing framework in which a $d$-level quantum controller mediates the dynamics of $d$ bosonic modes. For an arbitrary odd dimension $d=2s+1$, the $d$-level system can act as a ``quantum knob" by tuning its initial states to coherently control the direction of chiral excitation flow, such that different controller eigenstates generate distinct cyclic-routing branches of the bosonic excitations. By choosing $s$ harmonics, we construct an exact qudit-controlled cyclic permutation and use it to formulate a general protocol for preparing odd-$d$-mode NOON states. The same framework also enables programmable complex amplitudes through appropriate preparation of the controller state. As concrete realizations, we show that the three-mode case requires only a single harmonic and exhibits two oppositely directed chiral branches together with a stationary branch, whereas the five-mode case requires two harmonics and both nearest- and next-nearest-neighbor chiral hoppings. Meanwhile, taking the three-mode realization as a representative example, we assess the influence of systematic imperfections and dissipation on the conditional-routing dynamics and identify parameter regimes in which the routing operation remains accurate. Our results establish a systematic Floquet construction for controllable chiral routing and multimode NOON-state generation in arbitrary odd-dimensional networks, with potential applications in quantum information processing and quantum metrology.

Abstract

The classical capacity of a quantum channel is the highest rate at which classical information can be transmitted with vanishing error. Abstract

Summary. This theoretical paper rigorously analyzes quantum control universality for spin systems by examining the dynamical Lie algebra generated by low-order operations. Model / system. The system models quantum control on symmetric subspaces of identical spin-1/2 particles, which are isomorphic to spin-N/2 representations of su(2). The control operations include rotations and quadratic, spin-squeezing interactions.

Abstract

Universality relies on combining operators with different algebraic properties, which depend on the physical structure of the Hilbert space encoding the information. Systems of identical symmetric particles---including bosons and symmetric collections of spin-$1/2$ particles---can be mapped to angular-momentum systems, where rotations supplemented by quadratic, spin-squeezing interactions provide universal control. We revisit universality in this framework and confirm that such linear and quadratic operations provide universal control on every fixed-particle-number subspace. At the abstract level, however, we show that the dynamical Lie algebra generated by all degree-at-most-two elements does not exhaust the full universal enveloping algebra of $\mathfrak{su}_{\mathbb{R}}(2)$, contrary to a stronger claim made previously: higher powers of the quadratic Casimir element cannot be generated by commutators and linear combinations. This obstruction does not conflict with fixed-sector universality because, under a fixed irreducible spin-$n/2$ representation, all powers of the Casimir element act as scalar multiples of the identity. We determine the resulting abstract dynamical Lie algebras exactly and show that the central-free and full degree-at-most-two algebras map, respectively, onto $\mathfrak{su}_{\mathbb{R}}(n+1)$ and $\mathfrak{u}_{\mathbb{R}}(n+1)$, establishing special-unitary and full-unitary universality. We further derive a practical degree-two universality criterion and discuss finite-dimensional SSRC universality.

Abstract

Motivated by Haag duality in quantum spin systems, we show that Haag duality for a pair of commuting factors generated by increasing sequences of finite-dimensional matrix algebras is equivalent to the asymptotic vanishing of a suitable conditional mutual information. For two-dimensional spin systems, let an annulus separate a finite region from the exterior. Haag duality then holds for a region $A$ if and only if the mutual information of the inner region and the exterior, conditioned on the part of $A$ inside the annulus, vanishes as the outer radius becomes large. As a corollary, assuming a strict area law with subleading corrections, Haag duality holds for single cones, and it holds for unions of two or more disjoint cones precisely when the topological entanglement entropy vanishes. For translation-invariant pure states on spin chains, half-chain Haag duality is equivalent to vanishing entropy density.

Abstract

Unlike in Anderson localization - where any amount of uncorrelated disorder localizes all eigenstates in one dimension - a one-dimensional system with quasiperiodic potential supports a richer range of localization behavior. This paper investigates the fundamental question of which potential characteristics govern localization properties. We characterize quasiperiodic potentials using two independent parameters, correlation and bias, and demonstrate that bias, in addition to correlation, critically influences localization. Through the study of an extended Aubry-André model, in which a tunable parameter allows systematic control over both the bias and correlation of the potential, we show that bias is key in determining the fraction of delocalized states. Abstract

We study rare nonresonant regions in a canonical one-dimensional disordered quantum spin chain by directly implementing the iterated Schrieffer-Wolff construction underlying the recent work of De Roeck, Giacomin, Huveneers and Prosniak on subdiffusive transport. For finite systems, we compute the probability that a disorder realization remains nonresonant through successive scales of the flow, while separately monitoring operator proliferation and the spatial localization of dressed local observables. The survival probability decays at most exponentially with system size over the numerically accessible regime. Resolving this decay scale by scale yields failure rates associated with successive Schrieffer-Wolff steps. The first rate is obtained analytically in excellent agreement with numerics, while higher-scale rates decrease rapidly throughout the regime studied. These results support the summability mechanism required for exponentially rare but parametrically long nonresonant regions, which provide the insulating bottlenecks responsible for subdiffusive transport. At the smallest couplings studied, the survival probability under our conditions exceeds the rigorous lower bound by six to seven orders of magnitude, demonstrating how conservative the constants required by the proof are while confirming that its underlying physical mechanism is quantitatively visible at accessible scales. More broadly, our results show how direct numerical implementations can provide an independent and physically transparent test of technically demanding constructive proofs, a methodology that may become increasingly useful as machine-assisted proofs become more common.
