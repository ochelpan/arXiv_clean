Exact Breit-Rabi formulae for the nitrogen-vacancy center in diamond

Authors: Alex Tritt, Lincoln D. Turner, Michael S. J. Barson
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.14964v1

Summary. This paper derives exact, closed-form Breit-Rabi formulae for the energy levels and eigenstates of the NV center in diamond for both $^{14} ext{N}$ and $^{15} ext{N}$. By providing these analytic solutions, the authors improve upon existing numerical and approximate methods, which is vital for high-precision quantum sensing and quantum computing applications.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides fundamental, exact theoretical tools for understanding spin dynamics in solid-state quantum systems, which is crucial for designing robust quantum sensors and qubits based on NV centers.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To derive exact, closed-form expressions for the energy eigenvalues and eigenstates of the Nitrogen-Vacancy (NV) center in diamond under an axial magnetic field.

Main result. The authors provide analytic, closed-form solutions for the energy levels and eigenstates for both $^{14} ext{N}$ and $^{15} ext{N}$ isotopes, surpassing previous numerical or approximate methods.

Method. The derivation involves solving the characteristic polynomial of the spin Hamiltonian, utilizing trigonometric and algebraic techniques to find exact roots and corresponding eigenstates.

Model / system. The physical system is the NV center in diamond, modeled by a fine-structure spin Hamiltonian incorporating Zero-Field Splitting (D) and hyperfine coupling terms for both the electron spin ($S=1$) and the nitrogen nuclear spin ($I=1$ or $I=1/2$).

Key observables. Energy eigenvalues ($\lambda_j$ or $E_n$) and the corresponding eigenstates ($\psi_j$ or $|\psi_n angle$), particularly focusing on level anticrossings (GSLAC, ESLAC).

Important parameters / regimes. Zero-Field Splitting ($D$), hyperfine coupling constants ($A_{\parallel}, A_{\perp}$), and the magnetic field components ($\gamma, \gamma_I, B_z$).

Assumptions / limitations. The initial Hamiltonian is assumed to be solvable analytically, although approximations (like quadratic expansions) are used to analyze specific regimes such as anticrossings.

Figures summary. Figures compare the derived analytic eigenvalues and eigenvectors against point-wise numerical evaluations for both $^{14} ext{N}$ and $^{15} ext{N}$ centers, demonstrating agreement across different magnetic field regimes.

Paper structure. The paper systematically addresses the problem for both $^{15} ext{N}$ and $^{14} ext{N}$ isotopes, deriving the Hamiltonian matrix, solving the resulting characteristic equations using advanced mathematical techniques, and finally applying the results to predict key spectroscopic features like level anticrossings.

Abstract

The hyperfine structure that the nitrogen-vacancy (NV) center in diamond has with its nitrogen nucleus is of immense importance for quantum sensing and quantum computing. Current methods of calculating the NV hyperfine energy eigenvalues and eigenstates with respect to an axial magnetic field - i.e., calculating the NV Breit-Rabi formula - resort to numerical methods and analytic approximations. Here we provide an exact, closed-form expression to the NV hyperfine energy eigenvalues and eigenstates for both the nitrogen-14 and -15 cases.

Exact solutions of hidden free fermion models

Authors: Mingchen Zheng, Balázs Pozsgay, Shu Chen
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.14134v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work provides a comprehensive algebraic framework for understanding Hidden Free Fermion (HFF) models, unifying previously disparate integrable systems. By relating these models to the degeneration of a specific R matrix, the authors derive functional relations that determine the exact many-body spectrum. This advances the understanding of spectral organization and boundary effects in strongly correlated quantum chains.

Why it may be interesting. The deep connection between algebraic integrability (R-matrices, Yang-Baxter equations) and the emergence of simple, solvable spectra (free fermions) in complex interacting models is highly relevant for understanding quantum many-body physics in condensed matter systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper establishes a unified algebraic framework to understand Hidden Free Fermion (HFF) models, which exhibit free-fermion spectra without an explicit quadratic representation.

Main result. A broad class of HFF models is shown to arise from Lax operator realizations of a twisted multistate Perk--Schultz R matrix, providing a common description for various spectral regimes.

Method. The authors utilize the Yang–Baxter equation, fusion techniques, and the analysis of reflected degenerations of the R matrix to derive functional relations for commuting transfer matrices.

Model / system. The primary model is the Hidden Free Fermion (HFF) Hamiltonian, exemplified by the inhomogeneous free-fermions-in-disguise (FFD) chain. The analysis extends to face-type and general integrable chains.

Key observables. The many-body spectrum determined by the functional relations of the transfer matrices, and the existence of an emergent cubic finite-size gap coexisting with the exact z=3/2 bulk behavior.

Important parameters / regimes. Coupling constants ($h_j$), boundary conditions (open vs. periodic), and the size $N$ of the system.

Assumptions / limitations. The framework assumes the existence of an underlying integrable structure governed by the Perk–Schultz R matrix and its associated Lax operators.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate zero-root configurations for the FFD chain under both open and periodic boundary conditions, and compare rescaled finite-size gaps ($\Delta N$) across different system sizes.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the general algebraic origin of HFF models via the R matrix, then applying this to the FFD chain to analyze spectral organization and boundary effects, and finally generalizing the framework to include face-type and other integrable chains.

Abstract

Hidden-free-fermion (HFF) Hamiltonians exhibit free-fermion spectra despite lacking an explicit quadratic representation, yet their algebraic provenance and periodic-boundary behavior remain open questions. Here we establish that a broad class of HFF models arises from distinct Lax operator realizations of a twisted multistate Perk--Schultz \(R\) matrix. Its reflected degeneration generates a closed finite hierarchy of mutually commuting transfer matrices, whose functional relations determine the complete many-body spectrum. As a decisive example, we solve the inhomogeneous free-fermions-in-disguise (FFD) chain, which exhibits HFF, nested HFF, or interacting spectra, depending on its coupling. The homogeneous FFD chain with both one-wrap and periodic boundary conditions exhibits an emergent cubic finite-size gap while retaining the exact \(z=3/2\) bulk behavior. The construction extends to higher-state and face-type realizations, providing a common algebraic framework for coupling-dependent spectral organization and multiscale behavior in interacting quantum systems.

Faithful certification of steering- and incompatibility-breaking channels

Authors: Po-Ting Hsu, Shin-Liang Chen

Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5

Summary. This paper presents a theoretical framework to detect if a quantum channel destroys quantum correlations like steerability or measurement incompatibility. It achieves this by reformulating the problem as a Semidefinite Program (SDP) and deriving a corresponding, experimentally testable steering inequality. This method provides a powerful tool for characterizing the robustness of quantum resources against noise.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, general mathematical framework (SDP/inequalities) to quantify fundamental resource limitations (like steering) in noisy quantum channels, which is crucial for quantum communication protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to develop a rigorous method to certify whether a quantum channel is 'steering-breaking' or 'incompatibility-breaking' for any input state.

Main result. They show that the certification can be achieved by formulating the problem as a Semidefinite Program (SDP) and deriving an experimentally testable 'steering inequality' from its dual form.

Method. The core method involves adapting techniques from previous works to check if the output state admits a Local-Hidden-State (LHS) model, which is formulated as an SDP.

Model / system. The study focuses on quantum steering, where Alice sends a subsystem through a quantum channel ($\Lambda$) to Bob, and the goal is to detect if the channel destroys the ability to demonstrate entanglement.

Key observables. Steerability, measurement incompatibility, and the feasibility of the Local-Hidden-State (LHS) model for the conditional states.

Important parameters / regimes. The channel parameter $w$ (for depolarizing channels) and the radius $r$ used in the SDP formulation.

Assumptions / limitations. The SDP approach provides a sufficient certification, though it becomes necessary and sufficient in certain limits (e.g., all projective measurements).

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the general approach to verifying channel breaking properties; Figure 2 compares boundaries for entanglement, steerability, and incompatibility breaking for different measurement types.

Paper structure. The paper builds from defining the problem (certifying channel breaking) to applying SDP techniques, deriving the dual steering inequality, and finally applying this framework to specific models like the depolarizing channel to derive concrete bounds.

Abstract

We consider a quantum steering scenario where Alice prepares a bipartite state, sends one subsystem to Bob through a quantum channel, and aims to convince him that their shared state is entangled. Bob, however, will never be convinced if the channel is steering-breaking, that is, if the channel destroys steerability for any input state. To verify that a channel renders a state useless for demonstrating steering, one must consider all possible measurements performed by Alice and check if the conditional states Bob receives admit a local-hidden-state (LHS) model. This is generally a hard problem since it requires considering an infinite number of measurement combinations. Here, we show that the method proposed in [Phys. Rev. Lett. 117, 190401 (2016); Phys. Rev. Lett. 117, 190402 (2016)] can be utilized to tackle this problem. Furthermore, owing to the intimate relation between steering and measurement incompatibility, our approach can faithfully certify whether a channel destroys incompatibility for any set of measurements. Finally, we propose an experimental criterion for certifying steering- and incompatibility-breaking channels, which may be of independent interest.

Finite-temperature quantum Monte Carlo study of Dirac quantum criticality: Algorithmic advances and comprehensive analysis

Authors: Hou-Min Du, Qian Sun, Yuan-Yao He

Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 2/5

Summary. This paper performs a detailed study of the quantum phase transition in the honeycomb Hubbard model using advanced finite-temperature Quantum Monte Carlo simulations. The authors report locating the critical point and determining critical exponents for the transition between a Dirac semimetal and an antiferromagnetic Mott insulator. The work advances computational methodology while establishing a key benchmark for chiral Heisenberg criticality in this strongly correlated system.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on lattice models, the use of advanced QMC techniques and the determination of critical exponents provide rigorous benchmarks for understanding interacting quantum matter, which informs broader theories of many-body dynamics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the quantum phase transition from a Dirac semimetal (DSM) to an antiferromagnetic Mott insulator (AFMI) in the honeycomb-lattice Hubbard model.

Main result. The critical point was located at U_c/t = 3.731(4), yielding critical exponents such as $\nu = 1.097(7)$ and $\eta_\phi = 0.72(2)$.

Method. The research employs finite-temperature auxiliary-field quantum Monte Carlo (AFQMC) simulations, incorporating algorithmic advances like generalized FFT and delayed updates.

Model / system. The system is the honeycomb-lattice Hubbard model, which is analyzed at half filling and is known to preserve particle-hole symmetry, thus avoiding the fermion sign problem.

Key observables. Spin correlations, off-diagonal single-particle Green's function, quasiparticle weight, Fermi-liquid parameter, and fidelity susceptibility.

Important parameters / regimes. The critical point ratio $U_c/t$, and the scaling relation $\beta t = L$ used to approach the zero-temperature limit.

Assumptions / limitations. The simulation assumes the dynamical critical exponent $z=1$ and utilizes twist-averaged boundary conditions to minimize finite-size effects.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the honeycomb lattice structure and its wrapping onto a torus for periodic boundary conditions.

Paper structure. The paper systematically applies advanced AFQMC techniques to analyze the phase transition, performing finite-size scaling analyses on key observables to pinpoint the critical point and determine universal exponents.

Abstract

We present a systematic study of the quantum phase transition from a Dirac semimetal to an antiferromagnetic Mott insulator in the honeycomb-lattice Hubbard model, using finite-temperature auxiliary-field quantum Monte Carlo (AFQMC) simulations. Given the dynamical critical exponent $z=1$ at criticality, we scale the inverse temperature $β$ with the linear system size $L$, i.e., $βt=L$ (with $t$ as the hopping amplitude), to approach the thermodynamic and zero-temperature limits simultaneously. On the algorithmic front, we generalize the fast Fourier transform in path propagation to multi-sublattice systems and employ the delayed update technique in configuration updates to accelerate the AFQMC simulations. These advances enable us to access unprecedented system sizes of up to $3528$ sites (with $L=42$ and $βt = 42$). We additionally use twist-averaged boundary conditions to reduce the finite-size effects in computed observables. On the physical side, we determine the Dirac quantum criticality via finite-size scaling analysis of the spin correlations and off-diagonal single-particle Green's function, and locate the critical point at $U_c/t=3.731(4)$ with the critical exponents $ν=1.097(7)$, $η_φ=0.72(2)$ and $η_ψ=0.155(6)$. We further corroborate the critical point by examining several relevant quantities, including the quasiparticle weight, Fermi-liquid parameter, charge compressibility, $U$-derivative of double occupancy, and fidelity susceptibility. Our work establishes an independent benchmark for the chiral Heisenberg Gross-Neveu-Yukawa criticality in the honeycomb Hubbard model, offering a complementary perspective to previous ground-state simulations.

Pseudo entropy from entanglement entropy

Authors: Abhigyan Saha, Piotr Sułkowski, Tadashi Takayanagi

Summary. This theoretical paper introduces 'pseudo entropy' as an extension of entanglement entropy to pairs of nonorthogonal quantum states. It develops a powerful mathematical toolkit using complex analysis (like Kramers-Kronig relations) to derive the real and imaginary components of this pseudo entropy from standard entanglement entropy. The results are applied to model complex quantum phenomena in CFT and Gaussian states.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical framework connecting standard entanglement measures to complex extensions via analytic continuation is highly relevant for understanding non-equilibrium dynamics and boundary effects in quantum field theories.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the mathematical challenge of extending entanglement entropy, which is defined for a single state, to a complex quantity called 'pseudo entropy' for a pair of nonorthogonal states.

Main result. It derives explicit formulas for the real and imaginary parts of pseudo entropy using analytic continuation techniques, relating them to derivatives of the standard entanglement entropy.

Method. The core methodology involves leveraging Cauchy-Riemann equations, Kramers-Kronig relations, and Taylor series expansions applied to the reduced transition matrix derived through analytic continuation in complex parameters.

Model / system. The analysis is applied to various quantum systems, including Conformal Field Theory (CFT) boundary states, fermionic and bosonic Gaussian states, and general bipartite pure states in finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces.

Key observables. Entanglement Entropy ($S_E$), Pseudo Entropy ($S$), Pseudo-Rényi Entropies ($S_n$), and the reduced transition matrix ($ au_A$).

Important parameters / regimes. Complex parameters ($z = x + iy$) used for analytic continuation, and system parameters related to boundary conditions or time evolution.

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes the reduced density matrix function is holomorphic on a domain in the complex parameter space, and convergence of the required Taylor series is assumed for the derived formulas to hold.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the two-qubit example, comparing the real part of pseudo entropy, average entanglement entropy, and the excess pseudo entropy across a path parameter.

Paper structure. The paper builds from the definition of pseudo entropy for nonorthogonal states, develops the mathematical machinery using complex analysis (Cauchy-Riemann, Kramers-Kronig), derives general formulas for real/imaginary parts and Rényi generalizations, and concludes with applications to CFT and Gaussian states.

Abstract

Pseudo entropy extends entanglement entropy from a single quantum state to a pair of nonorthogonal states and is generally complex. Taking advantage of Cauchy-Riemann equations, Kramers-Kronig relations, and analytic continuation in state parameters, we show how and to what extent real and imaginary parts of pseudo entropy can be derived from ordinary entanglement entropy. For families with holomorphic coefficients in finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces, the reduced transition matrix equals the ordinary reduced density matrix formula evaluated at complex parameters. Then a convergent Taylor series gives the real and imaginary parts of pseudo entropy from even and odd derivatives of entanglement entropy at the real midpoint. The matrix identity also gives formulae for excess pseudo entropy and Renyi entropies, and interpolation formulae for families with polynomial coefficients. We apply these results to boundary-state quenches and thermal states in conformal field theory, and to fermionic and bosonic Gaussian states and quenches. In conformal field theory, Kramers-Kronig relations give the first moment of the imaginary part in terms of the central charge and one-point functions for boundary states.

Reconstruction of the superstatistical temperature distribution from single-particle kinetic energies

Authors: Sergio Davis
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.15466v1

Summary. This paper develops a method to reconstruct the underlying superstatistical temperature distribution from measured single-particle kinetic energies. By employing Jaynes' maximum entropy principle constrained by logarithmic moments, the authors successfully distinguish between three theoretical universality classes (Gamma, Inverse Gamma, Lognormal). This work provides a robust theoretical tool for analyzing non-Maxwellian statistical systems.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical statistical mechanics, the rigorous application of maximum entropy methods to infer underlying physical parameters from complex, non-equilibrium distributions is a powerful theoretical tool applicable to analyzing complex quantum measurements or system dynamics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of reconstructing the underlying superstatistical inverse temperature distribution ($eta$) from observed single-particle kinetic energy data.

Main result. The authors successfully demonstrate that using Jaynes' maximum entropy principle, constrained by the logarithmic moments of the kinetic energy, can accurately reconstruct the distribution for three distinct superstatistical universality classes.

Method. The core method involves applying the maximum entropy principle, maximizing relative entropy subject to constraints derived from the logarithmic moments of the kinetic energy distribution.

Model / system. The study uses the framework of superstatistics, which models non-Maxwellian velocity distributions as mixtures of Maxwellian distributions weighted by a probability density for the inverse temperature $eta$. Three specific universality classes—Gamma, Inverse Gamma, and Lognormal—are analyzed.

Key observables. Single-particle kinetic energy distribution $P(k|\lambda)$, the superstatistical inverse temperature distribution $P(eta|\lambda)$, and the dimensionless relative inverse temperature variance $u$.

Important parameters / regimes. The mean inverse temperature ($eta_S$) and the dimensionless relative variance ($u$) are key parameters used to characterize the distributions.

Assumptions / limitations. The reconstruction relies on the assumption that the underlying distribution can be accurately determined by constraining the maximum entropy functional using the logarithmic moments of the kinetic energy.

Figures summary. Figure 1 shows the reconstructed inverse temperature and kinetic energy distributions for the three classes. Figure 2 compares the three classes' kinetic energy distributions, showing their near indistinguishability. Figure 3 presents the worst-case signed relative errors for estimating $u$.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the superstatistical framework and the difficulty of direct inversion. It then details the maximum entropy approach using logarithmic moment constraints. Finally, it presents the results, demonstrating the successful reconstruction and discrimination between the three universality classes.

Abstract

The framework of superstatistics can be used to describe non-Maxwellian single-particle velocity distributions as mixtures of Maxwellian distributions, where the weight is imposed by a probability density associated to the inverse temperature $β= 1/(k_B T)$. Among the typical model choices for $β$ are gamma, inverse gamma and lognormal distributions, sometimes called the universality classes of superstatistics. Given a set of observed velocities, however, there is no direct method to determine the underlying temperature distribution, as temperature itself is not a phase-space observable, and numerically inverting the Laplace transform is an unreliable process. In this work, we show that Jaynes' principle of maximum entropy can be used to successfully reconstruct the inverse temperature distribution from data, by using the logarithmic moments of the kinetic energy as constraints. Although the kinetic energy distributions for the three universality classes are almost indistinguishable at the same mean and variance of $β$, the method correctly discriminates between them.

Abstract

We introduce a general mechanism for engineering moment-selective temporal designs with hierarchically structured aperiodic drives. At special control settings where the driven dynamics is constrained by a finite symmetry group, lower-order moments can already reproduce Haar statistics while a selected higher-order deviation remains nonzero. A small static detuning from such a setting generically produces an exponentially long lifetime whose exponent scales inversely with the square of the detuning. Abstract

We introduce the notion of the BKM coercivity constant and show that the positivity of this constant governs the exponential relative entropy decay of the semigroup. Indeed, we prove that the modified log-Sobolev constant is equivalent to the BKM coercivity constant up to a constant depending on the asymptotic conditional expectation. We also discover that the BKM coercivity constants for matrix amplifications are already attained with a qubit auxiliary system, which also coincides with the GNS spectral gap of the generating Lindbladian. As a corollary, we establish a criterion that the complete modified log-Sobolev inequality holds if and only if the GNS gap of the semigroup is strictly positive. All the discussion above applies to quantum Markov semigroups that admit a faithful asymptotic conditional expectation as long-time equilibration, with no detailed balance condition assumed. As an application, we show the finite-dimensional Chen-Kastoryano-Gilyén Gibbs samplers always satisfy the complete modified log-Sobolev inequality.
