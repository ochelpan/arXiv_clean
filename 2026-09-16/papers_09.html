Parameter-Efficient Quantum NLP for Paraphrase Detection: Performance, Robustness, and Entanglement

Authors: Farha Nausheen, Khandakar Ahmed, Farina Riaz
Type: both · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.14529v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work provides the first systematic, multi-benchmark empirical validation of hybrid quantum-classical circuits for NLP tasks like paraphrase detection. By showing statistically significant performance gains and enhanced robustness over classical models, the authors argue that quantum entanglement is the key resource driving this advantage. The research establishes a strong case for the potential of QML in complex data analysis, despite current reliance on classical simulation.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on NLP, the methodology—using entanglement measures (Meyer-Wallach) to quantify the advantage of a quantum circuit over classical feature representations—provides a general framework for understanding when quantum correlations provide computational power beyond classical feature engineering.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper rigorously evaluates the performance, robustness, and entanglement characteristics of Quantum Machine Learning (QML) applied to Natural Language Processing (NLP) tasks, specifically paraphrase detection.

Main result. The quantum variational circuits statistically outperform parameter-matched classical baselines on multiple benchmarks (MRPC, QQP, PAWS), with entanglement identified as the primary driver of this advantage.

Method. The authors employed a hybrid quantum-classical approach, comparing 10-qubit variational circuits against state-of-the-art classical NLP models using statistical tests (e.g., Cohen's d) and entanglement measures.

Model / system. The core model is a 10-qubit hybrid quantum-classical variational circuit. It uses SBERT embeddings, maps them through a classical pre-processor, feeds the result into the quantum circuit (using AngleEmbedding and StronglyEntanglingLayers), and outputs a classification via a classical post-processor.

Key observables. Accuracy, F1 score, Recall, Cohen's d effect size, Meyer-Wallach entanglement measure, and parameter count.

Important parameters / regimes. 10 qubits, 2,148 parameters (for one variant), 10 seeds (for statistical averaging).

Assumptions / limitations. All reported results are derived from classical simulation, and hardware validation is explicitly noted as future work.

Figures summary. Figures summarize performance comparisons (e.g., radar charts showing quantum advantage across metrics) and stability analyses across multiple seeds.

Paper structure. The paper systematically validates the QML approach by testing performance across multiple NLP benchmarks (MRPC, QQP, PAWS), analyzing the role of entanglement, and demonstrating superior robustness compared to classical counterparts.

Abstract

Rigorous empirical validation of quantum machine learning on natural language tasks remains scarce. We evaluate a 10-qubit hybrid quantum-classical variational circuit (2,148 parameters) for paraphrase detection across three benchmarks: MRPC, Quora Question Pairs (QQP), and adversarial PAWS. On QQP (n = 10 seeds), the circuit achieves 75.53% +/- 0.75%. accuracy, statistically outperforming parameter-matched classical baselines (DeepMLP: p = 0.015, Cohen's d = 1.20; F1: p < 0.001, d = 2.32) and surpassing DistilBERT-4bit with 31,191 fewer parameters. On MRPC the optimal 2-layer variant reaches 92% of BERT-base accuracy at 54,000 lower parameter cost. Circuit depth analysis reveals a dataset-depth scaling effect; entanglement analysis via the Meyer-Wallach measure identifies multi-qubit entanglement as the primary performance driver (r = 0.85 across four variants). Adversarial evaluation on PAWS reveals emergent robustness: 98.2% recall versus 81.6% classical (+16.6 pp, d = 1.24, p < 0.001), without adversarial training. These results constitute the first systematic parameter-matched multi-benchmark empirical validation of hybrid variational circuits for NLP. All results are from classical simulation; hardware validation is future work.

Partial Stabilizer Learning under Fixed Commuting Constraints and the Absence of a Copy-Rate Discount

Authors: Masahito Hayashi, Yimin Lu
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13923v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper analyzes the resource cost (number of quantum copies) required to learn partial information about an unknown stabilizer state, specifically the complementary stabilizer subspace. It proves that limiting the learning target to this partial information does not reduce the asymptotic scaling of the required copies compared to learning the entire state. This result is significant for understanding the fundamental limits of quantum state verification and characterization protocols.

Why it may be interesting. It provides rigorous bounds on the resource scaling (number of copies) needed for quantum state characterization, which is fundamental to quantum error correction and quantum computation protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Determining the minimum number of quantum copies required to learn only a complementary stabilizer subspace and its eigenvalues, rather than the entire unknown pure stabilizer state.

Main result. Restricting the learning target to the complementary stabilizer information does not reduce the leading-order copy complexity compared to full state identification.

Method. The analysis uses symplectic vector space geometry and moment decomposition of tensor-power states to establish necessary and sufficient conditions for state identification.

Model / system. The system involves an unknown pure stabilizer state of n qudits with a fixed prime local dimension p, constrained by a set of prescribed commuting Pauli observables W.

Key observables. The success probability of state recovery, the copy rate (k/n), and the dimension of the complementary stabilizer subspace (m).

Important parameters / regimes. The copy rate k/n, the number of qudits n, and the local dimension p.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the learner can perform arbitrary collective measurements and that the target information (K, eta) is intrinsic and independent of the unknown state.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the problem by defining the target information (complementary stabilizers) and then proves the main result by comparing the required copies to the known results for full state identification, showing the complexity remains the same.

Abstract

We consider an unknown pure stabilizer state of \(n\) qudits with a fixed prime local dimension \(p\), together with prescribed commuting Pauli observables. Rather than learning the whole state, the learner must recover only complementary stabilizers that commute with the prescribed observables, together with their eigenvalues. Once the prescribed measurement is performed, this information and the observed outcome determine the corresponding conditional stabilizer state. We compare the number \(k\) of copies available to the learner with the number \(n\) of qudits and refer to their ratio \(k/n\) as the copy rate. Suppose that the dimension \(m\) of the requested complementary stabilizer subspace satisfies \(m=βn+o(n)\) for a fixed \(0<β\leq1\). If the copy rate converges to a value below one, the optimal exact recovery probabilities and verification scores converge to zero, both in the worst case and under the uniform prior. Conversely, if the number \(k\) of copies minus the number \(n\) of qudits tends to positive infinity, the entire stabilizer state can be identified with probability tending to one, and the requested complementary information can then be extracted. Thus, restricting the learning target to the stabilizer information needed after the prescribed measurement yields no copy-rate discount.

Randomized query complexity can beat certificate complexity

Authors: Shalev Ben-David, Robin Kothari
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.15063v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical paper constructs a specific Boolean function to demonstrate a significant separation between its randomized query complexity and its certificate complexity. It achieves this separation using both classical randomized algorithms and quantum algorithms, showing that quantum query complexity can achieve a near-optimal bound of $eO(\sqrt{n})$ compared to the $\Omega(n^2)$ certificate complexity.

Why it may be interesting. While the core focus is computational complexity theory, the explicit use of quantum query complexity $Q(f)$ and quantum algorithms (like Grover search) places it directly at the intersection of quantum information theory and computational limits.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the open question of whether a total Boolean function exists where its randomized query complexity is significantly smaller than its certificate complexity.

Main result. The authors construct a function demonstrating a separation where the randomized query complexity $R(f)$ is $eO(n)$ while the certificate complexity $C(f)$ is $\Omega(n^2)$, and the quantum query complexity $Q(f)$ is $eO(\sqrt{n})$.

Method. The separation is achieved by modeling the problem using a complex scenario (a sporting event/tournament) and applying advanced query complexity techniques, including reductions to the SINK problem.

Model / system. The problem is abstracted into a Boolean function $f(X, Y)$ derived from a tournament structure involving $2n$ countries and $n$ athletes, whose outcome depends on race results ($X$) and proposal forms ($Y$).

Key observables. Certificate complexity ($C(f)$), Bounded-error randomized query complexity ($R(f)$), and Bounded-error quantum query complexity ($Q(f)$).

Important parameters / regimes. The separation scales are $R(f) = eO(n)$ vs. $C(f) = \Omega(n^2)$, and $Q(f) = eO(\sqrt{n})$ vs. $C(f) = \Omega(n^2)$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes familiarity with standard query complexity concepts and relies on specific quantum algorithms like the king-finding algorithm for the SINK problem.

Paper structure. The paper establishes lower bounds for $C(f)$ and $C_0(f)$ using the sporting event model, then constructs randomized and quantum algorithms to achieve upper bounds for $R(f)$ and $Q(f)$, demonstrating the separation.

Abstract

A long-standing open question in query complexity asks whether there is a total Boolean function f with R(f) << C(f), where R(f) and C(f) denote its bounded-error randomized query complexity and certificate complexity, respectively. We construct a function with R(f) = O~(sqrt{C(f)}), which is optimal up to log factors. The same function also has $Q(f) = O~(C(f)^{1/4}), where Q(f) is the bounded-error quantum query complexity of f, which is also nearly optimal.

Spin-spin entanglement at high energy

Authors: Michael Fucilla, Yoshitaka Hatta, Bo-Wen Xiao
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.14700v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper applies quantum information theory to study spin-spin entanglement in high-energy QCD scattering. It analyzes how entanglement manifests in different processes, such as coherent diffraction and dijet production, contrasting predictions from saturation physics against the dilute BFKL limit. The results offer novel, measurable signatures connecting quantum correlations to fundamental QCD dynamics.

Why it may be interesting. While the core physics is QCD, the methodology heavily employs quantum information tools (entanglement measures, nonlocality tests) to extract physical insights, which is relevant to understanding correlations in complex quantum many-body systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates spin-spin entanglement in partonic final states produced during high-energy scattering processes in Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD). It aims to provide a quantum-information perspective on these complex scattering phenomena.

Main result. The entanglement properties are highly dependent on the scattering process (coherent vs. inclusive) and the kinematic regime (saturation vs. dilute limit). Specifically, longitudinal photons yield maximally entangled pairs, while the azimuthal dependence of entanglement shows a clear signature distinguishing saturation from the BFKL regime.

Method. The analysis utilizes the two-qubit density matrix formalism, diagnosing entanglement via Concurrence and testing nonlocality using the CHSH inequality. Calculations are performed for specific high-energy QCD processes.

Model / system. The physical systems are partonic final states from high-energy scattering, including coherent diffractive production and inclusive dijet production. The theoretical framework incorporates QCD evolution models like the saturation model and the BFKL (dilute) limit.

Key observables. Concurrence ($C[ ho]$), Bell nonlocality (CHSH inequality violation), and the azimuthal modulation of entanglement observables.

Important parameters / regimes. The ratio of gluon distributions ($G_2/G_0$), the saturation scale ($Q_s$), and kinematic variables like $eta$ and the azimuthal angle $\phi_{P,q}$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on specific limits, such as the back-to-back dijet limit, and assumes that the partonic density matrix can be reconstructed from measured hadrons.

Figures summary. Figure 1 summarizes the hierarchy of correlations for longitudinal vs. transverse photons in diffractive production. Figure 2 illustrates the Concurrence dependence on scattering angles, highlighting the separation between separable and Bell nonlocality domains.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes entanglement in two complementary processes: coherent diffractive production (studying the Pomeron) and inclusive back-to-back dijet production. It contrasts predictions from the saturation model with the dilute BFKL limit, focusing on the resulting azimuthal modulations.

Abstract

Spin correlations offer a quantum-information perspective on the partonic final states produced in high-energy scattering. We discuss the spin-density matrix of a heavy quark-antiquark pair in two complementary small-$x$ processes. In coherent diffractive production, color-singlet exchange enforces an unusually strong relation between entanglement and Bell nonlocality: a longitudinal photon creates a maximally entangled pair, whereas for a transverse photon the pair is generically both entangled and Bell nonlocal, with a model-independent point of maximal entanglement. In inclusive back-to-back production, the density matrix factorizes into a hard spin tensor and the unpolarized and linearly polarized Weizsäcker--Williams gluon distributions. The latter generates an azimuthal modulation and can increase the concurrence when the dijet relative momentum and imbalance are approximately orthogonal. Strikingly, in the saturation model considered, nonlinear effects wash out this modulation for $q_\perp\lesssim 3Q_s$, whereas the dilute BFKL limit, in which $G_2/G_0\to1$, yields a maximal modulation independent of the imbalance magnitude. These results connect quantum-information observables with the Pomeron and saturation physics.

The Ergotropy of Quantum Batteries under Unruh Effect

Authors: Yiding Wang, Shengyan Ma, Xiaofen Huang, Tinggui Zhang
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.14919v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper analyzes the impact of the Unruh effect, induced by uniform acceleration, on the energy extractability (ergotropy) of quantum batteries. By comparing different acceleration scenarios in bipartite and tripartite models, the authors demonstrate that only accelerating the battery itself can trigger a sudden, beneficial emergence of ergotropy. This highlights the complex, context-dependent role of relativistic effects in quantum energy storage.

Why it may be interesting. This work bridges relativistic quantum field theory (Unruh effect) with quantum thermodynamics (quantum batteries), providing insights into how external physical environments can control energy extractability.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how uniform acceleration, modeled by the Unruh effect, influences the ergotropy of quantum batteries.

Main result. The results show that only accelerating the battery itself can induce a sudden emergence of ergotropy, while charger acceleration or simultaneous acceleration leads to different, less favorable outcomes.

Method. The authors employ theoretical modeling, analyzing the system dynamics using perturbation theory and calculating the ergotropy observable in both bipartite and tripartite setups.

Model / system. The system models quantum batteries using Unruh-DeWitt detectors coupled to a quantum field, analyzed in bipartite and tripartite configurations. The dynamics are governed by Hamiltonians incorporating acceleration terms.

Key observables. Ergotropy ($\varepsilon( ho)$), composite-system energy change ($\Delta E_{bc}( ho)$), and population inversion ($P_e > P_g$).

Important parameters / regimes. Acceleration parameter ($q$), critical acceleration threshold ($q_c$), and the structure of the multipartite coupling.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on first-order perturbation theory and uses natural units ($c = \hbar = k_B = 1$).

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the dependence of battery ergotropy and the composite-system energy change on the acceleration parameter ($q$) and initial time ($t_0$).

Paper structure. The paper systematically compares three acceleration scenarios (battery only, charger only, both) in a bipartite setting, extends the analysis to a tripartite system, and draws conclusions about the dual role of the Unruh effect.

Abstract

We study the effects of uniform acceleration on the ergotropy of quantum batteries modeled as Unruh-DeWitt detectors, in both bipartite and tripartite setups. In the bipartite system, we systematically compare three scenarios: accelerating the battery, accelerating the charger, and accelerating both simultaneously. We find that only battery acceleration can induce a sudden emergence of ergotropy at a critical acceleration threshold, while the corresponding composite-system energy change may increase or decrease depending on the initial state at the onset of acceleration. Charger acceleration leaves the battery ergotropy constant within the perturbative regime, while simultaneous acceleration leads to monotonic decay due to coherent cancellation of the q-dependence. Extending to a tripartite system with one battery and two chargers, we find that battery acceleration again induces ergotropy emergence, whereas accelerating adjacent charger does not--consistent with the bipartite charger-acceleration case. These results reveal that the Unruh effect plays a dual role, both enhancing and degrading quantum battery performance, depending on which subsystem is accelerated and on the multipartite structure, bridging relativistic quantum field theory and quantum thermodynamics with relevance to experimentally accessible platforms.

The Propagating Nature of Single- and Multimagnons in LaCrO$_3$

Authors: Masoud Lazemi, Fabian J. Mohammad, Ellen M. Kiens, Yorick A. Birkhölzer, Paras Mehta, Emma van der Minne, Stefano Agrestini, Frank M. F. de Groot, Hebatalla Elnaggar
Type: both · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.14792v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

Summary. This work uses RIXS spectroscopy on LaCrO3 to investigate the nature of magnetic excitations, particularly multimagnons. By measuring spectra across varying temperatures, the authors show that thermal effects reveal propagating magnon components hidden within seemingly localized, multi-magnon spectral features. This establishes temperature-dependent RIXS as a key tool for studying complex magnetic interactions in correlated materials.

Why it may be interesting. The use of RIXS to probe multi-magnon dynamics and magnon-magnon interactions provides a powerful experimental handle on strongly correlated magnetic systems, linking spectroscopic measurements directly to many-body quantum dynamics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to distinguish the true microscopic nature of composite excitations, such as multimagnons, from their observed spectral dispersion, especially when the composite feature appears nearly nondispersive.

Main result. The research demonstrates that thermal population of the magnon band, observed via temperature-dependent RIXS, can reveal propagating constituents hidden within seemingly nondispersive multi-magnon features.

Method. The study combines experimental measurements using momentum- and temperature-dependent Resonant Inelastic X-ray Scattering (RIXS) with theoretical calculations like linear spin-wave theory and multimagnon joint density of states calculations.

Model / system. The physical system is the antiferromagnetic perovskite material LaCrO3. The theoretical framework involves modeling the magnetic excitations using Heisenberg Hamiltonians and calculating joint density of states for single-, two-, and three-magnon processes.

Key observables. Single-magnon dispersion, two- and three-magnon spectral features, anti-Stokes scattering, and temperature-dependent spectral weight changes.

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature (especially approaching the Néel temperature, T_N), momentum transfer (Q), and the exchange coupling constant (J1).

Assumptions / limitations. The initial distinction between dispersive (propagating) and nondispersive (localized) modes is complicated for composite excitations; the analysis relies on the Bose-Einstein distribution for magnon occupation.

Figures summary. Figures show RIXS spectra at different temperatures, illustrating the single-magnon dispersion at low T and the emergence of anti-Stokes and new three-magnon channels at high T, compared against spin-wave calculations.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the ambiguity of interpreting spectral dispersion for composite excitations, presents RIXS data showing single vs. multi-magnon features at varying temperatures, and concludes by using thermal population effects to confirm the propagating nature of the constituent magnons via theoretical modeling.

Abstract

The dispersion of an excitation is commonly used to infer its microscopic character: dispersive modes are associated with propagation, whereas nondispersive features are often interpreted as localized. This distinction becomes less straightforward for composite excitations, whose spectral maxima can remain nearly dispersionless even when they are built from propagating quasiparticles. Here we use momentum- and temperature-dependent resonant inelastic X-ray scattering (RIXS) to reveal the origin of multimagnon excitations in antiferromagnetic LaCrO$_3$. Whereas the single magnon disperses strongly, the two- and three-magnon features remain nearly flat. On approaching the Neel temperature, thermal population of the magnon band produces an anti-Stokes branch and opens an additional three-magnon channel involving the creation of two magnons and annihilation of one. Its energy and momentum dependence are reproduced by a thermally weighted multimagnon joint density of states calculations. These results establish thermal population as a means of exposing the propagating constituents hidden within nondispersive composite excitations, and position RIXS as a probe of multimagnon dynamics and, ultimately, magnon-magnon interactions.

Twinning of domains and spin anisotropy in K$_5$Fe$_4$Ag$_6$Te$_{10}$

Summary. This work investigates the structural and magnetic properties of the Fe-based semiconductor KFAT using advanced neutron diffraction techniques. The researchers found that the material exhibits complex magnetic domain twinning and a distinct spin anisotropy. This research deepens the understanding of magnetic ordering in iron-based compounds, drawing parallels to high-temperature superconductors.

Why it may be interesting. The detailed mapping of magnetic domains and spin anisotropy in a strongly correlated material like KFAT provides crucial experimental benchmarks for theoretical models describing magnetic ordering and electronic structure in related superconducting systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to elucidate the superstructure, magnetic domains, and spin anisotropy in the Fe-based semiconductor K5Fe4Ag6Te10 (KFAT).

Main result. The complex scattering patterns are attributed to twinning of magnetic domains, and the material exhibits a prominent spin anisotropy with an easy-plane defined by the c-axis and the intra-block Fe-Fe bond direction.

Method. The research employed time-of-flight single crystal neutron diffraction and polarized neutron scattering to map the reciprocal space and probe magnetic ordering.

Model / system. The physical system is KFAT, an Fe-based semiconductor acting as an analogue to iron-based superconductors in the limit of localized electrons. It exhibits intertwined nematic and magnetic orders below TN ~ 35 K.

Key observables. Superstructure peaks, complex magnetic scattering patterns, and spin anisotropy measured via polarized neutron scattering.

Important parameters / regimes. The transition temperature TN ~ 35 K; the $\sqrt{5} imes \sqrt{5}$ superstructure periodicity.

Assumptions / limitations. The complex scattering patterns are modeled as arising from equally populated twin domains (Domain A and Domain B).

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the $ ext{Fe-Ag}$ planes, the $\sqrt{5} imes \sqrt{5}$ superstructure, and schematic scattering maps distinguishing between different magnetic domains.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the structural and magnetic ordering via neutron diffraction, then using polarized neutron scattering to quantify the spin anisotropy, and finally linking this anisotropy to observed macroscopic effects like strain-induced susceptibility.

Abstract

The Fe-based superconductors are derived from metallic parent compounds with nematic and stripe magnetic orders, which lead to two types of magnetic domains. Recently it was found that K$_5$Fe$_4$Ag$_6$Te$_{10}$ (KFAT), an Fe-based semiconductor, exhibits similar nematic and stripe magnetic orders, and is thus an analogue to the Fe-based superconductors in the limit of localized electrons. In this work, the superstructure and magnetic domains of KFAT are elucidated by fully mapping the reciprocal space using time-of-flight single crystal neutron diffraction. In KFAT, Fe and Ag atoms order to form a $\sqrt{5}\times\sqrt{5}$ superstructure containing $2\times2$ Fe blocks, which leads to two superstructure domains with identical main Bragg peaks but distinct superstructure peaks. Below $T_{\rm N}\approx35$~K, magnetic and nematic orders break in-plane rotational symmetry of the tetragonal $\sqrt{5}\times\sqrt{5}$ superstructure, and further give rise to two magnetic domains. These four equally populated domains account for the complex scattering pattern observed in our time-of-flight elastic neutron scattering measurements. Using polarized neutron scattering, we demonstrate a prominent spin anisotropy with an easy-plane spanned by the $c$-axis and the intra-block antiferromagnetic Fe-Fe bond direction. Such an anisotropy at ${\bf q}\neq0$ persists well above $T_{\rm N}$, accounts for the in-plane ${\bf q}=0$ magnetic anisotropy observed in uniaxial-strained KFAT, and offers an indicator for discovering similar piezomagnetic effects in other materials.

Why 2D Models Fail to Capture Flow Properties in 3D Heterogeneous Media: A Connectivity Perspective

Authors: Cristian Picighelli, Ivan Colecchio, Marcelo Piva, Alejandro D. Otero, Alejandro Boschan
Type: theory · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.14609v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work analyzes how flow properties in heterogeneous media are determined by spatial dimensionality and connectivity. By comparing 2D and 3D models, the authors demonstrate that 2D approximations fail to capture the true physics of 3D flow, especially when connectivity constraints are factored in. The study uses stochastic methods to quantify these critical differences in effective conductivity.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on fluid dynamics, the core methodology—analyzing how dimensionality and connectivity constrain macroscopic properties from microscopic disorder—shares conceptual parallels with understanding emergent behavior in complex, disordered quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates why simplified 2D models fail to accurately predict macroscopic flow properties when applied to complex, heterogeneous 3D media.

Main result. Significant differences exist between 2D and 3D flow properties, particularly when connectivity is considered, demonstrating that 2D models severely underestimate the complexity of 3D flow.

Method. The authors use a stochastic approach, analyzing the upscaling of equivalent hydraulic conductivity ($K_{eq}$) across various connectivity structures (HCS, ICS, LCS) in both 2D and 3D realizations.

Model / system. The system is flow through heterogeneous porous media, governed by Laplace's equation, where the local hydraulic conductivity $k(\mathbf{r})$ is modeled as a lognormal random field.

Key observables. Equivalent hydraulic conductivity ($K_{eq}$), the full distribution $P[\log(K_{eq})]$, and its moments (mean, log-variance, log-skewness).

Important parameters / regimes. Coarsening scale ($\lambda$) relative to the integral scale ($I$), and the spatial dimensionality ($D=2$ vs $D=3$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on characterizing the flow behavior across different connectivity regimes (HCS, ICS, LCS) and comparing the mathematical validity of 2D vs 3D upscaling formulas.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate spatial maps of conductivity realizations and show quantitative comparisons of $K_{eq}$ and its distribution moments across different connectivity types and dimensions.

Paper structure. The paper systematically compares the upscaling of $K_{eq}$ in 2D and 3D media, contrasting the behavior of different connectivity structures (HCS, ICS, LCS) and highlighting the mathematical and physical divergences between the two dimensions.

Abstract

We analyze how the equivalent hydraulic conductivity, $K_{eq}$, varies with the coarsening scale $λ/I$ in 2D and 3D heterogeneous media. Their local hydraulic conductivity, $k({\bf r})$, follows a lognormal distribution in all cases, while spatial connectivity of $k({\bf r})$ ranges from high (HCS), through intermediate or multi-Gaussian (ICS) and low (LCS), to unstructured (NS). Using a stochastic approach, we characterize the full distribution $P[\log(K_{eq})]$, and its moments: mean $\langle K_{eq}\rangle$, log-variance $σ_{\log(K_{eq})}^{2}$, and log-skewness $γ_{\log(K_{eq})}$, as a function of $λ/I$. Significant contrasts appear between 2D and 3D, which we interpret in terms of two key factors associated with spatial dimensionality. We observe that, compared with ICS, LCS and HCS exhibit two distinct features: existing analytical expressions for coarse-graining of $k({\bf r})$ in multi-Gaussian media do not apply, while $P[\log(K_{eq})]$ deviates from Gaussian at all scales $λ/I$. Critical path analysis is then used to quantify the connectivity of our samples, and show that $K_{eq}$ exhibits a power-law dependence on it. Our results reveal that connectivity can lead to substantial differences between 2D and 3D macroscopic flow properties, even for the same geostatistical parameters, highlighting the severe limitations of using 2D models to represent 3D flows.

A Classification of Hirota-Integrable Supersymmetric Bilinear KdV-Type Equations

Summary. This paper classifies supersymmetric bilinear KdV-type equations by deriving the necessary algebraic conditions for the existence of three-super-soliton solutions. By extending Hirota's classical criterion, the authors establish that integrability requires satisfying eight specific compatibility conditions. This work provides a unified mathematical framework for studying soliton dynamics in supersymmetric field theories.

While focused on classical integrable systems, the systematic extension of integrability criteria (like Hirota's method) into a supersymmetric framework provides powerful mathematical tools applicable to quantum field theories and quantum many-body systems exhibiting supersymmetry.

Abstract

We present a classification of supersymmetric bilinear KdV-type equations admitting unconstrained three-super-soliton solutions. Extending Hirota's classical three-soliton criterion to the supersymmetric setting, we derive the complete bosonic and fermionic compatibility conditions governing the existence of three-super-soliton solutions. We prove that every supersymmetric bilinear KdV-type equation possesses unconstrained one- and two-super-soliton solutions, whereas three-super-soliton solutions exist only when eight integrability conditions are satisfied. These conditions provide a supersymmetric analogue of Hirota's classical integrability criterion and naturally recover the fermionic relations previously introduced by Carstea, revealing their structural origin. As a consequence, we classify the supersymmetric extensions of Hirota bilinear KdV-type equations and show that only a subset of Hietarinta's classical classification remains valid in the unrestricted supersymmetric framework.

A Modular, Topology-Aware Software Stack for Entanglement-Based Distributed Quantum Computing

Despite the drastic simplification, BOND-1 achieves cut ratios above 0.95 relative to the best known values on standard GSet MaxCut benchmarks with up to 20000 variables, and in some cases it matches those values. It achieves these results without per-instance optimization and has linear memory cost, while per-instance tuning can provide further improvement. These results show that, in this regime, a substantial fraction of the optimization power of QAOA survives even in the complete absence of entanglement. Our conclusions, however, are specific to this setting and do not imply that entanglement is unnecessary for quantum optimization in general.

Abstract

We give a quantitative disorder comparison for the zero-field Sherrington--Kirkpatrick model at inverse temperature one. For any fixed number of replicas, replacing Gaussian couplings by independent couplings from a bounded symmetric variance-one law changes a bounded replica expectation by at most a constant times the Gaussian overlap second moment plus $N^{-1}$. A multiplicative comparison for nonnegative observables controls the tails. The proof uses Talagrand's positive-observable estimates and Yu-Ting Chen's fourth-order replica cancellation. Assuming the Gaussian critical overlap limit stated by Du and Huang, we obtain the same limit for the random quenched overlap measure in the iterated Wasserstein-2 topology. No separate moment assumption is needed for this transfer. Their additional exponential moment bound gives convergence of the rescaled spin-glass susceptibility in every finite Wasserstein distance. The coupling class includes fair signs, uniform disorder, and signs thinned at any fixed positive density.

Summary. This work addresses the critical flaw in using only projected energy in VQE calculations: the state might be energetically accurate but physically invalid due to leakage. The authors introduce a comprehensive diagnostic framework incorporating leakage measurement and compare advanced ansatz selection protocols to guide quantum chemistry simulations toward physically meaningful results.

Why it may be interesting. It provides crucial theoretical diagnostics for quantum chemistry simulations, helping practitioners understand the limitations of energy-only optimization and the necessity of enforcing physical constraints like particle number conservation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The projected-sector energy in VQE calculations is insufficient to guarantee that the prepared quantum state is physically valid because leakage outside the target particle-number sector can occur.

Main result. The authors propose a diagnostic framework using three metrics—projected-sector error, leakage outside the physical sector, and parameter count—to compare ansatz families beyond just energy minimization.

Method. The paper develops theoretical criteria for leakage (both state-level and channel-level) and compares various ansatz selection strategies (e.g., SelectedUCC, Adaptive Hybrid) using these new diagnostics.

Model / system. The study focuses on molecular Hamiltonian calculations for ground-state energy using the Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) applied to systems like BeH2 and LiH.

Important parameters / regimes. The penalty factor $\mu$ used in energy minimization objectives.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is performed in noiseless, small-basis, active-space simulations, and the projected energy only depends on the direction of the component inside the target sector.

Figures summary. Figures compare energy convergence trajectories and mean projected-sector errors for different ansatz selection protocols (e.g., Adaptive Hybrid vs. SelectedUCC) across various generator budgets.

Abstract

In molecular variational quantum eigensolver (VQE) calculations for a fixed electron-number sector, the projected-sector energy alone does not certify that a prepared state is physically valid. We show that a state can have zero projected-sector error while almost all of its probability mass lies outside the target particle-number sector. We therefore evaluate ansatz families using three quantities: projected-sector error, leakage outside the physical sector and parameter count. Our noiseless experiments show that particle-preserving UCC-style families eliminate leakage induced by ansatze, but do not guarantee ground-state accuracy. In the parameter-budget comparison experiments, the one-shot ranking rules select identical generator sequences and yield identical errors. The adaptive hybrid procedure achieves lower mean projected-sector errors at selected budgets. Using established Kraus invariant-subspace criteria, we also express channel-induced leakage through a positive operator and discuss global and local depolarizing noise analytically. This gives a practical diagnostic framework for comparing ansatze beyond energy alone.
