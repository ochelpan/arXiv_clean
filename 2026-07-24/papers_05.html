Anticoncentration of the Permanent in Ginibre Ensembles

Authors: Frederic Koehler, Pui Kuen Leung
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.20329v1

Summary. This theoretical paper proves the 'Anticoncentration' property for the permanent of Ginibre random matrices over real, complex, and quaternionic fields. By comparing the permanent to the determinant using advanced tools like Laplace transforms, it resolves a major conjecture (PACC) and establishes tight bounds on the distribution of this key random matrix observable.

Why it may be interesting. While highly mathematical, random matrix theory is foundational to quantum physics, especially in characterizing spectral statistics and entanglement measures derived from Gaussian ensembles. The study of permanents versus determinants touches upon non-Gaussian correlations relevant in complex physical models.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the 'Anticoncentration of the Permanent' for Ginibre ensembles, a problem related to the permanent Anticoncentration Conjecture (PACC). It seeks to establish bounds on the distribution of the normalized row-ordered permanent.

Main result. For $K=\mathbb{C}$, the authors resolve the PACC by proving that the normalized permanent has a radial density with specific concentration bounds. They also established general anticoncentration results for $\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{C},$ and $\mathbb{H}$ fields.

Method. The proof employs advanced techniques comparing the squared Gaussian permanent to the squared determinant in Laplace-transform order, utilizing inductive arguments and characteristic functions derived from properties of Gaussian distributions over different algebras ($\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{C}, \mathbb{H}$).

Model / system. The model involves $n imes n$ random matrices ($G_n^{\mathbb{K}}$) whose entries are independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) standard $\mathbb{K}$-Gaussian variables, where $\mathbb{K}$ can be the real ($\mathbb{R}$), complex ($\mathbb{C}$), or quaternionic ($\mathbb{H}$) numbers.

Key observables. The normalized row-ordered permanent $W_n^{\mathbb{K}} = ext{per}_{\mathbb{K}}G_n^{\mathbb{K}}/\sqrt{n!}$, and its associated radial density $p_n^{\mathbb{K}}$.

Important parameters / regimes. The dimension $eta = \dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{K} \in \{1, 2, 4\}$, and the matrix size $n$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies heavily on comparing moments and distributions using Laplace transforms ($\le_{Lt}$), which is shown to be a strong tool but requires careful handling across different algebraic structures.

Figures summary. Not specified, but notes mention Monte Carlo comparisons showing sign changes in CDF differences for $\Delta_K^3 - X_K^3$ versus non-negative signs for Laplace transform differences.

Paper structure. The paper develops results by first establishing benchmarks using the determinant and Study determinant across different fields. It then uses inductive arguments, characteristic functions, and specialized comparison theorems (like Lemma C.5) to prove the anticoncentration bounds for the permanent itself.

Abstract

Let $\mathbb{K}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H}\}$, put $β=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{K}$, and let $G_n^{\mathbb{K}}$ be an $n\times n$ matrix with i.i.d. standard $\mathbb{K}$-Gaussian entries, namely a standard $\mathbb{K}$-Ginibre matrix. We prove that the normalized row-ordered permanent $W_n^{\mathbb{K}}=\operatorname{per}_{\mathbb{K}}G_n^{\mathbb{K}}/\sqrt{n!}$ has a radial density $p_n^{\mathbb{K}}$ satisfying $\|p_n^{\mathbb{K}}\|_\infty=p_n^{\mathbb{K}}(0)\lesssim_βn^{(β+2)/4}$ and $\sup_{z\in\mathbb{K}}\mathbb{P}(|W_n^{\mathbb{K}}-z|\leq\varepsilon)\lesssim_βn^{(β+2)/4}\varepsilon^β$. In particular, for $\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}$, this resolves the Permanent Anticoncentration Conjecture of Aaronson and Arkhipov. The proof compares the squared Gaussian permanent with the squared (Study) determinant in Laplace-transform order.

Summary. This paper tackles the measurement bottleneck in Pauli Correlation Encoding (PCE) by developing 'efficiently simulable' variants. These methods allow the necessary expectation values to be computed efficiently using free-fermionic or IQP circuits, rather than requiring costly quantum measurements. This work establishes a strong classical baseline for evaluating quantum approaches to hard combinatorial optimization problems.

Why it may be interesting. The development of classically simulable quantum encoding schemes provides crucial theoretical tools for benchmarking the scaling and power of future quantum hardware implementations in optimization.

Main problem. The primary challenge addressed is the measurement overhead associated with Pauli Correlation Encoding (PCE) when estimating expectation values for combinatorial optimization problems.

Model / system. The framework encodes classical binary variables into expectation values of many-body Pauli observables. The simulation is instantiated using free-fermionic Hamiltonians and general IQP ansatzes parameterized by rotations.

Abstract

Pauli Correlation Encoding (PCE) is a heuristic framework for binary optimisation that encodes classical variables into many-body Pauli observables. While PCE requires fewer qubits than other approaches, it relies on estimating a large number of Pauli expectation values whose signs determine the variables' values, which can incur substantial measurement overhead. Here, we introduce efficiently simulable PCE, a class of dequantised PCE realisations where all expectation values needed can be computed efficiently classically. We instantiate this idea using free-fermionic evolutions, realised by matchgate circuits, and Instantaneous Quantum Polynomial (IQP) circuits. On MaxCut, Maximum Independent Set, Multi-Dimensional Knapsack, and Max3SAT benchmarks, these methods produce high-quality solutions across problem sizes ranging from tens to thousands of variables. Our results show that PCE is naturally understood as a correlation-based optimisation framework with both quantum and classically simulable realisations. This yields a dequantised baseline for evaluating future quantum PCE implementations.

Exact static exchange benchmark for the uniform electron gas: Dynamic-kernel tests and exchange-correlation cancellation near 2kF

Summary. This work establishes an exact theoretical benchmark for the static exchange-only local field factor in the uniform electron gas. By deriving this universal function, the authors critically test and demonstrate the quantitative failures of several established approximations (like BDL or McLVP) near the Fermi surface singularity at $2k_F$. The findings illuminate that observed physical behavior is governed by a precise cancellation between correlation and exchange effects.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on electron gas theory, the rigorous benchmarking of correlation effects at finite momentum ($q eq 0$) provides fundamental insights into how screening modifies particle interactions, which is crucial for understanding correlated materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To establish an exact, universal benchmark for the static local field factors of the uniform electron gas (UEG), particularly addressing unexplained features near $2k_F$.

Main result. The paper derives and presents the exact exchange-only local field factor, $G_x(q,0)$, which shows specific limiting behaviors ($q^2/4$ at small $q$, $\pi^2/6$ at $2k_F$, and $1/3$ at large $q$).

Method. The authors combine textbook local-field relations with exact static exchange polarizabilities derived from QMC calculations to construct a closed-form, universal benchmark.

Model / system. The system is the Uniform Electron Gas (UEG), analyzed in the static limit ($\omega=0$) using density functional theory concepts applied to correlation functions.

Key observables. Static local field factors ($G^+$), the exchange-only factor $G_x(q,0)$, and spin-resolved components of these factors.

Important parameters / regimes. The wavevector $q$, normalized by $2k_F$ (using variable $Q$), and the density parameter $r_s$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that QMC representations are smooth enough to reveal underlying systematic cancellations between correlation and exchange contributions.

Figures summary. Figures compare the exact universal $G_x(q,0)$ against approximations from older theoretical kernels (BDL, McLVP), showing significant quantitative discrepancies in the comparison plots.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the physical problem by noting unexplained features in QMC data; it then derives the exact benchmark formula for $G_x$; subsequently, it rigorously compares this benchmark against established approximations and analyzes the underlying cancellation mechanisms near $2k_F$.

Abstract

The static local field factors of the uniform electron gas are known accurately from quantum Monte Carlo (QMC) calculations, but two of their most striking features have lacked a mechanism: $G_+$ grows almost exactly as $q^2$ up to nearly $2k_F$, and the violent $2k_F$ physics of the Fermi surface leaves only a faint trace in it. Combining the textbook first-order local-field relation with the exact static exchange polarizability gives a compact exact benchmark: the universal exchange-only local field factor $G_x(q,0) = -I(Q)Q^2/L(Q)^2$, $Q = q/2k_F$, with exact values $(1/4)(q/k_F)^2$ at small $q$, $π^2/6$ at $2k_F$ -- apparently never before stated as a local-field value -- and $1/3$ at large $q$, giving the exchange-only spin-averaged contact value $g(0) = 1/2$ of the unpolarized gas (BDL 1977). $G_x$ rises to a finite maximum of 1.9924 at $1.9685k_F$ and passes through $π^2/6$ at exactly $2k_F$ with a slope diverging as $\ln^3|q-2k_F|$. Against this benchmark, the 1980 BDL tabulation is accurate to about one percent below $1.5k_F$, while the 2024 McLachlan-variational kernel of Nazarov and Silkin is two-percent accurate only below $0.5k_F$, is a factor of 1.74 low at $2k_F$, and its large-q limit is inconsistent with the contact constraint. A spin-resolved decomposition of QMC local field factors about the exact baseline shows that, within the resolution and smoothness assumptions of the QMC-based representations, both features follow from a strong exchange-correlation cancellation: at the QMC point closest to $2k_F$ ($q = 2.01k_F$, $r_s = 2$), correlation cancels 0.43(7) of the exact exchange value 1.33. Computed consistently at each wavevector, the static exchange-kernel thresholds derived from the closed form reproduce, to 0.2%, the finite-wavevector charge threshold $r_s \approx 10.6$ near $q = 1.94k_F$ obtained numerically in 1980, and recovered again in 2014.

Summary. This work develops a geometric framework using Lie group theory to analyze the redundancy inherent in Variational Quantum Circuits. By mapping the search space onto a homogeneous manifold defined by the stabilizer group of the Hamiltonian, it provides an explicit formula for the effective degrees of freedom. This theoretical insight is validated through numerical experiments on VQE optimization.

Why it may be interesting. It provides rigorous mathematical tools (homogeneous space geometry) to quantify how much information is truly encoded in variational quantum circuits, which is crucial for designing efficient algorithms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper analyzes the redundancy and effectiveness of Variational Quantum Circuits (VQCs) by characterizing the geometry of the space of achievable effective Hamiltonians.

Main result. The dimension of this effective Hamiltonian manifold is determined by the structure of a stabilizer group $S$, yielding $\dim( ext{MH}) = \dim(U(V)) - \dim(S)$.

Method. The authors use Lie group theory, identifying the space as a homogeneous quotient space ($S\setminus U(V)$), and employ spectral decompositions to derive tractable dimension formulas.

Model / system. The model involves VQAs (like VQE) acting on an $n$-qubit system defined by a Hamiltonian $H$. The circuit search space is mapped onto the orbit space $ ext{MH}$ under unitary transformations.

Key observables. Effective Hamiltonians ($ ext{MH}$), Stabilizer Group ($S$), and the Overlap Score $s(A)$ used to diagnose generator redundancy.

Important parameters / regimes. The dimension of the system ($n$) and the structure/eigenspaces of the Hamiltonian $H$ (determining $\dim(S)$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies heavily on treating the problem using the mathematical framework of Lie groups and homogeneous spaces.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the geometric setup showing how unitary evolution maps observables, identifying the redundant component $S$. Figure 2 shows the distribution of overlap scores $s(A)$ for tested circuit generators.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the theoretical connection between observable redundancy and quotient spaces using Lie theory. It then derives dimension formulas based on the stabilizer group structure. Finally, it applies these concepts to a numerical VQE experiment, testing generator candidates using the overlap score $s(A)$.

Abstract

We study redundancy and effectiveness of Variational Quantum Circuits via algebraic and geometric views of Lie groups. Considering unitary transformations acting on Hermitian observables, a stabilizer group decomposition is given. Subsequently, we identify the Hermitian orbit with a quotient space of a symmetric space. Through this connection, we characterize the effective circuit degrees of freedom. Our approach of spectral decompositions and homogeneous spaces yields tractable calculation criteria, which are verified by numerical experiments.

Summary. This paper proves the global well-posedness for the nonlocal Fokas-Lenells equation using an advanced Inverse Scattering Transform approach. The methodology involves solving associated Riemann-Hilbert problems and overcoming technical hurdles related to non-standard symmetries in the spectral problem. The result confirms that unique solutions exist globally in time, which is crucial for modeling physical systems like optical pulse propagation.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on nonlinear PDEs rather than quantum dynamics directly, the use of advanced spectral methods (IST/RH problems) to prove long-time existence is a powerful mathematical tool applicable to analyzing complex quantum field theories or open quantum systems governed by integrable Hamiltonians.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is establishing the global well-posedness of the Cauchy problem for the reverse space-time nonlocal Fokas-Lenells (nFL) equation on the line.

Main result. Global existence and uniqueness of solutions are proven, and the associated solution map is shown to be Lipschitz continuous on the admissible initial-data class.

Method. The analysis heavily relies on developing an Inverse Scattering Transform (IST) framework formulated via associated Riemann-Hilbert (RH) problems and utilizing spectral uniformization transforms.

Model / system. The model is the nonlocal Fokas-Lenells equation, $q_{xt} - q + iqq^{PT} = 0$, which generalizes integrable models like NLS and is relevant to modeling ultrashort optical pulses in nonlinear media.

Key observables. The potential $q(x, t)$, reflection coefficients ($r_j$), and the associated scattering data are key observables used throughout the analysis.

Assumptions / limitations. The proof requires establishing uniform bounds on reflection coefficients by assuming a smallness condition on the initial potential, which ensures positive definiteness of the Hermitian part of the jump matrix.

The paper follows a standard IST approach: defining the direct scattering problem, constructing associated Riemann-Hilbert problems, developing the necessary transforms (like spectral uniformization), proving global reconstruction via controlled singular integral operators, and finally establishing the Lipschitz continuity of the solution map.

Abstract

We establish the global well-posedness of the Cauchy problem for the reverse space-time nonlocal Fokas-Lenells equation with the weighted Sobolev initial data $q_0(x)\in H^{3}(\mathbb{R}) \cap H^{2,1}(\mathbb{R})$ on the line. We develop the inverse scattering transform formulated via the associated Riemann-Hilbert problems to study this issue. A spectral uniformization transform is introduced to resolve the singular behavior inherent in the KN-type negative flow spectral problem. Owing to the reverse space-time reduction, reflection coefficients no longer satisfy the usual Hermitian conjugation symmetry, and the coercivity of the jump matrix is therefore not available a priori. The quantitative smallness condition on the initial data yields uniform bounds on the reflection coefficients and ensures the uniform positive definiteness of the Hermitian part of the associated jump matrix. The resulting coercivity allows us to establish the bounded invertibility of the associated singular integral operator through a Fredholm and vanishing-lemma argument. Under this condition, we prove an $L^{2}$-Sobolev bijective correspondence between the potential and scattering data, exclude spectral singularities on continuous spectra, and obtain the global existence and uniqueness of solutions. Moreover, the associated solution map is Lipschitz continuous on the admissible initial-data class.

Summary. This paper introduces a cluster-based extension to Reinforcement Learning (RL) for optimizing Belief Propagation (BP) decoding of QLDPC codes. Model / system. The work focuses on decoding QLDPC codes used in fault-tolerant quantum computation. Decoding involves message passing over a Tanner graph structure defined by parity-check matrices.

Finally, it presents numerical results demonstrating the improved latency-parallelism tradeoff.

Abstract

Belief-propagation (BP) decoding for quantum low-density parity-check (QLDPC) codes is attractive due to its low complexity, but its performance is often limited by short cycles, degeneracy, and convergence failures. Recently, reinforcement-learning-based sequential variable-node (VN) scheduling (RL-S) was shown to improve BP decoding by learning state-dependent update orders. However, the VN-by-VN nature of that approach offers limited within-iteration parallelism, since only one VN is updated at a time. In this paper, we propose a cluster-based extension of RL-S for QLDPC codes. The VNs are partitioned into fixed clusters, and at each scheduling step the RL agent selects one cluster to update, after which all VNs in that cluster are updated in parallel using the same pre-update incoming messages. To keep the tabular state space practical for large cluster sizes, we introduce a permutation-invariant cluster state based on a normalized histogram of local mismatch weights, followed by quantization. This representation makes the number of cluster states depend on the quantization resolution rather than the cluster size. We also develop the corresponding cluster-level Markov decision process, reward function, and Q-learning update. Numerical results on representative QLDPC codes show that the proposed clustered learned scheduling preserves most of the error-rate benefit of VN-level learned sequential scheduling while substantially reducing the number of scheduling decisions per BP iteration, thereby providing an attractive latency-parallelism tradeoff.

Abstract

Pump-induced transient optical properties combine contributions from electronic states throughout the Brillouin zone, but the relative relevance of off-resonant and l-photon resonant crystal momenta has remained unexplored. We address this issue in pumped germanium by resolving the transient absorptive response into momentum-space classes defined by the presence or absence of 1-, 2-, and 3-photon resonances with respect to the pump. Using the Dynamical Projective Operatorial Approach together with the related generalized linear response theory, we compute the differential imaginary part of the dielectric function and evaluate the contributions of each resonance class. Resonant regions account for nearly the entire optical response, whereas points outside the identified resonance sets contribute only negligibly. Nevertheless, the 2-photon-resonant set, although containing more than 98% of the residual (post-pump) excitation population, does not reproduce the full transient spectrum. Conversely, resonance classes with very small residual populations generate non-negligible contributions to the transient optical properties. This mismatch shows that the transient optical weight is not determined solely by the real-charge dynamics (which results in post-pulse residual excitation population) and is consistent with substantial virtual pump-induced contributions, whose dominant optical weight nevertheless arises from the resonant regions of momentum space. The class-resolved phase of the dominant 2$ω_{\mathrm{pu}}$ oscillations further shows that, whenever a class contributes appreciably, the phase of its oscillatory component follows that of the corresponding full signal. The resulting decomposition provides a momentum-resolved connection among multi-photon resonances and transient optical observables in a realistic material.
