Constant sized support state distillation with qubit recycling

Authors: Victor Barizien
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.17044v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops a systematic, recursive framework for designing quantum state distillation protocols necessary for fault-tolerant quantum computation. By utilizing code doubling and qubit recycling techniques, the authors construct families of protocols that achieve high code distance with minimal resource overhead. This work advances the theoretical understanding of resource management in quantum error correction.

Why it may be interesting. The focus on resource optimization (qubit recycling) and achieving high-distance distillation protocols is highly relevant for scaling up quantum computation architectures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the limitation that quantum error-correcting codes alone cannot provide a universal set of fault-tolerant logical gates, necessitating high-fidelity magic state injection.

Main result. The authors provide a recursive family of distillation protocols that can produce diagonal states at arbitrary high distance on a fixed number of logical qubits, significantly reducing resource overhead via qubit recycling.

Method. The core method involves constructing and analyzing families of weakly r-orthogonal matrices using code doubling techniques and algebraic proofs over F_2.

Model / system. The model is based on quantum error-correcting codes and the distillation of magic states (like the |T> state). The mathematical framework uses r-orthogonal matrices G to characterize the resource requirements.

Key observables. Distance (d), Support (s), Depth (n), and the minimal recycled qubit support (s*).

Important parameters / regimes. The orthogonality level r, the distance d, and the number of output states k.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis primarily focuses on Z-type errors sufficient for stabilizer codes, and the construction relies on the availability of lower-level Clifford operations.

Figures summary. The notes mention a Table I comparing parameters (s, s*, n, d, effective overhead) of the constructed G(r,d) protocols against known literature protocols.

Paper structure. The paper builds by first defining the problem using r-orthogonal matrices, then introducing the code-doubling construction (Theorem 1), establishing a recursive family construction, and finally proving the properties (orthogonality, distance, support bounds) of the resulting matrices.

Abstract

In order to circumvent that no universal set of gates can be achieved on a given quantum error-correcting codes, additional gates can be performed through magic state injection. This however requires to have access to high fidelity magic state, which can be obtained by the distillation of low quality states to fewer higher quality ones. In this paper, we provide a family of distillation protocols able to produce diagonal states at every level of the Clifford hierarchy. This family is obtained recursively using code doubling techniques and leveraging qubit recycling, the fact that some idle qubits of the protocols can be measured and re-used. At a given level of the Clifford hierarchy, we show that the protocols we derive can be performed at arbitrary high distance on a fix number of logical qubits. This family recovers many known efficient protocols, and uncovers new ones, such as a $111 \to 1$ protocol for $|T\rangle$ state distillation at distance $7$, which are the most compact in term of volume. Finally, we discuss possible extension of this framework for distillation protocols producing more than one output state.

Floquet Theory and Average Hamiltonian Theory Revisited: Equivalence, Convergence and Applications to NMR

Authors: Antonia J. Bock, Matthias Ernst, Götz S. Uhrig
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.17121v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper rigorously compares Floquet Theory and Average Hamiltonian Theory for periodically driven quantum systems, using the Floquet-Magnus Expansion to derive higher-order terms for both. The authors conclude that the Floquet-Van Vleck approach is superior for numerical calculations in applications like NMR spectroscopy. It provides a unified theoretical understanding of how these methods relate while offering practical recommendations for implementation.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous, systematic comparison between two cornerstone theoretical tools (AHT and Floquet theory) used to analyze time-periodic quantum dynamics, offering practical guidance on which formalism yields the best numerical results for complex systems like those in NMR.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to provide a detailed mathematical analysis comparing the equivalence, convergence, and practical application of Floquet Theory and Average Hamiltonian Theory (AHT) for periodically driven quantum systems.

Main result. The Floquet-Van Vleck (FVV) approach is recommended over AHT because it is numerically more robust and accurate, despite the formal equivalence of the theories in the infinite limit.

Method. The authors utilize the Floquet-Magnus Expansion (FME) to derive systematic, higher-order terms for both AHT and FVV, avoiding explicit time integration.

Model / system. The theory is applied to periodically driven quantum systems, with a primary focus on Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) spectroscopy, modeling the time evolution governed by a time-periodic Hamiltonian H(t).

Key observables. Effective Hamiltonians (F), the kick operator, time evolution operators, and spectroscopic shifts like the Bloch-Siegert shift.

Important parameters / regimes. The driving period T, the ratio of driving frequency to system energies, and the order of the perturbation expansion (up to fourth order).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the mathematical framework of the Floquet-Magnus expansion and assumes the validity of perturbative expansions in the driving frequency.

Figures summary. Figure 13 shows histograms comparing the relative differences of operators calculated with and without the kick operator for 2-spin and 3-spin MAS systems, highlighting differences between random and MAS Hamiltonians.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the general framework using the FME, derives explicit formulas for higher-order terms in both FVV and AHT, and then validates these formulas and compare their predictions using specific NMR examples like the Bloch-Siegert shift.

Abstract

An accurate theoretical treatment of periodically driven quantum systems is crucial for various fields in the exact sciences, for instance Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) spectroscopy. Conventionally, either average Hamiltonian theory or Floquet theory is used to predict or describe experimental outcomes, such as the time evolution or the spectra yielding the information of the sample under study. A detailed analysis of the equivalence of these two approaches with an emphasis on applications in NMR will help to improve the theoretical understanding of NMR experiments. In this work, we identify the Floquet-Magnus expansion as essential to prove the mathematical equivalence of Floquet theory and average Hamiltonian theory. We advocate a calculation scheme which is less prone to algebraic mistakes because explicit integration is avoided. On this basis, we provide the first four orders of both theories. We further examine their applicability to some experiments in NMR. As examples, we investigate the Bloch-Siegert shift and dipolar coupled spin systems under magic-angle spinning. Based on our analysis, we recommend the use of the Floquet-Van Vleck approach including both the effective Hamiltonian and the kick operator. The consistent separation of secular and non-secular contributions appears to be especially advantageous for numerical robustness. Its accuracy is about three times better than the one provided by average Hamiltonian theory despite their formal equivalence. Our findings provide important insights into the theoretical background of Floquet theory and average Hamiltonian theory. This includes the extent of their algebraic and perturbative equivalence, with an emphasis on how these findings are of relevance in the analysis of solid-state NMR experiments.

Generalised quantum Stein's lemma more robust than ever

Authors: Filippo Girardi, Kuan-Yi Lee, Masahito Hayashi, Ludovico Lami
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.17309v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work generalizes the quantum Stein's lemma to handle quantum hypothesis testing scenarios where the underlying source of quantum states is not strictly independent and identically distributed. By employing the quantum Wasserstein distance, the authors provide a robust, universal bound that applies to sources merely 'close' to i.i.d. This significantly broadens the applicability of fundamental quantum information bounds for technological applications.

Why it may be interesting. The generalization of fundamental bounds like Stein's lemma to non-i.i.d. sources is crucial for modeling realistic quantum processes where local correlations or non-stationarity are present, which is relevant in quantum communication and sensing.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the generalization of quantum Stein's lemma for quantum hypothesis testing, particularly when the source of quantum states deviates from the ideal independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) case.

Main result. They establish a universal version of the lemma applicable to sources asymptotically close to i.i.d. with respect to the normalized quantum Wasserstein distance of order 1.

Method. The analysis relies on defining and utilizing the quantum Wasserstein distance ($W_1$) to characterize convergence for 'almost i.i.d.' sources, extending results based on relative entropy divergences.

Model / system. The framework involves distinguishing between two hypotheses ($ ho_n$ vs. $S(n)$) on a tensor product Hilbert space $H^{\otimes n}$, using quantum states and error exponents.

Key observables. Type II error exponent ($eta_\epsilon$), regularised relative entropy ($D_\infty( ho \| S)$), and the quantum Wasserstein distance ($W_1$).

Important parameters / regimes. The order of the Wasserstein distance (order 1), the error tolerance $\epsilon$, and the asymptotic limit $n o \infty$.

Assumptions / limitations. The null source must be asymptotically close to i.i.d. with respect to the normalized quantum Wasserstein distance, and the alternative set $S(n)$ must satisfy several structural axioms (e.g., compactness, convexity).

Figures summary. Not specified, but the notes mention Figure 7 illustrating a three-region decomposition of the Hilbert space $\mathbb{H}^{\otimes n}$ using projectors.

Paper structure. The paper builds from the standard GQSL for i.i.d. sources, extends the theory by introducing the Wasserstein distance to handle general sources, and culminates in solving the Stein exponent for arbitrarily varying null hypotheses.

Abstract

The generalised quantum Stein's lemma is a key result in quantum hypothesis testing, and connects this fundamental primitive of quantum information processing with quantum resource manipulation, a task that is central for technological applications. Prior works have proved this statement in the idealised setting of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) sequences of quantum states, and recent extensions consider also sources that are 'close' to i.i.d., according to the strict notion put forth by Mazzola, Sutter, and Renner. For several applications, however, one would need to consider yet more general sources. We establish a version of the generalised quantum Stein's lemma that is conceptually much simpler and general, as it applies to any source that is asymptotically close to an i.i.d. state with respect to the normalised quantum Wasserstein distance of order 1. As an immediate consequence, we solve the Stein exponent of a scenario where the null hypothesis is arbitrarily varying, expressing it in terms of i.i.d. Stein exponents corresponding to arbitrary states in the convex hull of the null hypothesis base set.

Geometric quantifier of the incompatibility of single-particle property attribution in indistinguishable boson systems

Authors: P. Céspedes, A. Valdés-Hernández, F. H. Holik, A. P. Majtey
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.17256v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work introduces a geometric quantifier to measure entanglement in indistinguishable boson systems by quantifying the incompatibility of assigning individual properties to the particles. It extends the initial qubit formulation, which uses the Majorana representation, to higher-dimensional qudits. The resulting measure, $E_N(\Phi)$, provides a powerful, general tool for characterizing quantum correlations in complex many-body quantum systems.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a rigorous, geometrically motivated tool to quantify entanglement in systems where standard local operations theory breaks down due to particle indistinguishability, which is crucial for understanding quantum resources in many-body quantum computation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses how to quantify entanglement in indistinguishable boson systems by measuring the incompatibility of assigning well-defined single-particle properties to the constituent particles.

Main result. A generalized geometric quantifier, $E_N(\Phi)$, is introduced that successfully extends the incompatibility measure from qubits to qudits, linking it to the geometry of the state relative to the manifold of symmetrized states.

Method. The method involves defining the quantifier as a combination of the state's fidelity to the symmetrized state manifold and a measure of pairwise property-attribution incompatibility derived from Fubini-Study angles.

Model / system. The study focuses on pure states of $N$ indistinguishable bosons, initially using the Majorana stellar representation for qubits, and generalizing this framework to $d$-level bosons (qudits).

Key observables. The geometric quantifier $E_N(\Phi)$, pairwise fidelity $F_{ij}$, and the minimum incompatibility measure $E_{\min}^{N,\Phi}$.

Important parameters / regimes. The number of particles $N$, the dimension $d$ of the single-boson Hilbert space, and the pairwise fidelity $F_{ij}$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on extending the GMW separability criterion and adapting geometric tools (like the Majorana representation) to higher dimensions where direct factorization is lost.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the Majorana constellation for small $N$ (e.g., $N=3, 4$) and show the dependence of the incompatibility measure on system parameters.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the concept using qubits and the Majorana representation, deriving an explicit quantifier $E_N(\Psi)$. It then generalizes this approach to qudits ($d>2$) by defining a composite quantifier $E_N(\Phi)$ that incorporates both fidelity distance and generalized pairwise incompatibility.

Abstract

Entanglement in indistinguishable particle systems can be characterized by the impossibility of unambiguously attributing a complete set of physical properties to the individual constituent particles. In this work, we introduce a geometric quantifier of the incompatibility of such simultaneous property attribution for pure states of $N$ indistinguishable bosons. Using the Majorana stellar representation, any symmetric multi-qubit state can be expressed as the symmetrization of constituent single-particle states, allowing the associated single-particle properties to be directly linked with the corresponding Majorana stars. This establishes a direct connection between the geometry of the Majorana representation on the Bloch sphere and the entanglement criterion based on the attribution of single-particle properties in systems of identical two-level bosons. We then generalize this geometric approach to higher-dimensional bosons, extending the quantifier from qubits to qudits, while retaining a geometric interpretation of property-attribution incompatibility in terms of Fubini-Study angles.

Graph construction in QUBO-based recursive phylogenetic tree reconstruction

Authors: Yoshiki Kanazawa, Ashish Joshi, Takahiko Koyama
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.16640v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work investigates how the initial representation of pairwise sequence relationships impacts the accuracy of phylogenetic tree reconstruction using a recursive normalized-cut (Ncut) approach. By comparing Ncut against Neighbor Joining (NJ) using various affinity matrices, the authors demonstrate that the graph construction method is crucial, requiring a joint evaluation of representation, optimization, and recursive assembly.

Why it may be interesting. While the core topic is bioinformatics, the methodology involves formulating graph partitioning as a QUBO problem and solving it using optimization techniques, which touches upon computational aspects relevant to quantum optimization.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of accurately reconstructing evolutionary relationships (phylogenetic trees) from molecular sequence data.

Main result. The choice of graph construction (pairwise affinity representation) is a critical determinant of the accuracy in recursive Ncut-based phylogenetic reconstruction, and this representation must be evaluated jointly with the reconstruction method.

Method. The study uses a recursive normalized-cut (Ncut) framework, formulating graph-partitioning subproblems as QUBO models solved via Simulated Bifurcation, and compares its results against Neighbor Joining (NJ).

Model / system. The system involves molecular sequence data (amino-acid and nucleotide alignments) used to model evolutionary divergence. Comparisons are made across various distance metrics (e.g., WAG, JC69, BLAST-derived).

Key observables. Internal split recovery (measured as percentage of recovered splits), and the mean paired difference in split recovery between Ncut and NJ.

Important parameters / regimes. Evolutionary divergence levels (branch lengths), sequence types (nucleotide vs. amino-acid), and the specific affinity representation used (e.g., normalized bit-score, local/global kernels).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the performance of the Ncut framework depends heavily on the quality of the input pairwise distance/affinity matrix.

Figures summary. The figures summarize the effect of post-swap refinement on accuracy, compare Ncut vs. NJ performance across different datasets, and show the overall mean paired differences in split recovery.

Paper structure. The paper systematically evaluates how different pairwise representations influence the recursive Ncut framework, compares these results against established distance-based methods like NJ, and analyzes the impact of refinement steps.

Abstract

Molecular sequence data are used to reconstruct evolutionary relationships among taxa, but reconstruction accuracy depends not only on the tree-building method but also on how pairwise sequence relationships are represented. We evaluated sequence-to-affinity representations in a recursive normalized-cut (Ncut) framework whose graph-partitioning subproblems were formulated as quadratic unconstrained binary optimization (QUBO) models and solved using Simulated Bifurcation. Using simulated amino-acid and nucleotide datasets spanning multiple tree-generation settings and evolutionary divergence, we compared normalized bit-score affinities with representations derived from transformed sequence similarities and evolutionary distances, examined post-swap refinement, and used neighbor joining (NJ) as a distance-based comparator. Affinity representation substantially affected internal split recovery, particularly for nucleotide data. JC69-based local affinities maintained comparatively high accuracy as divergence increased, whereas normalized bit-score and BLAST-derived kernel representations declined more markedly. Post-swap refinement generally improved recovery, but not consistently across individual reconstructions. NJ achieved higher mean split recovery than corresponding recursive Ncut reconstructions for WAG and JC69 distances across all evaluated conditions, whereas recursive Ncut outperformed NJ for BLAST-derived logarithmic distances under some conditions. These results show that graph construction is an important determinant of recursive Ncut-based phylogenetic reconstruction. A representation that performs well within Ncut does not necessarily provide the most accurate use of the underlying pairwise distances. Pairwise representation, affinity transformation, optimization, and recursive tree construction should therefore be evaluated jointly.

Hyperbolic color codes with constant rate and polynomial distance

Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.16125v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

Summary. This paper constructs a new family of hyperbolic color codes that overcome previous limitations by achieving both a constant encoding rate and polynomial code distance. The methodology combines advanced algebraic topology with hyperbolic geometry to define these codes. This breakthrough provides a powerful theoretical testbed for developing high-performance, fault-tolerant quantum computation protocols.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the theoretical limits of quantum error correction by providing concrete constructions for high-rate codes with large, polynomially scaling distances, which is crucial for building fault-tolerant quantum computers.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is to construct hyperbolic color codes that simultaneously achieve a constant encoding rate and polynomial code distance.

Main result. The authors construct a family of hyperbolic color codes that achieve both constant rate and polynomial distance, particularly in even dimensions, establishing a significant improvement over previous logarithmic distance bounds.

Method. The construction relies on building codes from arithmetic hyperbolic manifolds that support polynomial-distance hyperbolic toric codes, utilizing algebraic topology concepts like chain complexes and homology groups.

Model / system. The work focuses on quantum error-correcting codes, specifically hyperbolic color codes, which are a high-rate quantum low-density parity-check (qLDPC) code structure relevant for fault-tolerant quantum computation.

Key observables. Code distance ($d$), encoding rate (aiming for constant rate), and the number of logical qubits ($k$).

Important parameters / regimes. The construction applies to arbitrary dimensions $D \geq 4$, with specific results detailed for even dimensions ($D=2m$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis builds upon the existence of hyperbolic manifolds supporting the necessary structures and relies on applying advanced theorems like the Lück approximation theorem.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the construction process, showing the use of barycentric subdivision for the color code, the transformation to a toric code for distance bounding, and the explicit determination of scaling exponents for $k$ and $d$.

Paper structure. The paper develops the mathematical framework using chain complexes to define code parameters ($k$ and $d$), proves the existence of the desired codes via Theorem 1, and provides detailed algebraic and geometric machinery (e.g., analyzing symmetry groups $SOf$ and spectral gaps) to establish the polynomial bounds.

Abstract

Recent advances in quantum hardware relax the strict geometric-locality constraints traditionally imposed on quantum error-correcting codes, motivating interest in high-rate quantum low-density parity-check (qLDPC) codes. At the same time, color codes provide a particularly rich setting for fault-tolerant quantum computation, underlying protocols such as single-shot error correction and self-correcting quantum computation. Hyperbolic color codes provide a class of high-rate qLDPC codes that also retain the structural features of color codes relevant to fault-tolerant quantum computation. However, previous constructions of hyperbolic color codes have achieved at most logarithmic code distance. In this work, we construct hyperbolic color codes with both constant rate and polynomial distance by building on arithmetic hyperbolic manifolds that support polynomial-distance hyperbolic toric codes. Our construction applies in arbitrary dimension \(D\geq 4\). In even dimensions, the resulting type-\(D/2\) color codes have constant encoding rate and polynomial distance, while in other cases the number of logical qubits and the code distance both exhibit polynomial scaling. We further derive explicit exponents for polynomial lower bounds as functions of the dimension and code type. These results establish a family of hyperbolic color codes simultaneously achieving constant rate and polynomial distance and providing a testbed to explore fault-tolerant quantum computation protocols that combine high-rate quantum codes with the structural advantages of color codes.

Summary. This paper significantly advances the understanding of the gap between quantum and classical communication complexity for total functions. By refining existing frameworks, the authors achieve stronger lower bounds for randomized communication relative to the quantum communication required. This work is a major theoretical result in quantum information theory.

Why it may be interesting. While highly theoretical, the techniques used—especially the construction of complex functions and the use of extractors—are foundational tools in quantum information theory that underpin the understanding of computational limits.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to improve the known separations between the quantum and classical communication complexity of total Boolean functions.

Main result. It establishes significant separations, showing that for two quantum messages, the randomized communication complexity $R(f)$ can be exponentially larger than the quantum complexity $Q_2(f)$, achieving $R(f) = e\Omega(\sqrt{n})$ versus $Q_2(f) = ext{polylog}(n)$.

Method. The improvements rely on refining previous constructions by replacing lookup tables with seeded linear extractors and constructing total functions $F_{ ext{tot}}$ from partial functions $F$ to lift the separation bounds.

Model / system. The analysis focuses on the communication complexity of total Boolean functions $f: \{0, 1\}^n imes \{0, 1\}^n o \{0, 1\}$, involving quantum and randomized communication protocols.

Key observables. Randomized communication complexity $R(f)$, quantum communication complexity $Q_r(f)$, and the input size overhead $N$.

Important parameters / regimes. The separation scales involve polylogarithmic quantum communication versus bounds like $e\Omega(\sqrt{n})$ or $e\Omega(n^{1-\varepsilon})$ for randomized communication.

Assumptions / limitations. The proof heavily relies on the existence of strong seeded extractors and the ability to construct total functions $F_{ ext{tot}}$ from partial functions $F$.

Figures summary. Not specified.

Paper structure. The paper builds upon previous work by first establishing general conversion theorems (Theorem 4) relating partial to total function complexities, and then applying these techniques to specific functions (like Shapem or k-Forrelation) to derive concrete, improved separation bounds (Theorems 2 and 3).

Abstract

We refine Gavinsky's framework (arXiv:2608.18784) for exponential separations between quantum and randomized communication complexity of total functions and obtain larger separations: polylogarithmic quantum communication versus $\tildeΩ(\sqrt n)$ randomized communication with two quantum messages, and versus $Ω(n^{1-\varepsilon})$ for every fixed $0<\varepsilon<1$ with more quantum messages.

La substitution studies on the heavy-fermion superconductor CeRh$_2$As$_2$

Authors: Sushma Lakshmi Ravi Sankar, Manuel Brando, Jochen Wosnitza, Seunghyun Khim
Type: experiment · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.17080v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

Summary. This work systematically studies the effect of La substitution on the heavy-fermion superconductor CeRh2As2. By measuring transport and magnetic properties, the authors find that while the electronic state remains largely intact, the superconducting and magnetic ordering temperatures are rapidly suppressed. This suppression is primarily attributed to disorder-induced pair breaking, offering insights into the nature of unconventional superconductivity in correlated materials.

Why it may be interesting. The clear demonstration that disorder (from substitution) can suppress superconductivity in a manner distinct from hydrostatic pressure provides a powerful experimental tool for understanding unconventional pairing mechanisms in correlated electron systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how substituting Cerium (Ce) with Lanthanum (La) affects the low-temperature superconducting and magnetic phases in the heavy-fermion superconductor CeRh2As2.

Main result. La substitution rapidly suppresses both the superconducting transition temperature (Tc) and the magnetic ordering temperature (T0), suggesting that disorder-induced pair breaking is the dominant mechanism, rather than simple lattice expansion effects.

Method. The research employed single-crystal measurements, including electrical resistivity, specific heat, and magnetic susceptibility, complemented by theoretical modeling using the Abrikosov-Gor'kov formalism and Crystal Electric Field (CEF) theory.

Model / system. The system is the heavy-fermion superconductor CeRh2As2 and its doped derivative Ce(1-x)La(x)Rh2As2. The physics is governed by the interplay between localized Ce 4f moments and itinerant conduction electrons, characteristic of heavy fermion behavior.

Key observables. Superconducting transition temperatures (Tc, T0), resistivity coherence maximum (T*max), specific heat divided by temperature (C/T), and magnetic susceptibility ($\chi$).

Important parameters / regimes. La concentration (x up to 0.1), effective negative pressure (-0.3 GPa), Kondo energy scale, and superconducting gap anisotropy parameters.

Assumptions / limitations. The rapid suppression of Tc is attributed to substitution-induced disorder causing strong pair breaking, and the analysis relies on fitting magnetic data to CEF models.

Figures summary. Figures show the monotonic increase of lattice parameters with La substitution, the suppression of T*max, Tc, and T0 with increasing x, and the evolution of C/T vs. T, illustrating the impact of disorder.

Paper structure. The paper systematically examines the structural changes (lattice parameters), electronic state preservation (valence, non-Fermi liquid behavior), and the dramatic suppression of superconducting and magnetic ordering temperatures as a function of La doping concentration.

Abstract

CeRh$_2$As$_2$ has been receiving considerable attention due to its unusual two-phase superconductivity. The superconducting (SC) phase appears at $T_{\mathrm{c}}$ = 0.35 K in an ordered state (phase I) of the Ce-4$f$ moments, which develops below $T_{\mathrm{0}}$ = 0.55 K. The microscopic nature of phase I has not been fully established yet. We report a single-crystal study of the effect of La substitution on these low-temperature phases in Ce$_{1-\textit{x}}$La$_\textit{x}$Rh$_2$As$_2$ up to $x$ = 0.1. The lattice parameters increase monotonically with $x$, corresponding to an effective negative pressure of approximately -0.3 GPa for $x$ = 0.1. While the Ce$^{3+}$ local valence state and the non-Fermi-liquid behavior are preserved, the resistivity coherence maximum $T^{*}_{\mathrm{max}}$ $\approx$ 45 K in the pristine sample shifts to lower temperatures with increasing $x$, indicating a suppression of the Kondo energy scale upon lattice expansion. On the other hand, both $T_{\mathrm{c}}$ and $T_{\mathrm{0}}$ are rapidly suppressed to below 0.1 K for $x$ $>$ 0.05. Notably, such a rapid decrease of $T_{\mathrm{c}}$ under moderate negative pressure is unexpected, but is rather consistent with substitution-induced disorder effect which leads to strong pair breaking in unconventional superconductors. The observed fragility of phase I under both pressure and disorder may imply its itinerant origin.

Link-middle-cut lower bounds for Clifford circuit synthesis

Authors: Søren Fuglede Jørgensen
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.16208v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops advanced mathematical tools to find lower bounds on the minimum number of CNOT gates needed to implement any Clifford quantum circuit. By extending the Link-Middle-Cut framework to use symplectic tableaux, the authors derive multiple, computable invariants. These bounds are shown to be tight for simple cases like qubit permutations and significantly improve heuristic search algorithms for quantum circuit optimization.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical tools developed (LMC extension, determinantal invariants) provide powerful, computable lower bounds for circuit synthesis, which is fundamental to understanding the efficiency limits of quantum computation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The central problem is minimizing the number of CNOT gates required to synthesize a given Clifford operator on $n$ qubits.

Main result. The authors derive efficiently computable lower bounds for the CNOT complexity using an extension of the Link-Middle-Cut (LMC) framework, proving tightness for qubit permutations as $3(n-k)$.

Method. The method extends the LMC framework by mapping the problem to symplectic tableaux and introducing block-support connectivity graphs and determinantal invariants to establish rigorous lower bounds.

Model / system. The system is an $n$-qubit quantum circuit implementing Clifford operators, which are analyzed using the mathematical structure of the symplectic group $ ext{Sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{F}_2)$ via symplectic tableaux.

Key observables. CNOT-complexity (minimum CNOT count), link/cut invariants ($\hat{v}(F), \hat{e}(F)$), and determinantal invariants ($ ext{rank}(M(F) + I_n)$).

Important parameters / regimes. Number of qubits ($n$) and the number of cycles ($k$) in the corresponding permutation.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the ability to extend the LMC framework from CNOT-only circuits to general Clifford operators, and it addresses the case where output qubit relabeling is free.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the link–middle–cut bound for a four-cycle permutation, explicitly calculating the required links, middles, and cuts to match the known lower bound of 9 CNOT gates.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theory by first extending the LMC framework to Clifford operators using symplectic tableaux. It then derives multiple, efficiently computable lower bounds based on graph invariants (links/cuts) and determinantal invariants (middle contribution). Finally, it demonstrates the utility of these bounds by using them as an admissible heuristic in an A*-based search algorithm.

Abstract

Minimizing the number of CNOT gates required to synthesize a Clifford operator is a central problem in quantum circuit optimization. We extend the link--middle--cut (LMC) framework for linear reversible circuits to Clifford operators represented by binary symplectic tableaux. By introducing block-support connectivity graphs and determinantal invariants, we obtain efficiently computable lower bounds on ancilla-free CNOT complexity. We prove that these bounds are tight for qubit permutations: a permutation of $n$ qubits with $k$ cycles requires exactly $3(n-k)$ CNOT gates, even when arbitrary one-qubit Clifford gates are available. We also derive efficiently computable bounds for synthesis up to a permutation of the output qubits, corresponding to free qubit relabeling. Finally, we further demonstrate the utility of the bound by using it as an admissible heuristic for $A^*$-based Clifford synthesis and show that, on benchmark instances, the resulting search can prove optimality beyond what was possible with earlier SAT-based methods, and that it may be used in combination with earlier $A^*$ heuristics to improve the CNOT counts obtained through heuristic search.

Abstract

Sample-based quantum diagonalization (SQD) is a hybrid quantum-classical method for electronic-structure calculations. We applied SQD to a titanium-based metallocene catalyst system for 1-hexene production, where free-energy differences must be predicted within 1 kcal/mol to accurately determine product selectivity. However, currently accessible active spaces on quantum computers are limited in size, leaving significant dynamical correlation effects outside the active space. To address this challenge, we combined SQD with Tailored Coupled Cluster (TCC) theory. In this SQD-TCC framework, static correlation is treated by SQD, while dynamical correlation is incorporated through CCSD and its perturbative triples extension, TCC(T). We calculated the relative energies of two transition states governing 1-hexene selectivity. While SQD alone did not yield converged relative energies within practically accessible active spaces, SQD-TCC and SQD-TCC(T) provided reasonably converged results. Importantly, the relative energy obtained from SQD-TCC(T) differed by more than 1 kcal/mol from the corresponding CCSD(T) result, demonstrating the significance of static correlation in this system and its impact on predicted selectivity. Although TCC(T) calculations based on CASCI are feasible for small active spaces, the present system requires substantially larger active spaces that are beyond the reach of CASCI. By using SQD, we were able to access active spaces impractical for classical CASCI calculations. These results demonstrate that both static and dynamical electron correlation are essential for a reliable description of this chemistry and highlight the importance of incorporating dynamical correlation into quantum-computing approaches targeting chemically accurate simulations.

Abstract

Small high-rate non-CSS stabilizer codes provide compact platforms for encoded quantum computation, but mixed-Pauli checks and limited native transversal logical gates complicate fault-tolerant dynamics. Block-level constructions offer an alternative by mapping an entire logical block to a physical circuit rather than compiling separately protected logical gates. We investigate this approach using the high-rate [[8,3,3]] non-CSS code and logical Trotter circuits as a testbed. We construct flagged syndrome-extraction circuits and establish a circuit-level memory pseudo-threshold near \(1.5\times10^{-3}\). We then apply our symplectic-transvection construction, which maps a logical Trotter circuit to a physical circuit with the same block pattern for any stabilizer code. Although this mapping preserves the intended unitary algebraically, encoded Trotter circuits exhibit asymmetry between logical-\(X\) and logical-\(Z\) failure channels. Single-fault analysis identifies the mechanism: a fault on the shared parity ancilla can propagate through the uncomputation network into an undetectable logical operator, reducing the effective circuit distance in the affected sector. We evaluate flag-conditioned recovery, biased-noise decoding, CliNR resource verification, flag postselection, and asymmetric gate-noise models. These methods suppress propagated faults but do not simultaneously suppress both logical sectors in the realistic configurations studied. A diagnostic protected limit removing the identified malignant first-order locations restores pseudo-threshold behavior in both sectors, approaching memory performance. These results demonstrate the potential of block-level logical constructions for non-CSS codes without rich native transversal gate sets and the joint protection of the parity network, analog rotation, and recovery required to preserve fault-tolerant distance.
