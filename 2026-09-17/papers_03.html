Portable Vector NV-Diamond Magnetometer for Shot-Noise-Limited, Drift-Free Operation in Unshielded Environments

Authors: Annirudh K P, Vinayak Rane, Shradha Atakar, Sanika Joshi, Maheshwar Mangat, Jay Gharat, Siddharth Tallur, Kasturi Saha
Type: experiment · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.16901v1

Summary. This paper presents an advanced, integrated digital architecture for NV-diamond magnetometers designed for stable operation in unshielded environments. By employing differential readout, second-derivative tracking, and a magnetic eigenvector transformation, the system successfully suppresses both high-frequency laser noise and slow thermal drifts. The results confirm that the magnetometer achieves near-fundamental shot-noise limits while maintaining exceptional long-term stability.

Why it may be interesting. This work represents a significant engineering achievement in quantum sensing, providing robust methods to mitigate environmental noise sources (laser noise, thermal drift) that typically limit the practical deployment of quantum sensors, which is highly relevant to AMO physics applications.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is achieving stable, drift-free, and high-sensitivity quantum magnetometry using NV-diamond magnetometers in unshielded, long-term, real-world environments.

Main result. The proposed integrated digital architecture achieves an off-resonance noise factor matching the fundamental limit (1.0 $\pm$ 0.1) and demonstrates a ten-fold suppression of long-term drift.

Method. The authors combine a dynamic differential readout for laser noise suppression, second-derivative Lorentzian tracking for gain correction, and a magnetic eigenvector transformation to eliminate slow, common-mode thermal drifts.

Model / system. The system utilizes ensemble nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers in diamond for three-axis magnetic field sensing at room temperature. The measurement relies on tracking multiple Zeeman-split ODMR transitions to isolate the external magnetic signal from environmental drifts.

Key observables. Off-resonance noise factor, three magnetic projections ($B_1, B_2, B_3$), and the residual noise floor over long time scales.

Important parameters / regimes. Off-resonance noise factor of $1.0 \pm 0.1$; ten-fold suppression of long-term drift; ambient unshielded conditions.

Assumptions / limitations. Conventional stabilization techniques are insufficient for long-term, unshielded operation, necessitating intrinsic resonance tracking for drift correction.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the setup schematic for differential optical readout, the use of six simultaneously addressed ODMR transitions in three Zeeman-split pairs, and noise density plots comparing various filtering techniques.

Paper structure. The paper details the limitations of existing magnetometers, introduces the novel digital architecture (differential readout, second-derivative tracking, eigenvector transformation), presents the experimental results showing noise floor matching and drift suppression, and concludes with the feasibility of field-ready quantum magnetometry.

Abstract

Ensemble nitrogen-vacancy (NV) diamond magnetometers combine high sensitivity with vector-field reconstruction, but practical deployment is limited by errors arising from high-frequency laser noise during short-duration operations and slow-varying gain fluctuations and offset drift during long-term operation. Here, we present an integrated digital architecture for achieving NV magnetometry stability across distinct timescales. A dynamic differential readout continuously balances fluorescence and reference channels to suppress correlated laser noise. Second-derivative Lorentzian lineshape tracking enables in-situ correction of slope variations arising from slow changes in gain and optical excitation. We further identify temperature-induced bias-magnet fluctuations as a dominant source of long-term drift and introduce a magnetic eigenvector transformation that uses the intrinsic response of NV resonances to eliminate these variations. We show, under unshielded ambient conditions, that this architecture achieves an off-resonance noise factor of 1.0 $\pm$ 0.1, matching the fundamental limit with ten-fold suppression of long-term drift, enabling stable, field-ready quantum magnetometry.

Unsupervised Machine Learning of the Contact Process

Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.16506v1

Summary. This work applies unsupervised machine learning (PCA and VAE) to analyze the active-absorbing phase transition of the Contact Process. The authors overcome technical hurdles—such as the positive-definite nature of the data—by modifying the ML inputs (e.g., sign-reversing data augmentation or using adaptive priors). This demonstrates that successful ML analysis of complex physical transitions requires deep incorporation of the system's underlying physical symmetries and constraints.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a blueprint for applying advanced ML techniques to non-equilibrium statistical physics problems where standard equilibrium assumptions break down, offering insights into characterizing critical phenomena.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Characterizing the active-absorbing phase transition in the Contact Process using unsupervised machine learning methods.

Main result. The success of ML methods in phase transitions critically depends on tailoring the representation to respect the system's physical constraints, such as data augmentation for PCA or using parameter-dependent priors for VAEs.

Method. The study employs Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) to analyze the system's configuration space.

Model / system. The Contact Process (CP) is studied on lattices (1D, 2D, triangular), which exhibits a continuous active-absorbing phase transition belonging to the Directed Percolation universality class.

Key observables. Order parameter ($ ho$), finite-size scaling ratios (e.g., $\Lambda_2/\Lambda_1$), and reconstruction errors ($\mathcal{L}_{ ext{MSE}}$, $\mathcal{L}_{ ext{BCE}}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The control parameter $\lambda$ (infection rate relative to recovery rate) and system size $L$.

Assumptions / limitations. Standard ML techniques fail due to the positive-definite nature of CP configurations, necessitating data augmentation (sign-reversal) or specialized priors (heteroscedastic Gaussian prior).

Figures summary. Figures demonstrate the finite-size scaling collapse of various observables (eigenvalue ratios, order parameter estimates, reconstruction errors) for both PCA and VAE analyses across the critical point.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the physical problem (CP transition) and the failure modes of standard ML techniques. It then details the necessary methodological fixes (data augmentation for PCA, heteroscedastic prior for VAE) and demonstrates their success by showing the recovery of known critical scaling laws.

Abstract

We investigate how unsupervised machine-learning methods can characterize the active-absorbing phase transition in the contact process in one and two spatial dimensions. Our analysis focuses on principal component analysis (PCA) and variational autoencoders (VAEs), and we show that direct applications of these methods encounter a central difficulty: contact process configurations are intrinsically positive-definite. In the PCA case, standard centering procedures fail to isolate the order parameter in the leading component. To address this, we augment the data with sign-reversed configurations, creating a balanced dataset with zero mean while preserving the density information required to describe the transition. This preprocessing allows the dominant principal component to recover the order parameter and reproduce the expected finite-size scaling near criticality. For VAEs, we find a related limitation: a fixed Gaussian prior does not provide sufficient latent regularization across the full range of control parameters and system sizes for positive-definite data. We overcome this by adopting a heteroscedastic Gaussian prior that adapts to the control parameter, leading to a substantial improvement in the finite-size scaling of reconstruction-based observables near the critical threshold. Taken together, these results highlight a key lesson: the success of machine learning in phase-transition problems does not rely on algorithmic complexity alone, but on tailoring the representation to respect the physical structure of the system, including its intrinsic constraints and symmetries.

Summary. This paper details a rigorous analysis procedure for extracting the true source noise spectrum from measurements taken using large ensembles of quantum sensors. By mathematically incorporating the spatial distribution of the sensors, the authors overcome ambiguities inherent in ensemble averaging. This methodology paves the way for quantitative, broadband noise characterization using solid-state quantum platforms.

Why it may be interesting. This work is highly relevant to AMO physics as it deals with using quantum systems (NV centers) as highly sensitive probes to characterize the local electromagnetic environment (noise spectroscopy), a core topic in quantum sensing and open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of accurately reconstructing the true source noise spectrum $S_0(\omega)$ from ensemble coherence measurements $\langle C(T) angle$ when the sensing ensemble has a variable coupling depth distribution.

Main result. The authors present an analysis procedure that incorporates the coupling distribution $P(d)$ to accurately extract the source noise spectrum, demonstrating its applicability to NV centers in diamond.

Method. The method involves analyzing the ensemble coherence decay, which is modeled as an integral over the depth distribution, and using approximations (like the short-T limit) to invert the measured decay into the underlying noise spectrum.

Model / system. The system studied is quantum sensing ensembles, specifically using Nitrogen-Vacancy (NV) centers in diamond. The noise environment is modeled as separable, $S(\omega, d) = S_0(\omega)D(d)$, where $D(d)$ describes depth variation.

Key observables. Ensemble coherence $\langle C(T) angle$, the source noise spectrum $S_0(\omega)$, and the depth distribution $P(d)$.

Important parameters / regimes. Measurement time $T$, depth distribution parameters (e.g., depth range, width), and noise frequency $\omega$.

Assumptions / limitations. Key assumptions include modeling the noise as separable across depth and assuming specific functional forms for the depth distribution (e.g., $d^{-3}$ scaling). A major limitation is the inherent ambiguity between the amplitude of $S_0$ and the scale of $P(d)$.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the accuracy of fitting methods for depth profiles and source spectra, comparing 'naive' fitting against reconstruction methods that account for the depth distribution. Specific comparisons are made between 2 keV and 4 keV ensembles.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theoretical framework for ensemble averaging, analyzes the ambiguities in parameter extraction, and then applies this procedure to model systems (like NV centers) to demonstrate the quantitative extraction of broadband noise spectra.

Abstract

Noise spectroscopy with quantum sensors is a powerful tool for characterising the properties of the sensor environment and detecting critical magnetic phenomena. While replacing single sensors with large ensembles promises substantial gains in measurement sensitivity and field of view, the output becomes difficult to interpret when there is a variable coupling to targets across the sensing ensemble. In this work we present an analysis procedure for accessing the true source noise spectrum using ensemble coherence from dynamical decoupling measurements. We show that sequence timing plays a dual role in ensemble noise spectroscopy, setting both the frequency selectivity and the weighted collection of signal across the ensemble, and demonstrate how incorporation of the coupling distribution allows accurate parameter extraction of the noise. We apply this procedure to a model system, shallow nitrogen-vacancy centres in diamond, and show that consistent information about the spectral character of MHz-range surface noise can be obtained from two sensing ensembles featuring distinct depth distributions. Our results pave the way for using large quantum sensor ensembles for quantitative, broadband noise spectroscopy, with straightforward extension to other solid-state and molecular sensing platforms.

Summary. This theoretical paper develops an edge-KPZ theory to explain the anomalous scaling of the first passage time in neutral population evolution. By combining coalescent theory with the Kardar-Parisi-Zhang equation, the authors show that the time to reach a distant phenotype scales anomalously with mutation variance. This work provides a deep link between population genetics and non-equilibrium statistical physics.

Why it may be interesting. The connection between population genetics (Wright-Fisher model) and non-equilibrium statistical physics tools like KPZ theory provides a powerful, universal framework for understanding how collective fluctuations dictate macroscopic evolutionary rates.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the anomalous scaling of the first passage time (FPT) in evolutionary dynamics, specifically how the time required for a phenotype to reach a distant state is determined by mutation and population fluctuations.

Main result. Neutral Wright-Fisher evolution exhibits an anomalous FPT scaling proportional to $ ilde{L}^3$ in an intermediate regime, which is described by an edge-KPZ theory.

Method. The authors combine coalescent theory with the Kardar-Parisi-Zhang (KPZ) equation, analyzing the evolution of the population density via stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) and using Cole-Hopf transformations.

Model / system. The model is neutral Wright-Fisher evolution, where phenotypic change is driven by Gaussian mutations ($\sigma^2$) and population dynamics are governed by density evolution equations. The analysis focuses on the separation distribution of two lineages over time.

Key observables. Mean first-passage time ($\langle T_L angle$), population density $ ho(x, t)$, and the separation probability $p_{ ext{pair}}(S)$.

Important parameters / regimes. Mutation variance ($\sigma^2$), population size ($N$), and the target phenotypic distance ($L$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the diffusion approximation for smooth densities and uses scaling limits ($ ilde{t} = t/N$) to derive continuum equations.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the anomalous scaling of $\langle T_L angle$ vs. $\sigma^2$ (showing $\propto (\sigma^2)^{-3/2}$) and confirm the scaling collapse of FPT data onto a master curve.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining the physical problem (FPT in evolution) to deriving the continuum SPDE for density evolution, linking this to coalescence theory, and finally applying the KPZ framework to establish the three distinct scaling regimes ($ ilde{L}^2 o ilde{L}^3 o ilde{L}^2$).

Abstract

The pace of evolution depends on how rapidly new phenotypes arise. We show that neutral Wright-Fisher evolution exhibits anomalous first passage despite diffusive mutations. The mean time for the first individual to reach a prescribed phenotypic distance scales approximately as $(σ^2)^{-3/2}$ with mutation variance $σ^2$. Two crossovers bound this regime, with inverse-variance scaling on either side. Combining coalescent theory with Kardar-Parisi-Zhang (KPZ) fluctuations at the dilute population edge, we develop an edge-KPZ theory of all three regimes. The anomaly persists under weak selection.

Summary. This work classifies quantum computation capabilities based on how a processor interacts with a thermal bath while monitoring its environment. It establishes a critical parameter ($\chi$) that dictates whether the system is classically simulable or capable of universal quantum computation. The key finding is that even minimal environmental records can preserve enough quantum information to enable quantum advantage.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a rigorous, quantitative link between the thermodynamics of open quantum systems (thermal decoherence) and the fundamental limits of quantum computation, suggesting that environmental monitoring itself can be a resource.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper determines the computational classification (classically simulable vs. quantum universal) of a stabilizer processor coupled to a monitored thermal bath, based on the environmental records retained by its controller.

Main result. Quantum universal computation is possible if the system parameters exceed a critical boundary ($\chi > 0$), even when only retaining minimal environmental records like whether any exchange occurred.

Method. The analysis uses exact computational classification based on energy-counting thermal-idle instruments, deriving time-dependent stabilizer margins ($\gamma$) for different readout protocols.

Model / system. The model is a quantum processor with ideal stabilizer control coupled to independent local Markov baths, undergoing thermal idle periods where energy exchange is monitored.

Key observables. Signed stabilizer margins ($\gamma_q, \gamma_{par}, \gamma_{\emptyset}$), the calibration number ($\chi$), and the computational boundary $\chi=0$.

Important parameters / regimes. Relaxation time ($T_1$), homogeneous coherence time ($T_2$), and equilibrium excited-state population ($p_e$).

Assumptions / limitations. Assumes ideal stabilizer control and independent local Markov baths; the analysis compares resource-side records versus the averaged channel.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the computational boundary ($\chi_{\max} \le 0$ vs. $\chi > 0$) and compare the signed stabilizer margins for different readouts (quiet branch vs. terminal parity).

Paper structure. The paper establishes the computational boundary using the ratio $(1-p_e)T_2/T_1$. It then compares the information retained by different readouts (full record, terminal parity, no exchange bit) to show that retaining minimal information can still herald quantum advantage when $\chi > 0$.

Abstract

At a fixed thermal exposure, the same stabilizer processor can be classically simulable or quantum universal, depending on which environmental records its controller retains. We give an exact computational classification of energy-counting thermal-idle instruments in a quantum processor with ideal stabilizer control and independent local Markov baths. The relaxation time $T_1$, homogeneous coherence time $T_2$, and equilibrium excited-state population $p_e$ determine an exact computational boundary at $(1-p_e)T_2/T_1=1$. If every location lies at or below it, branchwise nonnegative stabilizer decompositions give an explicit efficient classical sampler for the adaptive circuit and its full time-resolved exchange record. Above it at a single repeatedly accessible location, a suitable idle duration and no-exchange conditioning supply distillable ancillas and enable universal quantum computation with polynomial overhead. At finite temperature on the resource side, erasing the record at a sufficiently long, unsplit exposure makes the averaged channel stabilizer measure-and-prepare, and even a terminal parity check then yields only simulable branches. Yet at that same exposure, retaining only the bit recording whether any exchange occurred still heralds distillable ancillas, because a thermal round trip restores the parity after its first exchange has already removed the coherence.

Odd diffusion and power-law correlations in chiral mass-transport processes

Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: non-equilibrium universality 3/5 · Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5 · correlated / nonlocal dissipation 1/5 · driven-dissipative phase transition 1/5 · methods for driven-dissipative 1/5

Summary. This paper analyzes mass transport on a lattice driven by chiral hopping, which breaks fundamental symmetries. The key finding is that this 'odd diffusion' mechanism alone can generate universal, power-law decay in density correlations, even when the underlying hopping rates are isotropic. This establishes a new theoretical link between chirality, transport coefficients, and long-range spatial correlations in non-equilibrium statistical mechanics.

Why it may be interesting. The connection between microscopic chiral transport rules and macroscopic power-law correlations in non-equilibrium steady states provides a powerful theoretical tool for understanding fluctuation phenomena in driven many-body systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how chiral mass-transport processes generate scale-invariant spatial correlations in non-equilibrium steady states, particularly in systems that are macroscopically isotropic.

Main result. Odd diffusion provides a mechanism for algebraic decay in two-point density correlations, $C(r) \sim |r|^{-4}$, which is distinct from correlations arising from simple anisotropic hopping.

Method. The authors employ exact calculations for steady-state density-density correlations and derive a non-equilibrium analog of the Green-Kubo relation by analyzing the continuum limit of the lattice dynamics.

Model / system. The model is a mass-conserving Markov jump process on a square lattice, termed a chiral mass chipping model (MCM). The dynamics are explicitly chiral, breaking time-reversal and mirror symmetries.

Key observables. Two-point density-density correlation function $C(r)$, structure factor in the zero-wavenumber limit, and the odd diffusion coefficient $D( ext{odd})$.

Important parameters / regimes. Chirality strength, the odd diffusion coefficient $D( ext{odd})$, and the system size/boundary conditions (periodic vs. open).

Assumptions / limitations. The derivation of the hydrodynamic equation relies on a gradient expansion, and the model assumes mass conservation throughout the process.

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically illustrates the chiral dynamics via an L-shaped path, while Figure 2 shows the relaxation of a localized density profile, indicating transient circulating currents.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the microscopic model dynamics, derives the macroscopic hydrodynamic equation via gradient expansion, calculates the steady-state correlation functions, and analyzes the dual role of odd diffusion on fluctuations.

Abstract

We study mass-conserving Markov jump processes on a square lattice, where masses hop with a preferred rotational sense, thus breaking both time-reversal and mirror symmetries. We consider closed systems with both periodic and open (reflecting) boundaries, the latter supporting a steady-state edge current. We show that odd diffusion, arising from the chiral transport, generically provides a mechanism for the emergence of scale-invariant two-point density correlations in nonequilibrium steady states even in the presence of lattice rotation symmetry -- a mechanism that is qualitatively distinct from the well-known mechanism of anisotropic hopping. We exactly calculate the steady-state equal-time density-density correlations, which exhibit an algebraic decay, $C(\mathbf{r}) \sim |\mathbf{r}|^{-4}$ for $|\mathbf{r}| \gg 1$. This algebraic behavior results from the interplay between the off-diagonal components of the diffusion and mobility tensors, demonstrating that chiral transport alone can generate power-law correlations in isotropic driven systems. Remarkably, the structure factor in the zero-wavenumber limit and the amplitude of the power laws depend nontrivially on chirality. While increasing chirality initially suppresses large-scale density fluctuations, the fluctuations beyond a threshold odd-diffusion strength vary nonmonotonically with chirality and develop a cusp singularity.

Summary. This paper presents a method to implement Quantum Error Correction in superconducting transmon circuits using fixed, open-loop control pulses instead of traditional syndrome measurement and feedback. By compiling the recovery logic into a single waveform, the authors demonstrate protection against noise for a four-bit code ring, offering a scalable, hardware-efficient path toward fault-tolerant quantum computation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the overhead associated with traditional Quantum Error Correction (QEC) by developing a method to compile syndrome measurement and recovery into a fixed, open-loop control waveform.

Main result. A local QEC rule can be compiled into a fixed, open-loop control cycle for transmon circuits, successfully slowing logical coherence decay and stabilizing logical populations for a four-bit repetition code ring.

Method. The authors use spectral compilation, designing a composite waveform consisting of a swap operation and a conditional auxiliary transition to implement the recovery logic without real-time feedback.

Abstract

Quantum error correction commonly relies on syndrome measurement, decoding, and conditional feedback. We numerically show that the conditional spectrum of an interacting transmon circuit can compile a local recovery rule into a fixed open-loop control cycle. In a four-bit repetition-code ring, the resulting input-independent control slows the decay of logical coherence under Pauli-\(X\) noise and stabilizes logical-one population under data relaxation relative to uncorrected references. Its local, bounded-degree architecture provides a hardware-native framework for extending compiled recovery control to larger quantum networks.

Orbital-angular-momentum partition in hydrogen photoionization by a monochromatic vortex beam

Summary. This theoretical study analyzes the transfer of orbital angular momentum from a vortex laser beam to a hydrogen atom during photoionization. By developing a center-of-mass-resolved theory, the authors demonstrate that ignoring atomic recoil leads to incorrect predictions for the electron's OAM. They show that the OAM is largely transferred to the recoiling center-of-mass motion, making recoil a key element in understanding this quantum process.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous quantum mechanical treatment of light-matter interaction where recoil effects are non-trivial, which is highly relevant for understanding quantum control and momentum transfer in strong-field atomic physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how the orbital angular momentum (OAM) carried by a monochromatic vortex beam is transferred to the matter (hydrogen atom) during photoionization, specifically accounting for the crucial role of atomic recoil and translational motion.

Main result. The transfer of OAM is highly dependent on the initial state of the atomic center-of-mass (CM); in the small-transverse-retardation regime, OAM is predominantly transferred to the CM motion (the proton), not solely to the electron.

Method. The authors developed a center-of-mass-resolved quantum theory, analyzing the photoionization process using the full Hamiltonian that explicitly includes CM motion, and comparing results across different initial CM state approximations.

Model / system. The system is the photoionization of a hydrogen-like atom by a monochromatic Laguerre-Gaussian (vortex) beam. The model uses a Hamiltonian incorporating the free evolution of the atom's CM motion and the light-matter interaction in the velocity gauge.

Key observables. Photoelectron angular distribution, OAM content ($\langle L_{e,z} angle$, $\langle L_{p,z} angle$), and the correlation term between electron and proton angular momenta.

Important parameters / regimes. The degree of initial CM momentum definition (momentum eigenstate vs. spatially localized wavepacket) and the transverse retardation scale.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the strong-field approximation (SFA) and treats the light-ion interaction as negligible compared to the electron-light interaction.

Paper structure. The paper builds from the general Hamiltonian to specific theoretical treatments, contrasting idealized fixed-target models with the full CM-resolved theory to derive the correct OAM partitioning.

Abstract

Understanding how optical orbital angular momentum (OAM) is transferred to matter requires treating recoil and translational motion alongside the internal electronic dynamics. We develop a center-of-mass-resolved theory of one-photon ionization of hydrogen by a monochromatic Laguerre--Gaussian beam and show that the Bessel-vortex photoelectron predicted in fixed-target models is a preparation-dependent limit. For a sharply defined atomic center-of-mass momentum, the recoil records the photon-cone azimuth, and tracing over it generally destroys the coherence required for a pure electron vortex. In the small-transverse-retardation regime, the optical OAM is transferred predominantly to the center-of-mass motion and hence, in the laboratory frame, to the proton. Finite-retardation corrections redistribute angular momentum between center-of-mass and relative motion, while an additional correlation contribution to the electron and proton angular momenta can be tuned through the spatial uncertainty of the atomic center of mass. These results reveal atomic recoil as a key element of OAM transfer in photoionization.

Abstract

We provide an analytic realization of spontaneous parity and time-reversal breaking without thermal restoration in a local, unitary, ultraviolet-complete quantum field theory in $2+1$ dimensions with finitely many fields. The theory is defined by a relevant Yukawa deformation of a product of the critical biconical vector model and free massless Dirac fermions. This deformation induces a renormalization group flow whose infrared endpoint is a decoupled product of the critical vector model and the conformal Gross-Neveu-Yukawa model. At zero temperature, the vacuum preserves both symmetries, whereas upon heating, the theory undergoes an inverse thermal transition at a finite critical temperature. Above this temperature, a fermion mass, odd under parity and time reversal, is dynamically generated, and the broken phase persists to arbitrarily high temperatures.

Abstract

A multiset of pure quantum states is said to be k-learnable if there is a measurement strategy that always narrows an unknown sample drawn from the list down to one of at most k candidates. The parameter k interpolates between distinguishability and antidistinguishability, and provides a unified framework for partial state identification. We prove two universal, and optimal, Gram-matrix criteria for k-learnability: a Frobenius-norm sufficient condition and an entrywise-$\ell_1$ necessary condition. We apply them to derive explicit learnability and copy-complexity guarantees for several well-known sets of states including SIC-POVMs, mutually unbiased bases, and stabilizer states. We further apply our results to zero-error mutation detection problems such as anomaly detection and changepoint detection.
