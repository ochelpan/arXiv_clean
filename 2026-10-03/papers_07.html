Microscopic origin of the chiral pressure in a two-dimensional fluid

Authors: Francisco Vega Reyes
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.02003v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5 · non-equilibrium universality 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work derives the microscopic origin of the chiral pressure in a 2D chiral fluid, showing it arises from an orbital moment related to the particle's spin mismatch with the local flow. The chiral pressure is found to be non-zero even in a steady, homogeneous state, demonstrating a fundamental, non-dissipative mechanical response to chirality.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical fluid dynamics, the concept of persistent, non-equilibrium stress (chiral pressure) arising from intrinsic particle asymmetry is analogous to emergent topological or chiral responses studied in quantum many-body systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper determines the microscopic origin and mathematical expression for the antisymmetric contribution to the pressure, known as the chiral pressure ($p_R$), in a two-dimensional chiral fluid.

Main result. The chiral pressure is proportional to the orbital moment ($\ell_O$), which quantifies the mismatch between the mean spin ($\Omega$) and half the local vorticity ($\omega/2$). This pressure persists even in a homogeneous, steady state (QCS).

Method. The derivation uses a combination of hydrodynamic comparisons (cell-scale vs. particle-scale) and microscopic kinematics, analyzing the spin balance equation to derive the constitutive relations.

Model / system. The system is a two-dimensional fluid composed of rough, actively rotating particles (disks). The analysis treats the fluid dynamics using concepts from micropolar hydrodynamics.

Key observables. Chiral pressure ($p_R$), Orbital moment ($\ell_O$), Rotational viscosity ($\mu_R$), Quiescent Chiral State (QCS).

Important parameters / regimes. The chiral pressure and viscosity are computed to Gaussian order in gradients, and the system depends on particle properties like moment of inertia ($mI$) and collision parameters ($eta, \kappa$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes structural asymmetry causing parity breaking and is accurate to second order in gradients, neglecting higher-order translational-rotational correlations.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the hydrodynamic derivation of the orbital moment ($\ell_O$) at different scales. Figure 2 shows the microscopic derivation relating particle spin, flow, and peculiar velocity. Figure 3 details the dependence of rotational viscosity ($\mu_R$) on restitution and inertia.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the need for $p_R$ by analyzing the spin balance equation. It then derives $\ell_O$ using both coarse-graining and microscopic kinematics. Finally, it computes the constitutive relation for $p_R$ and $\mu_R$, showing their persistence in the QCS.

Abstract

We show that a two-dimensional chiral fluid supports an antisymmetric contribution to the pressure, and determine its microscopic expression. Its origin lies outside the standard kinetic and collisional channels of the normal pressure tensor, stemming instead from the average of a parity-breaking pseudo-scalar. This chiral pressure is set by the rotational (vortex) viscosity of micropolar hydrodynamics, whose kinetic value for rough disks we compute here, to Gaussian order. Crucially, both the chiral pressure and the rotational viscosity survive at zeroth order in the gradients, meaning they persist even in the fully homogeneous fluid -- which we show is not only well defined but steady (the Quiescent Chiral State, QCS). Since this averaged pseudo-scalar does not vanish in a chiral fluid, any expansion of its distribution function must contain terms in this pseudo-scalar variable.

Optimal sampling strategies in event-chain Monte Carlo

Authors: James Gulliford, Gareth O. Roberts, Michael F. Faulkner
Type: theory · Category: numerical methods · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.01659v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Frenkel-Kontorova 1/5 · methods for driven-dissipative 1/5

Summary. This paper presents a significant advancement in computational sampling by proposing the 'Cell-horizon ECMC' method for simulating interacting particles on a 1D torus. It rigorously compares this new strategy against existing methods, demonstrating superior computational efficiency and robustness across various particle densities. The work provides a powerful, general framework for simulating complex statistical mechanical systems.

Why it may be interesting. The development of highly efficient, general sampling algorithms like this is crucial for simulating complex many-body systems in statistical mechanics, providing computational tools applicable to quantum many-body physics simulations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to develop and compare optimal sampling strategies for Event-Chain Monte Carlo (ECMC) methods to surpass the computational efficiency of established algorithms like Molecular Dynamics (MD) and Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC).

Main result. The authors introduce a universal framework, the 'Cell-horizon ECMC,' which achieves superior sampling efficiency, showing a better scaling and a higher mean event rate compared to non-factorised or standard-factorised ECMC methods.

Method. The work involves deriving and comparing the mean event rates ($\Lambda$) and autocorrelation times ($ au_{mix}$) for various ECMC formulations, including non-factorised and cell-horizon approaches, using mathematical analysis of continuous-time Markov processes.

Model / system. The primary model is $N$ pairwise-interacting particles confined to a 1D torus, governed by a potential $U$. Examples include the generalized harmonic chain (GHC) and the 1D soft-disk model (SD).

Key observables. Sampling efficiency, mean event rate ($\Lambda$), autocorrelation time ($ au_{mix}$), and the normalized pressure ($eta P$).

Important parameters / regimes. Number of particles ($N$), inverse temperature ($eta$), and the density ($ ho$) of the system.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes translational symmetry for the pairwise potentials and relies on approximations when deriving the optimal cell-horizon rate.

Figures summary. Figure 8 compares the mixing times ($ au_{mix}$) for standard-factorised and optimal cell-horizon ECMC, showing $O(N^{5/2})$ vs $O(N^2)$ scaling for the GHC. Figure 7 confirms thermodynamic consistency by equating the normalized pressure ($eta P$) with the ratio of index-space velocities ($v_{idx}/v_0$).

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first detailing existing ECMC strategies (non-factorised, standard-factorised), then introducing the novel cell-horizon framework, mathematically deriving its optimal rate, and finally comparing its performance and thermodynamic consistency against established methods.

Abstract

Event-chain Monte Carlo (ECMC) has revolutionised computational sampling over recent years, providing a powerful alternative to the molecular-dynamics (MD) and Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC) algorithms. Each method outperforms the ubiquitous random-walk Metropolis algorithm by advancing particles along deterministic trajectories, but ECMC achieves this without being constrained by Newtonian dynamics. Recent advances exploited this dynamical freedom to induce a collective particle dynamics that relax local density variations on fast timescales. In a foundational model of $N$ pairwise-interacting particles on the 1D torus, this led to an $O(N^{3/4})$ improvement on the industry-leading computational efficiency of MD and HMC - but with an impractically small prefactor at low/high mean particle density for general attractive/repulsive interactions. Here we present a universal framework that generalises this high-efficiency sampling strategy to all translationally symmetric pairwise models on the torus - creating the potential to surpass MD and HMC as the state of the art. We also numerically elucidate the collective particle dynamics and discuss broad impact across the physical sciences and computational statistics.

Robust exponential lower bounds for fermionic and bosonic Gaussian ranks

Authors: Fuchuan Wei, Kong-Wing Wu, Zhengwei Liu, Zi-Wen Liu
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.02172v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5 · analog quantum simulation 1/5

Summary. This paper derives rigorous, exponential lower bounds for the Gaussian rank of tensor powers of non-Gaussian fermionic and bosonic states. By showing that this rank grows exponentially, the authors establish fundamental limitations on the ability of classical simulation techniques to efficiently model these quantum systems. This work is crucial for understanding the computational power gap between quantum and classical computation.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the resource theory of quantum computation by quantifying the non-classical resources (non-Gaussianity) required for quantum advantage over classical simulation methods.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper establishes robust exponential lower bounds for the Gaussian rank of fermionic and bosonic quantum states, which quantifies the difficulty of classically simulating quantum systems.

Main result. It proves that for pure non-Gaussian states, the approximate Gaussian rank of their tensor powers grows at least exponentially with the number of copies, setting fundamental limits on decomposition-based classical simulation.

Method. The authors combine techniques including reduction to four modes with Majorana spectral bounds (fermions) and entropy-based rank bounds via Gaussian postselection (bosons).

Model / system. The study focuses on pure non-Gaussian states in both fermionic and bosonic systems, analyzing the tensor powers of states like the fermionic GHZ state and the bosonic single-photon state.

Key observables. Gaussian Rank ($\chi_G$), Border Gaussian Rank, Approximate Gaussian Rank ($\chi^{(\delta)}_G$), and the exponential growth rate of these ranks.

Important parameters / regimes. The approximation error $\delta$ must be less than 1, and the analysis depends on the number of tensor copies, $k$.

Assumptions / limitations. The results are proven for pure non-Gaussian states, and the techniques rely on the existence of specific Gaussian isometries and positive entropy gaps.

Figures summary. TABLE I provides finite-copy lower bounds on the border Gaussian rank for the four-mode fermionic GHZ state and the bosonic single-photon state for small numbers of copies.

Paper structure. The paper develops from defining the problem via Gaussian rank, establishing bounds for specific states (GHZ, single-photon), and culminating in general theorems proving exponential growth rates using entropy and isometry arguments.

Abstract

The power and limitations of classical simulation are central to understanding quantum computational advantages. A leading simulation paradigm is based on coherent decomposition into classically tractable free states where the decomposition rank determines the simulation complexity. Proving strong lower bounds on this number is a notoriously difficult and mathematically rich problem, as exemplified by the qubit stabilizer rank problem. Here, we study the Gaussian version of this problem in both bosonic and fermionic systems and establish robust exponential lower bounds on Gaussian rank. In particular, we prove that for every pure non-Gaussian state on finitely many modes, with definite parity in the fermionic case, the approximate border Gaussian rank of its tensor powers grows at least exponentially at any fixed norm error below one. Our proofs combine reduction to four modes with Majorana spectral bounds for fermions, and Gaussian postselection with an entropy-based rank bound for bosons. As concrete examples, we derive explicit exponential lower bounds for the four-mode fermionic GHZ state and the bosonic single-photon state. The bosonic results require no assumption on the energy of the target state. Our results show that non-Gaussianity universally entails exponential Gaussian decomposition complexity, setting fundamental limitations on decomposition-based classical simulation of bosonic and fermionic systems.

Scalar field theory for (chiral) active Brownian particles: bottom-up derivation revisited

Authors: Yuta Kuroda, Thomas Speck
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.01672v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5 · non-equilibrium universality 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper provides a rigorous theoretical framework to derive continuum scalar field theories for active matter systems, starting from the microscopic dynamics of Active Brownian Particles (ABPs). By employing the Renormalization Group method, the authors successfully connect particle interactions to macroscopic field equations, notably identifying unique chiral terms in the resulting theory.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical active matter, the rigorous application of the Renormalization Group method to derive effective, long-wavelength continuum theories from microscopic particle dynamics is a powerful theoretical tool applicable to non-equilibrium many-body systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is to establish a systematic, bottom-up derivation connecting microscopic particle-based models of active matter to continuum descriptions, specifically scalar field theories.

Main result. The authors successfully derived scalar field theories for both standard and chiral Active Brownian Particles (ABPs) using the Renormalization Group (RG) method. For the chiral variant, the resulting field theory contains two unique 'odd' non-equilibrium terms.

Method. The derivation employs a combination of the pressure expansion, the Renormalization Group (RG) method within singular perturbation theory, and closure approximations to systematically eliminate fast variables and derive flow equations for the slow dynamics.

Model / system. The study focuses on Active Brownian Particles (ABPs) and their chiral variant (cABPs), which are widely used models for scalar active fluids exhibiting phenomena like Motility-Induced Phase Separation (MIPS).

Key observables. Density field $ ho(\mathbf{r}, t)$, phase separation coexistence regions, and the coefficients of the derived scalar field theory up to $\mathcal{O}( abla^4)$.

Important parameters / regimes. Self-propulsion speed ($v_0$), rotational diffusion coefficient ($ au_p$), and the strength of the chirality bias (torque).

Assumptions / limitations. The derivation relies on the pressure expansion and the RG method, while the closure approximation used for the pair distribution function is noted as the only non-systematic step.

Figures summary. The figures schematically illustrate the dynamics for ABP and cABP, and one figure shows the MIPS coexistence region, analogous to equilibrium liquid-vapor separation.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first identifying the challenge of connecting microscopic ABPs to continuum field theories. It then details the methodology—using pressure expansion and RG—to derive the field theory, applying it first to ABPs, and finally extending the method to the chiral ABP (cABP) model.

Abstract

The active Brownian particle (ABP) model is one of the most widely used particle-based models in studies of scalar active fluids. A collection of interacting ABPs is known to exhibit intriguing phenomena that are absent in equilibrium counterparts. A prominent example is spontaneous phase separation, known as motility-induced phase separation (MIPS), which occurs even in the absence of explicit attractive interactions. In parallel, the large-scale behavior of scalar active fluids is often modeled using continuum descriptions, namely, scalar field theories. Such scalar active field theories are usually formulated in a top-down manner, and establishing their connections to particle-based models remains challenging, with these connections not yet fully understood. To advance the microscopic derivation of scalar active field theories, we revisit the connection between ABPs interacting via a two-body potential in two dimensions and scalar field theories that include all possible terms up to fourth order in spatial gradients. Our approach is based on a pressure expansion and the renormalization group (RG) method in the context of singular perturbation theory. The RG method provides a systematic way to eliminate fast variables and identify the dynamics on the slow invariant manifold. Within this framework, scalar field theories can be obtained as RG flow equations. We also apply this method to a chiral variant of the ABP model, the chiral ABP (cABP) model, in which a constant torque biases particle rotation to the left or right. We show that the scalar field theory corresponding to the cABP model contains two ``odd'' terms in addition to those present in the scalar field theory for ABPs. The method developed here may also be useful for deriving hydrodynamic descriptions of other types of active matter systems.

Why polar excitons stay sharp: parity protection of the center-of-mass recoil channel in exciton-phonon scattering

Authors: Michael O. Atambo
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.01600v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5 · correlated / nonlocal dissipation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical work explains the anomalous sharpness of excitons in polar semiconductors by rigorously including the center-of-mass recoil degree of freedom. It demonstrates that parity protection causes the primary recoil scattering channel to vanish in mass-symmetric materials. Consequently, the observed linewidths must instead be governed by internal-state-changing inelastic channels, establishing a clear hierarchy of controlling physical parameters.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a detailed, quantitative understanding of how fundamental symmetries (parity) and kinematic constraints (COM recoil) dictate the coherence properties of excitons in solid-state materials, which is crucial for designing stable quantum emitters.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the theoretical puzzle of why excitonic resonances in polar semiconductors, despite strong electron-phonon coupling, remain anomalously sharp.

Main result. The sharpness is governed by a hierarchy of scattering channels, where the internal-state-preserving center-of-mass recoil channel is suppressed by parity protection in mass-symmetric materials, forcing the linewidth to be determined by inelastic transitions.

Method. The authors use a theoretical framework based on the Fröhlich interaction, incorporating the exact center-of-mass (COM) recoil degree of freedom into the exciton-phonon scattering rate calculations.

Model / system. The system is excitons coupled to polar optical phonons in polar semiconductors, modeled using the hydrogenic Fröhlich Hamiltonian. The analysis distinguishes between mass-asymmetric and mass-symmetric materials.

Key observables. Recoil linewidth ($\gamma_{ ext{recoil}}^{ ext{LO}}$), Huang-Rhys factor ratio ($S_X/S_{ ext{ind}}$), and the overall exciton linewidth ($\gamma_{ ext{exp}}^{ ext{LO}}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Mass asymmetry parameter ($\eta$), recoil momentum ($q_*$), and the Rydberg detuning ($\Delta E_{np} - \hbar\omega_{ ext{LO}}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on extending standard treatments by including the full COM recoil kinematics and assuming the validity of the hydrogenic envelope functions for the exciton.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the form factors for internal-state-preserving and changing channels, showing the vanishing vertex due to parity protection, and present the universal suppression law for the Huang-Rhys factor ratio.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first identifying the failure of standard treatments, then introducing the COM recoil to derive a parameter-free $1s o 1s$ channel, proving its suppression via parity rules for $\eta=0$, and finally establishing the hierarchy of controlling parameters ($\eta, q^*a_X, \Delta E_{np} - \hbar\omega_{ ext{LO}}$) that dictate the observed linewidths.

Abstract

In polar semiconductors the Fröhlich interaction is the dominant electron--phonon coupling, yet excitonic resonances in materials such as halide perovskites remain anomalously sharp. We show that standard frozen-center-of-mass treatments of the exciton--phonon problem miss the decisive kinematic degree of freedom: restoring the exact center-of-mass (COM) recoil reveals a universally open, parameter-free $1s\to1s$ absorption channel at recoil momentum $q_*=\sqrt{2M_{\rm ex}\hbarω_{\rm LO}}/\hbar$, whose rate scales as $N_{\rm LO}(T)$. We prove that this recoil channel is controlled by destructive electron--hole interference: the recoil linewidth vanishes with the mass asymmetry as $γ_{\rm LO}^{\rm recoil}\propto\mathcal{F}_{1s,1s}(q_*)^2$, and the elastic dressing obeys the exact suppression law $S_X/S_{\rm ind}=η^2(6-η^2)/5$ within the hydrogenic Fröhlich model. The theory establishes a hierarchy of scattering regimes. In mass-asymmetric materials (GaAs, $η=-0.74$) the recoil channel is active ($γ_{\rm LO}^{\rm recoil}=2.2$~meV); in mass-symmetric materials (FAPbI$_3$, $η=0$; MAPbI$_3$, $η=-0.11$) it is killed by interference (0.00 and 0.12 meV), showing that the observed 27--40 meV perovskite linewidths cannot be accounted for by COM recoil and therefore require internal-state-changing and other inelastic channels, of which the constructive, $η$-robust $1s\to np$ resonance is the leading candidate within the present model. The Fröhlich constant $α$ alone is therefore insufficient as a figure of merit: after projection onto the correlated exciton, the controlling parameters are $η$, $q_*a_X$, and the Rydberg detuning.

A Code-Agnostic Graph Neural Network Decoder from the Detection Error Model

Authors: Federico Alberto Astolfi, Guido Pupillo
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.01683v1

Summary. This paper introduces POLYMECHANON, a Graph Neural Network decoder for quantum error correction that takes only the Detection Error Model (DEM) as input. It demonstrates that this single architecture can decode various stabilizer codes under different noise models, significantly outperforming existing methods in terms of error suppression and computational speed. This advances the field toward universal, hardware-independent quantum computation protocols.

Why it may be interesting. The focus on a code-agnostic decoder whose input is purely structural (the DEM) is highly relevant for building universal, hardware-agnostic quantum computation protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To develop a quantum error correction (QEC) decoder that is code-agnostic, meaning it can decode any stabilizer code under any noise model expressible as a Detection Error Model (DEM) without requiring redesign.

Main result. The proposed GNN decoder, POLYMECHANON, outperforms existing decoders on both surface and qLDPC codes, showing superior scaling and computational efficiency, and enabling confidence-based post-selection.

Method. The authors use a Graph Neural Network (GNN) decoder whose input is the tripartite graph representation of the Detection Error Model (DEM) of the quantum code and noise structure.

Model / system. The system involves quantum error correction using stabilizer codes, tested on the rotated surface code and high-rate qLDPC codes. The decoder operates on the DEM, which models detectors, error mechanisms, and logical observables.

Key observables. Logical Error Rate (LER), decoding time/cost, and the fraction of accepted shots after post-selection.

Important parameters / regimes. Physical error rate ($p$), code distance ($d$), and the confidence threshold ($ au$).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that the DEM alone contains sufficient information to characterize the decoding problem, allowing a single architecture to generalize across different codes and noise models.

Figures summary. Figures compare LER vs. physical error rate for different decoders (GNN vs. MWPM vs. BP+OSD) on surface and qLDPC codes, and show the effect of confidence-based post-selection on LER and shot acceptance.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the code-agnostic GNN decoder based on the DEM, validates its performance against state-of-the-art decoders on multiple code families and noise models, and details the benefits of probabilistic output for post-selection.

Abstract

We present POLYMECHANON, a graph neural network (GNN) decoder for quantum error correction whose only input is the detection error model (DEM) of a quantum code under a given noise model. We represent the DEM as a tripartite graph of detectors, error mechanisms and logical observables, where every input feature is computed by using the quantum code as data rather than design choice. In this way, the same architecture decodes in principle any stabiliser code, under any noise model that can be expressed as a detection error model, once trained on the DEM. We test this approach along four directions. Firstly, on the rotated surface code the decoder outperforms correlated MWPM under both phenomenological and circuit-level noise, with up to $25\%$ fewer logical failures. Secondly, on a family of high-rate qLDPC codes it matches BP+OSD on the smaller codes and surpasses it on the larger ones, with up to $16\%$ fewer logical failures on $[\![130,4,6]\!]$. Thirdly, its decoding time does not depend on the physical error rate, whereas that of BP+OSD grows with it: on $[\![130,4,6]\!]$ its effective cost per shot is of the order of a few ms on a single GPU against a few tens of milliseconds for BP+OSD on a single CPU core, and its fixed computational graph makes it a candidate for real-time decoding on neutral-atom processors. Finally, a single model trained across codes of different families outperforms uncorrelated MWPM on the graph-like codes seen during training and stays within $10\%$ of BP+OSD on the others, while it tends to fail to generalize to unseen codes, particularly larger ones. Its probabilistic output enables confidence-based post-selection, lowering the logical error rate by more than an order of magnitude on the surface code while keeping more than $90\%$ of the shots. Since only the DEM changes, new codes and noise models can be decoded without redesigning the decoder.

A No-Go Theorem for Order-Two Clifford Electric-Magnetic Duality

Authors: Shunta Takahashi, Zhi Li, Beni Yoshida
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.02097v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

Summary. This paper proves that the natural order-two electric-magnetic duality symmetry in the toric code cannot be realized by a simple, local Clifford unitary operation for the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ case. It generalizes this to $\mathbb{Z}_N$ codes, finding that the order-two realization fails for even $N$, demonstrating a deep link between the symmetry's algebraic structure and the underlying lattice topology.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the fundamental relationship between abstract topological symmetries (like e-m duality) and their physical realization within quantum error-correcting codes, which is central to quantum computation theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates whether the electric-magnetic (e-m) exchange duality, which has order two in the anyon types of the toric code, can be realized microscopically as an order-two Clifford unitary operation.

Main result. For the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ toric code, no locality-preserving Clifford realization of the e-m exchange can have order two. Furthermore, for the $\mathbb{Z}_N$ code, an order-two realization is only possible for odd $N$.

Method. The proof uses geometric arguments involving string crossings, relating the unitary operator's action on stabilizers to topological features analogous to a cross-cap.

Model / system. The study focuses on the $\mathbb{Z}_N$ toric code on a lattice, characterized by vertex and plaquette stabilizers ($A_v$ and $B_p$). The core object of study is the unitary operator $U_{em}$ implementing the e-m exchange.

Key observables. The order of the unitary operator $U_{em}$ (or its dressed version $W$) acting on the physical Hilbert space, and the commutation relations between local Pauli operators under this transformation.

Important parameters / regimes. The integer $N$ defining the $\mathbb{Z}_N$ code, and the lattice structure (e.g., $L_x \times L_y$ periodic boundary conditions).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary theorem assumes locality-preserving realizations of the e-m exchange. The general proof avoids assumptions about translation symmetry or fixed stabilizer pairings.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the anyon excitations, the conjugation rules for Pauli operators under $U_{em}$, and the string geometries used in the proof, relating the obstruction to topological crossings.

Paper structure. The paper first addresses the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ case, proving the order-two impossibility. It then generalizes to $\mathbb{Z}_N$, establishing the odd/even dependence. The methodology involves analyzing the action of the symmetry on stabilizers using Pauli symplectic formalism and geometric string arguments.

Abstract

Electromagnetic duality in the toric code has order two at the level of anyon types and acts as a Hadamard-type logical transformation on the encoded quantum information. Here, we ask whether it can likewise be realized microscopically as an order-two Clifford operation. For the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ toric code, we prove that any locality-preserving Clifford realization of electric-magnetic exchange cannot have order two. Our proof is fully general and requires neither translation symmetry with restricted families of system sizes nor a fixed pairing between vertex and plaquette stabilizers. Geometrically, the proof locally emulates the unavoidable crossing between electric and magnetic strings, similar to a cross-cap in a non-orientable manifold. Extending the problem to the $\mathbb{Z}_N$ toric code, we find that an order-two Clifford realization exists for odd $N$, whereas it is impossible for even $N$. Our results show that the group law of an emergent electromagnetic duality need not lift faithfully to its microscopic Clifford realization.

Summary. This paper introduces a Quantum Phase Neural Network (QPNN) designed to classify multi-input data, demonstrated using pixel recognition for letters 'A' and 'B'. It overcomes the 'exponential wall' by encoding inputs into quantum phases and deriving the output as a polynomial function of these phases. The methodology is implemented and verified using quantum circuits on the Qiskit platform.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a concrete, mathematically derived quantum circuit implementation for a machine learning task, offering a pathway to utilize phase encoding to tackle high-dimensional input data in quantum computation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of implementing multi-input recognition tasks using Quantum Phase Neural Networks (QPNNs) while circumventing the 'exponential wall' problem inherent in standard amplitude encoding.

Main result. A QPNN structure was successfully proposed and verified on the Qiskit platform to recognize letters 'A' and 'B' by encoding inputs into quantum phases, yielding polynomial output calculations.

Method. The method involves normalizing pixel inputs, encoding them into quantum phases, and then using a parameterized quantum circuit with controlled NOT gates to sum these phases before measuring the final output probability.

Model / system. The model is a Quantum Phase Neural Network (QPNN) designed for $m$-input/single-output tasks. It operates on quantum states initialized by the input data and manipulates phases using rotation and controlled gates.

Key observables. The primary observable is the real output $y \in [0, 1]$, which is derived from the final quantum phase measurement, and the training error function ($e_t^y$).

Important parameters / regimes. The number of inputs $m=35$ (from $7 imes 5$ pixels), and the adjustable weight phases ($ heta_j$) within the quantum gates.

Assumptions / limitations. The core assumption is that the output measurement can be effectively modeled by calculating a polynomial function of the quantum state phase, thereby avoiding the exponential complexity of amplitude measurement.

Figures summary. Figure 1 shows the general QPNN structure; Figure 2 illustrates the quantum circuit steps (initialization, rotation, controlled gates); Figure 3 shows the specific quantum circuit implemented on Qiskit.

Paper structure. The paper first proposes the QPNN structure for multi-input encoding. It then derives the mathematical relationship between the input, the adjustable phases, and the output $y$. Abstract

A 35 input/single output quantum phase neural network (QPNN) is proposed to recognize the English letters' A 'and' B 'composed 7*5 of pixels. Each node of the proposed QPNN has the input with 0 or 1. Through normalization and quantum phase encoding, each set of 35 with 0 or 1 digital inputs is encoded into a quantum superposition state described by 35 quantum phases. Then, a quantum rotation gate is used to introduce adjustable phase, and a controlled NOT gate is used to entangle the relative phase between the two inputs. The relationship between the input and output of the network is derived. This paper also derived the phase weight learning training algorithm with adaptive learning rate. The solutions with the recognition probability of 1 is designed, and the analytical expressions for all adjustable phase solutions with zero errors are not unique. The experimental performance is verified on the Qiskit platform. This paper uses phase drive to convert the probability calculation into an analytical polynomial function with adjustable rotation angles in the network, avoiding the bottleneck of generating all 2^35 complex exponential amplitudes and providing an effective way to solve the "exponential wall" problem, which provides a new implementation solution for the practical application of multi-input recognition problems.

Abstract

Abstract

Abstract

In this work, we study bipartiteness and expansion testing, two canonical problems in graph property testing in the bounded-degree model through the lens of quantum query complexity. In the classical setting, it is known that $\widetildeΘ(\sqrt{N})$ queries are necessary and sufficient for both these testing problems (Goldreich and Ron, 1999, 2000 & 2002), where $N$ denotes the number of vertices of the input graph. Due to their significance, (Ambainis, Childs, and Liu, 2011) initiated the study of these problems in the quantum setting and designed quantum algorithms for bipartiteness and expansion testing that perform $\widetilde{O}(N^{1/3})$ queries, showing a polynomial speedup. They also proved that $\widetildeΩ(N^{1/4})$ queries are necessary for expansion testing, but the possibility of an exponential quantum advantage for bipartiteness testing remained open. Despite significant effort, there has been no improvement in these results in the last decade and a half. In this work, we prove essentially tight $\widetildeΩ(N^{1/3})$ quantum query lower bounds for both bipartiteness and expansion testing, thereby completely characterizing the quantum query complexity of these problems up to polylogarithmic factors. While our proofs use the polynomial method similarly to Ambainis, Childs, and Liu, we use intermediate problems that we relate to the main problems via reductions, and perform a more precise analysis of the resulting polynomials, leading to the near-optimal lower bounds.
