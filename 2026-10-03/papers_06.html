Blind Unforgeability implies Plus-One Unforgeability

Authors: Mehil Agarwal, Calliope S. Reimann, Fang Song
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.00517v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5

Summary. This theoretical paper proves that in the quantum setting, the security notion of Blind Unforgeability is strictly stronger than Plus-One Unforgeability. The authors achieve this by developing a new smoothing technique that allows them to transform any successful PO attack into a BU attack with minimal loss. This settles an open question regarding the relative strength of these two quantum security definitions.

Why it may be interesting. While the topic is cryptography, the core mathematical tools—coherently reshaping quantum query histories and analyzing polynomial dependence on blinding parameters—are highly relevant to understanding robustness in quantum information protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper establishes the precise relationship between two security notions for quantum message authentication codes: Blind Unforgeability (BU) and Plus-One Unforgeability (PO).

Main result. It proves that quantum Blind Unforgeability implies quantum Plus-One Unforgeability (BU $\Rightarrow$ PO), demonstrating that BU is strictly stronger than PO in the quantum setting.

Method. The proof employs a novel smoothing technique that coherently rescales quantum query histories, transforming a successful PO attacker into a BU attacker with only inverse-polynomial loss.

Model / system. The analysis is set in the context of quantum-accessible keyed function families $F_k: X o Y$, modeling quantum message authentication codes (MACs) accessed via quantum oracles.

Key observables. The success probability of the adversary ($\delta$), the zero-touch forgery mass ($\Xi(A)$), and the singleton-puncture density ($ ho_F(A)$).

Important parameters / regimes. The number of queries ($q$), the blinding probability ($eta$), and the security parameter ($\lambda$).

Assumptions / limitations. The proof relies on a conceptual shift from simple history extraction to coherently reshaping quantum interference patterns, necessitating advanced smoothing techniques.

Figures summary. References are made to figures illustrating error propagation and the structure of the proof, though specific content is not detailed.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the core implication (BU $\Rightarrow$ PO) using smoothing, then exploring related equivalences (BU $\iff \partial$BU), and finally detailing the mathematical machinery (polynomial analysis, derivatives at $eta=0$) required to quantify the security gap.

Abstract

We prove that quantum blind-unforgeability implies quantum plus-one unforgeability, settling an open question in the literature. Since plus-one unforgeability is known not to imply blind-unforgeability, our result establishes that blind-unforgeability is \emph{strictly stronger} than plus-one unforgeability in the quantum setting. The implication is proven using a new \emph{smoothing} technique that coherently rescales the query histories of a quantum-query algorithm in a black-box manner. This enables us to transform any successful plus-one attacker into a blind-unforgeability attacker with only inverse-polynomial loss. Our proof represents a conceptual shift from merely extracting a useful query history as in earlier attempts, which we show inevitably fails in general, to first coherently reshaping how query histories interfere.

Abstract

We report the synthesis and comprehensive investigation of single-crystalline Tb$_2$IrAl$_4$Ge$_2$ and Er$_2$IrAl$_4$Ge$_2$, the first Ir-based members of the $Ln_2M$Al$_4$Ge$_2$ family. Both compounds crystallize in the tetragonal Tb$_2$NiAl$_4$Ge$_2$-type structure (space group $I4/mmm$) and exhibit easy-axis magnetic anisotropy with the crystallographic $c$ axis as the preferred magnetization direction. Tb$_2$IrAl$_4$Ge$_2$ undergoes antiferromagnetic ordering below $T_N = 25$ K and develops a sequence of field-induced metamagnetic transitions accompanied by pronounced magnetotransport anomalies, including a nonlinear Hall response. In contrast, Er$_2$IrAl$_4$Ge$_2$ exhibits three successive zero-field magnetic transitions and a substantially richer low-temperature magnetic phase diagram comprising multiple competing field-induced phases confined to relatively modest magnetic fields. The differences between the Tb- and Er-based compounds demonstrate the wide range of magnetic behavior that can be realized within the \ce{Tb2NiAl4Ge2}-type structure. More broadly, the successful realization of Ir-based $Ln_2M$Al$_4$Ge$_2$ compounds considerably expands the accessible chemical space of this family and opens a route toward the exploration of related $4d$- and $5d$-based aluminogermanides.

Efficient certification of time-reversal symmetry requires entanglement

Summary. This paper develops a quantum information-theoretic test to certify time-reversal symmetry (TRS) in unknown quantum dynamics. It proves that the efficiency of this test is fundamentally limited by the entanglement resources available in the quantum probes and measurements. By establishing a quantitative connection, it bridges fundamental physics principles with the resource theory of quantum information.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous, resource-theoretic understanding of fundamental physical symmetries (like TRS) within the framework of quantum information theory, connecting concepts like entanglement to physical laws.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the fundamental question of how efficiently one can certify the presence of time-reversal symmetry (TRS) in an unknown quantum unitary evolution using only forward quantum queries.

Main result. It establishes a quantitative lower bound on the query complexity required for this certification, showing that entanglement resources dictate the scaling, and demonstrates protocols that achieve this optimal scaling.

Method. The authors formulate a Bell-inequality-like test for dynamical TRS, analyzing the required number of queries by relating it to the logarithmic negativities of the quantum probes and measurements.

Model / system. The system involves an unknown $n$-qubit unitary channel $U(t) = e^{-iHt}$ whose time-reversal symmetry is tested by comparing its dynamics against known time-reversal symmetric ensembles.

Key observables. Logarithmic negativity ($E_N$) of the probe and measurement, and the query complexity $T$ needed to distinguish TRS from Haar-random dynamics.

Important parameters / regimes. The query complexity scales as $\Theta(2n - e^*)$, where $e^*$ is the minimum logarithmic negativity of the probe or measurement.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes only forward access to the unitary channel and relies on the existence of specific entanglement resources (probes and measurements) to achieve the bounds.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the protocol structure, showing how entanglement translates the temporal input-output relation into a measurable spatial exchange symmetry constraint.

Paper structure. The paper develops the test formulation, derives the lower bounds based on entanglement measures, and then presents protocols that match these bounds, including specialized versions for fixed resources and those without auxiliary systems.

Abstract

Time-reversal symmetry is a fundamental principle of physics describing the invariance of physical laws under reversal of the direction of time. We formulate a Bell-inequality-like test of this antiunitary symmetry using only forward access and trusted quantum operations: entanglement converts temporal input--output relations into measurable spatial exchange symmetry. For $n$-qubit unitary dynamics, we prove that reliably distinguishing the time-reversal-symmetric circular ensembles from Haar-random dynamics requires $Ω(\min\{2^{n/2},2^{n-e}\})$ queries for any classically adaptive protocol. Here, $e=\min\{e_{\mathrm s},e_{\mathrm m}\}$ with $e_{\mathrm s}$ and $e_{\mathrm m}$ representing the probe and measurement logarithmic entanglement negativities, respectively. Maximally entangled probes and SWAP measurements reduce this cost to a constant number of queries. Furthermore, we develop a time-reversal symmetry test for arbitrary fixed, compatible probes and measurements, relate its query complexity to their logarithmic negativities, and match the lower-bound scaling in the high-entanglement regime by optimizing the probe and measurement. Our results establish a quantitative connection between entanglement and time-reversal symmetry, bridging two central concepts in quantum information science and fundamental physics.

Summary. The researchers used VUV transient absorption on aligned $ ext{CO}_2$ to study vibronic states split by the Renner-Teller effect. By analyzing molecular-frame angular signatures, they separated states based on their electronic symmetry ($A'$ vs. $A''$). They found that the coherence lifetime differs between these symmetry classes, providing direct evidence that the excited-state potential energy surface topology dictates the rate of wavepacket dephasing.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a direct, ultrafast spectroscopic method to map out the symmetry-resolved dynamics on coupled excited-state potential energy surfaces, which is crucial for understanding non-adiabatic processes in molecular physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To resolve and characterize the symmetry-dependent ultrafast dynamics of vibronic states in molecules where electronic states are mixed due to non-adiabatic coupling.

Main result. The parallel ($A'$) vibronic states dephase measurably faster than the perpendicular ($A''$) states, which is attributed to the distinct, anharmonic topology of the $A'$ excited-state potential energy surface.

Method. Vacuum-ultraviolet (VUV) transient absorption spectroscopy of aligned $ ext{CO}_2$ molecules, combined with molecular-frame angular signature analysis.

Model / system. The system is $ ext{CO}_2$ undergoing Renner-Teller splitting, leading to vibronic states ($A'$ and $A''$). Dynamics are probed by measuring coherence dephasing times ($ au_d$) across the vibronic manifold.

Key observables. Molecular-frame anisotropy ($c_2/c_0$), differential absorption spectrum ($\Delta ext{OD}$), and symmetry-dependent coherence dephasing times ($ au_d$).

Important parameters / regimes. Femtosecond delay ($ au$) between VUV and NIR pulses; the energy difference between $A'$ and $A''$ states.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on reconstructing molecular-frame information from lab-frame measurements and modeling the coherence decay using an Exponentially Modified Gaussian (EMG) distribution.

Figures summary. Figures show $\Delta ext{OD}$ vs. delay $ au$ revealing rotational dynamics, anisotropy plots confirming parallel/perpendicular character, and $ au_d$ vs. energy plots demonstrating the symmetry-dependent decay rates.

Paper structure. The paper systematically uses alignment-resolved VUV transient absorption to first map the symmetry landscape via anisotropy, and then uses time-resolved measurements to extract symmetry-specific coherence dephasing times, linking these lifetimes to the underlying potential energy surface topology.

Abstract

When non-adiabatic coupling mixes electronic states of a molecule, vibronic states of different electronic symmetry become interleaved across the same spectral region. The symmetry of these states cannot be accessed in conventional spectroscopy due to orientation averaging. We show that molecular-frame angular signatures, which are preserved even when states with different symmetry overlap in energy, can be used to resolve them. Using vacuum-ultraviolet transient absorption spectroscopy of aligned carbon dioxide molecules, we separate two electronically distinct classes of vibronic states ($A^{'}$ and $A^{''}$) that arise from Renner-Teller splitting of the $^1Δ_u$ state. We reconstruct molecular-frame anisotropy continuously across the vibronic manifold showing that the anisotropy crosses over from perpendicular to parallel transition character and maps the interleaved electronic symmetry landscape. By scanning the femtosecond delay between the VUV and a near-infrared pulse through temporal overlap, we measure symmetry-dependent coherence dephasing times of these states and find that the parallel ($A^{'}$) states dephase faster than the perpendicular ($A^{''}$) states. We attribute this to faster departure of the $A^{'}$ wavepacket from the Franck-Condon region due to the distinct topology of the $A^{'}$ excited-state surface. These measurements probe symmetry-resolved ultrafast wavepacket dynamics on the coupled excited-state potential energy surfaces.

Geometry of Knill-Laflamme Coefficients for Pauli Error Detection

Authors: Baisong Sun, Ningping Cao, Yiu Tung Poon, Bei Zeng

Summary. This work develops a structural framework for analyzing the geometry of Knill-Laflamme coefficients in quantum error detection. It establishes that the geometric properties of the achievable coefficient range—such as convexity and interval structure—are directly controlled by the representation multiplicity of the underlying Pauli observables. This provides sharp criteria for understanding when error detection is robust and well-behaved.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a rigorous mathematical framework for understanding the geometric constraints imposed by physical detectability requirements in quantum error correction, which is crucial for designing practical quantum hardware.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper characterizes the geometry of Knill-Laflamme coefficients, which are used to quantify the feasibility of exact quantum error detection using Pauli observables.

Main result. The attainability of coefficients is governed by the representation multiplicity; sufficient multiplicity ensures the coefficient range is convex and connected, leading to a simple interval for the signature spectrum.

Method. The analysis uses concepts from convex geometry, specifically studying the joint higher-rank numerical range ($\Lambda_K(A)$) and its Euclidean norm image (the signature spectrum $\Sigma_K(A)$).

Model / system. The model is quantum error correction applied to $n$-qubit systems, utilizing Pauli observables ($X, Y, Z$) and associated code spaces defined by projectors ($P$).

Key observables. Joint higher-rank numerical range ($\Lambda_K(A)$) and the Signature Spectrum ($\Sigma_K(A)$).

Important parameters / regimes. Code dimension ($K$), representation multiplicity, and the structure of the error constraints (e.g., commuting vs. noncommuting Pauli families).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis often assumes sufficient multiplicity to realize all active states, which leads to the desirable convex/interval structure.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate how increasing multiplicity can expand the attainable range from a simple shape (like a Bloch sphere) to a filled ball, confirming the connection between multiplicity and geometric structure.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theory by first defining the geometric constraints, then proving the main theorem relating multiplicity to convexity, and finally examining special cases like commuting Pauli families and subsystem stabilizer codes.

Abstract

Knill-Laflamme coefficients characterize exact quantum error detection through scalar compressions of error operators to the code space. For prescribed Pauli observables and a fixed code dimension, attainable coefficient vectors form a joint higher-rank numerical range, whose Euclidean norm image is the signature spectrum. We study how operator representation, code dimension, and detection constraints govern this geometry. Our main theorem identifies representation multiplicity as sufficient to lift active expectation data to exact scalar compressions: a lift exists whenever each occupied block has multiplicity at least the code dimension times the rank of the corresponding active state. When multiplicity suffices to realize all active states, the coefficient range is the full active expectation body and is therefore convex and connected, with a closed interval as its signature spectrum. This framework unifies commuting Pauli families, where sufficient common-eigenspace degeneracy yields polytopes, and subsystem stabilizer codes, where protected logical subsystems supply multiplicity spaces for noncommuting gauge observables. Explicit examples show how increasing the code dimension can make a range shrink, collapse, or become empty, while increasing multiplicity can turn a Bloch sphere into a filled ball. In the subsystem setting, parent-Hamiltonian constructions realize exact detecting codes as degenerate ground spaces, selecting distinguished vectors and continuous paths within these ranges. A final three-qubit example shows additional noncommuting detection constraints reduce a tetrahedral range to its barycenter and four vertices, producing a disconnected signature spectrum. The results provide a structural framework for Knill-Laflamme coefficient geometry and motivate sharper criteria for connected coefficient ranges and interval signature spectra under structured error models.

Abstract

The layered honeycomb $α$-RuCl$_3$ has been much explored as a candidate to display unconventional cooperative magnetism between spin-orbit entangled $j_{\rm eff}=1/2$ Ru$^{3+}$ magnetic moments stabilized by strongly-frustrated, Kitaev and other bond-dependent anisotropic interactions. Most studies so far have focused on relatively large single crystals that undergo a structural phase transition from monoclinic to rhombohedral upon cooling below 150 K and a magnetic transition to zigzag order around 7 K. Here we study the magnetism of high-quality, very small (sub 100 $μ$m diameter) single crystals, which remain monoclinic upon cooling to low temperatures and display a sharp magnetic transition at 14 K. Using highly-sensitive piezo-cantilever magnetic torque measurements in fields up to 16 T, we report a comprehensive study of the magnetic phase diagrams for magnetic field rotated in three orthogonal crystallographic planes. Our extensive torque data collected upon varying almost continuously the angular orientation of the field as well as the field magnitude, allows clear detection of phase transitions via anomalies in the raw torque data and its higher order derivatives with respect to both angle and field magnitude. The obtained magnetic phase diagrams show many differences compared to rhombohedral samples, displaying reduced rotational symmetry, substantially higher fields required to suppress the spontaneous magnetic order, and distinct field-induced phases. We provide a direct comparison of the torque data with mean-field calculations for magnetic Hamiltonians appropriate for $α$-RuCl$_3$ allowing for the monoclinic lattice symmetry and propose minimal magnetic structure models for all explored regions of the magnetic phase diagrams.

One-Shot any Code

Summary. This paper develops a method to upgrade any quantum error-correcting code to support single-shot (SS) syndrome measurements, a significant advance over traditional time-consuming syndrome extraction. By constructing an output code $ ext{[[nm, k]]}$ from an input code $ ext{[[n, k]]}$ using an efficient RG decoder, the authors demonstrate robust error suppression over many correction rounds. This breakthrough addresses the temporal bottleneck in fault-tolerant quantum computation.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, general framework for achieving fault-tolerant quantum computation by upgrading existing quantum codes to support single-shot syndrome measurements, which is crucial for scaling quantum memory and computation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The goal is to equip an arbitrary Quantum Low-Density Parity-Check (QLDPC) code with single-shot (SS) quantum error correction while maintaining bounded check weight and qubit degree.

Main result. The authors show that any single-shot stabilizer code is self-correcting and provide a method to transform any input code into an output code with SS QEC, achieving error suppression $\Omega(m^\alpha n^eta)$.

Method. The core method involves constructing an output code $ ext{[[nm, k]]}$ from an input $ ext{[[n, k]]}$ code using a renormalization-group (RG) decoder that runs efficiently in $O(\log m)$ parallel time.

Model / system. The work analyzes Quantum Error Correction (QEC) using CSS QLDPC codes. The focus is on moving from conventional syndrome extraction (which requires multiple time steps) to single-shot syndrome measurements, controlling both physical and measurement noise.

Key observables. Logical failure probability over $T$ rounds, error suppression $\Omega(m^\alpha)$, and the threshold $p_{ ext{RG}}$.

Important parameters / regimes. The noise threshold $p_{ ext{RG}} > 0$, the error suppression exponent $\alpha > 0$, and the input/output code parameters $(n, k)$ and $(nm, k)$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes local stochastic (LS) rates for physical errors and measurement errors, and the construction does not require the code to have a three-dimensional geometric realization.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the self-correcting nature of single-shot codes, proves the general transformation from any QLDPC code to an SS-correcting code, and analyzes the resulting error suppression bounds over multiple correction rounds.

Abstract

We first provide a simple proof showing that any single-shot code is self-correcting. We then show that any $[[n,k]]$ CSS quantum low-density parity-check (QLDPC) code can be transformed into an $[[nm,k]]$ CSS QLDPC code with single-shot (SS) quantum error correction and an efficient local decoder which runs in $O(\log m)$ parallel time. Below a constant threshold $p_{\rm RG}>0$ for joint local stochastic physical and measurement noise, the logical failure probability over $T$ correction rounds is bounded by \begin{equation} O(T n)\exp[-Ω(m^α)] \end{equation} for a constant $α>0$; we say that the \textit{threshold} is $p_{\rm RG}$ and \textit{error suppression} is $Ω(m^α)$. Moreover, if the input code is equipped with a single-shot decoder with threshold $p_c>0$ and error suppression $Ω(n^β)$, then the output code \textit{enhances} the error suppression to $Ω(m^α n^β)$, while maintaining the decoder-independent threshold $p_{\rm RG}$, and thus \textit{enhanced} if $p_c < p_{\rm RG}$.

Quantum Advantage for Two-Party Differential Privacy

Authors: Daniel Alabi, Emil T. Khabiboulline
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.02113v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5

Summary. This work develops quantum protocols to compute the Hamming distance privately between two parties, achieving an $O(1)$ error rate under information-theoretic privacy guarantees. This significantly surpasses the classical lower bound of $\Omega(\sqrt{n})$ error required by classical protocols. The advantage stems from leveraging quantum mechanics in a specific honest-but-curious communication model.

Why it may be interesting. The core concept of using quantum information theory (like non-orthogonality) to break classical information-theoretic lower bounds in privacy-preserving computation is highly relevant to quantum cryptography and secure quantum computation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses achieving information-theoretically private quantum protocols for computing the two-party Hamming distance when both parties must output the same estimate.

Main result. The authors demonstrate that quantum communication can achieve an $O(1)$ information-theoretic error rate, matching the accuracy available classically only under computational assumptions, by exploiting quantum principles in the Klauck's honest, nonpreemptive model.

Method. The construction utilizes a 'guarded coherent round trip' and relies on the 'equal-Gram rigidity principle' to ensure privacy by preventing the observer from retaining input-dependent complementary information.

Model / system. The analysis is set within quantum communication protocols, comparing different security models: Prescribed-channel (PC), Klauck's honest nonpreemptive view (KHNP), and Retention-robust (RR).

Key observables. Expected error in estimating the Hamming distance, Quantum Differential Privacy (QDP) parameters ($\epsilon, \delta$), and communication complexity ($O(n)$ qubits/bits).

Important parameters / regimes. Input length $n$, privacy parameters $\epsilon$ and $\delta$, and the separation between classical $\Omega(\sqrt{n})$ and quantum $O(1)$ error bounds.

Assumptions / limitations. The quantum advantage is specific to the KHNP model and relies on the non-copyability of non-orthogonal quantum messages; it is not guaranteed against fully malicious retention-robust attacks.

Figures summary. Figure 1 compares classical DP protocols showing the $\Omega(\sqrt{n})$ gap; Figure 2 illustrates the quantum advantage in the KHNP model using non-orthogonal messages.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the problem gap classically, then introduces the quantum protocol using the guarded coherent round trip and rigidity principle. It details results for pure QDP and approximate QDP, concluding with a comparison to the fully retention-robust baseline.

Abstract

We introduce information-theoretically private quantum protocols for two-party Hamming distance when both parties must output the same estimate. Classically, for input length $n$, information-theoretic protocols require $Ω(\sqrt{n})$ error under pure differential privacy and $Ω(\sqrt{n}/\log n)$ error under strong approximate differential privacy, whereas computational security permits $O(1)$ error. In Klauck's honest, nonpreemptive, message-preserving model, we give an $O(n)$-communication quantum protocol with pure $\varepsilon$ quantum differential privacy (QDP) and expected error at most $\frac{2}{\sinh \varepsilon}+γ$, for every $γ>0$. For approximate $(\varepsilon, δ)$ QDP, an exact hockey-stick divergence calculation yields strictly smaller error, while preserving the $O(1)$-versus-$Ω(\sqrt{n}/\log n)$ separation for $δ=o(1/n)$. Thus, quantum communication achieves $O(1)$ information-theoretic error, matching the accuracy available classically only under computational assumptions. The main construction uses a guarded coherent round trip and an equal-Gram rigidity principle that prevents an honest player from retaining input-dependent complementary information. We separate this model from weaker prescribed-channel privacy, which already admits an exact classical realization, and from fully retention-robust security, against which measurement-and-abort attacks remain possible. Therefore, we identify preservation of non-orthogonal quantum messages as a resource for privacy.

Abstract

A recurring principle in many-body physics is that sufficiently distributed interactions or correlations lead to an effectively mean-field description. For fermionic Gaussian states, we establish a quantitative version of this principle directly at the level of Majorana covariance matrices. The main observation is that the admissibility condition for fermionic covariance matrices imposes a quantitative monogamy constraint on two-point correlations: a fixed region has only a bounded covariance budget to distribute among many disjoint regions. We use this principle in three directions. First, for quadratic fermionic Hamiltonians on finite simple graphs of high degree, we obtain product-state approximations to both the ground-state energy and the finite-temperature free energy, with error of order \(D^{-1/2}\) for \(D\)-regular graphs. Second, we prove a finite fermionic Gaussian de Finetti theorem: a \(k\)-copy Gaussian state that is \(n\)-exchangeable under graded fermionic copy permutations is \(O(k/n)\)-close in trace norm to the product of its one-copy marginal. In particular, infinite exchangeability within the Gaussian class implies exact product structure. Third, for spatial fermionic Gaussian states with exponentially clustering mutual information, we prove that the conditional mutual information across a separating buffer decays as \(O(r^{-1})\), and hence, by recoverability, the state admits an \(O(r^{-1/2})\) trace-norm approximation by a recovered state. These results show that product and de Finetti approximations and recoverability estimates for fermionic Gaussian systems can all be derived directly from covariance-level structure.

Abstract

At high temperature instantons form a dilute gas, so in QCD-like theories the breaking of the anomalous $U(1)_A$ symmetry is given by integral powers of the anomalous determinant, $\sim (\det Φ)^{Q}$, where $Φ\sim \overline{q}_L q_R$ is bilinear in the quark fields, and with untwisted boundary conditions, the topological charge, $Q$, is an integer. A syncretic model is constructed, which is manifestly "beyond Landau". In the chiral limit, at temperatures above the chiral phase transition, $T > T_χ$, only integral powers of the anomalous determinant appear. Below $T_χ$, following 't Hooft et al. I assume that the topological charge $Q$ is fractional, as an integer times $1/N_c$, where $N_c$ is the number of colors. I suggest that consequently, fractional powers of the anomalous determinant appear in the chiral effective Lagrangian. For $N_f$ degenerate flavors, this generalizes the Witten-Veneziano term, valid for small $N_f/N_c$, to arbitrary $N_f/N_c$. In this model the chiral phase transition is generically of second order. The two exceptions are for one flavor, where it is probably crossover, and three flavors, where it could well be weakly first order. This can be tested in lattice QCD with $2+1$ flavors by comparing the (known) temperature dependence of the difference of the $π^a$ and $a_0^a$ propagators, to the chiral condensate of the strange quark, between $T_χ$ and $\sim 2 \, T_χ$. Analogous measurements are possible for one to four degenerate flavors about $T_χ$. Lastly, I propose an operator for baryon number in the symmetric phase.

Abstract
