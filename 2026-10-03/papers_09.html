Exact $T$-counts of Toffoli layers from an isotropy bound

Authors: Arul Rhik Mazumder
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.01024v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper provides a rigorous proof establishing the exact minimum T-gate count required for implementing layers of CCZ gates in fault-tolerant quantum computation. It introduces and utilizes the 'isotropy floor' as a powerful mathematical tool to derive these lower bounds. The work is significant because it establishes optimality for general layer sizes, advancing the theoretical understanding of quantum circuit resource costs.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on gate complexity, the rigorous mathematical techniques used to derive resource bounds (like the isotropy floor) are highly relevant to understanding the resource scaling in complex quantum simulations or quantum error correction codes.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to determine the minimum T-count, which is the dominant cost, for implementing fault-tolerant Clifford+T quantum computations.

Main result. It proves that a layer of $m$ disjoint CCZ gates requires exactly $6m+1$ T gates, establishing a new optimal lower bound for general $m$.

Method. The core method involves deriving a lower bound called the 'isotropy floor' ($\delta$) by analyzing the phase polynomial of diagonal gates using isotropy constraints.

Model / system. The study focuses on Hadamard-free Clifford+T quantum circuits utilizing clean ancillas, specifically analyzing the cost associated with parallel Toffoli layers (CCZ gates).

Key observables. T-count ($\delta$), Isotropy Floor ($\delta$), Stabilizer Nullity ($ u$), and the phase polynomial.

Important parameters / regimes. The bound depends on the order-$\le 3$ moments of the phase polynomial, and the results are parameterized by the number of qubits ($n$) and the layer size ($m$).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary model assumes Hadamard-free circuits with clean ancillas; the bound is derived from an isotropy constraint on the phase polynomial.

Figures summary. The notes mention figures summarizing the end-to-end argument for the isotropy floor, specifically showing the CCZ case yielding a T-count of 7.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from analyzing a single gate (CCZ) to establishing general lower bounds (Isotropy Floor) using moment tensors, and then extending these results to layers of gates.

Abstract

The $T$-count is the dominant cost of fault-tolerant Clifford+$T$ computation. We prove that a layer of $m$ disjoint CCZ gates, the diagonal core of a parallel Toffoli layer, needs exactly $6m+1$ $T$ gates in every Hadamard-free Clifford+$T$ circuit with clean ancillas. Campbell and Howard gave the matching construction. To our knowledge this is the first proof that it is optimal for general $m$ (for $m=1$ the value $7$ is classical, and Campbell and Howard state the value $13$ at $m=2$). On every controlled unitary the same floor comes within one of their exact count, and it recovers their $4m+3$ for a fan-out of $m$ Toffolis from one control. The proof rests on an isotropy constraint: for a pure-cubic phase, the vectors recording which $T$ gates touch each qubit span a totally isotropic subspace. In general the constraint gives the isotropy floor $δ\ge2(n-d^{\ast})-r$ for every diagonal level-three gate, computed from the phase polynomial in polynomial time. The floor is never below stabilizer nullity $ν$, which equals $n-d^{\ast}$ on this class, and a separate parity argument raises it to $2ν+1$ on non-Clifford pure-cubic gates. On the output of the TODD optimizer for the $24$ benchmark circuits it completes, the floor certifies $193$ of its $311$ merged phase-polynomial blocks optimal for their Hadamard layering ($186$ to $193$ across five optimizer seeds), against $113$ for nullity. The floor also holds, under stated conditions, for circuits whose only internal Hadamards form unitarily uncomputed temporary AND blocks, while under adaptive feedforward only $t\geν$ is proved.

Exponential quantum advantages for decoded quantum interferometry in the streaming setting

Authors: Kewen Wu, Guangxu Yang
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.01902v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work demonstrates that Decoded Quantum Interferometry provides provable quantum advantages for solving polynomial intersection problems in the streaming setting. By analyzing the memory and time resources, the authors show that quantum algorithms can achieve high accuracy with polylogarithmic space, outperforming classical methods that require polynomial space even with unlimited time.

Why it may be interesting. The focus on quantum advantages in resource-constrained settings (streaming, memory) is highly relevant to developing practical quantum algorithms for complex data processing tasks.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper analyzes the quantum advantage of Decoded Quantum Interferometry (DQI) when applied to the Optimal Polynomial Intersection (OPI) problem in the memory-constrained streaming setting.

Main result. It proves that DQI yields provable quantum advantages in memory for this problem, showing that quantum algorithms can achieve high approximation guarantees with polylogarithmic space, while classical counterparts require significantly more resources.

Method. The authors establish quantum efficiency by designing one-pass quantum streaming algorithms and prove classical hardness by deriving lower bounds on the space required by classical algorithms.

Model / system. The problem is modeled as finding a low-degree polynomial satisfying constraints (values and Hasse derivatives/jets) over a finite field $\mathbb{F}_q$, analyzed within the streaming model.

Key observables. Approximation guarantee ($\alpha$), space complexity (qubits used), and time complexity (per stream entry).

Important parameters / regimes. Approximation ratio $\alpha$, stream length $N$, field size $q$, and polynomial degree $\ell$.

Assumptions / limitations. The classical lower bounds are shown to hold for uniformly random instances in a fixed grouped order.

Figures summary. The abstract summarizes a trade-off curve comparing quantum vs. classical performance across Memory, Data passes, and Time for different approximation levels.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theory by first establishing the quantum advantage using DQI, then deriving rigorous classical lower bounds, and finally providing a complete tradeoff curve for the tunable parameters.

Abstract

Decoded quantum interferometry (DQI) is a polynomial-time quantum algorithm introduced by Jordan et al. (Nature 2025). For a natural optimization problem, known as optimal polynomial intersection (OPI), it achieves approximation guarantees in regimes where all known classical algorithms require exponential time. Besides time, space is another central resource: storing and manipulating a massive input can be very challenging, especially when logical qubits carry substantial fault-tolerant implementation overhead. This motivates the following question: does DQI yield quantum advantages in memory, and can we prove it unconditionally? We give an affirmative answer to this question in the streaming setting. In particular, we consider a natural generalization of OPI using Hermite interpolation and Hasse derivatives, which asks for a low-degree polynomial satisfying as many constraints on its values and derivatives as possible. As a concrete example, we show [Quantum efficiency.] An adaptation of the DQI algorithm produces a polynomial satisfying $93\%$ of the constraints; moreover, it only reads the input stream in one pass, uses polylogarithmic space, and has polylogarithmic computation time per stream entry. [Classical hardness.] Any classical algorithm that produces an answer satisfying just $76\%$ of the constraints requires polynomial space, even if it can read the input stream with polynomially many passes and can use unlimited time. Our result provides a complete tradeoff curve for the tunable parameters, and implies that DQI has provable quantum advantages for the original OPI problem.

Factor Code Networks

Authors: Abhisek Sahu, Jeremy van der Heijden, Mark Van Raamsdonk, Rana Zibakhsh
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.00469v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops the mathematical theory of 'factor code networks,' generalizing quantum error-correcting codes to structured networks. It determines the precise algebraic and geometric conditions—such as dimension divisibility or specific angle multiplicities—required for these networks to be consistently defined by compatible operator maps. This work is crucial for understanding the structure of quantum information encoded across complex, hierarchical physical systems.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical framework developed for factor code networks provides powerful tools for analyzing tensor network structures arising in quantum gravity or quantum field theory, linking algebraic consistency to geometric constraints.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper characterizes the conditions under which a general quantum code network, defined by isometries on a partially ordered set, can be promoted to a consistent 'factor code network' via compatible unital operator maps.

Main result. Promotion is possible for tree structures if dimension divisibility holds, but for more complex geometries like diamond networks, it requires specific geometric conditions related to the multiplicities of principal angles between subspaces.

Method. The authors use abstract algebra, von Neumann algebras, and geometric analysis involving principal angles to establish necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of the required commuting operator maps.

Model / system. The model is a quantum code network, which generalizes quantum error-correcting codes by indexing the structure over a partially ordered set. The core mathematical objects are isometries $T_{ji}$ and the associated operator maps $\varphi_{ji}$.

Key observables. Principal angles between subspaces ($T_{42}H_2$ and $T_{43}H_3$), and the algebraic structure of the generated von Neumann factor subalgebras ($\mathcal{M}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The ratio of Hilbert space dimensions ($\dim H_{ ext{phys}} / \dim H_{ ext{code}}$) must be an integer for a factor code.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces, allowing the use of matrix algebra and von Neumann factor theory.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate Hasse diagrams for code networks, showing examples of structures that either satisfy or fail the promotion criteria, particularly for diamond-shaped networks.

Paper structure. The paper first defines factor codes and code networks, then develops characterizations for the maps ($\varphi$). It proceeds by analyzing specific network topologies: tree structures, diamond-shaped networks, and general conditions for commuting/intersecting subalgebras.

Abstract

For a quantum code defined by an isometry $T:H_{\rm code} \to H_{\rm phys}$, an operator map is a *-homomorphism $\varphi: B(H_{\rm code}) \to B(H_{\rm phys})$ from the space of operators on the logical Hilbert space to the space of operators on the physical Hilbert space satisfying $\varphi({\cal O}) T = T {\cal O}$ for every operator ${\cal O}$ on $H_{\rm code}$. This can additionally be taken to be unital $\varphi(I_{code}) = I_{phys}$ if and only if $\dim H_{\rm code}$ divides $\dim H_{\rm phys}$. In this case, $\varphi(B(H_{\rm code})$ is a von Neumann factor subalgebra of $B(H_{\rm phys})$ and we call the pair $(T,\varphi)$ a {\it factor code}. A code network is a family of Hilbert spaces $\{{\cal H}_i\}$ indexed by a partially ordered set with isometries $T_{ji}:{\cal H}_i \to {\cal H}_j$ for $i < j$ satisfying $T_{kj} T_{ji} = T_{ki}$ for $i < j < k$. In this paper, we ask when this can be promoted to a {\it factor code network} by defining unital operator maps $\varphi_{ji}$ so that $(T_{ji}, \varphi_{ji})$ is a factor code and $\varphi_{kj} \varphi_{ji} = \varphi_{ki}$. We show that the divisibility conditions on Hilbert space dimensions are sufficient for any code network with a tree structure but not in general. For a diamond-shaped network, we show that promotion is possible if and only if the principle angles between $T_{42} H_2$ and $T_{43} H_3$ all have multiplicity divisible by $H_1$. For incomparable $i_1,i_2 < j$, we characterize when $\varphi_{j i_1} (B(H_{i_1}))$ and $\varphi_{j i_2} (B(H_{i_2}))$ commute, when they generate all of $B(H_j)$, and when they intersect only on multiples of the identity. For $i < j_1,j_2 < k$, we characterize when $\varphi_{k j_1} (B(H_{j_1})) \cap \varphi_{k j_2} (B(H_{j_2})) = \varphi_{k i} (B(H_{i}))$ and when these algebras together with $B(H_k)$ form a nondegenerate commuting square.

Fourier Symmetrization for Geometric Quantum Machine Learning

Authors: Letao Wang, Abdel Lisser, Sreejith Sreekumar, Zeno Toffano
Type: both · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.01874v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work develops a rigorous framework for incorporating symmetry into Quantum Fourier Models (QFMs) for machine learning. By analyzing the Fourier spectrum, the authors derive tight bounds showing how symmetry constrains model expressivity. They validate this approach by demonstrating that symmetry-aware QPINNs achieve superior accuracy when solving complex physical PDEs.

Why it may be interesting. The explicit connection between group theory (symmetry), spectral analysis (Fourier decomposition), and physical modeling (PDEs) provides a powerful, structured inductive bias for quantum machine learning applied to physical systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses how symmetry constrains and shapes the expressivity of Quantum Fourier Models (QFMs) used in Geometric Quantum Machine Learning (GQML). It seeks to provide rigorous theoretical bounds on the performance of symmetry-enforced models.

Main result. Randomized encoding, which avoids deep circuits, achieves the lowest mean errors when using QPINNs to solve 2D PDEs like the screened Poisson and Hamilton-Jacobi equations.

Method. The authors use Fourier analysis to decompose symmetry-invariant QFMs, deriving tight theoretical bounds on coefficient variances by analyzing the deviation from ideal 2-design assumptions.

Model / system. The model is a Quantum Fourier Model (QFM) used as a Quantum Physics-Informed Neural Network (QPINN) to solve continuous Partial Differential Equations (PDEs) in 2D space.

Key observables. Mean error in solving PDEs (Screened Poisson, Stationary Viscous Hamilton-Jacobi), and the variance of symmetrized Fourier coefficients.

Important parameters / regimes. The approximation parameter $\epsilon$, the size of the symmetry group, and the dimensionality of the PDEs (2D).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the QFM viewpoint, the assumption of 2-design properties for trainable layers, and the use of randomized encoding to implement symmetry invariance without deep circuits.

Figures summary. Not specified, but the notes reference figures illustrating Fourier orbit decomposition and the comparison of various QPINN performance metrics.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining the symmetry problem in QFMs, developing the Fourier orbit decomposition theory, deriving tight mathematical bounds on coefficient variances, and finally validating the approach by applying it to solve specific 2D PDEs using randomized encoding.

Abstract

Geometric quantum machine learning incorporates symmetry into quantum models, but how symmetry shapes their expressivity and guides effective model design remains insufficiently understood. We address this question through the Fourier representation of quantum Fourier models (QFMs). Symmetry organizes the frequency spectrum into orbits and sums the Fourier coefficients within each orbit into a symmetrized coefficient. When QFM trainable layers form independent exact 2-designs, the variance of each symmetrized coefficient equals the sum of the coefficient variances in its orbit. For single-layer QFMs with $\varepsilon$-approximate 2-design trainable layers, we bound the deviation from this identity. The resulting bound for individual Fourier coefficients can be exponentially tighter than an existing bound. The hyperoctahedral group provides an example of orbit growth that can mitigate vanishing expressivity of the symmetrized coefficients. Symmetrization of QFMs over an elementary abelian 2-group also yields pure multivariate Chebyshev polynomial basis functions. We introduce randomized encoding, which implements invariant models without ancilla qubits or the additional circuit depth for quantum twirling. We evaluate the models as quantum physics-informed neural networks (QPINNs) on two-dimensional screened Poisson and stationary viscous Hamilton-Jacobi equations. Under hard boundary constraints, QPINNs using exact symmetrization and randomized encoding achieve the lowest mean errors in the two benchmarks, respectively.

From Permutation Symmetry to Communication Bounds and Additivity

Authors: Zahra Baghali Khanian, Debbie Leung, Graeme Smith
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.02176v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work analyzes the quantum capacity of quantum channels, focusing on the role of input symmetry. It proves that when inputs are permutation-invariant, the advantage gained from using many channel uses asymptotically vanishes, reducing the problem to the single-use capacity. The paper establishes deep structural links between additivity properties for various quantum information measures.

Why it may be interesting. It provides rigorous mathematical bounds on the fundamental limits of quantum communication, showing how symmetry constraints can simplify complex asymptotic problems, which is crucial for understanding resource theories in quantum optics and computation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the quantum capacity of memoryless quantum channels, specifically determining whether correlations across multiple channel uses (superadditivity) provide an asymptotic advantage over single-use performance.

Main result. Under the constraint of full permutation invariance on the input state, the asymptotic quantum capacity per use converges to the single-use coherent information maximum, implying that finite-block superadditivity effects vanish in the limit.

Method. The authors employ advanced quantum information theory tools, including analyzing the convergence of normalized coherent information, utilizing de Finetti reduction, and establishing common variational frameworks for various channel divergences.

Model / system. The analysis is performed on finite-dimensional memoryless quantum channels ($N$) acting on quantum states, with a specific focus on inputs that are permutation-invariant across multiple uses.

Key observables. Quantum capacity ($Q(N)$), coherent information ($I_c$), sandwiched Rényi divergence ($eD_\alpha$), and minimum output Rényi entropy ($h_\alpha$).

Important parameters / regimes. The block length $n$, the Rényi order $\alpha > 1$, and the single-use channel capacity $Q_1(N)$.

Assumptions / limitations. The results are strongly conditioned on the input state being fully permutation-invariant across all $n$ channel uses.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the convergence of quantum information measures (like coherent information and channel divergences) from finite blocks ($n$) to the asymptotic limit ($n o \infty$), showing how permutation symmetry constrains the achievable rates and establishes structural connections between additivity results.

Abstract

Correlations across channel uses can improve quantum communication rates, making optimization over arbitrarily large blocks a central difficulty in determining quantum capacity. We show how full permutation invariance limits this advantage for every finite-dimensional memoryless channel. The optimized coherent information per use converges to the single-use maximum and consequently attains its supremum at a finite block length. Thus finite-block superadditivity remains possible, while the asymptotic optimization reduces to a single channel use. We also establish a strong converse for pure-source entanglement generation with permutation-invariant inputs: at any fixed rate above the single-use coherent-information maximum, the fidelity tends to zero. Beyond these communication bounds, we identify a common structure underlying completely bounded norms and the sandwiched Rényi channel quantities governing discrimination against a replacer and entanglement-assisted communication. De Finetti reduction and permutation covariance yield systematic alternative proofs of their known multiplicativity and additivity results within a shared variational framework. We further show that, for permutation-invariant inputs, correlations cannot sustain an asymptotic reduction in output entropy per use below the single-use minimum. This entropy limit holds for Rényi orders greater than one and for the von Neumann entropy, extending the role of symmetry from communication bounds to the control of output entropy.

From Steane to A7: Quantum Codes from Invariant States

Authors: Ian Teixeira
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.00865v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops a powerful method to construct quantum error-correcting codes by analyzing invariant states derived from symmetry constraints. It successfully constructs a new two-parameter family of seven-ququart codes with $ ilde{A}_7$ symmetry, extending beyond previously known codes like the Steane code.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical framework connecting symmetry constraints (like $ ilde{A}_7$) to the existence and parameters of quantum codes is highly relevant for designing fault-tolerant quantum computation protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to construct quantum error-correcting codes with specific transversal symmetries, particularly $ ilde{A}_7$, realizing the finite component of a two-qubit super-golden gate set.

Main result. A two-parameter family of single-error-correcting seven-ququart codes, $((7, 4, 3))_4$, is constructed, showing that the Steane and Fake Steane codes are specific points within this continuous family.

Method. The construction method involves building invariant parent states by solving linear equations derived from Schur-Weyl sector weights, rather than searching directly for the codespace.

Model / system. The system is a quantum error-correcting code defined on ququart systems, analyzed using invariant states in the tensor product space $V \otimes n$ under the combined action of local unitary-design symmetry ($G \le SU(q)$) and permutation symmetry ($\Sigma \le S_n$).

Key observables. Code parameters $((7, 4, 3))_4$, the continuous parameter $ heta$, and the sector weights $(p_8, p_{44})$ determined by moment equations.

Important parameters / regimes. The local dimension $q=4$, the number of sites $n=7$ (for the code), and the symmetry group $ ilde{A}_7 \le SU(4)$.

Assumptions / limitations. The method relies on the equivalence between maximal mixing of marginals on $\le t$ sites and the feasibility of a linear system in Schur-Weyl sector weights.

Figures summary. The notes mention TABLE I comparing the Steane and $ ilde{A}_7$ families, and Equation (15) providing the unique solution for the sector weights in the $ ilde{A}_7$ case.

Paper structure. The paper develops the general theory by relating code construction to linear feasibility problems in Schur-Weyl weights. It then applies this theory to the specific case of $ ilde{A}_7$ symmetry, showing how the resulting parent state yields the desired two-parameter family of codes.

Abstract

We construct a two-parameter family of single-error-correcting seven-ququart codes with transversal $\tilde{A_7}$ symmetry, realizing the finite component of a two-qubit super-golden gate set. These $((7,4,3))_4$ codes encode two logical qubits and support non-Clifford operations by applying the same gate to each physical ququart. Our method constructs invariant parent states rather than searching directly for a codespace. For $n$ systems of local dimension $q$, suppose the local symmetry is a unitary $t$-group and the permutation symmetry is $t$-transitive. Maximal mixing of all marginals on at most $t$ sites is then equivalent to linear equations in Schur--Weyl sector weights. Every feasible solution yields parents of pure distance at least $t+1$; puncturing one site produces a $q$-dimensional code of distance at least $t$ with the prescribed local symmetry acting transversally. Steane and the nonstabilizer Fake Steane code reveal why the construction leaves continuous freedom. They share the same prescribed transversal Clifford and permutation symmetries, and their eight-qubit purifications lie on a circle: error correction fixes two invariant-sector norms but leaves the relative phase free. Keeping the same permutation group and replacing Clifford symmetry by $\tilde{A_7}$ $\leq SU(4)$ gives three fixed sector norms and two free relative phases, producing the new ququart family.

Good Quantum Locally Testable Codes from Lossless Cubical Complexes

Authors: Itay Cohen, Itai Leigh, Assaf Reiner, Amnon Ta-Shma, Elad Tzalik
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.00525v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops a theoretical framework to construct quantum locally testable codes by analyzing the properties of high-dimensional lossless cubical complexes. It establishes a local-to-global principle that links directional expansion within these complexes to the existence of good quantum codes. This advances the understanding of how combinatorial geometry can guarantee robust quantum error correction.

Why it may be interesting. The connection between high-dimensional combinatorial structures (lossless complexes) and the existence of robust quantum error-correcting codes provides a powerful, abstract tool for designing fault-tolerant quantum computation protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Developing asymptotically good Quantum Locally Testable Codes (qLTCs) using a higher-dimensional combinatorial approach based on lossless cubical complexes.

Main result. The existence of sufficiently imbalanced, two-sided lossless four-dimensional cubical complexes guarantees the existence of asymptotically good quantum locally testable codes.

Method. The authors associate a high-dimensional cubical complex with a level chain complex and establish a local-to-global theorem linking directional lossless expansion to small-set coboundary expansion.

Model / system. The model is quantum error correction, specifically constructing qLTCs. The mathematical framework involves high-dimensional lossless cubical complexes and their associated level chain complexes.

Key observables. Code parameters such as rate, distance, and the ability to achieve local testability soundness.

Important parameters / regimes. The dimension of the cubical complex (e.g., four-dimensional), the degree of imbalance, and the expansion properties (lossless expansion).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is investigating the implications of the existence of the required high-dimensional lossless cubical complexes, as explicit construction is not provided.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the structure of complexes (e.g., left-right Cayley complex) and the application of algorithms like the Greedy Flipping Algorithm for bounding parameters.

Paper structure. The paper builds from classical LTCs to qLTCs, formalizing the structure via level chain complexes. It develops a local-to-global principle and then analyzes the resulting code parameters, including bounding distance and presenting a linear-time decoder.

Abstract

Sipser and Spielman constructed LDPC codes from either bipartite \emph{spectral} expanders or one-sided \emph{lossless} expanders. In higher dimensions, \emph{spectral} expansion similarly played a central role in the constructions of asymptotically good classical LTCs and qLDPC codes by Dinur, Evra, Livne, Lubotzky, and Mozes and by Panteleev and Kalachev. Alternatively, Lin and Hsieh constructed classical LTCs and qLDPC codes from two-dimensional \emph{lossless} cubical complexes. In this work we develop the higher-dimensional \emph{lossless} approach. We do not construct the required high-dimensional lossless cubical complexes; rather, we investigate what their existence would imply. We associate with a high-dimensional cubical complex a \emph{level chain complex}, whose chain groups are supported on the level sets of the Boolean cube rather than on its cells. Our main technical contribution is a clean local-to-global theorem for this structure: suitable one-dimensional lossless expansion in the directional graphs implies small-set coboundary expansion of the global level complex. As a consequence, sufficiently imbalanced, two-sided lossless four-dimensional cubical complexes give rise to asymptotically good quantum locally testable codes. We expect the local-to-global principle developed here to have further applications.

Summary. This theoretical paper compares the performance of two attention-based architectures, the Kernel Learner and the Feature Learner, for performing nonlinear In-context Learning. Using advanced statistical physics techniques like the replica method, the authors derive phase diagrams that predict which architecture performs better based on system parameters. The work clarifies how architectural choices govern the ability of large models to learn tasks from limited demonstrations.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical comparison of different learning architectures in the context of deep learning models (Transformers) provides theoretical insights into how model structure interacts with data statistics, which is highly relevant to understanding complex physical systems modeled by neural networks.

Main result. The analysis yields phase diagrams showing that the optimal architecture depends critically on the interplay between pretraining data size, task diversity, and context lengths, with the Feature Learner showing advantages in certain regimes.

Abstract

In-context learning (ICL) enables a pretrained model to infer a task from demonstrations without updating its parameters. While much of the existing theory focuses on linear target functions, in this paper we study nonlinear cases by comparing two one-layer attention architectures on the same family of single-index tasks. A kernel learner first maps inputs through a fixed nonlinear feature map and then applies linear attention, whereas a feature learner applies attention to the original input, followed by a learned nonlinear readout. We derive predictions for their memorization and generalization errors using the replica method, retaining the effects of pretraining size, task-pool diversity, and training and inference context lengths. The resulting predictions closely match numerical experiments across a broad range of regimes. Our analysis yields phase diagrams that characterize when each architecture is advantageous as the amount of pretraining data, task diversity, and context lengths vary. We further identify qualitatively different context-length scalings for the two learners. Together, these results clarify how architectural choices interact with the dataset and govern nonlinear in-context learning.

Abstract

In the quantum inverse scattering method, the sine-Gordon model is solved on a lattice, and its continuum and infinite-volume limits become uniform only after the length of the box is rescaled. We show that this rescaling, supplemented by an on-shell condition, defines a renormalization scheme. The whole dependence on the cut-off is absorbed into a factor $Z_L$ multiplying the length of the box, with an exponent fixed by the scaling dimension of the vertex operator; neither the field nor the coupling $β$ is renormalized, no subtraction scale is introduced, and the mass parameter receives a finite renormalization, fixed by requiring that the lightest breather has the mass of the boson in the linearized theory. The soliton and breather masses follow from the eigenvalues of the monodromy operator as functions of parameters that do not run, and reduce to the classical and semiclassical results as $β\rightarrow0$. In particular, the mass ratios are produced directly from the regularized construction, while the relation between the lattice parameters and the physical scale is absorbed into $Z_L$ and never needs to be computed. The scheme is a reparameterization of the theory which makes explicit the interplay between integrability and renormalization. We compare it with Coleman's normal ordering and with conformal perturbation theory, and we argue that it extends to ultralocal integrable theories with a massive spectrum, possibly including asymptotically free models that admit an ultralocal lattice regularization.
