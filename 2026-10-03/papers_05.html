The half-filled optical Su-Schrieffer-Heeger-Hubbard model with uniaxial strain

Authors: Jonah Huang, James Neuhaus, Benjamin Cohen-Stead, Steven Johnston, Richard Scalettar
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.00681v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 3/5

Summary. This work models the half-filled SSH-Hubbard system under uniaxial strain to study the competition between Bond-Order-Wave (BOW) and Antiferromagnetic (AFM) phases. Using advanced Quantum Monte Carlo simulations, the authors map the phase diagram and demonstrate that strain significantly influences the stability and characteristics of the resulting electronic order. This provides theoretical insight into strain-tunable correlated electronic phases.

Why it may be interesting. The combination of electron-phonon coupling, strong correlations (Hubbard $U$), and external tuning (strain) makes this highly relevant for understanding emergent electronic phases in correlated materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the interplay between Bond-Order-Wave (BOW) correlations and Antiferromagnetic (AFM) correlations in the half-filled SSH-Hubbard model when subjected to uniaxial strain.

Main result. Uniaxial strain anisotropically modifies the phase diagram, shifting the competition toward the BOW phase and allowing for the quantification of the BOW transition temperature.

Method. The research employs Determinant Quantum Monte Carlo (DQMC) simulations combined with Hybrid Monte Carlo (HMC) updates for phonon fields, and uses analytic continuation to analyze spectral properties.

Model / system. The system is the optical Su-Schrieffer-Heeger-Hubbard (SSHH) model on a 2D square lattice, incorporating electron-phonon coupling ($\lambda$) and on-site Hubbard repulsion ($U$). Strain is modeled via anisotropic hopping, breaking rotational symmetry.

Key observables. Equilibrium phase diagram (BOW vs. AFM), spectral function showing directionally dependent gap formation, and correlation functions for both BOW and AFM order.

Important parameters / regimes. Half-filling ($ ho=1$), dimensionless e-ph coupling ($\lambda$), Hubbard coupling ($U$), and the strain parameter ($\delta$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis uses the leading-order expression for electron-phonon coupling and relies on the DQMC method being applicable due to particle-hole symmetry at half-filling.

Figures summary. The phase diagram maps the competition between AFM and BOW order in the $(\lambda, U)$ plane, showing how strain shifts the boundaries.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the SSH-Hubbard model with strain, details the DQMC/HMC simulation methodology, maps the phase diagram by varying $\lambda$ and $U$, and finally analyzes spectral properties in the ordered phases.

Abstract

Strain offers a direct route to control electronic phases by altering bond lengths, hopping amplitudes, and lattice symmetries. These aspects make electron-phonon (e-ph) coupled systems a natural setting to study strain-induced effects. The Su-Schrieffer-Heeger (SSH) Hamiltonian describes the coupling between itinerant electrons and lattice degrees of freedom that modulate hopping and is thus expected to be strongly affected by strain fields. At half-filling and on a bipartite lattice, the SSH interactions drive dominant bond-order-wave (BOW) correlations at low- temperature in which short bonds with high kinetic energy alternate with long bonds with low kinetic energy along four possible patterns. We model uniaxial strain through anisotropic hopping, which breaks the $90^\circ$ rotational symmetry and reduces the BOW phase degeneracy to two. We also include an on-site Hubbard U that promotes antiferromagnetic correlations that are also (weakly) driven by the SSH interaction itself. We map out the half-filled phase diagram in the plane of the e-ph and Hubbard couplings, and determine the critical temperature of BOW formation. Using analytic continuation, we also evaluate the spectral function and demonstrate directionally dependent gap formation in the BOW phase

Toward Optimal Circuit Depth for Geometrically Local Hamiltonian Simulation

Authors: Zhenyu Shen, Yusen Wu, Penghui Yao, Xiao Yuan, Yukun Zhang
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.01839v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 3/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops a method to simulate quantum dynamics for local Hamiltonians by decomposing the evolution into short-time steps on small spatial blocks. It achieves an upper bound on the circuit depth that is near-optimal, matching a proven lower bound for specific systems like the XXZ model. This work clarifies the role of spatial locality in determining the computational cost of quantum simulation.

Why it may be interesting. It provides concrete, near-optimal complexity bounds for simulating lattice Hamiltonians, which is crucial for designing efficient quantum algorithms for many-body physics simulations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to determine the optimal circuit depth required to simulate the time evolution of interacting quantum systems governed by geometrically local Hamiltonians.

Main result. The authors establish an upper bound of $O(T \log(nT/\epsilon))$ for circuit depth and prove a lower bound of $\Omega(\log n / \log \log n)$ for uniform XXZ Hamiltonians, showing near-optimality.

Method. The approach involves decomposing the simulation into short-time evolutions on small, overlapping spatial blocks using Lieb-Robinson bounds and applying advanced product formulas with error compensation.

Model / system. The system is an $n$-qubit, time-independent, finite-range Hamiltonian ($H$) defined on a fixed-dimensional lattice. The simulation targets the unitary evolution $e^{-iHT}$ to a specified accuracy $\epsilon$.

Key observables. Circuit depth, gate count, and simulation accuracy ($\epsilon$).

Important parameters / regimes. Evolution time $T$, number of qubits $n$, lattice dimension $D$, and local interaction strength $J$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the spatial locality of the Hamiltonian and the ability to decompose the evolution into blocks whose error can be controlled by the block diameter.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the problem of simulating local dynamics, then details the construction of the circuit using spatial decomposition and error correction techniques, culminating in the proof of the near-optimal circuit depth bounds by matching upper and lower bounds.

Abstract

Understanding the dynamics of interacting quantum systems is a central goal of quantum simulation. For geometrically local Hamiltonians, locality suggests that digital simulation should retain much of the parallelism of the underlying dynamics. For a target accuracy $ε$, we construct a nearest-neighbor quantum circuit that simulates the dynamics of an $n$-qubit, time-independent, finite-range Hamiltonian with normalized local interaction strength on a fixed-dimensional lattice for time $T\ge1$, with circuit depth $O(T\log(nT/ε))$ and gate count $O(nT\log(nT/ε))$. This reduces the polylogarithmic overhead of Haah, Hastings, Kothari, and Low to a single logarithm. On logarithmic-scale blocks from the Lieb--Robinson decomposition, a shallow but highly accurate implementation is essential for reducing the circuit depth. Our key observation is that spatial localization also controls the cost of high precision. In each block, the error of a constant-order product formula is compensated for by a correction, yielding exponential accuracy. The correction can be implemented with depth proportional to the block diameter, introducing no additional asymptotic depth scale beyond that required by spatial localization. Furthermore, we prove an unconditional circuit-depth lower bound for uniform XXZ Hamiltonians: at fixed evolution time $T$ in the stated regime and sufficiently small fixed error $ε$, any nearest-neighbor quantum circuit simulating these dynamics requires depth $Ω(\log n/\log\log n)$. This matches our $O(\log n)$ upper bound up to a doubly logarithmic factor, establishing near-optimal circuit depth for lattice Hamiltonian simulation in this regime. Together, these results clarify the role of locality and precision in determining the circuit complexity of quantum dynamics and suggest broader principles for spatially constrained quantum computation.

Unitary Schur Sampling of Qudits via Random SWAP Tests: Hunt for Antisymmetry

Summary. This work presents a novel, resource-efficient method to perform Unitary Schur Sampling on multi-qudit systems using only pairwise SWAP tests. By framing the process as a search for antisymmetry, the authors achieve a polynomial scaling in the number of qudits. This provides a powerful, experimentally feasible primitive for accessing deep structural information encoded in quantum many-body states.

Why it may be interesting. The development of resource-efficient measurement protocols using only basic quantum gates (like SWAP tests) is crucial for building practical quantum information processing devices.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of implementing Unitary Schur Sampling (USS) for extracting permutation-invariant information from multi-qudit quantum states.

Main result. USS can be realized efficiently using only pairwise SWAP tests, framing the process as a stochastic search for antisymmetry within the system's state space.

Method. The core method involves exploiting Schur-Weyl duality and repeatedly identifying and extracting the largest antisymmetric subsystem using controlled SWAP tests.

Model / system. The system consists of $n$ qudits, each of dimension $d$, whose total Hilbert space decomposes according to Schur-Weyl duality into sectors labeled by Young diagrams $\lambda$.

Key observables. The primary observables are the Young-diagram label $\lambda$ and the corresponding reduced state on the $ ext{SU}(d)$ irrep subsystem $Q_d^\lambda$.

Important parameters / regimes. The complexity scales polynomially with $n$ and $d$, achieving error $\epsilon$ in diamond distance with $O(n\min\{n, d\}^3/\epsilon \log^2 n)$ tests.

Assumptions / limitations. The protocol relies on approximating the full antisymmetric measurement and assumes the existence of a canonical pure state structure within the multiplicity subsystem.

Figures summary. TABLE I compares the complexity of different USS implementations, highlighting the efficiency of the proposed SWAP-test method.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces USS, contrasting it with existing complex methods. It then details the SWAP-test based approach by exploiting antisymmetry, developing algorithms, and finally applying the technique to Quantum Purity Amplification (QPA).

Abstract

Schur sampling is a fundamental primitive for extracting permutation-invariant information from many-body quantum systems and has broad applications in quantum information science. Circuit-based implementations typically rely on coherent representation-theoretic operations such as Clebsch-Gordan transforms, generalized phase estimation, or quantum Fourier transforms over the symmetric group. We show that unitary Schur sampling of arbitrary permutation-invariant mixed states on $n$ $d$-dimensional qudits can instead be implemented using only pairwise SWAP tests, namely, two-qudit projective measurements onto the symmetric and antisymmetric subspaces. Our protocol achieves error $ε$ in diamond distance using $O(n\min\{n,d\}^{3}\log^2(n/ε))$ random SWAP tests. The central idea is to repeatedly identify and extract the largest antisymmetric subsystem. This turns unitary Schur sampling into a search for antisymmetry and gives the algorithm a natural interpretation as a stochastic traversal of a Young diagram. The protocol preserves the $\mathrm{SU}(d)$-irrep state while preparing a canonical pure state in the multiplicity subsystem. As an application, we consider quantum purity amplification (QPA), in which multiple noisy copies of a pure quantum state are combined to produce a state of higher purity. We show that the optimal purified state can be obtained by retaining a single designated output qudit and discarding the rest.

Mass-asymmetry-controlled exciton dressing and dissociation in a quantum lattice model

Summary. This theoretical work analyzes how mass asymmetry controls the interaction between excitons and phonons in polar semiconductors. It reveals a paradox where the symmetry protecting the exciton from phonon dressing simultaneously removes the energy stabilization it needs, causing it to dissociate at strong coupling. The findings map out distinct regimes governed by internal dressing versus global energy balance.

Why it may be interesting. The interplay between symmetry protection (a quantum selection rule) and thermodynamic stability (energy balance) is a rich topic relevant to understanding charge transfer and localization in many correlated materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the non-perturbative fate of exciton-phonon coupling, specifically analyzing the interplay between internal exciton dressing and the thermodynamic stability (dissociation) of the exciton in a polar material.

Main result. Mass symmetry protects the exciton from phonon dressing via an exchange selection rule, but this same symmetry prevents polaronic stabilization, leading to dissociation at strong coupling. Increasing mass asymmetry breaks this protection, causing strong dressing.

Method. The study employs exact diagonalization of a Holstein-exciton model, utilizing parameterization of hopping terms to control mass asymmetry, and comparing the exciton energy to the sum of free-polaron energies.

Model / system. The system is a neutral exciton coupled to local Einstein phonons in a quantum lattice model (1D chain). The Hamiltonian includes kinetic, Coulomb attraction, and electron-phonon coupling terms.

Key observables. Zero-phonon weight ($Z_0$), total phonon number ($\langle N_{ph} angle$), exciton binding energy ($E_{bind}$), and the dissociation estimate ($g_c$).

Important parameters / regimes. Mass asymmetry ($\delta$ or $\eta$), phonon coupling strength ($g$), and Coulomb interaction strength ($V$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the source parity is key to the selection rule, and the dissociation boundary is determined by a global energy balance between Coulomb binding and polaronic stabilization.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the crossover in $Z_0$ and $\langle N_{ph} angle$ as mass asymmetry increases, show the energy level crossing defining the dissociation boundary, and present a regime map separating internal dressing effects from global energy balance.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the model and the role of source parity in protecting the exciton at mass symmetry. It then maps the crossover regime controlled by mass asymmetry, and finally determines the dissociation boundary via an energy balance comparison.

Abstract

In a polar material, a neutral exciton couples to phonons through the sum of the electron and hole deformation potentials. Because the total source vanishes by charge neutrality, the elastic exciton-phonon vertex is regularized by electron-hole interference. Here we determine the non-perturbative fate of this interference by exact diagonalization of a Holstein-exciton model. By parameterizing the mass asymmetry to decouple it from the small-polaron atomic limit, we map a regime map comprising an internal dressing crossover and a dissociation boundary. We prove analytically and verify numerically that for equal masses, the symmetric exciton ground state is protected from phonon dressing by an exact exchange selection rule, provided the phonon source is odd under electron-hole exchange (the neutral case). As mass asymmetry increases, this selection rule is broken and the exciton acquires a strong local polaronic cloud. We show that the dissociation boundary, conversely, is set by a global energy balance between Coulomb binding and polaronic stabilization, and is nearly independent of the internal dressing. Paradoxically, the very symmetry that protects the exciton from dressing denies it polaronic stabilization, driving it toward dissociation at strong coupling. We discuss these results in the context of lattice exciton-polaron models and their implications for sharp versus broad excitonic lines in mass-symmetric versus mass-asymmetric polar semiconductors.

Summary. This work investigates quantum complexity by tracking the negativity of the Wigner function for the Hartle-Hawking state evolving in JT gravity. The authors find that this negativity saturates at late times and demonstrate that the length basis provides a powerful, semi-classical tool for understanding chaotic quantum dynamics in this gravitational context.

Why it may be interesting. The connection between quantum complexity measures (like negativity) and gravitational physics (JT gravity) provides a deep link between quantum information theory and quantum gravity, which is highly relevant to open quantum systems dynamics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper studies the growth of Wigner function negativity as an operational measure of quantum state complexity during time evolution in Jackiw-Teitelboim (JT) gravity.

Main result. The negativity saturates at late times to a value dependent on the effective dimension, and the length basis is shown to be ideally suited for describing chaotic quantum dynamics semi-classically.

Method. The analysis uses the Wigner function formalism in the length basis, comparing the growth of negativity to the Spectral Form Factor and employing semiclassical approximations.

Model / system. The system is the Hartle-Hawking (HH) state evolving under time evolution governed by the Liouville Hamiltonian in JT gravity. The analysis is performed in the continuum length basis.

Key observables. Wigner Negativity, Spread complexity, Exact survival amplitude, and the thermal partition function $Z_eta$.

Important parameters / regimes. The semiclassical parameter $\beta$, the action $S_0$, and time $t$ are key parameters governing the complexity growth.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the length basis being suitable for a dual, semi-classical effective description, and involves approximations like saddle-point methods and leading-order expansions.

Figures summary. Not specified (The notes do not describe figures, only mathematical results and comparisons).

Paper structure. The paper progresses by defining the complexity measure (negativity), applying it to the HH state in JT gravity, deriving relationships between negativity and survival probability, and finally establishing the suitability of the length basis for semi-classical description.

Abstract

The negativity of the Wigner function gives an operationally meaningful measure of the complexity of simulating a quantum state on a classical computer. We study its growth for the Hartle-Hawking state under time evolution in Jackiw-Teitelboim gravity, in the length basis. At the disk level, and to leading order in the semiclassical parameter $β$, the Hartle-Hawking state is a minimum-uncertainty Gaussian wavepacket at rest at the turning point of the Liouville wall, centred at $x=2\log(2β/π)$ with $\langle p^2\rangle=π^2/8β$. Its Wigner function is positive, so its negativity is $1+o(1)$ for all sub-exponential times; it is even in $t$ by time-reversal symmetry, and exactly frozen once the reflected packet decouples from the wall, because free evolution is a Clifford shear. At late times the negativity saturates close to its upper bound, at $\sqrt{2/π}\sqrt{d_{\rm eff}(β)}$ with $d_{\rm eff}(β)=Z(β)^2/Z(2β)$, a value that requires the discrete spectrum. Unlike the spectral form factor, the negativity displays no ramp: the two-boundary wormhole contribution is suppressed by $e^{-2S_0}$ relative to a disk term that, unlike that of the spectral form factor, does not decay. The exact survival amplitude $Z(β+it)/Z(β)$ gives the spread complexity growing as $σ_E^2t^2$, and the seed-normalised negativity is, at the disk level, the inverse of the exact survival probability. We take this as evidence that the length basis is ideally suited for a dual, semi-classical effective description of chaotic quantum dynamics for large $e^{S_0}$ at sub-exponential times.

Abstract

Understanding how macroscopic wealth distributions emerge from microscopic transaction rules among agents is a central problem in econophysics. While many traditional models restrict agents' wealth to non-negative values, debt is an essential feature of realistic economic systems. In this paper, we introduce a stochastic transaction mechanism into the agent-based model with a central-bank-mediated collective debt limit, as studied in [14]. Our results rigorously identify these previously unconsidered limiting distributions and provide a mathematical framework for understanding how stochastic monetary exchanges give rise to macroscopic wealth distributions.

Abstract

Summary. This paper determines the optimal initial quantum states required to maximize the sensitivity (measured by QFI) when estimating the single-photon coupling in a cavity-optomechanical system. Main problem. The paper aims to find the optimal initial quantum states that maximize the Quantum Fisher Information (QFI) for estimating the single-photon coupling strength ($g_0$) in a linearized cavity-optomechanical system.

Main result. The optimal states depend on the constraints; for fixed total excitation number, the QFI saturates a Heisenberg bound achieved by states equivalent to a two-mode NOON state. Under cavity loss, information accumulation becomes linear at an optimal renewal duration of about 2.5 cavity lifetimes.

Method. The analysis uses the Quantum Fisher Information (QFI) derived from the system's generator, which for pure inputs simplifies to four times the variance of the excitation-exchange operator. Optimization is performed by maximizing this variance under various physical constraints.

Model / system. The system is a linearized cavity-optomechanical setup coupling an optical cavity mode ($\hat{a}$) to a mechanical oscillator ($\hat{b}$). The dynamics are governed by an effective Hamiltonian derived in the resolved-sideband regime, leading to an interaction term proportional to $\hat{n} = \hat{d}\hat{b}^\dagger + \hat{d}^\dagger\hat{b}$.

Key observables. Quantum Fisher Information (QFI), per-hour relative precision, and the variance of the excitation-exchange operator ($ ext{Var}_{\psi_0}(\hat{n})$).

Important parameters / regimes. Single-photon coupling ($g_0$), cavity lifetime ($\kappa$), detuning ($\Delta$), and the total excitation number constraint.

Abstract

We find the optimal initial states of a linearized cavity-optomechanical system for estimating the single-photon coupling by quantum Fisher information. In the red-detuned (beam-splitter) regime the interaction exchanges excitations between the two modes, and for pure inputs the Fisher information is four times the variance of the excitation-exchange operator, reducing the optimization to variance maximization with a closed-form solution. With the mechanical mode in a Fock state the probe is degenerate: any state saturating the photon-number constraint is optimal, and a Fock reference with many phonons enhances the per-photon sensitivity linearly. For an arbitrary fixed mechanical reference it becomes a phase-matched squeezed vacuum, from an exact bound on that coherence obtained by a Lagrange-dual argument. Jointly optimizing both modes at fixed total excitation number, the quantum Fisher information obeys a Heisenberg bound quadratic in the energy, saturated by equal superpositions of the two extremal eigenstates of the generator, equivalently a two-mode NOON state after a balanced beam splitter; balanced product Fock states reach only a linear enhancement. Exact diagonalization in truncated Fock spaces confirms all the results, the familiar single-photon input being the simplest saturating case. Three extensions are worked out: the blue sideband has an unbounded two-mode-squeezing spectrum, so the entangled optimum becomes impossible and the balanced product Fock state is exactly optimal at definite energy; under cavity loss the information accumulation turns linear on the cavity-lifetime timescale, at a renewal-optimal duration of about two and a half lifetimes; and for a state-of-the-art quantum-coherent coupling experiment the per-hour relative precision lies between one part in a million and ten parts in a million, number and thermal references being the most robust.

Abstract

We study the transverse field Ising model, defined by the Hamiltonian $H =\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i, j\in [n]} J_{ij} Z_i Z_j +\sum_{i=1}^n h_i^z Z_i + η\sum_{i} X_i$ where $J $ is the symmetric interaction matrix, and $η$ is the transverse field strength. Let $Δ(J)=λ_{\max}(J)-λ_{\min}(J)$ be the spectral width of $J.$ When the inverse temperature $β\geq0$ satisfies $Δ(J)\cdot\frac{\tanh(βη)}η\leq1$, we give a randomized classical algorithm that approximates the partition function $Z(β)=\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-βH})$ to a given relative error $ε\in(0,1)$ in time polynomial in $n$, $β$, the model parameters, and $ε^{-1}$. Furthermore, in the regime $Δ(J)\cdot \frac{\tanh(βη)}η\leq 1,$ we provide an efficient randomized classical algorithm that approximates Pauli string observables of the Gibbs state $ ρ_β= \frac{e^{-βH}}{\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-βH})}$ within an arbitrarily small additive error. Abstract

Codon optimization is a challenging combinatorial optimization problem with important applications in synthetic biology, protein expression, and biotechnology. While quantum annealing has previously been explored for this problem, the application of gate-based quantum algorithms remains largely unexplored. In this work, we present a gate-based quantum computing framework for codon optimization by formulating the optimization objective as an Ising Hamiltonian and expressing it in terms of Pauli operators suitable for gate-based quantum processors. The proposed framework is investigated using the Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE), Sampling Variational Quantum Eigensolver (SVQE), and the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA), with benchmarking against exact diagonalization, classical optimization, and quantum annealing. Using a three-amino-acid benchmark, the proposed implementations successfully reproduce the exact ground-state solutions while identifying optimized ansatz selections, optimization strategies, initialization schemes, and hardware execution settings that provide reproducible performance on current noisy quantum hardware. Hardware demonstrations are performed on IBM Quantum processors to evaluate the practical implementation of the proposed workflow. As a work in progress, this study establishes a reproducible computational framework for gate-based quantum codon optimization and provides the foundation for future investigations of larger protein sequences as quantum hardware and algorithms continue to advance.
