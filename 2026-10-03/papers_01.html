Summary. This work provides exact analytical results for the probability of first detecting a specific state in a quantum circuit undergoing local monitoring. By relating this probability to the subsystem Loschmidt echo, the authors characterize how measurement frequency dictates the detection statistics. The findings reveal a transition from simple geometric decay to complex, correlated behavior as the measurement rate changes.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a rare exact characterization of how local measurement backaction fundamentally reshapes detection statistics in an interacting quantum many-body system, which is crucial for understanding open quantum dynamics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper derives exact analytical results for the probability of first detecting a subset of qubits back in their initial state within a locally monitored quantum circuit.

Main result. The first-detection probability exhibits two distinct regimes: geometric statistics for infrequent monitoring, and correlated deviations from this behavior for frequent monitoring.

Method. The analysis employs a renewal equation linking the first-detection probability to the subsystem Loschmidt echo, utilizing generating functions and pole analysis for asymptotic behavior.

Model / system. The study focuses on the deterministic Floquet-quantum East model (DFQEM), an interacting quantum circuit subjected to local stroboscopic measurements.

Key observables. First-Detection Return Probability (FDRP), Subsystem Loschmidt Echo, Coefficient of Variation (CV).

Important parameters / regimes. Measurement probing time ($ au$), monitored subregion size ($\ell$), and the ratio between $ au$ and the relaxation timescale ($2\ell$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the initial state of the unmonitored qubits is a bi-translationally invariant product state and relies on the renewal equation framework.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the measurement protocol, the folded space representation for calculation, and the dependence of the Loschmidt echo and FDRP on measurement parameters.

Paper structure. The paper builds from defining the local monitoring protocol and the FDRP, deriving the renewal equation linking it to the Loschmidt echo, and then analyzing the resulting statistics in the limits of infrequent versus frequent measurements using generating functions.

Abstract

We study the interplay between local stroboscopic measurements and the unitary quantum circuit dynamics of the deterministic Floquet-quantum East model. We derive exact analytical results for the probability of first detecting a subset of qubits back in their initial state. This is achieved via a renewal equation linking the first-detection probability to the subsystem Loschmidt echo, which encodes the effective open dynamics of the monitored qubits. The resulting statistics exhibit two regimes set by the competition between the measurement probing time and the relaxation timescale of the unitary circuit dynamics: while infrequent monitoring leads to geometric first-detection statistics, frequent monitoring induces correlations that manifest as deviations from this geometric behavior. These results provide a rare exact characterization of how local measurement backaction reshapes detection statistics in an interacting quantum many-body system. In our local protocol, return probabilities are controlled by the size of the monitored subregion and can therefore be accessed on digital quantum simulators via local mid-circuit measurements.

Summary. This paper explores mixed-state quantum order, specifically SW-SSB, which cannot occur in pure states. By applying a long-range, power-law quantum channel to a 1D system, the authors construct a phase diagram exhibiting transitions between trivial, mixed SPT, and SW-SSB phases. The methodology combines advanced diagnostics like Rényi-2 measures with mappings to classical statistical mechanics, providing a deep theoretical understanding of environmentally induced quantum order.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the physics of open quantum systems and quantum phase transitions, showing how non-local environmental coupling (long-range channels) can stabilize exotic ordered phases inaccessible in local models.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the emergence and characterization of mixed-state quantum phases, such as Strong-to-Weak Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking (SW-SSB), which are fundamentally different from pure-state phenomena.

Main result. By introducing a long-range quantum channel with power-law decay, the authors construct a phase diagram hosting trivial, mixed SPT, and SW-SSB phases, demonstrating that long-range interactions can induce such mixed-state order in 1D.

Method. The analysis employs Rényi-2 diagnostics and involves mapping the quantum system dynamics to classical statistical mechanics models (like the Ising model) to analyze phase transitions.

Model / system. The system is modeled using a family of Matrix Product States (MPS) subjected to a long-range quantum channel implementing two-body dephasing whose probability decays according to a power-law in site separation.

Key observables. Rényi-2 diagnostics (e.g., Rényi-2 string order parameter, $C_{II}$), and the coherence information quantifying logical qubits.

Important parameters / regimes. The power-law exponent ($\alpha$), the channel strength ($p$), and the state parameter ($g$) which controls the interpolation between trivial and SPT phases.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the ability to tune the mixed states to high purity for experimental feasibility, and uses mappings to classical models for theoretical analysis.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the phase diagram showing transitions between different ordered phases, and plots show the scaling behavior of Rényi-2 correlators and the string order parameter as a function of the decoherence strength $p$.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the physical problem of mixed-state order, then details the construction of the model using MPS and power-law channels. It proceeds through theoretical analysis using Rényi-2 diagnostics, mapping to classical models, and finally presents numerical results confirming the phase transitions and scaling behavior.

Abstract

Open quantum systems can exhibit inherently mixed quantum orders, such as strong-to-weak spontaneous symmetry breaking (SW-SSB), which have no analogue in pure states. It is generally expected that a finite-depth local quantum channel cannot induce a phase transition to SW-SSB in one spatial dimension. We circumvent this obstruction by introducing a long-range quantum channel, which implements two-body dephasing on pairs of sites with probability falling off according to a power-law in the site separation. Applying this channel to a one-parameter family of matrix product states interpolating between trivial and symmetry-protected topological (SPT) phases, we construct a phase diagram that hosts trivial, mixed SPT, and SW-SSB phases with respect to Rényi-2 diagnostics. Notably, the mixed SPT phase is distinct from previously studied decohered SPT phases: it hosts two logical qubits and exhibits nonvanishing Rényi-2 string order, despite vanishing conventional string order. Through a series of mappings to a classical statistical mechanics model, we establish a direct connection between the loss of logical information and the onset of SW-SSB in this model. Finally, we consider the feasibility of experimental realization of the phenomena we have studied. We emphasize that our family of mixed states can be tuned to regimes of high purity, substantially reducing measurement overhead and enabling their experimental realization and detection on near-term quantum hardware.

Summary. This paper presents a novel protocol to learn the parameters of an unknown many-body Hamiltonian using measurements restricted to a single local qubit. This establishes a scalable route for characterizing complex quantum dynamics with minimal measurement access.

Main result. It demonstrates that a single measurable qubit is sufficient to learn all $O(N)$ parameters of a bounded-degree two-body Hamiltonian on $N$ qubits at the Heisenberg limit.

Method. The protocol combines parallel phase encoding with robust information transfer, utilizing synthesized SWAP gates to move state information from distant qubits to the local probe.

Important parameters / regimes. The scaling is characterized by achieving Heisenberg-limited precision ($\epsilon$) and a total query time of $e^{O(N)}$ for the chain, matching fundamental lower bounds.

Paper structure. Abstract

Hamiltonian learning provides a systematic framework for reconstructing unknown quantum dynamics. However, existing protocols typically assume direct measurement access to the entire system. With fast single-qubit control and a connected reference backbone, we show that a single measurable qubit suffices to learn all $O(N)$ independent parameters of a bounded-degree two-body Hamiltonian on $N$ qubits at the Heisenberg limit. Crucially, our protocol uses robust SWAP gates synthesized by quantum signal processing, enabling coherent transfer of states evolving under distant Hamiltonian parameters to the measurable qubit. This transfer requires no prior calibration of the Hamiltonian parameters of the intermediate links. A parallel learning architecture achieves total query time $\widetilde{O}(N)$ for an $N$-qubit chain, while retaining Heisenberg-limited precision scaling. On the chain, these scalings match the fundamental precision and information-propagation lower bounds up to logarithmic factors. The framework further extends to arbitrary bounded-degree interaction graphs. Our results establish a scalable route to learning an extensive number of many-body Hamiltonian parameters through only a local measurement interface.

Summary. This paper develops a unified message-passing framework to compute tensor network contractions, specifically reformulating the Corner Transfer Matrix Renormalization Group (CTMRG). By treating CTMRG as a message-passing algorithm on general planar graphs, it provides a systematic method that unifies previous, case-specific techniques. This advances the computational analysis of 2D statistical mechanics models on complex geometries.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical statistical mechanics, the message-passing formalism developed here (reconciling CTMRG and BP) provides a powerful, systematic computational tool applicable to quantum many-body systems described by tensor networks, which is relevant for simulating quantum Hamiltonians.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the computationally challenging problem of exactly contracting tensor networks defined on two-dimensional planar graphs.

Main result. It successfully formulates the Corner Transfer Matrix Renormalization Group (CTMRG) as a systematic message-passing algorithm analogous to Belief Propagation (BP) for general planar graphs.

Method. The method involves defining message tensors and 'corner' matrices that encode local information, establishing self-consistency equations for these tensors by ensuring consistency across shared faces.

Model / system. The theory is tested on the ferromagnetic Ising model and generalized to arbitrary planar graphs, including those with hyperbolic geometry.

Key observables. The partition function, correlation functions, and the message tensors/corner matrices themselves are the primary focus.

Important parameters / regimes. The virtual bond dimension ($\chi$) is a key parameter, with the Belief Propagation limit corresponding to $\chi=1$.

Assumptions / limitations. The derivation assumes the existence of a gauge condition for every polygon face to simplify the structure of the corner matrices.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the placement of tensors on vertices and dual graphs, define the message MPS environment, and show the derivation of the universal product form for the CTM equations and the biorthogonal projectors.

Paper structure. The paper builds by first establishing the tensor network setup, then introducing the message-passing framework using corner matrices, deriving the self-consistency equations for these matrices, and finally showing how these equations fully determine the message MPS.

Abstract

We formulate the corner transfer matrix equations, and the corner transfer matrix renormalization group built on them, as a set of local equations on general planar graphs. These equations depend only on the local subgraphs of the planar graph and are glued together consistently to describe the whole graph. Solving them yields message tensors and corner matrices, which can be used to construct an MPS environment for any connected subgraph. We test the proposed equations on the ferromagnetic Ising model, on a variety of infinite lattices (including hyperbolic ones) and on finite graphs, and find systematic improvement over belief propagation.

Summary. This work analyzes fermionic non-Gaussianity using the magic Rényi entropy (MRE) to characterize quantum states in many-body systems. The authors establish a universal upper bound for MRE and demonstrate that the MRE can detect a first-order phase transition in the SYK model that is not visible in the system's thermal free energy. This highlights MRE as a sensitive probe of quantum correlations beyond standard thermodynamic measures.

Main result. They prove that typical Haar-random states achieve a maximal MRE density of ln(4/3) per Majorana, and they identify a first-order phase transition in the MRE density for the SYK model that is invisible to the thermal free energy.

Method. The analysis uses the magic Rényi entropy (MRE) as a diagnostic tool, employing path integrals, saddle-point approximations, and solving Schwinger-Dyson equations.

Model / system. The study focuses on quantum many-body systems, specifically utilizing the Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev (SYK) Hamiltonian to model interacting dynamics, and analyzing purity measures for fermionic states.

Key observables. Magic Rényi entropy (MRE), Purity ($ u_2$), Universal Upper Bound, and the difference between annealed and quenched logarithmic averages.

Important parameters / regimes. Large-system limit (N->infinity), imaginary and real-time durations ($eta, t$), and the coupling strength J in the SYK model.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the phase diagram and the MRE density of the KM state, showing a kink marking a first-order transition.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the universal upper bound for MRE in random states, to analyzing the dynamics of the MRE in the SYK model, and finally deriving the necessary saddle-point equations to confirm the phase transition structure.

Abstract

Fermionic non-Gaussianity is a resource for universal quantum computation that can be generated by interactions in quantum many-body systems. Using the magic Rényi entropy (MRE) as a measure of non-Gaussianity, we derive its universal upper bound and Haar mean, proving that typical Haar-random states attain the maximal MRE density of $\ln(4/3)$ per Majorana in the large-system limit. We also show that the MRE can differ extensively between states with identical covariance matrices. To investigate how non-Gaussianity grows toward this maximal density, we evolve a Gaussian state in imaginary and real time under the Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev Hamiltonian. By varying the imaginary- and real-time durations, we identify a first-order transition marked by a kink in the MRE density and spontaneous breaking of the permutation symmetry among the four copies used to evaluate the MRE. This transition represents a qualitative change in the non-Gaussianity of the state that is not reflected in the thermal free energy.

Summary. This work analyzes mixed-state entanglement in spin systems possessing strong SU(2) symmetry, showing that thermal ordering transitions coincide with entanglement transitions. By developing a semiclassical theory, the authors prove that entanglement scaling changes dramatically between paramagnetic and ferromagnetic phases. This establishes a deep connection between the statistical mechanics of the system and its quantum information content.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a concrete, quantitative link between macroscopic thermodynamic phase transitions (ordering) and quantum information measures (entanglement) in the context of open quantum systems dynamics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how finite-temperature ordering transitions in strongly symmetric spin systems are directly linked to transitions in mixed-state entanglement.

Main result. The entanglement measures (distillable entanglement and logarithmic negativity) exhibit a transition in their system size scaling, moving from logarithmic to parametrically faster growth across the ferromagnetic phase transition.

Method. The authors combine establishing relations between entanglement and spin correlations with developing a semiclassical theory for the singlet sector, which is verified using Monte Carlo and exact numerical simulations.

Model / system. The system consists of locally interacting spin systems with ferromagnetic interactions, analyzed specifically within the total singlet sector ($ ext{SU}(2)$ symmetry). The analysis uses Gibbs states restricted to this singlet subspace.

Key observables. Mixed-state entanglement measures (distillable entanglement, logarithmic entanglement negativity) and spin correlations ($ ext{Tr}[ ho_0 \vec{S}_i \cdot \vec{S}_j]$).

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature ($T$), system size ($N$), and the ferromagnetic coupling strength.

Assumptions / limitations. The semiclassical theory is valid in the large $S$ limit, and the analysis relies on saddle-point approximations for path integrals.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the coincidence of thermal and entanglement transitions in the singlet sector, showing the scaling of the half-system spin ($\sim \sqrt{N}$ vs. $\sim N$) across the critical temperature $T_c$.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the connection between entanglement and correlations in the singlet sector, develops a semiclassical theory for the large $S$ limit, derives the scaling laws for entanglement measures, and finally confirms these predictions using numerical simulations and exact 1D calculations.

Abstract

We study mixed-state entanglement in Gibbs states with strong $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ symmetry, focusing on locally interacting spin systems with ferromagnetic interactions. Our main result is to show that finite-temperature ordering transitions are associated with entanglement transitions, and therefore the steady state of strongly symmetric Lindbladians can exhibit entanglement transitions. While in the paramagnetic phase it is known that the distillable entanglement and logarithmic entanglement negativity between two halves of a large system grow logarithmically with the square root of the number of spins, we show that in ferromagnetic phases these quantities grow parametrically faster with system size. To arrive at this result we first establish relations between these mixed-state entanglement measures and spin correlations in states that are singlets under global $\rm{SU}(2)$ symmetry transformations. We then introduce a semiclassical theory for $\rm{SU}(2)$ singlet thermal states. While the global singlet constraint generally enters this theory as a complicated function of the full semiclassical spin configuration, we show that in large $S$ limit it simplifies drastically to a Gaussian suppression of total magnetization in disordered phases as well as in ordered phases in the vicinity of continuous thermal phase transitions. This leads us to a field theory describing spin correlations in the singlet sector. Using this we determine the behavior of various probes of mixed-state entanglement at low and at high temperatures, supporting our analytical results using numerical Monte Carlo simulations of a three-dimensional lattice realization of our semiclassical theory. We also use exact numerics in one-dimensional spin-$1/2$ systems to confirm our predictions for the scaling of mixed-state entanglement with correlation length and system size in the paramagnetic phase.

Summary. This work introduces Pseudorandom Function-like Unitaries (PRFU) to efficiently generate entire families of unitaries mimicking true random quantum evolution. Abstract

Haar-random unitaries provide a canonical model of generic random quantum evolution, but they typically have exponential description and circuit complexity. Although pseudorandom unitaries efficiently emulate a single Haar-random unitary, many tasks require an entire reusable family of independently random-looking operations. We introduce pseudorandom function-like unitaries (PRFU), which generate such a family indexed by public labels using only a single short key. We distinguish classical- and coherent-label access and establish security under adaptive quantum queries. For classical labels, we provide a generic construction from a post-quantum pseudorandom function and a pseudorandom unitary. For coherent labels, we use an indexed path-recording framework to analyze interference across labels and prove security of a construction based on quantum-secure function and permutation primitives. We further show that PRFUs generate pseudorandom channels and quantum combs with private memory, which are secure against adaptive interventions and concurrent sessions. Combining PRFUs with unitary gluing yields a one-key family of pseudorandom unitaries whose supported register widths can be chosen after key generation. Further applications include nonce-resolved quantum authentication, coherently masked QRAM queries, and efficient emulation of random multi-time dynamics. Our results extend quantum pseudorandomness from individual operations to efficiently generated families of random-looking quantum dynamics.

Abstract

Non-ergodic quantum many-body dynamics offers a route to persistent quantum coherence far from equilibrium, beyond the conventional expectations of thermalizing statistical mechanics. Most known examples have been identified through intuition, analogy, or numerical search, rather than by systematic Hamiltonian design. This leaves few general methods for engineering robust non-ergodic dynamics in disorder-free interacting quantum many-body systems, especially in higher dimensions where enhanced connectivity generally disfavors ergodicity breaking. Here, we use an eigenstate-to-Hamiltonian construction approach to systematically engineer two-dimensional quantum spin Hamiltonians with tunable non-ergodic properties. Starting from a common parent model and input eigenstate, we construct two target Hamiltonians on the square lattice that differ in their interaction geometry: axial versus diagonal. We show that the resulting interaction geometry and coupling pattern control the Hilbert-space structure and dynamics. The axial model forms a connected Hilbert-space network and exhibits an anisotropy-driven crossover from ergodic to non-ergodic behavior through the suppression of resonances. In contrast, the diagonal model exhibits Hilbert-space fragmentation and, within its largest irreducible sector, signatures of quantum many-body scar dynamics including towers of highly localized, low-entanglement eigenstates, and long-lived coherent revivals. These results establish a direct link between Hamiltonian design, Hilbert-space network structure, and emergent non-ergodic dynamics.

Abstract

Abstract

Informational steady states (ISSs) arise when the rate at which information is gained from continuous measurements is exactly balanced by the rate at which information from earlier measurements is lost to the environment. Abstract

Many quantum systems are believed to thermalize slowly at low temperatures due to the existence of metastable phases. However, there are cases where further cooling can restore polynomial-time thermalization: we demonstrate that the Davies dynamics of the mean-field interchange model with local dimension $d\geq 3$ and single-site generalized Pauli couplings exhibits this behavior. The dynamical phase diagram is beholden to two distinguished inverse temperatures, $β_{\mathrm{min}}(d)$ and $β=d$, with the static first-order phase transition lying between them. At low and high temperatures surrounding the interval $(β_{\mathrm{min}}(d),d)$, the spectral gap of the Davies generator is bounded below by an inverse polynomial in system size $n$. Meanwhile, in this interval, the gap vanishes exponentially in $n$, indicating metastability. The proof uses Schur-Weyl duality to decompose the Davies generator into a classical Markov generator on Young diagrams, and the restriction to the remaining symmetry sectors whose gap is $Ω(n^{-1})$. Thus, exponential slowdown arises entirely from the Young diagram chain.
