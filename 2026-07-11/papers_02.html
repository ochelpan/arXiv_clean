Summary. This theoretical paper investigates whether a generalized 'quantum focusing' inequality holds for Rényi divergences in quantum field theory. By constructing a counterexample using specific divergence calculations, the authors prove that such an off-diagonal focusing statement does not hold universally for a broad class of information measures. This constrains the mathematical scope of generalizations of fundamental physical conjectures.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on QFT/Gravity analogies, the mathematical machinery involving divergences and information flow (like relative entropy generalizations) is highly relevant to understanding entanglement structure in many-body quantum systems far from equilibrium.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The authors investigate whether an off-diagonal quantum focusing inequality, analogous to those derived for diagonal Rényi divergences (RQNEC), holds universally for general Rényi-type divergences.

Main result. They show that for a broad class of Rényi divergences satisfying standard axioms, the required off-diagonal focusing statement fails in general, providing a negative answer to the question.

Method. The analysis constructs a counterexample using free quantum field theory on Minkowski spacetime and calculating the mixed variation ($\Delta_{12}D_\gamma$) of the Rényi divergence across different null generators.

Model / system. The model is a regulated Quantum Field Theory (QFT) setup involving spectator degrees of freedom and two active null pencils. The focus is on how entanglement/information divergences behave when considering correlations between distinct spatial regions.

Key observables. Off-diagonal variation $\Delta_{12}D^\gamma$, Rényi divergence $D_\gamma(\Psi\|\Omega)$, and the general structure of the focusing inequality.

Important parameters / regimes. The Rényi parameter $\gamma$ (or $\alpha$), which dictates the specific form of the divergence used in the analysis.

Assumptions / limitations. The results are restricted to divergences satisfying faithfulness, data processing inequality (DPI), tensor additivity, and matched classical-quantum conditioning rules.

Figures summary. Not specified by notes; the focus is on deriving algebraic counterexamples for the divergence variation.

Paper structure. The paper first reviews the context of quantum focusing conjectures, then develops calculations using specific block decompositions of states to compute the mixed variation $\Delta_{12}D_\gamma$ for various Rényi parameters, culminating in the explicit violation of the inequality.

Abstract

The quantum focusing conjecture is a mathematical expression of the idea that semiclassical gravity remains universally attractive. Its off-diagonal part is a monotonicity condition on the double null shape variation of relative entropy on distinct null generators, and has been argued to follow from strong subadditivity of entanglement entropy. Recent proof of a diagonal Rényi quantum null energy condition raises the question: does a full Rényi focusing statement also hold? We answer this question negatively for any Rényi-type divergence satisfying data processing, tensor additivity, and matched classical--quantum conditioning.

Summary. The paper develops a method to extract universal Conformal Field Theory data from real-time Loschmidt echoes following quantum quenches. By mapping the time evolution onto a boundary-CFT partition function, the authors show that ratios of spectral splittings encode fundamental CFT parameters like scaling dimensions and central charge. This provides a robust theoretical framework for analyzing out-of-equilibrium dynamics in critical systems.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides powerful theoretical tools to probe non-equilibrium quantum dynamics using measurable quantities (echoes) that bypass the need for complex state preparation, making it highly relevant for characterizing critical points in physical systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The goal is to develop a method to extract universal Conformal Field Theory (CFT) data from real-time quantum dynamics, specifically using Loschmidt echoes after critical quenches, without needing to prepare the difficult-to-access critical low-energy states.

Main result. Ratios of spectral splittings derived from finite-size dependence of Loschmidt echoes are shown to be universal and directly encode differences in boundary scaling dimensions ($\Delta_i - \Delta_j$), allowing for a practical extraction protocol.

Method. The analysis involves mapping the time evolution to a boundary-CFT partition function on a strip geometry, utilizing transverse transfer matrices whose spectra yield CFT data. Finite-size scaling protocols are developed by analyzing ratios of decay rates.

Model / system. The study focuses on one-dimensional quantum systems undergoing a quench from an initial product state $|\psi_0 angle$ to a critical Hamiltonian $H$. The dynamics are diagnosed using the Loschmidt amplitude, $A(T) = \langle\psi_0| e^{-iHT} |\psi_0 angle$, which is related to boundary-CFT partition functions.

Key observables. Loschmidt amplitude/echo ($L(T)$), transverse transfer matrix spectrum (eigenvalues $\lambda_i$), generalized temporal entropies, and ratios of dynamical exponents ($\Delta_{ii}/\Delta_{11}$).

Important parameters / regimes. System size dependence ($N_X$), quench time ($T$), central charge ($c$), and boundary scaling dimensions ($\xi$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on analytic continuation from imaginary time (Euclidean) to real time, and the extraction protocols assume that finite-size effects can be systematically removed through ratios.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the tensor network representation of the Loschmidt amplitude via transverse contraction. Plots show linear relationships between logarithms of observables versus scaled time/temperature, confirming power-law decays predicted by CFT theory (e.g., slope $-1/8$).

Paper structure. The paper builds from establishing the connection between Loschmidt echoes and boundary-CFT partition functions on a strip. It then details how spectral properties of the transverse transfer matrix encode universal data like central charge and scaling dimensions, culminating in proposing finite-size scaling protocols for experimental/simulation extraction.

Abstract

Conformal field theory provides universal predictions for Loschmidt amplitudes following quenches from product states to critical Hamiltonians. Building on this observation, we develop a route to extracting conformal data from real-time dynamics without preparing critical low-energy states. After analytic continuation, the Loschmidt amplitude is described by a boundary-CFT partition function on a strip, whose transverse transfer matrix encodes both the boundary operator spectrum and the central charge. Local space-time perturbations of the amplitude are governed by equilibrium correlation functions, and therefore provide access to critical exponents. In parallel, generalized temporal entropies exhibit scaling with time analogous to the equilibrium scaling of spatial entanglement entropy. We show that the low-lying boundary spectrum can be reconstructed from the system-size dependence of finite-chain Loschmidt echoes, whose damped oscillations encode differences of boundary scaling dimensions. Finally, we propose a finite-size scaling protocol that can extract these quantities from simulations or experiments on state-of-the-art quantum platforms.

Summary. This paper develops an efficient Monte Carlo simulation technique using the NKSOS model to study the multichannel Kondo effect. It achieves a unified description, successfully modeling the transition between weak and strong electron-impurity coupling regimes. This work provides crucial quantitative predictions for transport properties in complex correlated quantum devices.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous numerical treatment of crossover physics in a highly correlated impurity model provides advanced computational techniques applicable to open quantum systems where tunneling barriers or coupling strengths vary widely.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The central goal is establishing a unified theoretical description that connects different coupling regimes (weak vs. strong) for the Kondo effect, which is crucial for understanding strongly correlated electron systems.

Main result. The authors successfully use their model to describe the crossover between weak and strong tunneling regimes in the multichannel Kondo problem, predicting universal low-frequency conductance values.

Method. They employ cluster Monte Carlo algorithms on a discrete N-channel Kondo solid-on-solid (NKSOS) model, which efficiently simulates dynamics while eliminating critical slowing down.

Model / system. The study focuses on the multichannel Kondo model using the NKSOS model, derived from an anisotropic Kondo Hamiltonian. This system generalizes to arbitrary channel numbers ($N$) and incorporates features like anisotropy and interacting leads.

Important parameters / regimes. Number of channels ($N$), temperature ($T$), and various coupling strengths defining the weak/strong tunneling limits.

Assumptions / limitations. The initial assumption of identical microscopic transparencies for all channels is noted as a potential limitation, though the method handles general asymmetry terms.

Figures summary. Figures compare simulated conductance values (e.g., 2CK and 3CK) against established theoretical solutions (like Emery-Kivelson), illustrating universal scaling and RG flow in parameter space.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the NKSOS model, details the cluster Monte Carlo simulation method to calculate transport properties, presents results showing agreement with known limits (1CK, 2CK), and finally extends the analysis to higher channel numbers ($N \geq 3$) to unify weak and strong coupling descriptions.

Abstract

The Kondo model is a paradigmatic quantum impurity problem realized in a wide variety of experimental platforms and central to the study of strongly correlated electrons. We introduce a discrete model that exactly reproduces the multichannel Kondo model and demonstrate that it can be simulated efficiently. Using cluster Monte Carlo algorithms, we completely eliminate critical slowing down, providing direct access to universal crossover functions and transport properties across a broad range of parameters. Remarkably, the same model captures both the weak- and strong-coupling regimes, unifying descriptions traditionally derived in complementary limits and revealing their common origin. Our method naturally accommodates large channel numbers, anisotropy, interacting one-dimensional leads, and channel asymmetry, yielding predictions for transport properties in charge-Kondo devices.

Method. The research combines global structure prediction algorithms with first-principles calculations (DFT+U) to determine structural stability and magnetic coupling constants.

Model / system. The system under investigation is the Be–Ag(II)–F chemical space, specifically focusing on various polymorphs of AgBeF4. The magnetic interactions are modeled using a Heisenberg Hamiltonian approach based on calculated superexchange couplings (J).

Abstract

The search for novel silver(II) fluorides is driven by their potential as electronic and magnetic analogues to high temperature cuprate(II) superconductor precursors. Here, we explore the previously uncharted Be Ag(II) F chemical space using global structure prediction algorithms combined with first principles calculations. Focusing on the AgBeF4 stoichiometry, we identify the five lowest enthalpy polymorphs crystallizing in the C2, P minus 1, and P 21/c space groups. All polymorphs show an antiferromagnetic ground state, with AgBeF4_4 and AgBeF4_5 exhibiting unprecedented strong superexchange interactions of J equal circa to minus 460meV and J equal circa to minus 359meV respectively. Those high J values are due to the presence of either [Ag2F7] for AgBeF4_4, or related infinite [AgF2/2+2/1]2 minus chains for AgBeF4_5. Although the phases are found to be metastable with respect to binary difluorides, the thermodynamic analysis suggests that they could be targeted via synthetic routes employing fluorine radicals, with reaction enthalpies reaching minus 370 kJ/mol.

Summary. This work rigorously connects the concept of measurement incompatibility in quantum measurements to the ability to generate secret random bits. By formulating security bounds using semidefinite programming based on generalized robustness, the authors provide a powerful tool for quantifying device-independent randomness generation protocols.

Why it may be interesting. It provides rigorous, information-theoretic tools (SDPs) to quantify fundamental resource relations—namely, how much non-classicality (incompatibility) can be extracted into usable randomness against an adversary.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper establishes a quantitative trade-off between the observed measurement incompatibility of quantum measurements and the capabilities of an eavesdropper (Eve) in a prepare-and-measure setting.

Main result. Measurement incompatibility is shown to be equivalent to the security against Eve's knowledge, providing a direct link between geometric measures of incompatibility and randomness generation protocols.

Method. The authors utilize semidefinite programming (SDP) formulations involving generalized robustness witnesses to bound Eve's guessing probability ($P_g$) based on the joint measurability of Bob's measurements.

Model / system. The model is a prepare-and-measure quantum communication scenario where an eavesdropper intercepts the message and performs operations described by instruments, analyzed using POVMs and concepts like quantum steering.

Key observables. Measurement Incompatibility (quantified by generalized robustness), Eve's guessing probability ($P_g$), and the minimum entropy $H_{\min}$.

Important parameters / regimes. The rank of the input state ($\varrho$) is crucial; the analysis extends to scenarios with quantum memory for Eve.

Assumptions / limitations. Initial results rely on assuming full-rank input states, and later extensions involve bounding violations against qubit simulability.

Figures summary. Figure 2 illustrates the quantitative trade-off between Eve's guessing probability and observed incompatibility robustness for various measurement settings (MUBs).

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the core equivalence (JM $\iff$ security) in simple cases, to developing SDP bounds using generalized robustness, extending this to quantum steering, and finally proving the mathematical equivalence between the initial SDP formulation and a steering-based SDP.

Abstract

We present a trade-off between the amount of observed measurement incompatibility and the capabilities of a classical Eavesdropper in a prepare-and-measure scenario. The result is based on a qualitative connection between measurement incompatibility and randomness generation together with the utilization of incompatibility witnesses as randomness certificates. This allows one to use a geometric measure of incompatibility, the generalised robustness, to bound Eve's strategies through a semi-definite program, while providing an explicit protocol for generating randomness from any set of incompatible measurements. By translating the result to quantum steering, we find a tight connection between steerability and randomness generation in a setting using any finite number of measurement inputs. We further show how our techniques can be generalised to scenarios where Eve has a quantum memory by using a dimensional generalisation of joint measurability.

Summary. The paper introduces Code-Compiled Quantum Circuits (CCQCs) that allow the construction of deep quantum circuits exhibiting signs of classical hardness. By mapping these complex logical operations onto simple physical interactions using error-correcting codes, they show that simulation cost can be bounded by the encoder's bond dimension rather than entanglement growth. This provides a powerful theoretical benchmark for assessing the capabilities and limitations of near-term quantum hardware.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful theoretical tool to benchmark quantum hardware by creating complex logical circuits whose ideal reference state is efficiently computable classically, which is crucial for testing fault tolerance in AMO or quantum optics settings.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The goal is to determine when a quantum circuit becomes classically hard to simulate, challenging existing resource measures that define simulation boundaries.

Main result. The authors demonstrate that certain code-compiled circuits can exhibit indicators of classical hardness (high entanglement, magic) while remaining efficiently simulable via Matrix Product States (MPS). This shows standard resource measures are individually insufficient to predict simulation difficulty for some circuit families.

Method. They construct Code-Compiled Quantum Circuits (CCQCs) using high-rate CSS codes and simulate them by exploiting structural properties: diagonal transversal gates preserve the MPS bond dimension, and permutations are tracked classically. They use both MPS simulation and an exact phase-polynomial backend.

Model / system. The model involves mapping complex logical quantum circuits onto physical qubits via error-correcting codes (specifically polar CSS codes). The resulting physical evolution is shown to be equivalent to a simple, onsite interaction structure after encoding.

Key observables. Entanglement entropy, magic content, non-Gaussianity, and the MPS bond dimension ($\chi_E$).

Important parameters / regimes. The peak bond dimension of the encoder ($\chi_E$), which controls the simulation cost regardless of circuit depth for certain codes.

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes that the encoded state retains an efficient MPS representation, and the structural properties regarding diagonal transversal gates and classical permutation tracking hold.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the code-compilation framework, showing how logical nonlocal gates are compiled into simple onsite physical operations. Specific examples use the $[[16, 11]]$ polar code to show matched pairs of complex logical circuits versus their physically simulated counterparts.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the CCQC concept, details the simulation mechanism relying on structural properties (diagonal gates/relabeling), presents two complementary simulation backends (MPS and PhasePoly), demonstrates results using the infinite polar CSS code family, and concludes with a near-term application for fidelity estimation.

Abstract

We introduce a family of quantum circuits that possess standard indicators of classical simulation hardness including high entanglement entropy, magic, and non-Gaussianity, yet admit efficient classical simulation via matrix product states (MPS). Our construction uses logical circuits of high-rate Calderbank-Shor-Steane (CSS) codes with enhanced symmetries. Using code automorphisms and transversal diagonal gates from higher levels of the Clifford hierarchy, we realize nonlocal logical Clifford and non-Clifford gates, showing how error-correcting codes can compile complex logical circuits into simple physical operations. Simulation efficiency rests on two properties: (i) diagonal transversal gates do not increase bond dimension, and (ii) permutations are tracked classically via on-the-fly relabeling, avoiding costly SWAP networks. Unlike Clifford or matchgate simulation, our method accepts a broad class of initial states, including dense entangled, magic, and non-Gaussian inputs, provided the encoded state retains an efficient MPS representation. We also release an exact phase-polynomial backend for monomial subfamilies, whose cost is set by higher-degree phase terms rather than entanglement growth. We demonstrate the method on an infinite polar CSS code family, showing bond dimension stays bounded by the encoding cost regardless of circuit depth. These results show that for some circuit families, standard resource measures are individually insufficient to indicate simulation hardness. As a near-term application, we use the compiled MPS as a classical reference for direct fidelity estimation of a quantum device running nontrivial logical circuits. Pauli sampling on the encoded reference, with a Clifford pushback through the known encoder, provides the ideal expectation values, so the logical output fidelity can be estimated from local Pauli readout alone, without costly state tomography.

Summary. This work establishes 'correlator geometry' as a theoretical tool for analyzing families of higher-order correlators in quantum many-body physics. By decomposing the geometry into reducible and irreducible parts, it quantifies genuinely novel information encoded in the system dynamics. This framework offers a systematic way to interpret complex data from programmable quantum simulators.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a powerful, systematic geometric language to interpret complex, high-dimensional data generated by modern quantum simulators studying strongly correlated systems, moving beyond simple correlation functions.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Developing a structured framework for the collective analysis of higher-order correlator families, as these are increasingly accessible from quantum simulators but their physical meaning is difficult to infer due to redundancy and interdependence.

Main result. The authors introduce 'correlator geometry' which allows quantifying irreducible information via volume profiles, revealing hidden structures in many-body dynamics inaccessible from individual measurements.

Method. The framework represents correlators as inner products between operator words in an abstract 'operator space,' decomposing the resulting geometry using conditioning subspaces to separate reducible and irreducible components.

Model / system. The theory is applied to general quantum many-body systems, analyzing families of higher-order out-of-time-ordered correlators (OTOCs) relevant for studying dynamics in condensed matter contexts like spin chains or lattice models.

Key observables. Correlator families, irreducible volume profiles ($\pi_q$), Gram matrix determinant ($\det G(\Omega)$), and the residual geometry $Q(W)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The conditioning rank ($r$) used to define the resolved sector; system size ($L$); and comparison between different dynamical regimes (e.g., free-fermion vs. chaotic).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that correlator families can be systematically organized into a geometric structure amenable to subspace decomposition via conditioning.

Figures summary. Figures schematically illustrate the construction of correlator families as inner products and demonstrate the decomposition of the geometry into reducible (explained) and irreducible (residual) components.

Paper structure. The paper develops the formalism by first defining the geometric representation using the operator Gram matrix, then introducing various conditioning schemes (canonical, targeted, Krylov/cross), and finally applying this framework to diagnose differences between known quantum dynamics regimes.

Abstract

Programmable quantum simulators are beginning to access correlators of increasing complexity, ranging from four-point out-of-time-ordered correlators to even higher-order many-body correlators. The theoretical framework for interpreting such data, however, remains comparatively underdeveloped. Although a variety of higher-order correlators can be constructed straightforwardly, their physical meaning is often difficult to infer. A further complication is that different correlators are generally not independent: some may be mutually redundant, while others may encode genuinely distinct information. These features make it necessary to analyze correlators not as isolated quantities, but as a structured family. In this work, we develop a geometric framework for the collective analysis of higher-order correlator families. By representing correlators as inner products between operator words, we recast each family as a geometry in operator space. The key idea is to introduce conditioning subspaces that separate this geometry into reducible information, already explained by a chosen resolved sector, and irreducible information, encoded in the residual correlator geometry. Focusing on the latter component, we define irreducible volume profiles that quantify how broadly the unexplained part of a correlator family spreads over independent geometric directions. This perspective leads to several complementary forms of conditioning. Canonical conditioning optimally explains a correlator family. Targeted conditioning fixes the resolved sector to isolate a chosen physical feature. Krylov and cross conditioning extend the framework from a single correlator family to comparisons among correlator geometries. Our framework reveals irreducible structures hidden at the level of individual correlator values and establishes correlator geometry as a higher-level description of quantum many-body dynamics.

Abstract

Bulk reconstruction is a central problem in AdS/CFT, and entanglement wedge reconstruction is its subregion version. We argue that this subregion statement should be separated from the stronger holographic quantum error correction interpretation, in which one region-independent logical bulk operator has code-preserving representatives in several boundary regions. A simple locality argument shows that such a common reconstruction must commute with the code-preserving local algebras in the complementary regions. This is the mechanism realized in HaPPY-type codes: the erased regions are blind to a protected logical algebra. An ordinary finite $N$ holographic CFT does not have such a protected invisible sector for supergravity fields. Its low-energy local observables, in particular, suitably smeared stress tensors, detect the physical support and gravitational dressing of ordinary bulk operators, up to possible center or superselection data. Thus, there is no such holographic quantum error correction and the $N=\infty$ agreement of global and subregion HKLL formulae is a free-theory statement. What remains is entanglement wedge reconstruction without holographic quantum error correction, or subregion complementarity: each boundary region has its own code-preserving low-energy algebra and its own region-adapted bulk interpretation, rather than a shared logical operator.

Abstract

Abstract

Non-local magic has recently emerged as a fundamental resource for characterizing genuinely non-local non-stabilizer correlations. However, its direct calculation is an intractable numerical problem, except for small systems, and its understanding remains limited. We derive a representation of non-local magic in terms of the Walsh--Hadamard autocorrelations of the entanglement spectrum. Our representation makes the underlying harmonic structure explicit and enables a systematic analysis of its behaviors for various scenarios. We prove that non-local magic can be upper-bounded by an entanglement entropy and we derive exact analytical results for broad classes of quantum states, characterizing the scaling of non-local magic for volume-law states, as well as ground states of one-dimensional gapped and critical systems. Our results identify the spectral organization of the entanglement spectrum as the key ingredient governing non-local magic and provide a framework for further systematic analytical investigation.
