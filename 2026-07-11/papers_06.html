Summary. This paper provides an exact solution for the free energy of the Ising model on random regular graphs using advanced analytic combinatorics. It rigorously proves that the system undergoes a second-order phase transition at a specific critical temperature, confirming mean-field critical exponents. The methodology involves mapping the partition function to counting weighted edge-bicolored graphs in the thermodynamic limit.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical statistical mechanics, the rigorous mathematical tools developed—analytic combinatorics applied to large random structures—are highly relevant for analyzing complex quantum many-body systems where exact enumeration is intractable.

Abstract

We give an exact solution of the ferromagnetic Ising model on a random regular graph ensemble via analytic combinatorics. A simple analysis of the resulting formula reveals a second-order phase transition with critical exponents of the mean-field universality class.

Abstract

Although quantum key distribution (QKD) enables information-theoretically secure key distribution, it is mainly designed for point-to-point communication and cannot directly support multi-user collaborative scenarios. To address this limitation, quantum secret sharing (QSS) has been proposed to enable secure multiparty key sharing. However, most existing QSS protocols rely on a single-input single-output (SISO) channel, which limits the achievable secret key rate (SKR) and transmission distance. This paper proposes a continuous-variable (CV) QSS protocol based on a multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) architecture operating in the terahertz (THz) band. In the proposed scheme, transmit-receive beamforming decomposes the MIMO channel into multiple parallel SISO subchannels, thereby improving both the SKR and transmission distance. We describe the QSS transmission procedure and derive the SKR expressions for eight protocol variants under Gaussian collective attacks. Specifically, Gaussian modulation and passive modulation are considered at the transmitter, while homodyne and heterodyne detection are considered at the receiver. Both asymptotic and composable finite-size SKR formulas are derived to characterize the ideal upper-bound performance and the achievable performance under finite resources, respectively. Simulation results show that, under ideal assumptions including perfect channel state information, perfect phase synchronization, and ideal beamforming, the Gaussian-modulation protocol with a 32 x 32 antenna configuration and the passive-modulation protocol with a 1024 x 1024 antenna configuration achieve transmission distances of 14.99 m and 160 m in the atmospheric channel, respectively. These results provide an idealized theoretical benchmark for evaluating the potential performance gains of MIMO-assisted THz CV-QSS in indoor and short-range outdoor wireless networks.

Abstract

The results demonstrate that EQC provides a practical quantum-ready framework for secure and privacy-preserving clustering across heterogeneous sensitive datasets.

Summary. This work presents a novel quantum simulation technique, ADAPT-SSVQE, designed to solve the intractable problem of diagonalizing large nuclear shell model Hamiltonians. By mapping the fermionic Hamiltonian to qubits and using an adaptive variational approach, the authors can simultaneously find multiple excited states for small nucleon systems with high accuracy. This demonstrates a powerful pathway toward simulating complex quantum many-body physics beyond classical computational limits.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on nuclear physics, the core methodology—using advanced quantum algorithms like ADAPT-SSVQE to tackle exponentially hard many-body problems while enforcing symmetries—is directly transferable to simulating strongly correlated electron systems or complex molecular Hamiltonians in AMO physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is overcoming the exponential dimensionality of the nuclear shell model Hamiltonian diagonalization, which exceeds classical computational capacity.

Main result. The authors successfully demonstrated a quantum simulation approach that simultaneously extracts multiple orthogonal eigenstates for two and three nucleons with spectroscopic accuracy relative to exact diagonalization.

Method. They employ an ADAPT-SSVQE algorithm, combining the Subspace Search VQE with the ADAPT ansatz, applied after mapping the fermionic Hamiltonian using the Jordan-Wigner transformation.

Model / system. The system is based on the nuclear shell model Hamiltonian ($\hat{H}_{ ext{eff}}$) describing protons and neutrons interacting via a residual interaction (e.g., JUN45). The problem is mapped onto qubits while preserving fermionic anti-commutation relations.

Key observables. Simultaneous convergence of multiple mutually orthogonal eigenstates, restoration of total angular momentum ($\hat{J}^2$) symmetry, and energy eigenvalues compared to exact diagonalization.

Important parameters / regimes. The simulation was benchmarked for two and three identical nucleons in a $j = 9/2$ orbital within a 10-qubit active space.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary limitation addressed is the exponential scaling of the Hilbert space; the method assumes quantum computation can scale beyond classical limits, though circuit depth remains a concern for NISQ devices.

Figures summary. Figures track energy convergence and relative error over simulation iterations for two and three nucleons. They also show the growth of CNOT gate count, illustrating the increasing complexity of the required quantum circuits.

Paper structure. The paper details the theoretical framework by mapping the fermionic Hamiltonian to qubits (JW transformation). It then introduces and implements the ADAPT-SSVQE algorithm for simultaneous state extraction, culminating in benchmarking results for 2 and 3 nucleons showing convergence and symmetry restoration.

Abstract

The nuclear shell model is a general way of expressing the many-body nuclear Hamiltonian and deciphering the underlying nuclear structure. In today's era of modern and high-power computation, the primary limitation of the nuclear shell model is the enormous dimensionality of its Hilbert space, which far exceeds available storage capacity and prevents the diagonalization of the full Hamiltonian matrix in that space. Quantum computing offers a scalable solution to bypass this curse of dimensionality. In this work, we introduce a single-run quantum simulation capable of obtaining multiple shell-model eigenstates simultaneously. The nuclear Hamiltonian is transformed from a bit to a qubit basis using the Jordan-Wigner transformation, explicitly preserving fermionic anti-commutation. We employ a Subspace Search Variational Quantum Eigensolver (SSVQE) along with an Adaptive Derivative-Assembled Pseudo-Trotter (ADAPT) ansatz to construct the quantum circuit required to solve the shell-model problem. The ADAPT-SSVQE algorithm uses a symmetry-preserving single and double-excitation operator pool and optimizes a weighted energy sum to obtain the simultaneous convergence of all eigenstates within a targeted MJ subspace, eliminating the need for post-processing efforts to extract excited spectra. We benchmark this approach by solving the problem for two and three identical nucleons in a j = 9/2 orbital, successfully extracting five and ten mutually orthogonal states, respectively, within a 10-qubit active space. The algorithm achieves spectroscopic accuracy, in simulation, relative to exact diagonalization and intrinsically restores total angular momentum (\hat{J}^2) symmetry.

Summary. This theoretical paper establishes a powerful 'twofold universality' result for integrals involving large-$N$ melonic random tensors. It shows that these complex tensor integrals simplify significantly at large $N$, depending only on simple polynomial functions of the graph structure, regardless of the specific details of the interaction or dimension. This provides a universal framework for calculating correlation functions in high-dimensional statistical models.

Why it may be interesting. The concept of universal behavior emerging from complex combinatorial structures (tensors/graphs) at large $N$ is highly relevant in statistical mechanics, particularly when studying critical phenomena or random matrix theories generalized to higher dimensions.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The authors investigate constructing a measure exhibiting 'twofold universality' for integrating large-$N$ random tensors, which are generalizations of matrix integrals.

Main result. They prove that these tensor integrals simplify dramatically at large $N$, becoming independent of intricate combinatorics and the dimensionality $D$ (for $D\geq 3$), depending only on 'melonic polynomials'.

Method. The analysis involves computing connected correlation functions using Wick contractions ($\pi$) over graph structures, establishing uniqueness theorems for maximal contractions, and comparing cumulants across different theoretical models.

Model / system. The model is based on random tensors $T_{a_1,\ldots,a_D}$ integrated with respect to a Gaussian measure. The complexity arises from the need to contract indices using graph structures called 'melonic graphs' ($B$), which are analogues of matrix traces.

Key observables. Melonic polynomials (functions indexed by $n$ related to vertex counts), and the large-$N$ limit of tensor cumulants $\langle B_1 \dots B_n angle$.

Important parameters / regimes. Large $N$, dimensionality $D\geq 3$, and coupling constants ($g_i$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the tensors are 'melonic' (satisfying a recursive construction) and relies on proving convergence of ratios of cumulants to establish universality.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining the problem (tensor integration), establishing tools like maximal Wick contractions ($\pi_{ ext{max}}$) via graph theory, proving universality theorems (Theorem 4.2 and 5.1), and finally applying these results to confirm the 'twofold universality' of the large-$N$ limit.

Abstract

We construct a measure that exhibits two aspects of a new type of universality and dramatically simplifies the integration of tensors $T_{a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_D} \in \mathbb{C}$ ($a_1,\ldots,a_D=1,\ldots,N$) at large $N$. In contrast to matrix integration, in which matrix traces canonically yield the integrand, tensors need additional information (equivalent to a $D$-coloured graph $B$) to contract their indices and form a tensor trace $B(T)$. We show that, whenever each $B_1,\ldots, B_n$ can be obtained by a recursive construction known as melonicity, then the leading order in $N$ of the integral of $ {B_1}(T) {B_2}(T) \cdots {B_n}(T) $ is independent of the -- often intricate -- combinatorics of the traces $B_i$, but also, to our surprise, independent of $D$ as far as $D\geq 3$. Instead, at large $N$, these integrals are some functions (indexed by $n$) of the number of vertices $2p_i$ of $B_i$ which we call melonic polynomials. Melonic traces cumulants with respect to any ('interacting') measure \[ \exp\Big\{-N^{D-1} \sum_{i=1}^m g_i {B_i}(T)\Big\} \mathrm{d}μ_0(T) \quad (g_1,\ldots,g_m \in \mathbb{R}, \mathrm{d}μ_0(T) =\text{the tensor Gaussian}) \] with each $B_i$ melonic, can be computed with our universal measure that replaces each $B_i$ by a canonical trace depending only on $p_i$. We prove that any two melonic tensor models are indistinguishable at large-$N$, independently of the number of tensor indices (first universality aspect), and of the fine-grainedness of their interactions (second universality), being a sufficient condition that the couplings (the parameters $g_i$ above) agree and their respective traces are monomials with the same degree in $T$.

Summary. This work proposes that Two-Level Systems (TLS) responsible for qubit decoherence can originate from collective electronic excitations called Bogolyubov excitons. These excitons form due to repulsive interactions between quasiparticles in superconductors, particularly near boundaries or interfaces. The key prediction is the observable signature of these excitons through avoided crossings when coupled to an external microwave resonator.

Why it may be interesting. The prediction of an intrinsic, defect-free source for TLS in superconductors provides a novel mechanism relevant to superconducting quantum circuits and open quantum systems research.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The microscopic origin of Two-Level Systems (TLS), which cause decoherence in superconducting qubits, is unresolved.

Main result. The authors propose that TLS can arise intrinsically from subgap bound states of Bogolyubov quasiparticles (Bogolyubov excitons) formed by repulsive electron interactions within superconductors.

Method. Theoretical analysis using Green's functions and solving the Bethe-Salpeter equation to determine collective excitation spectra, followed by modeling coupling to external resonators.

Model / system. The model considers a conventional superconductor interacting with an external metallic layer or boundary inhomogeneity. The physics is described via superconducting gap parameters ($\Delta$) and electron-electron interaction terms ($V_q$).

Key observables. Avoided crossings in the spectral function when coupling excitons to an external resonator, and subgap resonance poles ($\omega_{ex}$) below the quasiparticle continuum.

Important parameters / regimes. Interaction strength (repulsive vs. attractive), superconducting gap ($\Delta$), proximity coupling strength, and resonator frequency ($\omega_{res}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on mean-field approximations and linear response theory; it assumes that the exciton resonance can be tuned into the microwave window via boundary effects.

Abstract

Two-level systems (TLS) are a leading source of decoherence and dielectric loss in Josephson-junction qubits, yet their microscopic origin remains unresolved. We propose an intrinsic, purely electronic TLS candidate: subgap bound states of Bogolyubov quasiparticles. We show that, in a model of a conventional superconductor with electron-electron attraction in the $s$-wave channel and repulsion in higher-angular-momentum channels, the latter produce an effective attraction between Bogolyubov quasiparticles, forming bound states. These Bogolyubov excitons resemble Bardasis--Schrieffer excitons but are driven by repulsive interactions. Bulk Bogolyubov excitons are not easily detectable through single-particle tunneling or other conventional probes, but we show that surface excitons couple to an external electric field and behave as two-level systems. We examine an idealized model of a bulk superconductor proximity-coupled to a two-dimensional repulsive metal, which could represent an external metallic or semiconductor layer or an underscreened region of the superconductor. The two levels correspond to the absence and presence of a single exciton, which carries an electric dipole moment and exhibits an avoided crossing with a resonator, as observed for TLS. Because this mechanism requires no defects and cannot be eliminated by screening or annealing, it suggests that TLS-like excitations may be intrinsic to superconductors.

Summary. This theoretical work examines disorder-induced loop currents in high-Tc superconductors by incorporating strong electronic correlations using the Gutzwiller Approximation. The research compares these results against simpler Hartree-Fock treatments, finding that while correlations generally suppress currents, GA allows for stable superconducting states under stronger repulsive interactions than HF. This provides insights into local magnetism arising from impurities in cuprates.

Abstract

Time-reversal symmetry breaking in superconducting systems can manifest itself in the form of currents which are induced by inhomogeneities in the charge and order parameter distribution. With regard to cuprates such states have been theoretically studied in the overdoped region of the phase diagram where the inhomogeneities are related to out-of plane dopants. In this paper we will extend previous work by including local correlations within the unrestricted Gutzwiller approximation in order to study its impact on the induced loop currents. In addition, we investigate the effect of next-nearest neighbor hopping which extends the TRSB phase towards half-filling. We find that in general correlations lead to a suppression of loop currents, however, the Gutzwiller approach can sustain such states to larger values of the local on-site repulsion as compared to the Hartree-Fock approximation. Our investigations allow for an estimation of the local magnetic moment emerging from impurity-induced loop currents for cuprate superconductors.

Summary. This work experimentally proves that spin-wave beams reflecting off a waveguide edge do not obey Snell's law. Instead, the reflection mechanism is dictated by transitions between caustic points on the material's anisotropic dispersion relation. This finding establishes a new physical law for magnonic beam steering and wave control.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on magnonics, the concept of anomalous wave reflection governed by geometric features (caustics) rather than simple conservation laws has parallels in advanced optics and wave physics relevant to open quantum systems modeling.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To demonstrate that caustic spin-wave beams reflect at an interface via a mechanism fundamentally different from conventional momentum conservation (Snell's law).

Main result. Reflection is governed by transitions between distinct caustic points on the anisotropic iso-frequency contour, leading to beam characteristics incompatible with Snell's law.

Method. Time-resolved Kerr microscopy (TR-MOKE) was used experimentally to image and measure reflected spin-wave beams while systematically varying an external magnetic field.

Model / system. The study uses a Yttrium Iron Garnet (YIG) film waveguide patterned on a GGG substrate, where spin waves propagate in an anisotropic medium.

Key observables. Reflected beam wave vector ($k_e$), wavefront orientation ($\phi_e$), and the comparison of these parameters against Snell's law predictions.

Important parameters / regimes. External magnetic field magnitude ($\mu_0H$) and angle ($\alpha_H$); the anisotropy of the material dispersion relation.

Assumptions / limitations. The reflection process is assumed to be dictated by the geometry of caustics rather than simple momentum conservation across the boundary.

Figures summary. Figures show TR-MOKE images of reflections at waveguide edges as a function of magnetic field strength and angle, comparing observed beam parameters against theoretical predictions based on both Snell's law and caustic transitions.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the problem by contrasting conventional wave reflection with spin waves. It then presents experimental measurements using TR-MOKE to observe reflections at waveguide edges under varying external magnetic fields, systematically demonstrating deviations from Snell's law in favor of a caustic transition mechanism.

Abstract

Reflection of waves at interfaces is conventionally governed by Snell's law, which follows from conservation of momentum parallel to the interface. Here we show experimentally that caustic spin-wave beams in anisotropic media obey a fundamentally different reflection mechanism. Applying time-resolved Kerr microscopy to a yttrium iron garnet waveguide, we observe that reflected beams are selected by transitions between caustic points on the anisotropic iso-frequency contour rather than by momentum conservation. As a consequence, the reflected carrier wave vector and wavefront orientation exhibit trends opposite to those predicted by Snell's law. By tuning the magnitude and orientation of an external magnetic field, we continuously control the resulting reflection process and beam routing. Our results establish caustic-point transitions as a distinct reflection law for anisotropic wave beams and provide a route towards reconfigurable magnonic beam steering.
