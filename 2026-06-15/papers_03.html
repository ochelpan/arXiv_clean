Summary. This theoretical paper calculates the rate at which a quantum superposition decays due to coupling with a scalar field near black holes. By analyzing different vacuum states—Boulware, Unruh, and Hartle-Hawking—the authors show that only thermal effects associated with Hawking radiation induce measurable decoherence. This demonstrates the profound dependence of quantum coherence on the chosen description of the spacetime environment.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete calculation of decoherence in curved spacetime environments, linking fundamental concepts like Hawking radiation and quantum field theory in curved spacetime directly to open quantum system dynamics (decoherence).

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how quantum spatial superpositions of a particle decohere when coupled to a quantum scalar field in the curved spacetime backgrounds of static black holes (Schwarzschild and Reissner-Nordström).

Main result. Decoherence is found to vanish in the Boulware vacuum but is induced by thermal effects associated with Hawking radiation in Unruh and Hartle-Hawking vacua, yielding a non-zero rate even for extremal black holes.

Method. The decoherence functional is derived locally using coherent state generation techniques, relating the rate to the low-frequency behavior of the Wightman function calculated via asymptotic matching methods.

Model / system. The system models a quantum superposition of a static particle coupled to a real scalar field ($\Phi$) in curved spacetime. The analysis uses the interaction Hamiltonian derived from the coupling $S_{int} = \lambda \int d au \Phi(x( au))$.

Key observables. Decoherence functional, entanglement particle number $\langle N angle$, and the decoherence rate ($\Gamma$).

Important parameters / regimes. Black hole mass ($M$), charge ($Q$), coupling constant ($\lambda$), separation distance ($d$), and vacuum choice (Boulware, Unruh, Hartle-Hawking).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on approximations such as treating the interaction profile as rectangular and using asymptotic matching methods for mode functions in different regimes.

Paper structure. The paper reviews the DSW gedankenexperiment, develops the coherent state formalism and local rate expression, applies it to Schwarzschild black holes, extends the analysis to Reissner-Nordström black holes, and concludes by summarizing the vacuum dependence.

Abstract

We investigate the decoherence of a quantum spatial superposition of a static particle in Schwarzschild and Reissner-Nordström black holes. By treating the particle as a localized classical source coupled to a quantum scalar field, we reformulate the decoherence process in the Danielson-Satishchandran-Wald (DSW) gedankenexperiment through coherent state generation and derive the local expression for the decoherence functional in terms of the Wightman function. In the long-time limit, the decoherence rate is shown to be characterized by the low-frequency behavior of the Wightman function. We then employ the asymptotic matching method to calculate the analytical expressions of the Wightman functions in the Boulware, Unruh, and Hartle-Hawking vacua. We show that the decoherence behavior depends on the quantum state of the environmental field. While the Boulware vacuum gives vanishing decoherence for a static superposition, the thermal effects associated with Hawking radiation in the Unruh and Hartle-Hawking vacua can induce nonvanishing decoherence.

Summary. This work numerically investigates how rod-shaped particles move through a fixed bed of spheres, revealing two key behaviors: getting permanently stuck (trapping) or moving continuously (passing). The results show that particle shape anisotropy introduces strong dynamical constraints, causing longer rods to slow down and become more easily trapped than shorter ones.

The identification of distinct dynamic regimes (trapping vs. passing) governed by geometric thresholds has parallels in understanding energy dissipation or flow through porous quantum structures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the percolation behavior of rod-like particles moving through a static, disordered bed of larger spheres under gravity.

Main result. Two distinct regimes—trapping and continuous passing—are identified, with percolation velocity depending strongly on particle aspect ratio and pore geometry. Longer rods are found to be more susceptible to geometric trapping than short rods.

Method. The research relies heavily on Discrete Element Method (DEM) simulations to model the mechanical interactions between the rod-like particles and the static sphere bed.

Model / system. The system consists of frictionless, non-interacting rod-like particles percolating through a disordered packing ($\phi=0.60$) of larger spheres. The rods are modeled as collinear small spheres, and dynamics are governed by linear spring-dashpot contact forces.

Key observables. Trapping probability ($N_{ ext{trap}}/N$), characteristic penetration length ($\lambda$), percolation velocity ($ ilde{v}_p$), mean contact number ($\langle Z angle$), and rod orientation distributions $P( heta)$.

Assumptions / limitations. Frictionless contacts are assumed for the primary analysis, and rod-rod interactions are disabled in some simulations. The system is treated as quasi-static or governed by constant mean velocity in the passing regime.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the initial packed bed structure ($g(r)$), the exponential decay of trapping probability vs. depth, phase diagrams mapping trapping/passing regimes based on particle geometry, and orientation distributions for trapped versus percolating rods.

Paper structure. Abstract

We numerically investigate percolation of independent frictionless glued-sphere rod-like particles under gravity through a disordered static bed of larger spheres. We identify two distinct regimes: a \emph{trapping} regime, where rods stop after percolating a limited distance in the bed and a \emph{passing} regime, where rods percolate continuously with constant mean velocity. The transition between these regimes is governed by the length of the rod and the geometrical trapping threshold for spherical particles based on the rod diameter and the minimum pore throat diameter defined by three touching large spheres. The percolation velocity for all rod geometries, including the single sphere limit, collapses onto a single curve when scaled with the gravitational acceleration and the bed sphere diameter. The results also demonstrate that short rods percolate nearly twice as fast as long rods due to the geometric constraints associated with the disordered pore structure of the static bed. Consequently, long rods are more susceptible to trapping via specific contact configurations with the bed spheres, which differ from those for short rods. These results reveal how shape anisotropy introduces dynamical constraints and thresholds in granular percolation, with implications for predicting segregation in mixtures of non-spherical particles.

Summary. This work develops a comprehensive theoretical and numerical framework to calculate how directional persistence affects the search efficiency of cells moving in an annular space. Detailed structure

Main problem. The study quantifies the mean first-passage time (MFPT) for a random walker to reach an absorbing target within a confined two-dimensional annular domain.

Main result. Directional persistence ($\kappa$) significantly reduces the search time compared to isotropic motion, with this benefit saturating at high persistence values. The MFPT can be accurately determined by combining diffusion-limit approximations with boundary layer corrections.

Method. The analysis employs a combination of analytical techniques (Fourier mode reductions, perturbation expansions) and advanced numerical solvers (semi-Lagrangian methods, event-driven Monte Carlo simulations).

Model / system. The model is a velocity-jump process describing cell migration in an annulus ($a < r < R$). It incorporates correlated random walks (CRW) via von Mises turning kernels to simulate directional persistence and can be extended to biased motion.

Key observables. Mean First Passage Time ($ar{T}$ or $T_{MFPT}$), relative gap between numerical solvers, effective diffusivity ($D_{ ext{eff}}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Persistence parameter ($\kappa$), annulus radii ($a$ and $R$), turning rate ($\mu$), bias strength ($d au$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes a 2D annular geometry, constant speed motion, and relies on approximations like the fast-turning limit for analytical tractability.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate schematics of the domain, mesh grading strategies (power-law graded meshes), and comparisons between direct solver results and Monte Carlo simulations showing convergence diagnostics.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining the physical problem and modeling various random walk types to deriving analytical approximations in limits ($\kappa o 0$ or $\kappa o \infty$), followed by rigorous numerical implementation using advanced solvers and extensive cross-validation against MC simulations.

Abstract

We study the mean first-passage time of a random walker to a small absorbing target at the center of a two-dimensional annulus with a specularly reflecting outer boundary. The problem is motivated by natural killer cell migration toward a target cancer cell, where the goal is to quantify how long it takes immune cells to reach the target and how search efficiency depends on directional persistence and chemotactic bias. Cell motion is modeled as a velocity-jump process. We first consider a correlated random walk with a von Mises turning kernel, with a concentration parameter controlling directional persistence. We then extend the model to a biased correlated random walk using a phase-shifted turning kernel that represents preferential motion, for example following a concentration gradient. Our analysis combines closed-form benchmarks for simple and biased random walks, Fourier-mode reductions of the transport equations for the correlated and biased correlated models, and a fast-turning perturbation expansion that gives an analytical correction to the diffusion-limit mean first-passage time for the random walker. Our analytical results are supported by numerical methods that include a semi-Lagrangian solver in radial and angular coordinates, a stationary discretisation designed to handle biased transport, and an event-driven Monte Carlo simulator for cross-validation. Together, our results provide a framework relating persistent and biased immune-cell motion to target-search times in confined two-dimensional domains.

Summary. The paper introduces Quantum-Classical Hierarchical Equations of Motion (QC-HEOM), a novel technique for simulating open quantum systems. Main problem. The core problem is simulating the time-evolution of open quantum systems, specifically addressing non-Markovian dynamics and accurately capturing residual quantum memory effects.

Main result. The proposed QC-HEOM method achieves superior efficiency compared to conventional HEOM by separating thermal fluctuations from quantum memory, especially at low temperatures. It reproduces benchmark results for complex anharmonic systems with significantly fewer auxiliary objects.

Method. It combines the ensemble-averaged classical path reference from the Quantum-Classical Path Integral formalism with a hierarchical treatment of quantum influence functionals (QC-HEOM).

Model / system. The method is applied to general open quantum systems, exemplified by the asymmetric spin-boson model and the seven-site Fenna–Matthews–Olson (FMO) complex. The Hamiltonian involves system coupling to an environment bath.

Key observables. Population dynamics $P(t)$ over time; convergence quantified by time-averaged root-mean-square (RMS) population error ($\Delta_j$).

It rigorously demonstrates its equivalence to established methods for harmonic baths before applying it successfully to anharmonic systems like FMO across varying temperatures, concluding with efficiency comparisons.

Abstract

We develop a quantum-classical hierarchical equations of motion (QC-HEOM) approach for simulating non-Markovian open quantum systems. The method combines the ensemble-averaged classical path reference of the quantum-classical path integral formalism with a hierarchy of auxiliary quantum influence functionals. By incorporating thermal fluctuations through an ensemble average over reference trajectories, the hierarchy is required to represent only the residual quantum memory associated with the imaginary part of the bath response function. Consequently, unlike conventional hierarchical equations of motion, QC-HEOM does not require Matsubara or Padé expansions of the thermal kernel and exhibits only weak temperature dependence of the hierarchy size. Furthermore, because thermal fluctuations are supplied through reference classical trajectories, the framework naturally extends beyond harmonic baths and enables the incorporation of anharmonic and molecular environments through externally generated trajectories. We derive the formalism and demonstrate its exactness for a harmonic bath. Applications to an asymmetric spin-boson model and the seven-site Fenna--Matthews--Olson complex illustrate the accuracy of QC-HEOM. It reproduces benchmark quasi-adiabatic path integral and hierarchical equations of motion results while requiring substantially fewer auxiliary objects, particularly at low temperatures. These results establish QC-HEOM as an efficient framework for treating residual quantum memory in quantum-classical descriptions of open-system dynamics. The separation of thermal fluctuations from residual quantum memory through the use of Wigner trajectories provides an approximate route toward hierarchical treatments of complex anharmonic environments that are inaccessible to conventional HEOM approaches.

Abstract

Our framework provides a way to quantify thermodynamic costs in dynamics on time-evolving spaces such as diffusion on membranes.

Summary. The paper investigates using parametrically driven multi-qubit couplers (like SNAIL) to achieve high-connectivity gate operations on superconducting qubits. By analyzing the system Hamiltonian, the authors demonstrate methods to selectively activate two-qubit ($\sqrt{ ext{iSWAP}}$) and three-qubit gates across non-adjacent qubit pairs. This advances quantum computing hardware by providing pathways for complex interactions needed for quantum simulation.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses hardware limitations in superconducting quantum computing by engineering complex, tunable interactions necessary for advanced algorithms and simulations like Lattice Gauge Theories.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is enhancing connectivity and achieving high-fidelity quantum gates (two-qubit, three-qubit) in superconducting processors beyond nearest-neighbor interactions.

Main result. Multi-qubit couplers, such as the SNAIL circuit, enable all-to-all coupling within a plaquette, demonstrating $\sqrt{ ext{iSWAP}}$ and three-qubit gates with fidelities reaching 95% or higher.

Method. The study employs numerical and analytical analysis of parametrically driven Hamiltonians, utilizing techniques like the Jacobi-Anger expansion to model time-dependent coupling strengths.

Model / system. The system consists of superconducting transmon qubits coupled via tunable multi-qubit couplers (dc-SQUID or SNAIL). The Hamiltonian is controlled by external flux and AC driving fields to selectively activate desired interactions.

Key observables. Gate fidelity ($F$), gate time ($t_{ ext{gate}}$), resonance spread ($\Delta\omega$), and coupling strengths ($g_{12}, g_{123}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Asymmetry parameter ($\alpha$), external flux bias ($\phi_{ ext{dc}}$), drive amplitude ($\delta$), and qubit frequencies ($\omega_i$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis often involves truncating the Hilbert space or using time-averaged approximations, which can lead to discrepancies when compared to full simulations.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate circuit layouts (dc-SQUID, SNAIL), state population evolution during gate operations for various pairs/couplers, and plots showing coupling coefficients derived from Bessel functions.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by introducing the physical platform and connectivity challenge, detailing the Hamiltonian modeling using both SQUID and SNAIL couplers, deriving the effective interaction strengths via normal mode analysis, and finally presenting fidelity benchmarks for two-qubit and three-qubit gates relevant to quantum simulation.

Abstract

Superconducting quantum processors could significantly profit from enhanced connectivity together with precise control of interactions and gates between qubits. Here we investigate plaquettes of four qubits that are coupled via a central tunable coupling circuit, so that not only gates between qubits connected by an edge of the plaquette can be executed but also between qubits across the diagonal. By numerically and analytically analyzing parametrically driven processes, we explore $\sqrt{\text{iSWAP}}$-gates between any pair of qubits, also across the diagonal, as well as three-qubit interactions and gates. For experimentally available circuit parameters, we for example find $\sqrt{\text{iSWAP}}$-gates with a gate time of 50 ns and 99.9\% fidelity, which is decreased to 99.4\% if two such gates are executed in parallel on disjoint qubit pairs in the plaquette. For three-qubit gates we find fidelities of 95\% fidelity at a gate time of 200 ns.

Summary. This paper introduces QCI Connect, a modular, full-stack quantum computing platform designed to overcome hardware fragmentation. It establishes a standardized architecture using an orchestrator and open interfaces to connect various QPUs and simulators. This allows domain experts to develop and run complex quantum applications regardless of the underlying physical quantum hardware.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a high-level software framework that abstracts hardware differences, which is crucial for any theoretical work aiming to test algorithms on diverse physical platforms without rewriting the core logic.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The fragmentation and lack of standardization across various competing quantum computing architectures and software stacks hinder the ability to bring the full power of quantum hardware to domain experts via the cloud.

Main result. QCI Connect provides a reference, modular, full-stack architecture that unifies disparate quantum hardware by separating concerns into distinct, standardized layers connected via open interfaces.

Abstract

In a world of various competing quantum computing architectures, hardware-agnostic, full-stack platforms are necessary to bring the full power of quantum computing hardware to domain experts via the cloud. QCI Connect and its Software Development Kit provide a reference architecture for a full-stack platform with a modular design and open-source interface definitions, built to facilitate a community-driven application ecosystem. Here, we present its overall design and features, central interfaces, and lessons learned, both for users of the platform and as a reference guide for future developments.

Summary. This paper introduces a Generalized Lyapunov Function (GLF) to characterize stability in complex nonlinear dynamical systems that lack simple gradient structures. They propose using Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) to learn this potential landscape by embedding physical constraints directly into the loss function. The method successfully demonstrates its utility across various non-gradient models, providing a powerful tool for analyzing system attractors.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical dynamics, the concept of constructing a global potential landscape via machine learning mirrors techniques used in finding effective Hamiltonians or energy functionals in open quantum systems where simple conservation laws are broken.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is constructing a global Lyapunov-type scalar potential landscape for nonlinear dynamical systems that are not gradient systems, which is crucial for understanding stability and transitions.

Main result. The authors successfully developed a data-free PINN framework to construct Generalized Lyapunov Functions (GLFs) that correctly map invariant sets to low-potential or constant-potential structures across various non-gradient flows.

Method. A Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) is employed, minimizing a loss function that embeds the Lyapunov inequality and a weak divergence-scale compatibility condition directly into the training process.

Model / system. The method is tested on canonical nonlinear systems including linear oscillators, the Hopf normal form, the van der Pol oscillator, and complex three-dimensional Hopf-link flows.

Key observables. The learned potential landscape ($\phi$), which must satisfy $\dot{\phi} \le 0$ along trajectories; invariant sets are characterized by constant or low potential values.

Important parameters / regimes. Weighting factors ($w_d \ll w_p \simeq w_0$) used in the total loss function to balance monotonicity enforcement against divergence constraints.

Assumptions / limitations. The method is designed to be data-free and does not assume the underlying vector field is close to a gradient system, though selecting appropriate weights is critical for convergence.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate PINN-generated landscapes and profiles for linear systems (stable/unstable foci), the Hopf normal form showing potential geometry, and results for the van der Pol oscillator and 3D linked cycles.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the GLF concept to unify various potentials, proposes the data-free PINN methodology with its specific loss terms ($L_{Lyap}, L_{zero}, L_{div}$), and then validates this framework by applying it sequentially to increasingly complex non-gradient systems.

Abstract

A scalar potential landscape is one of the most useful ways to understand the stability and transition of a dynamical system. For non-gradient dynamics, however, the construction of a global Lyapunov-type scalar for nonlinear flows with recurrent structures remains a major obstacle. We introduce the generalized Lyapunov function, a scalar function that is non-increasing along deterministic trajectories, as a unifying notion of nonequilibrium potential. Ordinary Lyapunov functions, Freidlin--Wentzell quasi-potentials, and Ao-type potentials are recovered as special representatives. We then propose a data-free physics-informed neural-network framework in which the Lyapunov inequality and a weak divergence-scale compatibility condition are directly embedded into the loss function. The method is tested on linear systems, the Hopf normal form, the van der Pol oscillator, and a three-dimensional Hopf-link flow with two linked limit cycles. The learned landscapes agree with available analytical benchmarks and reveal the invariant sets as low-potential or constant-potential structures, providing a practical route to potential-landscape construction for nonlinear non-gradient systems.
