Bond-operator analytical approach for the $t$-$J$ model

Authors: A. V. Syromyatnikov
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.14374v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5 · Kondo & dissipative impurity 1/5 · analog quantum simulation 1/5

Summary. This paper introduces the Bond-Operator Theory (BOT) as an analytical framework to study the strongly correlated $t$-$J$ model. By reformulating electron operators using localized spins and holon fermions, BOT systematically expands physical quantities in powers of $1/n$. The theory successfully reproduces key magnetic and superconducting properties, showing strong quantitative agreement with state-of-the-art numerical simulations.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on condensed matter magnetism/superconductivity, the use of an advanced operator formalism that systematically handles constraints and provides a controlled series expansion is valuable for theoretical physics dealing with complex many-body Hamiltonians.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to develop an analytical, constraint-free theory for the $t$-$J$ model, which is crucial for understanding high-temperature superconductivity in cuprates.

Main result. The Bond-Operator Theory (BOT) provides a systematic expansion in powers of $1/n$, yielding quantitative agreement with advanced numerical methods for ground state energy and excitation spectra.

Method. The core method is the Bond-Operator Theory, which reformulates electron operators using localized spins and mobile holon Fermi-operators to eliminate physical constraints inherent in simpler models.

Model / system. The system studied is the one-band $t$-$J$ model on a square lattice, described by an effective Hamiltonian involving hopping ($t$) and exchange coupling ($J$). The theory handles both magnetically ordered and disordered phases.

Key observables. Ground state energy, staggered magnetization, magnon spectra, polaron spectrum, and two-hole bound state binding energies.

Important parameters / regimes. The expansion parameter is $1/n$, where $n$ relates to the maximum number of quasiparticles per unit cell. The model parameters are $t$ and $J$.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that physical quantities can be expanded as a regular series in powers of $1/n$; however, the first-order expansion is noted to be insufficient for full quantitative accuracy.

Figures summary. Figures compare calculated spectra (magnons, polarons) and spectral weights using BOT against results from Self-Consistent Born Approximation (SCBA), Exact Diagonalization (ED), and Green's Function Monte Carlo (GFMC).

Paper structure. The paper introduces the BOT formalism by mapping electron operators to spin/holon operators, derives the effective Hamiltonian, calculates observables via diagrammatic expansion in $1/n$, and compares results for various excitations (magnons, polarons) against established numerical benchmarks.

Abstract

We present a bond-operator theory (BOT) for analytical consideration of the $t$-$J$ model and its extensions with longer-rage hopping terms. This technique is based on previously suggested representation of electron operators via localized spins 1/2 and spinless Fermi-operators of mobile holons which requires no constraint between them. We introduce a representation of operators of spins, holons, and electrons in (extended) unit cell containing several lattice sites via a zoo of Bose- and Fermi-operators acting in the Hilbert space of all quantum states of the whole unit cell. BOT provides a regular expansion of physical quantities in powers of $1/n$ using conventional diagrammatic technique, where $n\ge1$ is the maximum number of introduced quasiparticles (bosons and fermions) which can occupy a unit cell. The suggested representation reproduces commutation algebra of all operators at any $n>0$ and allows to consider both magnetically ordered and disordered phases. Some elementary excitations described in the BOT by separate bosons or fermions appear in common approaches as bound states of conventional quasiparticles. In particular, there are two-hole bound states (Cooper pairs of two holes) which are described within the BOT by separate bosons. We discuss in detail properties of the $t$-$J$ model on the square lattice with no more than two holes (polarons). Although the expansion parameter $1/n$ is not small in the physically meaningful case of $n=1$, we obtain a good quantitative agreement with previous numerical findings at $n=1$ even in the first order in $1/n$ after taking into account a few simple diagrams. Self-consistent calculations in the first order in $1/n$ bring our results to a very good quantitative agreement with previous numerical findings of the ground state energy, staggered magnetization, and spectra of magnons, polarons, and lowest-energy two-hole bound states.

Resurgence of the Thermal Transition between Bounce and Sphaleron

Authors: Shaun D. Hampton, Kyungsun Lee, Sungjay Lee

Summary. This paper uses Borel resurgence theory to analyze the thermal transition between two competing vacuum decay mechanisms: the bounce (quantum tunneling) and the sphaleron (thermal barrier crossing). By analyzing high-order perturbative expansions of the free energy, the authors successfully determine the critical temperature and whether the transition is first or second order for specific potential models. This demonstrates a powerful technique to extract non-perturbative physics without relying on traditional semiclassical approximations.

Why it may be interesting. The methodology provides a powerful, purely perturbative route to calculate non-perturbative quantities like tunneling rates and critical temperatures, which is highly valuable in understanding phase transitions in quantum field theory or condensed matter systems where semiclassical methods are traditionally used.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the thermal transition between two competing non-perturbative saddle points—the bounce (tunneling) and the sphaleron (thermal jump)—in a quantum system with a metastable vacuum.

Main result. The Borel resurgence analysis successfully determines the transition temperature, order (second or first), and one-loop prefactors purely from high-order perturbative expansions, without needing semiclassical inputs.

Method. The core method involves calculating the perturbative expansion of the thermal free energy and extracting non-perturbative information by analyzing its Borel singularity data $(A, b, S)$.

Model / system. The analysis is performed on quantum mechanical systems with metastable potentials, specifically modeling cases leading to second-order (cubic potential) and first-order (quintic potential) phase transitions.

Key observables. Thermal free energy $F(eta)$, Borel singularity location ($A$), characteristic exponent ($b$), and Stokes constant ($S$).

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature ($T$ or $eta$), the transition temperature ($T_c$), and the coupling/potential parameters defining the specific models.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on treating the weak-coupling expansion as a semi-classical approximation and assumes that Borel resurgence theory can fully encode the non-perturbative physics across the transition.

Figures summary. Figures compare the extracted Borel singularity $A(T)$ against classical saddle actions, showing smooth joining for second-order transitions and clear kinks/crossings for first-order transitions in the quintic model.

Paper structure. The paper systematically applies Borel resurgence to compute high-order coefficients of the free energy. It then analyzes how the extracted parameters $(A, b, S)$ characterize the nature (second vs. first order) and location ($T_c$) of the thermal transition between the bounce and sphaleron saddle points for different potentials.

Abstract

We study the thermal transition between the bounce and the sphaleron in quantum mechanics with a metastable vacuum from the viewpoint of Borel resurgence. For two models representing a second-order and a first-order transition, we compute the perturbative expansion of the thermal free energy to high orders and extract the leading Borel singularity data $(A,b,S)$ as functions of temperature. The Borel singularity location $A$ reproduces the on-shell action of the dominant saddle on both sides of the transition, joining smoothly in the second-order case and developing a kink in the first-order case. The characteristic exponent $b$ jumps between $0$ and $1/2$ across the transition, counting the zero modes of the corresponding saddle. The Stokes constant $S$ matches the one-loop determinant around the saddle. The perturbative expansion around the false vacuum thus determines the transition temperature, the order of the transition, and the decay rate including the one-loop prefactor without relying on semiclassical inputs.

Spin disorder competing with positional symmetry breaking governs the metal-insulator behavior in oxide paramagnets

Summary. This work develops a general theoretical framework using symmetry-broken DFT to explain metal-insulator transitions in paramagnetic transition-metal oxides. It shows that the electronic state depends critically on the competition between spin disorder and atomic positional displacements, successfully distinguishing why $ ext{NaFeO}_3$ is insulating while $ ext{NaOsO}_3$ is metallic.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on solid-state physics, the concept of emergent electronic behavior arising from competing structural/magnetic degrees of freedom (disorder) is relevant to understanding complex quantum many-body systems where multiple order parameters compete.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to determine the fundamental physical mechanism governing whether the high-temperature paramagnetic (PM) phase of transition-metal oxide paramagnets is metallic or insulating.

Main result. The metal-insulator behavior in these PM phases is governed by a competition between spin disorder (local moment distribution) and positional symmetry breaking (atomic displacements).

Method. Symmetry-broken Density-Functional Theory (DFT) calculations are employed to model the electronic structure of both insulating and metallic states.

Model / system. The research focuses on transition-metal oxides, specifically comparing $ ext{NaFeO}_3$ (insulating PM phase) and $ ext{NaOsO}_3$ (metallic PM phase). Calculations utilize spin special-quasirandom structures to model disorder without assuming long-range order.

Key observables. Band gap size ($ ext{eV}$), local magnetic moment distribution, and magnitude of off-center atomic displacements ($ ext{\AA}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The competition between spin disorder strength and the degree of positional polymorphism is key; strong correlation effects are explicitly avoided.

Assumptions / limitations. The approach avoids invoking strong electronic correlations by using DFT methods that model symmetry breaking via configurational averaging (spin-SQS).

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the DOS, showing gap closing for $ ext{NaOsO}_3$ and gap opening/insulating behavior for $ ext{NaFeO}_3$, correlating these changes with moment distributions and atomic displacements.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the problem by contrasting insulating vs. metallic PM phases in oxides. It then develops a general framework based on analyzing how spin disorder interacts with positional symmetry breaking, using DFT calculations on specific examples ($ ext{NaFeO}_3$ vs $ ext{NaOsO}_3$) to validate this competition.

Abstract

Numerous transition-metal oxides have low-temperature antiferromagnetic (AFM) states and high-temperature paramagnetic (PM) phases, where the AFM state is usually insulating while the PM phase can be either insulating or metallic. Without involving strong correlation, we use symmetry-broken density-functional theory (DFT) to obtain the PM phases of insulating NaFeO3 vs the recently discovered metallic NaOsO3. We develop the understanding of insulating and metallic behaviors in paramagnetic oxides by analyzing the interactions between magnetic and positional symmetry breaking: The insulating gap is governed by the competition between the spin disorder that induces a distribution of different magnitudes of local magnetic moments and the polymorphous distribution of off-center atomic displacements. NaFeO3, on the other hand, has large positional displacement with small spin-disorder-induced moments distribution, leading to insulating PM phase, whereas NaOsO3 has a pronounced spin-disorder-induced moments distribution that forces the PM phase to become metallic. Our work identifies this symmetry-breaking competition as a general framework to bridge seemingly disparate metal-insulator behaviors in transition-metal oxides paramagnets without invoking strong correlation.

Efficient Simulation of Szegedy Quantum Walk Formulations and Algorithms

Authors: Sergio A. Ortega, Daniel K. Park

Summary. The paper presents a highly efficient classical simulation framework for Szegedy quantum walks and related algorithms. It bypasses the need to construct massive unitary matrices by decomposing evolution into fundamental operator actions like updates and reflections. This allows researchers to numerically study complex quantum processes, such as search and annealing, on large graphs.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides concrete, scalable computational tools for simulating quantum dynamics that are otherwise intractable due to exponential Hilbert space size, bridging theoretical quantum algorithm design with practical numerical implementation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Developing efficient classical simulation methods for Szegedy quantum walks and related phase-estimation algorithms that avoid constructing large, explicit unitary evolution operators.

Main result. The framework achieves optimal O(N^2) complexity for dense graphs and linear scaling with edges for sparse graphs by simulating fundamental update and reflection operators directly.

Method. Simulation relies on expressing the walk evolution using fundamental operators (Update $V$, Reflection $R_0$) and employing techniques like expansion-recomposition or working in a reduced subspace representation for sparse graphs. Quantum Phase Estimation (QPE) is used to implement amplitude amplification steps.

Model / system. The model is the Szegedy quantum walk on a graph with N nodes, operating in an $N^2$-dimensional Hilbert space. Applications include quantum simulated annealing and quantum search algorithms.

Key observables. Probability distributions of finding the system at marked nodes or measuring specific phases ($ heta$) during QPE.

Important parameters / regimes. Graph size (N), number of edges, simulation time steps, and precision limits related to QPE repetitions.

Assumptions / limitations. The update operator $V$ is not unique, but the framework shows consistency for certain measurements. For sparse graphs, a reduced subspace representation $\mathcal{H}_R$ is used, assuming all necessary operators map within this space.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate probability distributions over time steps and marked nodes for quantum search; diagrams show QPE circuits and the mathematical procedure of expansion-recomposition in the reduced vector space.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining the walk evolution using fundamental operators, detailing the numerical simulation techniques (especially for sparse graphs via subspace reduction), applying these methods to complex algorithms like QPE and amplitude amplification, and finally demonstrating results on search problems.

Abstract

Quantum walks provide a versatile framework for quantum algorithms across a wide range of applications. We develop efficient classical simulation methods for Szegedy quantum walks that avoid explicit construction of the full unitary evolution operator. Unlike previous approaches restricted to a particular walk formulation, our framework is built from fundamental update and reflection operators, enabling the simulation of a broader class of Szegedy walk formulations. We further extend these methods to phase-estimation-based algorithms coupled to the walk, including implementations suitable for large sparse graphs. The resulting methods achieve optimal $O(N^2)$ complexity for dense graphs with $N$ nodes. For sparse graphs, the computational cost scales linearly with the number of edges, which is $O(N)$ in many cases. We implement the framework in the Python package SQWLib and illustrate its capabilities through simulations of representative algorithms, including quantum simulated annealing and quantum search on graphs. These results provide a practical tool for studying Szegedy-walk-based algorithms numerically beyond purely analytical treatments.

Summary. This work introduces an open-source Julia package, SmoQyElPhQMC.jl, designed for scalable Quantum Monte Carlo simulations of electron-phonon coupled models. By implementing advanced techniques like the Checkerboard Approximation and Kernel Polynomial Method preconditioning, it achieves near-linear scaling in system size and inverse temperature. The package is used to study phenomena such as charge density wave formation in canonical models like Holstein and oSSH.

Why it may be interesting. The development of highly efficient numerical tools like SmoQyElPhQMC.jl to tackle strongly coupled lattice dynamics is crucial for understanding emergent quantum phases relevant to superconductivity and charge ordering in condensed matter systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is to perform efficient and scalable Quantum Monte Carlo (QMC) simulations for electron-phonon coupled models.

Main result. The package successfully enables near-linear scaling in system size and inverse temperature, allowing the study of CDW and BOW order formation in Holstein and oSSH models.

Method. It utilizes advanced QMC techniques, including Hybrid Quantum Monte Carlo (HQMC) updates combined with the Checkerboard Approximation (MSCHK) and Kernel Polynomial Method (KPM) preconditioning for linear scaling.

Model / system. The focus is on electron-phonon coupled Hamiltonians, specifically generalized spin-symmetric models like the Holstein model and the oSSH model. These systems involve coupling between itinerant electrons and lattice vibrations (phonons).

Key observables. Charge structure factor ($S_{cdw}$), bond order wave structure factor ($S_{bow}$), single-particle electron spectral function $A(k, \omega)$, and correlation functions.

Important parameters / regimes. The simulations are performed at finite temperatures ($eta$) and involve coupling strengths ($\lambda_h=0.25$, $\lambda_o=0.36$) and phonon frequencies ($\Omega/t = 0.5$).

Assumptions / limitations. Key approximations include the MSCHK approximation to reduce scaling from $O(eta N^3)$ to $O(eta N)$, and using pseudofermion fields for determinant calculation.

Figures summary. Figures show structure factors ($S_{cdw}, S_{bow}$) increasing with system size/inverse temperature, indicating CDW/BOW order. The spectral function $A(k, \omega)$ reveals gap openings consistent with insulating CDW order in the Holstein model.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the package, details the Hamiltonian structure ($\hat{H} = \hat{U} + \hat{V} + \hat{K}$), explains the mathematical machinery for achieving linear scaling (MSCHK, pseudofermion fields, KPM preconditioning), and concludes with benchmark results on Holstein and oSSH models.

Abstract

We introduce version 1.0 of the SmoQyElPhQMC package, an open-source Julia code for performing scalable quantum Monte Carlo simulations of electron-phonon coupled model Hamiltonians. SmoQyElPhQMC is built upon the SmoQyDQMC codebase and implements improved versions of the algorithms presented in [B. Cohen-Stead \textit{et al}., Phys. Rev. E {\bf 105}, 065302 (2022)] to enable linear-scaling simulations of a broad class of uncorrelated $e$-ph models both in system size and inverse temperature. By extending the functionality of the flexible scripting interface introduced in SmoQyDQMC, the SmoQyElPhQMC package continues to allow users to adapt it to different workflows and interface with other software packages in the Julia ecosystem. The code for this package can be downloaded from our GitHub repository at https://github.com/SmoQySuite/SmoQyElPhQMC.jl or installed using the Julia package manager. The online documentation, including examples, can be obtained from our documentation page at https://smoqysuite.github.io/SmoQyElPhQMC.jl/stable/.

Spherical metadensity functional learning for inhomogeneous classical fluids

Authors: Stefanie M. Kampa, Matthias Schmidt, Florian Sammüller
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.14370v1

Summary. This work introduces a novel neural metadensity functional capable of modeling structural properties of inhomogeneous classical fluids in spherical geometries. By training on simulation data, it accurately predicts key correlation functions and demonstrates thermodynamic consistency across multiple calculation pathways. This represents an advanced ML approach to solving complex many-body statistical mechanics problems.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical fluids, the rigorous application of machine learning to derive complex many-body functionals and verify fundamental physical symmetries is a powerful methodological tool applicable to quantum systems where exact functional forms are unknown.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops a neural metadensity functional approach to model classical fluids with truncated interactions, specifically adapting the theory for three-dimensional spherical geometry.

Main result. The framework successfully predicts structural properties and verifies thermodynamic consistency relations (like test particle sum rules) by comparing results derived from the neural network against established theories like Fundamental Measure Theory (FMT).

Method. It uses supervised training of a neural network to represent the one-body direct correlation functional, $c_1$, which captures dependence on both density and pair potential in curved space.

Model / system. The system is inhomogeneous classical fluids interacting via repulsive pairwise potentials ($\phi(r)$), studied under various confinements such as spherical cavities or test particle setups. The theory builds upon Density Functional Theory (DFT) principles.

Key observables. One-body and two-body direct correlation functions ($c_1, c_2$), density profiles ($ ho(r)$), radial distribution functions ($g(r)$), and the excess chemical potential ($\mu_{exc}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Spherical geometry effects (curvature), scaled pair potential ($eta\phi$), and thermodynamic parameters like temperature ($eta$) and chemical potential ($\mu$).

Assumptions / limitations. The core assumption is that the functional dependence of $c_1$ can be accurately approximated by a neural network, and careful handling of spherical symmetry and geometric weighting functions is required.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate density profiles in various confinements (cavities/shells), demonstrate Henderson inversion recovering pair potentials from $g(r)$, and compare calculated two-body correlation functions ($c_2$ and $ar{c}_2$) across different theoretical routes (FMT vs. Neural Functional).

Paper structure. The paper progresses by establishing the DFT framework, detailing the adaptation of local learning for spherical geometry (incorporating curvature terms), deriving relationships for two-body correlations ($c_2$), and finally validating the functional's thermodynamic consistency across multiple calculation routes.

Abstract

We develop classical density functional learning to address fluids with truncated pairwise interparticle interactions in three-dimensional spherical geometry. Simulation data for systems with randomized repulsive pair potentials provide the basis for supervised training of a neural metadensity functional, thereby making efficient use of results for radial distribution functions in the bulk fluid via the test particle route. Specifically, we develop spherical local learning in order to represent the one-body direct correlation functional in terms of a neural network, which captures spatial curvature effects as well as the metadensity functional dependence on the thermally scaled pair potential. The framework yields efficient access to inhomogeneous structuring and related physical phenomena that occur in fluids and general solvents when adsorbed against curved solutes and confined inside of spherical and planar cavities. Test particle setups facilitate accurate prediction of the bulk fluid pair structure and verification of thermodynamic test particle sum rules via functional line integration. Applying the metadensity functional for Henderson inversion allows one to infer accurately the pair potential from the bulk radial distribution function. We address implications of the geometrical setup for two-body quantities and obtain the two-body direct correlation functional from automatic differentiation. For the hard sphere fluid, we confirm metadensity functional predictions against results from a standard neural density functional with fixed pair potential as well as to an analytic functional as given by fundamental measure theory. Simulation results provide further reference and corroborate reliable results of the spherical neural metadensity functional across a broad range of applications.

Summary. This paper develops the mathematical framework to treat tensor network representations of quantum states as points on a manifold. This provides a powerful geometric foundation for analyzing and optimizing these complex many-body quantum descriptions.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides rigorous mathematical tools—geometric invariants like Riemannian metrics on quotient spaces—to analyze and compare different numerical representations of quantum states derived from tensor networks, which is crucial for developing stable optimization algorithms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core problem is mathematically characterizing the redundancy (gauge freedom) inherent in representing quantum states or circuits using tensor networks.

Main result. The authors establish a Riemannian fundamental theorem by showing that the physical state space can be identified with a quotient manifold derived from the full tensor network manifold via a Riemannian submersion.

Method. The work employs advanced differential geometry, specifically utilizing concepts like group actions, Riemannian submersions, and isometric group actions to map the redundant parameter space onto a unique, geometrically defined quotient manifold.

Model / system. The model is general tensor networks (e.g., MPS, PEPS) used to represent high-dimensional quantum states or depth-two quantum circuits. The mathematical framework involves endowing these network structures with Riemannian manifold metrics.

Important parameters / regimes. Bond dimension ($D$), physical dimension ($d$), and the number of layers/gates in the circuit representation.

Abstract

Tensor networks provide a powerful framework for efficiently representing high-dimensional data and many-body quantum states. Endowing tensor networks with a Riemannian manifold structure provides a natural setting for numerical optimization and analysis. A central feature of tensor networks is their gauge freedom, whose characterisation (captured by so-called fundamental theorems) underlies both their intrinsic structure and the design of numerical algorithms. In this work, we study the interaction between the Riemannian manifold structure and the gauge freedom for several families of tensor networks. Using group actions and Riemannian submersions, we establish a Riemannian fundamental theorem for the tensor network families studied.

Summary. The paper demonstrates that the standard trace distance metric used for quantifying distinguishability between qubit states can be rigorously recovered from the Connes spectral distance by embedding the system into an extended 'scalar-qubit-scalar' model. This establishes a deep, quantifiable link between noncommutative geometry and quantum information theory metrics. The result is crucial because it shows how geometric structures derived from spectral analysis can precisely predict operational limits in quantum measurement.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful geometric framework connecting abstract mathematical tools (spectral triples/Connes distance) directly to fundamental physical quantities in quantum information theory (trace distance). It suggests that operational metrics can be derived from underlying spectral structures, which is highly relevant for theoretical physics exploring quantization procedures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The research aims to determine if the standard operational metric for qubit state discrimination, the trace distance, can be mathematically recovered from the Connes spectral distance within a finite noncommutative model.

Main result. The equal-prior Helstrom trace-distance geometry of qubit states is successfully recovered from the Connes spectral distance via a calibrated relationship involving a scale factor $\Lambda$. This establishes a deep connection between non-commutative geometry and quantum information theory metrics.

Method. The authors employ techniques from noncommutative geometry, specifically calculating the Connes spectral distance using a Dirac operator on an extended algebra incorporating scalar reference sectors. They derive relationships by optimizing over these auxiliary variables to match known quantum metrics.

Model / system. The system models qubit states ($ ho$) living on the Bloch ball, which is embedded into a larger structure comprising two scalar reference sectors ($C_L, C_R$) coupled isotropically to the qubit block ($M_2(\mathbb{C})$). Key observables. Trace distance $T( ho, \sigma)$, Connes spectral distance $d_D(\varphi, \psi)$, and the optimal success probability $ ext{p}_{ ext{succ}}( ho, \sigma)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The coupling strengths ($\mu_L, \mu_R$) defining the scalar link lengths ($\ell_L, \ell_R$), which determine the overall calibration scale $\Lambda = (\mu_L^2 + \mu_R^2)^{1/2}$.

Abstract

We show that the equal-prior Helstrom trace-distance geometry of qubit states is recovered from Connes spectral distance in a finite scalar-qubit-scalar model. The two scalar reference sectors couple isotropically to the qubit block through identity Dirac links, so that the full Bloch ball, including mixed states, inherits its standard chordal trace-distance geometry from the finite spectral metric. The scalar-sector distances serve a distinct calibration role: they determine the individual link lengths, satisfy a Pythagorean consistency relation, and reconstruct the middle-sector scale.

Abstract

Abstract
