KQFuzz: Knowledge-Guided Fuzzing for Quantum Libraries via Large Language Models

Authors: Fuyuan Xia, Qixin Zhang, Chenhao Ying, Haojin Zhu, Shuai Wang, Yuan Luo, Pingchuan Ma, Yuxuan Du

Summary. This paper introduces KQFuzz, a novel knowledge-guided fuzzer designed to test the reliability of quantum computing libraries. It enhances LLM-based testing by incorporating deep codebase knowledge and advanced mutation strategies. The resulting framework significantly improves bug discovery and code coverage across major quantum frameworks.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on software testing rather than physical dynamics, the rigorous validation techniques developed for quantum circuit execution are crucial for building reliable simulation tools necessary for studying complex many-body physics or open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Ensuring the reliability and correctness of quantum libraries is critical, but existing fuzzing methods lack flexibility and efficiency.

Main result. KQFuzz significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods, improving code coverage by up to 18.44% and discovering multiple confirmed bugs in major quantum libraries.

Method. The authors propose KQFuzz, a knowledge-guided fuzzer that uses codebase knowledge to ground LLM test generation, combined with fitness-guided evaluation and two-level mutations.

Model / system. The work targets software implementations of quantum computation within popular libraries like Qiskit, PennyLane, and Cirq, which operate on the quantum circuit model involving gates and qubits.

Key observables. Code coverage improvement (up to 18.44%), bug discovery rate (13 bugs found), code validity, and execution time/token consumption.

Important parameters / regimes. The two-level mutation strategy involves parameter-level and gate-level structural mutations; the fitness function incorporates gate diversity and entangled qubits.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that increased output token count in KQFuzz represents meaningful semantic content leading to higher quality tests, rather than mere redundancy.

Figures summary. Figure 1 compares the scope of different fuzzer types; Figure 3 shows empirical comparison of validity rates for classical vs. quantum domains; Table 8 and 9 detail computational cost analysis across various stages and models.

Paper structure. The paper first identifies the limitations of current fuzzing techniques, then introduces KQFuzz with its knowledge corpus construction (static metadata, API associations, semantic models), followed by detailed descriptions of the generation/mutation strategies. It presents empirical results comparing against baselines on multiple quantum libraries and concludes with an efficiency analysis.

Abstract

As quantum computing continually improves, ensuring the reliability and correctness of quantum libraries has become increasingly critical. To this end, many LLM-based fuzzing approaches towards quantum libraries have been proposed to uncover potential bugs. However, these methods still suffer from limitations such as insufficient flexibility and low efficiency, which hinder the progress of the quantum computing field. To address these challenges, we propose KQFuzz, a novel knowledge-guided fuzzer for quantum libraries. It leverages comprehensive codebase knowledge to ground LLM-based test generation, synergizing this with fitness-guided evaluation and two-level mutations to explore complex execution paths and trigger potential bugs. Firstly, KQFuzz introduces a novel prompting scheme tailored to quantum programs, which strategically incorporates knowledge of the codebase to efficiently generate high-quality quantum seed programs. Moreover, we develop evaluation and mutation strategies to handle the generated seed programs, facilitating efficient fuzzing execution while further enriching the diversity of the resulting test cases. We implement KQFuzz and conduct fuzzing on three popular quantum libraries, including Qiskit, PennyLane, and Cirq. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms other state-of-the-art methods, with coverage improved by up to 18.44%. During the development of KQFuzz, we discovered 13 bugs, all of which have been confirmed and 12 have already been fixed by the developers.

Landau Levels on the Surface of a Cube

Authors: Almila Kura, Bayram Tekin, Mehmet Özgür Oktel
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.25684v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

Summary. This paper analyzes the quantum mechanics of a charged particle on a cubic surface pierced by a magnetic monopole. It rigorously handles the gauge structure using overlapping patches and classifies eigenstates based on the cube's rotational symmetry group. The key findings are the splitting of Landau levels due to discrete symmetry and the emergence of corner-localized gap states in the lattice approximation.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete realization of how global topology (monopole flux) interacts with discrete geometric symmetry (the cube), leading to spectral splitting effects analogous to those seen in quantum Hall systems but constrained by crystal geometry.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the quantum mechanics of a charged particle confined to the surface of a cube enclosing a magnetic monopole.

Main result. The spectrum exhibits Landau-level-like manifolds whose degeneracies are split by the discrete cubic symmetry, and additional gap states localized near the cube corners appear in the tight-binding limit.

Method. The analysis uses continuum formulation with overlapping gauge patches (Wu–Yang construction) and is complemented by a gauge-covariant finite-difference discretization for numerical computation.

Model / system. The system models a charged particle on the surface of a cube subjected to a magnetic field ($\vec{B}$) representing a monopole flux. The Hamiltonian involves the vector potential $\vec{A}(\vec{r})$ and is analyzed using symmetry groups like the cubic rotation group $O$.

Key observables. Landau-level-like energy spectra, gap states localized at cube corners, and the classification of eigenstates by irreducible representations of $O$ or $2O$.

Important parameters / regimes. The magnetic flux ($\Phi_B$), which must satisfy the Dirac quantization condition; the size parameter ($L$) of the cube.

Assumptions / limitations. The method relies on using two overlapping gauge patches to handle the non-trivial topology imposed by the monopole flux, and that the physical symmetry is restored via gauge-modified rotation operators.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate wavefunction probability densities $|\Psi|^2$ and phase plots ($\arg(\Psi)$) across different symmetry multiplets ($A_1, T_1$, etc.) for varying monopole charges ($M$).

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the necessity of multi-patch gauge constructions to analyzing the resulting symmetries using group theory, culminating in numerical computation and visualization of the energy spectrum and wavefunctions.

Abstract

We study the quantum mechanics of a charged particle confined to the surface of a cube enclosing a magnetic monopole. The magnetic field is chosen to have a constant magnitude on each face and to point along the outward normal, preserving the rotational symmetry of the cube. We formulate the continuum problem using two gauge patches on the cube surface and show that consistency of the wavefunction gives the Dirac quantization condition. Since an explicit vector potential does not remain invariant under ordinary rotations, we construct gauge-modified rotation operators and use them to classify the eigenstates. Even monopole charges are described by the irreducible representations of the cubic rotation group O, while odd monopole charges require the spinorial representations of the binary octahedral group 2O. We compute the spectrum with a gauge-covariant finite-difference discretization and find Landau-level-like manifolds whose degeneracies are split by the discrete cubic symmetry. We also study the corresponding tight-binding Hofstadter problem on the discretized cube. The resulting spectrum contains the usual magnetic subband structure together with additional gap states localized near the cube corners.

Momentum-Selective Two-Component Excitations in Electron-Doped Mott Insulators

Authors: Zeyu Han, Can Cui, Jia-Xin Zhang, Zheng-Yu Weng

Summary. This work tackles the asymmetry between electron and hole doping in Mott insulators by modeling the system using the t-t'-J Hamiltonian. It reveals that the ground state has two coupled components: a coherent quasiparticle and an incoherent composite part. The relative dominance of resonance versus coherent propagation dictates whether low-energy excitations are concentrated at the node (hole-doped) or the antinode (electron-doped).

Why it may be interesting. The detailed momentum-space separation of spectral weight based on doping sign provides a concrete theoretical mechanism explaining the observed nodal-antinodal dichotomy in cuprates, which is crucial for understanding high-Tc superconductivity mechanisms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The work addresses the observed asymmetry in low-energy single-particle excitations between electron-doped and hole-doped cuprates, which challenges a unified description within standard doped Mott insulator frameworks.

Main result. The ground state exhibits a two-component structure; for hole doping, resonance dominates at the node, while for electron doping, coherent quasiparticle propagation is selectively enhanced in the antinode, causing momentum-space separation.

Method. Theoretical analysis using the t-t'-J model combined with Variational Monte Carlo (VMC) simulations for single-hole excitations and mean-field treatments of fractionalization.

Model / system. The study focuses on cuprates modeled by the t-t'-J Hamiltonian, investigating doping effects in both electron ($t'>0$) and hole ($t'<0$) regimes. The analysis involves decomposing the ground state into coherent quasiparticle and incoherent composite components.

Key observables. Low-energy single-particle spectral weight $Z_k$, momentum-space distribution of excitations (nodal vs. antinodal), and kinetic energy contributions from different component interactions.

Important parameters / regimes. Doping level ($\delta$), hopping parameters ($t, t', J$), and the sign of $t'$ which dictates the doping type (hole vs. electron).

Assumptions / limitations. The ground-state wave function is assumed to possess a generic two-component structure; approximations are made in mean-field treatments involving gauge fields and path integrals.

Figures summary. FIG. ES12 compares the quasiparticle spectral weight $Z_k$ for single holes across different doping regimes, showing distinct momentum dependence based on the sign of $t'$.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the two-component nature of the ground state in the t-t'-J model. It then analyzes the physics separately for hole-doped ($t'<0$) and electron-doped ($t'>0$) systems, detailing how resonance or coherent propagation dominates in each case to explain experimental asymmetries.

Abstract

Experimental studies reveal a striking asymmetry in low-energy single-particle excitations between electron- and hole-doped cuprates. Electron-doped cuprates display a nontrivial dichotomy: Fermi-liquid-like behavior (suggesting weaker electronic correlations) coexists with correlation-driven features typical of hole-doped systems. This dual nature challenges a unified description within a doped Mott insulator framework. The present work addresses this issue by establishing that the ground-state wave function of the $t$-$t'$-$J$ model generically possesses a two-component structure, comprising a coherent quasiparticle and an incoherent composite component. The kinetic energy arises from both the intrinsic propagation of the coherent quasiparticle and the resonance between these components. Using variational Monte Carlo at the level of a single hole, we show that for hole doping ($t'<0$), this resonance between components dominates and concentrates in the nodal region at low energies. This emergent propagation induced by resonance can be physically interpreted as originating from the recombination of fractionalized degrees of freedom, which drives various phenomena associated with strong correlations. Conversely, for electron doping ($t'>0$), the coherent quasiparticle propagation, which exhibits conventional properties of a Fermi liquid, is selectively enhanced in the antinodal region at low energies. This produces a separation in momentum space for systems with electron doping: the antinodal spectral weight at low energies is governed by the coherent quasiparticle, fundamentally differing from the nodal region, which remains dominated by the incoherent composite component. Motivated by such a structure and guided by experimental observations, we propose a phenomenological Green's function at finite doping, yielding spectral features consistent with experiments.

Organizing Principles for Moiré Quantum Matter

Authors: Qiaoling Xu, Yifan Gao, Tao Zhang, Ammon Fischer, Yi Jiang, Hanqi Pi, Zike Fan, Dongdong An, Kun Zhou, Yingjian Li, Yongqing Li, Yuhao Fu, Lei Wang, Lijun Zhang, B. Andrei Bernevig, Dante M. Kennes, Enge Wang, Angel Rubio, Lede Xian

Summary. This paper introduces a general, symmetry-based framework to classify emergent moiré Hamiltonians in twisted van der Waals heterostructures. By analyzing hundreds of bilayers using first-principles calculations, the authors move beyond simple single-orbital models. The resulting 'valley-orbital-symmetry framework' provides a systematic guide for engineering correlated and topological quantum phases.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful theoretical dictionary for designing strongly correlated quantum materials by linking fundamental crystal symmetry and orbital physics directly to the required Hubbard model parameters.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To develop a general organizing principle for understanding the diverse emergent low-energy moiré Hamiltonians arising from twisted van der Waals heterostructures, moving beyond conventional single-orbital/K-valley paradigms.

Main result. The authors established a comprehensive 'valley-orbital-symmetry framework' that classifies emergent moiré physics based on parent material properties and symmetry constraints. This framework reveals multiple routes to correlated phases, including honeycomb, kagome, and multi-orbital flat bands.

Method. The study combines fully relaxed first-principles calculations (DFT) with advanced analysis techniques like band unfolding, elementary band representation (EBR), and detailed symmetry group theory applied across hundreds of twisted bilayers.

Model / system. The physical system is the moiré superlattice formed by twisting van der Waals bilayers. The theoretical framework aims to map the parent material's electronic structure ($\mathbf{k}_0, \mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{k}_0}$) onto an effective low-energy Hamiltonian defined by symmetry ($G_M$) and orbital content.

Key observables. Wannier characterization (W), band connectivity (C), topological classification (I), and the resulting $Q$-lattice structure are key diagnostics used to classify emergent models.

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis spans over 600 commensurate twisted bilayers across all 2D lattice classes, focusing on low-energy manifolds derived from parent band edges.

Assumptions / limitations. The approach assumes that the physics can be systematically organized by mapping parent band edge properties and symmetry constraints to the emergent moiré Hamiltonian structure.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the design map connecting parent band edge properties to the resulting moiré Hamiltonians, while other figures detail specific flat-band manifolds realized in various lattice classes.

Paper structure. The paper introduces a general organizing map linking momentum location ($\mathbf{k}_0$), orbital character ($\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{k}_0}$), and symmetry ($G_M$) to the resulting moiré Hamiltonian. It then systematically applies this framework across numerous material examples, demonstrating how different symmetries enforce specific types of correlated models (e.g., honeycomb vs. kagome).

Abstract

Moiré flat bands in van der Waals bilayers are usually discussed through a small set of mechanisms associated with the $Γ$ and $K$ valleys of hexagonal crystals, and more recently with $M$-valleys systems. Here we show that this view is incomplete. The momentum-space location and effective local orbital character of the monolayer's band edge, in conjunction with the moiré symmetry and the symmetry representations of the resulting bands, provide a general set of organizing variables for the emergent low-energy moiré Hamiltonian. Applying fully relaxed first-principles calculations, band unfolding and symmetry-representation analysis to more than 600 commensurate twisted bilayers spanning all 2D lattice classes, we identify several routes to moiré quantum matter beyond the conventional single-orbital paradigm. The resulting flat bands realize trigonal, honeycomb, square, checkerboard and kagome-like Hubbard models with single-orbital, multi-orbital and multi-site Hilbert spaces; spin-orbit-coupled multi-orbital flat bands exhibit symmetry-indicated topology beyond the conventional $K$-valley setting; and nonsymmorphic moiré symmetries enforce semimetallic flat-band connectivity. Analogous quasi-one-dimensional flat-band structures are found in $M$-valley hexagonal systems and $X$-valley square or rectangular systems resulting from emergent momentum-space nonsymmorphic symmetries. Separately, coupled multi-valley manifolds with kagome-like connectivity are identified in several systems whose parent band edges lie at non-high-symmetry points. These results establish a valley-orbital-symmetry framework for connecting parent-material electronic structure to emergent moiré Hamiltonians relevant to correlated, topological and symmetry-enforced moiré phases.

Summary. This paper introduces a Quantum Transformer solver that uses Multi-Layer Fully-Connected Variational Quantum Circuits (FC-VQC) to solve complex, high-dimensional Partial Differential Equations reformulated as BSDEs. By integrating causal self-attention into the quantum circuit structure, the model achieves state-of-the-art performance compared to classical and non-attentive quantum baselines on several physical benchmarks.

Why it may be interesting. While the application is numerical PDE solving, the core innovation lies in mapping complex functional approximation tasks onto hybrid quantum-classical neural network architectures (QTransformer/FC-VQC), which has implications for simulating quantum dynamics or high-dimensional physical systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core problem is solving high-dimensional parabolic partial differential equations (PDEs) by reformulating them into Backward Stochastic Differential Equations (BSDEs). This is crucial in fields like stochastic control and physics.

Main result. The proposed Quantum Transformer solver consistently improves upon non-attentive quantum circuit baselines, demonstrating that combining causal attention with FC-VQC provides an effective architecture for high-dimensional BSDE trajectory learning.

Method. The authors propose a Quantum Transformer model where trainable parameters are confined within Multi-Layer Fully-Connected Variational Quantum Circuits (FC-VQC) modules integrated into the standard Transformer structure. Causal self-attention is used to process time-coordinate tokens derived from the state trajectory.

Model / system. The method targets solving BSDEs associated with semilinear PDEs (e.g., Black–Scholes, Burgers-type). The quantum component uses FC-VQC circuits to parameterize the necessary mappings within the Transformer architecture.

Key observables. Mean Trajectory Relative L2 Error (LRE) and Relative Mean Absolute Error (RelMAE), with lower values indicating better performance across three benchmark PDEs.

Important parameters / regimes. Model depth ($L \in \{3, 5, 7\}$) and hidden dimension ($d_m$). The comparison highlights the effect of attention versus non-attentive circuits.

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes that structural operations (like attention mechanisms) can remain classical and parameter-free while confining all trainable parameters to scalable FC-VQC modules.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem of solving PDEs via BSDEs. It then details the novel QTransformer architecture integrating FC-VQC into the Transformer framework. Results are presented by comparing performance against classical and non-attentive quantum baselines on multiple benchmarks, concluding with an analysis of architectural components.

Abstract

Solving high-dimensional parabolic partial differential equations (PDEs) is important in engineering, physics, and stochastic control. Deep BSDE methods reformulate semilinear PDEs as backward stochastic differential equations and admit a model-based reinforcement learning interpretation, where trajectories are generated from known stochastic dynamics while a trainable model learns the gradient-related control process. We propose a Quantum Transformer BSDE solver based on Multi-Layer Fully-Connected Variational Quantum Circuits (FC-VQC). The method treats the normalized state trajectory as time--coordinate tokens and applies causal self-attention to learn interactions in the adapted BSDE gradient process. All trainable model parameters are contained within the FC-VQC embedding, projection, feed-forward, and decoder modules, while attention and structural operations remain classical and parameter-free. Experiments on three d=36 PDE benchmarks show that QTransformer consistently improves over the non-attentive FC-VQC baseline and outperforms the classical Transformer at compact hidden widths, while the wider classical Transformer achieves the best overall accuracy. These results demonstrate that combining causal attention with FC-VQC provides an effective quantum architecture for high-dimensional BSDE trajectory learning.

Summary. This paper applies a machine learning approach to model working-age population growth using local municipal social statistics. It formulates this relationship as a Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) problem and solves it using quantum annealing techniques. The framework provides an interpretable tool for exploring potential policy configurations, though its results are limited to model-based exploration rather than causal prediction.

Why it may be interesting. While applied to social science, the core methodology involves mapping complex combinatorial optimization problems onto QUBO Hamiltonians for solving with quantum annealers, which is relevant to general NISQ algorithm development.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To develop an interpretable, optimization-ready framework connecting municipal social indicators to working-age population growth rates in aging societies.

Main result. The QUBO formulation successfully models the non-linear interactions between social indicators, and quantum/simulated annealing methods consistently find the optimal configuration predicted by exact solvers.

Method. A quadratic surrogate model is fitted using regression on discretized social data, then converted into a QUBO Hamiltonian to be minimized via quantum or simulated annealing optimization.

Model / system. The system models working-age population growth rate ($y$) as a function of ten discrete social indicators ($\vec{x}$), formulated as a quadratic objective function $G(\vec{x})$ subject to binary constraints.

Key observables. Test-set correlation coefficient (0.84) and average R-squared value (0.76); the optimal indicator configuration vector $\vec{x}_{opt}$.

Important parameters / regimes. The penalty coefficient ($\alpha=3$) used for enforcing one-hot constraints in the QUBO formulation.

Assumptions / limitations. The framework is explicitly an exploratory tool, not a causal estimate of policy interventions; the model assumes quadratic relationships between indicator levels.

Figures summary. Figures show observed vs. fitted growth rates, heatmaps of the coefficient matrix $Q$ detailing pairwise interactions, and local sensitivity analyses showing predicted changes based on single-indicator flips.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the need for modeling population decline; it then details fitting a quadratic surrogate model to social data; this model is converted into a QUBO problem; finally, optimization using quantum/simulated annealing is performed and validated against exact solvers.

Abstract

The decline of the working-age population is a major challenge for regional sustainability, particularly in ageing societies such as Japan. We present a methodological demonstration of a quadratic unconstrained binary optimization (QUBO)-based framework for exploring social-indicator configurations associated with working-age population growth. Using Japanese municipal data, we regressed the 2010-2020 working-age population growth rate on ten discretized social indicators. The resulting quadratic surrogate model showed reasonable predictive performance, with a test-set correlation coefficient of 0.84 and an average R-squared value of 0.76. Its coefficient matrix provides an interpretable representation of individual indicator-level contributions and pairwise associations. We converted the fitted model into a QUBO formulation with one-hot constraints and optimized it using quantum annealing, simulated annealing, and Gurobi. All three methods identified the same optimal feasible configuration, while the annealing-based samplers also generated feasible suboptimal configurations with different predicted growth rates. Municipality-level single-indicator analyses showed that changing one indicator can increase or decrease the predicted growth rate depending on the other indicators. The framework provides an interpretable and optimization-ready approach for connecting municipal social statistics, nonlinear interactions, and model-based scenario generation. It should be regarded as an exploratory tool for policy discussion rather than as a causal estimate of policy interventions.

Verifiable blind probabilistic error cancellation

Authors: Bo Yang, Elham Kashefi, Harold Ollivier

Summary. This paper presents Verifiable Blind Probabilistic Error Cancellation (VBPEC), a novel protocol that secures Quantum Error Mitigation (QEM) when running computations on untrusted quantum hardware. It achieves this by formally integrating PEC into composable security, allowing the active cancellation of noise rather than just tolerating it. This establishes a crucial pathway toward reliable and practical delegated quantum computation.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the practical gap between theoretical quantum algorithms and noisy, untrusted hardware by providing rigorous cryptographic verification for error mitigation techniques.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core problem is establishing end-to-end security guarantees for quantum computations performed on untrusted, noisy hardware by integrating Quantum Error Mitigation (QEM) into verifiable protocols.

Main result. The paper introduces Verifiable Blind Probabilistic Error Cancellation (VBPEC), the first secure protocol to integrate PEC within a composable security framework, providing cryptographic reliability for QEM.

Method. It extends trap-based verification from deterministic checks to statistical tests that actively cancel noise rather than merely tolerating it, formalizing delegated mitigation as a cryptographic resource.

Model / system. The work addresses the abstract model of quantum computation delegated to an untrusted server. The underlying physical processes involve observable estimation ($ ext{Tr}[ ho O]$) on near-future quantum hardware.

Key observables. Observable expectation value $ ext{Tr}[ ho O]$, empirical estimator $\mu$, and security error (must be exponentially small).

Important parameters / regimes. Estimation error $\epsilon$ and confidence level $\delta$; the number of samples $N_c$.

Assumptions / limitations. The protocol assumes access to a fully malicious adversary model, requiring cryptographic guarantees for noise cancellation.

Figures summary. Not specified in detail, but the notes imply schematics illustrating the integration of PEC into verifiable estimation protocols.

Paper structure. The paper builds from standard observable estimation theory to Probabilistic Error Cancellation (PEC), extends this to a blind setting (BDPEC), and culminates in the universal framework (VBPEC) that provides cryptographic security guarantees for QEM.

Abstract

Quantum error mitigation (QEM) is an essential tool for mitigating hardware noise without incurring space overhead. Yet, its reliability depends on modeling, calibration, and implementation, leaving end-to-end security on untrusted quantum hardware unresolved. We address this problem by introducing verifiable blind probabilistic error cancellation (VBPEC), the first secure verification protocol against a fully malicious adversary that integrates QEM. VBPEC brings probabilistic error cancellation (PEC), a widely studied QEM technique, within the scope of composable security by formalizing delegated mitigation as a cryptographic resource in the abstract cryptography framework. The protocol performs PEC with perfect blindness and an exponentially small security error. VBPEC retains the absence of quantum-space overhead from recent statistically-secure verified quantum computation protocols and from PEC. The only overhead takes the form of additional repetitions due to the QEM procedure. To achieve this, we extend trap-based verification from deterministic pass/fail checks to statistical tests that benefit from QEM and develop a new proof technique that integrates the corresponding additional deviation sources. Rather than merely tolerating honest noise below a fixed threshold, VBPEC actively cancels it, enabling correctly mitigated estimates to be accepted with high probability without compromising security. Our framework thus establishes an essential route towards secure, reliable, and practical delegated quantum computation on near-future quantum hardware: VBPEC fundamentally improves the practicality of verification.

Summary. This paper provides a rigorous spectral proof of the Hypergraph Moore Bound conjecture. It uses advanced linear algebra and graph theory tools—specifically analyzing weighted, memory-augmented graphs derived from hypergraphs—to relate the density of edges to the minimum size of an 'even cover.' The result establishes fundamental combinatorial bounds applicable across various dimensions.

Why it may be interesting. While purely combinatorial/graph theoretical, the reliance on spectral methods (eigenvalues, walk counting) applied to structured matrices shares deep mathematical parallels with techniques used in quantum information theory for analyzing entanglement or graph properties via adjacency operators.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to prove Feige's conjecture on the hypergraph Moore bound, establishing absolute constants for the size of an even cover in a $k$-uniform hypergraph.

Main result. It proves that any sufficiently dense $k$-uniform hypergraph must contain an even cover whose size is bounded by $A\ell\log(en/\ell)$, or alternatively, the number of hyperedges must be below a specific threshold dependent on $n, k,$ and $\ell$.

Method. The proof employs advanced spectral graph theory techniques applied to specialized matrices (Kikuchi matrices) derived from lifting the hypergraph structure into a memory-augmented graph. It uses trace methods and walk counting arguments.

Model / system. The mathematical model is an abstract $k$-uniform hypergraph $H$ on $n$ vertices, analyzed via its associated Kikuchi graph defined on an $\ell$-slice of the hypercube. The analysis involves constructing a 'lifted graph with memory' to track parity constraints.

Key observables. Minimum size of an even cover ($ ext{gev}(H)$), average degree ($ar{d}$) of the associated Kikuchi graph, and spectral norms of derived operators ($K$ or $L$).

Important parameters / regimes. The uniformity $k$, number of vertices $n$, hierarchy level $\ell$, and parameters $A$ and $C$ defining the bounds.

Assumptions / limitations. The proof relies on assuming no small even cover exists to derive spectral bounds, and it handles technical difficulties for odd vs. even uniformity using specific weighting schemes ($\delta_e$).

Paper structure. The paper develops the argument by first defining the problem via spectral analysis of the Kikuchi graph, then tackling the even and odd uniformity cases separately, establishing bounds on $ar{d}$ based on the non-existence of small even covers, and finally combining these results to prove the main theorem.

Abstract

A nonempty subfamily of a $k$-uniform hypergraph is an \emph{even cover} if every vertex lies in an even number of its hyperedges; for $k=2$ these are edge-disjoint unions of cycles, so the minimum size of an even cover is the natural hypergraph analogue of girth. We prove Feige's 2008 conjecture on the hypergraph Moore bound: there are absolute constants $A$ and $C$ (independent of $k$) such that for every $k\ge3$ and every $1\le\ell\le n$, any $k$-uniform hypergraph on $n$ vertices with more than $C\,n^{k/2}/\ell^{k/2-1}$ hyperedges contains an even cover of size at most $A\,\ell\log(en/\ell)$. Our proof is based on sharp spectral bounds for Kikuchi matrices, which we expect to be of independent interest; we apply them to the refutation of random constraint satisfaction problems in a companion paper.

Abstract

The nonlocal Majorana polarization, defined as the product of the expectation values of the particle-hole operator at opposite halves of the system, has been shown to be a reliable topological indicator that determines the presence and quality of Majorana zero modes in Hermitian topological superconducting setups. In this work, we extend the concept of nonlocal Majorana polarization to the non-Hermitian realm by taking into account the biorthogonal eigenstates and demonstrate its utility by exploring distinct non-Hermitian superconducting systems. In particular, we show that the Majorana polarization can distinguish between Majorana zero modes, trivial zero-energy states, and exceptional points in non-Hermitian superconductors. Also, we introduce the concept of nonlocal Majorana polarization sensitiviy for characterizing the contribution of non-Hermiticity to Majorana polarization. As a byproduct, we find that non-Hermiticity enhances Majorana zero modes robustness, a property captured by the nonlocal Majorana polarization.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Luminescence blinking remains a major obstacle for colloidal quantum dots, and existing quantitative assessment methods based on arbitrary intensity thresholds are ambiguous.

Main result. The authors propose a robust, threshold-free two-dimensional criterion using the variance of the recombination rate and relative quantum yield to quantitatively classify different blinking mechanisms.

Method. They derive and apply this 2D criterion $(\hat{\xi}, \langle\hat{Q} angle^{(s)})$ by analyzing time-correlated single-photon counting data, comparing it against simulations covering A-type, Trapping Mechanism (TM), and Hot Carrier (HC) blinking.

Model / system. The system is colloidal semiconductor quantum dots exhibiting luminescence blinking. The analysis models the dynamics using instantaneous quantum yield $Y(t)$ and recombination rate $\Gamma(t)$, which are extracted from single-photon counting data.

Key observables. Variance of recombination rate ($\hat{\xi}$), self-consistent relative quantum yield ($\langle\hat{Q} angle^{(s)}$), ON/OFF time distributions, and the two-dimensional parameter space map.

Important parameters / regimes. The criterion distinguishes regimes: $\hat{\xi} \approx 1$ (constant rate), $\hat{\xi} = 0$ (two-state switching), and $(\hat{\xi} \approx 1, \langle\hat{Q} angle^{(s)} \approx 1)$ for complete suppression.

Assumptions / limitations. The self-consistent estimator for quantum yield is preferred over naive estimators due to bias reduction; the analysis relies on extracting time-dependent rates from binned data.

Finally, it validates this criterion by simulating and mapping known blinking mechanisms (A-type, TM, HC).

Abstract

Colloidal semiconductor quantum dots are promising materials for numerous applications due to their tunable emission and high quantum yields. However, emission instability in the form of luminescence blinking remains a significant obstacle to their practical implementation. While recent advances in synthesis and surface engineering have demonstrated partial or complete blinking suppression, the quantitative assessment of the "quality" of this suppression remains challenging. Existing criteria typically rely on threshold-based classification into ON and OFF states, which becomes inherently ambiguous owing to the continuous distribution of emission intensities in single quantum dots. Here, we propose a threshold-free two-dimensional quantitative criterion based on the variance of the recombination rate and the relative quantum yield, both of which can be extracted from standard time-correlated single-photon counting measurements. We validate the criterion using simulated blinking trajectories covering all major blinking mechanisms and demonstrate that it cleanly separates them into distinct regions of the two-dimensional parameter space while providing a quantitative measure of the degree of suppression. This approach directly quantifies the temporal stability of emission without requiring arbitrary state definitions, thereby providing a robust metric for comparing different blinking suppression strategies.

Main result. The magnetic order is found to be strongly dependent on layer parity, with odd layers exhibiting net magnetization while even layers remain compensated AFM. Abstract

Van der Waals (vdW) based antiferromagnets (AFMs) are an ideal platform for probing and understanding thickness- and twist-angle-dependent emergent spin phenomena. However, element-specific nanoscale characterization of the spin structure in atomically thin vdW-based AFMs systems and layer-parity effects remain elusive, making them crucial for both fundamental insight into low-dimensional magnetism and the rational design of spintronic devices based on these materials. Here, we utilize X-ray magnetic circular and linear dichroisms paired with photoemission electron microscopy to resolve the magnetic order in atomically thin CrSBr. Our comprehensive measurements reveal CrSBr magnetic structure at the nanoscale and its dependence on the layer number, surface encapsulation, temperature, and applied field. Moreover, in the orthogonally twisted bilayer configuration, obtained by twisting two CrSBr ferromagnetic monolayers by 90$^\circ$, the magnetic easy axis fundamentally differs from the individual monolayers, unlocking a new pathway for moiré magnetism.
