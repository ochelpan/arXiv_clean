Automating Variational Quantum Sensing through Reinforcement-Learned Circuit Structures

Authors: Jie Liu, Xin Wang
Type: theory · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.17582v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces AutoQSense, an RL framework that automates the design of quantum sensing protocols by optimizing the circuit architecture itself, not just the parameters. By treating circuit design as a sequential decision process, it achieves superior sensitivity compared to fixed ansätze. This establishes a resource-aware methodology for adaptive quantum metrology applicable to large, complex quantum hardware.

Why it may be interesting. The integration of RL for circuit design directly addresses the resource-aware optimization needed in quantum metrology, providing a systematic way to navigate the vast space of possible quantum circuits.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core problem is automating Variational Quantum Sensing (VQS) by optimizing not only continuous variational parameters but also the underlying quantum circuit architectures themselves.

Main result. The proposed framework, AutoQSense, successfully learns adaptive, hardware-compatible quantum sensing protocols that outperform fixed, hardware-efficient ansätze while using fewer entangling gates.

Method. It employs a nested optimization loop: an inner VQS loop optimizes parameters using quantum gradients, while an outer Reinforcement Learning (RL) loop updates the circuit architecture (the policy) based on the resulting Fisher Information.

Model / system. The system is an $n$-qubit quantum processor used for parameter estimation. The methodology scales from single-agent search for small systems to a distributed multi-agent formulation for large, lattice-connected systems.

Key observables. Quantum Fisher Information (QFI) and Classical Fisher Information (CFI) are the primary objectives, used to define the RL reward function.

Important parameters / regimes. The optimization involves variational parameters ($ heta$) and the discrete architectural choices (gate sequences/connectivity $A, B$).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption addressed is that existing VQS methods are limited by fixed, a priori circuit ansätze, which the RL framework overcomes.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the AUTOQSENSE workflow, comparing its performance against benchmarks like the Standard Quantum Limit (SQL) and fixed ansätze, especially showing gains in QFI and reduced gate count.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the VQS problem, identifies the architectural limitation, proposes the RL framework (AutoQSense), details the single-agent and multi-agent implementations, and validates the approach by showing performance gains and structural insights.

Abstract

Variational quantum sensing offers a promising route to high-precision parameter estimation, but its performance depends strongly on the circuit architectures used for probe preparation and measurement. Existing approaches typically optimize continuous parameters within predefined ansätze, restricting the accessible design space and limiting adaptation to sensing tasks and hardware constraints. Here, we introduce \textsc{AutoQSense}, a reinforcement-learning framework that searches circuit architectures using Fisher-information-based objectives. For few-qubit systems, a single agent sequentially constructs preparation and measurement circuits. For larger systems, a distributed formulation assigns local circuit design to subsystem agents and inter-block entanglement to a budgeted agent. Numerical results show that the learned architectures recover known benchmark strategies, adapt to dephasing noise, and outperform fixed hardware-efficient ansätze while using fewer entangling gates. These results establish \textsc{AutoQSense} as a resource-aware approach to adaptive and hardware-compatible quantum sensing.

CLOPS: Benchmarking System Speed at Utility Scale

Authors: Andrew Wack
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.18044v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

Summary. This paper introduces CLOPSh, a new benchmark metric designed to accurately measure the sustained operational speed of large-scale quantum processors. It addresses the gap in current metrics by defining speed based on layered, hardware-aware circuits, ensuring that speed measurements are intrinsically linked to independently verified quality levels. This provides a necessary, holistic tool for assessing the true utility potential of quantum hardware.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a rigorous, multi-level framework for benchmarking quantum hardware performance, which is crucial for any theoretical work relying on realistic estimates of quantum computation time or resource scaling.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Existing quantum speed metrics fail to capture the sustained, end-to-end throughput required for utility-scale quantum algorithms, especially those involving classical-quantum feedback loops.

Main result. The authors propose CLOPSh (Circuit Layer Operations Per Second), a holistic benchmark that measures sustained throughput by defining speed over hardware-aware, layered circuits, thereby linking speed directly to independently verified quality envelopes.

Method. The work formalizes a new benchmarking metric, CLOPSh, which measures the rate of executing physical circuit layers, ensuring the speed measurement is structurally compatible with existing Layer Fidelity (LF) quality benchmarks.

Model / system. The focus is on scaling quantum processors to hundreds of qubits, analyzing performance across an abstraction hierarchy (Qubit $ ightarrow$ Device $ ightarrow$ System $ ightarrow$ Application) to address utility-scale workloads.

Key observables. CLOPSh (Circuit Layer Operations Per Second), Max Circuits Per Second (MCPS), Layer Fidelity (LF), and the overall system throughput rate.

Important parameters / regimes. Utility-scale workloads, layered/parameterized circuits, and the need to account for classical-quantum overheads.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that sustained, steady-state throughput, measured layer-by-layer, is the correct metric to guide hardware development beyond simple gate counts or fidelity measurements.

Figures summary. The notes mention a table organizing representative benchmarks across four levels of abstraction (Qubit, Device, System, Application) for Scale, Quality, and Speed, and a figure illustrating physical sublayers as the counting unit for CLOPSh.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the limitations of existing metrics (QV, gate rates), introduces the comprehensive CLOPS framework, and then details CLOPSh as the specific, necessary instantiation for the utility-scale regime, ensuring speed and quality are measured coherently.

Abstract

As quantum processors scale to hundreds of qubits, execution speed is a critical performance dimension alongside scale and quality. While substantial progress has been made in benchmarking circuit fidelity, existing speed metrics often fail to reflect the sustained, end-to-end throughput experienced by users running utility-scale workloads. This shortfall is especially pronounced for layered, parameterized circuits executed repeatedly within classical-quantum workflows, such as variational algorithms and error-mitigated simulations. In this work, we formalize CLOPS_h (Circuit Layer Operations Per Second) as a holistic speed benchmark defined over layered, hardware-aware circuits. CLOPS_h measures the sustained rate at which the system executes physical layers, parallel slices of qubit-disjoint two-qubit gates separated by synchronization barriers. Because each such layer is one time slice of an N-qubit circuit, this rate maps directly to the execution rate of layered $N$-qubit circuits, connecting CLOPS_h to published device capability claims, and to the device-level throughput ceiling we formalize as Max Circuits Per Second (MCPS). CLOPS_h is obtained under layer-fidelity operating conditions, binding the speed measurement to an independently verified quality envelope, and it shares its layer decomposition with scalable layer-fidelity (LF) quality benchmarks, enabling coherent interpretation of speed and quality without conflating the two.

Dynamic Entanglement-Weighted Pruning for Quantum Federated Unlearning in Supply-Chain Risk Prediction

Authors: Aditya Kumar, Sumit Chongder
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.17069v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work introduces Entanglement-Weighted Pruning (EWP), a novel unlearning technique for quantum federated learning. EWP prunes quantum circuit parameters by combining data sensitivity (via QFIM) and structural importance (via entanglement weight). It demonstrates that this method achieves near-perfect data removal accuracy while drastically reducing computational overhead compared to full model retraining.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a concrete, quantum-enhanced technique for model auditing and data removal, which is crucial for deploying quantum machine learning models in regulated, multi-party settings.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of 'machine unlearning' in Quantum Federated Learning (QFL) to efficiently remove the influence of a specific client's data contribution, satisfying data privacy regulations like GDPR.

Main result. The proposed Entanglement-Weighted Pruning (EWP) method achieved post-unlearning accuracy statistically indistinguishable from full retraining while requiring significantly less computational time.

Method. EWP scores each trainable circuit parameter by multiplying the diagonal entry of the Quantum Fisher Information Matrix (QFIM) by a structural entanglement weight derived from the gate's coupling.

Model / system. The model uses variational quantum classifiers trained on a four-qubit data-re-uploading ansatz within a federated learning framework (FedAvg).

Key observables. Classification accuracy, AUROC, forgetting score, membership-advantage gap, and wall-clock time.

Important parameters / regimes. The trainable circuit parameters ($ heta_k$), the QFIM diagonal entries ($F_{kk}$), and the structural entanglement weight ($w_{ent}(k)$).

Assumptions / limitations. The method relies on the diagonal approximation of the QFIM and assumes that parameters with low combined scores can be safely pruned without significant performance loss.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the end-to-end EWP pipeline across six stages, while Figure 2 details the federated communication workflow during training and forgetting.

Paper structure. The paper outlines the QFL setup, introduces the EWP mechanism combining QFIM and entanglement weights, details the pruning process, and benchmarks EWP against various baselines using simulated supply-chain risk data.

Abstract

Federated deployments of variational quantum classifiers are attractive for cross-organisation risk prediction in supply chains, because raw data never leaves the client, yet data-protection regulations such as the GDPR grant clients a right to request that their contribution be removed from a trained model after the fact. Retraining a federated model from scratch to honour such a request is correct but wasteful, and it is not obvious which quantum circuit parameters actually carry a given client's influence. We introduce Entanglement-Weighted Pruning (EWP), an unlearning procedure for quantum federated learning that scores every trainable circuit parameter with the product of two signals: the diagonal entry of the quantum Fisher information matrix estimated on the target client's data via the parameter-shift rule, and a structural entanglement weight associated with the parameter's gate. Parameters with the lowest scores are pruned, optionally followed by a short fine-tuning pass on the retained clients. We implement the full pipeline in Qiskit for a four-qubit data-re-uploading ansatz trained with FedAvg across five simulated supply-chain-risk clients, and benchmark EWP against full retraining, fine-tuning alone, random pruning, Fisher-only pruning, and entanglement-only pruning, over three random seeds. EWP attains a mean post-unlearning accuracy statistically indistinguishable from the full-retraining oracle, while producing a lower forgetting score and requiring roughly 16 times less wall-clock time. Ablations over pruning threshold, client count, and non-IID strength show that combining the two signals is necessary, as entanglement-only and Fisher-only pruning each substantially degrade accuracy relative to EWP.

Exact Moments of Gaussian Gram Hafnians Reveal an $n^2/\log n$ Threshold for Weak Anticoncentration

Authors: Hongru Zhao
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.17065v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical paper analyzes the moments of the Gaussian Gram Hafnian, a key observable in Gaussian Boson Sampling. By calculating the moment ratio $R_{k,n}$, the authors establish that the threshold for weak anticoncentration scales as $k \sim n^2 / \log n$. This provides a precise mathematical boundary for the performance limits of the surrogate model used to study quantum interference effects.

Why it may be interesting. The connection between moment calculations of quantum observables (like the Hafnian) and establishing fundamental scaling thresholds (like the $n^2/\log n$ boundary) is highly relevant for understanding the limits of quantum advantage in photonic quantum information processing.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper determines the scaling threshold for 'weak anticoncentration' by analyzing the exact moments of Gaussian Gram Hafnians.

Main result. The critical scaling order boundary for weak anticoncentration is found to be $k \sim n^2 / \log n$, contrasting with the smooth Bessel crossover observed at $k \sim n^2$.

Method. The analysis involves calculating the second and fourth moments of the Hafnian, $H_{k,n}$, by reducing them to rank two Gaussian integrals and expressing the resulting moment ratio in terms of terminating generalized hypergeometric polynomials.

Model / system. The study uses the independent Gaussian surrogate model for collision-free Gaussian Boson Sampling (GBS). The core mathematical object is the Gram Hafnian, $H_{k,n} = ext{haf}(X^T X)$, where $X$ has independent standard circular complex Gaussian entries.

Key observables. The moment ratio $R_{k,n} = (\mathbb{E}|H_{k,n}|^2)^2 / \mathbb{E}|H_{k,n}|^4$ is the primary diagnostic quantity.

Important parameters / regimes. The ratio $k/n^2$ (squeezed input modes to detected photons) and the logarithmic scaling factor $\log n$ are crucial parameters.

Assumptions / limitations. The results are strictly limited to the independent Gaussian surrogate moment criterion and do not apply to finite dimensional Haar moment transfer or high probability small ball anticoncentration.

Figures summary. Figure 1 compares the exact finite sum ratio $R_{k,n}$ against the theoretical Bessel limit, marking the $k=n^2$ crossover line.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by deriving exact moment formulas for the Hafnian, establishing the closed-form expression for the moment ratio $R_{k,n}$ using hypergeometric functions, and finally analyzing the asymptotic behavior to pinpoint the $n^2/\log n$ threshold for weak anticoncentration.

Abstract

Anticoncentration is central to hardness arguments for approximate sampling. In the independent Gaussian surrogate for collision free Gaussian boson sampling, the moment ratio studied here also determines the averaged ideal linear cross entropy reference value. Let $H_{k,n}=\mathrm{haf}(X^{\mathsf T}X)$, where $X\in\mathbb{C}^{k\times 2n}$ has independent standard circular complex Gaussian entries. We evaluate $\mathbb{E}|H_{k,n}|^2$ and $\mathbb{E}|H_{k,n}|^4$ exactly by reducing four hafnian copies to a rank two Gaussian integral. For $R_{k,n}=(\mathbb{E}|H_{k,n}|^2)^2/\mathbb{E}|H_{k,n}|^4$, we obtain $R_{k,n}=4^{-n}\binom{2n}{n}/F_{k,n}$, where $F_{k,n}={}3F_2(-n,-n,1/2;1,k/2;1)$ is a terminating generalized hypergeometric polynomial. If $k/n^2\to c>0$, then $F{k,n}\to e^{1/c}I_0(1/c)$, where $I_0$ is the modified Bessel function of the first kind of order zero, and consequently $R_{k,n}\sqrt{πn}\to[e^{1/c}I_0(1/c)]^{-1}$. Thus $k\asymp n^2$ is a smooth Bessel crossover, whereas the scaling order boundary for inverse polynomial weak anticoncentration is $k\asymp n^2/\log n$. These conclusions concern the Gaussian surrogate moment criterion; finite dimensional Haar moment transfer and high probability small ball anticoncentration remain separate problems.

Incidence-based random walks on simplicial complexes

Authors: C. T. Martínez-Martínez, Francisco J. Sevilla
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.17229v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work develops a framework for random walks on simplicial complexes, modeling transport via two coupled channels: vertex-mediated and face-mediated. By analyzing the effect of partially retained triangles, the authors demonstrate that intermediate levels of structural disorder create a pronounced transport bottleneck. This establishes a simple yet powerful method to quantify how higher-order connectivity reshapes network dynamics.

Why it may be interesting. The study of transport bottlenecks due to structural heterogeneity is highly relevant to understanding energy or information flow in complex, disordered physical systems, which shares conceptual links with glassy dynamics or disordered quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how incorporating heterogeneous higher-order connectivity (triangles) into a random walk fundamentally alters its spectral and transport properties compared to simple pairwise network dynamics.

Main result. The analysis reveals that the most pronounced transport bottleneck—manifested as maximum mean first-passage time—occurs in an intermediate regime of partial face dilution, suggesting heterogeneity is key.

Method. The authors analyze the effective transition operator governing the random walk on the edges of a simplicial complex, using spectral analysis and calculating mean first-passage times.

Model / system. The model is an incidence-based random walk defined on the edges of a random 2D simplicial complex. The dynamics combine two channels: one mediated by vertices (lower) and one by triangular faces (upper), controlled by a mixing parameter q.

Key observables. Second-largest eigenvalue modulus, inverse participation ratio, mean first-passage time, and the survival probability of first-passage time.

Important parameters / regimes. Face retention probability (p) and the mixing parameter (q), which control the degree of higher-order connectivity.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the underlying 1-skeleton is fixed (complete graph) and uses a random ensemble approach to study quenched structural heterogeneity.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the dependence of mean first-passage time on face density (p) and the survival probability of first-passage times across different dilution regimes.

Paper structure. The paper builds the effective transition operator by combining elementary steps via vertices and triangles. It then analyzes the resulting dynamics across different regimes (graph-like vs. fully simplicial) to pinpoint the conditions causing dynamical slowdown.

Abstract

We introduce an incidence-based random walk on the edges of a random two-dimensional simplicial complex with a complete $1$-skeleton and independently retained triangular faces. The dynamics combine two transport channels, one mediated by vertices and the other by triangular faces, through an effective transition operator controlled by a mixing parameter $q$. This construction isolates the effects of higher-order connectivity without modifying the underlying pairwise support of the walk. We characterize the model through structural observables, spectral relaxation, stationary localization, and first-passage transport. Our results show that partial face retention generates heterogeneous higher-order connectivity, giving rise to a pronounced transport bottleneck at intermediate face densities. In this regime, the second-largest eigenvalue modulus, the inverse participation ratio of the stationary distribution, and the mean first-passage time all exhibit non-monotonic behavior, reaching their largest values at intermediate face densities. The corresponding first-passage-time distributions reveal an enhanced probability of unusually long trajectories. Together, these results establish a simple framework for investigating how heterogeneous higher-order connectivity reshapes spectral and transport properties beyond pairwise network dynamics.

Iterative tensor network transformations for element-wise evaluation of elementary and filtering functions

Summary. The paper introduces Iterative Tensor Network Transformations (ITNT), a novel framework allowing general nonlinear functions to be evaluated directly on data compressed in the Tensor Train format. By leveraging iterative root-finding techniques, ITNT achieves high accuracy for complex physical simulations, such as calculating reaction rates in fluid dynamics and solving NP-hard optimization problems. This establishes tensor networks as a tool for general-purpose, large-scale data science.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, general-purpose computational tool that extends tensor network methods beyond linear operations, making them applicable to complex, nonlinear physics problems previously requiring computationally expensive full-space sampling.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core challenge is developing a general algorithmic framework to perform element-wise evaluation of arbitrary, nonlinear filtering functions directly on data encoded in the compressed Tensor Train (TT) format.

Main result. The proposed Iterative Tensor Network Transformations (ITNT) successfully enables high-fidelity computation of nonlinear functions (like reaction rates) and locating global extrema in exponentially large configuration spaces.

Method. ITNT utilizes iterative techniques, such as the Newton-Raphson method, combined with efficient TT operations (element-wise product, partial integration) to operate entirely in the compressed domain.

Model / system. The methods are demonstrated on several models: 3D reactive flow fields (methane/air jet flame) for continuous field evaluation, and classical Ising models/Max-SAT instances for combinatorial optimization.

Key observables. Reaction rates (Arrhenius factor), global energy extrema ($E_{opt}$), and the relative $\ell_2$ error ($ ilde{\epsilon}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Bond dimension ($\chi$), number of iterations ($N_{ ext{iter}}$), and the required preprocessing steps (e.g., self-multiplication power $P$).

Assumptions / limitations. The framework assumes that the required functions can be mapped to iterative root-finding problems solvable via the Newton-Raphson method or related fixed-point iterations.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the methodology for nonlinear evaluation (Fig. 1a, 1b) and the application to optimization, showing convergence comparisons between ITNT and interpolation methods (TCI).

Paper structure. The paper introduces ITNT, details its application to continuous nonlinear PDEs (reactive flow), and then extends it to discrete, high-dimensional optimization problems like Max-SAT and Ising model energy matching.

Abstract

Tensor networks are powerful formats for compressing large-scale data. However, their application to general data processing has been limited by the difficulty of performing nonlinear operations. Here, we introduce iterative tensor network transformations (ITNTs), a general algorithmic framework for the element-wise evaluation of elementary and nonlinear filtering functions on data encoded as tensor trains (TTs), a class of tensor networks. Our approach operates entirely in the compressed domain, enabling efficient computation on exponentially large datasets while maintaining a controlled computational cost. We demonstrate its power in two key areas: (I) evaluating highly nonlinear elementary and filtering functions on a 3D reactive flow field, enabling high-fidelity reaction rate computation and region filtering, and (II) finding extrema in complex optimization problems, such as solving Max-SAT instances on spaces up to $2^{70}$ configurations. These results establish ITNT as a foundational tool that provides tensor network methods with the capability for general-purpose data science and large-scale optimization.

Summary. This paper proposes QDAGer, a quantum-inspired graph Transformer, to enhance graph learning by incorporating dynamical features derived from the Transverse-Field Ising Model. The authors show that the time evolution of quantum observables acts as a powerful structural signal, enabling the model to learn complex metrics like Graph Edit Distance better than purely classical structural descriptors.

Why it may be interesting. The work bridges quantum many-body physics (Ising models, dynamics) with modern machine learning (Graph Transformers), suggesting that inherent symmetries and time-evolution properties of quantum systems can serve as powerful, structured inductive biases for solving classically hard problems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To develop graph representations for graph learning that capture structural information while respecting permutation invariance, particularly for hard tasks like graph comparison.

Main result. The proposed quantum-inspired architecture, QDAGer, demonstrates that dynamical features derived from the Ising model provide a stronger inductive bias for learning Graph Edit Distance than classical structural features.

Method. The authors introduce QDAGer, a Transformer that injects time-series data from quantum observables (node occupations and two-point correlators) into the attention mechanism for graph-pair comparison.

Model / system. The underlying physical model is the quantum evolution governed by a graph-indexed Transverse-Field Ising Hamiltonian ($H_G$). The dynamics are analyzed by measuring time-dependent expectation values of local observables.

Key observables. Node occupations $\langle \hat{ u}_u(t) angle$ and connected two-point correlators $\langle \hat{ u}_u \hat{ u}_v(t) angle - \langle \hat{ u}_u(t) angle \langle \hat{ u}_v(t) angle$.

Important parameters / regimes. The performance is tested across various Graph Edit Distance (GED) benchmarks using different edit cost settings.

Assumptions / limitations. The quantum dynamics are simulated via emulation, and the method is positioned as 'quantum-inspired' rather than claiming quantum computational advantage.

Abstract

We study the quantum evolution induced by graph-indexed Ising Hamiltonians as a source of structural signal for graph learning. Graph automorphisms preserve symmetries of the Hamiltonian, and these symmetries constrain the quantum evolution in a way that turns time-dependent local measurements into informative probes of graph structure. Leveraging this idea, we introduce QDAGer, a quantum-inspired graph-pair Transformer that injects quantum-dynamical features from time series of node occupations and connected two-point correlators directly into the attention mechanism. We apply QDAGer to learning Graph Edit Distance (GED), an NP-hard similarity measure, using either a direct permutation-invariant embedding discrepancy or an alignment-based surrogate loss. Experiments on multiple GED benchmarks under different edit cost settings show that the proposed dynamical features provide a stronger inductive bias than classical structural alternatives under the same training protocol. In addition, we report ablations where the dynamical signal is replaced by standard random-walk and heat-kernel features while keeping the architecture fixed, highlighting that the gain comes from the injected dynamics rather than model capacity alone.

Operational thresholds of Bell mixtures with complex X noise

Authors: Xuan Du Trinh
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.17609v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work rigorously maps the boundaries in parameter space where a noisy Bell state retains specific quantum resources like entanglement or teleportation usefulness. By analyzing the singular-value flow of correlation tensors, the authors establish universal ordering chains between these operational thresholds. The findings are vital for understanding the robustness of quantum information protocols under realistic noise models.

Why it may be interesting. It provides rigorous, quantitative boundaries for quantum resources in realistic noisy channels, which is crucial for designing fault-tolerant quantum communication protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper characterizes how various operational quantum abilities (entanglement, teleportation usefulness, steerability, etc.) are maintained or lost in noisy Bell mixtures as the noise admixture changes.

Main result. The operational abilities are distinct, forming a universal ordered chain of thresholds as the noise parameter varies, and the unique separable/unsteerable point is found at zero noise admixture ($\lambda=0$).

Method. The analysis relies on characterizing the singular-value flow of the Pauli correlation tensor and using semidefinite programming to determine exact thresholds for various quantum witnesses.

Model / system. The system is a mixture of a pure Bell state ($\Phi^+$) and an arbitrary complex two-qubit X noise state ($\sigma_X$), parameterized by the Bell weight $\lambda$: $ ho_\lambda = \lambda\Phi^+ + (1 - \lambda)\sigma_X$.

Key observables. Entanglement, teleportation usefulness, projective-measurement steerability, CJWR violation, and CHSH violation.

Important parameters / regimes. Bell weight ($\lambda$), relative phase ($ heta$) between noise coherence and Bell coherence, and the noise parameters defining $\sigma_X$.

Assumptions / limitations. For generic full-rank mixed X noise, upper bounds on steerability thresholds are obtained via finite-setting semidefinite programs.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates operational phase diagrams in the $(\lambda, heta)$ plane showing the nesting and widening of ability-absence intervals for various noise states.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the thresholds for different operational abilities, deriving closed-form flows for specific noise types (like product noise) and proving the unique unsteerable point ($\lambda=0$) using recurrence relations for a swapping chain.

Abstract

Noisy Bell mixtures may carry entanglement, standard teleportation usefulness, projective-measurement steerability, and optimized Cavalcanti-Jones-Wiseman-Reid (CJWR) and Clauser-Horne-Shimony-Holt (CHSH) violations. If the noise already has an ability, the mixture may lose and later recover it at distinct boundary crossings as the Bell weight decreases. We characterize ability-absence intervals and definitive thresholds for mixtures of $|Φ^+\rangle$ with arbitrary complex two-qubit $X$ noise. A closed-form singular-value flow of the Pauli correlation tensor gives the exact teleportation-useless, CJWR-satisfying, and CHSH-local intervals, while the positive partial transpose criterion gives the exact separable interval. At fixed populations and coherence magnitudes, these intervals widen as the relative phase between the $Φ$-block coherence of the noise and the Bell coherence increases from $0$ to $π$. The definitive thresholds form a universal chain from entanglement through teleportation usefulness and the three-setting CJWR witness to the common two-setting CJWR witness and CHSH-nonlocal threshold. Replacing teleportation usefulness by steerability gives a second chain, although their thresholds are not mutually ordered. For arbitrary pure two-qubit noise, the steerability thresholds in both directions for projective measurements and arbitrary positive operator-valued measures equal the entanglement threshold. For arbitrary product noise, an effective $X$-state singular-value flow gives the teleportation usefulness, CJWR witness, and CHSH-nonlocal thresholds. If a local noise factor is pure, the entanglement threshold and all four steerability thresholds are zero. For generic full-rank mixed $X$ noise, finite-setting semidefinite programs give upper bounds on the unknown directional projective-measurement steerability thresholds.

A geodesic distance interpretation of Lanczos coefficients

Authors: Le-Chen Qu
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.16982v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper analyzes the spread complexity of quantum states in large-N random matrix models by focusing on the Lanczos coefficients. It derives nonlinear equations governing these coefficients using the Coulomb-gas formalism. Crucially, it establishes a deep connection showing that these coefficients generate counts of planar maps, linking quantum dynamics to combinatorics.

Why it may be interesting. The connection between spectral properties (Lanczos coefficients) derived from complex many-body systems (RMT) and fundamental combinatorial objects (planar maps) provides a powerful, non-obvious mathematical structure applicable to understanding quantum chaos and complexity in physical systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the physical meaning of Lanczos coefficients in the context of the spread complexity of the infinite-temperature thermofield double state within large-N random matrix theory.

Main result. The Lanczos coefficients are shown to satisfy nonlinear 'Lanczos equations' derived from the Coulomb-gas equation, and they generate counts of planar maps related to the geodesic distance.

Method. The analysis uses the orthogonal polynomial approach associated with the leading density of states, relating the dynamics to a three-term recurrence relation whose coefficients are the Lanczos coefficients.

Model / system. The study focuses on the infinite-temperature thermofield double state in large-N random matrix ensembles, which is analyzed via its associated orthogonal polynomials and the resulting Krylov dynamics.

Key observables. Spread complexity, Lanczos coefficients ($a_n, b_n$), and the leading density of states ($ ho_0(E)$).

Important parameters / regimes. Large-N limit, Krylov index, and the structure of the matrix potential (e.g., quartic potential).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the large-N limit and the mapping of dynamics to the Krylov chain structure.

Figures summary. Not specified in the notes, but the text references figures illustrating the bijection between coefficients and planar maps.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by establishing the connection between the dynamics (survival amplitude $S(t)$) and the orthogonal polynomial structure, deriving the recurrence relations, finding the nonlinear 'Lanczos equations' from the Coulomb-gas equation, and finally interpreting these coefficients combinatorially via planar maps.

Abstract

We study the spread complexity of the infinite-temperature thermofield double state in large-$N$ random matrix theory and investigate the physical meaning of its Lanczos coefficients. The leading density of states defines a family of orthogonal polynomials whose recursion coefficients coincide with the Lanczos coefficients of the associated Krylov dynamics. Starting from the Coulomb-gas equation, we derive a pair of nonlinear relations for these coefficients, which we call the Lanczos equations. Since the Lanczos coefficients approach constants at large Krylov index, the spread complexity grows linearly at late times. We further show that the Lanczos coefficients can be interpreted as generating functions for planar maps. This proves $Λ(a,α) \asymp \log{(1/a)}$ as $a \uparrow 1$ and shows that the ratio $Λ(a,α)/\log{(1/a)}$ converges to a limit in $(0,α/2]$, equal to its supremum over $0 < a < 1$. Finally, a Lamperti transformation reduces identification of this constant to a dense-sampling persistence problem for a stationary stable Ornstein--Uhlenbeck process. Existing Gaussian theory determines the sharp value at $α=2$. For $0 < α< 2$, identifying the value requires controlling paths that cross below zero and return above zero between consecutive observations.

Abstract

Gate-level noise degrades the classification accuracy of Quantum Support Vector Machines (QSVMs) on Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) hardware, and the degree of degradation depends on how classical data is encoded into quantum states. We tested Z, ZZ, a Pauli, and an amplitude-inspired feature maps under depolarizing, bit-flip, and phase-flip noise channels in $52$ controlled experiments with error probabilities $p =0.01, 0.05, 0.10$, and $0.50$. The amplitude-inspired feature map had $100$\% test accuracy up to $p = 0.10$ across all three noise channels, while other feature maps fell to $65$-$90$\% under the same noise level and type. The Z feature map was found to be immune to phase-flip noise to a significantly high error rate, a consequence of the commutation relation $[R_Z, Z] = 0$. Entangled circuit variants produced generalization gaps in train-test sets of up to $17.5$\% under noise, whereas the amplitude variants maintained zero gap throughout. These results give practitioners data-driven criteria for a feature map on near-term quantum hardware.

Summary. This paper presents an improved method for compiling general unitary quantum circuits into the resource-constrained Clifford+T model. By employing block flattening and Quantum Singular Value Transformation, the authors reduce the worst-case T-gate count scaling from $O(2^{4n/3})$ to $O(2^{5n/4})$. This advancement is crucial for making large-scale, fault-tolerant quantum computation more resource-efficient.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the resource overhead in fault-tolerant quantum computation, which is critical for realizing large-scale quantum processors.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The central problem is synthesizing arbitrary n-qubit unitaries using the minimum possible number of non-Clifford gates (T-count) for fault-tolerant quantum compilation.

Main result. The authors achieve a worst-case T-count scaling of O(2^(5n/4)), improving upon previous bounds of O(2^(4n/3)).

Method. The technique involves treating the target unitary as a block-encoded object, which is then flattened using block flattening and recovered via Quantum Singular Value Transformation (QSVT).

Model / system. The model is quantum computation implemented by unitary transformations U in the Clifford+T circuit model, acting on n qubits.

Key observables. T-count (the primary cost measure), approximation error (ε), and the exponential scaling of the T-count.

Important parameters / regimes. n (number of qubits), ε (approximation error), and the scaling exponents (e.g., 5/4).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the error $\log(1/\epsilon)$ is polynomial in $n$, and that classical pre-processing time is excluded from the quantum resource count.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the need for efficient unitary synthesis, then detailing the block flattening technique using Walsh transforms, followed by the block encoding via Julia-Halmos dilation, and finally applying QSVT to achieve the improved T-count bounds.

Abstract

Synthesizing arbitrary $n$-qubit unitaries using as few non-Clifford gates as possible is a central problem in fault-tolerant quantum compilation. We present a Clifford+$T$ quantum circuit construction that approximately implements any classically specified unitary to within error $ε$ and achieves a worst-case $T$-count with leading exponential scaling of $2^{5n/4}$ whenever $\log(1/ε)=\operatorname{poly}(n)$. This improves upon the best previous $2^{4n/3}$ scaling. The key innovation lies in treating the target unitary as a single block-encoded object rather than a long product of simpler operations. A technique of block flattening controls the normalization while preserving an efficient implementation of the block encoding; subsequently, quantum singular value transformation maps its common singular value to one, thereby recovering the target unitary.
