Simplicial closure fragments the explosive cooperation transitions in higher-order public goods games

Authors: Wenjia Rao, Jianneng Huang
Type: theory · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.17968v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work compares cooperation dynamics in higher-order public goods games modeled on hypergraphs (HG) versus simplicial complexes (SC). The key finding is that simplicial closure selectively restructures the system's attractor landscape: it fragments sharp, first-order cooperation transitions into a broad ensemble of metastable states. This demonstrates that the geometric representation of interactions fundamentally dictates the nature of collective phase transitions.

Why it may be interesting. The concept of structural constraints (like simplicial closure) leading to qualitative changes in collective dynamics, particularly the fragmentation of stable attractors, is relevant to understanding phase transitions in complex systems modeled by network theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how the structural constraint of simplicial closure (SC) alters the cooperative dynamics observed in higher-order public goods games (PGGs) compared to the more general hypergraph (HG) model.

Main result. Simplicial closure selectively fragments the compact bistability seen in explosive, first-order cooperation transitions, replacing it with a broad ensemble of metastable states, while having only quantitative effects on continuous transitions.

Method. The authors compare the mean final cooperation level and the distribution of final states by simulating PGGs on both HG and SC representations derived from the same backbone.

Model / system. The model is a higher-order Public Goods Game (PGG) incorporating pairwise and three-player interactions, analyzed using a Fermi imitation rule for strategy updating.

Key observables. Mean final cooperation level ($\rho_C$), the shape of the cooperation transition (continuous vs. first-order), and the distribution of final-state configuration overlaps ($P(Q)$).

Important parameters / regimes. The payoff exponent $\gamma$ (controlling payoff curvature, e.g., $\gamma=0.5$ vs. $\gamma=2$) and the degree of closure interpolation ($p$).

Assumptions / limitations. The comparison assumes that the only difference between HG and SC is the enforcement of simplicial closure on the three-player interactions.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate that for first-order transitions ($\gamma=2$), HG shows compact bimodal bistability, whereas SC shows a broad distribution of metastable states, and this effect is visible across different payoff forms.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the model, compares the HG and SC representations across different payoff regimes ($\gamma$), and systematically analyzes the resulting changes in transition shape, bistability structure, and final-state distributions.

Abstract

Hypergraph (HG) and simplicial complex (SC) are two common representations of higher-order networks, and are often expected to differ mainly quantitatively when they encode comparable group interactions. Here we show that this expectation fails in evolutionary cooperation dynamics. Using a controlled higher-order public goods game (PGG) with minimal ad hoc parameters, we compare cooperation transitions on randomized HG and SC constructed from the same triangular backbone. We find that the impact of simplicial closure is selective: when the cooperation transition is continuous-like on HG, imposing simplicial closure mainly broadens the transition without changing its qualitative nature; however, when the transition is explosive and first-order-like on HG, simplicial closure will fragment the compact low/high bistability into a broad ensemble of metastable final states. Detailed analysis reveals that this selective effect arises from the dual role of simplicial closure in cooperative-nucleus dynamics: it promotes the survival of local cooperative nuclei while suppressing their conversion into system-wide cascades. Further experiments confirm that this fragmentation is not tied to a specific payoff form, but is a generic feature of explosive cooperation transitions in higher-order PGGs.

Summary. This theoretical study treats the Bethe-Heitler process as a quantum laboratory to study multipartite entanglement among the final electron, proton, and photon. By analyzing the helicity structure and using entanglement measures, the authors predict and simulate the existence of numerous GHZ and W states. The results confirm that genuine tripartite entanglement can be generated in this process, although these states occupy a much smaller volume in phase space than simpler bipartite entangled states.

Why it may be interesting. This work applies quantum information concepts (GHZ/W states) to a well-understood, high-energy particle physics process, providing a concrete, calculable physical realization for testing entanglement generation mechanisms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study focuses on quantifying and characterizing multipartite entanglement, specifically GHZ and W states, generated during the standard Bethe-Heitler scattering process.

Main result. The authors successfully identified hundreds of GHZ and W states in the process, demonstrating that the BH process serves as a viable, albeit highly constrained, laboratory for generating and studying genuine tripartite entanglement.

Method. The analysis involves calculating helicity amplitudes for the three-particle final state and using entanglement measures (concurrence, $F_{GHZ}^3$, $F_W^3$) to map out the phase space regions where these entangled states are formed.

Model / system. The system is the Bethe-Heitler process ($e+p o e+p+\gamma$), where the final electron, proton, and photon are treated as three qubits based on their helicity states. Entanglement is built up through successive elementary interactions.

Key observables. Bipartite concurrences ($C_{\ell p}, C_{\ell \gamma}, C_{p\gamma}$), genuine tripartite entanglement measures ($F_{GHZ}^3, F_W^3$), and the local-unitary fidelity ($F$).

Important parameters / regimes. Center-of-mass energy ($\sqrt{s} < 5$ GeV), mixing angles ($\alpha_e, \alpha_p$), and specific kinematic regimes (e.g., backward scattering, collinear emission).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on identifying local minima in the high-dimensional phase space and estimating the volume and overlap rates of these discrete entangled regions.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the distribution of found GHZ and W states in various parameter planes (e.g., $(\alpha_e, \alpha_p)$ plane) and compare the phase space volumes of entangled states versus bipartite states.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the entanglement generation via two-step processes, calculates entanglement measures, identifies specific kinematic conditions for maximal entanglement, and finally quantifies the phase space volume occupied by the discrete GHZ and W states compared to continuous bipartite entanglement regions.

Abstract

The familiar Bethe-Heitler process on the proton target $e+p\to e+p+γ$ is transformed into a laboratory for studying multiparticle entanglement. We discuss how bipartite and genuine tripartite entanglement between the final state electron, proton and photon are built up by successive $1\to 2$ and $2\to 2$ elementary interactions. We validate our argument by simulating events. Below 5 GeV center-of-mass energy, we identify more than 900 Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger (GHZ) states and 1200 W states, each with a fidelty exceeding 99%.

Towards the Impossibility of Imperfectly Complete Key Agreement in the QROM

Authors: Fuyuki Kitagawa, Ryo Nishimaki, Agi Villanyi, Takashi Yamakawa
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.17610v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper proves the impossibility of certain quantum key agreement schemes in the QROM by constructing unconditional quantum attacks. Using advanced techniques like heavy-query learning, the authors show that if the agreement probability is not perfect, the key can be recovered by an unbounded attacker. This result has direct implications for the security of quantum public-key encryption.

Why it may be interesting. While highly theoretical, the rigorous analysis of information-theoretic security boundaries in quantum cryptography provides foundational tools for understanding the limits of quantum communication protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the theoretical impossibility of achieving imperfectly complete Quantum-Computation, Classical-Communication (QCCC) key agreement protocols within the Quantum Random Oracle Model (QROM).

Main result. It rules out imperfectly correct quantum public-key encryption (QPKE) for classical messages whose length is polynomially bounded in the QROM under specific conditions.

Method. The analysis constructs unconditional quantum attacks by combining heavy-query learning techniques with random reprogramming lemmas.

Model / system. The model is the QROM applied to key agreement protocols, considering settings like two-message and multi-round interactions where parties perform quantum computations and make oracle queries.

Key observables. The valid agreement probability ($\alpha_{\Pi}(\lambda)$) and the attacker's ability to recover the shared key.

Important parameters / regimes. The query bounds ($q_{A,1}, q_{B}, q_{A,2}$, etc.) and the inverse-polynomial nature of the valid agreement probability.

Assumptions / limitations. The attacker is computationally unbounded, and the honest query bounds are polynomially bounded in $\lambda$.

Figures summary. Not specified, but the notes detail theorems and corollaries establishing quantitative bounds on the advantage of the attack.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by analyzing specific settings (two-message, multi-round) to establish attack bounds, culminating in the impossibility result for QPKE.

Abstract

We make progress towards the impossibility of imperfectly complete quantum-computation, classical-communication (QCCC) key agreement by constructing the first unconditional attacks on quantum key agreement in the following restricted settings. In the two-message setting, we assume that Alice makes only classical queries to the oracle in the first round and that her message to Bob is classical, but otherwise both parties may perform arbitrary quantum computation, make quantum queries, and send a quantum state in the second round. Our attack and analysis are based on the heavy-query learning techniques from Austrin et al. (CRYPTO 2022) and the reprogramming techniques of Katz and Sela (arXiv 2401.14319). In the round-independent setting, we show that the attack of Barak and Mahmoody (CRYPTO 2009; J. Cryptology 2017) can be extended to multiple rounds when Alice and Bob share classical communication and make only classical queries in all but the final round. In both settings, the attacker is computationally unbounded and makes $poly(λ)$ queries to recover the key whenever each honest query bound is at most $poly(λ)$ and the valid agreement probability is inverse-polynomial. As a consequence, we rule out imperfectly correct quantum public-key encryption for classical messages whose length is bounded by a polynomial in $λ$ in the QROM when key generation has classical oracle access, even if encryption, decryption, and the ciphertext are quantum. In particular, the one-bit case applies to the imperfectly correct PKE obtained from two-round OSP by Bartusek and Khurana (CRYPTO 2025) whenever the classical OSP sender makes only classical random-oracle queries.

Tracking nonlinear solar-wind dynamics over three solar cycles using Wind observations

Authors: Dario Javier Zamora, Facundo Maximo Abaca
Type: experiment · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.17037v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

Summary. This research analyzes decades of solar wind proton density data to characterize its statistical and dynamical properties using nonextensive statistical mechanics. By calculating the q-triplet, the authors confirm persistent heavy-tailed fluctuations and slow relaxation. Crucially, they demonstrate that these plasma properties are modulated by the solar cycle, showing a strong periodicity linked to solar activity.

Why it may be interesting. The application of nonextensive statistics and multifractal analysis to space plasma dynamics provides a rigorous, non-equilibrium framework for characterizing turbulence and long-range correlations in astrophysical plasmas.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To investigate the long-term evolution and solar cycle dependence of nonlinear statistical and dynamical properties of solar wind plasma.

Main result. The statistical properties, characterized by the q-triplet, confirm persistent heavy-tailed statistics and slow relaxation, showing modulation by solar activity with a dominant periodicity of $\sim 10.1$ years.

Method. The study uses nonextensive statistical mechanics to estimate the q-triplet ($ ext{qstat}$, $ ext{qrel}$, $ ext{qsens}$) from hourly proton density measurements using sliding time windows.

Model / system. The physical system is the turbulent, weakly collisional solar wind plasma, analyzed using the Wind spacecraft's proton density measurements over three decades (1995–2025). The analysis framework is nonextensive statistical mechanics.

Key observables. The q-triplet ($ ext{qstat}$, $ ext{qrel}$, $ ext{qsens}$), proton density increments, autocorrelation function decay, and multifractal spectrum.

Important parameters / regimes. Mean values: $ ext{qstat}=1.71\pm0.07$, $ ext{qrel}=4.62\pm0.52$, $ ext{qsens}=-0.45\pm0.27$. Key regimes include $q>1$ for heavy tails and slow relaxation.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on a single-spacecraft dataset (Wind) to provide an independent test, though potential orbital transition effects must be considered.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the temporal evolution of the three q-parameters, the correlation between $ ext{qstat}$ and sunspot number, and the Fourier power spectrum of $ ext{qstat}$ showing a dominant $\sim 10.1$-year peak.

Paper structure. The paper analyzes the time series of the q-triplet over 1995–2025, calculates correlations with solar/geomagnetic indices, and uses Fourier analysis to determine periodicity and modulation by solar activity.

Abstract

The solar wind is a turbulent, weakly collisional plasma characterized by non-Gaussian fluctuations, long-range correlations, and multifractal dynamics. We investigate the long-term evolution of these properties using hourly proton density measurements obtained directly by the Wind spacecraft over 1995--2025. The use of a single-spacecraft dataset provides an independent test of previous results derived from the multi-spacecraft OMNI database. The three components of the nonextensive $q$-triplet are estimated within one-year sliding windows shifted monthly: $q_{stat}$ from the distribution of density increments, $q_{rel}$ from the decay of the autocorrelation function, and $q_{sens}$ from the multifractal spectrum. Their mean values, $q_{stat}=1.71\pm0.07$, $q_{rel}=4.62\pm0.52$, and $q_{sens}=-0.45\pm0.27$, confirm the persistent presence of heavy-tailed statistics, slow relaxation, and weakly chaotic multifractal dynamics. The parameters nevertheless exhibit distinct temporal variability and relationships with solar activity. The Fourier spectrum of $q_{stat}$ contains a dominant period of approximately $10.1$ years, while its correlation with the sunspot number is positive and moderate ($r=0.611$). Correlation and mutual-information analyses show that $q_{stat}$ is primarily associated with solar proxies, whereas $q_{sens}$ displays stronger relationships with geomagnetic indices and $q_{rel}$ exhibits weaker dependencies. An anomalous enhancement of $q_{stat}$ around 2004 coincides with a sequence of intense interplanetary disturbances, although possible effects related to Wind's orbital transition must also be considered. These findings demonstrate the robustness of the nonextensive description and show that the statistical and dynamical properties of solar wind proton density show evidence of modulation by solar activity.

Summary. This paper develops a novel 'simultaneous Goldreich-Levin reduction' applicable to quantum cryptography when parties share a common random mask. It successfully upgrades the security guarantee of unclonable encryption schemes from a search-based security notion to the stronger indistinguishability notion. This advances the theoretical understanding of quantum cryptographic security proofs.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a rigorous, information-theoretic bridge connecting two fundamental security notions (search vs. decision) in quantum cryptography, which is highly relevant for quantum information security protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to establish a 'simultaneous Goldreich-Levin reduction' to upgrade unclonable encryption schemes from 'search' security to the stronger 'unclonable indistinguishability' (decision) security.

Main result. The authors provide a general, information-theoretic reduction showing that the search security implies indistinguishability security in the common-mask setting, and demonstrate this for the simplest BB84 candidate scheme.

Method. The core method involves adapting the classical Goldreich-Levin reduction framework to the quantum setting, specifically addressing the challenge of a common random mask shared by two entangled parties.

Model / system. The security analysis is built around unclonable encryption schemes, using the BB84 monogamy-of-entanglement game as a source of initial 'search' security.

Key observables. Success probabilities in the search game ($p_{ ext{Search}}$) and the common-mask GL game ($p_{ ext{Pred}}$); the relationship $\Delta = 2p_{ ext{Pred}} - 1$.

Important parameters / regimes. The reduction yields bounds like $p_{ ext{Search}} \geq \Delta^4$, and later theorems introduce parameters like $\gamma$ and $L_{\max}$ for efficiency analysis.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the existence of a common-mask GL reduction and later theorems rely on assumptions regarding the computability of parameters ($\gamma$) or the ability to truncate sampling ($L_{\max}$).

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the conceptual difference between the search game G and its common-mask GL variant GL(G).

Paper structure. The paper develops the general reduction from the GL game to the search game, proving the existence of the reduction ($p_{ ext{Search}} \geq \Delta^4$). Subsequent sections refine this reduction, providing uniform and truncated variants that improve efficiency and robustness.

Abstract

Goldreich-Levin reductions are ubiquitous in cryptography: they convert an algorithm capable of guessing $\langle r, m \rangle$ (mod $2$) for a hidden string $m$ and a random challenge $r$, to one that is capable of extracting the entirety of $m$. Here, we describe a "simultaneous" Goldreich-Levin reduction for two entangled parties who are capable of guessing $\langle r, m \rangle$ given uniformly random identical challenges $r$. This allows to upgrade any unclonable encryption scheme satisfying "search" security to one satisfying the gold standard of unclonable "indistinguishability". As a corollary, we show that the simplest candidate unclonable encryption scheme from BB84 states satisfies unclonable indistinguishability. Summary. This paper investigates topological superconductivity in a TI nano-SQUID by measuring both global critical currents and local tunneling conductance. While global measurements show clear, flux-tunable signatures consistent with a topological phase transition, the local measurements yield ambiguous results. The authors conclude that a combined, integrated approach is necessary to definitively confirm the presence of Majorana bound states.

Main result. Global superconducting signatures (e.g., $h/2e$ flux periodicity and SDE reversal) are observed, suggesting a topological phase transition, but local measurements show ambiguous zero-bias peaks that do not correlate robustly with global signatures.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the device structure, $I_c$ vs. $B_x$ showing oscillations, and simultaneous measurements comparing global $I_c$ anomalies with local conductance spectral jumps and nonlocal conductance changes across different flux regimes.

Paper structure. Abstract

Topological systems are defined by global properties that enforce the existence of local boundary modes. Three-dimensional topological insulators (TIs) were among the earliest proposed systems for hosting topological superconductivity, but experimental focus subsequently shifted to other platforms. Here, we revisit bulk-insulating TIs using a columnar nano-superconducting quantum interference device (nano-SQUID) architecture. This geometry optimises the proximity effect on the TI surface and enables simultaneous probing of global superconducting properties - via the critical current through the nano-SQUID - alongside the local states at the ends of the nano-SQUID via tunnel junctions. We observe several global superconducting features that appear to show a flux-driven global phase transition consistent with entering the topological regime, including periodic critical current oscillations and a sign reversal in the superconducting diode effect. Simultaneously, tunnelling spectroscopy reveals spectral jumps in local and nonlocal conductance that align with these global features. However, zero-bias peaks (ZBPs) in local conductance are present both within the predicted topological range of magnetic fields and in theoretically trivial regimes, including at zero magnetic field. Ultimately, the lack of correlation between local ZBP signatures and global signatures emphasises that conclusively identifying Majorana bound states will necessitate a combined approach, integrating the establishment of global topological properties with the use of local and other, more advanced, probes.

Abstract

Magnetic droplets are highly nonlinear spin-wave solitons that can be excited in nanoscale spintronic devices with strong perpendicular magnetic anisotropy. Although extensively studied in nanocontact-based spin-torque oscillators, their stabilization in pure spin current-driven devices such as spin Hall nano-oscillators (SHNOs) has remained elusive. Here, we micromagnetically demonstrate the robust stabilization of non-propagating high-frequency droplets in CMOS-compatible W/CoFeB/MgO nanoconstriction SHNOs under oblique magnetic fields. Summary. This paper reports on the design and experimental realization of a solid-state refrigerator that cools silicon chips to sub-Kelvin temperatures using arrays of NIS superconducting junctions. The methodology involves modeling quasi-particle diffusion and meticulously engineering the interfaces to minimize parasitic heat leakage. The success demonstrates a significant advance in solid-state cryocooling technology.

Why it may be interesting. The detailed modeling of non-equilibrium heat transport, involving energy filtering via superconducting gaps and diffusive quasi-particle dynamics, touches upon non-equilibrium statistical mechanics and energy relaxation processes relevant to quantum thermodynamics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To develop a scalable, solid-state refrigerator capable of cooling silicon chips to temperatures below 1 K by efficiently managing heat transfer and minimizing parasitic heat leakage.

Main result. The authors demonstrated cooling of a silicon chip down to 70 mK and achieved a cooling of -97 mK, marking the first demonstration of sub-1 K cooling of an entire silicon chip using NIS junctions.

Method. The work combines experimental fabrication of complex superconducting junctions with advanced 3D numerical simulations to model quasi-particle diffusion and interfacial heat flow.

Model / system. The system is a chip-scale refrigerator built from Normal-metal/Insulator/Superconductor (NIS) junctions, configured as SINIS units, which operate by filtering electrons via the superconducting energy gap. Heat transport is modeled using diffusive quasi-particle flow, incorporating both Acoustic Mismatch Models (AMM) and specific interfacial heat flow equations.

Key observables. Effective phonon temperature of the silicon chip, Coefficient of Performance (COP), and 2nd Law Efficiency.

Important parameters / regimes. Operating temperatures down to 70 mK, junction resistance ($ ext{621 } \Omega\cdot\mu ext{m}^2$), and the superconducting energy gap ($\Delta$).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumptions involve modeling quasi-particle flow as diffusive and engineering the geometry to limit heat leakage, though some models ignore quasiparticle branching mixing effects.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the lithography masks and cross-sections of the cooler, schematics of the SINIS unit, and comparative plots showing simulated vs. experimental phonon temperature profiles validating the heat leakage model.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the physical limitations of existing coolers to detailing the NIS junction physics, developing sophisticated models for heat leakage, and culminating in the measurement and validation of sub-Kelvin cooling performance metrics.

Abstract

Solid-state refrigerators using Normal-metal/Insulator/Superconductor (NIS) junctions have previously demonstrated excellent electron cooling but limited ability to cool phonons. The energy gap of the superconductor is used as an energy filter to allow higher than average energy electrons to preferentially tunnel from the normal-metal through the insulator into the superconductor where they travel as quasi-particles. Typically, the heat is moved and work is done to deposit hot quasi-particles into a normal-metal quasi-particle trap for rejection to the next refrigeration stage. Realizing that (1) the quasi-particles flow diffusively, driven by a concentration gradient in the electric field-free superconductor, and (2) that the undesirable backwards leaking of heat from the hot-side trap can be reduced by engineering the geometry and materials at the superconductor-to-trap interface, enhanced cooling can be achieved. Fabrication of the refrigerator was accomplished using a tungsten and titanium-tungsten alloy as the cold-side normal-metal, aluminum oxide as the insulator, aluminum as the superconductor, and gold as the trap, with the cold-side NIS portion being attached to the hot-side gold trap by bump bonding. The refrigerator consisted of 1121 junction pairs, each pair being an SINIS unit, all electrically connected in series. Using this we have measured the effective phonon temperature of a 3.9 mm x 3.9 mm x 0.65 mm silicon chip driven down to 70 mK from a bath temperature of 120 mK, and down to 174 mK from a 271 mK rejection temperature (a cooling of -97 mK). This is the first demonstration of the sub 1 K cooling of an entire silicon chip using NIS junctions.

Abstract

Large twist angles in twisted bilayer graphene are widely expected to be electronically trivial, with negligible interlayer coupling and no electronic reconstruction, in contrast to the rich moiré-driven band reconstruction and correlated physics that emerge at small twist angles. Here, we show that this paradigm breaks down near a twist angle of 29°, where the system crosses over between quasicrystalline and commensurate order. Atomic-resolution transmission electron microscopy directly reveals the coexistence of near-dodecagonal quasicrystalline symmetry and emerging moiré periodicity, indicating an intermediate, nonperiodic structural regime. Abstract

Thiele-based descriptions of magnetic vortex dynamics in thin ferromagnetic nanodots rely on magnetization ansätze that describe the equilibrium texture but cannot represent perturbation-induced deformations. We introduce the Deformed Image Vortex Ansatz (DIVA): a perturbation-aware ansatz in which the response to an external perturbation is built into the magnetization profile itself, rather than appended to the dynamics as a correction. Here, we demonstrate the concept for a uniform, stationary in-plane field applied to a Permalloy nanodot, for which the deformation is analytically tractable. A symmetry-based perturbative expansion identifies the leading deformation as a single $m = 1$ harmonic around the disk, while energy minimization and a dominant-balance analysis yield a closed-form interpolant for the radial profile. Benchmarked against micromagnetic simulations on Permalloy disks of aspect ratio $t/R = 0.1$ and $0.0125$, this realization reduces the disk-averaged angular deviation by a factor of 3 to 6 relative to the two-vortex ansatz, depending on geometry and field, and reduces the total-energy deviation by about a factor of six in the thicker disk.

Abstract

The thermal infrared emissivity of metallic nanowire networks - the property that makes them candidate low-emissivity transparent electrodes - follows regularities that have resisted physical explanation: a strong wire-diameter dependence with no wire-length dependence, a correlation with sheet resistance that breaks down above ~15 ohm/sq, and an angular signature that turns from dielectric-like to metallic as the network densifies. We show that all of them follow from one local geometric length, the average inter-wire gap g, acting through the geometric inductance of a mesh finer than the thermal wavelength: $X_L = Z_0 (g/λ) \ln(2g/πD)$, a parameter-free reactance that exceeds the ohmic loss and, unlike the DC sheet resistance, stays finite instead of diverging at the percolation threshold. Since the gap is fixed by areal density and wire diameter alone, the optics is length-independent and decoupled from percolative transport; what has been read as a second, optical percolation is instead a smooth impedance crossing. With a single fitted parameter the theory reproduces 56 silver-nanowire samples across four diameters to a mean absolute error of 0.04, collapses them onto one universal curve, and - unchanged - predicts the emissivity-transmittance data of five independent groups and the measured spectral emissivity, including the silica phonon band. Because this reactance contains no material constant, the inter-wire gap emerges as the master design variable for the radiative properties of metallic nanowire transparent conductors.

Abstract

We study integrable variable-mass sine-Gordon model (vmSG) with point defects. Using the Lax and Bäcklund-gauge formulations, we construct type-I and type-II defect matrices, derive the corresponding sewing conditions, and generate the bulk and defect contributions to an infinite hierarchy of conserved charges. The lowest members reduce to the standard sine-Gordon energy and momentum in the homogeneous limit, while for inhomogeneous backgrounds they define integrability-generated energy- and momentum-type quantities. Defect compatibility imposes matching conditions on the variable-mass functions across the defect. An analytical transmission factor $z$ characterizes soliton transmission, topological conversion, and absorption/emission processes. We then deform the type-I sewing conditions by parameters $α$ and $β$ and derive the associated defect anomalies. Consistent one-soliton transmission is recovered for $αβ=1$, whereas for $αβ\neq 1$ parity-centered kink-kink and kink-antikink transmissions display vanishing lowest order integrated anomalies but no generic vanishing at higher order. Numerical simulations reproduce the integrable transmission/conversion regimes and show that non-integrable defects generate weak radiative tails and lowest order quasi-conserved charges, while quasi-conservation of the higher order charges remains unestablished. These results elucidate how exact defect integrability deforms into charge-dependent quasi-conservation and establish a framework for defect-controlled soliton transport in inhomogeneous media, with potential applications to nonuniform Josephson junctions, magnetic and nonlinear-optical systems, and effective molecular and DNA models.

Abstract

Abstract

The electronic bandgap of solids is conventionally viewed as a static property at a given temperature, with only weak and stochastic thermal fluctuations under equilibrium conditions. Here, using ab initio molecular dynamics and first-principles electron-phonon calculations, we reveal a pronounced room-temperature bandgap pulsation at about 7.8 THz in a perovskite-like flat-band solid, with a maximum peak-to-peak variation approaching 0.95 eV.
