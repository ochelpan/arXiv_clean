Interference Engineering for Quantum Imaginary-Time Evolution through Multiple Energy Shifts

Authors: Hong-Jian Tang, Dan-Bo Zhang
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.17792v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 3/5 · methods for driven-dissipative 2/5 · interference shaping light 1/5

Summary. This paper introduces Multi-shift Quantum Imaginary-Time Evolution (MS-QITE) to overcome limitations in simulating quantum many-body systems on quantum computers. By engineering interference using a distribution of energy shifts, MS-QITE improves the efficiency and stability of ground-state and thermal-state preparation. This technique offers a general framework for optimizing quantum algorithms by exploiting spectral transformations.

Why it may be interesting. The technique provides a powerful, interference-based resource to optimize quantum algorithms for simulating thermal and ground states, which is a central theme in quantum simulation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of efficiently implementing imaginary-time evolution (ITE) for ground-state and thermal-state preparation on quantum computers.

Main result. The proposed Multi-shift Quantum Imaginary-Time Evolution (MS-QITE) uses a distribution of energy shifts to engineer interference, significantly improving the stability and reducing the required resources for state preparation.

Method. The core method involves modifying the standard ITE by introducing a distribution of energy shifts, which transforms the evolution into a controllable real-time superposition integral.

Model / system. The method is tested on quantum many-body systems, specifically using the transverse-field Ising model Hamiltonian. The implementation is demonstrated in both Monte Carlo sampling and continuous-variable-assisted schemes.

Key observables. Ground-state energy error, success probability, and trace distance for thermal state preparation.

Important parameters / regimes. The energy shift distribution $f(q)$, the number of shifts $k$, and the imaginary time $ au$.

Assumptions / limitations. The approach relies on the fact that energy shifts generate relative phases that interfere when implemented on a quantum computer, even if they are trivial in normalization.

Figures summary. Figures compare probability densities with and without multi-shift, show ground-state energy error vs. cutoff time, and illustrate success probability/resource consumption for thermal state preparation.

Paper structure. The paper introduces MS-QITE by relating it to the real-time superposition of evolutions. It then details its application in Monte Carlo and continuous-variable settings, culminating in numerical results on the Ising model.

Abstract

Energy shifting is usually trivial in imaginary-time evolution because it changes only the normalization of the evolved state. On a quantum computer, however, imaginary-time evolution can be implemented as a coherent or sampled superposition of real-time evolutions, in which energy shifts generate relative phases that can interfere. Here we introduce multi-shift quantum imaginary-time evolution (MS-QITE), which uses a distribution of energy shifts to engineer interference and optimize different implementations. In a Monte Carlo realization, multi-shift reshapes the sampling distribution and concentrates it within a shorter real-time window, thereby reducing the required Hamiltonian-evolution time and improving the stability of ground-state-energy estimation. In a continuous-variable-assisted realization, it permits postselection over a finite quadrature interval rather than near a single quadrature value, substantially reducing resource consumption while retaining accurate thermal-state preparation. Numerical results for transverse-field Ising models demonstrate that energy shifts provide a useful interference-based approach for optimizing quantum imaginary-time evolution.

A quantum optical concept of attosecond pulses: the attoquants

Authors: Sándor Varró, Ákos Gombkötő
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.17854v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: interference shaping light 3/5 · Full counting statistics 1/5 · quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper proposes 'attoquants,' a new quantum state concept, to explain attosecond pulse generation in High-Harmonic Generation (HHG) without relying on the classical requirement of phase-locking. By analyzing the coherent permanent state, the authors prove that this entangled state inherently supports pulse trains. This offers a quantum-mechanical framework for understanding ultrafast light sources driven by collective atomic effects.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly bridges quantum optics and attosecond physics by proposing a fundamentally quantum mechanism (entanglement) to achieve pulse synthesis, moving beyond semi-classical approximations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To develop a quantum optical concept for attosecond pulse generation that bypasses the classical requirement of mutual phase-locking among emitted harmonics.

Main result. The authors introduce 'attoquants,' a novel class of multimode quantum states, demonstrating that their electric-field expectation value yields an attosecond pulse train without requiring mode-locking.

Method. The analysis involves constructing and characterizing the coherent permanent state (an attoquant) and calculating quantum diagnostics like photon statistics and logarithmic negativity.

Model / system. The system is High-Harmonic Generation (HHG), modeled quantum mechanically as a harmonic radiation field composed of quantized electromagnetic modes. The core theoretical tool is the attoquant state, which is a permutation-symmetric superposition of coherent states.

Key observables. Electric-field expectation value, photon statistics, Wigner function, and logarithmic negativity.

Important parameters / regimes. The structure of the harmonic field (discrete frequencies $(2q+1)\omega$) and the concept of intermodal entanglement.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is theoretical, offering a phenomenological interpretation for the physical origin of these entangled states based on multi-atom effects during HHG.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the electric-field distribution for different pulse synthesis methods (mean-field vs. field-variance) and compare the temporal structure of field intensity for mode-locked versus random phase states.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the classical limitations of HHG to introducing the quantum attoquant concept, analytically deriving its properties, and finally providing a physical interpretation rooted in collective multi-atom radiation.

Abstract

High-harmonic generation (HHG) is conventionally understood to produce attosecond pulse trains only when the emitted harmonics are mutually phase-locked, in accordance with the classical theory of mode-locking. Such locking is necessary for pulse synthesis when the harmonic field is in a separable multimode quantum state. This requirement, however, can be circumvented entirely for harmonic combs exhibiting intermodal entanglement. We introduce a novel family of multimode quantum states, termed attoquants: states associated with highly structured pulse trains that are inherently insensitive to relative phases. As a concrete example, we analyse the coherent permanent state, constructed as a completely symmetric superposition of products of coherent states over all permutations of a fixed parameter set. It is shown analytically that its electric-field expectation value is a locked Fourier superposition of the driving-field harmonics, yielding an attosecond pulse train without requiring mode-locking. We further calculate the photon statistics, the Wigner function, and the logarithmic negativity of this state, confirming it to be genuinely nonclassical and entangled. Finally, we offer a phenomenological interpretation for the generation of such entangled states during HHG, as a consequence of multi-atom effects. Since the coherence volume of the driving field greatly exceeds that of its harmonics, a collectively-driven cluster of atoms can radiate the individual harmonics as spatially resolved, but fundamentally indistinguishable sources. This can generate the permutation-symmetric entanglement structure of an attoquant.

Block Encoding Non-Abelian Lattice Gauge Theory

Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.17115v1

Summary. This paper presents a resource-efficient quantum circuit implementation for simulating the magnetic part of the Hamiltonian in non-Abelian lattice gauge theories. By developing a block encoding oracle for the plaquette operator, the authors significantly reduce the computational overhead associated with simulating these complex quantum field theories. This work advances the feasibility of simulating QCD dynamics on fault-tolerant quantum hardware.

Why it may be interesting. The techniques developed—block encoding, programmed rotations, and lookup-based oracle construction—are highly relevant for implementing complex Hamiltonian terms in quantum simulation algorithms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Developing efficient quantum simulation methods for lattice gauge theories, particularly for real-time dynamics, which are challenging due to complex, correlated transitions in the magnetic part of the Hamiltonian.

Main result. The authors propose an efficient block encoding oracle for the plaquette operator in the irrep basis, significantly reducing the resource cost compared to previous methods.

Method. The method uses a block encoding oracle implemented via programmed rotation trees fed by quantum lookups of classically pre-computed, factorized matrix elements.

Model / system. The system is non-Abelian lattice gauge theory, exemplified by Lattice QCD (SU(3)). The Hamiltonian involves electric and magnetic terms, with the magnetic part built from the plaquette operator.

Key observables. The simulation targets the evolution governed by the full Hamiltonian, with specific focus on the plaquette operator $\square_p$ and the electric energy term $E_R$.

Important parameters / regimes. The resource cost is estimated in T gates (e.g., $10^5$ to $10^6$ for SU(3)), and the logical qubit count scales with the lattice volume $L^3$.

Assumptions / limitations. The simulation is designed for the fault-tolerant, error-corrected quantum computation regime, and the method relies on truncating the Hilbert space using a Casimir cutoff $B$.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the components of a plaquette and its state, while Figure 16 details the quantum circuit structure for calculating the electric energy term $H_E$.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by reviewing the Hamiltonian and basis, detailing the decomposition into electric and magnetic parts, describing the magnetic block encoding, presenting resource estimates, and discussing its application in QSVT.

Abstract

Gauge theories like lattice QCD present a complex problem for quantum simulation. In a basis where the electric part of the Hamiltonian is simple, the magnetic part, generally expressed as a sum over the plaquette operators of the lattice, is quite complicated, producing correlated transitions between several link and site degrees of freedom. We provide an efficient block encoding of the plaquette operator in the irrep basis, a refinement of the electric basis where the internal gauge-variant degrees of freedom are integrated out. The construction removes the plaquette matrix element scaling wall which has been a significant barrier for other approaches in this basis. The algorithm leverages a convenient factorization property of the matrix elements, cheap classical precomputation, and quantum oracles built from lookup tables and programmed rotations.

Chiral rotational dynamics in the molecular frame: Breaking symmetry with angular momentum

Authors: Alexander Blech, Monika Leibscher, Christiane P. Koch
Type: both · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.17479v1

Summary. This paper proposes a method to generate rotational chirality in achiral molecules by manipulating their angular momentum using precisely timed microwave or THz/optical pulse sequences. By satisfying strict symmetry breaking conditions, the induced chirality can be measured using Photoelectron Circular Dichroism. This opens a new avenue for studying time-odd chiroptical phenomena in fundamental molecular systems.

Why it may be interesting. This work is highly relevant to AMO physics as it tackles the fundamental control of molecular quantum states using tailored electromagnetic pulses, pushing the boundaries of chiroptical spectroscopy in the gas phase.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of generating and investigating time-odd (false) chirality in isolated, achiral molecules by controlling their rotational dynamics.

Main result. The authors demonstrate that rotational chirality can be induced in randomly oriented molecules using specific sequences of microwave or combined THz/optical pulses, which can be probed via Photoelectron Circular Dichroism (PECD).

Method. The theoretical framework involves deriving symmetry conditions for creating chiral superpositions of rotational states, while the experimental proposals utilize pulsed laser/microwave excitation sequences.

Model / system. The system studied is the rigid asymmetric rotor (e.g., $ ext{CH}_2 ext{O}$, $ ext{COFCl}$), whose dynamics are governed by the time-dependent Schrödinger equation under external pulsed fields.

Key observables. The expectation value of the angular momentum projection ($\langle \hat{J}_z angle$) and the Photoelectron Circular Dichroism (PECD) signal are the key diagnostics for induced chirality.

Important parameters / regimes. The required excitation involves at least three microwave or polarization directions (X, Y, Z) to break both spatial and molecular point group symmetries.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the rigid rotor approximation and that the probe pulse is short compared to the rotational dynamics timescale.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the excitation schemes (microwave/THz/optical) and show the time evolution of the induced angular momentum ($\langle \hat{J}_a angle$) and the corresponding PECD signal, demonstrating controllable handedness.

Paper structure. The paper moves from defining the problem (time-odd chirality) to deriving the necessary symmetry conditions for state superposition, detailing specific pulse sequences for microwave and optical excitation, and finally presenting PECD as the measurable diagnostic.

Abstract

Achiral molecules can be prepared in superposition states that are chiral. Here, we propose angular momentum orientation in the molecular frame to achieve the required symmetry breaking, exerting rotational control without the need for laboratory frame orientation. We derive the conditions for chiral rotational dynamics from the requirement to simultaneously break the continuous spatial rotational symmetry and the molecular point group symmetry. This can be achieved by three microwave pulses as well as two non-resonant optical pulses in combination with a THz pulse or three THz pulses, all with mutually orthogonal polarization directions, and the ensuing dynamics can be probed by photoelectron circular dichroism. Our results open the way for distinguishing structural from dynamical enantioselectivity and investigating time-odd chiroptical phenomena in randomly oriented molecules.

Irradiation-Induced Spin Bath Evolution and as-Grown Hydrogen Defects in CVD Diamond Revealed by NV-Based DEER Spectroscopy

Authors: Olga Rubinas, Jeroen Prooth, Michael Petrov, Remy Vandebosch, Emilie Bourgeois, David Chvatil, Milos Nesladek
Type: experiment · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.17619v1

Summary. This paper reviews the use of NV-DEER spectroscopy to characterize paramagnetic defects in CVD diamond. By tracking defect formation and transformation through annealing and irradiation, the authors developed a model linking defect control to the achievable spin coherence time. This establishes the method as a powerful tool for optimizing diamond for advanced quantum sensing applications.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a detailed experimental roadmap for controlling solid-state spin baths, which is crucial for developing robust quantum memories and sensors based on solid-state qubits.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To understand the fabrication process and optimize the material quality of CVD diamond for use in quantum sensing by characterizing the evolution of paramagnetic defects.

Main result. The study successfully mapped the transformation of various defects (X-centers, vacancies, hydrogen species) during annealing, demonstrating that NV-DEER spectroscopy is a powerful tool for achieving high spin coherence times.

Method. The research uses Double Electron-Electron Resonance (DEER) spectroscopy combined with controlled thermal annealing and electron irradiation of CVD diamond samples.

Model / system. The physical system is CVD diamond containing NV centers and various paramagnetic impurities (P1, vacancies, hydrogen). A Hamiltonian-based model is used to interpret the dipolar coupling measured by DEER.

Key observables. Spin coherence time ($T_2$), DEER spectral features corresponding to specific defect species ($ ext{V}^-$, $ ext{NVH}^-$), and defect concentrations.

Important parameters / regimes. Annealing temperatures ($650^\circ ext{C}$ to $1200^\circ ext{C}$), electron irradiation dose, and the coupling strengths between different spin bath components.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on developing and applying a comprehensive spin bath model that accounts for independent couplings between multiple defect types.

Figures summary. The notes mention Figure 1 showing the growth design and nitrogen incorporation trends for multilayer diamond samples.

Paper structure. The paper follows a structure of reviewing the state-of-the-art, detailing the experimental methodology (sample preparation, irradiation, annealing), presenting the defect evolution tracking via DEER, and concluding with a model linking defect control to high coherence times.

Abstract

The aim of this paper is to provide the reader with a review and current state of the art of the fabrication of high T2 coherence diamond, optimised by the use of double electron-electron resonance (DEER) spectroscopy. Using DEER, we study the formation, transformation, and annealing of paramagnetic defects in as-grown CVD diamond and after post-processing. Electron irradiation leads to the formation of an additional S = 1/2 resonance in the DEER spectrum, which we consider to be a composite X ensemble. By tracking the concentrations of X and P1 point defects during annealing from 650C to 1200C, we find that the X ensemble initially consists of a mixture of V- spins and interstitial spins, which disappear at about 650C. Vacancies migrate during annealing, forming clusters that persist to 1000C and disappear upon annealing at 1200C, contributing to the X ensemble signal. We have developed a model of the influence of the mixed spin bath on the coherence of NV centers, which includes independent couplings with P1 centers, V-, divacancies, and interstitials. Detailed DEER studies allowed us to reveal and resolve a weak signal from two additional S = 1/2 species associated with hydrogen: NVH- and consistent with a substitutional hydrogen defect, which overlaps the vacancy spectral line. Taken together, these results show that the NV-DEER method is a powerful tool for investigating paramagnetic defects in diamond with high precision and nanoscale resolution, essential for material optimisation. The achieved high T2 coherence time is consistent with the spin bath model, and the crystals reach the quality required for advanced quantum sensing applications.

Summary. This paper develops a stable training protocol for using Recurrent Neural Networks to represent quantum many-body wave functions. It overcomes the known instability of second-order optimizers like minSR by introducing regularization techniques. The stabilized method proves highly effective, offering a powerful tool for simulating complex quantum Hamiltonians.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly tackles the practical limitations of using advanced quantum machine learning techniques (RNN-NQS) for simulating complex quantum matter, providing a robust optimization pathway for variational quantum simulation.

Main problem. The paper addresses the instability of using second-order optimization methods, like minimum-step stochastic reconfiguration (minSR), when training Recurrent Neural Network Quantum States (RNN-NQS).

Model / system. The framework is applied to quantum many-body systems, including the 1D transverse-field Ising model, 1D cluster state, 2D Heisenberg, and J1-J2 models, using RNNs to represent the wave function $|\Psi_ heta angle$.

Abstract

Neural Quantum States (NQS) provide a powerful neural network-based variational framework for representing many-body wave functions and solving for ground states. Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) are particularly promising owing to their relatively low computational cost and their autoregressive property, which enables perfect sampling. Recently, RNNs have been reported to be unstable under curvature-based optimizers such as the minimum-step stochastic reconfiguration (minSR) method. In this paper, we address this perceived limitation and show that minSR can be stabilized through simple regularization techniques, enabling robust training of RNN-based NQS with only a few samples. Our approach outperforms the Adam optimizer on the one-dimensional transverse-field Ising model and the one-dimensional cluster state, and provides competitive results on the two-dimensional Heisenberg and $J_1-J_2$ models. This work offers a promising pathway for using modern optimization techniques with autoregressive NQS to address open questions in quantum simulation.

Second Harmonic Generation Spectroscopy of the Surface Charge Density Wave in the Weyl Semimetal CoSi

Type: experiment · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.17303v1

Summary. Researchers used RA-SHG spectroscopy on the CoSi surface to study a CDW transition at 90 K. They found the transition follows the 3D XY critical scaling, which is unexpected for a 2D surface. This suggests that the CDW order parameter has an intrinsic 3D character due to coupling with the underlying bulk topology of the Weyl semimetal.

Why it may be interesting. The finding of 3D XY critical behavior on a 2D surface challenges simple dimensionality arguments in critical phenomena, suggesting that topological coupling can induce higher-dimensional phase coherence.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To determine the nature and dimensionality of a Charge Density Wave (CDW) instability occurring on the surface of a Weyl Semimetal.

Main result. The CDW transition exhibits critical behavior consistent with the 3D XY universality class, despite being confined to a nominally two-dimensional surface layer.

Method. Rotational Anisotropy Second Harmonic Generation (RA-SHG) spectroscopy was used to measure the temperature dependence of the surface nonlinear susceptibility tensor elements.

Model / system. The study investigates the CoSi (001) surface, a Weyl Semimetal, where the CDW order parameter is probed. The analysis compares the observed critical exponent to theoretical predictions for phase transitions.

Key observables. Second Harmonic Generation (SHG) intensity, which tracks the CDW order parameter amplitude; the critical exponent $\beta$; and the transition temperature $T_{CDW}$.

Important parameters / regimes. Critical exponent $\beta = 0.30 \pm 0.03$; Transition temperature $T_{CDW} = 90.0 \pm 0.8$ K; Comparison to 3D XY universality class.

Assumptions / limitations. The apparent contradiction between 2D surface confinement and 3D XY scaling is resolved by assuming the CDW order parameter possesses an intrinsic three-dimensional structure due to coupling between surface sublayers.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the raw RA-SHG data showing anisotropy changes across the transition, and plots detail the temperature dependence of background-subtracted SHG intensity and ratios of tensor elements.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the experimental setup and the CDW transition using RA-SHG. It then analyzes the temperature scaling of the SHG signal to extract the critical exponent, concluding that the 3D XY class applies due to underlying 3D coupling mechanisms.

Abstract

Using temperature-dependent rotational anisotropy second harmonic generation (RA-SHG), we identify a charge density wave (CDW) instability on the CoSi (001) face with an onset temperature at $90.0 \pm 0.8$ K. The SHG response tracks the order parameter amplitude, dominated by two nonlinear tensor elements, whose background-subtracted intensity evolves with temperature as a power law. The extracted critical exponent $β= 0.30 \pm 0.03$ is consistent with the 3D XY universality class, an unexpected result for a nominally two-dimensional surface layer. No corresponding anomaly is observed in the bulk response, establishing the transition as a purely surface-driven phase transition. We attribute this unexpected scaling to the coupling between surface Fermi-arc states and the bulk topology they are tied to, grounded in a previously observed intra-unit-cell phase relationship between surface sublayers that gives the order parameter intrinsic three-dimensional structure.

Abstract

Abstract

We investigate whether the second-order purity-corrected stabilizer Rényi entropy (SRE) of reduced two-qubit density matrices can serve as a reliable local indicator of quantum phase transitions (QPTs) in frustrated quantum spin systems. We consider the one-dimensional isotropic \(J_1-J_2\) Heisenberg model, the one-dimensional XXZ \(J_1-J_2\) model, and the two-dimensional \(J_1-J_2\) Heisenberg model on a \(4\times4\) square lattice. Unlike several previously studied quantum information measures, which fail to detect QPTs in the ground state of these frustrated systems, the reduced ground-state purity-corrected SRE successfully identifies most transitions. For the remaining cases, we consider a low temperature subjacent state, modeled as a statistical mixture of the ground and first excited states with a Maxwell--Boltzmann-type occupation probability. For the 1D isotropic model, the subjacent-state SRE shows a discontinuity at the critical point, yielding \(α_c(\infty)=0.24116\), in excellent agreement with established values; the ground-state SRE shows a point of inflection, yielding \(α_c(\infty)=0.2681\). For the 1D XXZ model, the subjacent-state SRE reproduces the full anisotropy dependent phase diagram, while the ground-state SRE captures transitions only at low anisotropy. For the 2D model, the subjacent-state SRE detects two transitions, at \(α_c(4\times4)=0.40781\) and \(0.6208\), while the ground-state SRE identifies the second at \(0.6230\). Compared with conventional two-qubit entanglement, purity-corrected SRE shows a clear advantage in revealing otherwise-invisible phase transitions, establishing it as a robust, efficient, local probe of frustrated quantum criticality.

Abstract

Biological networks feature recurring motifs, but their uneven abundance remains poorly understood. Feed-forward loops (FFLs) are important motifs in the transcriptional networks of \textit{Escherichia coli} and \textit{Saccharomyces cerevisiae}, yet their eight types appear in highly unequal frequencies. This study presents an information-theoretic framework that decomposes input-output mutual information into pathway and interference components. The interference mutual information (IMI) quantifies the joint influence of direct and indirect regulatory pathways on information transmission. Our results show that IMI closely follows the observed abundance patterns in both organisms, whereas the input-output mutual information and the pathway component do not. We also trace the IMI hierarchy to the strength of pathway-interference interactions and local pathway sensitivities. Notably, a stronger IMI is also associated with reduced robustness to parameter variation, suggesting a trade-off between information processing and resilience in gene regulatory circuits. Our results, thus, identify a topology-dependent information-theoretic feature that relates to the abundance patterns.
