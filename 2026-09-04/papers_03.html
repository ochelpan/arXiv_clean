Heuristically optimizing, synthesizing, and prioritizing measurement settings for quantum state tomography

Authors: Sumukh S. Moudghalya, Anton Frisk Kockum, Akshay Gaikwad
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.02633v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 3/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces a novel, efficient computational framework to optimize Quantum State Tomography (QST) by reformulating the measurement setting selection as a Graph Coloring problem. By applying heuristic graph coloring algorithms, the authors drastically reduce the number of required measurements compared to exhaustive methods. This enables practical, priority-based scheduling for characterizing complex quantum systems on NISQ devices.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a scalable, computational framework for optimizing the data acquisition stage of quantum characterization, which is crucial for experimental quantum computing platforms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core problem is efficiently determining the minimal and prioritized set of measurement settings required for Quantum State Tomography (QST) for an arbitrary set of operators.

Main result. The framework successfully uses Graph Coloring heuristics to substantially reduce the number of required measurement settings compared to brute-force methods, enabling priority-based scheduling that maximizes information gain.

Method. The problem is reformulated as a Graph Coloring (GC) problem, solved using efficient heuristic algorithms like DSATUR and RLF, followed by synthesizing Clifford circuits for joint measurements.

Model / system. The work is validated on multi-qubit (up to five qubits), multi-qutrit (up to three qutrits), and hybrid qubit-qutrit systems, focusing on reconstructing the density matrix $\rho$.

Key observables. The estimated density matrix ($\rho_{est}$), the number of required measurement settings (color classes), and the information gain per experiment.

Important parameters / regimes. The dimensionality of the Hilbert space (up to 5 qubits/3 qutrits) and the number of operators to be measured.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that the problem can be effectively modeled and solved using graph coloring heuristics, bypassing the intractable nature of brute-force search.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the workflow: partitioning observables via GC, compiling quantum circuits, and using scheduling to reconstruct the density matrix, showing fidelity improvement with more settings.

Paper structure. The paper reviews standard QST, introduces the GC formulation for measurement setting optimization, and details the measurement-scheduling strategy for maximizing information gain.

Abstract

A key task in many quantum-computing applications, e.g., quantum simulation and quantum state tomography (QST), is to partition an arbitrary set of operators into mutually commuting subsets for efficient measurements. However, brute-force approaches to this task quickly become intractable as the number and dimensionality of operators grow. Here, we reformulate operator partitioning as a graph-coloring (GC) problem and develop an efficient computational framework to solve it, balancing accuracy and efficiency. Our framework enables leveraging a range of GC algorithms, which we benchmark for operator partitioning. Then, we demonstrate their utility in optimizing QST experiments, where determining non-overlapping data acquisition settings for QST is a major challenge, and prioritizing among these settings, i.e., selecting the experiments that provide the most information. We further show how to perform these experiments by synthesizing Clifford circuits for joint measurement of commuting Pauli operators in multi-qubit systems. We validate our framework across multi-qubit (up to five qubits), multi-qutrit (up to three qutrits), and hybrid qubit-qutrit systems. Our results show that heuristic GC methods substantially reduce the number of required measurement settings for QST and enable priority-based scheduling that maximizes the information gain per experiment. The optimization converges within minutes on a student-grade laptop, providing speedups of several orders of magnitude over brute-force methods already for these relatively small quantum systems. This demonstrates the potential of GC heuristics as a scalable and practical tool for characterization of noisy intermediate-scale quantum devices. We have made the Python implementation of our GC framework to optimize and schedule QST experiments publicly available at https://github.com/ssm8015/QST_GT.git.

Non-Additivity of Microwave-Shielded Interactions between Dipolar Molecules

Authors: Fabian Prieschl, Andreas Schindewolf, Robert E. Zillich
Type: theory · Category: quantum gases · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01940v1

Summary. This theoretical work demonstrates that the effective interactions between microwave-shielded dipolar molecules are not simply the sum of pairwise interactions. By analyzing the many-body potential energy for small clusters, the authors show that non-additive, many-body effects dominate the attractive potential well, which is critical for forming self-bound quantum clusters.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the limitations of simple mean-field or pairwise interaction models in strongly correlated quantum systems, which is crucial for designing stable quantum matter like dipolar BECs.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the validity of assuming pairwise additivity when calculating the effective interactions between multiple microwave-shielded dipolar molecules.

Main result. The pairwise approximation fails for planar configurations, as many-body effects significantly deepen the attractive potential well, necessitating the use of the full many-body potential energy.

Method. The authors calculated the many-body Potential Energy Surface (PES) for three and four molecules, comparing the full calculation against approximations based only on pair interactions.

Model / system. The system consists of dipolar molecules interacting via dipole-dipole forces, whose interactions are modified (shielded) by an external microwave field. The analysis uses the Born-Oppenheimer approximation and focuses on the resulting effective many-body Hamiltonian.

Key observables. The depth and position of the attractive potential well, and the repulsive barrier, as a function of molecular geometry (planar vs. out-of-plane).

Important parameters / regimes. The number of molecules (N=3, 4), the geometry (planar vs. out-of-plane), and the strength of the microwave field.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is restricted to single-MW shielding and assumes the validity of the Rotating Wave Approximation (RWA) for time-averaging.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the PESs for 3 dipoles in an equilateral triangle and 4 dipoles in a square, showing the dependence on side length and highlighting the many-body nature of the attractive well.

Paper structure. The paper builds from the general Hamiltonian formulation to the effective time-independent Hamiltonian via RWA, then systematically compares the full many-body PES against lower-order approximations (pairwise, triplet) for small clusters.

Abstract

Theoretical models of the effective interactions between microwave-shielded polar molecules typically rely on the assumption of pairwise additivity. In this work, we test the validity of this pairwise approximation by evaluating the exact many-body potential energy for configurations of three and four molecules. We demonstrate that this approximation fails for molecule configurations in the plane of the circularly polarized microwave field. In these planar geometries, many-body effects significantly increase the depth of the attractive potential well and shift the position of the repulsive barrier. Although out-of-plane interactions in three-dimensional arrangements weaken this non-additivity, the effective interactions between microwave-shielded dipoles are not a simple sum of pair interactions. In particular, their attractive well, crucial for self-bound clusters, can only be described by the full many-body potential energy.

Precise spectral asymptotics, exponential localization, and spectral gap estimates for the three-boson lattice Schrödinger operator

Authors: Abdikhurayra Toshturdiev, Abdumalik Eshniyozov, Janikul Abdullaev, Mikhail Dolgopolov
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.02488v1

Summary. This theoretical work provides a detailed spectral analysis of three interacting bosons on a 2D lattice, determining the asymptotic behavior of bound states and the spectral gap in the strong-coupling limit. Using advanced spectral methods, the authors establish precise energy formulas and prove exponential localization of the ground state. These results are highly relevant for understanding quantum simulation experiments using optical lattices.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous spectral analysis of few-body systems in optical lattices provides fundamental insights into quantum many-body physics, directly impacting the theoretical understanding of quantum simulation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the spectral properties, including bound states and spectral gaps, of the three-boson Schrödinger operator on a two-dimensional lattice.

Main result. The study derives precise asymptotic formulas for the bound state energies at zero and pi quasimomenta, and establishes exponential localization of the ground-state wavefunction in the strong-coupling limit.

Method. The analysis employs advanced spectral theory tools such as the Birman-Schwinger principle, invariant subspace decomposition, the discrete Agmon comparison method, and the Krein-Rutman theorem.

Model / system. The model is a three-boson system interacting via pairwise contact interactions on the two-dimensional integer lattice ($\mathbb{Z}^2$). The Hamiltonian is analyzed in both real space and momentum space.

Key observables. Bound state energies ($z_s(\mu)$), spectral gap ($\Delta(\mu)$), and the decay rate ($\alpha(\mu)$) of the ground-state wavefunction.

Important parameters / regimes. Interaction strength ($\mu$) in the strong-coupling limit ($\mu o \infty$), and the total quasimomentum ($K=0$ and $K=\pi$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on systematic invariant subspace decomposition and rigorous remainder estimates to control strong-coupling asymptotics; the sharp asymptotic rate for localization is conjectured.

Figures summary. Not specified, but the notes mention Table 1 comparing spectral properties across different few-body lattice systems, and Table 5 comparing Fredholm determinant asymptotics.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the Hamiltonian via direct integral decomposition over quasimomentum $K$, focusing on the extremal points $K=0$ and $K=\pi$ to derive asymptotic results for bound states and spectral gaps.

Abstract

We study the three-boson Schrodinger operator on the two-dimensional integer lattice with pairwise contact interactions. First, we obtain precise asymptotics of the two bound states below the essential spectrum at zero total quasimomentum in the strong-coupling limit mu to infinity: the ground state energy is -3 mu + 6 + O(1/mu), and the first excited state energy is - mu + C + O(1/mu), where C = 4 - delta approximately 3.96458, with delta determined by the transcendental equation b0(delta) = 1/(1+delta) involving the lattice Green's function. The associated spectral gap is 2 mu + O(1). Second, using the discrete Agmon comparison method and the Paley-Wiener theorem, we establish exponential localization of the ground-state wavefunction with a logarithmic upper bound on the decay rate alpha(mu) at most ln(3 mu) + O(1/mu), reflecting the bounded nature of the lattice dispersion; the sharp asymptotic rate is conjectured. Third, at quasimomentum pi, the parity symmetry is preserved, but the odd quadratic form becomes positive definite with a unique eigenvalue of order 1/mu that never reaches the Birman-Schwinger threshold, so the odd subspace yields only a virtual level. The even subspace supports exactly one bound state with energy -2 mu + 6 + 8/mu + O(1/mu^2), and the spectral gap to the essential spectrum is mu - 2 - 8/mu + O(1/mu^2). The reduction from two bound states at zero to one at pi preserves the total spectral flow and is a lattice-specific phenomenon. Our approach uses an invariant subspace decomposition of the Birman-Schwinger operator and the Krein-Rutman theorem to guarantee uniqueness and strict positivity of the ground state. These results have direct implications for quantum simulation of few-boson systems in optical lattices.

Quantum MeanFlow: single-shot generative sampling on NISQ hardware

Authors: Ashish Joshi, Eshaan Mistry, Takahiko Koyama
Type: both · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.02186v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: QC/QI experiment 3/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces Quantum MeanFlow (QMF), a novel quantum generative model designed to overcome the sequential circuit overhead of standard Quantum Flow Matching (QFM) on NISQ devices. By learning the average velocity over a time interval, QMF enables single-step sample generation, making quantum generative modeling significantly more viable in practice. The work demonstrates that this single-shot approach maintains competitive performance while drastically reducing quantum resource consumption.

Why it may be interesting. It directly addresses the practical implementation gap between theoretical quantum algorithms and current NISQ hardware constraints by proposing a fundamentally different, more efficient sampling mechanism.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is developing efficient generative sampling methods for quantum computation that overcome the high cost and sequential nature of multi-step sampling on Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) hardware.

Main result. The proposed Quantum MeanFlow (QMF) formulation allows for single-step sample generation, significantly reducing the required quantum circuit evaluations per generated sample compared to standard Quantum Flow Matching (QFM).

Method. QMF is introduced as the quantum analogue of MeanFlow, learning the average velocity over a time interval rather than the instantaneous velocity, enabling a single-shot sampling process.

Model / system. The models utilize Parameterized Quantum Circuits (PQCs) to learn velocity fields in a latent space derived from data like MNIST. The process is benchmarked on IBM quantum computers, operating within the framework of quantum generative models.

Key observables. Image quality (compared across single-step QMF, multi-step QFM, and single-step QFM), and the number of quantum circuit evaluations required per sample.

Important parameters / regimes. The latent space dimension (32-dimensional vector), the number of time steps (for comparison), and the quantum circuit depth/ansatz parameters.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that reformulating the generative process to use average velocity (QMF) can circumvent the hardware limitations imposed by sequential circuit execution required by instantaneous velocity methods (QFM).

Figures summary. The figures compare the sampling quality and efficiency of QFM versus QMF, highlighting QMF's single-shot capability.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the limitations of sequential QFM sampling, proposes QMF based on MeanFlow theory, details the training using the MeanFlow identity loss, and benchmarks the method on MNIST, concluding with a comparison of computational cost.

Abstract

Quantum generative models offer a promising framework for exploring whether quantum computation can enhance generative machine learning. Flow matching is a generative method in which samples are generated by transporting a simple, known distribution to the target data distribution with a learned velocity field. Its quantum counterpart, known as quantum flow matching (QFM), was introduced recently, and, like its classical counterpart, requires integrating an ordinary differential equation over many time steps during inference. As each step requires the output from the previous step, the circuit submission is sequential and a drawback on quantum computers as they have high input/output costs. To alleviate this problem, we introduce Quantum MeanFlow (QMF), the quantum analogue of the MeanFlow formulation, which allows single-step sample generation. While the QFM learns an instantaneous velocity field at each time step, QMF learns the average velocity over a time interval. We use a parameterized quantum circuit to learn these velocity fields and benchmark the two methods on the MNIST dataset. We show that while single-step QMF has lower image quality compared to multi-step QFM, it performs better than the single-step QFM sampling at every shot count. Both of our models are executed on IBM quantum computers and best-of-N rejection sampling recovers most of the accuracy lost to device noise without modifying the circuit. This is especially advantageous for QMF which has only one circuit evaluation per image. Here, We establish QMF as a viable method for single-step quantum generative sampling, saving on quantum circuit evaluations per generated sample.

Reliable Sample-Level Quantum Error Mitigation via Dominance-Aware Clustering

Authors: Mohsen Ghodrati, Kausthubh Chandramouli, Dror Baron
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01744v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 3/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work proposes a novel, classical post-processing technique called dominance-aware (DA) refinement for Quantum Error Mitigation. It tackles the difficult problem of recovering specific quantum states (bitstrings) from noisy measurement distributions. By combining responsibility thresholding with a local dominance screen, the method significantly improves the accuracy of center recovery in simulated quantum optimization tasks.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical post-processing, the rigorous statistical framework for identifying underlying quantum states from noisy measurements is highly relevant to understanding the limitations and potential of quantum characterization techniques.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of performing reliable quantum error mitigation (QEM) when the goal is to recover specific bitstrings (samples) from noisy measurements, rather than just estimating expectation values.

Main result. The proposed dominance-aware (DA) refinement, combining responsibility thresholding and a local dominance screen, successfully recovers true centers in simulated MaxCut-QAOA experiments, significantly outperforming standard clustering methods.

Method. The core method involves formulating the problem as a mixture model and applying DA refinement, which uses source responsibilities and a dominance score to ensure that majority voting recovers the correct center with exponentially decreasing error probability.

Model / system. The model assumes the measured probability mass is a mixture of contributions from several latent, task-relevant bitstrings (centers), each associated with a source and characterized by specific bit-flip error rates.

Key observables. Dominance Score ($\Delta_A^k$), Precision ($ ext{P}_C$), Recall ($ ext{R}_C$), and the combined F1 score ($ ext{F}_C^1$).

Important parameters / regimes. The dimensionality of the bitstring ($n$), the number of centers ($K$), and the noise parameters (bit-flip rates $\epsilon_{k,i}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The dominance condition (source $k$ contributing $>50\%$ probability mass at every coordinate) is a sufficient, but not necessary, condition for successful center recovery.

Figures summary. The notes do not provide a summary of figures, but the evaluation benchmarks include synthetic $n=10$ streams, QAOA on $n=10$ MaxCut instances, and high-dimensional $n=100$ tests.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theoretical framework for sample-level QEM, introduces the DA refinement module (combining responsibility thresholding and local dominance screening), and validates this approach through extensive synthetic and simulated QAOA benchmarks.

Abstract

Many quantum algorithms for classically difficult optimization tasks must return high-quality bitstrings from finitely many circuit executions, whereas most quantum error-mitigation methods target expectation values. We study sample-level recovery when measured probability mass is distributed around multiple latent bitstrings, called centers. Each component of the measured probability mass is called a source and we assume that each center is associated with one source. We identify dominance-at every coordinate, more than half of a retained region's probability mass comes from one source and agrees with its center-as a sufficient condition under which majority voting recovers that center with exponentially decreasing error probability. We show that nearest-center assignment, as used in clustering algorithms such as the $k$-modes algorithm, can fail to produce dominated regions even when the true centers are known. This failure motivates responsibility thresholding and a local dominance screen, whose combination we call dominance-aware (DA) refinement. Synthetic and simulated MaxCut-QAOA experiments show that DA refinement favors precision, while $k$-modes with DA refinement improves overall center recovery. All procedures are classical post-processing and require no additional quantum-circuit executions.

Thermodynamic Electric Toroidal Dipole and Intrinsic Longitudinal Spin Transport

Authors: Taisei Yamanaka, Takumi Sato, Satoru Hayami
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.02443v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 3/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work develops a thermodynamic theory for the Electric Toroidal Dipole (ETD), a bulk order parameter characterizing ferroaxial electronic phases. By coupling the ETD to the spin-induced polarization, the authors establish a direct, quantitative relationship between the ETD and the intrinsic longitudinal spin conductivity. This provides a new theoretical tool to diagnose and quantify ferroaxial order in crystalline solids.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on topological/symmetry physics, the rigorous derivation of a bulk, gauge-invariant order parameter and its direct link to a measurable transport coefficient (spin conductivity) provides a powerful theoretical tool for characterizing novel electronic phases in condensed matter systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of defining a gauge-invariant, bulk thermodynamic order parameter for the Electric Toroidal Dipole (ETD) in periodic crystals, and linking it to intrinsic longitudinal spin transport.

Main result. The ETD is shown to be a finite order parameter exclusively in ferroaxial phases, and its chemical-potential derivative is directly proportional to the intrinsic longitudinal spin conductivity.

Method. The authors formulate the ETD thermodynamically by coupling a spatially varying electric field to the relativistic spin-induced electric polarization, utilizing Green's function and Kubo formalism techniques.

Model / system. The study uses a minimal ferroaxial extension of the Kane--Mele model, which incorporates symmetry-breaking hopping terms ($H_{FA}$) to realize ferroaxial order in a honeycomb lattice.

Key observables. Electric Toroidal Dipole (ETD, $G_k$), intrinsic longitudinal spin conductivity ($\sigma_{z}^{xx}$), and the longitudinal spin current.

Important parameters / regimes. The ferroaxial coupling strength ($\lambda_{FA}$), the chemical potential ($\mu$), and the band gap size (controlled by hopping parameters like $t'$).

Assumptions / limitations. The conventional definition of ETD fails in periodic crystals due to the ill-defined position operator, necessitating a thermodynamic formulation. The analysis also uses semiclassical Boltzmann theory in some contexts.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the honeycomb structure and the effect of ferroaxial hopping on the band structure. Subsequent figures show the dependence of the ETD ($G_z$) and spin conductivity ($\sigma_{z}^{xx}$) on the chemical potential and the strength of the ferroaxial coupling.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the thermodynamic definition of the ETD. It then applies this framework to the Kane-Mele model, demonstrating that the ETD is non-zero only when ferroaxial symmetry is broken. Finally, it derives a quantitative relationship linking the ETD's derivative to the longitudinal spin conductivity.

Abstract

Electric toroidal dipoles (ETDs) characterize ferroaxial order, yet their bulk definition in periodic crystals has remained elusive because conventional multipole operators involve the ill-defined position operator. Here we formulate a thermodynamic ETD by coupling a spatially varying electric field to the relativistic spin-induced electric polarization. The resulting expression is gauge invariant and provides a bulk order parameter for ferroaxial phases. We further establish a direct relation between the chemical-potential derivative of the ETD and the intrinsic longitudinal spin conductivity in insulating systems. To demonstrate the formulation, we construct a minimal ferroaxial extension of the Kane--Mele model. The ETD becomes finite exclusively in the ferroaxial phase and is strongly enhanced near a small band gap, accompanied by a sizable longitudinal spin current. Our results establish a thermodynamic theory of ETDs in crystalline solids and identify the longitudinal spin conductivity as a direct transport manifestation of ferroaxial order.

Universal scaling and protocol-dependent amplitude in hybrid quantum walks

Authors: Cook Hyun Kim, Jaesub Park, Namshik Han
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01951v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: non-equilibrium universality 3/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical work analyzes hybrid quantum walks, which combine quantum and classical random walk steps. By comparing two distinct operational protocols (additive vs. multiplicative), the authors show that while both lead to diffusion, the specific temporal organization dictates the amplitude of the diffusion coefficient in the quantum limit. This highlights that the structure of the quantum-classical coupling is crucial for determining transport dynamics.

Why it may be interesting. The comparison between the two protocols (QRW-A vs. QRW-M) provides deep insight into how the temporal ordering of quantum and classical operations dictates the macroscopic transport properties, a key concept in open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper compares the universal scaling and protocol-dependent amplitude of diffusion coefficients in hybrid quantum-classical random walks.

Main result. Both additive and multiplicative hybrid quantum walks exhibit diffusive spreading for any classical admixture, and their diffusion coefficients diverge with a first-order pole in the quantum limit, with the pole amplitudes differing by a factor of two.

Method. The analysis uses the transfer operator method in momentum space, determining long-time behavior from the leading eigenvalue of the evolution operator for both additive (QRW-A) and multiplicative (QRW-M) protocols.

Model / system. The system is a hybrid quantum walk on a 1D ring, combining unitary quantum steps (using Hadamard coin) with classical steps. Two protocols are compared: additive (QRW-A) and multiplicative (QRW-M).

Key observables. Mean Squared Displacement (MSD), transport exponent ($eta$), and the long-time diffusion coefficient ($D$).

Important parameters / regimes. Quantum admixture ($\alpha$ or $r$), coherence timescales ($t^*_A, t^*_M$), and the ratio of amplitudes.

Assumptions / limitations. QRW-A requires truncation of the transfer matrix at a finite size $L$, and QRW-M assumes the long-time density matrix approaches a specific form.

Figures summary. Figure 1 shows the MSD vs. time for both protocols, confirming the transition from ballistic to diffusive spreading.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theory by analyzing the evolution via a linear transfer operator in momentum space. It derives the leading eigenvalue expansion for both QRW-A and QRW-M, leading to explicit formulas for the diffusion coefficients in the large-time limit.

Abstract

Quantum walks spread ballistically but become diffusive with classical admixture. We compare two hybrid walks using identical quantum and classical steps at the same classical-step rate but ordered differently in time. Any nonzero admixture yields diffusion. Near the quantum limit, both diffusion coefficients have first-order poles, but their amplitudes differ by about a factor of two. The coherence timescale sets the pole order, while temporal organization sets the amplitude. We expect our analytical framework to apply broadly to quantum--classical hybrid systems.

Abstract

Stochastic resetting has emerged as a powerful mechanism for optimizing search processes by curtailing long, unproductive excursions inherent to diffusive dynamics. Most theoretical studies, however, assume perfectly absorbing targets -- an idealization that overlooks the finite reactivity commonly encountered in realistic chemical and biological systems. As a consequence, the optimal resetting rate becomes intrinsically reactivity dependent. Our results identify target reactivity as a crucial control parameter governing stochastic transport and provide insights into reaction-diffusion processes in complex media.

Summary. This work rigorously compares three quantum error-correcting codes (RHG, FFCC, rFFCC) for use in a photon-atom quantum computer designed for MBQC. Abstract

We compare the Raussendorf-Harrington-Goyal (RHG) code, the Foliated Floquet Color Code (FFCC), and the reduced FFCC in a compound photon-atom architecture that directly generates measurement-based quantum computation (MBQC) resources with near-deterministic photon-atom CZ gates. RHG serves as a natural benchmark, while the FFCC variants allow us to study whether reduced graph degree improves performance under an architecture-aware circuit-level loss model with delayed heralding and correlated bond-loss propagation. We construct two generation schemes compatible with the compound hardware and evaluate circuit-level thresholds under periodic boundary conditions. RHG achieves the highest circuit-level threshold, 2.75%, and its threshold falls below that of reduced FFCC only for large excess loss on intermodule CZ connections. RHG also achieves the lowest logical error rate in most resource-matched comparisons, but some low-loss windows favor reduced FFCC. Overall, we show that when the hardware supports the native gates and connectivity required for MBQC, the benefits of lower graph degree must be weighed against each code's intrinsic IID loss tolerance, generation-scheme details, and hardware-aware resource overhead.

Abstract

Abstract

Every finite-dimensional real or complex Lie algebra has a largest solvable ideal, its radical. In the compact dynamical Lie algebra (DLA) of a closed quantum system, this radical is the center; projecting onto it isolates directions that commute with the supplied algebra. With sparse Lie-bracket data and a known spectral gap, we construct a quantum circuit that approximately encodes this coefficient-space projector. The general construction combines the derived-algebra map with the Killing form, whereas compactness makes the Killing form alone sufficient. This distinction matters for numerical sensitivity, and our main theorem gives its exact law under an invariant-orthonormal basis adapted to the center and semisimple part. In a compact real algebra with a nonzero semisimple part, the condition number of the general operator is the three-halves power of the Killing-form condition number on that part. The compact projector in turn yields a bounded-error test for whether the center is trivial, provided that any nonzero center has at least a stated minimum dimension. This gives a controlled test for internal conserved directions in compact quantum dynamics.

Abstract

We analytically characterize the maximal quantum violation of the extended Mermin-Ardehali-Belinskii-Klyshko (EMABK) family of inequalities by $n$-qubit generalized Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger (GHZ) states. We develop a correlation-tensor approach in which the Bell value is expressed as the Frobenius inner product of the quantum correlation tensor and an effective coefficient tensor. A rank constraint on the latter, together with the von Neumann trace inequality, yields an upper bound governed by the two largest singular values of the reshaped correlation-tensor. For generalized GHZ states, we determine the complete singular spectrum and obtain a piecewise analytic upper bound. We then construct two complementary measurement strategies that saturate the bound for EMABK throughout the entire parameter range. The first is a purely MABK strategy in which all measurement directions lie in the equatorial plane of the Bloch sphere. The second is a hybrid strategy that recursively combines lower-order MABK anti-diagonal operators with the fully-$σ_z$ tensor product. The resulting piecewise analytic expression recovers the standard MABK Tsirelson bound $2^{(n-1)/2}$ in the maximally entangled limit and approaches the classical local-hidden-variable bound in the product-state limits. It further shows that every entangled generalized GHZ state exhibits a strict quantum-classical separation under the EMABK inequality, thereby eliminating the nonviolation region of the standard MABK inequality in the partially entangled regime.
