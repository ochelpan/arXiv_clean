Asymmetric optical transmission from broken interface-orientation symmetry in self-shadowed grating metallizations

Authors: Can Yesilyurt
Type: both · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01755v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: interference shaping light 3/5 · analog quantum simulation 1/5 · quantum optics experiment 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work investigates how breaking the interface-orientation symmetry in a grating structure, achieved by oblique metal deposition, causes asymmetric optical transmission. By combining advanced full-wave simulations with sample-reversal measurements, the authors quantify a substrate-favored transmission contrast. This demonstrates a robust method for engineering directional optical responses in metamaterials.

Why it may be interesting. The study provides a concrete, experimentally verifiable platform for observing symmetry breaking in optical response, which has analogues in engineered quantum systems or anisotropic media.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper demonstrates and quantifies asymmetric optical transmission arising from broken interface-orientation symmetry (IOS) in grating metallizations.

Main result. The oblique deposition of metal creates a one-sided, louver-like structure that leads to measurable, substrate-favored directional transmission ratios, which are successfully predicted by full-wave Maxwell calculations.

Method. The study combines experimental measurements using sample-reversal techniques with theoretical predictions derived from full-wave Maxwell simulations (FDTD).

Model / system. The physical system is a diffraction grating metallized with silver (Ag) via oblique-angle deposition, creating a self-shadowed, one-sided profile. The analysis uses principles of reciprocity and diffraction theory.

Key observables. Directional transmittance ratio ($T_{ ext{back}}/T_{ ext{front}}$), polarization-dependent contrast, and broadband substrate-favored transmission.

Important parameters / regimes. Deposition angle ($\alpha$), grating period ($d$), and wavelength ($\lambda$), with optimal asymmetry observed near $45^\circ$ deposition angle.

Assumptions / limitations. The model assumes ideal ballistic growth and that the observed asymmetry is entirely due to the broken IOS, while the zeroth-order specular reflection remains reciprocal.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the one-sided Ag deposition profile, measured substrate-favored ratios for various periods, and comparisons between computed Maxwell results and scalar wave-packet models.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the theoretical basis using reciprocity, details the fabrication via oblique deposition, presents the full-wave simulation framework, and validates the predictions with quantitative sample-reversal measurements.

Abstract

Reciprocal optical structures can transmit finite-angle illumination asymmetrically when the two illumination directions couple differently to the available diffraction channels. We realize this in a single overlay-free step: oblique metallization of a conventional diffraction grating, where ballistic self-shadowing grows a one-sided, louver-like silver profile. Electron microscopy and Ag mapping show the one-sided silver redistribution on oblique-coated films and its absence on a normal-incidence control. Full-wave Maxwell calculations on the ballistic-growth geometry predict broadband substrate-favored transmission, carried by the transverse-magnetic (TM) polarization in the diffraction regime (contrast up to 2.1) and polarization-independent and spectrally flat (1.5-1.9) deep in the geometric regime; the rejected power is redirected predominantly into specular reflection, and the deposition angle selects the magnitude and sign of the effect (optimum near $45^\circ$). Calibrated three-color sample-reversal measurements yield dark-corrected substrate-favored ratios that persist in both output-analyzer projections on a $45^\circ$-coated 1-$μ$m-period film (up to 1.8) and lie within the computed geometric-regime band on a 30-$μ$m-period film (1.5-1.8, with reference-normalized absolute transmittances).

Topological Lithography via External Field: Creating Topological States Anywhere Beyond the Edge

Authors: Haoran Nie, Chaoran Jiang, Xiangying Shen, Lei Xu
Type: both · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.02670v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 3/5 · driven-dissipative phase transition 1/5 · quantum measurements 1/5

Summary. This work introduces 'topological lithography,' a method to create robust topological states by applying controllable external scalar potentials to existing systems. By modifying the effective mass term, the approach bypasses the need for complex material fabrication, allowing for programmable and reconfigurable topological patterns. This universality is demonstrated across mechanical, electronic, and circuit platforms.

Why it may be interesting. The concept of using external fields to engineer topological properties in real space, rather than relying on material composition or symmetry breaking, offers a highly tunable paradigm for designing quantum materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Conventional topological insulators require distinct bulk symmetries, limiting flexibility; the goal is to induce tunable topological states anywhere (edge, bulk, remote) using only external fields.

Main result. An external scalar potential field can induce programmable topological states by modifying the Jackiw-Rebbi mechanism, enabling 'topological lithography' across mechanical, electronic, and acoustic platforms.

Method. The approach uses an External Scalar Potential (ESP) field to modify the effective mass term in the Hamiltonian, inducing a local topological marker (LTM) characterizing the transition.

Model / system. The system is modeled on a hexagonal lattice, analyzed via tight-binding Hamiltonians, and experimentally validated using mechanical vibrational systems and LC oscillatory circuits.

Key observables. Local Topological Marker (LTM), band structure evolution, and in-gap mode transmission ratios.

Important parameters / regimes. ESP strength ($\gamma$), potential shape (quadratic, Gaussian), and lattice geometry parameters.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the ability to map the physics onto effective 1D chains or lattice models, and that the external field control is highly precise.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the comparison between traditional and ESP-induced edge states, the evolution of band structures with ESP strength, and experimental setups showing LTM mapping and lithographic patterning.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the limitations of conventional TIs to introducing the ESP mechanism, detailing the theoretical framework (JR mechanism, LTM), validating it across multiple physical platforms (mechanical, electronic, LC circuits), and concluding with demonstrations of programmable topological lithography.

Abstract

Topological insulators (TIs), recognized for their robust boundary states and unconventional phase transitions, have emerged as one of the most impactful discoveries in recent decades, attracting considerable interest across diverse fields. However, conventional TIs require topological contrasts between adjacent bulk regions, typically achieved by distinct symmetries, which limits their flexibility and broader applicability. In this work, we introduce a "lithography" approach to inducing topological states by applying external fields in time-reversal-symmetric systems. These states transcend the conventional bulk topology design paradigm and offer exceptional tunability: they can exist at geometric edges, within the bulk, or even be induced remotely. Because the external field is highly controllable, the induced states are programmable, reconfigurable, and can be easily tailored into desirable patterns. Theoretically, we demonstrate that the external field modifies the Jackiw-Rebbi mechanism, inducing a real-space topological transition characterized by a local topological marker (LTM). We further establish an extended valley bulk-edge correspondence, which explains both the conventional scenario and our findings. Our results, validated across mechanical, electronic, and acoustic platforms, highlight the broad applicability of this approach to various systems. This work not only advances the theory of topology but also enhances the diversity, tunability, and practical implementation of topological states and materials.

Rank-Selective Optical Tomography of Higher-Wave Altermagnetism

Authors: Meysam Bagheri Tagani, Carmine Autieri, Sahar Izadi Vishkayi
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.02429v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: interference shaping light 3/5 · quantum measurements 1/5

Summary. This work presents a novel optical technique to determine the spatial rank of higher-wave magnetic order in altermagnets. By measuring the absorption spectrum using finite photon momentum, the authors show that a joint Fourier projection isolates the magnetic harmonic's rank via a characteristic power-law dependence on momentum. This method provides a robust diagnostic tool for characterizing complex magnetic textures in materials like MnTe.

Why it may be interesting. The technique of using momentum-resolved spectroscopy to extract higher-order spatial harmonics is a powerful, non-linear optical diagnostic tool applicable to complex magnetic order parameters, bridging condensed matter theory with advanced quantum optics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is optically identifying the spatial rank of higher-wave altermagnetism because local electric-dipole response cannot uniquely resolve distinct continuum harmonics.

Main result. By utilizing finite photon momentum and a joint Fourier projection, the spatial rank ($\ell$) can be selectively isolated, yielding a power law scaling of $|T_\ell| \propto q^{\ell-2}$.

Method. The authors derive a rank-matching rule and employ a joint Fourier projection in polarization and momentum angle, which is verified by a Ward-consistent finite-q microscopic calculation.

Model / system. The study focuses on altermagnetic materials exhibiting higher-wave order (d-, g-, or i-wave), using MnTe as a testbed. The analysis involves calculating the joint Fourier projection of the spin-resolved absorption rate.

Key observables. The joint Fourier projection $T_\ell(q, \omega)$, which exhibits a power law scaling $|T_\ell| \propto q^{\ell-2}$ corresponding to the rank $\ell$.

Important parameters / regimes. The photon momentum $q$ is crucial for achieving rank selectivity; the scaling exponent is $\ell-2$.

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes that the finite photon momentum supplies the necessary missing spatial rank information, and the joint projection successfully filters out lower-order crystalline aliases.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the rank-matching principle via joint angular tomography ($d: q^0, g: q^2, i: q^4$) and show that the parent $g$-wave component remains strongly encoded in MnTe despite strong SOC effects.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the theoretical necessity of finite photon momentum for rank-selective tomography, derives the projection formula, and then applies this method to first-principles calculations on MnTe to confirm the persistence of the higher-wave magnetic texture.

Abstract

Identifying the spatial rank of higher-wave altermagnetic order optically is challenging because local electric-dipole response does not uniquely resolve distinct continuum harmonics. We show that finite photon momentum turns one-photon spin-resolved absorption into a rank-selective tomography. For the planar $|m|=\ell$ sector of an even-parity $\ell$-wave component, a joint Fourier projection in polarization and momentum angle isolates $\mathcal T_\ell\proptoη_\ell q^{\ell-2}$, yielding the hierarchy $d:q^0$, $g:q^2$, and $i:q^4$; phase changes track rotations of the selected magnetic harmonic. A Ward-consistent finite-$q$ microscopic calculation reproduces these powers without imposing them. Although discrete crystal symmetry can generate lower-order local aliases, they are orthogonal to the selected momentum harmonic and cannot contaminate it below $q^{\ell-2}$. In MnTe, whose nonrelativistic parent order is three-dimensional $g$ wave while spin--orbit coupling lowers the exact relativistic spin-momentum-locking symmetry, first-principles calculations show that more than $99.9\%$ of the Fourier power of the Néel-projected $A$-region spin-energy contrast remains in the parent $g$-wave-derived $m=3$ harmonic. Structured near fields place the required momentum window within experimental reach.

Anomalous Superfluid Response in Altermagnetic Superconductors

Authors: Christian Wiedemann, Danilo Nikolić, Matthias Eschrig, Wolfgang Belzig
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.02263v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 3/5

Summary. This theoretical work analyzes the anomalous paramagnetic superfluid response in altermagnetic superconductors. Using quasiclassical Green's functions, the authors show that the inherent anisotropy of the altermagnetic state causes a measurable paramagnetic Meissner effect. This effect is highly sensitive to external fields and impurity levels, providing a new diagnostic tool for gapless superconductivity.

Why it may be interesting. The study of unconventional superconducting responses, particularly those involving magnetic order (altermagnetism), provides rich theoretical ground for understanding how broken symmetries influence macroscopic quantum phenomena like the Meissner effect, which is relevant to advanced quantum materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the emergence and characteristics of an anomalous (paramagnetic) superfluid response in superconducting materials that possess altermagnetic order.

Main result. The altermagnetic state leads to anisotropic gapless superconductivity, manifesting a paramagnetic Meissner effect strongest when the altermagnetic and Zeeman fields are parallel and in clean samples.

Method. The analysis employs quasiclassical theory using the Eilenberger equation framework to calculate the linear response current density via the Meißner kernel.

Model / system. The system is an altermagnetic superconductor subjected to an out-of-plane Zeeman field. The theoretical framework incorporates anisotropic exchange fields, Zeeman coupling, and impurity scattering into the Green's function formalism.

Key observables. Paramagnetic Meißner kernel components ($K_{ij}$), momentum-resolved Density of States (DOS), and the superconducting free energy ($\Delta\Omega$).

Important parameters / regimes. The relative strength of the altermagnetic field, the Zeeman field magnitude ($h$), and the impurity scattering rate ($\Gamma_{imp}$) relative to the superconducting gap ($\Delta_0$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes weak altermagnetic exchange fields compared to the Fermi energy, allowing for a nearly spherical Fermi surface approximation.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate spin-split Fermi surfaces, momentum-resolved DOS showing gap closing, and the Meißner kernel components ($K_{xx}, K_{yy}$) as functions of applied fields and impurity concentrations.

Paper structure. The paper builds from establishing the theoretical framework (quasiclassical Green's functions) to calculating the superfluid response (Meißner kernel), deriving the key results regarding anisotropy and impurity effects, and concluding with the physical implications of the paramagnetic response.

Abstract

We report on the emergence of the anomalous (paramagnetic) superfluid response in altermagnetic superconductors at arbitrary impurity concentrations. Due to anisotropic gapless superconductivity, altermagnetic superconductors with an out-of-plane Zeeman field display an anisotropic paramagnetic Meissner effect. The effect is strongest for parallel altermagnetic and Zeeman exchange field vectors and in the clean sample. The presence of nonmagnetic impurities leads to isotropisation and, consequently, weakens the effect; however, the paramagnetic response sustains intermediate amounts of impurities in the system. As demonstrated in recent experiments, microwave superfluid stiffness measurements can serve as a sensitive probe of gapless superconductivity.

This validates CVD as a viable platform for studying strongly correlated physics in moiré materials.

Why it may be interesting. The realization of correlated electronic states in a scalable, solid-state platform like CVD graphene is crucial for studying strongly correlated electron physics outside of highly controlled, small-sample environments.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal was to determine if the fragile flat-band and correlated electronic states characteristic of magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene (MATBG) could be realized using scalable, large-area growth methods like Chemical Vapor Deposition (CVD).

Main result. The study successfully observed electronic bands in CVD-grown TBG that are comparable to those measured in exfoliated samples, confirming the potential for scalable moiré materials hosting flat bands.

Abstract

Advances in chemical vapor deposition (CVD) growth have driven graphene crystal quality to unprecedented levels, yet it is still unknown whether this route can realize the fragile flat-band and correlated states of the magic-angle (MA) twisted bilayer graphene (TBG). Here, we report on the experimental observation by room-temperature nano-angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (nano-ARPES) of flat bands in a TBG sample close to the MA, assembled via a grow-and-stack protocol based on low-pressure CVD of graphene on copper. Our study indicates electronic bands fully comparable to those measured in exfoliation-based samples and determines the size of the largest near-MA domain to be compatible with electronic transport experiments, motivating further experiments on flat band physics in CVD-graphene.

Vortex-core Majorana coupling to a chiral edge in a $p_x+ip_y$ superconductor: Nonmonotonic spectral reorganization and coherent fermion-parity dynamics

Authors: Peiyao Liu, Yi Zhou
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.02260v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 3/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work analyzes the spectral reorganization in a superconducting disk containing a vortex by studying the coupling between the vortex core and the boundary. Using BdG calculations, the authors show that while the localized wave packet retains high norm, the resulting spectral properties and parity memory are highly sensitive to the vortex-boundary separation. This highlights that simple measures of wave function overlap are insufficient predictors of topological memory.

Why it may be interesting. The study of topological superconductivity, Majorana fermions, and spectral dynamics in confined geometries is highly relevant to understanding topological quantum computation and superconducting quantum circuits.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how the coupling between a vortex core and the boundary reorganizes the low-energy electronic spectrum of a finite 2D p_x+ip_y superconductor, and what this implies for the coherence and memory of Majorana wave packets.

Main result. A large retained norm of the reference packet does not guarantee spectral concentration or persistent parity memory, as the spectral measure and parity dynamics vary significantly with the vortex-boundary separation.

Method. Bogoliubov-de Gennes (BdG) calculations are employed, supplemented by analyzing regional coherence measures and parity correlators.

Model / system. The system is a finite 2D p_x+ip_y superconducting disk containing a vortex, modeled using the grand-canonical mean-field Hamiltonian and solved via BdG equations.

Key observables. Nonmonotonic core-edge reorganization of eigenstates, retained norm of the reference packet, spectral measure (concentration/fragmentation), and signed parity correlator.

Important parameters / regimes. Vortex-boundary separation (d), superconducting gap ratio (Δ_0/E_F = 0.36), and disk radius (R=30ξ).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis uses a finite-disk approximation and an idealized reference preparation method for the vortex core packet.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate vortex geometry and image construction; other figures show the spectral measure and parity correlator varying nonmonotonically with the vortex-boundary distance d.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining the physical system and Hamiltonian to calculating spectral reorganization via BdG, and finally analyzing the implications for parity memory using time-domain correlators.

Abstract

We study how vortex--edge coupling reorganizes the low-energy sector of a finite two-dimensional \(p_x+ip_y\) superconducting disk as a function of the vortex--boundary separation \(d\) and examine what this reorganization implies for the parity memory associated with a prescribed vortex-core Majorana wave packet, a resource relevant to Majorana-based quantum operations. Bogoliubov--de Gennes calculations reveal nonmonotonic core--edge reorganization of the lowest positive-energy finite-disk eigenstate, with particularly rapid variation near \(d\simeq7ξ\), where \(ξ\) is the coherence length. To separate this eigenstate reorganization from the spectral representation of a prescribed state, we rigidly translate a centered-vortex core-reference packet to each fixed vortex position, restrict it to the target disk, and project it, without intermediate normalization, onto the particle-hole-complete low-energy subspace. For \(Δ_0/E_F=0.36\) and disk radius \(R=30ξ\), the resulting retained norm exceeds \(0.98\) at all six sampled separations, \(4.25\leq d/ξ\leq8.25\), while, depending on \(d\), the spectral measure is concentrated near zero energy, fragmented over several low-energy levels, or dominated by finite-energy weight. Correspondingly, the signed parity correlator displays slow temporal variation, rapid coherent dephasing, or sign-changing oscillations, with possible finite-size recurrences at later times. Thus a large retained norm does not by itself imply spectral concentration or persistent parity memory.

Abstract

Moiré superlattices in heterobilayers of atomically thin transition metal dichalcogenides provide a versatile platform for exploring quantum many-body physics as they can trap excitons, leading to the formation of quantized moiré exciton states. However, such moiré excitons have been predominantly studied at cryogenic temperatures, which severely limits their practical applications. Here, we demonstrate room-temperature activation and control of moiré excitons in MoS$_2$/WSe$_2$ heterobilayers using local strain engineering. We provide a theoretical model that captures all experimentally observed features, including the unconventional spectral shape of the moiré exciton emission. Our findings establish local tip-induced strain as a powerful tool for the on-demand manipulation of moiré excitons, paving the way for room-temperature quantum excitonic devices.

Abstract

Summary. This theoretical study uses the Stochastic Self-Consistent Harmonic Approximation (SSCHA) to model the structural and electronic properties of the superhydride YScH8 under high pressure. The authors demonstrate that neglecting quantum and anharmonic lattice effects leads to significant overestimations of the superconducting critical temperature (Tc). A full treatment is necessary to accurately predict the material's behavior, matching theory to experiment.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on solid-state superconductivity, the rigorous application of free-energy minimization and quantum fluctuations (SSCHA) to determine equilibrium structure and vibrational properties shares methodological parallels with advanced quantum many-body treatments in AMO physics.

Main problem. The paper aims to accurately predict the superconducting critical temperature (Tc) in high-pressure ternary superhydrides by accounting for the effects of quantum and anharmonic ionic motion.

Method. The Stochastic Self-Consistent Harmonic Approximation (SSCHA) is employed to minimize the free energy, providing a quantum-mechanically consistent treatment of lattice vibrations and structural relaxation.

Abstract

The recent synthesis of P4/mmm-YScH$_{8}$ at 191.7 GPa with a superconducting critical temperature Tc of 113 K has attracted considerable interest in the study of ternary superhydrides. Here we systematically evaluate the effects of quantum and anharmonic motion of ions in P4/mmm-YScH$_{8}$ using the stochastic self-consistent harmonic approximation (SSCHA). Summary. This paper demonstrates that the K-theoretic classification of topological phases is insufficient for systems with P and T symmetries. The authors introduce a new topological invariant, $\eta$, derived from analyzing the symmetry-constrained path space. This invariant reveals distinct topological phases that are invisible to standard K-theory diagnostics.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous mathematical extension to topological classification beyond standard K-theory, which is crucial for understanding novel topological phases in condensed matter systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The standard K-theoretic framework for classifying topological phases is shown to be incomplete, even for simple spinless systems possessing both P (inversion) and T (time-reversal) symmetries.

Main result. The authors identify a new topological invariant ($\eta$) that cannot be determined from the parity spectra at inversion-invariant momenta, revealing crystalline topological phases absent from K-theory.

Method. The classification is achieved via a first-principles analysis of the topological classes by analyzing the 'symmetric clutching data' and utilizing Wilson-loop spectral flow methods.

Model / system. The analysis focuses on spinless systems with P and T symmetries, using the Bloch Hamiltonian $H(\mathbf{k})$ and examining its behavior at inversion-invariant momenta ($\Lambda_i$).

Important parameters / regimes. The parity spectrum of occupied states at inversion-invariant momenta, and the structure of the fundamental group $\pi_1(O(N))$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the existence of a continuous orthonormal basis of occupied states on the fundamental domain $ au$.

Abstract

The K-theoretic framework provides a complete topological classification of the tenfold symmetry classes and has been generalized to incorporate crystalline symmetries. Here, we show that this classification is incomplete even in the elementary case of spinless systems possessing both P and T symmetries. We obtain the complete classification through a first-principles analysis of the topological classes of P- and T-symmetric bands, namely, by classifying the corresponding symmetric clutching data. We identify a topological invariant that is invisible from the K-theoretic perspective when the occupied states at each inversion-invariant momentum have uniformly positive or uniformly negative parity. In special cases, such as when all inversion-invariant momenta have uniformly positive parity, this invariant can be interpreted as the second Stiefel--Whitney class or the Euler number defined over an inversion fundamental domain, namely, half of the Brillouin zone. Our work not only reveals a new topological invariant that cannot be determined from the parity spectra at inversion-invariant momenta under P and T symmetries, but also demonstrates the existence of crystalline topological phases that are absent from the K-theoretic classification.
