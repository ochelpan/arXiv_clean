Bilinear correlations in Fluctuating Gaussian States with anti-unitary symmetries

Authors: Xu Zhang
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01724v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 2/5

Summary. This paper analyzes correlation functions in Fluctuating Gaussian States exhibiting anti-unitary symmetries, identifying two competing ordered phases: a saddle point phase and a non-local contraction phase. The authors use advanced correlation techniques and entanglement bounds to determine which phase dominates, showing that the non-local phase imposes severe constraints on the system's structure.

Why it may be interesting. The connection between anti-unitary symmetries, the emergence of different ordered phases (saddle point vs. non-local contraction), and the constraints imposed by entanglement structure (PEPS/bond dimension) provides deep insights into the nature of quantum ground states and steady states.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates bilinear order-parameter correlations in a specialized class of sign problem-free quantum systems known as Fluctuating Gaussian States (FGS). The core goal is to characterize the competition between different broken-symmetry phases.

Main result. The system generally exhibits two competing phases—a saddle point phase and a non-local contraction phase—whose dominance depends on the stability of the fluctuation saddle point. Furthermore, the non-local contraction phase imposes stringent constraints on the required entanglement structure.

Method. The analysis relies on calculating two-point correlation functions using Wick's theorem and decomposing observables into symmetry-related matrix bases. Theoretical bounds, such as those involving bond dimension, are also derived.

Model / system. The model is Fluctuating Gaussian States (FGS) possessing anti-unitary symmetries, applicable to both fermionic and bosonic systems. These states are defined via a path integral decomposition over imaginary time slices.

Key observables. Bilinear order-parameter correlations, two-point Green's functions, von Neumann entropy, and the average bond dimension ($\langle D_\phi angle$).

Important parameters / regimes. The competition is governed by parameters related to the stability of the fluctuation saddle point, and the analysis involves varying coupling strengths ($\Delta$) and correlation lengths ($z$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the existence of anti-unitary symmetries and utilizes approximations like the spectral function approximation for low-temperature properties.

Figures summary. Figures schematically illustrate the relationship between anti-unitary symmetry and the order parameter set for different particle types, and a phase diagram is used to show the transition between the saddle point and non-local contraction phases.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by defining the FGS framework and symmetries, calculating correlation functions for the two competing phases, deriving theoretical bounds relating entanglement to correlations, and finally discussing implications for non-equilibrium dynamics.

Abstract

We study bilinear order-parameter correlations in a general class of sign problem-free systems that are described by what we call Fluctuating Gaussian States (FGS) with anti-unitary symmetries, where the weight of each Gaussian measurement in the space-time path integral is positive-definite. Supported by Monte Carlo simulations on fluctuating Gaussian fermionic and bosonic examples, we argue that such systems generally exhibit two types of broken-symmetry phases: a \emph{saddle point phase} and a \emph{non-local contraction phase}, with the competition between the two determined by the stability of the fluctuation saddle point of FGS. Due to the Fermi liquid instability in fermionic FGS with anti-unitary symmetries, we also discuss the possibility of representing fermionic FGS with a particular Projected Entangled-Pair State (PEPS) ansatz, which we argue a certain construction provides an efficient description for the saddle point phase, but not for the non-local contraction phase. Finally, we discuss the potential implication of our result in non-equilibrium systems and stabilizing a target bilinear order.

Summary. This study develops a robust computational framework to define ecological 'sites' from raw GPS tracking data for two raven species. By applying this framework and modeling movement using the Individual Mobility Model, the authors successfully quantify distinct space-use strategies, showing that the species differ in their rates of site discovery and spatial predictability.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous statistical framework developed for quantifying memory and site fidelity in complex, non-equilibrium spatial processes (like animal movement) shares conceptual parallels with modeling memory effects or non-Markovian dynamics in open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of defining ecologically meaningful residence sites from raw GPS tracking data, which is complicated by heterogeneous sampling and uncertain boundaries.

Main result. The study reveals distinct space-use strategies between two raven species: Brown-necked ravens show higher site entropy and a higher rate of novel site discovery compared to Fan-tailed ravens.

Method. It employs a three-stage method involving greedy nearest-neighbor clustering on stop detections (derived from speed thresholds) to create non-overlapping, polygon-based site visit sequences.

Model / system. The system is the movement ecology of two sympatric raven species (Fan-tailed and Brown-necked) in the Dead Sea region, analyzed using the non-Markovian Individual Mobility Model (IMM).

Key observables. Site visit sequences, IMM parameters ($eta$ and $\alpha$), site entropy, and conditional entropy of site-to-site transitions.

Important parameters / regimes. Maximum site size ($ ext{Rmax}$), stop detection thresholds ($v_{ ext{trig}}$), and the parameters governing the IMM ($eta$ for novel discovery, $\alpha$ for preferential return).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that sites are repeatedly and collectively used, and the method assumes that the non-Markovian IMM accurately captures the balance between novel site discovery and preferential return.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the spatial distribution of inferred stops using three different detection methods (speed-threshold, residence-time, recurse) and compare cluster-diameter distributions across these methods.

Paper structure. The paper first details the site inference framework (greedy clustering on stop detections) and then applies this framework to model movement dynamics using IMM. It includes extensive validation by comparing the results against established, but computationally slower, site detection methods.

Abstract

A central challenge in movement ecology is to describe ecologically meaningful residence sites from raw tracking data due to heterogeneous sampling frequency and uncertain site boundaries. Here, we develop a greedy nearest-neighbor clustering approach with local reassignment and polygon-based site construction that generates timestamped sequences of site visits for each tracked individual, and apply it to nine years of GPS data from two sympatric raven species in the Dead Sea region---the Fan-tailed raven (\emph{Corvus rhipidurus}) and the Brown-necked raven (\emph{C. ruficollis}). Using the resulting visitation sequences, we quantify recursion patterns using the non-Markovian individual mobility model (IMM), which captures the balance between novel-site discovery $β$ and preferential return $α$. The inferred dynamics are consistent with IMM predictions and reveal clear interspecific differences: Brown-necked ravens continue to discover new sites at a higher rate (lower $β$), whereas Fan-tailed ravens show a steeper concentration of visits among top-ranked sites. Entropy analyses further separate the species, with Brown-necked ravens exhibiting higher site entropy and higher conditional entropy of site-to-site transitions. Together, these results provide a robust framework for describing interspecific differences in movement strategies that can be used to infer memory from GPS data and suggest distinct space-use strategies in two closely related sympatric species.

Summary. This paper analyzes the long-time decay of survival probability for Brownian motion in a wedge with partially reactive boundaries. Using matched asymptotic analysis, the authors derive the power-law decay exponent and explicitly calculate the prefactor for specific wedge angles. The work provides deep insights into boundary-induced transport phenomena in confined geometries.

Why it may be interesting. The study of anomalous diffusion and boundary effects in confined geometries is crucial for understanding transport phenomena in mesoscopic systems, which has relevance to quantum transport and open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the long-time power-law decay of the survival probability of a Brownian particle diffusing in a planar wedge whose sides exhibit partial reactivity.

Main result. The long-time survival probability decays as $S_q(t|\mathbf{x}_0) \simeq B_\alpha(q\mathbf{x}_0) (q/\sqrt{Dt})^{-\pi/\alpha}$, and the prefactor $B_\alpha$ is explicitly determined for specific angles $\alpha = \pi/n$.

Method. The primary method is matched asymptotic analysis, relating the long-time behavior to a stationary harmonic Robin problem near the apex. Numerical methods (FEM) and conformal mapping are also used to verify conjectures.

Model / system. The system is a Brownian particle diffusing in an infinite planar wedge of angle $\alpha$. The boundaries are modeled by Robin boundary conditions, introducing a characteristic length scale $q^{-1}$ related to the partial reactivity.

Key observables. Survival probability $S_q(t|\mathbf{x}_0)$, persistence exponent ($\pi/\alpha$), and the prefactor $B_\alpha(q\mathbf{x}_0)$.

Important parameters / regimes. Wedge opening angle $\alpha$, Robin parameter $q$, and time $t$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on matched asymptotic expansions, assuming separation between the reaction scale ($q^{-1}$) and the diffusion scale ($\sqrt{Dt}$).

Figures summary. Table 1 provides numerical values for connection coefficients $1/v_{ u,R}(0, 0)$ and compares them to the conjectured theoretical value $1/\Gamma(1 + u)$.

Paper structure. The paper develops the asymptotic formula for survival probability using matched asymptotics, derives explicit prefactors for specific angles, and then uses numerical/conformal mapping techniques to confirm conjectures regarding the connection coefficients and derive the PDFs for boundary local time and first-crossing time.

Abstract

We investigate the power-law decay of the survival probability of a Brownian particle diffusing in an infinite planar wedge whose two sides are partially reactive and described by Robin boundary conditions. We employ matched asymptotic analysis to relate the long-time asymptotics to a stationary harmonic Robin problem near the apex. This approach determines the persistence exponent for arbitrary opening angles and yields the prefactor explicitly for the family of wedges with $α=π/n$ ($n=1,2,\ldots$). A simple extension of the apex prefactor to arbitrary angles is conjectured and supported numerically. This asymptotic behavior describes the crossover to the well-known result for perfectly absorbing wedges. As immediate applications, we also deduce the long-time behavior of the probability density function of the boundary local time on wedge sides, as well as the probability density function of the associated first-crossing time.

Error accumulation in dissipative quantum circuits

Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01848v1

Summary. This paper analyzes error accumulation in quantum processors by modeling them as dissipative random quantum circuits. Using fidelity as a diagnostic, the authors derive a universal scaling law for the average fidelity decay dependent only on dissipation strength and circuit depth. This provides essential theoretical insight into noise resilience for current quantum computing hardware.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides crucial theoretical tools for characterizing noise resilience in NISQ devices, showing how fundamental physical parameters govern decoherence regardless of specific gate implementations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how information degrades in quantum processors due to dissipation and decoherence within random quantum circuits.

Main result. The average fidelity decay is found to be universal, depending only on the dissipation strength, system size, and circuit depth, and is independent of the microscopic details like the Kraus rank.

Method. The authors use random-matrix approximations and derive closed-form expressions for the ensemble-averaged fidelity, analyzing the decay across different noise regimes.

Model / system. The model is a dissipative random quantum circuit (RQC) where each two-qubit gate is treated as a 'diluted unitary' channel, interpolating between the ideal unitary and a random dissipative channel.

Key observables. State Fidelity ($F$), which quantifies the deviation between the ideal and noisy output states.

Important parameters / regimes. Dissipation strength ($\kappa$), coherent two-qubit gate-error strength ($\alpha$), and connectivity-error probability ($p$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on approximating the random permutations with Haar-random global unitaries to derive an exact closed-form expression for the average fidelity.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the circuit layer comparing faultless, dissipative, and combined dissipative/noisy circuits. Figure 2 compares the theoretical mean fidelity decay against different ensemble assumptions.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by defining the noisy evolution via diluted unitaries, deriving the average fidelity using random matrix techniques, establishing the universal scaling laws, and finally deriving a closed-form expression incorporating multiple error parameters.

Abstract

Present-day quantum processors are open systems in which dissipation and decoherence degrade the information carried by a quantum state as it propagates through a circuit. We study this degradation in dissipative random quantum circuits, modeling each two-qubit gate as a diluted unitary that interpolates between the intended unitary operation and a random dissipative quantum channel. Using the fidelity between the ideal and noisy output states as a diagnostic, we show that sufficiently random unitary gates or dissipative Kraus operators lead to a universal decay of the average fidelity. This decay is determined only by the dissipation strength, system size, and circuit depth, while microscopic details of the gates and noise, including the Kraus rank, appear only in subleading corrections to higher moments. We derive a closed-form expression for the average fidelity in terms of three physically meaningful error parameters, dissipation strength, coherent two-qubit gate-error strength, and connectivity-error probability. Finally, we identify the cases when dissipative effects can be reproduced by an unitary-noise model and when dissipation-induced decoherence remains distinguishable from coherent noise at the level of the average fidelity.

Summary. This work presents a multivariate extension of hyperdensity functional theory to analyze multiple correlated observables in inhomogeneous fluids. It derives exact theoretical relationships, such as hyper-Ornstein-Zernike equations, governing the statistical interrelations between these observables. By coupling this rigorous theory with machine learning, the authors provide a powerful, systematic tool for predicting complex many-body behavior from simulation data.

Why it may be interesting. Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops a multivariate generalization of hyperdensity functional theory (HDFT) to systematically investigate the equilibrium properties and interrelations of multiple, arbitrary observables in inhomogeneous classical many-body systems.

Main result. The framework establishes universal density functionals for averages, variances, and covariances of these observables, and derives exact hyper-Ornstein-Zernike equations linking these correlation measures.

Method. The theory is built upon extending the Mermin-Evans classical density functional mapping to an extended multivariate ensemble, and its practical implementation is achieved by integrating the functional structure with supervised machine learning via neural networks.

Model / system. The theory is applied to classical many-body systems, illustrated specifically by studying the clustering behavior in confined hard rod fluids.

Key observables. Hyperobservables ($\{\hat{A}_\ell\}$), equilibrium averages, variances, covariances, and hyperfluctuation profiles ($\chi_{\hat{A}}(\mathbf{r})$).

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature (via $eta$), chemical potential ($\mu$), and the coupling constants ($\lambda_\ell$) associated with the hyperobservables.

Assumptions / limitations. The theory relies on the formal structure derived from the Mermin-Evans map, and practical calculation requires approximating the functionals using machine learning techniques.

Figures summary. Not specified by the notes.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the multivariate HDFT framework via the extended ensemble, derives the universal density functionals and the hyper-Ornstein-Zernike equations, and then details the mathematical machinery using functional derivatives (Gateaux derivatives) to compute these higher-order correlation functions, concluding with an application to hard rod fluid clustering.

Abstract

Hyperdensity functional theory facilitates the investigation of the equilibrium behavior of a general order parameter or statistical mechanical observable in spatially inhomogeneous classical many-body systems. The approach is based on applying the exact Mermin-Evans classical density functional mapping to an extended ensemble. Here we present the multivariate generalization for investigating simultaneously the properties and interrelations of several different hyperobservables of choice. The resulting framework gives rise to a systematic characterization and prediction scheme for general many-body phenomena. All pertinent equilibrium averages, variances, and covariances constitute universal density functionals, as we demonstrate explicitly. Associated one-body hyperfluctuation profiles quantify the degree of correlation of the local density with first- and second-order combinations of hyperobservables. These multivariate hyperfluctuation profiles are accessible in many-body simulations and they satisfy exact hyper-Ornstein-Zernike equations, which we derive from the minimization principle in the extended multivariate ensemble. The formal structure of the theory integrates naturally with supervised machine learning, which renders all hyperdensity functionals accessible in practice via training of neural networks on simulation data. We demonstrate all salient techniques using the illustrative case of clustering in confined hard rod fluids, thereby choosing the total number of particles and the largest cluster size as the representative hyperobservables of interest. Our numerical methodology enables the efficient and successful prediction of all statistical quantities induced by the chosen hyperobservables, which we verify via comparison to test data and which we attribute to the tight interplay of first-principles and machine-learning concepts that our general approach combines.

Summary. This work numerically investigates how amorphous solids respond mechanically when subjected to localized force dipoles, or 'pinching.' The study reveals a clear transition from linear elastic behavior at small deformations to complex plastic rearrangements at larger extensions. The results show that the resulting plasticity depends critically on the initial stability of the glass, providing a minimal local probe into the material's collective mechanical response.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical mechanics, the study of amorphous solids, yielding, and the transition between elastic and plastic regimes shares conceptual links with understanding energy landscapes and structural relaxation in complex quantum many-body systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the mechanical response of amorphous solids to localized force dipoles (pinching) under athermal quasistatic conditions.

Main result. The response transitions from an elastic, long-range power-law decay regime to a plastic regime where rearrangements can be localized (stable glasses) or system-spanning (poorly annealed glasses).

Method. Numerical simulations using athermal quasistatic methods, complemented by theoretical analysis based on linear elasticity theory and multipolar expansions.

Model / system. The system modeled is an amorphous solid (model glass) composed of interacting particles, whose mechanical response is probed by applying a controlled force dipole (pinching).

Key observables. Displacement field u(x), plastic activity patterns (localized vs. delocalized), potential energy evolution, and the principal axis of plastic deformation.

Important parameters / regimes. Imposed extension (tuning from elastic to plastic), glass stability (well-annealed vs. poorly annealed), and the force dipole strength.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the continuum description of linear elasticity remains valid down to particle-scale distances, and the protocol involves athermal quasistatic loading.

Figures summary. Figures schematically show the pinching process (initial contact to force dipole separation) and illustrate the evolution of potential energy and displacement fields under different loading protocols.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the elastic response to pinching, then analyzing the transition to plasticity based on glass stability, and finally introducing advanced diagnostics like the principal axis of deformation to characterize the resulting rearrangements.

Abstract

We numerically investigate the mechanical response of amorphous solids to localized force dipoles, referred to as pinching, using athermal quasistatic simulations of model glasses with varying degrees of stability. We employ a control parameter, the imposed extension, corresponding to the increase in rest length between the two pinched particles relative to their initial separation. Increasing this extension allows us to continuously tune the system from the elastic to the plastic regime. For small extensions, the response remains elastic. In this regime, the displacement field induced by pinching is well described by linear elasticity and exhibits a long-range power-law decay consistent with dipolar forcing. Averaged responses display anisotropic, quadrupolar-like patterns, with quantitative agreement between simulations and analytical predictions. This continuum description remains valid down to particle-scale distances. As the imposed extension increases, the response becomes plastic. Pinching can then trigger either localized or system-spanning rearrangements, depending on glass stability. Well-annealed glasses exhibit localized plastic events, whereas poorly annealed systems display delocalized cascades. We introduce a method to extract the principal axis of plastic deformation and analyze the associated displacement fields and plastic activity. Overall, our results demonstrate that pinching provides a minimal local probe of amorphous solids. The resulting response, governed by both glass stability and imposed extension, offers insight into the interplay between elasticity, elementary rearrangements, and the emergence of collective plasticity.

Summary. This work models the training dynamics of deep neural networks using the framework of stochastic gradient flow and percolation theory. It shows that structural symmetries force the network to undergo discrete, block-wise collapses, manifesting as variance spikes. These transitions exhibit Discrete Scale Invariance, providing a quantitative link between optimization dynamics and critical phenomena.

Why it may be interesting. Main problem. The paper investigates the dynamics of Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) in deep neural networks, aiming to understand how structural symmetries force the network to collapse toward simpler, invariant subnetworks.

Main result. The structural transitions are modeled as percolation processes leading to Discrete Scale Invariance (DSI), characterized by variance spikes and a geometrically scaling cascade.

Method. The authors model the optimization process using the Stochastic Gradient Flow (SGF) and analyze the structural evolution using percolation theory, supermartingale properties, and spectral entropy measures.

Abstract

We study the dynamics of Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD), which is known to steer deep neural networks toward invariant sets that correspond to simpler subnetworks. How this steering unfolds over time remains poorly understood. We answer this by modeling the stochastic gradient flow (SGF) as a percolation process, in which architectural symmetries force subnetworks to merge in discrete simultaneous blocks rather than one at a time. These structural transitions register as variance spikes in a macroscopic order parameter, echoing physical phase transitions. We further show this trapping mechanism and its associated scaling cascade extend to Adam and AdamW under an explicit heavy-tailed noise model.

$\mathcal{N}$-bein formalism for degenerate states in the parameter space of quantum geometry

Summary. This paper develops an advanced geometric formalism using the N-bein to study the parameter space of quantum systems with degenerate spectra. It introduces a torsion tensor capturing non-commutativity and derives several gauge-invariant invariants. The framework is successfully applied to coupled harmonic oscillators, providing new tools to quantify correlations in degenerate quantum states.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, general geometric machinery for analyzing the parameter dependence of quantum systems, especially those with degeneracies, which is crucial for understanding topological phases and quantum control.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper extends the N-bein formalism, originally for quantum parameter spaces, to analyze systems exhibiting degenerate spectra.

Main result. The formalism successfully defines new gauge-invariant tensors and invariants that quantify state transitions and correlations in degenerate quantum systems, even when the standard QGT breaks down.

Method. The authors employ concepts from Cartan geometry, introducing an N-bein as the 'square root' of the Quantum Geometric Tensor (QGT) and defining a torsion-like tensor via the covariant derivative.

Model / system. The theoretical framework is applied to a concrete model of coupled harmonic oscillators immersed in an electric field, which naturally leads to a degenerate energy spectrum.

Key observables. N-bein, Quantum Geometric Tensor (QGT), Torsion-like tensor, and several derived scalar invariants (e.g., $(N\Xi\Theta)$).

Important parameters / regimes. The number of parameters ($\mathcal{N}$), the quantum numbers defining the energy subspaces ($n_1, n_d$), and the coupling strengths in the oscillator model.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the existence of a slowly varying parameter dependence and relies on extending the formalism from Abelian to non-Abelian matrix-valued descriptions.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the degeneracy structure of the Hilbert space and show how state changes occur via parameter variation, particularly highlighting jumps in quantum numbers.

Paper structure. The paper builds from the initial N-bein definition to analyzing the two-state QGT for transitions, introduces the torsion tensor, develops a geometrical formulation using differential forms, and concludes with calculating and analyzing multiple gauge-invariant scalar observables for the coupled oscillator model.

Abstract

Recently, we introduced a geometric object analogous to an orthonormal frame in the Cartan formalism to study the parameter space of quantum systems; we called it $\mathcal{N}$-bein, with $\mathcal{N}$ being the number of parameters that characterize the quantum system. Acting as the ``square root'' of the quantum geometric tensor (QGT), the $\mathcal{N}$-bein allows us to define new tensors to improve our understanding of the structure beneath the parameter space of quantum mechanics. In this work, we extend this mathematical framework surrounding the $\mathcal{N}$-bein to analyze the parameter space of quantum systems with degenerate spectra. As in the non-degenerate case, we define a non-Abelian two-state QGT to identify possible transitions between degenerate states after two consecutive parameter variations. Additionally, using the Wilczek-Zee connection, we introduce a torsion-like tensor as the covariant derivative of the $\mathcal{N}$-bein. This torsion captures the noncommutativity of successive parameter variations and coincides with the antisymmetric part of the two-state QGT. We also present a geometrical formulation using differential forms and discuss the physical implications of the newly defined tensors. Furthermore, we construct several gauge-invariant observables from the $\mathcal{N}$-bein and its derivatives to highlight the utility of the new tensors. Finally, to illustrate the convenience and applications of this formalism, we apply the theoretical framework to a system of coupled harmonic oscillators immersed in an electric field. The coupling between the oscillators results in a degenerate system. Thus, using the new formalism, we found correlations among the quantum states quantified by the new invariants.

A generalized harmonic oscillator problem for a spin-1/2 fermion

Summary. This theoretical paper solves the bound-state problem for a spin-1/2 fermion in a generalized harmonic oscillator potential incorporating scalar, vector, and tensor couplings. The authors derive exact wavefunctions using specialized mathematical techniques and determine the conditions for bound energy levels by analyzing the roots of the resulting energy equation. This work provides a comprehensive framework that unifies several previously studied, but distinct, relativistic quantum oscillator problems.

Why it may be interesting. While the core physics is relativistic quantum mechanics, the mathematical techniques used—analyzing the roots of complex transcendental equations derived from physical constraints—are highly relevant to finding exact solutions in complex quantum models.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper calculates the exact bound-state wavefunctions and determines the conditions for the existence of bound solutions for a novel generalized harmonic oscillator problem involving a spin-1/2 fermion.

Main result. The general solution encompasses several known spherically symmetric cases, and the existence of bound states is determined by analyzing constraints on quantum numbers derived from the energy equation.

Method. The authors use advanced mathematical techniques, including solving coupled radial equations via specific Ansätze and employing real analysis on the resulting energy equation to determine the number of real roots corresponding to bound energies.

Model / system. The system is a spin-1/2 fermion governed by a Dirac-like equation in 3+1 dimensions, subjected to simultaneous scalar, vector, and tensor couplings (including harmonic oscillator and Dirac oscillator potentials) acting in a plane of motion.

Key observables. Bound-state energy eigenvalues ($\varepsilon$), and the classification of solutions as particle or antiparticle.

Important parameters / regimes. Potential coupling constants ($\mu, \omega, a, b$), quantum numbers ($n, ilde{k}$), and the dimensionality of the motion.

Paper structure. Abstract

The exact bound-state wavefunctions and the corresponding energy equation are calculated for a new generalized harmonic oscillator problem describing a spin-1/2 fermion in 3+1-dimensions, involving scalar, vector, and tensor couplings acting simultaneously within a particular plane of motion. For the scalar and vector coupling, singular harmonic oscillator shapes are considered, such that the singular term is needed to allow analytical solutions for the wavefunctions while preserving binding under adequate conditions. For the tensor sector,the Dirac oscillator potential is employed, which adds another independent binding mechanism to the problem. The exact bound-state solutions are computed by specifically tuning coefficients for an appropriate pair of \textit{Ansätze} for the radial functions, which leads to wavefunctions in terms of generalized Laguerre polynomials. Abstract

The negatively charged boron vacancy ($\VB$) in hexagonal boron nitride (\hBN{}) was the first optically addressable spin qubit identified inside a van der Waals crystal, allowing atomically defined placement relative to a target. Its coherence in bulk \hBN{} is limited by the boron nuclei of the layers flanking the defect plane, which a thin flake removes. Here, we use a generalized cluster-correlation expansion with an extended central-spin block to calculate the Hahn-echo coherence time of $\VB$ in h$^{11}$B$^{15}$N as a function of layer number. Our results show that $T_2$ rises from $199\ns$ in the bulk limit to $653\ns$ in a monolayer, a factor of $3.3$ that is already saturated at three layers and that a sublattice decomposition attributes entirely to boron. The enhancement is confined to low field, is insensitive to stacking registry and twist angle, and requires alignment to within about two degrees, an onset we reproduce with no free parameters. Under dynamical decoupling, a $1/e$ threshold returns $206\ns$ for every thickness, but this is a zero of the first-shell modulation rather than a decay: measured without a threshold, the layer contrast survives and reaches a factor of $170$. Layer number thus emerges as a design parameter for $\VB$-based sensing.
