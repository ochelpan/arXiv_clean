Unfolded Krylov complexity: universal chaotic dynamics without false positives

Authors: Johanna Erdmenger, Kyoung-Bum Huh, Hyun-Sik Jeong, Juan F. Pedraza
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.02228v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces 'unfolded Krylov complexity' as a superior diagnostic tool for quantum chaos. By mathematically unfolding the energy spectrum, the authors successfully remove non-universal, smooth spectral contributions that cause false positives in integrable systems. This allows researchers to reliably probe the genuine, universal signatures of many-body quantum scrambling.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a mathematically rigorous tool to filter out model-dependent spectral artifacts, offering a cleaner diagnostic for identifying true quantum chaos in complex many-body systems, which is relevant for understanding thermalization and scrambling in quantum matter.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The central challenge is distinguishing genuine many-body quantum scrambling (chaos) from spurious signals arising from kinematic effects or integrable systems when using complexity diagnostics.

Main result. Unfolding the spectrum prior to calculating Krylov complexity successfully suppresses false-positive peaks in integrable systems while preserving the universal spectral signatures characteristic of genuinely chaotic quantum dynamics.

Method. The authors propose and implement 'spectral unfolding,' which separates the smooth, non-universal density of states from the microscopic spectral correlations, allowing for the construction of 'unfolded Krylov complexity.'

Model / system. The analysis is applied to various quantum systems, including Random Matrix Theory (RMT) ensembles (GOE, GUE, GSE), quantum billiards (stadium, circular), and the logarithmic model, using the Lanczos iteration framework.

Key observables. Krylov state complexity (spread complexity), Spectral Form Factor (SFF), and the unfolded spectrum $\xi_i$.

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis focuses on the infinite-temperature limit ($eta=0$) and the distinction between the smooth density $ar{ ho}(E)$ and the fluctuating part $\delta ho(E)$.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that the smooth component of the density of states can be accurately estimated and removed via unfolding.

Figures summary. Figures compare the raw vs. unfolded complexity/SFF for RMT spectra and quantum billiards, demonstrating the removal of spurious peaks in non-chaotic cases.

Paper structure. The paper systematically reviews the theory of unfolding and complexity, applies it to RMT and quantum billiards, develops the polynomial framework using the Lanczos iteration, and concludes with specific applications like the logarithmic model.

Abstract

A central challenge in diagnosing quantum chaos is to distinguish genuine many-body scrambling from kinematic effects of the spectrum. Krylov state complexity, or spread complexity, has emerged as a powerful diagnostic, with its characteristic growth, peak, and relaxation often taken as signatures of chaos. However, previous work has shown that this criterion can give false positives: saddle-dominated integrable systems may display prominent peaks even without random-matrix level correlations. We argue, based on complementary numerical and analytical evidence, that this ambiguity can be resolved by unfolding the spectrum prior to constructing the ensuing Krylov dynamics. By removing the non-universal smooth density of states while retaining microscopic spectral correlations, unfolding suppresses spurious peaks in integrable systems while preserving the universal spectral signatures of chaotic systems. Analytically, the formulation of the Lanczos iteration in terms of orthogonal polynomials yields an exact complexity kernel with a robust near-diagonal structure whose fine-grained features reflect the underlying spectral correlations. Moreover, for the logarithmic model, unfolding can be performed exactly, mapping the spectrum to a uniform lattice and yielding an analytic spread complexity that removes the false-positive peak. These findings establish unfolded Krylov complexity as a more reliable probe of genuine many-body scrambling.

Summary. This paper tackles the challenge of preparing complex Thermofield Double states for SYK models using deep quantum circuits. The authors introduce and test sequential angle-pruning algorithms applied to the multi-angle QAOA ansatz. These methods significantly reduce circuit depth while maintaining high fidelity, demonstrating a practical pathway toward implementing quantum simulations on near-term noisy quantum hardware.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the resource limitations of quantum computation by developing systematic, data-driven methods to compress variational quantum circuits used for simulating complex many-body physics models like SYK.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is preparing Thermofield Double (TFD) states using quantum circuits that are deep and resource-intensive, necessitating effective circuit reduction while maintaining high fidelity.

Main result. The proposed sequential angle-pruning algorithms (SAP/OSAP) successfully reduce the circuit depth by removing a large fraction of Pauli-string evolutions (up to 92.1%) while retaining an average fidelity near 95% for SYK models.

Method. The study employs the multi-angle QAOA (ma-QAOA) ansatz to approximate the TFD state, followed by two top-down sequential angle-pruning techniques that iteratively remove the least significant Pauli-string evolutions and reoptimize the remaining parameters.

Model / system. The work is applied to Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev (SYK) models, tested in both Gaussian and binary forms, across dense and sparse connectivity regimes. The TFD state is the target state, relevant for simulating quantum phenomena in holographic duality.

Key observables. Fidelity of the prepared TFD state, circuit depth (number of gates/blocks), and the percentage of removed nonlocal Pauli-string evolutions.

Important parameters / regimes. Inverse temperature ($eta$), number of qubits ($N$), and the pruning strength/candidate pool size ($S, N_C$).

Assumptions / limitations. The core assumption is that removing Pauli-string evolutions with small optimized angles does not significantly degrade the fidelity of the target TFD state, provided reoptimization is performed.

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically illustrates the SYK models and the pruning procedures (SAP-ma-QAOA and OSAP-ma-QAOA). Other figures track infidelity trajectories as a function of pruning strength or candidate pool size.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the need for circuit reduction in TFD state preparation, introduces ma-QAOA, and then details the two sequential angle-pruning algorithms (SAP and OSAP). The methods are validated by applying them to various SYK models, comparing fidelity retention against circuit depth reduction.

Abstract

Variational preparation of thermofield double (TFD) states can require deep quantum circuits, particularly for interacting many-body systems. Reducing these circuits while retaining high fidelity is therefore crucial for TFD-state preparation on noisy quantum processors. We study this problem by applying the multi-angle quantum approximate optimization algorithm (ma-QAOA) to TFD-state preparation and introducing two top-down sequential angle-pruning algorithms. Starting from the optimized initial ma-QAOA circuit, both algorithms sequentially remove Pauli-string evolutions with small optimized angles and reoptimize the remaining parameters after each removal. We apply these algorithms to Gaussian and binary Sachdev--Ye--Kitaev (SYK) models in both dense and sparse cases. We find that ma-QAOA prepares the target TFD states with high fidelity and that sequential small-angle pruning retains high fidelity while reducing the circuit depth, particularly at low temperature. Moreover, using the post-reoptimization cost in sequential small-angle pruning further improves the fidelity. For the binary sparse $N=10$ SYK model at $β=10$, $88.8\%$--$92.1\%$ of the nonlocal Pauli-string evolutions are removed while retaining an average fidelity of approximately $95\%$. Finally, we propose extensions of the sequential pruning algorithms toward quantum--classical hybrid implementation.

Summary. This paper resolves a major open question in quantum complexity by proving a quantum oracle separation between QMA and QMA(2). By constructing a specific oracle, the authors demonstrate that the ability to use two unentangled proofs provides a computational advantage over standard QMA protocols. This separation also yields a strong, exponential lower bound on the resources required by quantum disentanglers.

Why it may be interesting. The techniques used—polynomial methods applied to quantum query complexity and the analysis of subspace projectors—are highly relevant to understanding the fundamental computational power of quantum protocols, which underpins quantum information theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to determine if the quantum complexity class QMA(2) (verifiable with two unentangled quantum proofs) is strictly more powerful than QMA (standard quantum proofs).

Main result. They prove a quantum oracle separation, showing that there exists an oracle U such that QMA_U != QMA(2)_U, and consequently establish an exponential lower bound on the input size required by any disentangler for $\epsilon + \delta < 1$.

Method. The proof combines the unitarily invariant polynomial method (adapted from She and Yuen) with a novel construction utilizing symmetric and antisymmetric subspace projectors to derive the necessary lower bounds.

Model / system. The analysis is conducted within the framework of quantum query complexity, focusing on the separation between QMA and QMA(2) using a constructed unitary oracle U acting on bipartite Hilbert spaces.

Key observables. Acceptance probability, polynomial degree of the acceptance probability, and the required input size ($m$) for an $(\epsilon, \delta)$-disentangler.

Important parameters / regimes. The separation relies on the parameters $\epsilon$ and $\delta$ defining the disentangler approximation, and the ambient dimension $D$ related to the oracle structure.

Assumptions / limitations. The argument assumes the existence of polynomial bounds for running time, witness length, and oracle queries for the underlying QMA verifiers.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the polynomial degree lower bound shape, showing $p(0)=0$ and $p(j)$ bounded away from zero for consecutive $j$. Figure 2 is a tensor network diagram related to trace calculations.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the separation QMA_U != QMA(2)_U using a constructed oracle U and the polynomial method. It then leverages this separation to derive the exponential lower bound for disentanglers, resolving the Watrous conjecture.

Abstract

We find a quantum oracle relative to which $\mathsf{QMA} \neq \mathsf{QMA}(2)$. As a consequence, we resolve the no-disentanglers conjecture of Watrous: for every $ε+δ<1$, any $(ε,δ)$-disentangler requires input size exponential in the number of output qubits. Our proof combines the unitarily invariant polynomial method of She and Yuen (ITCS '23) with a new construction based on the symmetric and antisymmetric subspace projectors, reducing the $\mathsf{QMA}$ lower bound to the approximate degree of $\mathrm{OR}$.

Combinatorial optimization of connected UAV communication bridges for emergency response

Authors: Matteo Vandelli, Daniele Dragoni
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.02562v1

Summary. This work tackles the critical optimization challenge of deploying UAVs for emergency communication networks by formulating it as a complex combinatorial problem. Due to the exponential scaling of classical solvers, the authors reformulate the problem into a QUBO suitable for quantum computation. The research demonstrates the potential of quantum algorithms to solve these time-sensitive, large-scale optimization problems in real-time disaster response scenarios.

Why it may be interesting. The direct mapping of a complex, real-world combinatorial optimization problem (UAV placement) onto a QUBO framework and testing it with quantum algorithms makes it highly relevant for quantum algorithm development in applied physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the combinatorial optimization problem of strategically deploying UAVs to establish connected communication bridges in disaster zones.

Main result. Classical solvers exhibit exponential time-to-solution scaling, necessitating the reformulation of the problem as a QUBO suitable for quantum algorithms like the Quantum Adiabatic Algorithm (QAA).

Method. The problem is formulated as an Integer Quadratic Program (IQP) and subsequently mapped to a QUBO, which is then analyzed using both classical solvers (CPLEX) and quantum emulation (QAA).

Model / system. The system involves UAVs in a flying ad-hoc network (FANET) needing to maximize signal coverage and maintain connectivity via a backbone structure while mitigating interference using multi-frequency assignments.

Key observables. Signal coverage, network connectivity (backbone/leaves), interference levels, and Time-to-Solution (TTS).

Important parameters / regimes. Number of candidate sites ($N$), number of available drones ($V$), number of backbone nodes ($B$), and number of frequencies ($F$).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that quantum computing offers a scalable alternative to classical solvers for this time-critical optimization task; approximations are made regarding higher-order interference overlaps.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the network components (coverage, connectivity, interference) and show performance metrics for QAA on small instances, demonstrating the feasibility of quantum approaches.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the c-BUP problem, formulates it as an IQP/QUBO, analyzes the classical computational bottlenecks (exponential TTS), and then proposes and tests the quantum adiabatic algorithm approach.

Abstract

We present a combinatorial optimization problem for the strategic deployment of UAVs equipped with 5G antennas to assist rescue operations in regions hit by natural disasters. Our goal is to optimize the placement of UAVs to provide coverage in flying ad-hoc networks among given candidate sites. Our formulation aims to maximize signal coverage and minimize interference while ensuring network connectivity. To mitigate interference effects, we incorporate the use of multiple frequencies. We formulate this problem as an integer quadratic program (IQP). We present numerical solutions obtained via the CPLEX solver and conduct a preliminary analysis of the problem's scalability in realistic network configurations. Our findings reveal a significant exponential increase in Time-to-Solution (TTS) as the number of sites grows, which poses a critical challenge in urgent, time-sensitive scenarios. To address this issue, approximate suboptimal solutions can be produced by enforcing a time limit on the solver. Although these solutions are not optimal, they preserve connectivity in most cases, providing a practical trade-off between solution quality and computational times that remain within feasible limits for real-time UAV redeployment. Recognizing the limitations of classical solvers in these contexts, we explore quantum computing as a promising alternative. Specifically, we reformulate the problem as a quadratic unconstrained binary optimization (QUBO) problem, suitable for most quantum algorithms. Through high-performance computing emulation, we show that the quantum adiabatic algorithm (QAA) can accurately solve small-scale instances, paving the way for future application of quantum computing to large-scale, time-critical optimization problems in disaster response.

Summary. This paper provides a deep mathematical analysis comparing two independent, recent improvements to the asymptotic upper bound on binary code rates (the MRRW bound). It unifies these results by showing that both proofs fundamentally rely on counting the number of associated subspaces within the ambient space. The work is significant for both coding theory and for establishing rigorous mathematical limits in related quantum information contexts.

Why it may be interesting. While the core topic is classical coding theory, the paper draws deep parallels between classical bounds and quantum information concepts (like Holevo information and typical subspaces), making it relevant for understanding the mathematical limits of quantum communication protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to improve the asymptotic upper bound on the rate of binary codes, known as the McEliece–Rodemich–Rumsey–Welch (MRRW) bound.

Main result. The authors establish that two recently derived bounds, $\kappa_H(\delta)$ and $RMQC(\delta)$, are strictly better than the original MRRW-1 bound for $0 < \delta < 1/2$, and prove their equality.

Method. The analysis involves advanced techniques from coding theory, including moving from packing vectors to packing subspaces, utilizing Delsarte certificates, and employing spectral methods on the Hamming space.

Model / system. The mathematical model is based on binary codes $C \subset \{0, 1\}^n$ and their achievable rate $R_2(\delta)$ as a function of relative distance $\delta$. The analysis is generalized to involve subspaces attached to each codeword.

Key observables. The primary observables are the achievable rate $R(C)$ and the relative distance $\delta$. The bounds themselves, $M_1(\delta)$, $\kappa_H(\delta)$, and $RMQC(\delta)$, are key quantities.

Important parameters / regimes. The relative distance $\delta$ is the main parameter governing the bound. The analysis also involves parameters $a$ and $b$ when considering quantum channel analogies.

Assumptions / limitations. The derivation relies on the multiplicative notation for the Hamming space and the use of adjoint operators to decompose function spaces. A key assumption in one part is $\Lambda > s$ for the main bound to hold.

Figures summary. Table 1 is used to numerically compare the values of the original MRRW bound $M_1(\delta)$ against the improved bound $ ext{RMQC}(\delta)$ across various values of $\delta$, demonstrating the improvement.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the context of improving the MRRW bound. It then details the mathematical machinery, comparing the Delsarte certificate approach with the classical-quantum channel approach. The structure progresses from general coding theory bounds to specific spectral and harmonic-analytic derivations.

Abstract

The asymptotic McEliece--Rodemich--Rumsey--Welch bound (1977) limits the largest attainable rate of binary codes as a function of the relative distance. After a nearly half-century hiatus, this result was recently improved in two concurrent works, by OpenAI and by O. Alrabiah and V. Guruswami. The two arguments look entirely different, a Delsarte certificate on the one hand, a classical-quantum channel and the pretty good measurement on the other, and they yield the same bound. The purpose of this note is to explain why: in both proofs, a subspace is attached to every codeword and moved with it, and the bound counts how many such subspaces fit in the ambient space, exactly in the first case and in the probabilistic sense of typicality in the second. We also present the OpenAI proof in the language and context of coding theory, as an extension of the spectral method in which the single vector attached to a codeword is replaced by a subspace.

Abstract

Inhomogeneous stress is a driving force for diffusion of vacancies in crystals. The other way around a non-uniform distribution of vacancies induces stress. The accepted theory of composition-stress coupling in crystals is Larché-Cahn (LC) theory. Composition is defined in terms of the population of lattice sites with a limit of one-particle per site. Coupling is modelled by adding a compositional strain term to the elastic strain in the constitutive relation for stress. An alternative mechanism, proposed here, is letting the site binding energy vary with spatial (deformed) density. This generates a chemical pressure adding to the elastic stress. The governing equations for this model are derived using non-equilibrium continuum thermodynamic methods. The gradient of the chemical pressure has a dual function acting both as the drift force for migration and as an effective internal one body force in the Cauchy equation for the elastic stress. The practical evaluation presented in the paper is restricted to equilibrium properties. We examine the effect of externally applied non-hydrostatic stress, either in the form of surface tractions or a one-body force density (gravitation). The model system is a one component crystal with a fixed number of lattice sites. The number of particles can be variable but is always smaller than the number of lattice sites. The linear elastic response is modelled by the standard Lamé stress tensor. The results for systems deformed by surface tractions are in qualitative agreement with LC theory allowing for differences in the expression for the elastic moduli. Deviations are more serious for the crystal deformed by gravitation.

Abstract

Analogue black-hole systems have been proposed in various physical platforms, including magnetic materials, offering rich physics and promising applications such as wave lasing. However, their practical realization and characterization remain largely unexplored. Here, we present an in silico study of magnonic black-hole phenomena in constricted ferromagnetic waveguides driven by spin-polarized currents. Using micromagnetic simulations with a two-dimensional Poisson solver to obtain realistic current-density profiles, we demonstrate the formation of a double-hole cavity bounded by analogue event horizons, enabling resonant spin-wave amplification. We characterize the resonances as a function of magnetic and geometric parameters and identify the corresponding spin-wave modes. We further show that gradual tapers enhance transmission by suppressing spin-wave reflections, while interfacial Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction can mimic the current-induced Doppler shift, substantially reducing the critical current density required for applications and, consequently, Joule heating. Finally, we demonstrate a spin-wave laser in which thermally excited spin waves undergo selective amplification and coherent emission at well-defined resonance frequencies. These results provide design principles for magnonic analogues of gravitational systems and point toward their potential for advanced spintronic applications.

Abstract

We investigate how sensory coupling mediates phase coherence between spatially separated, confined active systems. Using two fish schools separated by a water-filled transparent gap, we isolate vision as the primary interaction channel. Under boundary confinement, each school's center-of-mass heading exhibits noisy bistable dynamics, spontaneously switching between two antiparallel directions along the partition wall. Turning events in one school bias the orientation of the other, driving distance-dependent phase synchronization. We model this behavior using two coupled stochastic bistable oscillators whose exact solution quantitatively reproduces the empirical results. Our work establishes a precise connection between nonequilibrium active systems and coupled stochastic oscillators, offering insight into boundary-spanning information transfer in living collectives.
