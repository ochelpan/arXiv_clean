Summary. This work introduces a novel, efficient algorithm within the Stochastic Series Expansion framework to compute imaginary-time correlation functions for both diagonal and off-diagonal operators. By implementing discrete imaginary-time slicing, the method overcomes previous computational bottlenecks associated with measuring non-local correlations in spin models like the TFIM and XXZ chain. This advancement significantly broadens the scope of quantum simulations for strongly correlated matter.

Why it may be interesting. The ability to efficiently measure off-diagonal correlation functions in strongly correlated spin systems is crucial for understanding quantum phase transitions and excitations (like spinons) that are often masked by diagonal measurements.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is to develop an efficient, general algorithm for calculating imaginary-time correlation functions in Quantum Monte Carlo simulations.

Main result. The authors present a method using discrete imaginary-time slicing of the SSE operator string that allows for the calculation of both diagonal and off-diagonal correlators with minimal overhead.

Method. The technique involves splitting the inverse temperature $eta$ into $m$ time slices ($\Delta au = eta/m$) within the Stochastic Series Expansion (SSE) framework. This slicing enables the direct estimation of correlation functions at discrete imaginary times $ au_k$.

Model / system. The method is benchmarked on spin systems, specifically the one-dimensional transverse-field Ising model (TFIM) and the XXZ Heisenberg chain. These models are used to test the algorithm's applicability across different types of operators.

Key observables. Imaginary-time correlation functions ($G( au)$), including both diagonal and off-diagonal spectral functions, which can be analytically continued to real frequencies.

Important parameters / regimes. Inverse temperature ($eta$), number of time slices ($m$), system size ($L$), and the specific coupling constants defining the Hamiltonian (e.g., $\Delta$ for XXZ).

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes that discretizing imaginary time into $m$ slices provides a representation accurate enough to avoid significant discretization errors, while maintaining the ergodicity of existing cluster update schemes.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the implementation details: one shows the directed-loop update for XXZ chains, and another demonstrates the TFIM cluster update mechanism used within the SSE framework.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the challenge of calculating off-diagonal correlators in QMC. It then introduces the core technique—time-slicing the SSE operator string—and derives estimators for both diagonal and off-diagonal operators. Finally, it validates this general algorithm on established models like TFIM and XXZ chains.

Abstract

Combined with numerical analytic continuation techniques, quantum Monte Carlo (QMC) methods enable the extraction of real-frequency dynamical properties from imaginary-time correlation functions. However, the efficient computation of imaginary-time correlation functions by QMC simulations can (depending on the particular model used) be challenging, particularly for operators that are off-diagonal in the computational basis. In this work, we present an efficient and general algorithm within the stochastic series expansion (SSE) framework for evaluating imaginary-time correlation functions of both diagonal and off-diagonal operators. The algorithm builds on a discrete imaginary-time slicing of the SSE operator string, which provides correlation functions on a grid of well-defined imaginary-time points with no discretization error. For off-diagonal operators, we derive estimators that integrate directly into the existing SSE directed-loop or cluster updating schemes, introducing only minimal computational overhead. We benchmark the method on the one-dimensional transverse-field Ising model (sampling with cluster updates) and XXZ spin chain (using directed-loop sampling), demonstrating excellent agreement (with only statistical errors) with exact diagonalization of small systems. We also study larger systems to demonstrate efficiency.

Abstract

Interactions among emitters provide a powerful means of controlling collective light--matter phenomena, yet their role in superradiant criticality has not been thoroughly investigated. Here we construct a minimal model that can yield analytical insights --- a Dicke model with two interacting atoms coupled to a single mode cavity --- to study such interaction effects. We show that interaction changes the phase boundary, and may even completely suppress the atom-photon coupling threshold for superradiance and change the universality class of the phase transition. We further study the dissipative phase transition and quantum dynamics in the presence of dissipation channels such as photon loss and spin relaxation. Our results demonstrate important roles played by the atom-atom interaction and how it can be engineered to control atom-photon coupling.

Summary. The paper presents CAMERA, a novel method to encode Circuit-SAT into an MWIS problem on Rydberg atom arrays using compact logic gadgets. By avoiding the massive overhead of CNF representations, it drastically reduces the required number of atoms while demonstrating end-to-end simulation capability for complex arithmetic circuits.

Main problem. The primary goal is to encode the NP-complete Boolean Circuit Satisfiability problem (Circuit-SAT) onto a physical quantum hardware platform.

Main result. A novel, atom-efficient encoding method called CAMERA was introduced, which reduces the required number of atoms for Circuit-SAT by an average factor of 22.4 compared to conventional CNF encodings.

Model / system. The physical platform is Rydberg atom arrays, which naturally encode the Maximum-Weight Independent Set (MWIS) problem via the blockade mechanism. The system's Hamiltonian governs interactions that restrict excitations to independent sets on a graph structure like the king subgraph.

Key observables. Maximum Weight ($W^\star$) of the MWIS, which corresponds to circuit functionality; port occupations read out from the ground state solution.

Important parameters / regimes. The atom cost reduction factor ($\approx 22.4$); the blockade radius ($R_b$); and the weights assigned to gadgets (e.g., $\{1, 2\}$).

Abstract

Rydberg atom arrays natively encode the maximum-weight independent set (MWIS) problem through the blockade mechanism, so the Boolean circuit satisfiability problem (Circuit-SAT) can be brought onto the platform once it is reduced to MWIS. The conventional encoding of Circuit-SAT in the Rydberg atom array proceeds through conjunctive normal form (CNF) and incurs a substantial atom overhead. We introduce CAMERA (Circuit-SAT Atom-efficient MWIS Encoding for Rydberg Arrays), a method that provides MWIS encodings of Circuit-SAT instances on the king subgraph geometry of the array. CAMERA represents each logic gate as a compact weighted gadget and assembles the gadgets with a placement and routing compiler inspired by very large scale integration (VLSI) design. On random multi-gate benchmarks, the direct encoding route lowers the atom cost relative to the CNF route by an average factor of $22.4 \pm 1.8$. To demonstrate that the encoding extends from individual weighted gadgets to multi-gate arithmetic blocks, we compile a full adder and a multiplier, verifying each against its complete truth table by exact classical ground state calculations. We further showcase solving a representative Circuit-SAT instance end-to-end, from gate level compilation through a closed-system tensor-network simulation of a hardware-compatible annealing protocol on the encoded 30-atom instance to readout of a satisfying assignment. These results establish a complete encoding and simulation workflow as a proof of principle, and a concrete route toward solving a broader family of combinatorial problems on Rydberg atom arrays.

Summary. The paper investigates how topology behaves in dissipative superconducting chains by analyzing the Kitaev model under Lindblad dynamics. It reveals that while local measurements can be 'sector-blind' due to cross-talk between Majorana sectors, nonlocal diagnostics like the entanglement spectrum zero events successfully reveal the underlying, hidden open-boundary topological structure.

Why it may be interesting. This work bridges non-Hermitian physics (dissipation) with topological invariants and entanglement measures, providing advanced diagnostics for open quantum systems where standard bulk-boundary correspondence fails. This is highly relevant to AMO/open system research.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how non-Bloch topology manifests and breaks down in dissipative topological superconductors, particularly when multiple Majorana sectors are present.

Main result. The system exhibits a sector-resolved non-Bloch bulk-boundary correspondence at zero chemical potential, which is masked by local observables due to cross-sector covariance. This hidden edge content can be recovered dynamically via finite-time zero events in the entanglement spectrum.

Method. The analysis employs third quantization on a bond-dissipative Kitaev chain, analyzing the Liouvillian spectrum and utilizing the spatial entanglement spectrum (ES) dynamics under quenches.

Model / system. The model is a bond-dissipative dimerized Kitaev chain governed by a Lindblad master equation. The Hamiltonian includes hopping terms ($t_{1a}, t_{2b}$) and onsite potentials, alongside dissipation rates ($\gamma_\ell, \gamma_g$).

Key observables. Non-Bloch winding numbers ($ u$), local density relaxation (cross-sector covariance), and finite-time zero events in the entanglement spectrum ($\xi_\ell = 1/2$).

Important parameters / regimes. Zero chemical potential ($\mu=0$) is crucial for sector separation; finite $\mu$ causes hybridization. The ratio of hopping parameters ($\Delta/t$) controls the topological regime.

Assumptions / limitations. The exact sector decomposition into two independent non-Hermitian sectors is only valid at $\mu=0$. For $\mu eq 0$, the analysis must use the full rapidity matrix due to hybridization.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the schematic of the chain structure and sector split ($\mu=0$), map out non-Bloch winding numbers vs. coupling ratios, show observable selection rules (e.g., $ ext{G}_{14}$ decay), and demonstrate ES zero events signaling hidden OBC topology.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the general framework of dissipative non-Hermitian topology. It then analyzes the specific case at $\mu=0$ to reveal sector-resolved diagnostics. Finally, it extends the analysis to finite chemical potential ($\mu eq 0$), showing how hybridization complicates the simple sector separation but that total ES dynamics remain a robust diagnostic tool.

Abstract

A core characteristic of dissipative non-Hermitian topology is that the relaxation dynamics tracks the non-Bloch bulk-boundary correspondence, rendering an algebraic decay in the gapless regime and an exponential falloff in the gapped phase, so that local observables directly diagnose the topology. We show that this correspondence breaks down in a dissipative topological superconductor, where the local observables turn blind to the very topology they are expected to decipher. Via a bond-dissipative dimerized Kitaev chain in a third-quantized rapidity-matrix formulation, we find that at zero chemical potential the Majorana rapidity matrix decomposes into two independent non-Hermitian sectors, each with its own generalized Brillouin zone and non-Bloch winding number, thereby revealing a sector-resolved non-Bloch bulk-boundary correspondence. The local density is a cross-sector covariance and relaxes at the sum of the two sector rates, so it remains sector-blind even when one sector is gapless and topological. For balanced gain and loss, the finite-time zero events of the entanglement spectrum under purely periodic-boundary Lindblad evolution recover this hidden edge content sector by sector, serving as a dynamical invariant that returns the open-boundary edge rapidities without physically opening the chain.

Abstract

Real-to-complex spectral transitions mark the onset of amplification in non-Hermitian systems, but their thresholds are often treated as model-specific quantities. Abstract

Abstract

We identify a general mechanism for the finite-time thermalization of macroscopic observables, such as coarse-grained charge densities, in terms of elementary forms of the quantum dynamics of initial states: (1) aperiodicity, which provides a computable measure of (2) a dynamical partially ergodic exploration of the Hilbert space.
