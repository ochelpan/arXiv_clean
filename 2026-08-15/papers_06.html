Summary. This paper analyzes the Casimir interaction and edge physics in Maxwell--Chern-Simons theory on a finite strip. Using advanced field theory techniques, it derives that the vacuum energy exhibits distinct asymptotic behaviors depending on whether the topological mass term (from the Chern-Simons part) is zero or non-zero. This work provides a rigorous framework for understanding how boundary topology modifies bulk quantum interactions.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on gauge theory, the explicit calculation of vacuum energy (Casimir effect) in a confined geometry provides a concrete example of how topological terms modify standard QFT results, which is relevant for understanding boundary effects in quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates edge physics and the resulting Casimir interaction within Maxwell--Chern-Simons (MCS) theory defined on a strip geometry.

Main result. The analysis derives a closed determinant representation for the finite vacuum interaction energy, showing that the force transitions from a power-law decay in the pure Maxwell limit to an exponentially screened Yukawa potential when topological mass is present.

Method. The authors employ the Symanzik framework for quantum field theory with boundaries, deriving boundary conditions via variational principles and analyzing the resulting scattering determinant structure.

Model / system. The system is MCS theory on a strip $\mathbb{R}_{1,1} imes [0, h]$, characterized by both Maxwell and Chern-Simons terms. The bulk supports one massive physical helicity, while boundary physics involves two coupled $U(1)$ current algebras.

Key observables. The Casimir interaction energy ($E_{ ext{int}}$), reflection coefficients, and the structure of the secular determinant governing vacuum fluctuations.

Important parameters / regimes. Strip width ($h$), Chern-Simons coupling ($\kappa$), and the resulting topological mass ($m = \kappa g^2$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on retaining terms up to one tangential derivative in the boundary functional and assumes a specific 'edge-active' choice for boundary conditions.

Paper structure. The paper follows a structured derivation: establishing boundary equations from local functionals, deriving conserved currents via Ward identities, reducing the problem to physical modes, and finally calculating the interaction energy using scattering determinants.

Abstract

We study Maxwell--Chern--Simons theories on a strip $\mathcal M=\mathbb R^{1,1}\times[0,h]$ within the Symanzik framework for quantum field theory with boundaries. We add the most general local boundary functional within the quadratic one-tangential-derivative truncation adopted throughout the paper and derive the admissible boundary conditions from locality and the variational principle, with gauge symmetry implemented through boundary Ward identities. The strip supports two boundary $U(1)$ current algebras, with opposite levels fixed by the Chern--Simons coupling $κ$ and by boundary orientation, while edge velocities depend on local boundary couplings. They are equal and opposite on the flip-symmetric branch, which is the natural choice for a spatially symmetric strip. The same boundary data determine the reflection coefficients for bulk modes and yield a closed determinant (scattering) representation of the finite interaction energy. In the Maxwell limit ($κ=0$) the inter-edge interaction is long-ranged and produces a power-law Casimir force, whereas in Maxwell--Chern--Simons theory the topological mass $m=κg^2$ generates Yukawa suppression at large separations $mh\gg1$. The determinant form also gives analytic control of the asymptotic regimes and separates topological boundary data (levels and anomaly inflow) from dynamical bulk effects (dispersion, boundary mixing and Casimir interaction).

Summary. This work unifies the seemingly separate phenomena of ion migration and electron-phonon coupling in metal halide perovskites using an orbital hybridization descriptor, $R$. By showing that both processes are governed by the same underlying electronic structure parameter, the authors propose a generalized mechanism for designing soft semiconductors with optimized charge and ionic transport simultaneously.

Why it may be interesting. While not directly focused on quantum information, the rigorous theoretical coupling of lattice vibrations (phonons) to electronic structure parameters ($R$) provides a powerful model for understanding strongly coupled open quantum systems where environment interaction dictates transport properties.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The research aims to develop a unified microscopic description linking lattice dynamics, electron-phonon coupling (EPC), and ion migration in metal halide perovskites.

Main result. A single orbital hybridization descriptor ($R$) was introduced to show that both ionic transport barriers and EPC strengths arise from the same underlying electronic structure framework. This suggests a cooperative mechanism for optimizing charge and ionic transport simultaneously.

Method. The study employs Density Functional Theory (DFT) calculations, utilizing an orbital hybridization descriptor derived from a dual-atom model to quantify how lattice displacements modulate electronic coupling.

Model / system. The physical system is Metal Halide Perovskites ($ ext{CsPbI}_3$, $ ext{CsSnI}_3$, and double perovskites). The theoretical framework uses an orbital hybridization descriptor $R = 2V / |\Delta E|$ to model the coupling between metal and halide orbitals.

Key observables. Electron-phonon coupling strength ($\Gamma_{e- ext{ph}}$), ion migration barrier energies ($E_b$), and the orbital hybridization descriptor ($R$).

Important parameters / regimes. The relative values of $R$ across different perovskite compositions (e.g., $ ext{Cs}_2 ext{AgBiBr}_6 > ext{Sn-I} > ext{Pb-I}$), and the dependence of $R$ on specific phonon modes (stretching vs. shearing).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the descriptor $R$ acts as a universal electronic parameter governing both lattice restoring forces (migration) and dielectric screening (EPC). Calculations are often performed on archetypal cubic phases.

Figures summary. Figures conceptually link the hybridization descriptor $R$ to phonon modes: low-frequency shearing modes govern ion migration, while high-frequency stretching modes govern carrier scattering via the Fröhlich interaction. Supplementary figures detail how $R$ changes under specific atomic displacements (shortening/elongating bonds).

Paper structure. The paper establishes a conceptual framework linking bonding to dynamics using $R$. It then systematically calculates and analyzes how perturbing bond lengths or angles modulates $R$, which in turn dictates both the ion migration barrier and the EPC strength across multiple perovskite types.

Abstract

The remarkable optoelectronic properties of metal halide perovskites are closely linked to their unusually soft and polar chemical bonds that enable both strong electron-phonon interactions and ion migration. Yet these two defining characteristics have largely been treated as independent consequences of the same underlying chemical bonding. Here we show that they originate from a common electronic-structure framework by developing a general description linking lattice dynamics, electron-phonon coupling, and halide ion migration across representative Pb-based, Sn-based, and double perovskites. Spectrally resolved phonon-mode contributions demonstrate that the low-frequency shearing modes dominate halide migration, whereas high-frequency stretching modes govern carrier scattering through the Fröhlich interaction in all three compositions. We introduce an orbital hybridization descriptor to unify these findings, which connects metal-halide bonding characteristics with the migration barrier energies and Fröhlich coupling strengths, indicating a cooperative evolution of these two properties. These findings provide a generalized microscopic mechanism for simultaneously optimizing charge and ionic transport in soft semiconductors.

Abstract

This work numerically validates the application of bio-inspired concepts on CMOS derived from bacterial photosynthetic light harvesters. We investigate a modification of symmetric inverted pyramid array CMOS image sensors into an asymmetrically shaped texture to explore the structural boundary of passive non-reciprocity. Diverging from traditional macroscopic continuum assumptions, we analyze whether structural asymmetry at sub-wavelength scales can induce non-reciprocal scattering under passive, linear, and time-invariant conditions. A theoretical framework based on perturbation theory is developed, estimating a potential efficiency enhancement of 5\% to 15\%. Numerical simulations performed via the MEEP finite-difference time-domain (FDTD) platform reveal that the linear response is highly localized, showing a subtle 0.02\% change. This suggests that macroscopic Lorentz reciprocity remains robust at the investigated scale due to apex field concentration, defining a clear geometric threshold for microscopic non-reciprocity.

Summary. This work investigates 'moiré magnetic chaos' in twisted bilayer CrI3, demonstrating that the inherent interlayer exchange frustration generates chaotic dynamics without external driving. Using micromagnetic simulations, the authors quantify this chaos via Lyapunov exponents and analyze the resulting domain patterns, showing they are statistically random yet governed by underlying topological constraints.

Why it may be interesting. The study bridges concepts from nonlinear dynamics (chaos theory) into condensed matter magnetism, providing a concrete physical realization of autonomous chaos in a solid-state system, which is highly relevant for understanding complex magnetic switching and memory effects.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To demonstrate a new type of magnetic chaos, termed 'moiré magnetic chaos,' arising from mesoscopic domain variables in twisted bilayer CrI3 without external driving.

Main result. The system exhibits intrinsic, undriven chaos characterized by extreme sensitivity to initial conditions and stochastic domain configurations due to interlayer exchange frustration. This identifies a microscopic mechanism for nonlinear magnetism beyond conventional driven regimes.

Method. Micromagnetic simulations are used extensively, employing techniques like calculating finite-time Lyapunov exponents, analyzing damage spreading from perturbations, and performing statistical ensemble characterization.

Model / system. The system is twisted bilayer CrI3, modeled by a Heisenberg spin Hamiltonian incorporating interlayer exchange coupling ($J_{ ext{inter}}$). The moiré superlattice structure creates regions of frustration (AFM patches) that provide multiple dynamical degrees of freedom necessary for autonomous chaos.

Key observables. Finite-time Lyapunov exponents ($\lambda$), Hamming Fraction ($H/N_d$), Shannon entropy ($\langle H_n angle$), and topological charge ($Q$) of reversed domains.

Important parameters / regimes. Twist angle ($ heta = 1.61^\circ$); characteristic energy gap $\Delta E/|E| \approx 1.8 imes 10^{-4}$; Lyapunov exponent scales (e.g., $9 ext{ ns}^{-1}$ vs. $290.8 ext{ ns}^{-1}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes weak coupling between neighboring AFM patches, allowing for quasi-independent polarization selection in each patch; the chaotic behavior is understood as transient sensitivity rather than sustained attractor dynamics.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate interlayer exchange maps showing FM/AFM patterns (Fig. 1a), representative metastable domain configurations (Fig. 1b), damage spreading evolution ($\delta m(t)$) confirming chaos, and topological charge distributions classifying domains as skyrmion-like or bubble-like.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the physical platform (twisted CrI3) and the mechanism for frustration to quantifying chaos using Lyapunov exponents and perturbation analysis. It then characterizes the resulting domain states statistically (entropy, correlations) and topologically (skyrmions vs. bubbles), concluding with a refined understanding of transient versus sustained chaotic dynamics.

Abstract

The study of magnetic chaos has traditionally focused on macroscopic variables under external driving. Here we demonstrate a new type of magnetic chaos, termed moiré magnetic chaos, associated with mesoscopic magnetic domain variables in twisted bilayer CrI3 without external driving. The domains are stabilized by a characteristic interlayer exchange frustration, which supplies the multiple dynamical degrees of freedom required for autonomous chaos. Through micromagnetic simulations, we show that relaxation toward moiré magnetic textures is extremely sensitive to minute local perturbations of the initial state, characterized by substantial finite-time Lyapunov exponents and a final-state sensitivity that persists over five decades of perturbation amplitude. Statistical analysis further reveals that the resulting domain configurations are stochastic and pairwise uncorrelated. Our results identify a form of microscopic, undriven chaos in twisted magnets that extends nonlinear magnetism beyond the conventional driven regime.

Summary. This paper introduces Adiabatic Perturbation Theory (APT) to accurately model energy and charge transfer in electron-ion systems. It systematically derives corrections beyond the standard Born-Oppenheimer approximation by treating the small parameter $\epsilon$ (electron-to-nucleus mass ratio) perturbatively. The resulting framework allows for calculating advanced transport properties like nonadiabatic conductivity with controlled accuracy.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides rigorous, high-order theoretical tools for treating nonadiabatic coupling effects in condensed matter systems, which is crucial for understanding energy and charge flow where electronic and nuclear motions are strongly coupled.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core problem is quantifying energy transfer and charge transport in condensed matter systems with controlled accuracy concerning the adiabatic small parameter ($\epsilon$, the electron-to-nucleus mass ratio). This requires going beyond standard approximations like the harmonic approximation.

Main result. The authors develop Adiabatic Perturbation Theory (APT) to systematically derive effective Hamiltonians and calculate nonadiabatic corrections to transport properties, such as frequency-dependent conductivity and Drude weight, to any desired order in $\epsilon$.

Method. The primary method is Adiabatic Perturbation Theory (APT), which treats the electron-ion Schrödinger equation as a singularly perturbed differential equation in $\epsilon$ by employing near-identity unitary transformations to decouple electronic and nuclear degrees of freedom.

Model / system. The model is an electron-ion system described by a full nonrelativistic Hamiltonian. The theory analyzes how coupling between fast electronic motion and slow, heavy ionic motion (nuclei) affects transport properties.

Key observables. Rate of change of the nuclear kinetic energy (energy transfer formula), nonadiabatic frequency-dependent conductivity, and Drude weight.

Important parameters / regimes. The adiabatic small parameter $\epsilon$ (electron-to-nucleus mass ratio); the bare nuclear mass tensor $M_0$.

Assumptions / limitations. The theory systematically improves upon approximations by controlling errors in powers of $\epsilon$, avoiding assumptions inherent to the harmonic approximation or standard Born-Oppenheimer treatments.

Paper structure. The paper builds from identifying the failure points of simpler models (like the Harmonic Approximation) to introducing APT as a systematic, order-by-order expansion in $\epsilon$ via unitary transformations to derive an effective Hamiltonian for transport calculations.

Abstract

Energy and charge transfer in condensed matter systems are often described in terms of quasiparticles. The order of accuracy of the results in terms of the electron-to-nucleus mass ratio $ε$, an adiabatic small parameter, is usually not known. To quantify energy transfer with controlled accuracy in $ε$, we use adiabatic perturbation theory to derive a formula for the rate of change of the nuclear kinetic energy. Applying a sequence of near-identity unitary transformations decouples the electronic and nuclear degrees of freedom to higher and higher order and produces an effective Hamiltonian. In the special case that the nuclei are treated classically, the energy transfer formula reduces to the usual formula for the rate of work done on a classical particle but the mass of the particle is enhanced by the factor $M_0^{-1}(M_0+εM_1)$, where $M_1$ is a nonadiabatic correction to the bare nuclear mass tensor $M_0$. Additional quantum geometric corrections appear at higher orders. Adiabatic perturbation theory is further used to incorporate, order-by-order in $ε$, nonadiabatic transitions into linear response calculations. The nonadiabatic frequency-dependent conductivity and Drude weight of an electron-ion system are calculated.

Emergent trans-moiré orbitals and topology in rhombohedral graphene

Summary. The paper reports the discovery of 'trans-moiré orbitals' in rhombohedral graphene/hBN moiré superlattices. These orbitals emerge on the side distant from the primary moiré interface and are responsible for stabilizing topological features like the FQAHE. The findings resolve a long-standing paradox by showing that electron interactions force the system into these specific, topologically active orbital states.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses emergent topological phases in moiré systems using spectroscopic measurements, which is highly relevant for understanding correlated electron behavior in engineered quantum materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to resolve the microscopic mechanism behind the Fractional Quantum Anomalous Hall Effect (FQAHE) in rhombohedral graphene moiré superlattices, specifically addressing the paradox of observing a small-twist-angle interface only when electrons are kept distant from it.

Main result. The authors discovered emergent 'trans-moiré orbitals' on the moiré-distant surface, which enforce moiré periodicity and vanish at critical twist angles ($ heta \sim 1^\circ$), providing a microscopic explanation for the topological effects observed.

Method. The research combines Scanning Tunneling Microscopy/Spectroscopy (STM/STS) measurements with self-consistent mean-field calculations to map out the electronic structure reshaping.

Model / system. The physical system is rhombohedral hexalayer graphene ($ ext{R6G}$) stacked on hexagonal boron nitride ($ ext{hBN}$), forming a moiré superlattice. The electronic properties are tuned by gate voltage and controlled by the moiré twist angle ($ heta$).

Key observables. Trans-moiré orbitals (e.g., hollow-cage shape), flat-band renormalization strength, Chern minibands, and quantized Hall plateaus.

Important parameters / regimes. Moiré twist angle ($ heta$), filling factor ($v$), and gate voltage ($V_{ ext{bg}}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The mechanism is attributed to an interaction-driven charge-redistribution across layers, forcing electrons into these topological orbitals.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate STM topographies and dI/dV spectra showing moiré renormalization on the distant surface for small $ heta$, contrasting with null results at large $ heta$. Simulations map these emergent orbitals to theoretical Chern minibands.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the problem (FQAHE mechanism), presents experimental evidence via STM mapping of trans-moiré orbitals under specific twist angles, explains this emergence through interaction-driven charge redistribution, and concludes by linking this finding to a general platform for synthetic FQAHE realization.

Abstract

The fractional quantum anomalous Hall effect (FQAHE) exhibited in fractional Chern insulators has recently been demonstrated in twisted MoTe2 and rhombohedral graphene/hBN moiré superlattices, promising new routes toward topological quantum computation. Central to realizing this promise is the understanding of the underlying microscopic mechanism. This, however, remains elusive in the case of rhombohedral graphene, with the crux being its two seemingly paradoxical conditions: a pronounced small-twist-angle (θ) moiré interface, yet only when electrons are kept distant from it. Here, by scanning tunnelling microscopic imaging with both conditions fulfilled, we capture dramatic electronic structure reshaping in rhombohedral hexalayer graphene by unforeseen 'trans-moiré orbitals', which emerge on the other, distant side of the moiré interface but nevertheless enforce the moiré periodicity at all measured fillings. We visualize a hierarchy of spatially and energetically distinct trans-moiré orbitals which doped electrons must sequentially occupy--the lowest-energy orbital, expectedly responsible for the FQAHE at small fillings, carries a hollow-cage-like shape. Remarkably, these trans-moiré orbitals vanish at θ {\gtrsim} 1°, and so do QAHE plateaus in similar devices. Simulations reveal an interaction-driven charge-redistribution mechanism which shapes the trans-moiré orbitals and corresponding Chern minibands. With our findings providing the missing microscopic link, the paradoxical conditions find a natural explanation: electrons are not simply kept distant from a small-θ moiré interface; they are forced into topological trans-moiré orbitals, forged precisely under such conditions. Our microscopic diagnostics unlocks a wide range of possible 'synthetic' FQAHE platforms.

Abstract

In this article, the nonlinear Helmholtz equation with non-Kerr nonlinearity, such as self steepening and self frequency shift, is considered. A travelling wave transformation is applied, and the extended nonlinear Helmholtz equation is reduced to a Hamiltonian dynamical system. Then, the reduced Hamiltonian system is analyzed by classification of equilibrium points, phase space analysis, and the construction of exact wave solutions. The relationship between the reduced dynamical coefficients and the original physical parameters is further established through a parameter space analysis. It is shown that self steepening directly modifies the reduced dynamics, whereas self frequency shift acts through the compatibility condition for the real travelling wave reduction. Together, these non-Kerr effects reshape the phase space geometry and travelling wave structure. Localized and periodic travelling waves are obtained, with their existence determined by the balance among dispersion, nonparaxiality, Kerr nonlinearity, and non-Kerr effects. Furthermore, a periodically forced version of the reduced system is examined to study the transition from regular to irregular dynamics. It has been observed that external forcing can induce complex oscillatory behavior. Bifurcation analysis, time series evolution, phase space analysis, largest Lyapunov exponent, and Poincaré section demonstrate the emergence of quasiperiodic and chaotic responses under sufficiently strong forcing. All analytical branches are verified through full-equation residual evaluation, while a few selected branches are additionally examined through direct numerical propagation and robustness tests under complex Gaussian perturbations. The results show that self steepening directly renormalizes the effective nonlinear dynamics, whereas self frequency shift restricts the admissible real-envelope travelling wave manifold.

Abstract

We report simulations of the dissolution and diffusion of Nb's surface oxide layer in vacuum. While this chemical doping process is important for the surface preparation of Nb superconducting radio frequency (SRF) cavities - common components of particle accelerators - quantitatively linking the resulting oxygen distributions to superconducting performance remains challenging. In this work, we simulate the reaction-diffusion process numerically for treatment temperatures $T = 50^{\circ}$C to $200^{\circ}$C and times $t = 0.5$ h to $120$ h, and calculate the effect of the spatially inhomogeneous oxygen doping on Nb's superconducting properties. We find that oxygen doping redistributes the Meissner screening current, reducing its value at the surface and shifting its maximum several nanometres into the material. These results provide a microscopic link between oxygen diffusion profiles and the electromagnetic response of Nb relevant for SRF cavity operation. This work provides a quantitative framework linking oxygen diffusion profiles to superconducting performance and establishes a foundation for future studies involving time-dependent and multi-step heat treatment protocols.
