Summary. This paper advances the theoretical understanding of Device-Independent Quantum Key Distribution by reformulating security analysis within the general commuting-operator framework. It overcomes limitations of previous methods that required restrictive structural assumptions on the underlying Hilbert space. The work establishes a rigorous mathematical link between observed quantum statistics and achievable key rates via non-commutative polynomial optimization.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on quantum cryptography, the reliance on advanced operator algebra techniques (von Neumann algebras) and optimization theory provides deep mathematical tools applicable to analyzing open quantum systems where standard Hilbert space assumptions break down.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To develop a maximally general mathematical framework for analyzing Device-Independent Quantum Key Distribution (DIQKD) security that avoids restrictive assumptions found in existing proof techniques.

Main result. The authors provide a representation-independent route from observed statistics to an AEP-based security statement using the commuting operator framework, and show key rate computation maps to a solvable Non-Commutative Polynomial Optimization (NPO) problem.

Method. The work employs advanced tools from von Neumann algebras, generalizing relative entropy calculations (Frenkel's formula) and casting the optimization problem into an NPA hierarchy relaxation scheme.

Model / system. The study models DIQKD experiments using the commuting-operator framework, which utilizes universal C*-algebras to represent local operations for Alice and Bob, thereby avoiding assumptions about tensor product structures in the Hilbert space.

Key observables. Secure key rates, relative entropy, and conditional von Neumann entropy.

Important parameters / regimes. The security analysis depends on observed input-output statistics of the uncharacterized device.

Assumptions / limitations. The framework aims to overcome assumptions regarding tensor product structure and hidden finite dimensionality inherent in prior DIQKD analyses.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the general setup of a DIQKD experiment involving Alice, Bob, a source, and local random number generators.

Paper structure. The paper builds by first establishing the commuting-operator framework, then generalizing necessary tools like relative entropy to von Neumann algebras, proving key technical results (like PVM assumption), and finally formulating the security rate calculation as an NPO problem solvable via NPA relaxations.

Abstract

Device-independent quantum key distribution (DIQKD) is arguably the gold standard for secure quantum communication, as it aims to rely only on observed input-output statistics of an uncharacterized device which is only assumption to obey the laws of quantum physics. A corresponding security analysis hence demands a description of a quantum experiment from a most general perspective. Under close inspection, existing proof techniques do not always meet this goal as they tend to rely on subtile assumptions on a tensor product structure of the underlying Hilbert space and a 'hidden but finite' dimensionality. In this work, we collect the tools needed for a full analysis of DIQKD in the commuting operator framework, which avoids these subtilities and provides the arguably most general view on a quantum experiment. We rigorously proof the common assumption that in DIQKD measurements can be w.l.o.g. assumed to be projective. Furthermore, we show that task of computing key rates can be casted as a non-commutative polynomial optimization (NPO) problem to which the Navascués-Pironio-Acín (NPA) hierarchy gives a correct and converging relaxation. As a tool, we generalize the integral representation for the relative entropy by Frenkel [Quantum 7, 1102 (2023)] to general von Neumann algebras and apply techniques from Kossmann and Schwonnek [arXiv: 2411.04858] for the approximation in an NPO program.

Abstract

Quantum machine learning (QML) could have the potential to leverage advantages of quantum over classical computing but still lacks strong evidence of actual improvements and scalability, partly due to phenomena such as barren plateaus. In this paper, we employ a hybrid quantum neural network (QNN) on a dataset on cloud microphysics, containing processes for phase transitions of water in the atmosphere and its related temperature changes, which are highly relevant for accurate climate predictions and projections. Summary. This paper introduces stellar braid monodromy as a novel, robust topological invariant for classifying finite-rank non-Gaussian bosonic states of light. By mapping the state space to an unordered configuration space, it shows that the movement of the state's defining zeros generates elements in the Artin braid group. This provides a powerful geometric tool complementing existing diagnostics like stellar rank.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful topological tool for characterizing quantum resources (non-Gaussianity) in photonic systems that goes beyond simple algebraic measures, linking quantum optics directly to deep mathematical structures like braid groups.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To assign a robust topological invariant, called stellar braid monodromy, to characterize regular parameterized families of finite-rank non-Gaussian bosonic quantum states.

Main result. The regular stratum of these state spaces is biholomorphic to the unordered configuration space of $r$ points in $\mathbb{C}$, and its fundamental group is the Artin braid group. The resulting monodromy invariant complements existing scalar measures of non-Gaussianity.

Method. The method involves mapping the quantum states (via their Bargmann representation) to a polynomial whose zeros define the stellar divisor. Braid monodromy is then calculated by tracking how these zeros move under continuous deformations in the state space, utilizing tools from algebraic topology and classical root theory.

Model / system. The system concerns single-mode photonic quantum states restricted to finite-Fock subspaces $H_r$. These non-Gaussian states are mathematically characterized by a finite stellar divisor encoded within their Bargmann representation polynomial.

Key observables. Stellar braid monodromy (an element of the Artin braid group $ ext{Br}$), Stellar Rank, Wigner negativity, and approximate stellar rank.

Important parameters / regimes. The degree $r$ of the finite-rank state; parameters defining the path or family within the regular stratum.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is restricted to 'regular' paths where all stellar zeros are simple, and it primarily concerns single-mode states, excluding mixed states or multi-mode dynamics.

Figures summary. Not specified in the notes; the focus is on mathematical structures (configuration spaces, braid groups) rather than explicit figure descriptions.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the connection between non-Gaussian states and polynomial zeros, identifies the regular stratum with a configuration space whose fundamental group is $ ext{Br}$, defines the monodromy invariant by tracking zero motion, and finally applies this to concrete physical examples like cubic subspaces.

Abstract

Finite-rank non-Gaussian bosonic states admit a holomorphic description in the Bargmann representation: after a zero-free Gaussian factor is separated off, their non-Gaussian structure is encoded by a finite stellar divisor. This article introduces a topological refinement of stellar rank for regular parameterized families of such states. Rather than only counting the zeros of the stellar divisor, we follow their motion under deformations of the state and record the associated braid monodromy. In the finite-Fock chart, a regular degree-r stellar state is represented by a monic polynomial with r simple zeros. The regular stratum is biholomorphic to the unordered configuration space of r points in the complex plane, and its fundamental group is the Artin braid group on r strands. Thus braid monodromy is an intrinsic invariant of loops in the regular finite-rank stellar state space. We then extend the construction to admissible finite stellar divisors of the form E_tau,mu(z) P(z); the zero-free Gaussian parameters form a contractible fiber over the same configuration-space base. Experimentally motivated finite-Fock families, especially the cubic subspace spanned by the first four Fock states, provide concrete laboratories, while trinomial slices yield explicit discriminants and local half-twists. The resulting invariant is post-tomographic and applies to preparation loops and parameterized families; it complements Wigner negativity, stellar rank, approximate stellar rank, and other scalar diagnostics of non-Gaussianity.

Summary. This theoretical work tackles an inverse problem in Density Functional Theory by calculating the precise external wall potential needed to force a fluid's density profile to remain constant near a substrate. Using FMT, the authors derive explicit analytical solutions for planar and spherical geometries, providing powerful tools for understanding structure-cancelling boundary fields.

Why it may be interesting. While purely classical, the rigorous application of DFT and FMT to control boundary conditions is foundational knowledge for understanding confinement effects relevant to quantum systems like ultracold atoms near surfaces or optical lattices.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study solves the inverse problem of classical Density Functional Theory (DFT): determining the external wall potential required to enforce a constant equilibrium density profile near an inhomogeneous substrate.

Main result. Explicit analytical expressions for 'structure-cancelling' wall potentials are derived for planar and spherical geometries, confirming that these potentials successfully recover the prescribed flat density profiles in independent DFT calculations.

Method. The authors utilize Fundamental Measure Theory (FMT) within the framework of classical DFT to derive self-consistent equations relating the required potential to the direct correlation functions and weighted densities.

Model / system. The system is a one-component fluid modeled using hard-sphere repulsion, optionally supplemented by a mean-field treated truncated Lennard-Jones attraction. The analysis covers planar, spherical, and cylindrical confinement geometries.

Key observables. Equilibrium density profile ($ ho(r)$), the required wall potential ($V(r)$), weighted densities ($n_\alpha$), and one-body direct correlation functions ($c^{(1)}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The geometry (planar, spherical, cylindrical) dictates the mathematical tractability; the interaction strength (hard-sphere vs. attractive tail) influences the required potential.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on solving the Euler-Lagrange equation derived from minimizing the grand-potential functional, and the attraction is treated only at the mean-field level.

Figures summary. Figures likely compare calculated density profiles against a constant reference value, demonstrating the accuracy of the constructed potentials across different geometries (e.g., planar vs. spherical).

Paper structure. The paper systematically tackles the inverse problem for three symmetries (planar, spherical, cylindrical), deriving explicit mathematical forms for weighted densities and correlation functions in each case before presenting the final wall potential required to maintain a constant density.

Abstract

We study the inverse problem of classical density functional theory for inhomogeneous fluids: finding the wall potential that produces a constant equilibrium density profile, i.e., a perfectly flat density distribution in the accessible region adjacent to a substrate. Within Rosenfeld's fundamental measure theory, we solve this problem for a one-component fluid in planar, spherical, and cylindrical geometries, considering both a hard-sphere fluid and a fluid with an additional truncated Lennard-Jones attraction treated at the mean-field level. Explicit analytical expressions are obtained for planar walls, while spherical walls also admit an analytical treatment in a more cumbersome form. The cylindrical case is treated numerically. The construction provides an explicit microscopic realization of structure-cancelling wall fields, related to flat-profile conditions that occur under special matching conditions in interfacial theories of wetting and drying. The theory also yields a compact collection of formulae for weighted densities and one-body direct correlation functions in the three fundamental geometries, providing useful reference expressions for density-functional implementations. The resulting analytic wall potentials are validated in independent density functional calculations, which confirm that the prescribed flat profiles are recovered within numerical accuracy.

Abstract

We introduce the notion of an Ising emulator for two-dimensional Ising models: flat, unit-edge-length lattices can be represented as site- or bond-diluted supercells of a single host lattice, for which the Feynman--Vdovichenko/Kac--Ward solution is fixed once and for all. We construct explicit square and triangular emulators and show that a single transition matrix, supplemented by lattice-specific binary masks, gives all the thermodynamic quantities of interest for both ferro- and antiferromagnetic couplings. We apply the framework to all eleven Archimedean lattices, to all twenty $2$-uniform lattices -- whose thermodynamics is obtained here for the first time -- and to several pentagonal lattices, and show that the same construction extends directly to fractal and disordered Ising models, with no modification to the underlying machinery.

Abstract

Quantum low-density parity-check (LDPC) codes are a promising route to fault-tolerant quantum computation, but their use requires efficient preparation of encoded states. Standard encoder constructions generate circuits through fixed algebraic procedures, yet the resulting circuit can contain substantial redundancy. We formulate LDPC encoder preparation as a circuit-resynthesis problem: given the linear-reversible matrix implemented by the encoder's CNOT block, we seek a lower-cost equivalent circuit that can be routed efficiently on the target hardware and which mitigates noise. We propose a novel optimization approach referred as two-sided Hamming descent and a noise-aware optimization pipeline for this task. Across several families of Calderbank-Shor-Steane (CSS) LDPC encoders, including Bivariate Bicycle, hypergraph-product, and entanglement-assisted codes, the proposed pipeline produces substantially smaller and shallower encoder circuits than the standard constructions and the synthesis baselines considered, cutting gate counts by 53.8% in aggregate across the benchmark and by up to 68% on the Bivariate Bicycle family. The gains remain visible after routing, where the two-qubit depth is reduced by up to 71% and translate into higher-fidelity state preparation under circuit-level noise. On the Bivariate Bicycle family, live-range scheduling further reduces routed preparation failure by up to 13.7% without adding two-qubit gates to the selected circuit. These results indicate that encoder-matrix resynthesis, combined with hardware-calibrated selection and scheduling, is an effective compiler-level tool for preparing quantum LDPC code states.

Abstract

Expressive parameterized quantum circuits (PQCs) are often designed under a dilemma: the growth of expressibility and entangling power (EP) that improves Hilbert-space coverage is also expected to randomize an ansatz and activate barren-plateau (BP) conditions. Abstract

Large language models can generate plausible quantum code, but it is unclear whether they can reliably target the specific software development kit (SDK) version requested by the user. We study this problem as API drift and introduce quantum-api-drift, a benchmark for measuring version fidelity, defined here as execution success on the requested SDK version, cross-version compatibility, failure modes, and documentation-guided repair in LLM-generated quantum SDK code. We instantiate the benchmark with Qiskit, a representative quantum SDK that underwent substantial interface changes across v0.43, v1.3, and v2.0. We evaluate 17 models on 50 tasks with 3 samples per prompt, yielding 450 generated samples and 1,350 executions per model. Sixteen models are tested in a matched REST API setting with a 1024-token output cap, while GPT-5.4 (Codex CLI) is reported separately as a reference configuration. Across the 16 matched REST models, diagonal Pass@1 ranges from 0.02 to 0.85. Claude Opus 4.7 is strongest on v0.43 and v2.0, while Grok 4.20 is strongest on v1.3 at 0.513. Error profiles differ systematically by model strength: weaker models fail mainly with broken imports, while stronger models more often reach deprecation-level failures. Documentation-guided repair succeeds for 0.19 to 0.59 of repair attempts overall and is consistently much more effective for migration to v2.0 than to v1.3. The benchmark artifacts are publicly available at https://github.com/arasyi/quantum-api-drift. These results show that version alignment is a distinct evaluation axis for quantum code generation and that API drift remains only partly recoverable even with migration guidance.

Abstract

Matrix Brownian motion provides a powerful framework for studying crossover ensembles in quantum chaos and quantum transport, as well as thermalization and information scrambling in many-body dynamics. Here, we develop a unified diagrammatic framework to characterize Brownian ensembles for orthogonal and symplectic random matrices, which describe systems with particle-hole symmetry. We compute polynomial averages up to fourth order and construct an orthogonally invariant interpolation for the disconnected $\mathrm{SO}^-(q)$ sector of the orthogonal group. We consider applications relating to the fields of quantum information, quantum chaos, and quantum transport.
