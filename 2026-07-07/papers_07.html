Canonical quantization of neurons

Authors: Alexander He, Nana Liu, Mark M. Wilde
Type: theory · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.05000v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work establishes canonical quantization as a systematic method to build quantum machine learning components by quantizing classical neurons. By mapping the energy function to a Hamiltonian observable, they develop hybrid algorithms to train these 'quantized neurons.' The results show that this quantum approach enhances the expressive power of the resulting neural architectures.

Why it may be interesting. It bridges fundamental quantum mechanical concepts (quantization, observables) directly into the structure of artificial neural networks, providing a physical interpretation and potential computational advantage for ML models.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper applies canonical quantization, a physical procedure, to construct quantum models for machine learning primitives like neurons.

Main result. Quantized neurons exhibit enhanced expressive capabilities compared to their classical counterparts on representative function approximation tasks.

Method. Hybrid quantum-classical algorithms are developed for training and evaluation, relying on techniques like Hamiltonian simulation and Hadamard tests to estimate gradients and measure observables.

Model / system. The model quantizes a classical neuron defined by an energy/Hamiltonian $H( heta)$ and an activation function $\varphi$. The input variables are promoted to non-commuting Pauli operators ($\sigma^Z$).

Key observables. The primary observable is the quantum activation observable, $\varphi(H( heta))$, whose expectation value is measured on the input state $ ho$.

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature parameter $T$ (for the hyperbolic tangent activation function), and the Hamiltonian parameters $ heta$ defining the interaction strength.

Assumptions / limitations. The approach assumes that canonical quantization provides a principled framework for building quantum ML primitives, and it relies on basic quantum simulation techniques being available.

Figures summary. Figure 3 compares the training performance (Squared Loss) of noncommuting Hamiltonians ($H_{ ext{TFIM}}$) versus commuting ones ($H_{ ext{IM}}$), showing superior performance for the former.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces quantizing a classical neuron model by replacing the energy function with a Hamiltonian and applying functional calculus to get an observable. It then develops HQC algorithms for training, followed by numerical experiments comparing different types of Hamiltonians on learning tasks.

Abstract

Canonical quantization provides a systematic procedure for constructing quantum models from classical Hamiltonians. Here, we apply this principle to a fundamental computational primitive of machine learning: the neuron. Specifically, by viewing a neuron as a composition of an energy function and an activation function, we quantize this model by replacing the energy function with a quantum Hamiltonian and applying the activation function to it through matrix functional calculus. This results in an activation observable that can be measured on an input quantum state. We investigate the use of these quantized neurons for function approximation, where the objective is to learn an unknown observable from labeled quantum data. For this purpose, we develop hybrid quantum-classical algorithms for training and evaluation, including procedures for measuring the activation observable and estimating gradients of the squared loss error. Our algorithms for gradient estimation rely on basic primitives like classical random sampling, the Hadamard test, and Hamiltonian simulation, and those for measuring an activation observable rely on quantum algorithms known as the power of one qumode and Schroedingerization. Numerical experiments demonstrate that our quantized neurons exhibit enhanced expressive capabilities relative to corresponding classical neurons on representative learning tasks. Our work establishes canonical quantization as a principled framework for constructing quantum machine learning primitives and provides a foundation for developing neural architectures tailored to quantum data.

Summary. This paper constructs the partition function for a Type-IIB axion--dilaton wormhole using charge-sector data. It develops a sophisticated mathematical framework to analyze this function via its $ heta$-expansion coefficients. The core contribution is establishing necessary and sufficient conditions—such as positivity or symmetry—that these coefficients must satisfy to correspond to physically meaningful quantities.

Why it may be interesting. While highly theoretical, the rigorous development of coefficient constraints (like moment positivity or Cauchy-Schwarz inequalities) provides powerful mathematical tools for characterizing physical theories where observables are represented by Fourier series expansions, which has parallels in quantum field theory and statistical physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is constructing the Type-IIB axion--dilaton wormhole partition function, Z_wh(theta; b), entirely from underlying charge-sector data.

Main result. The paper establishes a rigorous mathematical framework detailing how physical constraints (like positivity or symmetry) on the coefficient sequence dictate the nature of the theta expansion, connecting it to known statistical distributions like Bessel/Skellam laws.

Method. The method involves deriving coefficients from long-distance saddle point calculations in supergravity, followed by a reduction procedure that maps these matrix elements into scalar coefficients forming a Fourier series representation for Z_wh.

Model / system. The system is the Type-IIB axion--dilaton wormhole geometry within string theory. The mathematical structure involves an integer charge lattice ($ u \in \mathbb{Z}$) and a compact Fourier variable $ heta$ conjugate to this charge.

Key observables. Wormhole partition function $Z_{wh}( heta; b)$, reduced scalar coefficients $W_{ u}[b]$, and the absolute convergence bound $Z_{abs}[b]$.

Important parameters / regimes. The axion charge $ u$, the compact Fourier variable $ heta$, and the reduction data $b$. The analysis explores limits like the dilute Bessel/Skellam limit.

Assumptions / limitations. The calculation relies on a controlled semiclassical input derived from supergravity functional integrals, and the final physical interpretation depends on imposing positivity or symmetry constraints on the coefficients.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the geometric objects involved (two-ended throat, neck-cut geometry) alongside the two-end operator term coefficient matrix $C_{ij}^{ u}$. Figure 2 maps the calculation flow from $C_{ij}^{ u}$ to $W_{ u}[b]$ and finally to $Z_{wh}( heta; b)$.

Paper structure. The paper follows a hierarchical structure: first, defining the charge-sector coefficient matrix ($C_{ij}^ u$); second, applying reduction data ($b$) to obtain scalar coefficients ($W_ u[b]$); third, forming the partition function via Fourier summation ($\sum W_{ u}[b]e^{i u heta}$); and finally, analyzing mathematical constraints (positivity, symmetry) on these coefficients.

Abstract

I construct the Type-IIB axion--dilaton wormhole partition function from charge-sector data. In a chosen axion charge, equivalently form-field flux sector, the long-distance saddle calculation supplies a two-end operator term with coefficient matrix \(C^{ij}_ν\). The labels \(i,j\) label end-insertion operators; the labels \(A,B\) label parent universes. Reduction data \(b\) convert this matrix into scalar coefficients \(W_ν[b]\). The wormhole partition function in the theta variable is \(Z_{\rm wh}(θ;b)=\sum_νW_ν[b]\e^{iνθ}\). I analyze properties and constraints this coefficients satisfy: discrete-symmetry covariance, phase, absolute bounds, moment positivity, Cauchy--Schwarz inequalities for the unreduced coefficient matrix, complex-\(θ\) domains, charge-lattice tails, and the dilute Bessel/Skellam limit. The \(θ\)-dependence of the wormhole partition function is the Fourier transform of the charge-sector scalar coefficients.

Data-Driven Prediction of NaCl-Type Entropy-Stabilized Oxide Compositions from First-Principles and Supervised Learning

Authors: Sebastien Junier, Celine Barreteau, David Berardan, Yann-Andrev Kerneur, Jean-Claude Crivello

Summary. This work presents a data-driven computational framework to explore Entropy-Stabilized Oxides by combining DFT calculations, SQS modeling of disorder, and supervised machine learning. The method efficiently screens thousands of potential oxide compositions to predict their thermodynamic stability relative to competing phases. This establishes an efficient route for the materials discovery process in complex solid-state chemistry.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on materials science, the core methodology—combining high-dimensional physical simulation (DFT/SQS) with advanced machine learning for predictive screening in complex parameter spaces—is highly relevant to developing ML potentials or predicting quantum phases in strongly correlated systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The challenge of identifying stable, promising candidates within the vast compositional space of Entropy-Stabilized Oxides (ESOs) due to complex phase stability assessment.

Main result. A high-throughput computational framework was developed that efficiently screens thousands of quinary ESO compositions using machine learning predictions, requiring explicit DFT calculations for only about 10% of the systems tested.

Method. The approach combines Density Functional Theory (DFT) with Special Quasirandom Structures (SQS) to model disorder, and then trains supervised machine learning models (like MLP) on these results to predict thermodynamic stability metrics.

Model / system. The study focuses on equimolar quinary ESOs in the NaCl structure type, involving 16 selected cation species. Stability is assessed by calculating the formation enthalpy relative to the convex hull ($\Delta ext{hull}H$) and determining a finite-temperature stabilization point ($T_{ ext{stab}}$).

Key observables. Formation enthalpy ($\Delta fH$), distance to the convex hull ($\Delta ext{hull}H$), and stabilization temperature ($T_{ ext{stab}}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The system size involves 4368 possible quinary compositions; ML models achieve a test error of $\sim 4 ext{ kJ}\cdot ext{mol}^{-1}$.

Assumptions / limitations. Absolute stabilization temperatures are affected by systematic thermodynamic approximations, and the method relies on extrapolations from smaller systems.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate SQS modeling (pair cluster distribution), ML model performance curves (prediction vs. learning), and tables list predicted stability trends for various quinary compositions.

Paper structure. The paper systematically builds a reference database using DFT/SQS, develops thermodynamic criteria ($\Delta fH$, $\Delta ext{hull}G$), trains supervised ML models on this data, and applies the resulting framework to screen large compositional spaces.

Abstract

Entropy-stabilized oxides (ESOs) open access to vast multicomponent compositional spaces, but identifying promising candidates remains challenging because of the large number of possible mixtures and the need to assess their stability against competing phases. In this work, we develop a high-throughput computational framework to screen equimolar quinary ESOs in the NaCl structure type by combining density functional theory (DFT), special quasirandom structures (SQS), convex-hull thermodynamics, and supervised machine learning. A consistent reference database of binary and ternary ordered oxides, including disordered phases such as all binary cation combinations in the NaCl-type oxide, is first constructed using GGA and meta-GGA calculations. Quinary disordered phases are then described by SQS supercells and used to train machine-learning models that predict the distance to the convex hull and the corresponding stabilization temperature over the full set of 4368 possible equimolar quinary compositions generated from 16 cation species. Among the tested models, an optimized multilayer perceptron provides the best predictive performance, with a test error of about 4 kJ/mol, while requiring explicit DFT calculations for only about 10% of the quinary systems. Comparison with experimental synthesis tests and computed decomposition paths further shows that the approach captures the main stability trends and the dominant competing phases, although absolute stabilization temperatures remain affected by systematic thermodynamic approximations. These results establish an efficient route for the data-driven exploration of multicomponent oxides and provide practical guidance for the experimental search for new ESOs.

Summary. This paper calculates the binding energies of tin isotopes near the neutron drip line using advanced ab initio methods based on chiral Effective Field Theory. By employing the BCCSD[T] approximation, the authors achieve high precision and map out trends in nuclear structure, highlighting discrepancies between theoretical models and experimental constraints.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on nuclear physics, the rigorous application of many-body perturbation theory techniques like Coupled Cluster methods for calculating ground state properties is a highly sophisticated theoretical tool relevant to strongly correlated quantum systems in general.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To perform high-precision calculations of nuclear binding energies for tin isotopes across the neutron drip line.

Main result. The BCCSD[T] approach significantly reduces many-body uncertainty, showing sensitivity in predicting the drip-line location compared to Energy Density Functional models.

Method. Bogoliubov Coupled-Cluster (BCC) theory, extended to include leading triples corrections (BCCSD[T]), is used to solve the many-body Schrödinger equation.

Model / system. The study focuses on even-even Tin isotopes ($ ext{Sn}$) ranging from $N=50$ towards the neutron dripline. Calculations utilize chiral Effective Field Theory ($\chi ext{EFT}$) two- and three-nucleon interactions within the BCC framework.

Key observables. Binding energies ($E_0$) and two-neutron separation energies ($S_{2n}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The calculation uses a one-body spherical harmonic oscillator basis ($\hbar\omega = 12 ext{ MeV}$) and incorporates interactions up to the three-nucleon level.

Assumptions / limitations. Approximations are made in solving the cluster amplitudes, notably using the non-iterative BCCSD[T] method, which introduces systematic uncertainties.

Figures summary. Figure 1 compares ground-state energies ($E_0$) for $ ext{Sn}$ isotopes using BCCSD and BCCSD[T] against VS-IMSRG results. Figure 2 shows $S_{2n}$ around the $N=50$ shell closure.

Paper structure. The paper details the theoretical framework (BCC theory, Hamiltonian formulation), presents the systematic improvement achieved by including triples corrections (BCCSD[T]), and compares resulting binding energies and separation energies across the isotopic chain to experimental data and other advanced calculations.

Abstract

The location of the neutron drip line in tin isotopes has important consequences for our fundamental understanding of nuclear structure and nuclear forces as well as for astrophysical nucleosynthesis. Performing high-precision ab initio calculations of even-even tin isotopes from $N=50$ to $N=126$ based on chiral two- and three-nucleon interactions, the predicted drip-line location is found to be highly sensitive to the employed nuclear interactions and to exhibit tension with recent energy-density-functional predictions. On the neutron-deficient side, results are consistent with extrapolated two-neutron separation energies constrained by recent Penning-trap mass measurements.

Holographic heat engines for Schwarzschild black holes

Authors: Ripunjay Dwivedi, Manus R. Visser

Summary. This work develops a model for reversible heat engines powered by Schwarzschild black holes using quasi-local gravitational thermodynamics. By treating the boundary as a thermal system, the authors calculate efficiencies for various cycles, demonstrating that the regenerated Stirling efficiency approaches the Carnot limit from below at high temperatures. This provides a novel diagnostic tool linking general relativity to classical thermodynamic limits.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on gravity, the rigorous application of classical thermodynamics principles (Carnot/Stirling cycles) to a non-standard working substance provides an advanced theoretical framework for understanding energy conversion in extreme physical systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper constructs and analyzes reversible heat engines operating using a holographic model derived from Schwarzschild black holes.

Main result. It derives efficiencies for standard heat engines (Carnot, Otto, etc.) showing that the regenerated Stirling efficiency approaches the Carnot bound from below in the high-temperature limit.

Method. The analysis uses quasi-local gravitational thermodynamics applied to a black hole enclosed in a cavity, treating the boundary as the working substance.

Model / system. The system models a Schwarzschild black hole contained within a finite spherical cavity. The thermodynamic properties of the dual thermal system on the cavity boundary are used to define the heat engine cycles.

Key observables. Efficiencies ($\eta$) for Carnot, Otto, Diesel, Brayton, and Stirling engines; quasi-local equations of state ($E(S, V)$); regenerated Stirling efficiency ($\eta_{ ext{reg}}^{ ext{Stirling}}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature ratio ($T_c/T_h$); large black hole branch restriction ($0 < x < 1$); high-temperature limit ($T_h o \infty$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is restricted to the stable, large black hole branch; heat rejection is modeled as controlled quasi-static extraction via Hawking radiation.

Paper structure. The paper systematically derives thermodynamic relations for various strokes (isochoric, adiabatic, isobaric, isothermal) before applying these concepts to calculate the efficiencies of standard idealized heat engines and analyzing the high-temperature limits.

Abstract

We construct reversible black hole heat engines in asymptotically flat spacetime using quasi-local gravitational thermodynamics. We enclose a Schwarzschild black hole in a finite spherical cavity and identify the working substance with the dual thermal system on the cavity boundary. The surface pressure and boundary area define a thermodynamic pressure-volume pair in the dual system, while all coupling constants of the gravitational theory remain fixed. We derive exact efficiencies for the Carnot, Otto, Diesel, Brayton, and Stirling engines and compare them numerically. These efficiencies probe the quasi-local equations of state of the black hole. The regenerated Stirling efficiency remains remarkably close to the Carnot bound and approaches it in the high-temperature limit.

QuTuner: Feature- and Learning-Guided Optimization Pass Tuning for Quantum Compilers

Authors: Ming Zhong, Xiangyu Ren, Jinglei Cheng, Shaohua Li, Zhiding Liang
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.04586v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: methods for driven-dissipative 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. QuTuner is a novel framework designed to automatically and efficiently tune the sequence of passes used by quantum compilers. It overcomes limitations of previous methods by incorporating 'optimization-aware pass embeddings' that capture how circuits react to specific compiler steps. The approach significantly boosts optimization performance while drastically cutting down the computational time needed for tuning.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on software compilation, the core concept of using learned representations (embeddings) derived from system responses to guide complex sequential decisions is highly relevant for modeling optimization paths in open quantum systems or resource allocation problems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Quantum compilers require effective pass tuning to minimize resource costs (gates, depth) while maintaining high execution fidelity.

Main result. QuTuner significantly improves evaluation-metric reduction (up to 84.85% on Qiskit) and drastically reduces the time required for tuning compared to existing state-of-the-art methods.

Method. The framework builds a large dataset by characterizing circuits using both static features and novel optimization-aware pass embeddings, which are then used to train offline models for candidate retrieval and ranking of optimal pass sequences.

Model / system. The system is quantum computation implemented via quantum circuits (OpenQASM code) that undergo compilation through various passes (layout, routing, optimization).

Key observables. Optimization metrics include 2Q gate count reduction ($\Delta 2Q$), 1Q gate count reduction ($\Delta 1Q$), and circuit depth reduction ($\Delta ext{Depth}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The tuning objective is a weighted combination of these metric reductions, prioritizing hardware-agnostic, circuit-level metrics.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary limitation addressed is that prior methods only search small optimization subspaces and rely on static features that fail to capture how circuits react dynamically to specific compiler passes.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the quantum compilation stages (Qiskit example), the overall two-stage QuTuner workflow (offline dataset construction and online prediction/refinement), and detailed feature representations for a sample circuit.

Paper structure. The paper first identifies limitations in current pass tuning approaches. It then introduces QuTuner, detailing its methodology: building an optimization dataset, characterizing circuits via static features and pass embeddings, training retrieval/ranking models offline, and finally applying these to unseen circuits with lightweight refinement online.

Abstract

Quantum compilers play a key role in transforming quantum circuits into lower-cost implementations with improved execution fidelity. This process is commonly guided by circuit-level metrics, such as gate counts and circuit depth. Although compiler pass tuning has been widely studied in classical compilation, directly transferring these techniques to quantum compilers is challenging, because quantum programs are expressed as circuits and exhibit optimization behaviors that are shaped by quantum-specific structures. Prior quantum compiler tuning approaches have begun to use circuit features to guide pass selection, but they remain limited in two aspects: they search only a small portion of the optimization-pass space, and they mainly rely on static features that do not explicitly reflect how a circuit reacts to compiler optimizations. We present QuTuner, a feature-guided quantum compiler pass tuning framework that generalizes across compilers and tuning objectives. QuTuner first builds a large optimization dataset. It then characterizes each circuit from two complementary views: static circuit features that describe circuit structure, and optimization-aware pass embeddings that summarize the circuit's responses to individual optimization passes. Using these representations, QuTuner trains two offline models to retrieve and rank candidate pass sequences for unseen circuits, followed by lightweight refinement. We evaluate QuTuner on Qiskit and PyTKET using two benchmark suites. On Qiskit, QuTuner improves the evaluation-metric reduction by up to 84.85% over the strongest baseline while reducing tuning time by 73.59%. On PyTKET, it improves metric reduction by up to 18.68% with a 64.49% reduction in tuning time. These results show that QuTuner provides an effective approach to adaptive pass tuning for quantum compilers.

Summary. This work uses muon spin rotation ($\mu$SR) to study superconductivity in $ ext{Cu}_{1-x} ext{Zn}_x ext{Ir}_2 ext{S}_4$, a material near a metal-insulator transition. The results confirm an s-wave superconducting state, arguing that high electronic disorder forces the pairing symmetry into an isotropic form. This suggests that structural scattering and proximity to the Mott-Ioffe-Regel limit are key tuners of superconductivity in this correlated system.

Why it may be interesting. The paper provides experimental evidence of how strong scattering (disorder) can stabilize an isotropic s-wave superconducting state even when underlying physics suggests complex, anisotropic pairing mechanisms expected in correlated materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Investigating superconductivity in Zn-substituted thiospinel $ ext{Cu}_{1-x} ext{Zn}_x ext{Ir}_2 ext{S}_4$ near a metal-insulator transition to understand the pairing mechanism.

Main result. The superconducting state is confirmed to be an isotropic, fully gapped s-wave symmetry, which is robustly maintained deep within the 'dirty limit' due to strong electron scattering.

Method. Transverse-field muon spin rotation ($\mu$SR) measurements were used to probe the temperature and field dependence of the superfluid density.

Model / system. The system is the thiospinel $ ext{CuIr}_2 ext{S}_4$, whose superconducting properties are tuned by Zn substitution ($x$), driving it away from a high-temperature metal-insulator transition. The analysis compares results to models incorporating disorder and pressure effects.

Key observables. Superfluid density ($\sigma_s$) derived from $\mu$SR linewidth, normal state resistivity ($ ho$), magnetic susceptibility, and the superconducting critical temperature ($T_c$).

Important parameters / regimes. The system operates in the 'dirty limit' ($\ell \ll \xi_0$) and near the Mott-Ioffe-Regel (MIR) limit due to structural disorder/frustration.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes a Gaussian approximation for field distributions in $\mu$SR fitting, and that high scattering averages out potential gap anisotropy.

Figures summary. Figures show the temperature dependence of the $\mu$SR linewidth ($\sigma$) below $T_c$, confirming superconducting transitions, alongside magnetic susceptibility data showing order parameter development.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the background on the MI transition and Zn-induced superconductivity. Finally, it proposes a unified framework linking electronic disorder, proximity to the MIR limit, and high pressure effects.

Abstract

The thiospinel CuIr$_2$S$_4$ undergoes a metal-insulator transition below $\approx$230 K, which is suppressed by substitution of Cu with Zn (Cu$_{1-x}$Zn$_x$Ir$_2$S$_4$) to induce superconductivity for $0.2\lesssim x\lesssim0.8$. We show that the temperature/field dependence of superfluid density in samples with $x = 0.3$ and 0.4 ($T_{\rm c} \approx 3$ K and 2.5 K) investigated by muon spin rotation and relaxation ($μ$SR) is consistent with a fully gapped $s$-wave pairing. Meanwhile, the relatively high resistivity (10$^{-3}$-10$^{-2}$ $Ω\:$cm) in their normal state suggests that the superconductivity is in the "dirty limit" where the mean free path is much shorter than the coherence length ($\ell \ll ξ_0$). This indicates that the potential anisotropy associated with unconventional pairing mechanisms expected under the strong electron correlations is smeared out by the electron scattering. Based on these observations, we discuss potential link between the Zn substitution-induced superconductivity and that recently discovered in CuIr$_2$S$_4$ under high pressure ($>18$ GPa) where the existence of strong electron scattering is also suggested.

Abstract

The results provide a relativistic quantum framework for controlling accelerated vortex particle beams using electrostatic fields.

Topological effects in the polarization of the Fulling-Rindler vacuum

Authors: A. A. Saharian, G. V. Mirzoyan, V. S. Torosyan
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.04483v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5

Summary. This paper analyzes the vacuum structure of a charged scalar field confined to Rindler spacetime with compactified dimensions. It calculates how topological effects, quantified by enclosed magnetic fluxes, modify the vacuum expectation values of observables like $\langle T_{\mu u} angle$. The results highlight distinct decay behaviors—exponential versus power-law—depending on whether the field is massless and if the flux conditions are trivial.

Why it may be interesting. The study connects concepts from quantum field theory in curved spacetime (Rindler/Fulling-Rindler) with topology via magnetic fluxes, yielding non-trivial vacuum energy corrections that decay differently based on mass and flux conditions.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how compactifying spatial dimensions within Rindler spacetime affects the vacuum expectation values (VEVs) of field observables for a charged scalar field.

Main result. The VEVs exhibit topological contributions dependent on enclosed magnetic fluxes, and the difference between Fulling-Rindler and Minkowski vacua is generally exponentially small unless the field is massless with zero phases, leading to power-law decay.

Method. Calculations rely on evaluating vacuum expectation values using Hadamard functions and mode summation techniques, comparing results across different topological limits (Minkowski vs. Rindler).

Model / system. The system models a charged scalar field in a $(D+1)$-dimensional spacetime that is locally Rindler, incorporating compact dimensions with quasi-periodicity conditions related to magnetic fluxes.

Key observables. Vacuum expectation values of the field squared ($\langle \phi^2 angle$) and components of the energy-momentum tensor ($\langle T_{\mu u} angle$).

Important parameters / regimes. Proper acceleration, compactification lengths ($L_l$), field mass ($m$), and enclosed magnetic fluxes ($\Phi_l$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes a locally Rindler geometry and utilizes approximations in various asymptotic limits (e.g., near the horizon or for small accelerations).

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the VEV of the field squared for massless fields showing dependence on acceleration/compactification parameters, comparing results to trivial topology cases.

Paper structure. The paper analyzes the problem by first setting up the field equation and periodicity conditions; it then calculates $\langle \phi^2 angle$ and $\langle T_{\mu u} angle$, deriving explicit forms for the topological contributions in different limits, and finally applying these results to physical scenarios like black hole horizons.

Abstract

We investigate how the compactification of some spatial dimensions in Rindler spacetime affects the vacuum expectation values (VEVs) of the field squared and the energy-momentum tensor for a charged scalar field prepared in the Fulling-Rindler vacuum state. A toral compactification is considered with quasi-periodicity conditions on the field operator along the compact dimensions. The phases of these conditions are interpreted in terms of the magnetic flux enclosed by the compact dimensions. For a general number of spatial dimensions, the components of the VEVs are explicitly separated, corresponding to the expectation values in the Minkowski vacuum. As a limiting case, the VEVs are retrieved in Rindler spacetime with trivial topology. We demonstrate that, for non-zero phases in the periodicity conditions, the vacuum energy-momentum tensor exhibits off-diagonal components with indices in the compact subspace. Near the Rindler horizon, the leading terms in the asymptotic expansions of the field squared and the diagonal components of the energy-momentum tensor coincide with the corresponding VEVs in Rindler spacetime with trivial topology. The off-diagonal components of the energy-momentum tensor vanish on the Rindler horizon. For small accelerations, the difference between the VEVs in the Fulling-Rindler and Minkowski vacua is exponentially small. The exception to this is a massless field with zero phases in the periodicity conditions. In this special case, the difference decays according to a power law as a function of acceleration. As an application, we consider the VEVs near the horizon of cylindrical black holes and topological black holes with toroidal horizons.

Abstract

We study the ratio between the trace speed and the Hilbert-Schmidt speed for differentiable finite-dimensional quantum states, $\mathcal R(φ)=\|\partial_φρ(φ)\|_1/\|\partial_φρ(φ)\|_2$. Because $\partial_φρ(φ)$ is always Hermitian and traceless, this ratio is constrained more strongly than for a generic operator. For any nonzero tangent operator $X=\partial_φρ$ of rank $r$, we prove the sharp bounds $\sqrt{2}\le \|X\|_1/\|X\|_2\le \sqrt r$. The lower bound is attained exactly for rank-two tangents, while the upper bound is attained exactly when all nonzero singular values are equal, which in the traceless Hermitian setting requires even rank. At every nonstationary point of a pure-state family, the tangent has rank two, implying $\mathcal R=\sqrt2$. For odd Hilbert-space dimension $d$, we further prove the sharp global maximum $\mathcal R\le \sqrt{d-1/d}$, with equality characterized by full-rank spectra whose positive and negative eigenvalues are separately degenerate and have multiplicities differing by one. We identify $\mathcal R^2$ as the inverse participation ratio of the singular-value distribution of the tangent operator, giving $\mathcal R$ a natural interpretation as an effective-rank diagnostic for local quantum dynamics. Furthermore, we decompose the effective rank into classical (eigenvalue) and quantum (eigenvector) contributions and prove the bound $r_{\mathrm{eff}}\le r_C + r_Q$, with equality guaranteed when either component vanishes. We establish a direct inequality linking the effective rank to the quantum Fisher information (QFI), which forces a large number of active singular modes when the QFI is small relative to the squared trace speed. Finally, we derive a hierarchy of quantum speed limits in which the effective rank controls the tightness of bounds expressed through the Hilbert-Schmidt speed.

Abstract

We establish a strict logical separation between two distinct phenomena in orthogonality hypergraphs: chromatic completeness, the possibility of assigning a single globally consistent nondegenerate spectrum to all contexts, and geometric coordinatizability, the existence of a faithful orthogonal representation by rays. Abstract

Ising-Machine-Assisted Large Neighborhood Search with Flexibly Tunable Subproblem Size

Authors: Koshiro Fujimoto, Masashi Yamashita, Shu Tanaka
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.05169v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper addresses limitations in using Ising machines for large combinatorial problems by proposing $ ext{LNS-VT}$, a method that flexibly controls the size of subproblems solved during Large Neighborhood Search. By tuning the number of variables, the authors achieve significant performance gains over existing methods on VRP and QMKP benchmarks. The work emphasizes that controlling problem structure is as vital as ensuring mathematical feasibility.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical optimization, the reliance on Ising machines (which map to quantum annealing/computation) makes this highly relevant for understanding how heuristic solvers interact with quantum hardware constraints in complex physical modeling.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Improving solution quality for large-scale, constrained combinatorial optimization problems like VRP and QMKP.

Main result. A novel LNS approach ($ ext{LNS-VT}$) that flexibly controls the subproblem size significantly outperforms existing methods by up to 10% on VRP benchmarks.

Method. The authors propose an Ising-machine-assisted Large Neighborhood Search (LNS) framework, specifically $ ext{LNS-VT}$, which adaptively tunes the number of variables in the subproblem solved at each step while maintaining feasibility.

Model / system. The problems are modeled using QUBO energy functions derived from combinatorial optimization objectives (VRP and QMKP). The core mechanism involves iteratively solving smaller, constrained subproblems using an Ising machine solver.

Key observables. Solution objective value improvement (e.g., 10% reduction in VRP), total profit for QMKP, and the number of binary variables used in the subproblem.

Important parameters / regimes. The 'number of consecutive steps re-optimized per vehicle' acts as a crucial control parameter determining the subproblem size; optimal settings vary with solution quality.

Assumptions / limitations. Feasibility preservation is necessary but insufficient; controlling the variable count in the subproblem is critical for performance on Ising machines.

Figures summary. Figure 8 shows that for QMKP, the highest total profit is achieved when the number of binary variables in the subproblem is maintained within a specific range (100 to 150).

Paper structure. The paper first defines the VRP and its QUBO formulation. It then introduces LNS-V and proposes the improved, tunable $ ext{LNS-VT}$ framework. Results are presented for both VRP and QMKP, demonstrating the necessity of subproblem size control.

Abstract

Ising machines are heuristic solvers for combinatorial optimization, but their solution quality can degrade when large-scale constrained problems are solved directly. Ising-machine-assisted large neighborhood search (LNS) instead repeatedly updates a feasible current solution by solving smaller subproblems. An existing feasibility-preserving method for the vehicle routing problem (VRP) re-optimizes the entire routes of selected vehicles and thus cannot adjust the subproblem size finely. We propose LNS-VT, which introduces the number of consecutive steps re-optimized per vehicle as a parameter to control the subproblem size finely while preserving feasibility. For a 300-site, 5-vehicle VRP, its best setting reduced the objective value by approximately 10\% relative to the existing method after 100 iterations, and the appropriate setting changed with the current solution quality. Applying the same principle to the quadratic multiple knapsack problem, we confirmed that an appropriate subproblem size also exists, indicating that subproblem-size control is important in Ising-machine-assisted LNS.

Logical Spectroscopy: Lifted-Product Codes with Addressable Bases

Authors: Jong Yeon Lee
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.05386v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. The paper introduces 'logical spectroscopy' as a novel technique to systematically construct and label usable conjugate logical bases for complex lifted-product qLDPC codes. By leveraging the Chinese Remainder Theorem, it replaces failing global algebraic reductions with independent finite-field computations across spectral packets. This provides crucial structural diagnostics necessary for building reliable quantum memory systems.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful algebraic framework for analyzing structured quantum codes, offering systematic diagnostics that go beyond simple dimension counting to certify the usability of logical qubits in fault-tolerant architectures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is developing a systematic method to construct and label explicit, addressable conjugate logical operators (X/Z pairs) for high-rate Abelian lifted-product quantum low-density parity-check (qLDPC) codes.

Main result. The authors introduce 'logical spectroscopy,' a spectral construction that replaces problematic global row reduction with finite-field computations via the Chinese Remainder Theorem, yielding a complete addressable conjugate logical basis.

Method. The method decomposes the group algebra using the CRT into spectral packets. Linear algebra is then performed independently within each packet to compute kernels and quotients, which are subsequently lifted back to define structured logical operators.

Model / system. The work focuses on quantum error-correcting codes, specifically quasi-cyclic qLDPC codes based on lifted-product (LP) constructions over group algebras $R = \mathbb{F}_2[x]/(x^l+1)$.

Key observables. Complete addressable conjugate logical basis; packet ranks showing sector splitting; certified upper bounds on basis width; and packet-attributed dimensions for erasure failures.

Important parameters / regimes. The construction is most robust when the lift group order is odd, allowing clean CRT decomposition into finite fields.

Assumptions / limitations. The construction relies on assumptions of bounded seed-shape and bounded group-basis-support. For the spectral method to apply cleanly, the lift group order must be odd.

Figures summary. Figures/Tables detail various diagnostic results, including sparse parity-systematic probes (S matrices), pivot optimization certificates ($ ext{W cert}^{ ext{full}}$), and exact fractions of one- and two-left-orbit erasure patterns ($ ho_{1 ext{orb}}, ho_{2 ext{orb}}$).

Paper structure. The paper develops the theory by first identifying the failure point (global row reduction over non-fields) and then proposing logical spectroscopy. This involves detailing the CRT decomposition, performing packet-wise linear algebra, pairing X/Z operators via reciprocal trace-duals, and finally presenting diagnostic tools derived from these packets.

Abstract

Quantum LDPC memories can encode many logical qubits, but dimension alone does not make them usable: applications need explicit conjugate logical operators with structured labels and physical representatives. For hypergraph-product (HGP) codes this structure is transparent, since the input matrices are binary and can be row-reduced over $\mathbb{F}_2$. Abelian lifted-product codes are subtler. Their seed entries are shifts, or sparse sums of shifts, in a group-algebra ring rather than a field, so pivot blocks need not be invertible and global row reduction can fail. We address this with \emph{logical spectroscopy}, a spectral construction that replaces global row reduction by finite-field computations in the Frobenius character packets of the Abelian lift group. The Chinese remainder theorem (CRT) decomposes the group algebra into these packets. In each packet, we compute kernels, quotients, and product-complex homology; we then lift the resulting representatives back with CRT idempotents and pair $X$ and $Z$ logicals through reciprocal trace-dual packets. This gives complete addressable conjugate logical bases for finite Abelian lifted products $\mathsf{LP}(A,B)$. The same packet data also gives design diagnostics. Packet ranks show how logical sectors split, the lifted representatives give certified upper bounds on the width of the constructed conjugate basis, and whole-orbit erasures decompose into packet-attributed erased-logical dimensions. Thus, CRT packets also serve as working coordinates: they label logical sectors, certify the constructed basis width, and attribute structured erasure failures. Under bounded seed-shape and group-basis-support assumptions, this construction gives Abelian lifted-product qLDPC families an HGP-like feature while preserving the layout freedom of group-algebra lifts.
