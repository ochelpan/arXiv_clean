Precoding-based protocols for entanglement assisted linear computation over a quantum many-to-one network

Authors: Ruoyu Meng, Aditya Ramamoorthy
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.13756v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: entanglement & information structure 3/5 · non-equilibrium dynamics 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops advanced protocols for performing linear computations over a quantum network where multiple senders share entanglement. By allowing senders to precode inputs, they overcome limitations found in previous models, enabling the computation of more general transformations. Crucially, the authors prove that jointly computing several such functions can reduce the total required communication cost below the sum of individual costs.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on abstract quantum communication complexity, the concepts of resource quantification (communication cost) and achieving super-additive benefits are fundamental theoretical tools applicable to optimizing complex quantum information processing tasks.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The work addresses computing a general linear combination of classical data vectors ($Y = \sum V_i W_i$) over a noiseless quantum many-to-one network.

Main result. They propose protocols that support a more general class of linear transformations than previously constrained, demonstrating subadditivity in the communication cost across multiple instances.

Method. The approach involves encoding classical inputs into local quantum subsystems and transmitting them through an entangled quantum channel to a single receiver for joint measurement and post-processing.

Model / system. The model is a noiseless quantum many-to-one network where $k$ senders share entanglement, and the computation aims to reconstruct a linear combination $\mathbf{Y}$ at Bob's location.

Key observables. Communication cost (total number of transmitted qudits) and the resulting output vector $\mathbf{Y}$.

Important parameters / regimes. Number of senders ($k$), number of instances ($L$), dimensions of input/output spaces ($\mathbb{F}^{m_i}, \mathbb{F}^m$).

Assumptions / limitations. The computation is assumed to be zero-error, and the analysis builds upon prior work concerning constraints like the Strong Self-Orthogonality (SSO) condition.

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically illustrates the three stages of the coding scheme: entanglement distribution, local unitary encoding/transmission by $Alice_i$, and final measurement/post-processing at Bob.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining the general linear combination problem to developing protocols that relax prior constraints (like SSO), demonstrating cost improvements, and finally proving subadditivity across multiple instances.

Abstract

In this work, we consider the problem of computing a linear combination over a noiseless quantum many-to-one network. There are $k$ senders, Alice$_1$, $\ldots$, Alice$_k$, and a single receiver, Bob. Each Alice$_i$ has a data vector $W_i \in \mathbb{F}^{m_i}$, where $\mathbb{F}$ is a finite field. Bob wants to compute the linear combination $Y = V_1 W_1 + V_2 W_2 + \cdots + V_k W_k \in \mathbb{F}^m$, where $V_i$ is an $m \times m_i$ matrix over $\mathbb{F}$. The senders transmit quantum states to Bob through a noiseless many-to-one quantum network, but they are not allowed to communicate with each other. The senders share entanglement among themselves, while Bob does not share this entanglement. They encode their classical information $W_i$, $i=1,\ldots,k$, into their local subsystems and transmit them to Bob so that he can recover $Y$ through a quantum measurement and subsequent post-processing. The N-Sum Box protocol proposed by Allaix et al. (2025) considers this problem under certain constraints on the linear combination and the distribution of the data vectors among the senders. We present protocols that support the computation of a more general class of linear transformations by giving the senders access to more qudits and allowing them to judiciously precode their input symbols. The communication cost of our schemes is at most that of the best-known prior results in this area and is strictly lower in certain cases. Finally, we demonstrate that the communication cost is subadditive across instances. Specifically, we identify two linear functions for which the total cost of computing them individually is strictly larger than the cost of computing them jointly.

Tensor Network decoding under inter-qubit correlated errors

Summary. This work addresses the difficulty of decoding quantum codes corrupted by correlated errors by developing an efficient multi-index tensor network approach. The authors overcome the challenges of non-factorizable probabilities and exponentially large tensors using index transformations and reparametrization techniques. They demonstrate that this new decoder achieves superior error correction thresholds compared to existing methods.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a sophisticated computational tool (tensor networks) to analyze performance limits in quantum error correction under realistic, complex noise conditions that go beyond simple independent errors.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is developing an efficient maximum likelihood decoder for quantum error correction under inter-qubit correlated noise models.

Main result. The proposed multi-index tensor network construction achieves a higher finite-size threshold than the widely used MWPM decoder in numerical simulations.

Method. The authors introduce additional binary indices and transformations to build a multi-index tensor network, followed by reparametrization and index classification to ensure efficient contraction of otherwise huge tensors.

Model / system. The analysis focuses on decoding the 2D surface code when subjected to correlated Pauli errors, specifically modeling noise combining independent single-qubit errors with nearest-neighbor correlated two-qubit interactions (ENN model).

Key observables. Finite-size threshold of the decoder; Coset probabilities $p(C(r'))$.

Important parameters / regimes. Code distance ($d$); Noise parameters ($p_1, p_2$).

Assumptions / limitations. The method relies on reparametrization and index classification to decompose high-dimensional tensors into efficiently contractible forms.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the Nearest-Neighbor Error Model (ENN) structure; Figure 6 compares the estimated finite-size threshold vs. code distance for the proposed decoder against PyMatching 2.0.

Paper structure. The paper reviews surface codes and ML decoding, introduces the general correlated error model, details the multi-index tensor network construction using transformations, presents numerical simulations, and concludes.

Abstract

The maximum likelihood decoder based on tensor networks has proven highly successful for the 2D surface code, achieving the optimal decoding success rate. However, existing tensor network decoders are typically designed for independent single-qubit error models, and their performance under inter-qubit correlated error models remains unexplored. This is due to two major challenges. The first challenge lies in constructing the tensor network for correlated errors, since the same final Pauli error can arise from many different combinations of independent and correlated errors, preventing a direct factorization of the error probability. The second challenge is that even after a tensor network is constructed, it generally contains huge-dimensional tensors and is therefore not efficiently contractible. In this work, to address the first difficulty, we introduce additional binary indices and two transformations to construct a multi-index tensor network for maximum-likelihood decoding with correlated errors. To address the second difficulty, we use reparametrization, elimination, and index classification to decompose the huge-dimensional tensors into lower-dimensional tensors. This yields an efficiently contractible tensor network for error models satisfying the tractability conditions derived in this work. We perform numerical simulations for a representative correlated error model and show that the maximum-likelihood decoder implemented with our multi-index tensor network construction achieves a higher finite-size threshold than the widely used MWPM decoder.

Three-Dimensional Non-Foliated Fractional Quantum Hall Phases with Irrational Anyons in Twisted van der Waals Multilayers

Summary. The paper proposes that large-angle twisted van der Waals multilayers offer a viable route to achieving intrinsically three-dimensional fractional quantum Hall phases. By suppressing interlayer tunneling via twist angles, the system avoids metallic instabilities common in conventional 3D stacks. The resulting generalized Halperin states host exotic quasiparticles with irrational braiding statistics, opening new avenues for topological quantum information.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete materials platform (twisted van der Waals layers) for realizing exotic topological matter predicted in strongly correlated electron systems, linking theoretical concepts of irrational anyons to solid-state physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is realizing intrinsically three-dimensional (3D) fractional quantum Hall (FQH) phases that exhibit exotic properties like irrational anyons, moving beyond simple stacks of 2D liquids.

Main result. Large-angle twisted van der Waals multilayers stabilize generalized Halperin liquid states exhibiting non-foliated 3D topological order with quasiparticles possessing irrational braiding statistics.

Method. The study employs Monte Carlo calculations to compare the energies of a large set of competing trial wavefunctions, determining the ground state by minimizing the total energy functional.

Model / system. The system is modeled using twisted van der Waals multilayers (like graphene) under a strong magnetic field. The key mechanism relies on large twist angles suppressing interlayer tunneling while preserving strong Coulomb interactions.

Key observables. Topological order structure (non-foliated), quasiparticle braiding statistics (irrational vs. rational), and the relative stability of generalized Halperin states versus metallic phases.

Important parameters / regimes. Large twist angles ($ heta \gtrsim 10^\circ$), small interlayer tunneling ($t_{\perp} \approx 1.78$ meV), and specific filling fractions like $ u_L = 1/7$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the suppression of coherent interlayer tunneling due to momentum mismatch is the critical factor stabilizing the novel liquid phases.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the transition from dispersive 3D Landau levels favoring density waves (Fig. 1) and present phase diagrams comparing different twisted graphene regimes based on magnetic field and filling fraction (Fig. 3).

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the physical problem of achieving 3D FQH states, then details how large-angle twisting solves the metallic instability issue by suppressing tunneling, followed by extensive Monte Carlo calculations comparing candidate wavefunctions to identify the stable generalized Halperin liquid.

Abstract

Three-dimensional fractional quantum Hall phases offer a route to intrinsically higher-dimensional topological order beyond simple stacks of two-dimensional quantum Hall liquids. Such phases can exhibit non-foliated, intrinsically three-dimensional entanglement structures, exponentially large topological degeneracies and quasiparticles with irrational braiding statistics. Their microscopic realization has remained elusive because Landau quantization in three dimensions generally leaves dispersive one-dimensional bands, favoring metallic and density-wave states over incompressible fractional liquids. Here we show that large-angle twisted van der Waals multilayers provide a practical route around this obstruction. Large twist angles suppress coherent interlayer tunneling through momentum mismatch, while the atomic-scale layer separation preserves strong interlayer Coulomb interactions. Using Monte Carlo calculations to compare the energies of an extensive set of 862 competing trial wavefunctions, we find that generalized Halperin states with quantum coherence extending across multiple consecutive layers are stabilized. In experimentally accessible magnetic-field regimes, these states replace the metallic spontaneous-interlayer-coherent phases that dominate conventional untwisted graphite-like multilayers. The resulting liquids realize non-foliated fractional quantum Hall order closely related to fractonic topological order, hosting quasiparticles with rational electric charges but irrational braiding statistics. Their large topological degeneracy and non-rational statistical phases may offer unconventional resources for quantum information storage and processing. Our results establish twisted van der Waals multilayers as a realistic materials platform for three-dimensional fractional Hall matter beyond conventional two-dimensional quantum Hall systems.

Summary. This work investigates the coupled thermodynamic and electronic properties of the half-Heusler material TbAuPb. By combining single-crystal measurements with advanced DFT calculations, the authors demonstrate that transport properties are highly sensitive to the material's magnetic state. The findings suggest that TbAuPb is a semimetal whose topology can be tuned by external magnetic fields.

Why it may be interesting. The strong coupling observed between charge transport anomalies (e.g., $ ext{AMR}$ symmetry changes) and magnetic phase transitions provides a rich platform for studying emergent quantum phenomena driven by magnetic order, relevant to correlated electron physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the interplay between magnetic ordering, electronic band topology, and charge transport properties in the half-Heusler plumbide TbAuPb.

Main result. TbAuPb exhibits semimetallic behavior whose magnetotransport properties are highly sensitive to its magnetic state. Theoretical calculations suggest it is a band inverted semimetal in the non-magnetic state, becoming topologically trivial upon field-induced ferromagnetism.

Method. The research combines experimental measurements (magnetization, specific heat, resistivity, magnetoresistance) with first-principles electronic structure calculations using DFT/MBJGGA+U functionals.

Model / system. The physical system is the single-crystal half-Heusler compound TbAuPb, which possesses a cubic MgAgAs-type structure and exhibits intrinsic magnetism due to partially filled 4f orbitals on Tb. The analysis covers both antiferromagnetic (AFM) and field-induced ferromagnetic (FM) magnetic phases.

Key observables. Electrical resistivity $ ho(T)$, magnetoresistance (MR), angular magnetoresistance ($ ext{AMR}$), Hall resistivity ($ ho_{xy}(B)$), specific heat $C(T)$, and electronic band structure/Density of States ($ ext{DOS}$) at the Fermi level.

Important parameters / regimes. Antiferromagnetic transition temperature ($T_N = 5 ext{ K}$), critical magnetic field ($B_c = 5 ext{ T}$), carrier mobilities ($\mu_n, \mu_p$), and lattice parameters ($a = 6.745(4) ext{ Å}$).

Assumptions / limitations. DFT calculations utilize GGA/MB-JGGA functionals with SOC included; the analysis models charge transport using a two-band model fit at high temperatures.

Figures summary. Figures detail temperature dependence of magnetic susceptibility and specific heat, alongside $ ho(T)$, MR vs. angle ($ heta$), and electronic band structures comparing nonmagnetic and ferromagnetic states.

Paper structure. The paper systematically presents structural confirmation (XRD/EDX), followed by thermodynamic measurements ($C(T)$), then detailed magnetotransport studies ($ ho(T)$, MR, AMR, Hall effect) across magnetic transitions, culminating in theoretical calculations of the electronic band structure and topological analysis.

Abstract

Structural, thermodynamic and electrical transport properties of TbAuPb were investigated on single crystals. The compound was found to crystallize with the cubic MgAgAs-type structure characteristic of half-Heusler materials. It orders antiferromagnetically at TN = 5 K and undergoes a transition into a different antiferromagnetic phase emerging in high magnetic fields. Electrical transport in TbAuPb exhibits a multiband character, with a predominance of hole-like carriers. Angular magnetoresistance evolves systematically with applied magnetic field and changes its symmetry near the spin-reorientation transition, highlighting strong coupling between the charge transport and the magnetic order. The results of first-principles calculations indicate that TbAuPb is a band inverted semimetal in the non-magnetic state, which becomes topologically trivial in the field-induced ferromagnetic state.

Summary. This paper presents a microscopic constitutive theory to explain the nonlinear mechanical behavior of amorphous solids like metallic and polymer glasses. It successfully models the sequence from elastic loading through stress overshoot, yielding, and strain hardening by coupling nonaffine elasticity instability with structural relaxation dynamics. The results provide a unified theoretical framework validated against multiple experimental materials.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on condensed matter mechanics, the underlying mathematical framework involving complex relaxation functions (stretched exponentials) and coupling between structural disorder (nonaffine effects) and macroscopic response shares conceptual links with modeling glassy dynamics in quantum or many-body systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Developing a microscopic constitutive theory for the nonlinear deformation of amorphous materials, specifically addressing stress overshoot, yielding, and strain hardening.

Main result. The model successfully reproduces the full stress-strain response across various glasses by unifying nonaffine elasticity instability with viscous structural relaxation dynamics. This establishes a unified framework linking these complex mechanical phenomena in amorphous solids.

Method. The theory couples nonaffine elasticity (derived from free energy considerations) to irreversible many-body relaxation processes, modeling the total stress as a sum of elastic, viscous, and hardening components.

Model / system. The study focuses on metallic and polymer glasses (e.g., polycarbonate, PMMA). The theory uses concepts like nonaffine displacements, strain-dependent coordination number, and stretched exponential relaxation forms to describe mechanical response.

Key observables. Stress-strain response curve, stress overshoot magnitude, yielding point, steady plastic flow regime, and strain hardening behavior.

Important parameters / regimes. Elastic modulus (E), viscosity ($\eta$), structural relaxation time ($ au_c$), viscous relaxation time ($ au_v$), and the stretched exponential exponent ($eta$).

Assumptions / limitations. The total stress is assumed to be a linear sum of elastic, viscous, and hardening contributions. For polymers, finite chain extensibility is incorporated.

Figures summary. Figures compare theoretical predictions (solid lines) with experimental data (symbols) for MG, PC, and PMMA across various strain rates and temperatures, illustrating the characteristic sequence from loading to flow/hardening.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theory by defining components of stress ($\sigma_{el}, \sigma_{visc}, \sigma_{hard}$), deriving constitutive equations based on nonaffine elasticity and relaxation dynamics, and then applying these models to quantitatively reproduce experimental data for multiple amorphous materials across different deformation regimes.

Abstract

We develop a microscopic constitutive theory for the nonlinear deformation of metallic and polymer glasses based on nonaffine elasticity coupled to irreversible many-body relaxation. The theory predicts the full stress--strain response, from linear elasticity through stress overshoot and yielding to steady plastic flow. We show that stress overshoot originates from the competition between a nonaffine elastic instability induced by strain-driven loss of mechanical connectivity at the atomic/molecular level, and viscous dissipation associated with structural relaxation. For polymer glasses, finite chain extensibility naturally accounts for strain hardening at large deformation. The stretched-exponential relaxation exponent is obtained independently from stress or modulus relaxation measurements and provides the primary dynamical input to the theory. Using a small set of physically meaningful parameters, the model quantitatively reproduces experimental stress--strain curves for metallic glasses, polycarbonate, PMMA, and epoxy resins over a broad range of strain rates. These results establish a unified microscopic framework linking relaxation dynamics, yielding, plastic flow, and strain hardening in amorphous solids.

Abstract

The potential capabilities of quantum computers motivated the development of cryptographic protocols suitable for securing communication against adversaries with access to large fault-tolerant quantum computers. However, even though current quantum computers are limited in terms of size and precision, they can still be useful for finding loopholes and weaknesses in the post-quantum cryptographic protocols. In this work, we present an attempt to utilize the capabilities of Quantum Generative Adversarial Networks (QGANs), one of the promising architectures used in quantum machine learning, for this purpose. We describe an example application of QGAN architecture for the purpose of loading the probability distribution of the hash-based digital signatures into the memory of a quantum computer. Our results confirm that near-term hybrid quantum-classical methods possess capabilities required for this purpose. The presented approach can be used as a first step in the workflow, enabling the utilization of quantum computing for attacking post-quantum cryptographic primitives.

Summary. This paper proposes an advanced method to create reliable single-electron traps on solid neon, a promising platform for qubits. The authors use detailed 3D simulations to show that uncontrolled surface roughness causes trapping noise, but they demonstrate that selectively etching an underlying dielectric layer can deterministically engineer the potential minimum required for stable qubit operation.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses hardware realization challenges for solid-state quantum computing platforms by providing a theoretical blueprint for controlling nanoscale potential landscapes using material engineering.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is achieving deterministic, controlled single-electron trapping on solid neon surfaces for use as a quantum qubit, overcoming issues caused by uncontrolled surface roughness.

Main result. Engineered dielectric templates via selective etching provide a practical pathway to create reproducible potential minima that reliably capture electrons while suppressing unwanted surface bound states.

Method. The authors employed large-scale 3D Schrödinger–Poisson simulations to model the quantum electrostatics of electron confinement in patterned solid neon structures.

Model / system. The system is a levitating single-electron qubit on solid neon, modeled using a 3D finite-difference Schrödinger–Poisson framework. The potential landscape is controlled by engineering an underlying dielectric layer beneath the neon film.

Key observables. Lowest-energy bound states ($E_n$), orbital splitting ($\Delta E = E_2 - E_1$), probability density $|\psi(\mathbf{r})|^2$, and wavefunction localization.

Important parameters / regimes. Dielectric constant ($\varepsilon_r$), dielectric thickness ($H$), surface roughness parameters ($\sigma_{rms}$), and gate voltages (e.g., $V_{ ext{bottom}} = 1.0 ext{ V}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The model assumes the validity of the single-particle limit for initial analysis, though future work aims to include spin dynamics.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the comparison between random roughness effects (causing unintended trapping) and the controlled potential landscape achieved by etching a dielectric trench, showing localized wavefunctions in the engineered geometry.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the promise of solid neon qubits to identifying surface morphology as the critical failure point. It then details simulations comparing uncontrolled roughness vs. engineered dielectric templates, culminating in demonstrating deterministic trapping via selective etching.

Abstract

Levitating electron qubit on the surface of solid neon has recently emerged as a promising and intrinsically noise-resilient platform for quantum information processing. Their ultra-clean, inert environment suppresses conventional decoherence pathways associated with lattice disorder, charge traps, and nuclear-spin baths that limit coherence in semiconductor qubits. Yet, uncontrolled surface features such as bumps, valleys, and electrode-defined gaps can bind electrons unintentionally, contributing charge noise and inducing spin-orbit coupling mediated decoherence. To address this challenge, we propose an engineered interface in which a dielectric layer is deposited beneath the solid neon to provide an atomically smooth template, eliminating surface-roughness induced trapping. By selectively etching this dielectric layer at desired qubit locations, deterministic potential minima can be engineered to reliably capture electrons while suppressing unwanted surface bound states. We perform large-scale Schrodinger and Poisson simulation to compare the existing and proposed strategies of electron trapping on neon, obtaining good agreement with recent experimental measurements.

Abstract

Here, we demonstrate a MOT optimized for inertial quantum sensing and precision metrology applications. Following polarization-gradient cooling (PGC), the system produces atom samples with temperatures below 10 $μ$K, similar to those achieved in conventional MOTs that use collimated laser beams. Abstract

Fast and accurate classical simulation of quantum systems is a central challenge in the design and control of quantum computers, but the highly oscillatory dynamics of these systems severely limit the efficiency of standard numerical methods. To address this, we adapt Filon quadrature for oscillatory integrals into two numerical methods, called Filon and Controlled Filon, for solving linear systems of ODEs with highly oscillatory solutions. We tailor both methods for efficient implementation in controlled quantum systems, and the Controlled Filon method additionally accounts for the oscillatory structure of the control pulses. We show by numerical experiments that these methods significantly reduce the computational cost of accurately simulating systems of superconducting transmon qubits by decreasing the number of timesteps needed to reach a given level of precision, with only a modest increase in the cost per timestep. For a realistic simulation of the dynamics of a CNOT gate, the Controlled Filon method is the most efficient method tested at every target accuracy, outperforming the best Hermite method by up to 6x and the Hermite method of the same order by up to 500x.

Abstract

Hybrid quantum-classical application performance is increasingly limited by classical control, host-to-QPU communication, and scheduling rather than quantum execution. Existing simulators and runtime interfaces analyze individual kernels but fail to address system-topology questions, such as controller bottlenecks, diminishing returns of QPU capacity, or resource contention under heterogeneous workloads. We introduce HybridQC, a topology-aware discrete-event simulator for tightly coupled hybrid compute units (HCUs). HybridQC models HCUs as configurable graphs of classical processors, memory, controllers, quantum annealing (QA) and digital quantum computing (DQC) devices, and communication links. It decomposes jobs into typed, directed acyclic graphs of stages, ranging from input preparation to classical postprocessing, executed under interchangeable scheduling policies. Calibrated with live measurements from D-Wave (Advantage 1 and 2) and IBM (Kingston, Marrakesh, and Fez) processors, HybridQC distinguishes physical QPU occupancy from cloud wall-clock latency. Scalability varies heavily by workload dimension: a 100x input data increase yields a 306 s median runtime, whereas a 100x joint increase in circuit count, shot count, and circuit depth drives runtime to 4.806x10^7 s on an unchanged HCU. HybridQC offers a systematic framework for evaluating the topology, scheduling, and scaling limits of hybrid architectures prior to physical deployment.

Summary. The paper addresses the challenge of local minima trapping in quantum annealing by introducing diagonal catalysts. Abstract

Quantum annealing is often limited by population trapped in local minima many spin flips from the solution. We introduce a mathematical framework to understand the connection between energy and Hamming distance in optimization problems. Using this, we build ZZ-catalysts from ground-state patterns of small frustration-free subproblems that make configurations far from the solution less energetically competitive. On sparse problems they multiply the near-solution probability at short sweeps, with gains persisting on fully-connected models and tunable via subproblem choice.
