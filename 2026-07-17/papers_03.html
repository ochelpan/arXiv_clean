Summary. This paper investigates the Brownian Castle (BC) interface growth model, finding it belongs to a new universality class distinct from standard models like Ballistic Deposition. Through extensive simulations, the authors determine specific scaling exponents and show that the height fluctuations follow non-Gaussian statistics characterized by Cauchy-Lorentz tails. This work advances the understanding of complex, driven interfaces in statistical physics.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical interface growth, the analysis of non-Gaussian statistics (Cauchy-Lorentz tails) and deviations from standard scaling laws provides insights into complex, non-equilibrium dynamics relevant to open quantum systems or driven many-body processes where simple thermal assumptions fail.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study characterizes a new universality class associated with interface growth dynamics by analyzing modified Family-Vicsek scaling properties and probability distributions.

Main result. The Brownian Castle (BC) interface exhibits modified scaling exponents ($eta \approx 0.47$, $\alpha \approx 1.01$) and shows that the height change distribution follows a Cauchy-Lorentz form, suggesting large jumps.

Method. Numerical simulations are used to extract growth and roughness exponents via fitting double logarithmic plots, complemented by data collapse analysis and statistical testing of probability distributions.

Model / system. The system is the Brownian Castle (BC) interface growth model, a variation on Ballistic Deposition that allows for material removal. It is compared against established models like BD, which falls into the KPZ universality class.

Key observables. Interface width $w(L, t)$, growth exponent ($eta$), roughness exponent ($\alpha$), and probability distributions of height changes ($p(dh)$).

Important parameters / regimes. System size ($L$) up to 512 sites, time steps up to $10^6$, and the derived scaling exponents $eta=0.47 \pm 0.01$ and $\alpha=1.01 \pm 0.01$.

Assumptions / limitations. The standard scaling relationship $z = \alpha/eta$ may break down due to finite size effects, which is noted when comparing derived exponents to the exponent observed during data collapse ($z=3$).

Figures summary. Figures illustrate schematic growth rules for BD vs. BC, show interface width evolution over time and system size (determining $eta$ and $\alpha$), and demonstrate near-perfect data collapse using a specific scaling function.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the BC model variation, presents numerical simulations to calculate scaling exponents ($\alpha, eta$) via fitting, analyzes data collapse for consistency checks, and finally examines probability distributions (heights and height changes) to characterize the underlying stochastic process.

Abstract

The Brownian Castle is a new interface growth model that is a variation on the well-known ballistic deposition model that results in an entirely new universality class. We present numerical verification that the interface width for BC interfaces displays modified Family-Vicsek scaling properties up to finite size corrections. Specifically, we find a growth exponent of $β=0.470 \pm 0.012$ and a roughness exponent of $α=1.01 \pm 0.018$. The scaling is modified at short times with a size scaling exponent that described the early time dependence of interface width on length. The probability distribution of heights for the BC interface shows significant deviations from simple Gaussian behavior and the probability distribution of height changes shows a Cauchy-Lorentz form consistent with expectations for a process involving relatively large jumps.

Summary. This paper models how high-energy phonons, which can damage superconducting qubits, are suppressed through a process called down-conversion within an adjacent normal metal film. By solving coupled kinetic equations for electron and phonon populations, the authors determine that this mitigation mechanism is most effective when the normal metal layer has a thickness near one micron.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses energy dissipation mechanisms in superconducting circuits, which is crucial for maintaining quantum coherence. The detailed kinetic modeling of phonon relaxation provides insights into open quantum system dynamics driven by the environment (phonons).

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is mitigating quasiparticle poisoning in superconducting devices caused by pair-breaking phonons.

Main result. Phonon down-conversion efficiency, which reduces the damaging high-energy phonons, is found to be nearly optimal over a finite range of normal metal thicknesses, typically around one micron.

Method. The study employs kinetic equation modeling solved in the steady state to track phonon and electron distributions. Analytical and numerical solutions are used to calculate the down-conversion efficiency based on material parameters.

Model / system. The system involves a superconducting device coupled via a normal metal film to a substrate, where high-energy phonons scatter off electrons, transferring energy and lowering the phonon energy below the pair-breaking threshold.

Key observables. Down-conversion efficiency ($R_{qp}$ or $R_\lambda$), electron distribution function ($f(E)$), and phonon distribution functions ($n(\Omega)$, $n_s(\Omega)$).

Important parameters / regimes. Material parameters like electron-phonon interaction strength, phonon transmission coefficients ($ au_{sm}, au_{ms}$), film thickness ($d_m$), and substrate thickness ($d_s$).

Assumptions / limitations. Approximations include neglecting spatial gradients, assuming particle–hole symmetry for electrons, using the Debye model for phonons (ignoring anharmonic effects), and neglecting electron-electron scattering.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the physical setup (superconducting qubit chip with normal metal islands) and show the steady-state evolution of electron and phonon distributions under various injection conditions.

Paper structure. The paper develops a kinetic equation model for coupled phonon/electron dynamics, solves for the steady state using limiting cases (no-metal, perfect absorption), derives explicit formulas for down-conversion efficiency via asymptotic analysis, and discusses parameter dependence on geometry.

Abstract

Thanks to low dissipation, superconducting devices are promising for a number of applications, such as detectors and implementations of quantum computation. However, their working can be adversely impacted by quasiparticles, which is why so-called quasiparticle poisoning mechanisms and their mitigation are under intense investigation. Here we focus on one poisoning mechanism, namely pair-breaking phonons, and its mitigation through down-conversion by a normal-metal film - the process in which scattering of high-energy phonons by electrons lowers the energy of the former below the pair-breaking threshold. To study the down-conversion, we introduce a model based on kinetic equations, which we solve both analytically (approximately) and numerically in the steady state. We use the solution the estimate a properly-defined down-conversion efficiency which depends on material parameters (such as the strength of electron-phonon interaction and the phonon transmission coefficient at interfaces) and film and substrate thicknesses. Interestingly, we find that the efficiency is nearly optimal over a finite range of metal thicknesses, with the minimum near-optimal thickness being typically of the order of a micron.

Summary. This paper develops an exact theoretical framework for analyzing how multiple active agents interact when their movement is dictated by a shared, memory-retaining 'trail field.' It derives precise formulas for collective first-passage statistics, notably proving that under saturating interaction conditions, the order of exploration does not affect the final absorption probabilities.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical random walks, the mathematical techniques used—analyzing non-equilibrium dynamics governed by environmental memory and deriving exact scaling laws—are highly relevant to open quantum systems where environment coupling leads to persistent correlations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study characterizes collective first-passage statistics for multiple interacting random walkers whose movement is influenced by a shared, history-dependent environmental 'trail field.'

Main result. For saturating interactions (SATW$\phi$), the splitting probabilities are exactly identical whether the walkers start simultaneously or sequentially, establishing an exact framework for such memory effects.

Method. The analysis involves deriving exact analytical expressions by representing the collective trail field using squared Bessel processes and generalizing Ray-Knight theorems to $N$ particles.

Model / system. The model consists of N one-dimensional random walkers (SIRWs) whose jump probabilities depend on a shared local time profile, distinguishing between saturating ($ ext{SATW}_\phi$) and non-saturating interaction regimes.

Key observables. Persistence exponents ($ heta_{k,N}$) and splitting probabilities ($P_{+k|x_0}$), which quantify the probability of $k$ walkers reaching one boundary before another.

Important parameters / regimes. The number of walkers ($N$), the interaction strength parameter ($\phi$), and the dimensionality ($d=1$).

Assumptions / limitations. The rigorous analytical results are established for the saturating class ($ ext{SATW}_\phi$); deviations occur in non-saturating cases.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate schematic dynamics, numerical verification of persistence exponents across different models, and comparisons showing that splitting probabilities match theory for SATW$\phi$ but show protocol dependence for TSAWs.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining the physical problem to deriving exact expressions for observables like survival probabilities and splitting probabilities. It then demonstrates the key invariance property for SATW$\phi$ by relating sequential and simultaneous protocols via local time additivity, concluding with general scaling arguments for exponents.

Abstract

Memory encoded in the environment mediates interactions between active agents, from trail-following organisms to synthetic active matter depositing persistent tracks. Although such memory is known to strongly affect transport, its consequences for collective first-passage phenomena remain largely unexplored. Here we study $N$ one-dimensional random walkers interacting through a shared trail field. We characterize the $k^{\rm th}$ (among $N$) arrival time at a fixed target, and the probability that exactly $k$ walkers in $[0,1]$ reach one boundary before the other. For the broad class of self-interacting walkers with a saturating response to the trail, we derive exact expressions for the corresponding persistence exponents and splitting probabilities. Strikingly, despite the strong history-dependent correlations generated by the common environment, splitting probabilities are exactly identical whether walkers explore simultaneously or one after another. This invariance breaks down for nonsaturating trail interactions. Our results follow from an exact representation of the collective trail field and establish a framework for first-passage phenomena in systems coupled through persistent environmental memory.

Summary. This paper develops a novel framework to describe probabilistic classical particles using quantum mechanical formalism. It shows that robust 'quantum observables' can be defined for these systems, demonstrating how standard quantum mechanics (like the hydrogen atom spectrum) emerges as a specific subsystem of this larger statistical structure. This offers a theoretical bridge between classical probability theory and fundamental quantum principles.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a deep theoretical link between classical statistical mechanics and quantum theory by reformulating observables using non-commuting operators, which is highly relevant for understanding open quantum systems or measurement processes.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The classical observables of position and momentum are inadequate for describing particles in microphysical situations with substantial probability distribution dispersion.

Main result. The paper proposes a quantum formalism where statistical observables, represented by non-commuting operators, can describe probabilistic classical particles, showing how standard quantum mechanics emerges as a subsystem description.

Method. It uses a quantum formalism applied to the Liouville equation for classical statistics, introducing complex wave functions derived via Fourier transforms and analyzing coupled particle-mirror dynamics.

Model / system. The model involves probabilistic classical particles governed by the Liouville equation. The analysis is formalized using a complex Schrödinger equation describing a system composed of the original particle and its time-reversed 'mirror' counterpart.

Key observables. Quantum observables (statistical observables), expectation values of position ($\langle x angle$), momentum ($\langle p angle$), and conserved statistical quantities like $\langle H^2 angle$.

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis is general but demonstrates specific results for the harmonic potential and the Coulomb potential.

Assumptions / limitations. It assumes that a quantum system can emerge as a subsystem of a larger classical statistical framework, though it does not claim this applies to all physical reality.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the inadequacy of classical observables, then introducing the quantum formalism via the Liouville equation mapping, developing the coupled particle-mirror Hamiltonian, and finally demonstrating recovery of known quantum results (e.g., Hydrogen atom spectrum) in specific limits.

Abstract

The classical observables of position and momentum are not well adapted to particles in a microphysical situation where typical probability distributions are characterized by a substantial dispersion. We propose the use of more robust quantum observables for probabilistic classical particles. The quantum observables are statistical observables which do not take fixed values for a given classical position and momentum. Solutions of the Liouville equation are discussed in the quantum formalism for classical statistics. Statistical observables are represented by non-commuting operators. No classical correlation function is defined for these observables and Bell's inequalities do not apply. We demonstrate for a general potential how a quantum system emerges from classical statistics. For the particular cases of a harmonic potential and a Coulomb potential we investigate subsystems which describe all features of a quantum particle. This covers the discrete energy spectrum of the hydrogen atom and quantum harmonic oscillator. We discuss the interference for the double-slit experiment. Conserved statistical observables may also be relevant for the probabilistic dynamics of dust or planets.

Summary. Abstract

Abstract

We present a two-stage inverse design procedure for producing disordered stealthy hyperuniform trivalent photonic networks in two dimensions with isotropic complete photonic band gaps (PBGs) blocking light regardless of direction or polarization (TE or TM) over a wide frequency range. Most ordinary disordered systems fail to maintain complete PBGs as system size increases. The only known exceptions that remain open in the largest simulations have been generated by mapping stealthy hyperuniform point patterns into trivalent networks. Our designs can be fabricated using additive manufacturing, offering new pathways to manipulate electromagnetic waves for photonic technologies.

Abstract

Multipartite Bell nonlocality is a central resource for device-independent quantum information protocols, but its practical certification is inevitably affected by imperfect measurements. We analyze how coherent angular misalignment and incoherent outcome flipping affect Bell-value degradation and nonlocality thresholds in $n$-partite GHZ states based on the Mermin, Svetlichny, and Mermin--Ardehali--Belinskii--Klyshko (MABK) inequalities. Coherent misalignment produces periodic angular violation windows whose individual widths shrink with the number of parties. In contrast, incoherent outcome flipping yields a single critical outcome-flipping probability, which increases with $n$ for MABK and the odd-$n$ Mermin inequalities, but decreases with $n$ for the Svetlichny inequality. Connecting the degraded Bell values to asymptotic Devetak--Winter key-rate bounds under a convex-combination attack model shows that secret-key generation imposes stricter constraints on measurement imperfections than nonlocality certification. These results provide quantitative benchmarks for robust multipartite nonlocality certification and key-rate estimation under measurement imperfections.

Abstract

The mechanism governing the resistivity in dilute Cu-3d transition-metal alloys at ambient temperature -- the regime relevant to most practical applications and distinct from the low-temperature, Kondo-screened regime addressed by earlier theoretical work -- is investigated using first-principles calculations based on the Korringa--Kohn--Rostoker coherent potential approximation combined with the Kubo--Greenwood formalism. The paramagnetic state is described within the disordered local moment (DLM) framework, corresponding to a local-moment paramagnet rather than a Pauli paramagnet. We show that the experimentally observed resistivity trends are reproduced only within the DLM description, while nonmagnetic and ferromagnetic states fail to capture the correct element dependence. Contrary to conventional interpretations based on the density of states at the Fermi level, the resistivity exhibits a strong correlation with the full width at half maximum (FWHM) of the Bloch spectral function (BSF) on the Fermi surface. This correlation reflects the disorder-induced lifetime broadening of electronic states, directly related to the scattering rate that governs electrical resistivity. A common power-law scaling between resistivity and BSF broadening is identified across different magnetic states. These results demonstrate that the resistivity is governed by disorder-induced band broadening in momentum space rather than by local density-of-states effects, providing a unified microscopic interpretation of the breakdown of Linde's rule in Cu-based alloys.
