Dynamical resource theory of time-reversal symmetry breaking

Summary. This paper introduces a dynamical resource theory to quantify time-reversal symmetry breaking in general quantum channels. It resolves a long-standing ambiguity by decomposing total T-violation into a thermodynamic component (nonunitality) and a kinematic component. This framework provides a powerful, operational measure for studying symmetry breaking in open quantum systems.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous, operational tool to quantify fundamental symmetries in open quantum systems, which is crucial for understanding decoherence and non-equilibrium dynamics in AMO physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to develop an operationally meaningful framework to quantify intrinsic Time-reversal symmetry breaking (T-violation) in general quantum channels, resolving the conceptual confusion between T-violation and non-invertibility.

Main result. T-violation is decomposed into two distinct, additive components: kinematic T-violation (from antiunitary motion-reversal) and nonunitality driven by thermodynamics. The resource theory of kinematic T-violation is shown to be robust and applicable even to classical channels.

Method. The authors employ dynamical resource theory, defining T-symmetry operationally for quantum channels and decomposing the total T-violation into its constituent parts using specific antiunitary operators.

Model / system. The analysis is performed on general quantum channels ($\mathcal{E}$), extending beyond simple Hamiltonians to account for non-unitary, dissipative processes. The framework uses concepts like motion-reversal operators ($\Theta$) and the Oreshkov–Cerf formalism.

Key observables. T-violation, kinematic T-violation, nonunitality, transition probabilities, and resource measures like trace distance and relative entropy.

Important parameters / regimes. The decomposition relies on the relationship $ ext{T-violation} = ext{nonunitality} + ext{kinematic T-violation}$.

Assumptions / limitations. The definition of motion reversal is relative to a chosen reference time displacement ($U_{\Delta t}$), and the analysis focuses on the simplest setting without decomposing into superselection sectors.

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically relates original and motion-reversed processes for a system with momentum. Figure 5 shows the correspondence between process theory elements and quantum circuit elements.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the operational definition of T-symmetry for processes, then develops the resource theory framework by decomposing T-violation, and finally analyzes the resulting resource theory of kinematic T-violation.

Abstract

Time-reversal symmetry and its violation (T-violation) are fundamental across diverse physics domains, from particle physics to fluctuation theorems and reciprocity. While time-reversal symmetry for isolated systems is simply determined by the Hamiltonian, quantifying the magnitude of T-violation, especially in noisy quantum processes, has been a subject of ongoing debate. Here, we propose an operationally meaningful framework to quantify the intrinsic T-violation of quantum channels. We integrate operationally defined time-reversal transformations into dynamical resource theory to formulate T-violation as a resource. T-violation decomposes into two distinct components: kinematic T-violation originating from antiunitary motion-reversal, and nonunitality driven purely by thermodynamics. This decomposition resolves the longstanding confusion between broken time-reversal symmetry and non-invertibility. Finally, we analyze the resulting resource theory of kinematic T-violation, showing that it is neither quality-like nor quantity-like, and proving the existence of a universal golden unit even within classical channels.

Summary. This paper develops an extended formalism for calculating Out-of-Time-Ordered Correlators (OTOCs) in open quantum systems, showing that the standard Lindblad approach is insufficient. By including a crucial 'replica-correlation term,' the authors restore physical properties lost in the reduced description. This allows for more accurate study of quantum chaos and operator spreading in realistic, dissipative environments.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous theoretical tool to accurately calculate quantum chaos measures (OTOCs) in realistic open quantum systems, bridging the gap between unitary and dissipative dynamics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates Out-of-Time-Ordered Correlators (OTOCs) in open quantum systems, specifically addressing the limitations of using the standard Lindblad formalism for these higher-order correlators.

Main result. The full OTOC dynamics requires an additional 'replica-correlation term' beyond the standard Lindblad approach, which restores fundamental properties like the conservation of the operator Hilbert–Schmidt norm.

Method. The authors derive the OTOC evolution by systematically tracking correlations between environment replicas, leading to an extended formalism that corrects the standard reduced dynamics.

Model / system. The study uses a microscopic model of a system coupled to a random bath, exemplified by a dissipative Ising chain with boundary dissipation. The dynamics are analyzed by comparing the full unitary evolution to the reduced Lindblad evolution.

Key observables. Out-of-Time-Ordered Correlators (OTOCs), operator spreading, and the conservation of the operator Hilbert–Schmidt norm.

Important parameters / regimes. Dissipation strength ($\gamma$), time ($t$), and the replica number ($k$).

Assumptions / limitations. The standard Lindblad formalism is insufficient for OTOCs because it neglects correlations between replicas, necessitating the inclusion of the replica-correlation term.

Figures summary. Figures compare OTOCs for different dissipation strengths ($\gamma$), showing that the full OTOC retains unitary-like behavior, while the reduced OTOC exhibits premature decay near the dissipative boundary.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the failure of the standard Lindblad approach for OTOCs to deriving the necessary replica-correlation term, and finally applying this extended formalism to study operator spreading in dissipative spin chains.

Abstract

Out-of-time-ordered correlators (OTOCs) provide a powerful probe of quantum chaos and scrambling. While the Lindblad formalism is often used to describe OTOCs in open systems, microscopic mechanisms that give rise to the Lindblad equation at the level of time-ordered correlation functions do not necessarily justify this approach at the level of OTOCs. Starting from a microscopic model of a subsystem coupled to a random bath, we show that the resulting OTOC dynamics contains an additional contribution compared to the standard Lindblad approach. This contribution, induced by correlations between different replicas, allows key properties of the underlying unitary dynamics such as long-time freeness to be retained and information scrambling to be captured more faithfully. As an application, we show that this extended formalism can be used to numerically study OTOCs in large closed systems by replacing part of the system with a dissipative boundary, offering a way to study operator spreading and higher-order correlations beyond directly accessible system sizes.

Summary. This work advances stochastic path-space thermodynamics by expanding the set of measurable thermodynamic variables to include complex boundary functionals like residence time and integral current. Using the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process as a model, the authors derive generalized fluctuation theorems and analyze how external forces break Onsager symmetry in the system's free-energy landscape. This provides a powerful, mathematically rigorous framework for understanding non-equilibrium processes in molecular motors and ion channels.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical stochastic processes, the rigorous application of fluctuation theorems and the extension of thermodynamic variables into path space provide powerful mathematical tools applicable to open quantum systems where non-equilibrium dynamics are crucial.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper extends stochastic path-space thermodynamics by systematically incorporating a broad spectrum of boundary and relative functionals of random processes into the description of thermodynamic variables.

Main result. It derives a modified path-space fluctuation theorem of the Gallavotti-Cohen type and establishes an exact thermodynamic potential for systems governed by combinations of two additive functionals.

Method. The analysis employs the Feynman-Kac formalism and parabolic cylinder theory to calculate the joint Laplace transform, leading to the determination of the effective Hamiltonian's eigenvalues.

Model / system. The primary model is a one-dimensional Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process, used to simulate the waiting phase of a molecular Brownian motor or an ion channel sensitive to external stimuli.

Key observables. Residence time ($ au_+$), integral current ($ ext{T}I$), Maximal Deficit (Maximum Drawdown), and the mutual cross-sensitivity coefficient ($\chi$).

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis explores regimes far from equilibrium, such as the deep suppression regime ($ au^+ o 0$) and the continuous activation regime ($ au^+ o 1$), and the effect of external drift ($V$).

Assumptions / limitations. The framework relies on the mathematical tractability afforded by considering the combination of only two additive functionals, and the system dynamics are modeled by the OU process.

Figures summary. The figures illustrate the topographic contour map of the path-space free energy profile, showing the transition from symmetric quadratic ellipses near equilibrium to asymmetric, non-parabolic contours when far from equilibrium or when external drifts break symmetry.

Paper structure. The paper systematically builds its argument by first extending the formalism via boundary functionals, then detailing the mathematical framework using the Feynman-Kac approach for the OU process, applying it to a biological ion channel model, and finally discussing the derived results and implications.

Abstract

This paper extends the formalism of stochastic path-space thermodynamics by systematically expanding the space of thermodynamic variables with a spectrum of boundary and relative functionals of random processes. Generalizing the approaches introduced in preprints arXiv:2607.24078 and arXiv:2609.08477, we investigate the fluctuation statistics of a one-dimensional Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process with an asymmetric linear drift and a step penalty potential at the origin, which models the waiting phase of a molecular Brownian motor. Utilizing the Feynman-Kac formalism and the parabolic cylinder theory, a numerical scheme based on matching logarithmic derivatives via the secant method is constructed. A high-precision eigenvalues of the effective Hamiltonian is obtained, which define the cumulant generating function. We formulate a modified path-space fluctuation theorem of the Gallavotti-Cohen type for the conjugate drift current at a fixed integral occupation time of the system in the dissipative half-plane. The physical and thermodynamic significance of the new variables is elucidated. A combination of "residence time" and "integral current" functionals is applied to describe an ion channel sensitive to mechanical or electrical stimuli.

Summary. This paper develops a theoretical framework to enhance the robustness of Spontaneous Parametric Down-Conversion (SPDC) by implementing a technique called composite segmentation governed by SU(1, 1) dynamics. The authors demonstrate that this method significantly mitigates performance losses due to temperature drift, angular misalignment, and fabrication defects. The results show that the system maintains high efficiency and phase coherence across realistic environmental variations without needing complex, continuous feedback stabilization.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a highly structured, mathematically rigorous framework (SU(1, 1) algebra) for designing robust quantum light sources, which is crucial for scaling quantum technologies beyond idealized laboratory conditions.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge addressed is mitigating performance degradation, specifically reduced pair-generation efficiency and phase instability, in Spontaneous Parametric Down-Conversion (SPDC) caused by environmental fluctuations and fabrication imperfections.

Main result. The proposed composite segmentation approach maintains high relative conversion efficiency ($\eta \ge 0.954$) and phase coherence across a $5^\circ ext{C}$ thermal window without continuous feedback, and it tolerates up to two missing poling domains.

Method. The analysis employs a theoretical framework based on the SU(1, 1) Lie algebra, utilizing spatial Fourier decomposition and deriving asymptotic scaling laws for error cancellation.

Model / system. The system is SPDC in quadratic chi^(2) media, generating two-mode squeezed vacuum states. The dynamics are modeled using coupled-mode equations whose algebra is isomorphic to SU(1, 1), analyzed via Detuning Modulated Composite Segmentation (DMCS).

Key observables. Relative pair-generation efficiency ($\eta$), modeled phase-coherence metric ($C_\phi$), and the exit pair conversion metric ($w(L; m)$).

Important parameters / regimes. Thermal variation ($\Delta T$), angular misalignment ($ heta$), spatial harmonic order ($m$), and the number of missing poling domains ($\Delta M$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the low-gain regime for the pair generation process and relies on the existence of a coordinate-dilation symmetry for wavelength translation.

Figures summary. Figure 1 numerically integrates the evolution showing the predicted $1/m^2$ scaling of the pair-generation rate with harmonic order $m$.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the physical problem (error sources in SPDC), develops the theoretical model using SU(1, 1) algebra and composite segmentation, derives key scaling laws ($m^{-2}$), and then applies these tools to demonstrate robustness against thermal drift, angular misalignment, and fabrication defects.

Abstract

Spontaneous parametric down-conversion is a primary resource for generating non-classical light, yet phase mismatch fluctuations from temperature drift, angular misalignment, and domain fabrication errors reduce pair-generation efficiency and phase stability. Here, we analyze error mitigation in spontaneous parametric down-conversion using composite segmentation governed by SU(1, 1) spatial dynamics. We show that odd spatial Fourier harmonics preserve error-cancellation flatness with pair-generation rates scaling asymptotically as $m^{-2}$, while a coordinate-dilation symmetry provides a design prescription for translating configurations across distinct operating wavelengths (405, 532, and 775 nm). Furthermore, an open-loop thermo-optic bias counteracts off-axis angular drift to broaden the 90%-efficiency angular acceptance half-width by 3.46x, and up to two missing poling domains are tolerated with no numerically resolved loss in relative conversion efficiency. Across a 5$^\circ$C thermal window, the generated pairs sustain a relative conversion efficiency of $η\ge 0.954$ with the modeled phase-coherence metric remaining above 0.95, providing a structurally robust approach for entangled photon generation without requiring continuous closed-loop feedback stabilization.

Summary. This theoretical paper establishes a fundamental, exponential lower bound on the number of measurements needed to detect non-equilibrium dynamics in a stochastic system. The cost scales exponentially with the minimum measurement time gap ($ au_0$) and is governed by the slowest damping rate ($\gamma_{ ext{irr}}$) of the system's irreversible modes. This result sets a rigorous benchmark for experimental detection limits in non-equilibrium statistical mechanics.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a fundamental, quantitative limit on the experimental feasibility of detecting true non-equilibrium dynamics in physical systems, linking measurement limitations directly to the spectral properties ($\gamma_{ ext{irr}}$) of the underlying master equation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Determining the minimum number of measurements required to statistically distinguish a true non-equilibrium stochastic process from any hidden equilibrium process, given a finite minimum measurement gap.

Main result. The required number of snapshots scales exponentially with the minimum measurement gap ($ au_0$), specifically $N^* \propto e^{2\gamma_{ ext{irr}} au_0}$, where $\gamma_{ ext{irr}}$ is the slowest damping rate of the rotating modes.

Method. The analysis uses information-theoretic bounds, relating detectability to relative entropy and causal stopping times, establishing both lower and upper bounds on the required sample size.

Model / system. The study analyzes stochastic systems governed by finite Markov generators ($Q$), comparing the true irreversible dynamics against the null class of reversible Markov chains.

Key observables. Minimum expected number of snapshots ($N^*$), entropy production rate, and the measurable current ($V$).

Important parameters / regimes. Minimum measurement gap ($ au_0$), the slowest damping rate ($\gamma_{ ext{irr}}$), and the statistical significance level ($\alpha$).

Assumptions / limitations. The process starts in stationarity, and the analysis holds for every causal timing strategy and prespecified measurement schedule.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the exponential scaling of the observation cost for a driven ring, showing the analytic lower bound matching the achievable upper bound, and demonstrating the robustness of the test against hidden equilibrium controls.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the core problem by comparing non-equilibrium vs. equilibrium dynamics. It derives the exponential lower bound using information theory and then shows that this bound is achievable by a fixed, non-adaptive measurement protocol.

Abstract

A detector that cannot take two snapshots closer than a time $τ_0$ still identifies a fully observed finite Markov generator exactly, provided its gaps are randomized by two clocks. Identification is not detection. For a normal nonequilibrium network, the number of snapshots needed to reject every hidden equilibrium explanation grows as $e^{2γ_{\mathrm{irr}}τ_0}$, where $γ_{\mathrm{irr}}$ is the slowest damping of rotating modes. No adaptive schedule beats this rate, and a test on one dependent trajectory attains it.

Summary. This paper presents a novel witness-based method to quantify non-Markovianity in open quantum systems without requiring the complete reconstruction of the system's dynamical map. By focusing on positive-semidefinite witnesses, the authors establish a rigorous lower bound on established measures like the RHP measure. This approach offers an efficient and experimentally feasible diagnostic tool for quantum memory effects.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a crucial operational tool for characterizing quantum memory effects in open systems, making the theoretical concept of non-Markovianity experimentally accessible by reducing measurement requirements significantly.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Quantifying non-Markovianity, which is essential for exploiting environmental memory as a resource in quantum technologies.

Main result. The authors developed a witness-based framework that provides a certifiable lower bound on non-Markovianity measures (like the RHP measure) using only experimentally accessible correlation measurements.

Method. The framework uses positive-semidefinite witnesses (which act as POVM elements) to establish a quantitative hierarchy connecting witness expectations, the RHP measure, and the geometric distance measure, bypassing the need for full Choi state reconstruction.

Model / system. The analysis focuses on open quantum systems undergoing evolution described by CPTP dynamical maps, applied specifically to amplitude-damping and general Pauli dynamics.

Key observables. Witness expectation values, RHP measure, geometric distance measure, and specific correlation functions like Bell-parity projectors.

Important parameters / regimes. The time evolution parameter $t$, the environmental correlation time, and the structure of the dynamical map $\Lambda(t, t_0)$.

Assumptions / limitations. The framework is derived for dynamics generated by Lindblad-type master equations, and the focus is on positive-semidefinite witnesses for experimental feasibility.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the theoretical necessity of quantifying non-Markovianity, critiques existing methods for requiring full map knowledge, introduces the witness-based framework, proves the lower bound relationships, and demonstrates the method's application to specific physical models.

Abstract

Quantifying non-Markovianity is a fundamental problem in the theory of open quantum systems, as it is essential for exploiting environmental memory as a resource in quantum technologies. Existing resource-theoretic and operational quantifiers, however, generally require complete knowledge of the intermediate dynamical map and are therefore experimentally demanding. Here, we develop a witness-based framework for the quantification of non-Markovianity that bypasses complete reconstruction of the Choi state associated with the intermediate dynamical map. We first show that every non-Markovianity witness provides a certifiable lower bound on the geometric resource measure of non-Markovianity and identify the necessary and sufficient conditions under which this bound is tight. Motivated by the fact that non-positive-semidefinite witnesses are generally difficult to measure in real experimental scenario, we then focus on positive-semidefinite witnesses, which admit a natural implementation as positive operator valued measure (POVM) elements. We prove that every positive witness yields a certified lower bound on the Rivas--Huelga--Plenio (RHP) measure, thereby establishing a complete hierarchy connecting witness-based quantification, the RHP measure, and the geometric distance measure. Applying our framework to amplitude-damping and general Pauli dynamics, we find that the optimal positive witnesses reduce to simple Bell-parity projectors. Furthermore, we show that the resulting witness values can be obtained directly from a small number of experimentally accessible correlation measurements, thereby eliminating the need for complete Choi-state reconstruction and providing an efficient and experimentally feasible approach for the quantification of non-Markovianity in open quantum systems.

Summary. The paper introduces MASH-PLDM, an advanced surface hopping technique that extends the standard MASH formalism by using a partially linearized density matrix. This allows the method to accurately treat electronic coherences, leading to improved results for nonadiabatic dynamics. Crucially, it provides a pathway to simulate complex multi-time correlation functions required for nonlinear spectroscopy.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the simulation of open quantum systems and nonadiabatic processes crucial in molecular physics and quantum optics, providing an advanced, theoretically grounded trajectory-based method.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Developing a robust method to simulate nonadiabatic dynamics and calculate multi-time correlation functions in molecular systems.

Main result. The proposed MASH-PLDM method improves upon original MASH dynamics, particularly when electronic coherences are significant, and naturally extends to nonlinear spectroscopy measurements.

Method. It employs a partially linearized mapping approach (PLDM) applied to the surface hopping formalism, utilizing two distinct spin vectors to track both population on single surfaces and coherence on an average surface.

Model / system. The study focuses on nonadiabatic molecular dynamics, modeled using a two-level electronic system coupled to nuclear degrees of freedom, often analyzed via path integrals and spin vectors on the Bloch sphere.

Key observables. Time-dependent excited-state population, multi-time correlation functions (e.g., for time-resolved pump-probe spectroscopy).

Important parameters / regimes. The dynamics are governed by the electronic Hamiltonian and the coupling to nuclear motion, analyzed in the quasiclassical limit.

Assumptions / limitations. The core approximation is the Partially Linearized Density Matrix (PLDM), which linearizes nuclear variables into an average path while treating electronic spin vectors separately.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the PLDM approximation to correlation functions and the time evolution of the spin vector, showing precession and rotation dynamics.

Paper structure. The paper develops the MASH-PLDM formalism by applying the PLDM approximation to the path integral representation of the correlation function, deriving new equations of motion for the nuclei, and validating the method against standard MASH approaches.

Abstract

We derive a partially linearized version of the mapping approach to surface hopping (MASH) that provides a new method for simulating nonadiabatic dynamics in molecular systems. As in the original MASH formalism, trajectories travel on adiabatic surfaces with hops determined by the dynamics of a spin vector on the Bloch sphere. However, because the new approach is based on the partially linearized density matrix (PLDM), it has two spin vectors, which may be in the same or different hemispheres of the Bloch sphere. The former scenario describes an electronic population moving on a single adiabatic surface, whereas the latter describes a coherence moving on the average surface. We show that this new method, called MASH-PLDM, leads to improved results over the original MASH dynamics for a variety of scattering problems and spin-boson models, especially in cases where coherences play an important role. A further benefit of the new methodology is that it can treat multi-time correlation functions such as those required for nonlinear spectroscopy. We demonstrate this capability with a proof-of-principle simulation of time-resolved pump-probe spectroscopy.

Abstract

By generalizing the density matrices for pulsed Gaussian states of light, we can describe pulsed light of varying statistics in the same basis and relate the photocount rate to the pulse spectrum.

Ergodicity in a hole-dopped Anderson spin insulator

Summary. This work examines how doping a spin Anderson insulator with a single mobile hole affects its insulating nature in the presence of disorder. The authors find that weak disorder allows the hole to delocalize the spin system, but strong disorder pins the hole, thereby restoring the original Anderson insulating state. The transition is robustly confirmed using spectral, dynamical, and localization diagnostics.

Why it may be interesting. The interplay between charge dynamics (hole motion) and spin localization in a disordered environment provides a rich platform for studying how mobile excitations can fundamentally alter the nature of quantum phases, relevant to disordered quantum materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the stability of an Anderson insulator in the spin sector when coupled to a mobile charge degree of freedom (a single hole) via charge doping.

Main result. The hole acts as a bath that delocalizes the spin background in the weak disorder limit, but this delocalization is suppressed, restoring the Anderson insulator state, when the hole itself localizes in the strong disorder limit.

Method. The study employs theoretical analysis using the t-JXX model, analyzing real-time dynamics, spectral statistics (adjacent-gap ratio), and eigenstate localization diagnostics (fractal dimensions).

Model / system. The system is a one-dimensional lattice of two-component fermions described by a t-JXX model, incorporating hole hopping ($H_t$), antiferromagnetic exchange ($H_J$), and a random magnetic field ($H_h$).

Key observables. Localization length ($\xi$), spectral statistics ($\langle r angle$), participation entropies ($S_q$), and mean-square displacement ($\sigma^2(t)$).

Important parameters / regimes. Disorder strength ($W$), hole hopping amplitude ($t$), and antiferromagnetic coupling ($J$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is restricted to the single-hole sector, and the model is studied at the isotropic point $J/t=1$.

Figures summary. Figures show the crossover of spectral statistics ($\langle r angle$) from Wigner-Dyson (weak disorder) to Poissonian (strong disorder), and the transition of $\sigma^2(t)$ from diffusive ($\propto t$) to saturated (finite localization length).

Paper structure. The paper develops the model Hamiltonian, analyzes the weak disorder regime where hole motion induces spin delocalization, and contrasts this with the strong disorder regime where hole localization restores the insulating state, using multiple diagnostic tools to confirm the transition.

Abstract

An interacting many-body system being localized in the presence of disorder can fail to serve as its own bath. In an electronic system, the charge and spin degrees of freedom can act as thermal baths for each other. In this paper, we study the stability of an Anderson insulator in the spin sector with respect to charge doping. We consider one-dimensional two-component fermions in a lattice with large onsite repulsion ($t-J_{XX}$ model) and with a single hole in a random magnetic field. In the absence of the hole the spin sector is localized in arbitrarily weak random magnetic fields. We argue that a single hole thermalizes the spin chain in the weak disorder limit. In this regime our numerical results indicate ergodicity of the system as a whole and delocalization of the hole. At larger magnetic fields the hole eventually localizes with a finite localization length, and the Anderson spin insulator is restored.

Abstract

We theoretically investigate quantum correlations and energy storage in a single-electron silicon double quantum dot (eDQD) coupled to a single-mode microwave cavity. The charge and spin degrees of freedom are hybridized by Rashba spin-orbit coupling (SOC), while the cavity interacts with the eDQD through spin-photon and charge-photon couplings. Using the reduced thermal density matrix, we characterize spin-charge entanglement by concurrence and quantum coherence by the $l_1$ norm. At low temperature, the cavity strongly modifies the spin-charge correlations, producing distinct regimes of enhanced and suppressed entanglement. We identify a nonlinear crossover between an eDQD-dominated regime and a photon-dressed regime. This crossover is revealed independently by two signatures: a rapid change in the concurrence and the onset of finite photon occupation in the cavity. Its boundary exhibits a dominant $G_{\rm c}\propto\sqrtΩ$ dependence and is robust against cavity Hilbert-space truncation. Beyond the Rashba coupling studied in [Ferreira et al., Phys. Rev. A 107, 052408 (2023)], we show that the cavity provides an additional tunable means of controlling quantum correlations and energy storage. We further characterize the eDQD as a quantum battery under coherent charging and evaluate its stored energy and ergotropy. The ergotropy varies markedly across a cavity-dressing crossover resembling that identified from entanglement, connecting cavity-induced modification of the dressed states to extractable work. Optimization over the charge-photon coupling reveals parameter-dependent optimal charging regimes and enhanced maximum ergotropy with increasing Rashba coupling. Our results establish cavity coupling and SOC as complementary controls of quantum correlations and extractable energy in semiconductor eDQD-cavity systems.

Abstract

Abstract

Efficient end-to-end entanglement distribution in quantum information networks requires routing under limited resources and fidelity constraints. We study entanglement routing as a fidelity-constrained unsplittable multicommodity flow problem that maximizes the number of admitted requests. As a proof of concept, we integrate neutral-atom quantum optimization into a hybrid classical--quantum column-generation framework. A classical restricted master problem selects routes, while a pricing problem generates fidelity-feasible paths. We formulate this NP-hard constrained shortest-path pricing problem as a quadratic unconstrained binary optimization (QUBO) problem and address it with hardware-aware register embedding and instance-driven pulse shaping. To our knowledge, this is the first study of neutral-atom quantum optimization as a pricing oracle for fidelity-constrained entanglement routing. On small benchmark instances evaluated with neutral-atom processor emulators, the method, combined with warm-start and post-processing, achieves an optimality gap below 1% across all tested sizes; the selected simulated-annealing route-generation baseline exhibits gaps of up to 6%. These results indicate that the generated bitstrings can provide useful candidate routes for classical refinement within column generation. This proof of concept does not establish quantum utility or scalability; further evaluation on larger instances, against stronger classical baselines, and on quantum hardware is needed.
