Closing a single-copy gap to the Holevo bound with two-copy measurements

Authors: Changhao Li
Type: both · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.36874v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: 🔥 QC/QI experiment 4/5 · quantum measurements 3/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper tackles the fundamental question of how close finite quantum measurements can get to the ultimate theoretical precision limit (the Holevo bound). By constructing a specific, rank-deficient model, the authors prove that the gap between single-copy and two-copy estimates can close. They validate this theoretically derived advantage experimentally using superconducting qubits, showing the two-copy measurement significantly outperforms single-copy estimates even when noise is present.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the fundamental limits of quantum measurement precision, providing concrete physical examples (superconducting qubits) where collective measurements can surpass single-shot estimates, linking fundamental theory (model rank) to experimental capability.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the finite-copy gap in quantum multi-parameter estimation, specifically comparing the single-copy optimum bound to the ultimate Holevo bound.

Main result. The authors disprove the universal persistence of this gap beyond two parameters by constructing a rank-deficient three-parameter model where the two-copy measurement achieves the Holevo bound. Experimentally, they demonstrate a two-copy advantage over single-copy bounds using superconducting qubits.

Method. The study combines theoretical proofs regarding model rank deficiency with experimental implementation using superconducting transmon qubits to measure estimation costs under controlled noise.

Model / system. The theoretical model is a three-parameter, rank-two quantum estimation model. Experimentally, the platform uses superconducting transmon qubits on an IBM processor, implementing a hybrid two-transmon protocol.

Key observables. The primary observables are the estimation error costs: the single-copy optimum ($C^{(1)}_{opt}$), the two-copy measurement cost ($C^{(2)}_{exp}$), and the theoretical Holevo bound ($CH$).

Important parameters / regimes. The key parameters include the number of parameters (three), the number of copies (two), and the injected depolarizing noise strength ($\epsilon$).

Assumptions / limitations. The theoretical analysis distinguishes between rank-deficient and full-rank models, where rank deficiency is shown to be necessary for the gap closure. Experimentally, the analysis relies on readout error mitigation techniques.

Figures summary. Figure 1 compares the measured two-copy cost below the single-copy and Holevo bounds, showing the advantage persists over a finite noise range. Figure S2 tracks the difference between the single-copy lower bound and measured costs across various injected noise levels.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the theoretical framework by constructing a specific rank-deficient model to close the gap at two copies. It then proves that full-rank models maintain the gap for any finite copy number. The latter part details the experimental realization on superconducting qubits, measuring the advantage and its robustness against controlled depolarization.

Abstract

The Holevo Cramér-Rao bound sets the asymptotically attainable precision of quantum multiparameter estimation, but its attainability with finite quantum state copies remains unresolved in general. While a gap between the single-copy optimum and the Holevo bound persists at any finite copy number for two-parameter estimation models, the higher-parameter case has remained open. Here we disprove universal finite-copy gap persistence beyond two parameters by constructing a three-parameter rank-two model whose Holevo bound is unattainable with one copy but attained exactly with two. We further prove finite-copy persistence for full-rank models with any number of parameters. We then experimentally implement the two-copy collective measurement for the constructed model using superconducting qubits. We observe a two-copy estimation error below the single-copy and separate-measurement bounds determined from state tomography. By introducing controlled depolarization, we show that this precision advantage persists over a finite noise range. Our results connect the attainability of ultimate quantum precision to the structure of the multiparameter estimation model and the resources available for collective measurements.

Generalized Effective Spin-Chain formalism for multicomponent anyons in one-dimensional optical lattices

Authors: Sagarika Basak, Xi-Wen Guan, Han Pu

Summary. This paper introduces the Generalized Effective Spin-Chain (GESC) formalism to study multicomponent anyons in 1D optical lattices. The method achieves a spin-charge separation, allowing researchers to map complex anyonic interactions onto an effective spin chain. It reveals that fractional statistics govern transport dynamics through virtual spin exchanges, offering a computationally efficient alternative to simulating the full, intractable model.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, computationally efficient theoretical tool (GESC) to analyze exotic quantum statistics (anyons) in highly correlated systems, directly relevant to ultracold atomic simulations in optical lattices.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops a generalized effective spin-chain (GESC) formalism to understand how fractional exchange statistics are preserved in strongly interacting multicomponent anyons confined to a one-dimensional optical lattice.

Main result. The GESC formalism successfully separates spin and charge dynamics, showing that while the charge Hamiltonian is insensitive to the statistical phase, the phase manifests in spin-exchange coefficients, leading to direction-dependent, inversion-asymmetric transport out of equilibrium.

Method. The authors use a perturbative expansion in the strong-interaction limit to map the complex Anyon-Hubbard model onto an effective spin-chain Hamiltonian, achieving a spin-charge separation perspective.

Model / system. The system models multicomponent anyons (fermion-type and boson-type) in a 1D optical lattice, governed by generalized Anyon-Hubbard Hamiltonians. The core methodology involves mapping particle motion to spinless fermions (charge) and spin states to an ordered spin chain.

Key observables. Spin-exchange coefficients ($C_\ell( heta, t)$), impurity density profiles ($\langle n_{\ell,\downarrow} angle$), and the Root Mean Square (RMS) displacement ($W(t)$).

Important parameters / regimes. The statistical phase ($ heta$), the ratio of interaction energy to tunneling energy (strong-interaction regime), and the component identity/interaction anisotropy.

Assumptions / limitations. The derivation relies on a perturbative expansion in the strong-interaction limit, and the initial analysis assumes the statistical phase $ heta$ is spin-independent.

Figures summary. Figures benchmark the GESC formalism against the full Anyon-Hubbard model for impurity density and $W(t)$ across various statistical phases ($ heta$), demonstrating quantitative agreement while showing significant computational savings.

Paper structure. The paper systematically introduces the multicomponent Anyon-Hubbard models, develops the GESC formalism via perturbative decoupling, analyzes ground-state correlations, and finally investigates dynamic processes like impurity transport.

Abstract

We develop a generalized effective spin-chain (GESC) formalism for strongly interacting multicomponent anyons in a one-dimensional (1D) optical lattice. By mapping particle motion onto spinless fermions and spin states onto an ordered chain, this framework provides a spin--charge-separated perspective on the physical effects of fractional exchange statistics. In the strong-interaction regime, the leading-order charge Hamiltonian becomes independent of the statistical phase; instead, virtual tunneling through doubly occupied states directly imprints this phase on the spin-exchange coefficients. The GESC formalism captures ground-state properties of the full Anyon--Hubbard model reported in S.~Basak, X.-W.~Guan, and H.~Pu, unpublished manuscript (2026), and reveals that their statistical dependence is predominantly encoded in the explicit anyonic structure of the observables rather than the underlying spin ground state. This formalism uncovers that, out of equilibrium, successive spin exchanges accumulate direction-dependent phases; the resulting interference drives crossover from dispersive to localized impurity transport and generates inversion-asymmetric propagation at intermediate statistics. Suppressed expansion persists for identical and distinguishable impurities, with differences in propagation governed by the interplay between impurity identity and interaction anisotropy. Ultimately, GESC offers a versatile, computationally efficient theoretical framework that connects macroscopic dynamics to microscopic virtual exchange processes, conferring a unique spin--charge-separated vantage for resolving how fractional exchange statistics survives strong interactions and manifests distinctly across static and dynamical regimes.

Finite-momentum trimers and Cooper quartets in a one-dimensional Fermi gas with coexistent $s$- and $p$-wave interactions

Authors: Yixin Guo
Type: theory · Category: quantum gases · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.37032v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: 🔥 analog quantum simulation 4/5 · Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 2/5

Summary. This paper theoretically examines the formation and binding of Cooper quartets and trimers in a 1D Fermi gas subjected to both s-wave and p-wave interactions. Using variational methods, the authors demonstrate that the quartet binding can be robustly strong, even when considering the competition from other bound states. The findings highlight that the binding mechanism is complex, depending on more than just the strongest pairwise interaction.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses complex many-body physics in ultracold atomic gases, specifically the interplay between different pairing symmetries (s-wave vs. p-wave) and the formation of exotic few-body states like quartets.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the competition and coexistence of different pairing channels (s-wave and p-wave) and the formation of multi-particle clusters (trimers and Cooper quartets) in a Fermi gas.

Main result. The analysis shows that the Cooper quartet binding can be significantly stronger than the trimer binding, and that the binding is not determined solely by the strongest pair interaction or the dominant interaction energy.

Method. The research employs a variational approach to minimize the energy of the multi-particle clusters (trimers and quartets) within the framework of a generalized Cooper theory.

Model / system. The system is a one-dimensional two-component Fermi gas described by a Hamiltonian including kinetic energy and coexistent s-wave and p-wave attraction terms. Interactions are regularized in the relative momentum to maintain Galilean invariance.

Key observables. Binding energy of the quartet relative to its breakup channels, trimer dispersion, and density dependence of binding.

Important parameters / regimes. Coexistence of s- and p-wave interactions, finite total momentum of clusters, and varying interspecies attraction strength.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes equal densities ($k_{F,a} = k_{F,b}$) and is performed above an inert Fermi sea; the variational energy provides an upper bound on the true energy.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate weak-coupling binding scales for the quartet versus the trimer, and show the momentum dependence of trimer and trimer-atom energies at various coupling strengths.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining the model Hamiltonian and using variational methods to calculate cluster energies, analyzing the hierarchy of binding energies, and finally comparing in-vacuum versus in-medium (finite density) results.

Abstract

We study finite-momentum trimers and Cooper quartets in a one-dimensional two-component Fermi gas with coexistent $s$- and $p$-wave interactions, regularized in the relative momentum of each pair so that they remain Galilean invariant. Pauli blocking alone then makes the trimer dispersion deviate from free center-of-mass motion, so the trimer energy can be lowest at finite total momentum. Allowing this motion expands the $abb$ region among pairs and trimers and removes the region where an odd-wave pair is lowest. The $aabb$ quartet lies below all these branches at every sampled coupling, and in a strip starting on the crossing of the two dimer-pair channels its lowest breakup channel is a moving $abb$ trimer and an atom. At weak interspecies attraction, a variational bound shows that two odd-wave Cooper pairs bind into a quartet, whose binding exceeds that of the coexisting trimer by orders of magnitude as the attraction weakens. At stronger interspecies attraction, the quartet binding relative to two unlike-species dimers disappears when the odd-wave interaction is switched off, although the even-wave interaction dominates the interaction energy. Relative to the in-vacuum case, the Fermi sea enhances the weak-coupling quartet binding, and at fixed scattering lengths the quartet binding grows with density while the coexisting trimer becomes much shallower. These results show that neither the most strongly bound pair nor the dominant interaction energy alone determines whether the quartet binds.

Margenau-Hill distribution as a Necessary and Sufficient Signature of Measurement Incompatibility

Summary. This paper establishes that the positivity of the Margenau-Hill quasi-probability distribution serves as a necessary and sufficient condition for two unsharp quantum measurements to be jointly measurable. The authors derive the mathematical criteria and propose an experimental protocol using an interferometer to directly measure this distribution, providing a powerful test for quantum incompatibility.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete, experimentally testable diagnostic tool (the MH distribution) to quantify quantum incompatibility, bridging abstract mathematical concepts with physical measurement limitations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to establish a rigorous, operational link between the concept of measurement incompatibility and the mathematical positivity of the Margenau-Hill (MH) quasi-probability distribution for dichotomic observables.

Main result. It is proven that for unsharp dichotomic measurements, the positivity of the MH distribution is necessary and sufficient for the measurements to be jointly measurable (i.e., compatible).

Method. The authors derive joint measurability conditions by analyzing the positivity of the MH distribution, which is then experimentally tested using a modified Mach-Zehnder interferometer setup.

Model / system. The study focuses on general quantum systems described by density matrices, specifically analyzing pairs (and generalizations to $n$) of dichotomic observables in an arbitrary finite dimension $d$.

Key observables. The Margenau-Hill (MH) quasi-probability distribution, $q(a_1, a_2)$, and the expectation value $\langle P_\Psi angle$ measured in an interferometer.

Important parameters / regimes. The dimension of the Hilbert space ($d$), and the parameters ($\lambda, \mu$) defining the unsharpness of the measurements.

Assumptions / limitations. The equivalence between MH positivity and joint measurability is rigorously proven for two dichotomic unsharp observables; the generalization to $n$ observables relies on the assumption that they are mutually anticommuting.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the interferometric setup, inspired by quantum-switch architectures, designed to directly measure the MH quasi-probability distribution.

Paper structure. The paper first derives the general joint measurability condition for two observables, then proves the equivalence between MH positivity and joint measurability, generalizes this to $n$ observables, and concludes by detailing the experimental realization of the test using interferometry.

Abstract

Measurement incompatibility and the negativity of quasi-probability distributions both demonstrate the signature of nonclassicality. However, their relations largely remained qualitative, and no explicit operational connection has been established. We provide an operational link between measurement incompatibility and the Margenau-Hill(MH) quasi-probability distribution associated with two dichotomic observables. { We first derive the joint measurability (measurement compatibility) condition for any pair of dichotomic observables in arbitrary finite dimension $d$.} We then rigorously prove that for any pair of unsharp dichotomic measurements in dimension $d$, the positivity of the MH distribution is equivalent to joint measurability \emph{i.e.}, the MH distribution is positive \emph{if and only if} the measurements are jointly measurable. We further introduce the MH-like quasi-probability distribution for $n$ dichotomic unsharp observables in arbitrary finite dimension and derive a sufficient condition of joint-measurability when the observables are mutually anticommuting. Finally, we propose an interferometric setup, inspired by quantum-switch architectures, that directly reconstructs MH quasi-probability which in turn provides a test of incompatibility.

Abstract

We demonstrate proximity-induced localization and re-entrant delocalization in a detuned quasiperiodic ladder. In the weak-quasiperiodic regime, where uncoupled Aubry-André chains are extended, a staggered detuning applied to only one leg induces localization in the neighboring leg through inter-leg hybridization. With further increasing detuning, the pure chain re-enters a delocalized regime, producing a delocalized-localized-delocalized sequence without directly modifying its quasiperiodic potential. We identify these regimes using static localization diagnostics and demonstrate experimentally accessible dynamical signatures on IBM Quantum hardware: strongly suppressed wavefunction spreading in the proximity-localized regime and its re-entrant recovery at larger detuning. These results establish leg-selective detuning as a programmable control knob for inducing and reversing localization in coupled quasiperiodic systems.

Exponential Advantage of Quantum over Classical References in Leakage Detection

Authors: Zheng An
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.36541v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: 🔥 quantum measurements 4/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces a method for detecting quantum leakage from an unknown encoding subspace using multiple independent quantum references measured coherently. It rigorously proves that this quantum approach is exponentially superior to classical measurement-first protocols, especially when the encoding structure is unknown. This advances the understanding of quantum resource utilization in quantum information security and verification.

Why it may be interesting. The focus on quantum reference measurements to overcome the need for classical knowledge of the encoding subspace is highly relevant to quantum error correction and quantum communication protocols where full state tomography or classical description is impossible.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of detecting quantum leakage from an encoding subspace when the subspace itself is not classically specified.

Main result. A coherent measurement using independent quantum references provides an optimal detection law with a sample advantage exponential in the number of physical qubits compared to measurement-first classical protocols.

Method. The authors derive detection laws by comparing the performance of a coherent joint measurement (quantum) against a sequential measurement-first (classical) protocol.

Model / system. The model involves quantum information processing where a message is encoded into a subspace, and leakage is defined as population outside this subspace. The comparison is made between coherent reference measurements and classical POVM measurements.

Key observables. Detection probability ($D_0$), false alarm rate ($\epsilon$), miss probability, and the required number of quantum references ($M$).

Important parameters / regimes. Number of references ($M$), ambient dimension ($d$), and tolerance ($\epsilon$).

Assumptions / limitations. The coherent check is assumed to be optimal among all tests with zero false alarm for every encoding, and the analysis often assumes ideal reference operations or specific noise models (e.g., local Markov Pauli channels).

Figures summary. Figures compare the required number of references ($M$) for quantum vs. measurement-first approaches, demonstrating an exponential advantage for the coherent check, and illustrate the process flow difference between the two detection methods.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first defining the problem of leakage detection without classical knowledge of the subspace. It then derives the optimal detection law for the coherent measurement, contrasts this with the limitations of measurement-first receivers, and quantifies the resulting exponential quantum advantage.

Abstract

Leakage from a known encoding subspace can be detected by projection. However, the corresponding projector is unavailable when the encoding subspace is not classically specified. Here we show how independent quantum references prepared by the same encoder enable leakage detection without a classical description of the encoding. A coherent measurement on the references and a single message leaves every state within the two-dimensional encoding subspace, including its entanglement with a remote system, exactly unchanged. We derive the exact detection law for $M$ ideal references and prove optimality among tests with zero false alarm for every encoding. For orthogonal leakage, the miss probability is asymptotic to $4/M$, independent of the ambient dimension $d$. Measuring all references first, even collectively, gives zero detection at every finite budget under the same zero-false-alarm requirement. For a fixed detection target between zero and one, the optimal measurement-first cost is $Θ(d/ε)$ at sufficiently small tolerance $ε$ on normal false alarm and conditional disturbance; a dimension-independent coherent budget suffices. For one logical qubit encoded in three physical qubits, seven coherent references achieve at least $50\%$ orthogonal-leakage detection, whereas any measurement-first receiver needs at least $588$ under the same $1\%$ normal tolerances. Quantum references thus support leakage checks without classical reconstruction, with a sample advantage exponential in the number of physical qubits.

Fast Hamiltonian engineering from cut polytope geometry

Authors: Thomas Joachim Friese, Özgün Kum, Aram W. Harrow, Martin Kliesch
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.36006v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: 🔥 analog quantum simulation 4/5

Summary. This work presents a unified, mathematically rigorous framework for Hamiltonian Engineering, solving the problem of simulating a target quantum evolution using limited native controls. By reformulating the problem as an optimization over a polytope, the authors develop an efficient algorithm that achieves provable, near-optimal quantum run times. This establishes a powerful tool for the automatic design and programming of analog quantum simulators across various quantum systems.

Why it may be interesting. It provides rigorous, provable guarantees for the automatic programming of analog quantum simulators by establishing a concrete, efficient optimization procedure for Hamiltonian decomposition.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses Hamiltonian Engineering (HE), which is the task of simulating a target quantum evolution ($H_{ ext{target}}$) using a native system Hamiltonian ($H_{ ext{native}}$) and controlled pulses.

Main result. The authors provide a unified framework that reformulates time-optimal HE as an NP-complete polytope problem, solving it via an elliptope relaxation and yielding an approximation ratio of $O(\sqrt{m})$ for quantum run times.

Method. The core method involves reformulating the problem as a complex $k$-cut polytope optimization, relaxing it to an elliptope, and solving the resulting Linear Program (LP) using informed and mixed pulse sets.

Model / system. The framework is applicable to broad classes of 2-local qubit, qudit, and fermionic systems, aiming to decompose the target Hamiltonian into a sum of controlled conjugations of the native Hamiltonian.

Key observables. Optimal Quantum Run Time, Approximation Ratio, Feasibility of the LP solution.

Important parameters / regimes. Number of interaction terms ($m$), number of distinct phases ($k$), and the required pulse weights ($\lambda$).

Assumptions / limitations. The initial formulation is NP-hard, necessitating relaxation to the elliptope; the mixed LP algorithm is used to guarantee feasibility against finite pulse time errors.

Figures summary. Benchmarks are shown on a fully connected Ising model, a chiral clock model for qudits, and the fermionic Harper-Hofstadter model, demonstrating near-optimal run times.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the HE problem, proves its NP-hardness, relaxes it to an elliptope, develops the informed and mixed LP algorithms, and concludes with benchmarks comparing the approach to state-of-the-art methods.

Abstract

Hamiltonian engineering (HE) simulates quantum dynamics under a target Hamiltonian using a native entangling system Hamiltonian and restricted control, with applications from quantum gate design to analog quantum simulation. Observing that, for many systems, formulating HE as a linear program only requires specific commutation relations between the control pulses and the system Hamiltonian, we provide a unified framework for broad classes of $2$-local qubit, qudit, and fermionic systems. We reformulate time-optimal HE as a complex $k$-cut polytope problem, with $k$ the number of distinct phases in these commutation relations, and prove it NP-complete for every finite $k$. We therefore relax the polytope to the elliptope and apply a Krivine-type rounding, yielding pulses informed by the system and target Hamiltonians, which we then use in a linear program. We derive how many informed pulses are necessary and sufficient for the linear program to be reliably feasible. Additionally mixing in uniformly sampled pulses guarantees a solution close to the relaxation value from $\mathrm{O}(m)$ pulses, with $m$ the number of interaction terms. Together with upper and lower bounds on the optimal quantum run time, this yields an $\mathrm{O}(\sqrt{m})$ approximation ratio. In benchmarks on a fully connected Ising model, a chiral clock model for qudits, and the fermionic Harper-Hofstadter model, our approach attains near-optimal quantum run times wherever the optimum is computable, reaches run times that saturate with the lattice size in the fermionic case, and outperforms state-of-the-art methods with $\mathrm{O}(m)$ pulses. This establishes a unified approach with provable guarantees to the automatic programming of analog quantum simulators.

Abstract

We investigate the ground-state and dynamical properties of multicomponent interacting anyons confined in a one-dimensional (1D) optical lattice. Adopting the Anyon--Hubbard model, we explore spin and charge correlations as functions of interaction strength and the anyonic statistical parameter. Abstract

This dissertation investigates the transport of Brownian particles driven by active fluctuations in periodic potential landscapes. Unlike thermal noise, active fluctuations break detailed balance and violate the fluctuation-dissipation theorem. They model self-propulsion mechanisms and active-bath interactions in biological and synthetic micro-systems. By focusing on white Poisson shot noise with non-Gaussian amplitude statistics, this work systematically uncovers the mechanisms governing three major transport anomalies: giant transport enhancement, absolute negative mobility, and the ratchet effect. Combining Monte Carlo simulations with analytical modeling, this work demonstrates that active fluctuations adhering to a bidirectional skew-normal amplitude distribution enhance directed velocity compared to the free-particle by several orders of magnitude. This giant transport enhancement is fully quantified via a phenomenological jump-relaxation model that decouples particle displacement into instantaneous stochastic jumps and deterministic relaxation towards potential minimum. Extending this model to inertial dynamics reveals that finite particle mass m acts as a dynamic control parameter capable of strengthening, weakening, constructively inducing, or completely suppressing transport enhancement depending on noise variance. Furthermore, an ultra-minimal model for absolute negative mobility is established in a one-dimensional, purely overdamped system, showing that discontinuous shot noise drives transport opposite to an applied bias without requiring particle inertia, nonlinearity of spatially periodic potential, or explicit time-dependent periodic driving. Conversely, under an alternative scaling of the average stochastic force, an asymmetric distribution of fluctuation amplitudes generates net directed transport through a tilting ratchet effect in the zero-bias limit.

Abstract

Topological phases of matter support edge states protected from noise and perturbations, and the classification of which symmetry and dimension support such phases was developed for quantum systems and analogous platforms. While topological phases have also been discovered in stochastic systems and posited as mechanisms for biochemical processes, a classification consistent with their Markovian constraints remains lacking, impeding generalization to new scenarios.
