Minimal building blocks for molecular quantum circuits with exact spin symmetry

Authors: Mengwei Liu, Zhenyu Li
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.37101v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper theoretically determines the minimal quantum operations needed to achieve full controllability (generating all state-space rotations) in molecular quantum circuits. It establishes that the required complexity depends on the electronic sector, needing two orbitals in the interior but three on certain boundaries. This has direct implications for designing efficient and complete quantum algorithms for molecular simulations.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides fundamental theoretical constraints on the gate set required for simulating molecular electronic structure, directly impacting the resource estimation for quantum chemistry algorithms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper determines the minimal set of additional quantum operations needed, beyond simple orbital rotations, to ensure that a quantum circuit can generate every possible real state-space rotation within a fixed molecular electronic subspace.

Main result. The minimum number of orbitals required for this additional generator is two in the interior of the subspace and three on nontrivial boundaries, achievable using only one- and two-electron terms.

Method. The analysis uses structural questions from Lie algebra theory to classify the required generators, comparing the generated algebra to the target dynamical Lie algebra $\mathfrak{so}(d)$.

Model / system. The study models nonrelativistic, spin-free molecular Hamiltonians analyzed in fixed subspaces defined by particle number ($N$), total spin ($S$), and spin projection ($M_S$).

Key observables. The required minimum number of spatial orbitals (2 vs. 3), and the resulting ability to generate the full dynamical Lie algebra $\mathfrak{so}(d)$.

Important parameters / regimes. Fixed quantum numbers ($N, S, M_S$), the distinction between interior and maximal-spin boundaries, and the use of one- and two-electron terms.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the system is spin-free and the Hamiltonian is real, and the results are structural, independent of the specific molecular Hamiltonian.

Figures summary. Not specified (Notes focus on theoretical results and comparisons rather than figure descriptions).

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first defining the problem in terms of generating the full state-space rotation group, then analyzing the interior and boundary sectors separately to derive the minimum orbital support required for the necessary additional generators.

Abstract

Preserving particle number and spin helps quantum circuits target molecular electronic states, but does not guarantee access to every state with the required quantum numbers. We determine which additional operations, combined with spin-independent orbital rotations connecting all spatial orbitals, generate every real state-space rotation within each complete subspace of fixed particle number $N$, total spin $S$, and spin projection $M_S$. We consider spin-free molecular calculations in real orbitals, without an additional spatial-symmetry restriction, and continuously tunable operations that preserve particle number, full spin symmetry, and real amplitudes. Below the maximal-spin limits set by the electron and hole numbers, repeated singlet-pair transfer between any two fixed spatial orbitals is sufficient. On nontrivial maximal-spin boundaries, pair transfer vanishes, and an additional generator built from one- and two-electron terms is sufficient exactly when its action in the target subspace is not a linear combination of orbital-rotation generators. The minimum number of spatial orbitals needed by one additional generator is two in the interior and three on nontrivial boundaries, even when terms involving more than two electrons are allowed. Both minima are attained using only one- and two-electron terms. The three-orbital optimum rotates two orbitals according to the occupation of a third, and its unitary factors exactly into eight commuting Pauli rotations. Molecular benchmarks show that this operation removes the observed boundary energy-error plateaus, while sparse interior constructions attain the prescribed energy accuracy with fewer compiled CNOT gates in selected fixed-orbital comparisons.

Quantum Computing for Network Security Classification: Near-Term Classification and Long-Term Memory Efficiency

Authors: Yuqing Li, Poonam Bala Nehru, Yunpeng Zhang, Danindu Gammanpilage, Xin Jin, Zeguan Wu, Junyu Liu

Summary. This paper assesses the utility of quantum computing for network security classification by examining two aspects: immediate performance and future resource scaling. Near-term results show quantum kernels are sometimes competitive but not universally superior to classical methods. More significantly, the work demonstrates that quantum methods may offer a substantial memory advantage for handling massive, streaming datasets compared to explicit classical storage models.

Why it may be interesting. While the core application is in cybersecurity, the methodology involves quantum feature mapping, kernel methods, and resource estimation (memory scaling), which are relevant theoretical tools in quantum information processing.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper systematically investigates how quantum computing can contribute to network-security classification, analyzing both near-term performance gains and long-term memory efficiency.

Main result. Quantum kernels are competitive but task-dependent for near-term classification, while the primary long-term advantage lies in memory-efficient data access via Quantum Oracle Sketching (QOS).

Method. The study employs two complementary methods: evaluating quantum-kernel Support Vector Machines (SVMs) on standard datasets for near-term testing, and using Quantum Oracle Sketching (QOS) to estimate memory requirements for streaming data.

Model / system. The model involves applying quantum feature mapping (via angle encoding of SVD coordinates) to classical network-security data (KDD Cup 1999, CICIDS2017, BoT-IoT) within the framework of quantum-kernel SVMs.

Key observables. Classification metrics (ROC-AUC, AP, F1 score) for near-term; Effective memory-size proxy ($S_{QOS}$) compared to classical storage ($S_{sparse}$) for long-term.

Important parameters / regimes. Dataset choice, kernel type (quantum fidelity vs. classical RBF), feature filtering threshold ($\min df$), and qubit counts (for ablation studies).

Assumptions / limitations. The near-term comparison assumes the data pipeline and SVM objective are fixed, isolating the kernel's contribution. The long-term analysis is an estimate-based comparison of memory size, not a runtime speedup claim.

Figures summary. Figure 2 shows ROC-AUC ablation across different kernel choices; Figure 3 compares the required machine size (QOS vs. Classical streaming/sparse) versus accuracy for the memory efficiency analysis.

Paper structure. The paper is structured around two main experiments: first, a near-term comparison of quantum vs. classical SVM kernels on multiple datasets; second, a long-term analysis using QOS to estimate memory advantages for streaming data access.

Abstract

Quantum computing has already been explored in several network-security applications. However, how quantum computing may contribute to network-security classification in both the near term and the longer term has not been systematically discussed. This paper studies this question through two complementary experiments. First, we evaluate near-term quantum-kernel support vector machines (SVMs) on practical network-security classification tasks and compare them with classical SVM baselines on KDD Cup 1999, CICIDS2017, and BoT-IoT. Across these runs, quantum kernels are competitive. They can match or improve classical baselines in some settings, while classical RBF kernels remain stronger in others. This suggests that near-term quantum-kernel methods should be evaluated as practical, dataset-dependent alternatives to classical kernels rather than as uniformly superior replacements. Second, we use quantum oracle sketching (QOS) to study a longer-term memory advantage for classification with streaming classical samples. In QOS, samples are processed online and used to incrementally construct an approximate quantum oracle, which provides coherent query access for downstream quantum algorithms without retaining the entire dataset. Under the QOS-inspired machine-size estimate, comparable accuracy corresponds to a substantially smaller effective memory-size proxy than explicit sparse/QRAM-style storage. Compared with a simple streaming proxy, the result is more nuanced because aggressive feature filtering can make the streaming dimension small. This suggests that the long-term value of quantum computing for network-security classification may lie in memory-efficient data access rather than immediate runtime speedup. Together, these experiments show how quantum computing may contribute to network-security classification from near-term classification performance and longer-term memory efficiency.

Quantum Fidelity Landscape-Guided Prior Calibration for Single-Circuit QGAN Image Generation

Summary. This paper introduces BasicQGAN, a novel QGAN framework for single-circuit image generation that avoids patch-based decomposition. It leverages the Quantum Fidelity Landscape (QFL)—a measure of pairwise quantum fidelity—to theoretically constrain and calibrate the quantum prior before adversarial training. Experiments show this calibration leads to stable, high-quality image synthesis using fewer quantum resources.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a rigorous geometric constraint (QFL) derived from quantum information theory to stabilize the training of complex quantum machine learning models, which is highly relevant for understanding resource limitations in NISQ devices.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To improve Quantum Generative Adversarial Networks (QGANs) for end-to-end image generation by overcoming the limitations of patch-based methods.

Main result. The proposed BasicQGAN framework, which incorporates Quantum Fidelity Landscape (QFL) calibration, achieves stable and effective pixel-level image generation with fewer resources than patch-based alternatives.

Method. The authors introduce the QFL, a theoretical structure based on pairwise quantum fidelity, and propose an offline calibration step to align the prior-induced QFL with the data-induced QFL before adversarial training.

Model / system. The model is a single-circuit QGAN operating in the NISQ era, utilizing an initial state ensemble $\{ ho_i\}$ whose structure is governed by the QFL. The generation process involves a shared unitary transformation and a fixed amplitude readout.

Key observables. Quantum Fidelity Landscape (QFL), Squared Uhlmann fidelity, and the resulting bound on decoded sample separation.

Important parameters / regimes. The required number of qubits and trainable parameters; the learning rate used during QFL calibration (e.g., 0.3).

Assumptions / limitations. The approach assumes a fixed Lipschitz readout to derive the output-separation bound, and the core assumption is that the QFL captures the necessary structural information for stable training.

Figures summary. Figure 1 compares QFL histograms for initial, generated, and true sample ensembles. Figure 2 illustrates the two-stage BasicQGAN process: offline QFL calibration followed by online adversarial training. Appendix I shows FID curves and sample generations across various datasets.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the QFL theory, derives bounds relating fidelity to output separation, proposes the BasicQGAN framework with an offline calibration stage, and validates the method through extensive experiments on multiple grayscale image datasets.

Abstract

Quantum Generative Adversarial Networks (QGANs) have emerged as representative generative models in the Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) era and have attracted increasing attention in quantum machine learning. However, most existing QGAN methods rely on patch-based decomposition strategies, which weaken the global consistency of generated images and increase quantum resource overhead. In this work, we investigate a simpler approach: pixel-level, end-to-end image generation using a single-quantum-circuit QGAN. By analyzing the structural matching relationship between the quantum prior and the target data distribution in Hilbert space, we provide a new theoretical perspective for understanding the training behavior of naive end-to-end QGANs. Specifically, we introduce the Quantum Fidelity Landscape (QFL), defined as the pairwise-fidelity structure induced by an ensemble of quantum states and preserved under shared unitary transformations of the quantum generation process. We show that, under a fixed Lipschitz readout, this invariant imposes a one-sided bound on decoded sample separation, motivating calibration of the prior-induced QFL before adversarial training. To validate this theoretical insight, we propose BasicQGAN, a QGAN framework incorporating quantum prior calibration. Before adversarial optimization, BasicQGAN aligns the prior-induced QFL with the data-induced QFL. Experimental results on small-scale grayscale image datasets show that BasicQGAN achieves stable and effective end-to-end pixel-level image generation while requiring fewer qubits and trainable parameters than representative patch-based quantum generators. Furthermore, experiments with different initial quantum-state ensembles show that QFL-calibrated ensembles achieve better generative performance.

Quantum Leakage Resilience of Shamir Secret Sharing

Authors: Rishabh Batra, Fuyuki Kitagawa, Ryo Nishimaki, Takashi Yamakawa
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.37276v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

Summary. This work analyzes the security of Shamir's secret sharing scheme against quantum leakage attacks. Using advanced Fourier analysis on finite fields, the authors derive critical threshold rates required to ensure that the leaked quantum information is insufficient for an adversary to distinguish between possible secrets. The findings establish quantitative limits on the resilience of secret sharing in the presence of quantum side channels.

Why it may be interesting. It provides rigorous, information-theoretic bounds on the security of cryptographic primitives (secret sharing) against sophisticated quantum side-channel attacks, which is fundamental to quantum information security.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the quantum leakage resilience of Shamir's secret sharing scheme, analyzing how easily an adversary can distinguish between two secrets based on leaked quantum information.

Main result. Security is maintained if the threshold rate $t/n$ exceeds a critical threshold $ au^\star \approx 0.73339$ when no entanglement is shared, and security can be extended to linear entanglement support under high threshold rates.

Method. The analysis employs finite-field Fourier analysis, bounding the distinguishing advantage using properties of the dual MDS code and techniques like the MDS Brascamp-Lieb inequality.

Model / system. The model is Shamir's secret sharing over prime fields, where the leakage is modeled as a local quantum channel outputting qubits from each share.

Key observables. Distinguishing advantage ($ ext{Adv}$), threshold rate ($ au = t/n$), and the critical threshold ($ au^\star$).

Important parameters / regimes. Threshold rate $ au$, field size $p$, and the entanglement support size ($\alpha n$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the structure of the dual MDS code and relies on bounding the Fourier coefficients of the leakage operators.

Figures summary. The notes mention tables summarizing sufficient thresholds for different block sizes and theorems relating to the security bounds.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the basic quantum leakage bound without entanglement, then generalizing to disjoint entangled blocks, and finally analyzing the case where a linear number of devices share entanglement with the adversary.

Abstract

We initiate the study of quantum leakage resilience of unmodified Shamir secret sharing over prime fields. A well-studied leakage model for Shamir's secret sharing classically is single-bit local leakage from each share. We consider its quantum analogue where, for each party, a local leakage channel takes as input the party's share and outputs a leaked qubit. Without preshared entanglement, we show that the distinguishing advantage is $2^{-Ω(n)}$ when the threshold rate $t/n=τ$ exceeds $τ_\star\approx0.73339$ by a fixed positive margin. More generally, we allow disjoint entangled blocks of any fixed maximum size where there is no entanglement between different blocks or with the adversary, and each block emits at most a fixed number of qubits. Security holds when the threshold rate is high enough (sufficiently close to one). We then allow a specified set of devices to share entanglement with the adversary. We show that security holds even when a linear number of devices ($αn$ for small $α>0$) share entanglement with each other and with the adversary for a large enough threshold rate. As a complementary negative result, we also show that even classical single-bit leakage makes Shamir scheme insecure if we allow arbitrarily large entanglement between the leakage devices. A GHZ state shared by exactly $t$ leakage devices makes even classical one-bit leakage insecure, without any entanglement with the adversary. In this attack, each participating device emits only one classical bit, and their joint parity distinguishes any chosen pair of secrets with a constant advantage. Thus, for fixed threshold rates above $τ_\star$, the maximum number of devices that may share arbitrary entanglement with one another and with the adversary while preserving security is linear in $n$ up to constant factors, although the optimal support fraction remains open.

Abstract

A hidden low-temperature phase with possible loop-current order has been proposed in the kagome metals AV$_3$Sb$_5$ (A=K, Rb or Cs), but its experimental signatures remain subtle and indirect. Authors: Yu Chen, Ananta Mukherjee, Mingyang Yang

Summary. This paper determines quantum query lower bounds for graph problems like triangle listing and spanner construction within the general graph query model. It extends the quantum query recording framework to prove these bounds by reducing the graph problems to intermediate search tasks. The results establish significant theoretical limits on the efficiency of quantum algorithms for analyzing graph structure.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous application of advanced quantum query complexity techniques, particularly the recording method, to fundamental graph problems provides deep insights into the inherent computational difficulty of graph structure discovery.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper establishes quantum query lower bounds for two specific relational graph problems: listing all triangles in a graph and constructing a multiplicative spanner.

Main result. It achieves a first non-trivial quantum lower bound for triangle listing ($\Omega(n^{3/2-o(1)})$) and provides a bound for $k$-spanner construction ($\Omega(n^{1 + 1/2\mu_k})$).

Method. The analysis extends the quantum query recording framework to handle bidirectional oracles, deriving bounds by reducing the graph problems to intermediate multi-block search problems (BHVF and RMIF).

Model / system. The work operates in the general graph query model, utilizing quantum query complexity theory. The core technical tool is the 'recording method,' which tracks information across multiple query steps in a shared quantum record.

Key observables. Quantum query complexity, recording progress ($\Delta_{t,k}$), and the success probability of finding hidden values.

Important parameters / regimes. Graph size ($n$), number of blocks ($B$), size of the hidden space ($N$), and the target spanner parameter ($k$).

Assumptions / limitations. The results are derived under the general graph query model (mixed model). The $k$-spanner bounds are compared against conjectures related to the Erdős girth conjecture.

Paper structure. The paper first extends the quantum query recording framework for bidirectional oracles and establishes a generic blockwise soundness lemma. It then uses this framework to derive lower bounds for intermediate search problems (BHVF/RMIF), which are subsequently used to prove the final bounds for triangle listing and $k$-spanner construction.

Abstract

This paper gives quantum query lower bounds for two relational graph problems, triangle listing and explicit multiplicative spanner construction, in the general graph query model, where quantum adjacency, degree and neighborhood queries are all available in arbitrary superposition. Both results are obtained by reductions via intermediate multi-block search problems. We extend the quantum query recording framework by Zhandry (CRYPTO 2019) and Hamoudi and Magniez (ToCT 2023) to handle a recording architecture for bidirectional oracles and give a generic blockwise soundness framework, which for arbitrary families of local accepting projectors, gives an exact operator-norm characterization of their maximum overlap with the subspace of bounded weight records. Using the intermediate search problems, we exhibit a family of $n$-vertex graphs with $Θ(n)$ triangles on which listing any constant fraction of the triangles requires $Ω(n^{3/2-o(1)})$ quantum queries. This is the first nontrivial quantum lower bound for triangle listing, a question raised by Jiang and Peng (ICML 2026). Further, we show that, for every fixed $k\ge 7$, constructing a multiplicative $k$-spanner requires $Ω(n^{1+\frac{1}{2μ_k}})$ quantum queries, where $μ_k=k/3+O(1)$. For $k\in\{7,8\}$ the bound is $Ω(n^{5/4})$, which matches what would be implied by an unproven instance of the Erdős girth conjecture, and for large $k$ the exponent $1+\frac{3}{2k}$ exceeds the $1+\frac{4}{3k}$ implied by the provable high girth dense graphs due to Lazebnik, Ustimenko and Woldar, 1995.

Shared Phase Arithmetic for Parallel Quantum Rotations

Summary. This paper presents the Parallel Phase Kickback (PPK) technique to efficiently implement quantum gates defined by complex phase arithmetic. It achieves this by separating the cost of calculating the phase function from the cost of applying it. The technique shows significant gate count savings, particularly when the rotation parameters have disjoint binary supports, making it crucial for scaling quantum algorithms.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on quantum computation, the rigorous analysis of resource scaling (T-gates) for implementing complex unitary transformations is foundational for designing quantum algorithms that might interact with AMO systems, especially those requiring precise phase control.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of efficiently computing and applying complex quantum phase rotations, specifically those defined by a weighted sum of parameters, using shared quantum arithmetic.

Main result. It develops the Parallel Phase Kickback (PPK) technique, showing that when rotation weights have disjoint binary support, the T-gate cost can be significantly reduced, achieving near-linear scaling with the number of rotations.

Method. The core method involves three steps: coherently encoding the weighted sum into an auxiliary register, adding this sum to a phase-gradient state to imprint the phase, and then uncomputing the auxiliary information.

Model / system. The model is quantum computation utilizing diagonal quantum gates, analyzed within the Clifford+T gate set. The process involves multiple registers: data qubits, a phase sum register, an encoder workspace, and a phase-gradient reference state.

Key observables. Gate counts, specifically T-gate counts, and the resulting phase accuracy ($\delta_{ ext{ang}}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The bit resolution $b$ (determining the phase precision $2^b$), the number of weights $M$, and the structure of the weights (e.g., disjoint binary support).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the weights are supplied and focuses on the logical circuit cost, separating the cost of phase function representation from its application.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the PPK process flow, detailing the sequence of Encode $ o$ Add $ o$ Decode across the data, shift, workspace, and reference registers.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the problem of phase accumulation via weighted sums. It then details the PPK construction, proving correctness and analyzing costs for general weights, followed by specialized analyses for two rotations and weights with disjoint binary support, culminating in cost comparisons across different techniques.

Abstract

A layer of diagonal quantum gates can be specified by an integer-valued function on computational basis states. This description suggests implementing the layer by evaluating that function reversibly and translating its value into a phase using a shared quantum register. We develop this viewpoint for parallel phase kickback (PPK): the weighted sum of several rotation parameters is computed coherently, added once to a phase-gradient state, and then uncomputed. The construction separates the cost of representing a phase function from the cost of applying it. We prove correctness, give explicit error bounds, and analyze logical gate counts for general weights, pairs of rotations, and weights with disjoint binary support. With Clifford-only encoding, a batch uses at most $4(b-1)$ T gates at $b$-bit phase resolution, excluding initialization. The first phase reference is prepared with independent rotations synthesized by Gridsynth; additional phase-gradient states then follow from the disjoint-support construction with $O(b)$ T gates each. The resulting bounds identify when shared phase arithmetic reduces amortized rotation cost and when encoding overhead limits that advantage.

Summary. This work extends topological band theory beyond the standard 2D quantum Hall effect by analyzing systems on high-dimensional parameter spaces or curved manifolds. Abstract

We study the topological band theory of quantum Hall systems in which the adiabatic flux parameters of an external gauge potential form a high-dimensional manifold. This situation arises both for strongly correlated matter on spatial Riemann surfaces of genus $g > 1$, as well as in the formal study of matter on compact oriented manifolds of dimension $4\ell+2 = 2p$ coupled to $p$-form gauge potentials. In these cases, the underlying topological invariants defined over the parameter space are significantly richer than the low-dimensional (i.e., two-dimensional) case and involve both the curvature (i.e., first Chern class) as well as higher order curvature invariants. These higher curvature invariants correspond to topologically protected contributions to Kubo-like formulae, constructed from correlation functions of the physical current operators. We illustrate these general considerations with an explicit example from hyperbolic band theory based on the genus-two Bolza Riemann surface, a case which has recently been simulated on a synthetic-dimension platform.

Abstract

Low-space-overhead fault-tolerant quantum computation requires not only high-rate quantum error-correcting codes but also space-efficient implementations of logical operations. Transversal and fold-transversal gates are promising since they limit error propagation and require no additional qubits. However, the logical operations they enable are typically restricted, and a central challenge is to construct codes that combine a complete set of logical Clifford gates with favorable code parameters. In this work, we introduce a family of quantum codes with an asymptotically constant encoding rate and sublogarithmically growing stabilizer weight, while supporting the entire logical Clifford group using only transversal and fold-transversal gates. Our construction is based on classical codes whose code spaces are absolutely irreducible Steinberg modules of their Tanner-graph automorphism groups. Taking hypergraph products of these classical codes yields quantum codes for which all logical Clifford operations can be synthesized from a fixed set of transversal and fold-transversal gates. Moreover, the slow growth of the stabilizer weight enables a high error-correcting performance in small instances. These results provide a path toward fault-tolerant quantum computation with low space overhead using transversal and fold-transversal gates.

Abstract

We report the magnetic and transport properties of single-crystalline Ce$_4$Ge$_7$, which crystallizes in a non-centrosymmetric orthorhombic structure (space group C222$_1$). It undergoes an antiferromagnetic transition at $T_N$ = 7.3 K. When a magnetic field is applied along the $b$-axis, a metamagnetic transition occurs at 1.3 T (at 2 K). Correspondingly, this transition gives rise to an anomalous Hall effect, which is dominated by the intrinsic Karplus-Luttinger mechanism. Under hydrostatic pressure, $T_N$ of Ce$_4$Ge$_7$ initially increases slightly and is then gradually suppressed. For pressures above $P_c$ = 10.2 GPa, no magnetic order is observed. The divergence of the $A$ coefficient, the maximum of the residual resistivity, and the non-Fermi liquid behavior around $P_c$ indicate the possible existence of an antiferromagnetic quantum critical point in Ce$_4$Ge$_7$.
