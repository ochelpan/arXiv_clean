Consistent histories and the ultrametric on infinite tensor products

Authors: Andrew Lesniewski
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.37375v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5 · interference shaping light 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper establishes a novel, rigorous geometry for quantum histories by defining a complete ultrametric based on the rate at which accumulated quantum records become distinguishable. This 'consistency rate' ($\delta$) quantifies the transition from quantum superposition to classical probability, linking fundamental concepts like interference decay and quantum error bounds. It provides a powerful mathematical tool for understanding decoherence in the infinite limit.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a deep mathematical tool (ultrametric geometry) to quantify the operational distinguishability between quantum histories, offering a rigorous framework for decoherence and the emergence of classicality in open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops a rigorous geometric framework for histories in quantum mechanics, specifically addressing how to assign classical probabilities to sequences of coarse-grained events when interference terms are considered in the infinite limit.

Main result. It constructs a complete ultrametric on the space of quantum histories, whose convergence exponent, the 'consistency rate' ($\delta$), has equivalent interpretations related to sector separation, interference decay, and minimum discrimination error.

Method. The analysis relies on extending the consistent histories formulation using the infinite tensor product structure and analyzing the decay of the normalized Dowker-Halliwell interference, which is controlled by the accumulated squared Bures distance.

Model / system. The model is the abstract quantum mechanical history space, formalized using an infinite tensor product structure to represent the accumulation of quantum records ($\Omega = (\Omega_1, \Omega_2, \dots)$). It compares this idealized infinite limit to finite-dimensional record packing constraints.

Key observables. Normalized Dowker-Halliwell interference, accumulated squared Bures distance, the consistency rate ($\delta$), and the minimum error probability for discriminating cumulative records.

Important parameters / regimes. The rate $\delta$ is determined by the asymptotic behavior of the per-step record defect $\{b_m\}$, which can follow power-law profiles $d^2m \propto m^{-s}$.

Assumptions / limitations. The use of the infinite tensor product is an asymptotic idealization, which bypasses finite-size packing constraints encountered in physical, finite-dimensional systems.

Paper structure. The paper builds from the definition of the consistent histories formulation to analyzing the decay of interference via the Bures distance. It applies the Kakutani-von Neumann dichotomy to characterize the asymptotic structure, culminating in the definition and interpretation of the ultrametric consistency rate $\delta$.

Abstract

The consistent histories formulation of quantum mechanics assigns classical probabilities to sequences of coarse-grained events when the corresponding interference terms vanish. In finite models these terms generically need not vanish exactly, and their smallness alone supplies no asymptotic geometry on the space of histories. We construct such a geometry for histories that accumulate quantum records. When the normalized Dowker - Halliwell interference between two histories factorizes as the cumulative fidelity of their records, its decay is controlled by the accumulated squared Bures distance. In the infinite registration limit, the Kakutani - von Neumann dichotomy gives a sharp sector alternative: summable Bures defect yields weakly equivalent record sequences, whereas nonsummable defect produces orthogonal superselection sectors and vanishing normalized interference. The convergence exponent of the Bures defect defines a pseudo-ultrametric on histories. After quotienting its zero distance degeneracy, it becomes a complete ultrametric, requiring neither compactness of the outcome space nor continuity of the record family, and coincides with the gauge invariant sector metric $\tilde d$ introduced in \cite{LesTP}. Its value, the \emph{consistency rate}, has three equivalent interpretations: it is the sector separation metric, the polynomial growth exponent of the negative logarithm of normalized interference, and, by the Helstrom formula, the corresponding exponent of the negative logarithm of the minimum error probability for discriminating the cumulative records. Classical separation profiles determine the rate: positive long time mean defect gives the maximal value $δ=1$, while the borderline profile $d_m^2\asymp m^{-1}$ produces distinct superselection sectors at zero polynomial rate, $δ=0$.

Correlation-assisted topological and metamagnetic transitions in Rashba-coupled superconductors: $t$-$J$-$U$ model study

Authors: Tushar Dey, Maciej Fidrysiak, Józef Spałek
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.37812v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 2/5 · analog quantum simulation 1/5

Summary. This theoretical study examines how combining strong electronic correlations with Rashba spin-orbit coupling affects superconductivity in cuprate-like materials. Using the $t$-$J$-$U$ model, the authors pinpoint a correlation-driven topological superconducting state ($C=\pm 2$) that manifests alongside distinct metamagnetic transitions. The work highlights the necessity of correlation effects beyond mean-field theories to accurately map the phase diagram of such complex superconductors.

Why it may be interesting. The explicit inclusion of RSOC and its interaction with strong correlations provides a rich platform for studying topological superconductivity beyond standard BCS theory, which is relevant for understanding exotic superconducting phases in correlated materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the joint effects of electronic correlations and Rashba-type spin-orbit coupling (RSOC) on superconducting pairing, focusing on emergent topological and metamagnetic transitions.

Main result. The authors identify a distinct $C = \pm 2$ Topological Superconducting (TSC) state driven specifically by electronic correlations, which is accompanied by discontinuous metamagnetic and Lifshitz transitions.

Method. The analysis employs the Statistically-Consistent Variational Approximation (SGA) applied to the $t$-$J$-$U$ model, analyzing phase diagrams and free-energy differences.

Model / system. The system is modeled using the $t$-$J$-$U$ Hamiltonian augmented with RSOC and a Zeeman field, applied to a square lattice structure relevant to cuprate superconductors.

Key observables. Chern number ($C$), SC order parameter components ($\Delta_d$, $\Delta_p$), magnetization, and the free-energy difference ($\Delta F$).

Important parameters / regimes. On-site Coulomb repulsion ($U$), RSOC magnitude ($V$), and Zeeman field ($h$) are varied across different doping regimes near half-filling.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the SGA approximation and restricts the variational space to maintain lattice translational symmetry, excluding long-range SDW or CDW orders.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the evolution of SC amplitudes, energy gaps, and magnetization as a function of band filling, showing hysteresis loops and transitions between different topological states ($C=\pm 4$ vs $C=\pm 2$).

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the phase diagram by varying correlation strength ($U$) and doping, comparing results obtained from the $t$-$J$-$U$ model against simpler weak-coupling schemes (BdG) to isolate correlation-driven physics.

Abstract

Unconventional superconductivity commonly emerges in systems characterized by strong electronic correlations, with layered copper-oxides serving as a canonical example. Observation of spin-momentum locking within Bi-family of the cuprates, compatible with the presence of non-negligible Rashba-type spin-orbit coupling (RSOC), calls for an investigation of the joint effects of electronic correlations and RSOC on pairing in copper-oxide and related superconductors. Employing statistically-consistent variational approximation (SGA), we carry out such an analysis by constructing the phase diagram for the case of square-lattice \textit{t-J-U} model incorporating RSOC. We also investigate the effects of time-reversal-symmetry breaking by Zeeman field, as well as characterize emergent topological superconducting (TSC) states. Chern number $C = \pm 4$ TSC is found in a broad regime of on-site Coulomb repulsion close to half-filling. The latter is not governed by correlations and emerges also within the weak-coupling Bogoliubov-de Gennes (BdG) scheme. Yet, we identify a distinct $C = \pm 2$ TSC state that is driven specifically by electronic correlations via a topological transition occurring with no bulk quasiparticle gap closure, and is accompanied by discontinuous metamagnetic and Lifshitz transitions. Moreover, a qualitatively distinct doping evolution of the $d$- and $p$-wave components of the underlying mixed-parity SC order parameter above the metal-to-insulator transition is demonstrated. Our work points toward the relevance of joint correlation and RSOC effects beyond BdG scheme to phase diagrams of RSOC-coupled superconductors.

Resonance Breaking in Noisy Shor's Algorithm

Authors: Zhengwei Liu
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.37636v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5 · Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper proves that Shor's algorithm loses its exponential quantum advantage when subjected to even arbitrarily small amounts of depolarizing noise. By analyzing the measurement probability using the Pauli path integral, the authors show that the characteristic resonance peaks decay exponentially with the noise rate. This suggests that practical quantum computation requires error correction beyond the scope of the analyzed noise model.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a concrete, quantitative demonstration of how realistic noise models can fundamentally undermine the theoretical speedup of quantum algorithms, which is crucial for assessing near-term quantum hardware feasibility.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper analyzes how the exponential quantum advantage of Shor's algorithm breaks down when the quantum circuit is subjected to noise.

Main result. The presence of any small, non-zero depolarizing noise rate causes the measurement probability associated with the resonant peaks to decay exponentially, destroying the quantum advantage.

Method. The analysis uses the Pauli path integral expansion to calculate the measurement probability distribution in the presence of noise, comparing the noisy result to the noiseless ideal case.

Model / system. The model is Shor's quantum circuit used for factoring, specifically analyzing the effect of independent single-qubit depolarizing noise of rate $\lambda$ applied to the control register.

Key observables. The probability of measuring a specific bit-string $x$, denoted $P_\lambda(x)$, and the measurement rate within the resonance set $E_r$.

Important parameters / regimes. The noise rate $\lambda$, the number of qubits $n$, and the Hamming weight $d$ of the Pauli word.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is restricted to a single layer of depolarizing noise, and the conclusion does not rule out fault-tolerant quantum advantage.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the noiseless resonance peak probability using the Pauli path integral. It then derives the noisy probability $P_\lambda(x)$ and shows that the high-frequency terms decay as $(1-\lambda)^d$, while the low-frequency terms are bounded by $O(nd)/2^{n/2}$, leading to the conclusion that the total rate vanishes for $\lambda > 0$.

Abstract

We argue that the exponential quantum advantage in Shor's algorithm is broken under one-layer depolarizing noise of arbitrary small error rate, by analyzing a resonance breaking phenomenon in noisy quantum circuits. First, we express the distribution of measurements on $n$-qubit strings as the superposition of the $4^n$ wave functions in the Pauli path integral. In the noiseless case, the bit-strings achieving resonant peaks guaranteed a constant rate of successful measurements to factor a large number. Secondly, when the middle layer of the quantum circuit has independent depolarizing noise of rate $λ$, for any Hamming weight $d$, we obtain corresponding measurement rate bounded by $2(1-λ)^d$ for higher frequency terms and by $O(n^d)/2^{n/2}$ for low frequency terms. The rate approaches to zero as $n$ and $d$ approaches to infinity. Resonance breaking destroys the exponential quantum advantage. Furthermore, we design a classical factoring algorithm in polynomial time $O(n^d)$ to substitute the low frequency contribution.

A commuting operator self-test for exact entanglement embezzlement

Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5

Summary. This theoretical paper develops a framework for 'commuting operator self-tests' to characterize quantum correlations. It applies this framework to a game based on entanglement embezzlement, proving that the optimal correlation is a unique self-test. Crucially, the result demonstrates a correlation that cannot be modeled by simple tensor products, advancing the understanding of quantum resource theories.

Why it may be interesting. This work pushes the boundaries of quantum information theory by using abstract operator algebra tools to classify quantum correlations, providing a rigorous mathematical tool (the self-test) to detect non-local quantum resources that defy simple tensor product descriptions.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the characterization of quantum correlations, specifically focusing on determining when the optimal correlation for an entanglement embezzlement protocol can be uniquely described by a commuting operator self-test.

Main result. The optimal correlation for the entanglement embezzlement game ($G_{emb}$) is shown to be a commuting operator self-test that cannot be realized by any standard quantum tensor-product model, providing a novel example in the field.

Method. The analysis heavily employs advanced $C^*$-algebraic techniques, including the use of Cuntz algebras and abstract state characterizations, to prove the uniqueness and non-factorizability of the optimal quantum state.

Model / system. The model is a generalized two-player nonlocal game ($G_{emb}$) involving commuting measurements on a joint state, which is mathematically formalized using commuting operator correlations ($\mathcal{C}_{qc}$) within the framework of operator algebras.

Key observables. The optimal correlation $\hat{p}$ for the game $G_{emb}$, and the associated unique abstract state $f$ on the tensor product of local measurement algebras.

Important parameters / regimes. The structure of the local measurement algebras (related to $M_3(\mathbb{C})$ and $M_2(\mathbb{C})$) and the entanglement state $|\Phi_2 angle$.

Assumptions / limitations. The proof relies on advanced theorems from operator algebras, such as Harris' unique abstract state characterization and the properties of GNS representations.

Paper structure. The paper builds from defining the commuting operator self-test framework, applying it to the specific entanglement embezzlement game $G_{emb}$, and culminating in the proof that the optimal correlation is a unique self-test lacking a tensor-product realization.

Abstract

We show that the optimal commuting operator correlation for a variant of Coladangelo's two-player generalized nonlocal game, based on the embezzlement of entanglement, is a commuting operator self-test. Our result gives the first correlation self-test for an exact entanglement embezzlement protocol in the commuting operator framework. Because the unique optimal correlation necessitates the exact embezzlement of entanglement, the correlation is unrealizable using quantum tensor-product models, even if the local spaces are allowed to be infinite dimensional. As such, our result provides an example of a commuting operator self-test where the Hilbert space shared by the two parties cannot be factored into a tensor-product of local spaces that is compatible with the local measurement operators. Our main proof technique relies on Harris' unique abstract state characterization of exact embezzlement of entanglement involving Cuntz algebras.

Summary. This paper develops a method to classically verify quantum computations (BQP) using quantum self-testing techniques. It achieves a quasilinear resource bound by leveraging the LWE assumption and adapting protocols from the multi-prover setting to a single-prover setting. This advances the understanding of verifiable quantum computation in the circuit model.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on complexity theory, the rigorous analysis of quantum state verification and the use of advanced cryptographic tools like LWE make it highly relevant for understanding the physical limits of quantum information processing.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to construct the first argument system for BQP (Bounded-error Quantum Polynomial time) with quasilinear total resource requirements in the circuit model, enabling classical verification of quantum computation.

Main result. They achieve this by using computational self-testing based on the LWE assumption, resulting in a resource requirement of O(poly(λ, log g) * g) for a circuit with g gates.

Method. The approach combines computational self-testing, the LWE assumption, and the application of a compiler (Kalai et al.) to translate multi-prover nonlocal games into a single-prover argument system.

Model / system. The model is quantum computation realized through quantum circuits involving single-qubit Clifford observables ($\sigma_X, \sigma_Y, \sigma_Z$). The verification process is classical, relying on cryptographic assumptions.

Key observables. Winning probabilities ($p_{win}$), commutator norms ($\|[W_1, W_2]\|^2$), and the verification error rate.

Important parameters / regimes. LWE security parameter ($\lambda$), number of gates ($g$), and the error parameter ($\epsilon$).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumptions include the Learning with Errors (LWE) assumption and the sub-exponential hardness of LWE for non-uniform quantum adversaries.

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically illustrates a compiled nonlocal game structure. Figure 3 shows the structure of a quantum circuit implementing a specific transformation.

Paper structure. The paper builds the argument system by first establishing a computational self-test for certifying quantum states, then using this test to dequantize efficient verification protocols, and finally applying a compiler to adapt the result from a multi-prover to a single-prover setting.

Abstract

Computational self-testing gives a classical verifier command over the quantum register of a single computationally bounded prover. We use this framework to construct the first argument system for BQP with quasilinear total resource requirements in the circuit model. Our argument system is based on the learning with errors (LWE) assumption and requires total resources of $O(\mathrm{poly}(λ, \log g)\cdot g)$ for delegating a circuit with $g$ gates, where $λ$ is the LWE security parameter. This is achieved by constructing a new computational self-test for certifying the prover's quantum state and using it to dequantize the efficient verification protocol of Broadbent (ToC 2018). Specifically, this self-test enables the verifiable, random remote state preparation of tensor product states of the single-qubit Clifford observables $σ_X, σ_Y, σ_Z, (σ_Y-σ_X)/\sqrt{2}$ and $(σ_Y+σ_X)/\sqrt{2}$, with constant robustness: the verification error is independent of the number of prepared qubits. This approach was first proposed by Coladangelo et al. (ToC 2024) in the multi-prover setting. We replicate their result in the single-prover setting by applying the compiler proposed by Kalai et al. (STOC 2023)---which turns any nonlocal game into a single-prover argument system---to a modified version of their self-test.

Summary. This work extends the concept of device-independent quantum steering quantification to three parties, analyzing two distinct tripartite scenarios. By employing the Assemblage Moment Matrices framework, the authors construct Semidefinite Programs to derive lower bounds on steering robustness. This provides a powerful, experimentally verifiable tool for certifying genuine multipartite nonlocality.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous, device-independent quantification of multipartite entanglement measures like steering is fundamental for certifying genuine multipartite quantum correlations in experimental settings.

Abstract

Quantum steering captures the ability of one party, through local measurements on a shared entangled state, to affect the conditional states held by distant parties in a way that admits no local explanation. Its device-independent (DI) quantification -- requiring no characterization of any state or measurement -- has been developed in the bipartite setting, notably through the framework of assemblage moment matrices (AMMs) [Phys. Rev. Lett. 116, 240401 (2016)], but has remained largely unexplored beyond it. Here, we extend the AMM framework to tripartite steering, covering both the scenario in which two parties jointly steer a third one (2-steer-1) and that in which a single party steers the remaining two (1-steer-2). As a by-product, our bounds also certify, in a DI manner, the incompatibility of the measurements performed by the steering parties.

Summary. This paper explores the mechanism by which global symmetries can appear in quantum gravity theories when averaging over an ensemble of possibilities. It uses advanced mathematical tools, like the Siegel-Weil formula applied to CFTs, to show that this averaging process yields symmetries that are otherwise absent. This work proposes a new conjecture linking symmetry breaking in mixed states to the structure of ensemble averages in holography.

Why it may be interesting. The concept of symmetry emergence via averaging is a powerful theoretical tool that connects concepts from condensed matter (topological order, symmetry breaking) to fundamental questions in quantum gravity and string theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how global symmetries, generally believed absent in quantum gravity, can emerge when considering ensemble averages of quantum field theories.

Main result. Global symmetries emerge from the ensemble average calculation, which is mathematically realized by equating the average partition function to a specific Eisenstein series, suggesting a mechanism for symmetry emergence in holographic contexts.

Method. The core method involves computing ensemble averages of partition functions (theta functions) using the Siegel-Weil formula, relating them to sums over bulk geometries (Eisenstein series).

Model / system. The study uses 2D Narain-type CFTs whose ensemble average is dual to a 3D Abelian Chern-Simons theory in the context of holography. It also analyzes Strong-to-Weak Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking (SWSSB) in the thermofield-double construction.

Key observables. The ensemble average of the theta function $\langle \vartheta_{Q, \alpha} angle$, the R\'enyi-2 correlator, and the resulting Eisenstein series $E_{Q, \alpha}$.

Important parameters / regimes. The quadratic form $Q(\ell)$, the moduli space $M_Q$, and the large-distance limit $|x-y| o \infty$ for correlation functions.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the ensemble average resolves the 'factorization puzzle' in AdS/CFT, and it proposes a new Swampland conjecture based on these findings.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the problem of symmetry emergence in quantum gravity, to applying the Siegel-Weil formula to CFT ensembles, and finally generalizing the concept to recast SWSSB in holographic models.

Abstract

It is widely believed that there are no exact global symmetries in quantum gravity. We review recent works showing that global symmetries can nevertheless emerge after ensemble averages of theories, as encountered in holography. As a precision case study, we discuss the ensemble average of two-dimensional Narain-type conformal field theories associated with an even quadratic form of general signature, which is computed by the Siegel-Weil formula and interpreted as a sum over geometries in a three-dimensional Abelian Chern-Simons theory. Global symmetries of the bulk anyons can emerge after the average, as T-dualities relating different theories in the ensemble are "folded" into symmetries of a single theory (duality origami). We discuss the relation to the Swampland program, including the emergence of global symmetries at infinite distance. Finally, we recast strong-to-weak spontaneous symmetry breaking (SWSSB) of mixed states as an emergence of symmetries in ensemble averages; in holography, wormholes connecting the two sides of a thermofield-double construction provide its bulk realization. This motivates a new Swampland conjecture.

Excitonic Stripe Order in the Two-Orbital Hubbard-Kanamori Model

Authors: Rafael D. Soares, Luke Staszewski, Chunhan Feng, Alexander Wietek
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.36051v1

Summary. This work explores excitonic stripe order in the two-orbital Hubbard-Kanamori model, treating it as an excitonic analogue of charge/spin stripes. Using Hartree-Fock and RPA, the authors demonstrate that excitonic stripes form a distinct symmetry-broken state whose spin character depends critically on the Hund's coupling sign. The results provide a detailed phase diagram mapping out various correlated electronic phases.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on condensed matter, the study's rigorous use of symmetry analysis and mean-field techniques for identifying distinct symmetry-broken phases is relevant to understanding order parameters in complex quantum many-body systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates excitonic stripe order, an excitonic analogue of stripe order, in a multi-orbital correlated electron system.

Main result. Excitonic stripes are established as a distinct symmetry-broken state in multi-orbital correlated systems, with the spin character (singlet vs. triplet) dictated by the sign of Hund's exchange coupling.

Method. The study employs unrestricted real-space Hartree-Fock calculations complemented by Random-Phase-Approximation (RPA) instability analysis to map out phase diagrams.

Model / system. The system is modeled by the two-orbital Hubbard-Kanamori model on a square lattice. The Hamiltonian includes hopping, crystal-field splitting, and various repulsive/exchange interactions.

Key observables. Excitonic order, stripe order (spatial modulation of inter-orbital particle-hole coherence), and the spontaneous breaking of a relative orbital Z2 symmetry.

Important parameters / regimes. Hund's exchange coupling (J_H), crystal-field splitting (Delta_cf), and doping level are key parameters controlling the resulting ordered phase.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on mean-field approximations (Hartree-Fock) and RPA, which are noted to be limited by the Mermin-Wagner theorem for true long-range order at finite temperature.

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically illustrates principal ordered phases (Néel, orbital density wave, excitonic order) at the Hartree-Fock level. Figure 2 shows temperature-electron density phase diagrams for various regimes.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the internal symmetries of the Hamiltonian, develops gap equations for different ordered phases (AFM, Triplet Excitonic, ODW, Singlet Excitonic), and uses RPA susceptibility calculations to determine instability boundaries.

Abstract

Excitonic condensation and stripe formation are two distinct manifestations of electronic correlations. While excitonic order naturally arises in multi-orbital systems, stripe order is a prominent feature of doped correlated-electron models. Here, we investigate an excitonic analogue of stripe order in the two--orbital Hubbard-Kanamori model on the square lattice, characterized by a spatial modulation of inter-orbital particle-hole coherence and, in the orbital-parity-symmetric limit, spontaneous breaking of a relative orbital $\mathbb{Z}_2$ symmetry. Using unrestricted real-space Hartree-Fock calculations complemented by random-phase-approximation instability analysis, we determine how the Kanamori interactions select different excitonic channels. The sign of Hund's exchange controls the spin character of the condensate: ferromagnetic Hund coupling favors triplet excitonic order, whereas antiferromagnetic Hund coupling stabilizes singlet excitonic order. Upon doping, commensurate excitonic density waves develop incommensurate textures, including excitonic stripes and, in the triplet sector, spiral excitonic density waves. We further show that crystal-field splitting strongly reorganizes the excitonic instability by modifying inter-orbital nesting and can stabilize triplet excitonic order even in the absence of pair hopping. Our results establish excitonic stripes as a distinct symmetry-broken state of multi-orbital correlated systems and identify microscopic routes for their stabilization.

Abstract

We derive the Fokker-Planck equation and its entropy production from the master equation. To this end we consider a discrete space of states where the master equation is defined and appropriate transition rates are introduced. A transition rate has two parts, one of which is the ordinary term proportional to the inverse of the spacing and the other is inversely proportional to the square of the spacing corresponding to the variance of the independent stochastic variable. In the continuous space limit, we obtain the Fokker-Planck as well as the rate of entropy production and the flux of entropy. We also consider the case where the space of states is the phase space and discuss the equations appropriate for systems in contact with several heat reservoirs and for isolated systems.

Abstract

Network reconstruction from partial information is usually performed at the same resolution level at which constraints are observable. This becomes problematic when only coarse-grained information is available, while the relevant process occurs at a finer scale. Here we employ the multiscale model of weighted networks introduced in a companion paper and turn it into a probabilistic framework for reconstructing weighted networks across arbitrary aggregation levels. The model is built to preserve its functional form under coarse-graining, so that global parameters calibrated on an observable aggregate layer can be transferred to finer layers without refitting. We test the method on two empirical systems with different aggregation mechanisms. In the International Trade Network, countries are aggregated into geographic macro-regions and the observed coarse-grained layer is used to infer the underlying country-level network. In the Dutch production network, sectoral flows are reconstructed across the hierarchical industrial classification, using coarser sectoral layers to infer finer ones. In both geographical and sectoral settings, we benchmark our genuinely multiscale reconstruction method against a state-of-the-art weighted reconstruction model calibrated directly at the target resolution, and therefore using additional information available at the same (finer) scale at which performance is evaluated. Remarkably, despite this informational disadvantage, our method recovers the fine-scale binary structure with high accuracy, improving precision, specificity, accuracy and maximum degree-error diagnostics, while remaining nearly equivalent in sensitivity.
