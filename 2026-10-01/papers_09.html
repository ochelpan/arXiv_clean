Foundation Neural-Network Quantum States for Molecular Potential Energy Surfaces in Second Quantization

Authors: Lizhong Fu, Jianan Wei, Wenguan Wang, Honghui Shang
Type: theory · Category: numerical methods · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.37733v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces Foundation Neural-Network Quantum States (FNQSs) to efficiently calculate molecular Potential Energy Surfaces (PESs) in second quantization. By using a single, geometry-conditioned autoregressive model and implementing orbital alignment, the method achieves chemical accuracy at novel geometries without retraining. This approach drastically reduces the computational cost of mapping complex molecular energy landscapes.

Why it may be interesting. This work represents a significant advancement in applying deep learning to quantum chemistry, providing a highly scalable and efficient framework for mapping complex, high-dimensional potential energy surfaces that is directly relevant to molecular dynamics and quantum simulation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core challenge is developing a method to construct Molecular Potential Energy Surfaces (PESs) by sharing a single, optimized representation of the electronic wavefunction across different molecular geometries, thereby avoiding repeated, expensive variational optimization at every geometry.

Main result. The proposed method achieves chemical accuracy at untrained geometries for small molecules and yields massive computational cost reductions (estimated $25.8 imes$ end-to-end GPU-cost reduction) compared to independent optimization.

Method. The authors introduce geometry-conditioned Foundation Neural-Network Quantum States (FNQSs) using an autoregressive Transformer architecture to parameterize the wavefunction in second quantization. Orbital alignment is crucial for maintaining basis consistency across geometries.

Model / system. The system is molecular electronic structure, treated in second quantization using an occupation-basis model. The Hamiltonian is parameterized, and the state $|\Psi_ heta(R) angle$ is learned by conditioning the autoregressive network on the molecular geometry $R$ and aligned orbital descriptors.

Key observables. Electronic energies (PES), dipoles, quadrupoles, and natural occupations.

Important parameters / regimes. Chemical accuracy threshold (1.6 mHa), Mean Absolute Error (MAE) values (down to 0.049-0.085 mHa), and computational cost reduction factors ($\sim 25.8 imes$).

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes that orbital identities and phases can be consistently aligned and transported across different geometries to ensure a shared, continuous basis representation.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the process of orbital matching and phase alignment; comparisons show energy errors for various molecules ($ ext{N}_2$, $ ext{CO}$, $ ext{H}_4$) across different training/conditioning strategies, highlighting the necessity of alignment and descriptor conditioning.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the FNQS architecture, details the necessity of orbital alignment for basis transport, presents results showing high accuracy on PESs at untrained geometries, and quantifies the massive computational savings achieved by this shared representation.

Abstract

Second-quantized neural-network quantum states have achieved accurate molecular energies, but extending them across molecular geometries requires a shared representation of the geometry-dependent wavefunction coefficients. We introduce geometry-conditioned foundation neural-network quantum states for molecular electronic structure in second quantization. A single autoregressive model learns a family of ground states from sparse anchor geometries and provides wavefunctions at untrained geometries without further optimization. Orbital alignment matches orbital identities and transports their phases, establishing an aligned orbital basis across geometries. Frozen energies reach chemical accuracy at every untrained query geometry for N$_2$, CO, and H$_4$. On additional molecular paths, the energy-trained wavefunctions yield dipoles, quadrupoles, and natural occupations without property labels. Across three paired N$_2$ training seeds, orbital alignment lowers the mean absolute energy error over all untrained query geometries from 34-37 mHa to 0.049-0.085 mHa. At approximately 1 mHa mean absolute error, frozen evaluation reduces the per-geometry cost by $986\times$ relative to independent optimization, yielding an estimated $25.8\times$ end-to-end GPU-cost reduction on a 161-point N$_2$ grid.

From Chemical Complexity to Tunable Magnetic Ordering in Highly Disordered High-Entropy Spinel Oxides

Authors: Neha Sharma, Sushanta Mandal, Nikita Sharma, Amritpal, Sangeeta Thakur, Viktor Ukleev, Chen Luo, Florin Radu, S. D. Kaushik, Tirthankar Chakraborty, Sanjoy Kr. Mahatha, Denis Pelloquin, Sourav Marik

Summary. This work demonstrates that high-entropy spinel oxides maintain robust, long-range ferrimagnetic ordering despite extreme chemical disorder. By systematically varying the cation composition, the researchers found that the magnetic transition temperature ($T_C$) follows a simple, predictable linear average. This establishes a powerful, disorder-driven pathway for tuning magnetic properties in complex functional oxides.

Why it may be interesting. The concept of emergent, predictable magnetic order arising from quenched, high-entropy disorder is highly relevant to understanding complex magnetic ground states in strongly correlated materials, suggesting new design rules beyond simple superexchange models.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how extreme configurational disorder, induced by high-entropy stabilization, affects the magnetic ordering in spinel oxides.

Main result. Robust, long-range ferrimagnetic ordering is observed, and the magnetic transition temperature ($T_C$) exhibits a predictable, linear, arithmetic average dependence on the constituent single-site systems.

Method. The research combines structural characterization (XRD, STEM) with advanced magnetic measurements (XMCD, Neutron Powder Diffraction) and theoretical modeling (mean-field approximations).

Model / system. The system is spinel-type high-entropy oxides, $ ext{AB}_2 ext{O}_4$, where both the A and B sites are composed of multiple randomly distributed principal cations. The magnetic behavior is analyzed using mean-field theory.

Key observables. Magnetic transition temperature ($T_C$), total angular momentum ($J$), magnetic moments ($\mu_B$), and the magnetic structure determined via NPD.

Important parameters / regimes. Compositional fractions ($p_i$), temperature ($T$), and the average exchange interaction ($ ext{J}_{ ext{ex}}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The magnetic ordering is explained within a mean-field framework, assuming the exchange interaction is governed by an effective, averaged interaction across the disordered lattice.

Figures summary. Figures show structural confirmation (Vegard's law, STEM homogeneity), magnetic measurements (FC magnetization vs. T), and NPD patterns confirming long-range magnetic order at low temperatures.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the structural stability of the high-entropy phase to characterizing the magnetic response using multiple techniques, culminating in the demonstration of predictable, long-range ferrimagnetism despite extreme disorder.

Abstract

High-entropy stabilization chemistry is redefining materials design by transforming configurational disorder, arising from the deliberate incorporation of multiple principal cations, into a thermodynamic advantage that promotes phase stability and enables emergent functionalities. In this work, we investigate the evolution of magnetic ordering in spinel-type high entropy oxides by systematically varying the cation composition of the B site within a fixed high-entropy A-site matrix, (Ni$_{0.2}$Mg$_{0.2}$Co$_{0.2}$Cu$_{0.2}$Zn$_{0.2}$)B$_2$O$_4$. Upon introducing multicomponent B-site configurations, we uncover a strikingly linear dependence of the magnetic transition temperature (T$_C$) on the T$_C$s of the corresponding single B-site high-entropy systems. Remarkably, this trend persists even in highly complex (Ni$_{0.2}$Mg$_{0.2}$Co$_{0.2}$Cu$_{0.2}$Zn$_{0.2}$)(Cr$_{0.2}$Mn$_{0.2}$Fe$_{0.2}$Ga$_{0.2}$X$_{0.2}$)$_2$O$_4$, X = Al and Ti. Despite the material's extremely high degree of disorder, absence of a dominant magnetic ion or a straightforward superexchange pathway, detailed magnetization measurements, low-temperature X-ray magnetic circular dichroism, and neutron powder diffraction studies reveal robust long-range ferrimagnetic ordering. These results reveal an emergent predictability in ferrimagnetic high-entropy spinel oxides, where, despite extreme configurational disorder and competing interactions, robust ferrimagnetic order can arise from, rather than be hindered by, extreme configurational disorder. This establishes a pathway for predictively tuning magnetic transition temperatures in high-entropy oxides beyond conventional ordered systems.

From Classical to Quantum Channels: Achieving Positive Covert Rates

Authors: Hassan ZivariFard, Xiaodong Wang, Alexei Ashikhmin
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.36162v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper rigorously determines the conditions required for achieving positive covert communication rates across classical, classical-quantum, and quantum channels. It establishes necessary and sufficient conditions using geometric and information-theoretic tools, showing that channel structure dictates the achievable secrecy capacity. The findings generalize fundamental limits for secure communication in modern quantum information theory.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides fundamental information-theoretic bounds on secrecy and detectability in quantum communication, which is crucial for designing secure quantum networks and understanding the limits of quantum key distribution.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the fundamental conditions under which positive covert communication rates can be achieved across various communication channels, including classical, classical-quantum, and fully quantum settings.

Main result. Positive covert rates are achievable under specific geometric conditions related to the dimensions of input and output probability simplices, and the analysis shows that mixing the innocent state can guarantee feasibility for quantum channels.

Method. The analysis employs geometric interpretations of the covertness constraint, utilizing concepts like the dimension of probability simplices, mutual information, and trace distance bounds derived from random coding techniques.

Model / system. The study models communication over Discrete Memoryless Channels (DMCs), encompassing point-to-point, Multiple-Access Channels (MACs), classical-quantum, and general quantum channels.

Key observables. Covert communication rate, channel input/output probability simplices, and the trace distance between the actual and innocent output states.

Important parameters / regimes. The dimensionality of the input vs. output probability simplices, and the rate pair $(R, R_K)$ for covert capacity.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis often relies on the assumption that the innocent symbol/state can be chosen appropriately, and it contrasts with known limitations like the square-root law for certain channels.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the concept of covert communication over point-to-point channels and show the structure of the output probability simplex for binary-output DMCs.

Paper structure. The paper develops results sequentially, first for classical point-to-point channels, then extending to MACs and classical-quantum settings, culminating in a detailed analysis of the covertness constraint for general quantum channels.

Abstract

In this paper, we study the conditions under which covert communication at positive rates is feasible over discrete memoryless classical, classical-quantum, and quantum channels. For classical point-to-point channels, we show that if the dimension of the channel input probability simplex exceeds that of the channel output probability simplex, equivalently, if the input alphabet has larger cardinality than the output alphabet, then positive covert rates are achievable for certain classes of Discrete Memoryless Channels (DMCs). We further show that allowing the innocent symbol (i.e., the symbol transmitted in the no-communication mode) to be chosen appropriately can enlarge the class of DMCs for which positive covert rates are achievable. We also study covert communication over classical Multiple-Access Channels (MACs), where the additional transmitter effectively enlarges the set of available channel input pairs, and we show that the conditions required to satisfy the covertness constraint are less restrictive for MACs than for point-to-point channels. We extend these results to classical-quantum DMCs by showing that if the dimension of the input probability simplex exceeds the affine dimension of the set of output states that can be induced at the channel output, then positive covert rates are achievable for certain classes of classical-quantum DMCs. Finally, for quantum channels, we show that if the innocent state (i.e., the state transmitted in the no-communication mode) is mixed, then the covertness constraint can always be satisfied by a non-trivial input ensemble. Consequently, positive covert rates are achievable whenever the legitimate receiver can distinguish at least two states in a suitable such ensemble.

From Projected Subspaces to Full-Space Implementations: Representation-Equivalence Audits for Open-Shell ADAPT-VQE

Authors: Lucia Malíčková, Petr Klenovský
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.36060v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work develops a rigorous validation framework to audit the equivalence between quantum states optimized in symmetry-projected subspaces and their full-space parent generators. By testing Cu(II) and NO systems, the authors show that parameter transfer from reduced to full space often fails catastrophically. The key takeaway is that quantum simulation requires validating not just the energy, but the entire computational pipeline: the state representation, the generating sequence, the circuit compilation, and the final measurement.

Why it may be interesting. It provides crucial methodological rigor for quantum chemistry simulations, warning that simply minimizing energy in a restricted basis is insufficient proof of the underlying physical state when mapping to quantum hardware circuits.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper audits the representation equivalence between a quantum state optimized in a symmetry-projected subspace and the state generated by exponentiating the corresponding unprojected parent generators in the full Hilbert space.

Main result. The study demonstrates that low energy achieved in a reduced-space optimization does not guarantee the correct full-space circuit implementation, requiring explicit representation-equivalence checks.

Method. The authors use ADAPT-VQE within a 'validation ladder' framework, comparing results from projected ansätze against full-space reoptimization and circuit synthesis.

Model / system. The calculations are performed on open-shell electronic structure problems, specifically using a canonical CAS(15e,9o) type-1 Cu(II) Hamiltonian, and are extended to larger NO systems.

Key observables. Energy error ($ ext{mEh}$), projected fidelity, quartet weight, and the violation of the representation-equivalence condition ($ ext{QAP}=0$).

Important parameters / regimes. The comparison involves comparing results from projected-doublet ADAPT to full-space sequences, and later testing 18- and 24-qubit NO systems.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that the reduced-space optimization only certifies the variational object within that subspace, necessitating external validation for the full circuit.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the 'Validation ladder' showing multiple independent validation steps are necessary, and show energy error convergence for Cu and NO systems under different compilation schedules.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the theoretical condition for equivalence ($ ext{QAP}=0$), demonstrating failures using the Cu(II) Hamiltonian, then showing improvements via constrained reoptimization, and finally extending the validation to larger, more complex qubit systems (NO).

Abstract

A variational state optimized in a symmetry-projected space need not be the state prepared by exponentiating the corresponding unprojected parent generators. For an orthogonal target-space projector $P$, $Q=I-P$, and an anti-Hermitian generator $A$, exact equivalence for all amplitudes requires target-space invariance, $QAP=0$. We formulate this condition as a representation-equivalence audit for open-shell ADAPT-VQE. For a canonical CAS(15e,9o) type-1 Cu(II) Hamiltonian, 274 of 323 bare Hartree--Fock-origin single, double, and triple excitations violate this condition. Projected-doublet ADAPT reaches 1.5719 m$E_h$ with 121 operators, whereas the same amplitudes in the corresponding full-space sequence give 464.730 m$E_h$ error, 0.07343 fidelity with the projected state, and 54.45\% quartet weight. Reoptimizing the same 121 parent generators in the same order under the hard constraint $p_D\ge0.999$ reduces the best converged error to 8.482 m$E_h$, showing that the catastrophic same-angle failure is primarily a representation/parameter-transfer failure but is not completely repaired by full-space reoptimization of the fixed sequence. An independent 12-qubit NO benchmark exhibits the same mechanism more mildly. A spin-preserving generalized full-space construction removes the representation ambiguity and reaches 1.493 and 1.181 m$E_h$ at 18 and 24 qubits. For the 18-qubit positive control, selectively refining only four Suzuki-2 factors and re-transpiling with a fixed compiler seed passes the predeclared circuit-equivalence gate at 19,660 controlled-X (CX) gates and depth 23,016. Circuit synthesis and final-energy measurement therefore constitute separate validation layers. The contribution is a validation framework, not a new spin-adapted ADAPT algorithm.

Near-Optimal Parallel Unitary Process Tomography in Diamond Distance

Authors: Noam Scully, Sisi Zhou
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.36127v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

Summary. This paper develops a novel, explicit parallel circuit protocol for unitary process tomography, demonstrating that its resource requirements are nearly equivalent to the best sequential methods. By closing the gap between parallel and sequential circuit complexity up to a logarithmic factor, it significantly improves our understanding of the computational overhead for characterizing quantum gates.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly impacts the resource estimation for quantum computation, providing concrete, improved bounds for implementing quantum gates, which is fundamental to quantum information theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of performing unitary process tomography—determining an unknown unitary gate $U$—with near-optimal query complexity.

Main result. It proves the existence of a novel parallel circuit protocol that achieves a query complexity only logarithmically worse than the best known sequential protocol, closing a major gap in resource estimation.

Method. The authors employ advanced mathematical tools, including the Weyl integral formula and Fourier analysis on the torus, to bound the error variance in the diamond distance.

Model / system. The system involves a $d$-dimensional unitary operator $U \in U(d)$ acting on a quantum system. The tomography is measured using the diamond distance, $d_{\diamond}$, which quantifies the error between the estimated and true process.

Key observables. The primary observable is the error $\epsilon = d_{\diamond}(U(U), U(c_U))$, and the complexity is measured by the required number of queries (the circuit depth/size).

Important parameters / regimes. Key parameters include the dimension $d$, the error tolerance $\epsilon$, and the parameter $r$ used to construct the parallel protocol.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis heavily relies on the unitarily covariant nature of the diamond distance and the use of specific mathematical techniques like the Weyl integral formula and Fourier decomposition.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the problem, compares sequential vs. parallel resource bounds, and then develops the novel parallel protocol $A(r, d, n, U)$ whose error variance is bounded using Fourier analysis techniques.

Abstract

Unitary process tomography is the act of learning how an unknown unitary gate acts on a quantum system. It is an essential procedure for realizing a functioning quantum computer. As quantum technologies develop, it is important to understand the computational resources needed to conduct unitary process tomography using a variety of types of circuits. In particular, when measuring error in diamond distance, it is known how to achieve optimal query complexity using a sequential circuit architecture, but it had not been known whether a parallel circuit architecture could achieve the same optimal query complexity. In this work, we prove that unitary process tomography with near-optimal query complexity is indeed possible using a parallel circuit by introducing an explicit, novel parallel procedure. This closes the gap between sequential and parallel circuits up to a logarithmic factor, showing that parallel and sequential strategies are nearly equally capable for this task.

Near-Optimal Quantum Algorithm and Complexity Analysis for Riccati Problems

Authors: Jingyao Wang, Yanqiao Wang, Bowen Li, Jin-Peng Liu, Zhengfeng Ji

Summary. This paper presents quantum algorithms to efficiently compute the solution matrices for various Riccati equations, which are crucial in fields like optimal control and quantum chemistry. The authors achieve near-optimal query complexity bounds and rigorously prove that solving certain instances of these equations is BQP-hard. This work bridges advanced mathematical physics problems with the theoretical limits of quantum computation.

Why it may be interesting. While the application is in physics (LQR, RPA), the core contribution is establishing computational complexity (BQP-hardness) for solving fundamental equations arising in quantum dynamics, which is highly relevant to quantum information theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Developing quantum algorithms to construct block-encodings of solution matrices for various Riccati equations (CAREs, DAREs, DREs, and finite recursions).

Main result. The algorithms achieve near-optimal query complexity for CAREs and DAREs, and the problem of encoding solutions for certain Riccati equations is proven to be BQP-complete.

Method. The core method is the Quantum Weighted Riesz Method, which involves embedding the problem, constructing a graph projector via weighted Riesz operators (QET), and recovering the solution matrix.

Model / system. The mathematical model is the set of Riccati equations (CARE, DARE, DRE), which arise in optimal control (LQR) and quantum chemistry (RPA). The quantum computation is framed around constructing the block-encoding of the solution matrix $P$.

Key observables. Solution matrices $P$, query complexity $Q$, generalized singularity factor $\mathcal{R}$, and initialization conditioning factor $\kappa_{ ext{init}}$.

Important parameters / regimes. Spectral separation ($\mathcal{R}$), output normalization ($\alpha$), and initialization/output scales.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on stated access and normalization assumptions, and the lower bounds are established for specific input-oracle families.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the Quantum Weighted Riesz Method pipeline (Embed $ ightarrow$ QET $ ightarrow$ Recover) for various Riccati problems. Other figures detail spectral branches and weights.

Paper structure. The paper develops the quantum algorithm framework, analyzes the query complexity bounds by establishing lower bounds, and culminates in proving BQP-completeness for specific Riccati problem instances.

Abstract

We develop quantum algorithms that construct block-encodings of solution matrices for continuous- and discrete-time algebraic Riccati equations (CAREs and DAREs), differential Riccati equations (DREs), and finite Riccati recursions. The Quantum Weighted Riesz Method, unifying four kinds of Riccati problems, constructs projectors onto the solution graphs using weighted Riesz branching operators and recovers the solution matrices. Under the stated access and normalization assumptions, our algorithms for CAREs and DAREs have near-optimal query complexity $Θ(\mathcal Rα)$ up to logarithmic factors, with the generalized singularity factor $\mathcal R$ that reflects spectral separation, and the output normalization $α$. For DREs and finite-horizon problems, the query complexity has an additional initialization conditioning factor $κ_{\rm init}$. We establish product query lower bounds for specified input-oracle families, demonstrating necessary joint dependence on spectral separation and initialization or output scale. We also prove that selected-value promise problems are BQP-complete on explicit circuit-generated families of single-control discrete-time algebraic and zero-terminal differential Riccati equations. Applications include classical feedback evaluation for linear-quadratic control, a query-complexity comparison for stable random-phase approximation Riccati equations in quantum chemistry, and heated boundary control with numerical validation.

Optimal Entanglement Routing in Quantum Repeater Chains: Beyond Fixed Operation Order and Purification Schedule

Authors: Aikaterini Mandilara, Antonia Tsili, Dimitris Syvridis, Konstantinos Christodoulopoulos
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.37206v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work tackles the complex problem of optimizing entanglement routing in quantum repeaters by relaxing previous constraints on operation order and purification scheduling. By developing advanced optimization frameworks, the authors prove that the purification schedule, rather than the sequence of operations, is the primary determinant of achievable network throughput. This advances the theoretical understanding of scalable quantum communication networks.

Why it may be interesting. It provides rigorous, exact optimization tools (MILP/DP) for resource management in quantum networks, which is crucial for scaling quantum communication infrastructure.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to find the optimal strategy for maximizing the expected end-to-end throughput of entanglement across a quantum repeater chain while maintaining a required fidelity threshold.

Main result. The purification schedule is the dominant factor governing feasibility, as a free operation order can only increase feasibility but not the throughput beyond what the schedule dictates.

Method. The authors develop a unified optimization framework using both Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (MILP) for fixed operation orders and Dynamic Programming (DP) for arbitrary operation orders.

Model / system. The system modeled is a linear quantum repeater chain where entanglement is distributed via entanglement swapping and probabilistic purification protocols (like BBPSSW).

Key observables. Expected end-to-end throughput ($T$), fidelity ($F$), and the feasibility of the entanglement link.

Important parameters / regimes. Finite link capacities ($c_e$), baseline link fidelities ($F^{(0)}_e$), and the fidelity threshold ($F_{th}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is primarily restricted to linear repeater chains and assumes specific noise models (e.g., symmetric Pauli noise).

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically illustrates the linear quantum repeater chain, showing elementary links, baseline fidelities, and capacities.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the physical model and limitations of prior work, then develops the unified optimization framework using MILP and DP, and finally proves key results comparing the impact of operation order versus purification schedule.

Abstract

Entanglement routing establishes entangled pairs between distant nodes of a quantum network by purifying and swapping pairs generated on elementary links. Existing methods typically restrict the decision space along two axes: the operation order, often fixed to purify-then-swap (PtS), and the purification schedule of each link, often restricted to pumping. Focusing on linear repeater chains with finite link capacities, we relax both restrictions and study the maximization of the expected end-to-end throughput subject to a fidelity threshold under two quantum-noise models. We develop a unified capacity-aware optimization framework comprising an exact mixed-integer linear program (MILP) under PtS, instantiated with either pumping or general tree purification schedules, and an exact dynamic program (DP) over arbitrary operation orders. Under symmetric Pauli noise, we prove that pumping converges to a fidelity strictly below unity, imposing a capacity-independent feasibility bound and a maximum chain length on every pumping-based PtS method, whereas general tree schedules yield a capacity-dependent bound. We further prove that post-swap purification never increases the throughput, so a free operation order can only increase feasibility. Numerical evaluations confirm the bounds: tree schedules serve over 90\% of the requests that pumping cannot at moderate fidelity thresholds; within the pumping class, a free operation order recovers much of this advantage; but once links use tree schedules, the tree-based MILP and the order-exact DP serve identical request sets throughout. Operation order and purification schedule are thus substitutes, with the purification schedule the dominant factor governing feasibility under symmetric Pauli noise.

Summary. This paper rigorously studies the separation between classical, randomized, and quantum query complexities for learning concepts. By constructing specific concept classes, the authors disprove a long-standing conjecture relating randomized and quantum query bounds. This work demonstrates that quantum speedups in learning can exceed those predicted by simple Grover or Bernstein-Vazirani type analyses.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides fundamental theoretical tools for understanding the limits of quantum computation in information processing tasks, which underpins the theoretical understanding of quantum algorithms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the optimal relationship between deterministic, randomized, and quantum query complexities for exact learning with membership queries.

Main result. It refutes the conjecture $R(\mathcal C)=O(\mathsf{Q}(\mathcal C)^2+\mathsf{Q}(\mathcal C)\log N)$ by constructing concept classes where the randomized complexity scales significantly faster than predicted by the quantum complexity.

Method. The authors use sophisticated adversary arguments and constructive proofs based on bilinear forms and polynomial structures to establish lower bounds on query complexity, contrasting them with upper bounds derived from quantum algorithms like amplitude amplification.

Model / system. The model is the theoretical setting of exact learning for concept classes $\mathcal C \subseteq \{0, 1\}^N$, analyzed through the lens of query complexity measures ($D, R, Q$). The constructions involve concepts defined over finite fields using bilinear forms or polynomial structures.

Key observables. Deterministic query complexity $D(\mathcal C)$, Randomized query complexity $R(\mathcal C)$, and Quantum query complexity $Q(\mathcal C)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The size of the domain $N$, and the complexity parameters $q$ or $t$ used in the specific constructions.

Assumptions / limitations. The separation results are non-trivial only when the quantum query complexity $Q(\mathcal C) = \omega(1)$.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates a concept class based on a bilinear form, showing a marked address within a block. Figure 2 illustrates a concept class involving a 'Block part' and a 'Cheat-sheet part' related to polynomial coefficients.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by first establishing separations using specific constructions (e.g., bilinear forms) to refute the randomized/quantum conjecture. It then explores related theoretical bounds by analyzing various information-theoretic parameters ($\gamma, ext{ETD}$) and relating them to query complexity bounds.

Abstract

We study exact learning with membership queries for concept classes $\mathcal C\subseteq\{0,1\}^N$, focusing on the relationships among their deterministic, randomized, and quantum query complexities, denoted $\mathsf{D}(\mathcal C)$, $\mathsf{R}(\mathcal C)$, and $\mathsf{Q}(\mathcal C)$, respectively. The two canonical quantum speedups in this model are witnessed by Grover search and Bernstein-Vazirani, leading to the longstanding conjecture $$ \mathsf{R}(\mathcal C)=O(\mathsf{Q}(\mathcal C)^2+\mathsf{Q}(\mathcal C)\log N). $$ We first refute this conjecture by constructing concept classes $\mathcal C$ and $\mathcal C'$ satisfying \[ \mathsf{R}(\mathcal C)=Ω\!\left(\frac{\mathsf{Q}(\mathcal C)^3\log N}{\log \mathsf{Q}(\mathcal C)}\right) \qquad\text{and}\qquad \mathsf{D}(\mathcal C')=Ω(\mathsf{Q}(\mathcal C')^3\log N). \] The first bound matches the upper bound of Arunachalam et al.~[Quantum'21] up to constant factors, while the second matches the upper bound of Servedio and Gortler~[SICOMP'04]. In particular, this shows that the saving in the randomized upper bound of Arunachalam et al. fundamentally relies on randomness. Apart from characterizing the optimal relationship between classical and quantum query complexity, our results are the first to show that quantum speedups for learning can go beyond the Grover and Bernstein-Vazirani paradigms.

Out-of-time-order Correlators in Volcano potentials

Authors: Aritra Banerjee, Reetik Jangir, Rishant Sharma
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.35990v1

Summary. This paper analyzes the Out-of-Time-Order Correlator (OTOC) for the generalized Volcano potential, a model capable of exhibiting diverse potential behaviors. The authors demonstrate that the initial exponential growth of the OTOC, often associated with quantum chaos, is not a global indicator but rather a signature linked to the excited states sampling regions of negative potential curvature. This provides a refined diagnostic tool for understanding quantum dynamics in complex potentials.

Why it may be interesting. The work connects a fundamental measure of quantum chaos (OTOC) to the local geometry (curvature) of the underlying potential, providing a nuanced understanding of when local instability predicts observable scrambling signatures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the Out-of-Time-Order Correlator (OTOC) as a diagnostic tool to characterize quantum information scrambling and the nature of quantum chaos in specific physical potentials.

Main result. The short-time exponential growth of the OTOC does not universally signal global quantum chaos; instead, it is shown to correlate specifically with the sampling of negative curvature regions within the potential.

Method. The study employs a combination of analytical derivations, numerical diagonalization (using DVR), and semiclassical analysis to compute the OTOC for the generalized Volcano potential.

Model / system. The system is modeled by a particle governed by the Schrödinger equation with the 'Volcano potential,' $V(x) = -(\alpha_1 \cosh^{2 u} x + \alpha_2 ext{sech}^2 x)$, which interpolates between various potential shapes, including the Pöschl-Teller case.

Key observables. The Out-of-Time-Ordered Correlator (OTOC), $C_n(t)$, which quantifies the growth of an initially localized operator under time evolution.

Important parameters / regimes. The potential parameter $ u$, and the potential coefficients $\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$, which dictate the potential's asymptotic behavior and curvature.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on projecting calculations onto the bound state manifold for analytic tractability and uses finite-grid discretization for numerical computations.

Figures summary. Figures qualitatively show the profiles of the Volcano potential for different $ u$ and illustrate the calculated OTOC values for various parameter regimes.

Paper structure. The paper is structured to introduce the potential, detail the OTOC computation, analyze the dependence on potential curvature, provide a detailed analysis of the exactly solvable Pöschl-Teller case, discuss implications for braneworlds, and conclude.

Abstract

A large code distance does not by itself guarantee a well-protected quantum memory, as faults during syndrome extraction can propagate into correlated data errors. Certifying circuit distance becomes demanding as circuits grow. In this work, we introduce distance amplifiers, a modular approach to increasing both code distance and certified circuit-level protection in quantum low-density parity-check codes. The construction tensors a base code with an amplifier, adapting their syndrome-extraction circuits to the amplified code. To certify the resulting memory, we develop a fault-response certificate that yields rigorous lower bounds on circuit distance. Suitable gate ordering and flag qubits control correlated errors, allowing us to establish explicit conditions under which the amplified circuit distance equals the product of the constituent circuit distances. We prove this multiplicative relation for three types of amplifiers: the flagged $[[4,2,2]]$, hypergraph-product, and rotated surface codes. The certificate extends recursively, so the circuit distance multiplies exactly at every amplification step. For example, four successive applications of the flagged $[[4,2,2]]$ amplifier to a $[[18,4,4]]$ seed yield a $[[13320,64,64]]$ code with the circuit distance $d_{\mathrm{circ}} \geq 48$ and check weights $w \leq 14$. We further show how individual logical qubits can be tracked explicitly through amplification. By enabling high-distance quantum memories to be built and certified from smaller building blocks, our framework offers a systematic route toward scalable fault-tolerant quantum computing.

Abstract

Entanglement distillation from noisy bipartite states and reliable quantum communication over noisy channels are fundamental tasks in quantum information theory, yet optimal coding schemes typically rely on prior knowledge of the underlying state or channel. Here, through Schur-Weyl duality, we establish a universal quantum error-correction principle that removes this dependence entirely. We show that the irreducible permutation spaces arising from the local Schur-Weyl decomposition of many identical copies of an unknown bipartite mixed state form maximally entangled quantum code spaces, with an error structure determined entirely by representation theory. Correcting these symmetry-resolved errors yields universal protocols for entanglement distillation and quantum communication that achieve coherent-information rates with exponentially vanishing error and optimal second-order correction. The protocols are invariant under local unitaries and allow both input marginals to be recovered exactly. At finite block length, their achievable rates are governed by an associated Schur-Weyl information spectrum, which we relate to Petz-Rényi conditional entropies of the underlying state. The resulting sample complexity depends only on the relevant spectral ranks, rather than on the respective Hilbert space dimensions. In particular, for $n$-qubit states of $O(\operatorname{poly} n)$ global rank, we show that a Petz-Rényi conditional-entropy promise suffices for optimal sample-efficient entanglement distillation. Altogether, these results identify Schur-Weyl duality as a fundamental and general mechanism for optimal, tomography-free universal quantum coding, placing entanglement distillation, quantum error correction and quantum communication within a unified representation-theoretic framework.
