From Bellissard's gap labeling to atomistic quasiperiodic bilayers

Authors: Thomas D. Kühne, Vladislav Efremkin, Emil Prodan
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.36981v1

Summary. This work bridges the gap between abstract topological band theory (gap labeling) and practical electronic structure calculations for complex, aperiodic materials like moiré bilayers. It establishes a detailed computational protocol to interpret finite-size spectra, ensuring that observed spectral changes are correctly classified as mere hybridization effects versus true topological responses. This is crucial for accurately characterizing quantum materials in condensed matter physics.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous connection between abstract topological invariants (like those from K-theory) and measurable quantities from computational solid-state physics is highly relevant for understanding emergent quantum phenomena in engineered materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to rigorously connect the abstract mathematical framework of Bellissard's gap labeling for aperiodic solids to the concrete electronic structure calculations performed on quasiperiodic bilayers.

Main result. It develops a detailed computational framework specifying how to translate atomistic spectral measurements (like finite state counts) into quantities that can be compared with bulk topological invariants, while carefully distinguishing between spectral weight redistribution and true topological response.

Method. The methodology involves analyzing spectral moments, calculating finite state counts, and developing protocols for handling nonorthogonal orbitals and basis function movement in electronic structure codes.

Model / system. The primary model is a quasiperiodic bilayer, exemplified by a honeycomb structure, whose electronic properties are governed by a tight-binding Hamiltonian incorporating interlayer coupling and twist angles.

Key observables. Integrated Density of States (IDS), spectral variance (second-moment identity), finite state count ($F_R$), and topological invariants like the Chern number.

Important parameters / regimes. Interlayer coupling ratio ($t_{\max}^{\perp} / t_{ ext{nn}}$), system size ($R$), and relative sliding/twist angle ($ heta$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that a gap label requires more than just a plateau in the IDS, necessitating a pairing of the spectral projector to define a topological response.

Figures summary. The notes reference figures showing spectral redistribution in DOS maps and the comparison between spectral moments and state counts, illustrating the sensitivity to interlayer coupling.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining the theoretical gap labeling framework using covariant observables to applying this theory to a finite honeycomb bilayer model. It then details the necessary computational steps for atomistic codes, culminating in a hierarchy of diagnostic checks to claim a topological invariant.

Abstract

Bellissard's framework uses covariant observables to describe aperiodic solids. We examine how butterfly-like spectra in twisted bilayers can be related to bulk gap labels and topological response. A bulk gap permits a spectral projector and an integrated density of states. A topological response requires an additional pairing of that projector. In an illustrative finite honeycomb bilayer, an exact second-moment identity isolates the interlayer contribution to spectral variance. Angle-resolved spectra, finite state counts, an uncoupled reference, and size and broadening checks show how coupling redistributes spectral weight without assigning a bulk gap label. We then examine the requirements for atomistic electronic-structure calculations, including nonorthogonal orbitals, projected densities of states, and basis functions that move during relative sliding. The resulting framework specifies the steps needed to connect atomistic spectra with bulk state counting and topological response.

Operational theory for photonic circuits: generalizing linear optics beyond quantum theory

Summary. This paper generalizes linear optics theory by mapping quantum quadrature operators onto classical phase-space coordinates. It investigates fundamental quantum phenomena like the Hong-Ou-Mandel effect and MZI using this generalized framework. The key finding is that these interference effects persist across classical and alternative theories, suggesting a broader physical principle governs them.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the foundations of quantum optics by testing the necessity of quantum mechanics for observed photonic phenomena, which is highly relevant for understanding photonic quantum computing architectures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To generalize the theory of linear optics beyond quantum mechanics by developing a framework based on quadrature operators and analyzing interference phenomena.

Main result. The vanishing of photon coincidences at a beam splitter and the behavior of Mach-Zehnder interferometry are found to occur in both classical and modified quantized theories, suggesting these effects are general features of the generalized framework.

Method. The authors use an analogy between quantum quadrature operators and phase-space coordinates, developing a generalized probabilistic theory framework using pseudo-probability distributions on phase space.

Model / system. The study focuses on photonic circuits involving linear optical elements like beam splitters and phase shifters, modeled using two coupled harmonic oscillators in phase space.

Key observables. Coincidence probability ($P_{co}$), visibility ($V$), and the joint numerical range of coincidence vs. Hadamard coincidence measurements.

Important parameters / regimes. Relative phase ($ heta_{rel}$), energy threshold ($E_{th}$), and the comparison between classical, quantum, and sawtooth theory limits.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on describing states via a pseudo-probability distribution $ ho(x, p)$ on phase space, and comparing results across different theoretical models (classical, quantum, etc.).

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the HOM dip and MZI behavior for different theories, and Figure 11 compares the joint numerical ranges for coincidence and Hadamard measurements across quantum, classical, and sawtooth models.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by establishing the generalized framework using quadrature operators, applying it to fundamental linear optical setups (HOM, MZI), and culminating in a comparison of predictions across classical, quantum, and alternative theories, including contextuality tests.

Abstract

We use the analogy between the quadrature operators in quantum optics and the phase-space coordinates in a mechanical harmonic oscillator to generalise the theory of linear optics beyond quantum theory. For this, we introduce a framework for analysing photonic circuits within generalised probabilistic theories based on quadrature operators. Using this framework, we ask whether phenomena like the Hong-Ou-Mandel effect, Mach-Zehnder interferometry are specific to quantum theory or also occur across a broader class of theories. We find the latter: the vanishing photon coincidences at the output of a beam splitter also occur for classical light and for a modified version of quantised light. Similarly, for Mach-Zehnder interferometry we find that there is no qualitative difference between quantum theory and these alternative theories. Our framework can serve as a foundation to study generalised theories of complex photonic set-ups and photonic quantum computers.

Abstract

The equation of motion with multistate quantum geometric tensors and torsion culminates in transient nonlinear photocurrent responses distinct from the recently measured resonant steady-state photovoltaic effects induced by circularly polarized light. Summary. This paper introduces a polarization-assisted protocol to enhance clock synchronization for quantum devices communicating over lossy channels like satellite links. By exploiting polarization correlations, the method effectively suppresses background noise that plagues standard timing measurements. This results in a significant extension of the operational range, proving its practical importance for the quantum internet.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core challenge is achieving reliable clock synchronization for quantum devices operating over high-loss, long-distance quantum channels, such as satellite-to-ground links.

Main result. The proposed polarization-assisted protocol significantly improves synchronization robustness, extending the reliable operational range by up to 40% compared to methods relying only on temporal coincidence measurements.

Method. The authors compare a temporal-only estimation method against a polarization-assisted estimator, which uses polarization post-selection to suppress background noise.

Model / system. The system involves entangled photon pairs generated via SPDC and distributed over free-space channels. The analysis models the timing error ($\Delta t$) and compares the performance of estimators based on time-only versus polarization-resolved coincidence counts.

Key observables. Synchronization-acquisition probability ($P_{ ext{succ},l}$), Fisher Information ($I$), and the clock offset ($ au$).

Important parameters / regimes. Channel loss ($\eta$), background count rate ($R_b$), and the visibility ($V$) of the entangled source.

Assumptions / limitations. Timing errors are assumed to be independent Gaussian variables, and the temporal statistics are assumed independent of the polarization statistics.

Figures summary. Figure 5 compares temporal-only vs. polarization-assisted histograms, showing the polarization method reveals a clear peak obscured by noise in the former. Figure 6 quantifies the increased loss threshold ($\eta_{90}$) achieved by the polarization-assisted estimator.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theoretical framework by deriving performance metrics (like Fisher Information) for both estimators, analyzes the improvement in the strongly background-dominated limit, and culminates in experimental/simulation results demonstrating the extended operational range in high-loss regimes.

Abstract

Due to their tight temporal coupling, entangled photon pairs produced by spontaneous parametric down-conversion offer a new route to improved network synchronization among quantum-enabled devices. In the emerging space-based quantum internet, such synchronization will be pivotal to operational success. Here, we propose and experimentally demonstrate a new polarization-assisted protocol that allows quantum-enabled devices to remain synchronized under the high loss conditions anticipated for satellite-to-ground quantum channels. Our experimental results show that polarization assistance extends reliable synchronization acquisition to higher channel losses and an increase of up to 40$\%$ in satellite-to-ground distance. Supporting Monte Carlo simulations show that this performance improvement extends over a wide range of photon-starved conditions. Collectively, these results not only provide a proof of concept for polarization-assisted network synchronization but also demonstrate its pragmatic importance in significantly extending the range under which quantum-enabled devices forming the backbone of the quantum internet can remain highly synchronized.

Summary. This work proposes using a fork-tip Josephson Scanning Tunneling Microscope (STM) to overcome the difficulty of measuring the complex phase winding in topological superconductors. By acting as an interferometer, the setup measures the relative phase difference between two points, allowing researchers to reconstruct the full angular dependence of the superconducting order parameter. This offers a direct pathway to experimentally verify theoretical predictions about topological superconductivity.

Why it may be interesting. The technique provides a novel, phase-sensitive probe for superconducting order parameters, which is crucial for understanding exotic states like topological superconductivity, bridging condensed matter theory with advanced experimental diagnostics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is accessing the complex phase winding of superconducting order parameters in topological superconductors using conventional real-space measurement techniques.

Main result. The proposed fork-tip Josephson STM setup allows for the reconstruction of the angular dependence of both the gap-function magnitude and its complex phase by measuring the relative phase difference between two points.

Method. The method involves deriving the Josephson current in the weak tunneling limit, which is then adapted for a fork-tip geometry to measure interference patterns sensitive to relative phase differences.

Model / system. The system is a topological superconductor probed by a fork-tip Josephson STM near an impurity. The theoretical framework utilizes anomalous Green's functions derived from the Bogoliubov-de Gennes (BdG) Hamiltonian.

Key observables. The interference contribution to the critical current, $I_c(R)$, and the spatial pattern of the squared critical current, $I_c^2(R)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The relative phase difference ($\varphi_1 - \varphi_2$) between the two tips, and the superconducting gap function harmonics ($\Delta_m$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the weak tunneling limit and semiclassical approximations for the anomalous Green's function in the vicinity of the impurity.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the schematic setup, the resulting critical current formula, and the successful reconstruction of the order parameter's magnitude and phase from the measured spatial pattern.

Paper structure. The paper builds from the general derivation of the Josephson current for arbitrary order parameters, introduces the limitations of single-tip measurements, and then details the fork-tip interferometer setup to extract phase information via numerical computation and fitting procedures.

Abstract

The complex phase winding in a topological superconductor remains challenging to access by conventional real-space probes. We propose a scheme to probe this winding with a fork-tip Josephson STM near an impurity, where two superconducting tips form an interferometer sensitive to phase differences between two positions. The signal remains accessible even if the relative phase between the fork-tip and sample fluctuates, establishing a route towards a local phase-sensitive STM probe of topological superconductivity. We numerically compute the interference pattern to demonstrate that the magnitude and phase of the order parameter can be reconstructed as a function of angle, providing a route towards resolving the longstanding open experimental problem of directly detecting phase winding in topological superconductors.

Quantum Complexity of Ancilla-Free Unitary Embeddings for Nonlinear Dynamics via Generalized State-Dependent Double-Bracket Flows

Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.37802v1

Summary. This paper establishes a general framework for simulating nonlinear quantum dynamics using fully coherent, ancilla-free unitary circuits. It shows that the required quantum resources scale dramatically based on whether the dynamics are contractive, nonexpansive, or expansive. The results provide optimal query complexity bounds for specific models like the Gross-Pitaevskii equation.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a deep theoretical link between the mathematical complexity of nonlinear physical systems and the required computational resources (query complexity) for quantum simulation, which is crucial for designing practical quantum algorithms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core problem is developing a quantum algorithm to simulate nonlinear quantum dynamics using only unitary, ancilla-free circuits.

Main result. The query complexity for simulating nonlinear dynamics is rigorously related to the stability properties (contractive, nonexpansive, expansive) of the dynamics, yielding bounds like $\exp(O(T))$ to $\exp(\exp(O(T)))$.

Method. The authors generalize the double-bracket quantum algorithm by replacing the state-independent Hamiltonian with a state-dependent Hermitian operator, analyzing the resulting evolution via trace-norm Lipschitz bounds.

Model / system. The model involves nonlinear dynamics governed by a state-dependent operator $G( ho)$, exemplified by the Nonlinear Schrödinger Equation (NLSE) and the discrete Gross-Pitaevskii equation.

Key observables. Query cost (number of oracle calls), trace-norm distance ($\|\cdot\|_1$), and the stability characterization $\Lambda(t)$.

Important parameters / regimes. Target evolution time $T$, nonlinearity strength $g$, and approximation accuracy $\epsilon$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the existence of an initial state preparation oracle and relies on bounding the Lipschitz continuity of the state-dependent operator $G( ho)$.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the recursive, ancilla-free implementation structure for the quantum simulation, showing how subsequent unitary steps are built from previous ones.

Paper structure. The paper develops a general framework by generalizing the double-bracket algorithm to handle arbitrary state-dependent nonlinearities, followed by detailed complexity analysis based on the dynamics' stability properties.

Abstract

Simulating nonlinear dynamics with quantum computers has gained increasing attention. In general, such simulations require additional quantum resources because unitary quantum evolution is linear. A fundamental question is how nonlinear dynamics can be embedded into fully coherent, ancilla-free unitary circuits and how the complexity of the dynamics governs the required quantum resources. In this work, we generalize the ancilla-free double-bracket quantum algorithm for imaginary-time evolution by replacing its state-independent Hamiltonian with a state-dependent Hermitian operator. Our framework recursively calls an initial state preparation oracle and its inverse, and prepares the target solution to any prescribed accuracy using a fully coherent, ancilla-free unitary embedding. We relate the query cost to the complexity of the nonlinear dynamics, specifically their sensitivity to initial conditions. We obtain query upper bounds of $\exp(O(T))$, $\exp(O(T^2))$, and $\exp(\exp(O(T)))$ when the distance between solutions contracts at least exponentially (contractive), does not increase (nonexpansive), or grows at most exponentially (expansive), respectively, where $T$ is the target evolution time. For the discrete Gross--Pitaevskii equation, our ancilla-free double-bracket circuit achieves optimal worst-case query complexity $Θ(e^{gT/2})$ over a specified family of single-qubit initial states, where $g>0$ is the nonlinearity strength. These results connect the complexity of nonlinear dynamics to the query cost of coherent quantum simulation and provide a foundation for designing ancilla-free unitary embeddings with optimal query complexity.

Abstract

In these lecture notes, we introduce the variational Monte Carlo method for the Hubbard model. First, we briefly introduce the single-orbital Hubbard model and its multi-orbital extension, focusing only on electronic models. Then, we introduce the general scheme under which variational Monte Carlo works and we present the variational wave functions that can properly describe superconductive and magnetic states in single-orbital and multi-orbital Hubbard models. We also detail how to perform Monte Carlo calculations for the given wave functions. We also describe how to optimize a variational wave function with the stochastic reconfiguration method. Finally, we present a few results for correlation functions that can be computed in variational Monte Carlo, to determine, for instance, the presence of charge or spin order and superconductivity.

Summary. This work pioneers the use of holographic duality to model quantum turbulence in superfluids, moving beyond phenomenological models. By simulating the fully backreacted system, the authors demonstrate that the normal fluid actively influences the dynamics, yielding non-Gaussian statistics. This establishes a rigorous, first-principles framework for understanding quantum turbulence in cold atomic systems.

Why it may be interesting. The use of holographic duality to derive transport coefficients and dynamics from first principles is a powerful tool for AMO physicists studying non-equilibrium quantum systems, providing a non-perturbative approach to superfluid dynamics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the fundamental challenge of modeling active, two-way coupling between the normal fluid and quantized vortices in quantum turbulence, overcoming historical reliance on phenomenological parameters.

Main result. The holographic framework successfully models this coupling, revealing that normal-fluid velocity statistics exhibit non-Gaussian tails and spatial anisotropy directly from vortex backreaction, and that temperature suppresses fluctuations via superfluid depletion.

Method. The authors employ an ab initio holographic simulation using a fully backreacted gravitational model (AdS/CFT correspondence) to solve coupled Einstein-Maxwell-scalar equations in real time.

Model / system. The system is a superfluid at finite temperature, described by Landau's two-fluid model. The dynamics are simulated holographically, mapping the strongly coupled quantum fluid dynamics to classical gravity in a higher dimension.

Key observables. Normal-fluid velocity statistics (PDFs), energy spectra ($E_s(k), E_n(k)$), superfluid and normal fluid velocities ($\xi_i, \mathbf{v}_n$), and the superfluid condensate density ($ ho_s$).

Paper structure. Abstract

The two-fluid model provides a powerful phenomenological framework for superfluids, where components interact through vortex-induced mutual friction. Quantum turbulence in such coupled two-fluid systems has become a central theme in low-temperature physics. However, the fundamental incompatibility between discrete vortex-line and continuum descriptions has historically forced reliance on phenomenological parameters. Here, we present an ab initio holographic simulation of this active two-fluid dynamics. Using a fully backreacted gravitational model, we intrinsically incorporate reciprocal momentum exchange and finite-temperature dissipation without empirical inputs. Contrasting continuously driven and freely decaying turbulence, we uncover pronounced non-Gaussian tails and spatial anisotropy in the normal-fluid velocity statistics, arising directly from the dynamical quantized vortex backreaction. Raising the temperature suppresses normal-fluid velocity fluctuations despite increasing the vortex density. This suppression is unambiguously traced to the thermal depletion of the superfluid condensate. Our results establish holographic duality as a rigorous first-principles framework for quantum turbulence, revealing the active role of the normal fluid far from equilibrium and yielding predictions for ongoing cold-atom and superfluid experiments.

Abstract

We construct a supersymmetric factorization for generalized driven anisotropic Rabi--Stark Hamiltonians using an affine factor $\hat{A}=\hat{C}+\hat{a}\hat{D}$ in an inhomogeneous orthosymplectic superalgebra, with two real invertible spin matrices $\hat{C}$ and $\hat{D}$. The six real pencil parameters constrain nine Hamiltonian frequencies and one energy offset up to an overall frequency scale. The normal product $\hat{A}^{\dagger}\hat{A}$ has an exact zero-energy ground doublet and, with its antinormal partner $\hat{A}\hat{A}^{\dagger}$, gives a Witten index of two. For distinct pencil roots with finite baseline slopes, coherent gauges define a Bargmann hierarchy and endpoint conditions for exceptional finite-polynomial states. The spin grading organizes four limiting cases, comprising two driven-oscillator families with every baseline exact and the constrained anisotropic-Rabi and Rabi--Stark sheets, which meet at the degenerate-qubit isotropic Rabi model. An aligned interior example adds direct and longitudinal drives, Stark deformation, and intensity-dependent coupling while retaining the factorization.

Abstract

We study fermionic non-local magic in higher-dimensional free-fermion systems through the lens of the entanglement spectrum. For translationally invariant Gaussian states in strip geometries, dimensional reduction maps the problem to independent lower-dimensional momentum sectors, leading in two dimensions to a multiplicative logarithmic violation of the boundary law analogous to Gioev-Klich-Widom scaling. We further show that the second Rényi fermionic non-local magic is bounded by the capacity of entanglement and introduce an entanglement-temperature deformation that probes the low-energy structure of the entanglement Hamiltonian. Applied to the matter-Majorana sector of the Kitaev model, this response is exponentially suppressed in the Abelian phase and algebraic in the non-Abelian phase, where its ratio with the capacity approaches a universal constant. Out of equilibrium, we extend the quasi-particle description to fermionic non-local magic in higher-dimensional quenches and show that random Gaussian circuits exhibit diffusive spreading in two spatial dimensions.

Abstract

We consider a gradient, speed-change, symmetric exclusion process on the discrete torus $T^d_n$, $d\le 3$, whose empirical measure evolves, on the diffusive scale, according to a non-linear parabolic equation. We prove that, starting from a sequence of initial states whose relative entropy with respect to the inhomogeneous product measure associated with the initial profile is $o(n^{d/2})$, and whose density fluctuation field converges, the density fluctuation field converges, in $L^1(0,T; H_{- r})$, to the solution of a linear stochastic partial differential equation with time-dependent coefficients driven by a conservative space-time white noise.
