Summary. The researchers successfully demonstrated photoelectrical readout and coherent control of single NV centers implanted near the surface of diamond. By using photocurrent measurements instead of fluorescence, they proved that these engineered shallow qubits can be read out electrically with coherence times matching deep centers. This breakthrough paves the way for integrating quantum sensing and computing components onto semiconductor chips.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses hardware implementation challenges in solid-state quantum memories. The ability to use electrical detection for spin readout is crucial for scalable, chip-based quantum devices, providing a pathway beyond purely optical methods.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is demonstrating reliable photoelectrical readout and coherent control of single, shallowly implanted Nitrogen-Vacancy (NV) centers in diamond.

Main result. Photoelectric readout was successfully demonstrated for shallow NV centers, showing that the measured Ramsey coherence time agrees with conventional fluorescence measurements. This establishes PE readout as a viable route for chip-integrated quantum sensing.

Method. The study uses photoelectrical detection (measuring photocurrent) to read out spin states and measure dephasing times ($T_2^*$) via pulsed microwave sequences like Ramsey interferometry.

Model / system. The system is single NV centers in diamond, specifically focusing on those that have been artificially implanted at shallow depths ($\sim 10$ nm) and subsequently covered by overgrowth. Measurements compare these to naturally occurring (ingrown) deep centers.

Key observables. Ramsey dephasing time ($T_2^*$), spin contrast (photocurrent vs. fluorescence), Rabi oscillation visibility, and background photocurrent levels.

Important parameters / regimes. Shallow implantation depth ($\sim 10$ nm), overgrowth thickness, applied bias voltage ($\pm 3 ext{ V}$), and laser power/duration optimization.

Assumptions / limitations. The PE readout assumes that the spin-dependent charge conversion mechanism is robust against background photocurrents from defects, which are mitigated by diamond overgrowth.

Figures summary. Figures show optical/photoelectrical confocal imaging, pulsed ODMR and Rabi measurements comparing implanted vs. ingrown centers, and Ramsey $T_2^*$ decay curves demonstrating agreement between PE and PL readouts.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the feasibility of PE readout for shallow NVs, confirming coherence times match optical methods, then detailing the measurement techniques (Rabi/Ramsey), and finally discussing how overgrowth improves signal quality while noting power-dependent decoherence effects.

Abstract

Photoelectrical readout of the electronic spin state of the nitrogen-vacancy (NV) center in diamond is attracting significant interest due to the numerous advantages it possesses compared with conventional fluorescence readout. The higher charge carrier rate compared to the photon rate and the integration of the detection scheme on a chip can significantly advance quantum sensing and computing with color centers in diamond. Until now, photoelectric readout has been performed on ensembles of NV centers or single NV centers deep in ultrapure diamond substrates. However, many applications require the artificial creation and precise placement of shallow NV centers, and photoelectric detection of such centers has been challenging. Here we demonstrate photoelectrical readout and coherent control of the electronic spin of implanted shallow ($\sim$10 nm) NV centers buried by diamond overgrowth. The photoelectrically measured Ramsey $T_2^*$ agrees with conventional fluorescence readout and shows no measurable dependence on the readout photocurrent, for both shallow implanted and deep ingrown NV centers. We further find that overgrowth improves photoelectric readout by suppressing the background photocurrent. These results establish photoelectric readout as a viable route to chip-integrated, electrically detected nanoscale sensing and to spin registers based on engineered shallow NV centers.

Main problem. The study investigates how multipartite entanglement, quantified by the Quantum Fisher Information (QFI), characterizes quantum phase transitions in strongly interacting magnetic systems.

Main result. The QFI associated with transverse spin components reveals multipartite entanglement in the magnetically ordered Kondo-destroyed phase, while longitudinal components remain trivial.

Abstract

Strange metals exemplify highly collective quantum many-body systems that call for new means of characterization, and there is considerable potential for quantum information approaches contributing to the cause. We investigate multipartite entanglement across the quantum phase transition of a Kondo lattice model using the quantum Fisher information (QFI). We show that the QFI associated with the spin components transverse to the order parameter characterizes the destruction of heavy quasiparticles in the Kondo-destroyed magnetic-ordered phase. The physical origin of this observation is elucidated through an analysis of the antiferromagnetic Heisenberg model. We propose to test the results in terms of both unpolarized and polarized inelastic neutron scattering measurements in the ordered part of the heavy fermion phase diagram. Our findings illustrate how different operators of a many-body system can be employed to not only witness multipartite entanglement in different sectors and but also elucidate the overall physics across different parts of the phase diagram.

Summary. The paper introduces the statistical disturbance bound, a novel measure connecting the statistics of a quantum measurement (POVM) to the minimum state disturbance it must cause. By formulating this as an SDP problem, the authors provide a powerful tool that can be experimentally determined without full knowledge of the measurement apparatus. Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous, quantifiable tool—the statistical disturbance bound—to assess the fundamental trade-off between information gain and physical disturbance in quantum measurements, which is crucial for device-independent security proofs.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To establish a fundamental 'statistical disturbance bound' that quantifies the minimum state disturbance induced by any quantum measurement channel compatible with observed statistical outcomes.

Main result. The derived bound, $FE(A)$, successfully links the statistical description (POVM) to the physical disturbance, proving it is superior to existing information-disturbance relations because it constrains the entire statistical structure.

Method. The bound is formulated as an optimization problem solved via Semidefinite Programming (SDP), allowing for efficient computation and theoretical derivation of tight limits.

Model / system. The analysis concerns quantum measurements on a $d$-dimensional system, characterized by a Positive Operator-Valued Measure (POVM) $\{A_a\}$, and the associated dynamical process described by an instrument $I$.

Key observables. Average fidelity ($F_E(A)$), Informativeness ($E(A)$), and the eavesdropper's guessing probability ($p_g$).

Important parameters / regimes. The dimension of the system ($d$), the specific input state ensemble ($\mu_E$, e.g., Haar ensemble $H$), and the POVM elements $\{A_a\}$.

Assumptions / limitations. The initial focus is on pure input states, though extensions consider general ensembles; the method relies on solving SDPs.

Figures summary. Figure 1 sketches the weighted state exclusion technique using probe states. Figure 6 illustrates how the certified fidelity bound restricts Eve's guessing probability in quantum randomness generation protocols as a function of dimension $d$.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by defining the statistical disturbance bound via optimization, solving it for specific ensembles (like Haar), proving its relationship to informativeness, and finally applying it to practical scenarios like bounding eavesdropper knowledge.

Abstract

Quantifying the disturbance caused by a quantum measurement typically requires detailed knowledge of the underlying measurement channel. In this work, we introduce a statistical disturbance bound, which connects the statistical properties of a quantum measurement to the state disturbance induced by any compatible measurement channel. Specifically, we show that the average fidelity between input and output with respect to an arbitrary ensemble of pure input states is fundamentally bounded in terms of the measurement, described as a positive operator-valued measure (POVM). We further develop the weighted state exclusion technique, which enables an experimental determination of the statistical disturbance bound without requiring explicit knowledge of the measurement effects. To see the advantages of our approach over existing information-disturbance relations, we show that our bound distinguishes between measurements with equivalent informativeness. Furthermore, we demonstrate that the weighted state exclusion technique can detect and quantify measurement-induced disturbance using state preparations that are insufficient for tomographic reconstruction of the measurement operators. Finally, we illustrate how disturbance bounds defined with respect to specific input ensembles can be used to bound an eavesdropper's guessing probability in a simple protocol for quantum randomness generation.

Abstract

We review the framework of operator Hilbert space and introduce the one- and two-particle super reduced density matrices (1-SRDMs and 2-SRDMs), as well as the super mutual information (SMI). The eigenvectors of the 1-SRDMs define what we term natural single particle operator bases, and provide a way to compress vibrational and vibronic Hamiltonians with controlled error. The SMI is defined from the operator entanglement entropy of the 1-SRDMs and 2-SRDMs, and captures the correlation between operators acting on different one-mode subspaces, which may be used to reveal and quantify both direct and indirect couplings that might otherwise be difficult to extract. Efficient numerical approaches for the calculation of SRDMs and the SMI are developed and applied to a set of prototypical vibrational and vibronic Hamiltonians, as well as approximations to the corresponding time-evolution operators. Through this, we demonstrate that: (i) commonly used vibronic Hamiltonians are amenable to extremely high levels of compression without compromising accuracy, and; (ii) SMI analysis can be used to systematically and quantitatively reveal both direct and indirect couplings that might otherwise be difficult to extract, including indirect couplings of vibrational modes via intermediary electronic-vibrational interactions.

Abstract

Motion-mediated trapped-ion gates typically apply synchronized state-dependent forces to both target ions. We consider the complementary setting in which one addressing channel alternates between the targets, so their force windows do not overlap. In a complex-amplitude near-resonant description, each window generates a displacement vector, and ordered symplectic products of vectors on different ions produce the ZZ phase. For fixed envelopes and timings, relative rotations of the window displacement vectors redirect the sign and magnitude of this phase, whereas a common rotation of all displacement vectors leaves the pair phase invariant. Motivated by experimental alternating-addressing protocols, we design matched-envelope phase and contrast controls that distinguish the boundary-phase response from closure error and residual force in dark gaps. Waveform synthesis suppresses differential motion, while a reduced-spin map that is exact within the linear spin-dependent-force model accounts for projector-common motion, deterministic local-Z phases, spectator coupling, and control-parameter drift. A conditional-Ramsey protocol predicts continuous and reset contrasts of 0.998 and 0.996, with the reset rule redirecting the extracted pair phase by 0.581 rad relative to the continuous rule, modulo pi/2. In the representative comparison, sequential control uses only one active target channel at a time, but requires 1.826 times the normalized squared-force action of independently calibrated simultaneous control. For representative experimental parameters and within the stated approximations, the inter-window force-quadrature reference therefore provides an experimentally testable control resource for sequential trapped-ion interactions.

Abstract
