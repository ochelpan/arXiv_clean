Quantum-classical crossover in fault-tolerant quantum dynamics simulation

Summary. The paper develops a comprehensive, fault-tolerant framework to simulate complex quantum many-body dynamics, specifically using the mixed-field Ising model. By co-designing error correction with observable estimation, they establish concrete benchmarks showing that quantum computation can achieve an exponential speedup over classical tensor network methods for simulating non-equilibrium quantum systems.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the 'quantum advantage' question by providing quantitative engineering targets and a scalable roadmap for fault-tolerant quantum simulation in condensed matter physics, which is highly relevant to AMO and open quantum systems research.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The central challenge is establishing a concrete, quantifiable crossover point where fault-tolerant quantum computation surpasses the best available classical simulation algorithms for complex many-body dynamics.

Main result. For realistic error rates ($p=10^{-3}$), the authors demonstrate that fault-tolerant quantum simulation of 1D systems requires significantly less time and fewer qubits than classical tensor network methods, projecting minutes or seconds for 2D models under better error control.

Method. The work introduces a full-stack framework that co-designs observable estimation protocols with fault-tolerant Quantum Error Correction (QEC) techniques, specifically improving the implementation of non-Clifford rotations to suppress logical errors.

Model / system. The study focuses on simulating quantum many-body dynamics using the mixed-field Ising model in both 1D and 2D lattices. The goal is estimating time-evolved expectation values $\langle\psi(t)| O | \psi(t) angle$ under a Hamiltonian $H$.

Key observables. Observable expectation value $\langle\psi(t)| O | \psi(t) angle$, physical qubit count, required runtime (hours/minutes), and logical error accumulation.

Important parameters / regimes. Physical error rates ($p=10^{-3}, p=10^{-4}$); system sizes (1D up to 100 sites, 2D lattices); target precision $\epsilon$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the feasibility of implementing a hybrid fault-tolerant architecture combining Clifford+T synthesis with optimized rotation state injection.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the quantum-classical runtime crossover for 1D Ising models, schematic diagrams detailing the algorithm-QEC co-design, and error analyses comparing different simulation methods (e.g., PEPS vs. Trotter) on 2D lattices.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the problem by defining the quantum dynamics task; it then details a novel fault-tolerant framework that integrates observable estimation with QEC protocols; finally, it benchmarks this approach against state-of-the-art classical methods (TN/VMC) to quantify the crossover point.

Abstract

While quantum computers promise to solve classically intractable problems, identifying the point at which fault-tolerant quantum computation outperforms the best classical algorithms for practical applications remains an outstanding challenge. Here we establish a concrete quantum-classical crossover for quantum many-body dynamics under realistic hardware conditions. We introduce a scalable fault-tolerant framework that combines coherent observable estimation with a space-time-efficient implementation of non-Clifford rotations, suppressing the residual logical errors that limit existing partially fault-tolerant approaches. A benchmark against state-of-the-art tensor-network and variational Monte Carlo algorithms reveals a concrete crossover for mixed-field Ising dynamics at modest system sizes. For a physical error rate of $p=10^{-3}$, fault-tolerant simulation requires approximately 2 hours and $3.7 \times 10^5$ physical qubits for a 100-site 1D system, whereas tensor network approaches would require about 100 years. For 2D models, where rapid entanglement growth limits the classical evolution time, we project quantum runtimes within minutes. A physical error rate of $p=10^{-4}$ leads to at least an order of magnitude reduction in qubit count ($3.1 \times 10^4$ physical qubits) and runtime (minutes for 1D and seconds for 2D). The reduction in quantum runtime arises from our improved rotation-state injection and co-design of quantum error correction and observable-estimation protocols, which jointly suppress logical-error accumulation and reduce sampling overhead. Our results establish a scalable route towards practical quantum advantage and identify quantitative engineering targets for future fault-tolerant architectures.

Summary. This paper extends the study of entanglement preservation beyond the standard PPT conjecture by analyzing map composition in the qutrit setting. It proves that combining 1-undistillable channels with low-Schmidt number channels results in entanglement breaking. This has direct implications for understanding limitations in quantum repeaters and entanglement swapping protocols.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides rigorous mathematical tools for characterizing when quantum operations fail to preserve entanglement, which is crucial for designing robust quantum communication protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the EB-composability problem, which generalizes the PPT squared conjecture by analyzing whether the composition of two specific cones of quantum channels is always entanglement breaking.

Main result. For qutrit systems, the composition of maps from the 1-undistillable cone ($ ext{UND}_1$) and the Schmidt number $\le 2$ cone ($ ext{SP}_2$) is proven to be entanglement breaking in both orders.

Method. The authors utilize the Choi–Jamiołkowski correspondence to translate state properties (like separability) into map properties, proving composition theorems for defined cones of completely positive maps.

Model / system. The analysis focuses on quantum channels acting on two-qutrit systems ($d=3$), specifically characterizing the behavior of $ ext{UND}_1$ and $ ext{SP}_2$ cones under map composition.

Key observables. Entanglement breaking (EB) property of composed maps, separability ($\in ext{SEP}$), and the Schmidt number of Choi matrices.

Important parameters / regimes. The dimension $d=3$ (qutrit case); the structure of the two cones $ ext{UND}_1$ and $ ext{SP}_2$.

Assumptions / limitations. The results are proven specifically for the two-qutrit setting; map composition is compared against general selective measurements.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the schematic for EB-composability, showing $( ext{PPT}, ext{PPT})$ as a special case, and confirm inclusions like $ ext{PPT} \subseteq ext{UND}_1 \subseteq ext{SP}_2$ in the qutrit setting.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by defining the EB-composability problem, proving composition theorems for $( ext{UND}_1, ext{SP}_2)$ (Theorem 4), establishing the maximality of $ ext{UND}_1$ (Theorem 6), and finally translating these map results to a state-level entanglement swapping condition (Corollary 10).

Abstract

Entanglement swapping is a fundamental operation in quantum repeaters for establishing entanglement between distant parties. The positive partial transpose (PPT) squared conjecture asks whether two PPT entangled links can generate terminal entanglement through entanglement swapping, or equivalently, whether the composition of two PPT maps is always entanglement breaking. Motivated by this conjecture, we investigate the map-composition problem beyond the PPT setting. For qutrit completely positive (CP) maps, we prove that the composition of any CP map whose Choi matrix is $1$-undistillable with any CP map whose Choi matrix has Schmidt number at most two is entanglement breaking in either order. Moreover, we show that the cone of $1$-undistillable CP maps is exactly the largest qutrit cone of CP maps whose composition with every CP map whose Choi matrix has Schmidt number at most two is entanglement breaking in both orders. Finally, although map composition captures only the standard maximally entangled outcome in entanglement swapping, we prove that any $1$-undistillable two-qutrit state and any state of Schmidt number at most two cannot generate terminal entanglement under an arbitrary selective measurement on the intermediate systems.

Summary. This paper reveals that bandwidth mismatch in CVQKD systems creates a security loophole allowing an eavesdropper to extract key information from out-of-band spectral components. By developing a spectral attack model and verifying it with simulations, the authors demonstrate how this leakage necessitates revising security estimates, providing crucial guidelines for designing robust quantum communication hardware.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a concrete, practical vulnerability assessment for quantum communication protocols, bridging the gap between idealized theoretical models and real-world hardware limitations like bandwidth mismatch.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses a practical security loophole in Continuous-Variable Quantum Key Distribution (CVQKD) caused by bandwidth mismatch between the transmitter and receiver.

Main result. This loophole allows an eavesdropper to directly obtain raw key information from out-of-band frequency components, significantly lowering the achievable secret key rate compared to conventional estimates.

Method. The authors propose a spectral attack scheme based on Fourier analysis of signal spectra and construct a proof-of-principle attack verified through numerical simulations and experimental considerations.

Model / system. The study focuses on Gaussian-modulated coherent states (GMCS) CVQKD, specifically analyzing Local-Oscillator (LLO)-CVQKD systems operating under spectral spreading due to channel imperfections.

Important parameters / regimes. Bandwidth mismatch, insertion loss from wavelength splitters, transmission distance (e.g., 25 km), and cutoff frequencies (e.g., 100 MHz to 150 MHz).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on asymptotic limits and heterodyne-detection conditions for calculating leaked information; it also models spectral broadening via a spreading kernel $L(f)$.

Figures summary. Figures compare Eve's obtained information against security thresholds at various distances/losses, and show the reduction in the secret key rate when accounting for out-of-band leakage.

Paper structure. The paper first identifies the bandwidth mismatch loophole using spectral analysis, then develops the mathematical framework for the attack, presents simulations showing quantitative vulnerabilities across varying loss/bandwidths, and concludes by proposing defense strategies involving both system design (bandwidth) and key rate correction.

Abstract

Continuous-variable quantum key distribution (CVQKD) has attracted extensive attention due to its compatibility and low costs. However, bandwidth mismatch exists to varying degrees between the transmitter and receiver. This may prevent frequency components carrying modulation information from being fully perceived by the legitimate party. In this paper, we identify a practical security loophole caused by bandwidth mismatch and propose a corresponding spectral attack scheme. Different from previous approaches that exploit security loopholes to conceal the excess noise introduced by intercept-resend attacks, this scheme can directly obtain raw-key information without introducing additional disturbances. A proof-of-principle attack on a CVQKD system with filtering operation is constructed to verify the feasibility. Experimental results indicate that Eve can obtain enough information to render the system insecure if this practical security loophole is ignored. Based on the identified security loophole, corresponding defense strategies are proposed. This work helps bridge the gap between theoretical models and practical implementations, providing a reference for defense design in practical quantum communication systems.

Absence of Spin-Glass Order on Migdal--Kadanoff Hierarchical Lattices near Three Dimensions

Summary. This paper rigorously proves that for Ising spin glasses on specific Migdal--Kadanoff hierarchical lattices, spin-glass order is absent by relating the problem to bond percolation. The authors establish a sufficient criterion using exact RG steps, providing strong theoretical evidence contrasting previous numerical estimates regarding critical dimensions.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical statistical mechanics, the techniques involving renormalization group flow and critical phenomena are foundational tools used in analyzing quantum phase transitions and many-body systems in AMO physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The research aims to rigorously determine the existence or absence of spin-glass order in Ising spin glass models on Migdal--Kadanoff (MK) hierarchical lattices, particularly near three dimensions.

Main result. The authors provide a sufficient condition based on bond percolation that proves the absence of spin-glass order and stiffness for specific lattice constructions, even when their fractal dimension approaches three from below.

Method. The methodology employs an exact Renormalization Group (RG) step combined with a connectivity argument derived from bond percolation theory to analyze the decay of correlations.

Model / system. The system is the Ising spin glass model with symmetric binary couplings, studied on MK hierarchical lattices defined by scale factors and branching numbers. The Hamiltonian involves nearest-neighbor interactions across generations of the lattice structure.

Key observables. Spin-glass correlation ($\langle \sigma_A \sigma_B angle^2$), stiffness parameter ($ heta$), and vacancy probability ($\Psi(q)$) relative to the percolation threshold.

Important parameters / regimes. The branching number ($s$) must be even, and the lattice structure is characterized by its fractal dimension $d = 1 + \log s / \log b$.

Assumptions / limitations. The derived criterion is only sufficient (not necessary) for proving the absence of order; the method relies on discrete couplings and specific RG steps.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the first two generations of the MK hierarchical lattice structure used in the analysis, showing its recursive nature.

Paper structure. The paper develops a rigorous criterion based on an exact single-step RG transformation that maps the spin glass problem onto bond percolation. The core proof involves comparing induced dilution probabilities against known percolation thresholds to establish the decay of order parameters across generations.

Abstract

We derive a rigorous sufficient condition for the absence of spin-glass order in the Ising spin glass with symmetric binary couplings on Migdal--Kadanoff (MK) hierarchical lattices with even branching number. The key observation is that a single exact renormalization-group step creates zero effective bonds with positive probability, thereby reducing the problem to bond percolation on the corresponding hierarchical lattice. When the induced dilution exceeds the percolation threshold, both spin-glass order and stiffness are absent at all temperatures, including zero temperature. As a consequence, our criterion gives a rigorous proof that the Ising spin glass on the square lattice does not exhibit a spin-glass phase within the MK approximation. More unexpectedly, by choosing sufficiently large scale factors and branching numbers, we construct MK hierarchical lattices whose fractal dimensions are arbitrarily close to three from below but still exhibit no spin-glass order. This sharply contrasts with numerical estimates obtained for MK hierarchical lattices with relatively small scale factors and branching numbers, which placed the lower critical dimension near \(2.52\).

Summary. This paper introduces a novel, resource-efficient method for performing joint logical measurements in quantum LDPC codes by utilizing only cat states. By introducing a scheduler code, the authors significantly accelerate the process of implementing complex gates like Clifford and Toffoli circuits compared to previous state-of-the-art methods. This work is crucial for advancing the feasibility and speed of fault-tolerant quantum computation.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on abstract quantum coding theory, the underlying concepts of resource state management and efficient measurement scheduling are highly relevant to designing practical fault-tolerant quantum computation architectures using physical platforms like trapped ions or superconducting qubits.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is to develop significantly faster protocols for performing joint logical measurements of multiple commuting Pauli operators within quantum LDPC codes.

Main result. The proposed cat-state measurement approach achieves up to a $74 imes$ speed-up over Viterbi measurements for random Clifford circuits, and shows substantial gains for Toffoli gates as well.

Method. The authors propose using a scheduler code to manage the joint measurement sequence of $\ell$ commuting operators, relying solely on cat states rather than complex resource states used in prior work.

Model / system. The study focuses on quantum LDPC codes, specifically mentioning implementations suitable for platforms like trapped ions or neutral atoms (the 'walking cat architecture'). The operations involve implementing logical Clifford and Toffoli gates via the CliNR partial error correction scheme.

Key observables. Speed-up factor compared to Viterbi measurements; Logical Measurement Counts ($ ext{LM}$) and Cat-based Measurement Counts ($ ext{CM}$); Logical Error Rate ($\epsilon$).

Important parameters / regimes. $\ell$ (number of commuting operators), code sizes (Q70, Q102), target error rates ($10^{-10}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The approach assumes that fault-tolerance is not required to achieve the target logical error rate for a specific algorithm, and it utilizes specialized scheduler codes.

Figures summary. Table IV compares Logical Measurements (LM) and Cat-based Measurements (CM) across Viterbi gate-based, Viterbi CliNR, and MEDM CliNR protocols for Toffoli circuits and random Cliffords.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem of fast logical operations in LDPC codes. It details the proposed cat-state measurement protocol using a scheduler code (MEDM/MECM). The core argument involves comparing this new method against established Viterbi protocols for implementing logical Clifford gates via CliNR, culminating in quantitative speed-up results.

Abstract

Quantum LPDC codes provide a substantial reduction in qubit overhead required for fault-tolerant quantum computation compared to surface code, thanks to their high encoding rate. However, operating simultaneously on multiple logical qubits encoded in the same block is more challenging and may slow down logical operations. Prior work addresses this problem by designing complex resource states to perform logical measurements in LDPC codes. Here, we propose an approach that only consumes cat states. Whereas previous work on cat-based measurements focuses on a single logical measurement, we design a protocol for the joint measurement of $\ell$ commuting logical operators. The key ingredient is the design of a scheduler code determining the measurement sequence and allowing for the decoding of all logical measurement outcomes. Numerical simulations with the LDPC codes Q70 and Q102 of the walking cat architecture show a speed-up of nearly $3\times$ over Viterbi measurements for the measurement of $\ell=20$ commuting logical operators. Combining our fast logical measurements with a new variant of the CliNR partial error correction scheme, we achieve a speed up of up to up to $74\times$ for random Clifford circuits. Our approach also applies to non-Clifford gates, producing a speed up of up to $5\times$ for Toffoli gates.

Summary. This paper develops the path integral formalism for quantum mechanics on an arbitrary Lie group G, extending previous work by rigorously handling compact directions via winding number sums. It applies this framework to compute semiclassical approximations up to two-loop order for systems like the Euler-Arnold model. This advances the theoretical understanding of quantum dynamics in highly symmetric, non-Euclidean settings.

Why it may be interesting. While highly abstract, the techniques developed for quantizing constrained or geometrically structured systems (like those on Lie groups) are foundational to understanding quantum field theories in curved backgrounds or effective descriptions of many-body dynamics where symmetries play a crucial role.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study focuses on developing a rigorous path integral formulation for quantum dynamics occurring on a general Lie group G.

Main result. The authors provide explicit, two-loop order semiclassical approximations for propagators and partition functions associated with Euler-Arnold systems on the group manifold.

Method. The methodology involves adapting Feynman's path integral approach to the non-commutative setting of a Lie group, utilizing techniques like summing over winding numbers in maximal tori and performing systematic loop expansions.

Model / system. The system is quantum mechanics defined on a Lie Group G. The primary physical model analyzed is the Euler-Arnold system, whose dynamics are governed by Hamiltonians derived from quadratic kinetic terms on the group manifold.

Key observables. Propagators, partition functions, and semiclassical approximations of transition amplitudes for Euler-Arnold systems.

Important parameters / regimes. The order of approximation (up to two-loop), the structure constants of the Lie algebra, and the geometry of the maximal tori within G.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes a quadratic kinetic term for simplification in deriving the Lagrangian action, and it addresses complexities arising from compact directions and non-unimodular groups.

Figures summary. Not specified (Notes refer to mathematical equations and expansions rather than figure content).

Paper structure. The paper builds upon previous work by first establishing the path integral framework on L^2(G), then deriving the classical dynamics via Hamiltonian/Lagrangian formalisms for Euler-Arnold systems, and finally executing complex two-loop perturbative calculations.

Abstract

We continue our study of quantum dynamics on a Lie group $G$, initiated in arXiv:2512.19840, by building the path integral that governs transition amplitudes in the Hilbert space $L^2(G)$. This relies on the proper handling of both a noncommutative momentum space and the presence of compact directions in $G$. We show that compactness can be handled through a sum over winding numbers in maximal tori of $G$, generalizing the similar sum commonly encountered for path integrals on a circle. As applications, we compute semiclassical approximations of propagators and partition functions of Euler-Arnold systems, up to (and including) two-loop order.

Summary. This paper develops and analyzes Combinatorial Holographic Quantum Secret Sharing (CHQSS) within the AdS3/CFT2 correspondence. It introduces key metrics—distance, reconstruction threshold, and secret threshold—to quantify the scheme's robustness against boundary erasures. The research demonstrates how these quantum information properties are dictated by the underlying holographic geometry and its phase transitions.

Why it may be interesting. This work connects advanced concepts from quantum information theory (secret sharing) directly to gravitational physics via holography, providing a concrete framework for understanding how geometric properties encode quantum security features.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper characterizes combinatorial holographic quantum secret sharing (CHQSS) for a bulk subregion in AdS3/CFT2 to understand how logical information is encoded on the boundary while remaining protected from erasures of boundary parts.

Main result. They derive relations between the distance, reconstruction threshold, and secret threshold characterizing CHQSS schemes, constructing families including perfect threshold and non-threshold types.

Method. The analysis involves defining combinatorial parameters ($d_b, r_b, s_b$) based on holographic entanglement structures (entanglement wedges) in AdS3/CFT2.

Model / system. The model is a holographic setup involving $n$ boundary subregions $\{A_1, \dots, A_n\}$ on the boundary of AdS3. The geometry is analyzed using an RT-region graph structure to study entanglement wedges.

Key observables. Distance ($d_b$), reconstruction threshold ($r_b$), and secret threshold ($s_b$) are key observables used to quantify the security and encoding quality of the scheme.

Important parameters / regimes. The number of boundary subregions ($n$), the holographic phase, and the specific choice of bulk subregion determine the values of the thresholds and distance.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on coarse-graining the holographic geometry using an RT-region graph and assumes that access structures satisfy monotonicity and no-cloning theorems derived from entanglement wedge nesting.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate three distinct holographic phases for $n=3$ boundary subregions, showing reconstructability dependence on phase. Other figures plot parameter values ($d_b, r_b, s_b$) versus distance along a path in the symmetric $n=5$ setup.

Paper structure. The paper introduces CHQSS, defines characterizing parameters ($d_b, r_b, s_b$), analyzes holographic phase transitions of entanglement wedges, derives relations between these parameters, and finally constructs specific families of CHQSS schemes in the symmetric setting.

Abstract

We introduce combinatorial holographic quantum secret sharing (CHQSS) for a bulk subregion in AdS$_3$/CFT$_2$ to study how logical information of the bulk subregion is encoded in the boundary and protected from erasures of boundary subregions. We introduce a distance, a reconstruction threshold, and a secret threshold to characterize CHQSS schemes. We present the phase transitions of multipartite entanglement wedges in a symmetric setup and observe multiple distinct phase transition points. The distance and thresholds depend on the holographic phase and the choice of bulk subregion. We derive the maximum distance. Moreover, we derive the relations between the distance and the thresholds. We construct a family of CHQSS schemes in the symmetric setting that includes perfect threshold CHQSS and perfect non-threshold CHQSS.

Abstract

Fermion parity is a fundamental property of superconducting many-body states. We further show that changes in the molecular ground-state parity manifest as characteristic $π$-phase shifts in the nonlocal response. Supported by a minimal theoretical model, these results identify global fermion parity as an experimentally accessible degree of freedom in hybrid superconducting circuits that can be readily revealed through the nonlocal Josephson effect.

Abstract

Recent observations of superconductivity in twisted bilayer WSe$_2$ (tWSe$_2$) have motivated theoretical proposals for unconventional pairing mechanisms. A central question is whether band topology plays an essential role in the system's correlation physics. In this letter, we develop a first-principles-based description of the top moiré valence bands in tWSe$_2$. Using density functional theory (DFT) calculations, we identify the bands in the relevant range of twist angles to be topologically non-trivial, with the top valence bands carrying Chern numbers $C=(+1,+1)$ for the $K$ valley. In order to treat the strong correlation physics, we construct compact molecular orbitals directly from the DFT wave functions through a partial Wannierization procedure and with the guidance of spinful $C_{3z}$ symmetry representations. This yields a localized $f$ orbital together with a complementary topological $c$ orbital, allowing us to extract hopping and hybridization amplitudes from first principles. The resulting parameters provide an ab initio benchmark for the effective Hamiltonian. Our work establishes a foundation for understanding superconductivity in moiré TMDs and highlights tWSe$_2$ as a promising platform for exploring topological superconductivity.

Abstract

In Hamiltonian systems, the only basis-independent quantity is the spectrum. Given a spectrum, quantum mereology seeks preferred tensor-product decompositions into local subsystems, leading to the emergence of locality. Causal order, however, remains fixed by the Hamiltonian time evolution. In contrast, higher-order quantum theory permits more general processes that need not possess a definite global causal order. In the process-matrix framework, specifying a process requires a choice of subsystems corresponding to the input and output Hilbert spaces of each agent. A change of basis redefines both the agents and the corresponding decompositions into subsystems, while leaving the spectrum of the process matrix invariant. Here, we study how causality arises from this spectrum. First, we derive spectral constraints on processes compatible with definite causal order. Second, we show that, in the thermodynamic limit, generic quantum processes admit a preferred decomposition with a definite causal order. This suggests a mechanism for the emergence of classical causality only from algebraic ingredients.
