Observation of Magnetic-Anisotropy Crossover and High-Temperature Skyrmions in the Dirac Magnet Fe3Ge with a Distorted Kagome Lattice

Authors: Yalei Huang, Xiaowei Lv, Bin Li, Chunqiang Xu, Dhanarajagopal Alltrin, Xiaoxuan Ma, Wanting Yang, Wei Zhou, Xiangzhuo Xing, Wen-Chin Lin, Raman Sankar, Michael Smidman, Shixun Cao, Dong Qian, Renchao Che, Xiaofeng Xu
Type: both · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10081v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work reports on the discovery of robust, high-temperature skyrmions in the Dirac magnet Fe3Ge. The material exhibits a unique combination of a high Curie temperature, large intrinsic anomalous Hall effect, and a temperature-dependent magnetic anisotropy crossover. By combining advanced transport measurements with LTEM, the authors establish Fe3Ge as a leading candidate for high-temperature spintronics.

Why it may be interesting. The combination of high operating temperature, topological electronic effects (AHE), and stable magnetic textures (skyrmions) in a single material system is highly relevant for designing next-generation spintronic devices, which often involves complex many-body interactions.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to observe and understand the coexistence of robust, high-temperature skyrmions and nontrivial electronic band structures in a magnetic material.

Main result. The Dirac magnet Fe3Ge was shown to host stable skyrmions up to a high Curie temperature (~650 K) while simultaneously exhibiting a large intrinsic anomalous Hall conductivity, making it a promising spintronic platform.

Method. The research combines experimental measurements (magnetotransport, LTEM, magnetization) with theoretical calculations (FPLAPW, tight-binding models) to characterize the magnetic and electronic properties.

Model / system. The physical system is the Dirac magnet Fe3Ge, which possesses a distorted Kagome lattice structure. The analysis covers its magnetic anisotropy crossover (easy-plane to easy-axis) and its topological electronic band structure.

Key observables. High Curie temperature (~650 K), large intrinsic anomalous Hall conductivity (~380 Ω-1cm-1), spin reorientation transition (~375 K), and visualization of mesoscopic skyrmions via LTEM.

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature (especially the transition points 375 K and 650 K), magnetic field strength, and the material's lattice structure (distorted Kagome).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the observed AHE is intrinsic, linked to Berry curvature, and that the observed skyrmion stability window is robust across the measured temperature range.

Figures summary. Figures detail structural characterizations (EDX, STEM), transport measurements ($ ho-T$, Hall resistivity), magnetic hysteresis loops, and LTEM images showing the evolution of magnetic textures like vortices and skyrmions across different temperatures.

Paper structure. The paper systematically presents structural confirmation, followed by transport measurements revealing topological effects and anisotropy changes. It then uses LTEM and theoretical modeling to visualize and confirm the existence and stability of skyrmion phases across the temperature-dependent magnetic anisotropy crossover.

Abstract

Topological materials that simultaneously host robust high-temperature skyrmions and nontrivial electronic band structures have attracted tremendous interest owing to their distinctive advantages for both fundamental research and prospective technological applications. Here, we report the observation of robust skyrmions in the Dirac kagome magnet Fe3Ge, which exhibits a high Curie temperature of ~ 650 K. At room temperature, Fe3Ge shows a large intrinsic anomalous Hall conductivity of ~ 380 Ω-1cm-1, originating from its nontrivial electronic band topology. Systematic magnetization measurements reveal a spin reorientation transition at ~ 375 K, indicating a crossover from easy-plane to easy-axis magnetic anisotropy. Below the spin reorientation temperature, a large topological Hall effect is observed, arising from microscopic noncoplanar spin structures. Lorentz transmission electron microscopy shows that mesoscopic skyrmions are stabilized in the easy-axis magnetic anisotropy regime and persist over an exceptionally wide temperature window of 375-650 K, far exceeding that of most previously reported skyrmion-hosting materials. These results establish Fe3Ge as a promising platform for exploring diverse topological properties, with strong potential for advancing future high-temperature spintronic applications, ranging from next-generation information storage to logic computing devices.

One Relative Phase Orders Operational Thresholds of Noisy Bell Pairs

Authors: Xuan Du Trinh
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10034v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: correlated / nonlocal dissipation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work develops theoretical tools to precisely determine the operational thresholds for quantum abilities in noisy Bell pairs. It establishes that these thresholds are governed by the relative phase between the noise and coherence terms, proving a general monotonicity law. This provides a powerful, general method for certifying quantum capabilities even when exact analytical criteria are unknown.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical framework developed for analyzing thresholds based on phase dependence and convexity is highly relevant for understanding noise resilience in quantum communication protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper determines the operational thresholds at which quantum communication abilities (like entanglement or steerability) are lost or regained in noisy Bell pairs.

Main result. It proves a monotonicity law showing that the operational threshold is nondecreasing with the relative phase between the noise and Bell coherences, even when a closed-form criterion is unavailable.

Method. The analysis uses the X-part approximation of the noise state and relies on the convexity and local-unitary closure of the ability-absence set to establish bounds and monotonicity theorems.

Model / system. The system is modeled by noisy Bell pairs represented by a Bell mixture $ ho_\lambda(\sigma) = \lambda\Phi_+ + (1 - \lambda)\sigma$, where $\sigma$ is the noise state.

Key observables. Operational thresholds ($\lambda_K$), the relative phase ($ heta$), and the critical radius ($R$) for steerability.

Important parameters / regimes. Bell weight ($\lambda$), relative phase ($ heta$), and the seven Pauli expectation values defining the X-part of the noise.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the ability-absence set $K$ is convex and closed under local unitaries, and it often relies on approximating the noise state by its X-part ($\sigma_X$).

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the loss/recovery of various quantum abilities along the Bell-mixing line, showing distinct thresholds, and demonstrate that these thresholds are nondecreasing functions of the relative phase $ heta$.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the X-part approximation for bounding the threshold, then proving the general monotonicity law concerning the relative phase $ heta$, and finally applying this framework to specific abilities like steerability using geometric constraints.

Abstract

Unlike the two-qubit entangled and CHSH-nonlocal sets, the steerable and Bell-nonlocal ones have no known general closed-form criterion. For any such operational ability whose failing states form a convex set closed under local unitaries, and for a Bell mixture with arbitrary noise, the $X$ part of the noise gives an upper bound on the definitive threshold, the Bell weight above which the mixture attains the ability. That bound is the tightest that the seven of the fifteen Pauli expectation values fixing the $X$ part can support. The same theorem shows that a state has the ability whenever its $X$ part does, so partial tomography of a state can certify its ability. When noise is of $X$ form, the mixture carries a relative phase between the noise and Bell coherences that cannot be gauged away by local unitaries, and it controls the threshold through interference. We prove a monotonicity law: the threshold is nondecreasing in this phase, whether or not a closed-form criterion for the ability exists.

Probing quantum chaos near a wormhole throat with a circular string

Authors: Ai-chen Li, Xin-Fei Li, Xuanting Ji
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09613v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper examines quantum chaos near traversable wormhole throats by analyzing the OTOC growth of quantum fluctuations of a probe string. Using a quadratic approximation of the world-sheet action, the authors find evidence of finite-time exponential growth in the OTOCs for both polarizations. This work establishes a transferable, non-holographic diagnostic tool for probing quantum dynamics in regular, curved spacetimes.

Why it may be interesting. The methodology—applying OTOC diagnostics to a regular, horizonless geometry—provides a non-holographic, transferable framework for studying quantum chaos in curved spacetimes, which is highly relevant for theoretical work on quantum gravity and spacetime dynamics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates whether quantum fluctuations of a circular probe string develop a quantum-chaotic response while traversing a wormhole throat, even when the classical radial motion is stable.

Main result. Both the Ellis–Bronnikov and global-monopole wormholes show finite intervals of exponential Out-of-Time-Ordered Correlator (OTOC) growth associated with the first throat passage. This indicates local dynamical sensitivity distinct from asymptotic chaos.

Method. The authors canonically quantize the transverse fluctuation modes by expanding the world-sheet action to quadratic order and constructing OTOC amplitudes from unequal-time commutators.

Model / system. The system involves a circular probe string embedded in curved spacetimes, specifically analyzing the Ellis–Bronnikov and global-monopole wormhole geometries. The analysis uses the Polyakov action and focuses on the quadratic fluctuations around a stable classical trajectory.

Key observables. Out-of-Time-Ordered Correlator (OTOC) amplitudes and the corresponding dimensionless rate of exponential growth.

Important parameters / regimes. Probe energy relative to the throat scale, and the solid-angle deficit ($\Delta$) in the global-monopole extension.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is restricted to the quadratic approximation of the world-sheet action and the circular background ansatz, meaning the results characterize the Gaussian fluctuation sector.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the effective frequency profiles distinguishing polarizations, showing finite intervals of exponential OTOC growth at the throat, and summarizing the dependence of the dimensionless physical-time rates on the solid-angle deficit.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the physical problem by contrasting the regular classical motion with the potential for quantum chaos. It details the mathematical framework by quantizing the transverse fluctuations via the quadratic action, calculates the OTOCs, and presents results for specific wormhole models, concluding with a discussion of the physical interpretation and limitations.

Abstract

We investigate whether quantum fluctuations of a circular probe string develop a quantum-chaotic response while traversing a wormhole throat. The classical circular-string embedding is periodic and radially stable, but its two physical transverse polarizations experience time-dependent tidal potentials. Expanding the world-sheet action to quadratic order, we canonically quantize these modes and construct out-of-time-ordered correlator(OTOC) amplitudes from their unequal-time commutators. For the Ellis--Bronnikov wormhole, both polarizations exhibit finite intervals of approximately exponential OTOC growth associated with the first throat passage. The corresponding dimensionless rate measured with respect to physical time is positive over the parameter range studied and generally decreases as the probe energy is increased relative to the throat scale. In the global-monopole extension, increasing the solid-angle deficit narrows the band of locally amplifiable modes and suppresses the extracted rates; a sufficiently strong defect can nearly quench the radial signal, while the angular channel retains a polarization-dependent non-monotonic structure when the energy-to-throat-scale ratio is small. These quantities characterize finite-time dynamical sensitivity in the Gaussian fluctuation sector and should not be identified with asymptotic many-body chaos or a thermodynamic phase transition. Although the numerical analysis uses two representative wormhole geometries, the construction depends only on covariant world-sheet fluctuations and real-time commutators. It therefore provides a transferable, non-holographic framework for applying quantum-chaos diagnostics directly to quantum probes in curved spacetimes.

The Arithmetic of Spectra: Factorization, Statistics, and Symmetric Functions

Summary. This paper develops a comprehensive mathematical framework to study how the factorization of a quantum system's single-particle spectrum determines its many-body statistics. By translating spectral properties into algebraic identities involving symmetric functions, it systematically relates bosonic and fermionic partition functions. The work is significant for providing a unified combinatorial calculus for quantum statistical mechanics.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a deep mathematical link between spectral theory, combinatorics (symmetric functions), and fundamental quantum statistics, which is highly relevant for understanding correlation functions in many-body quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper studies the arithmetic structure of spectra, focusing on how factorizations of the single-particle spectrum dictate the resulting many-particle statistics (Bose-Einstein vs. Fermi-Dirac). It aims to provide a unified combinatorial framework for calculating canonical partition functions.

Main result. The bosonic and fermionic statistics are systematically related through spectral factorization identities, which can be mapped onto combinatorial structures like descent sets or occupation matrices. The framework successfully relates product spectra to specific algebraic structures governing the partition functions.

Method. The methodology involves representing the spectrum via a formal alphabet and utilizing spectral factorization, connecting tensor factorizations to multiplicative factorizations. Advanced tools include symmetric functions, $\lambda$-rings, and the RSK correspondence to translate spectral properties into combinatorial identities.

Model / system. The analysis is applied to non-interacting quantum systems, generalizing from the imaginary-time propagator ($e^{-eta H_1}$) to an arbitrary trace-class one-particle kernel. Concrete examples include the harmonic trap and the rectangular infinite well, where the spectrum is separable into multiple sectors.

Key observables. Canonical partition functions ($Z_{n,B}, Z_{n,F}$), the spectral alphabet $X$, and cumulants ($\kappa_j$).

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature ($eta$), dimension ($d$), and the structure of the underlying one-particle kernel $K$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the underlying physical structure can be described by spectral alphabets and their arithmetic operations, though limitations exist regarding the natural infinite spectrum.

Figures summary. The notes detail mathematical relationships (Equations 13, 15, 18, 20, 21) showing how additive and product spectra lead to specific forms for bosonic and fermionic partition functions, and how these relate to combinatorial objects like descent sets.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the general framework using spectral alphabets and factorizations for non-interacting systems. It then details the relationship between bosonic and fermionic statistics using symmetric functions and combinatorial tools (like descent sets). Finally, it applies this to specific physical models, such as the harmonic trap, to derive concrete results regarding symmetry and dimensionality.

Abstract

We study factorizations of the single-particle spectrum of non-interacting quantum systems and their consequences for many-particle statistics. Representing the single-particle partition function by a spectral alphabet, tensor factorizations become multiplicative factorizations of the alphabet, which can be lifted to canonical Bose and Fermi partition functions using standard symmetric-function and $λ$-ring identities. We show how product spectra arise from different factorizations, how antisymmetrization can be assigned across an odd number of factors, and which tensor factorizations are compatible with a fixed spectrum. The paper therefore studies spectral factorization and provides a consolidated combinatorial framework for canonical Bose and Fermi partition functions.

The Geometry of Transport in Quantum Walks and Parrondo's Paradox

Authors: Jose Alfredo de Leon, Mariana Pérez-Muralles, Jan Neuser, Carlos Pineda
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09269v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work applies the concept of Parrondo's paradox to quantum walks, reformulating the problem as a geometric condition on the transport vector. It shows that the paradox arises when the combined transport vector lies outside the cone spanned by the individual strategies. This provides a powerful, general criterion for understanding how quantum interference can lead to counter-intuitive macroscopic behavior.

Why it may be interesting. The connection between quantum walk transport geometry and classical paradoxes provides a novel, physically measurable diagnostic tool for analyzing non-trivial quantum dynamics, relevant to open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates Parrondo's paradox—where combining losing strategies yields a winning one—within the framework of quantum transport.

Main result. The paradox is recast as a geometric condition: it occurs if and only if the composite transport vector falls outside the convex cone spanned by the individual strategy vectors.

Method. The analysis uses the transport vector, derived from the coin's steady state in discrete-time quantum walks, to establish a geometric criterion for the paradox.

Model / system. The model is a 1D discrete-time quantum walk (DTQW) governed by a unitary step operator $U=SC$, analyzed using Bloch sphere representations for initial states and transport vectors.

Key observables. Asymptotic velocity ($v$), Transport Vector ($\vec{T}$), Stationary Bloch vector ($\vec{r}_{ ext{stat}}$), and the probability of paradoxical outcomes.

Important parameters / regimes. The initial coin state ($\vec{r}_0$), the structure of the combined operators (composition vs. alternation), and the critical rotation angle ($\chi_c$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis focuses on the long-time, asymptotic limit of the quantum walk dynamics, and certain calculations assume Haar-distributed independence of coin operators.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the Bloch sphere being divided into winning/losing hemispheres based on the sign of the asymptotic velocity, and show the mean position $\langle x angle$ vs. time $t$ for different initial states.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by defining the transport vector, establishing its geometric interpretation, comparing the conditions for paradox in coin composition versus simple alternation, and finally calculating the joint probabilities of outcomes.

Abstract

We study Parrondo's paradox -- the phenomenon where combining losing strategies yields a winning one -- in a minimal discrete-time quantum walk. We introduce the transport vector, encoded in the coin's steady state, whose inner product with the initial coin state gives the walker's asymptotic velocity. More generally, the paradox emerges exactly when the transport vector of the combined strategy falls outside the cone spanned by the individual ones -- a geometric criterion valid for any combination of strategies. It explains, for instance, why composing two coin operators within a single step can produce the paradox while simple alternation between them cannot, since alternation keeps the combined vector confined to the cone. The paradoxical set has nonzero measure, and we compute its probability explicitly in representative cases. This casts the paradox as one instance of designing reachable transport in quantum walks.

Unbounded degree overhead for Alice-conditioned quantum Bell certificates

Authors: Fumin Wang
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10162v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper rigorously analyzes the resource cost of certifying quantum Bell inequalities when measurements are conditioned on one party's outcomes. It proves that this conditioning imposes an unbounded overhead in the required algebraic degree compared to standard certification methods. The work significantly separates the complexity of standard quantum bounds from those requiring single-question structure.

Why it may be interesting. It provides deep theoretical insights into the limitations of polynomial methods (like SOS/NPA) to capture the full complexity of quantum correlations, which is fundamental to quantum information theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the resource overhead required to certify quantum Bell inequalities when conditioning the test on one party's measurements (Alice-conditioned certificates). It aims to compare this overhead against standard certification methods.

Main result. The required certification degree for Alice-conditioned certificates grows unboundedly as the Bell test approaches certain endpoints, demonstrating a separation between the resources needed for standard versus conditioned certification.

Method. The authors use the framework of the NPA hierarchy, formulating the problem as a Sum-of-Squares (SOS) decomposition problem, and employ techniques like Fejér-weighted traces and Gram matrix analysis.

Model / system. The study focuses on quantum Bell inequalities, specifically using the CHSH scenario, and analyzing the required levels of the NPA hierarchy to certify quantum correlations.

Key observables. The primary observables are the minimum required certificate levels ($d_{std}$ vs. $d_{os}$) and the resulting bounds on the CHSH expectation value ($s$).

Important parameters / regimes. The parameter $\alpha$ controls the specific Bell test instance, and the analysis examines behavior near endpoint tilts ($\epsilon o 0$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the assumption that the optimal certificate lies on the boundary of the PSD cone, and it distinguishes between numerical saturation fits and true exact upper bounds.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the divergence of the required conditioned degree (purple staircase) from the standard, fixed degree line as the test parameter approaches an endpoint.

Paper structure. The paper develops results by first establishing the unbounded overhead for conditioned certificates (Theorem 1), then characterizing finite exactness intervals (Theorem 2), and finally providing rigorous, solver-free verification of these optimal bounds.

Abstract

Requiring each sum-of-squares term to involve only one of Alice's measurement questions can impose an unbounded certification cost. In the simplest Bell scenario, we prove that no finite level of the Alice-conditioned NPA hierarchy contains all standard level-two Bell certificates. An explicit family of truncated positive functionals on the infinite dihedral group exceeds the tilted-CHSH quantum bound at every prescribed finite level, while a standard degree-two certificate is exact. A Fejer-weighted trace reduces positivity to a rank-one subtraction from a moving-average Gram matrix. The required conditioned level grows at least as $(2-α)^{-1/2}$ near the endpoint tilt. This bounds the degree of exact nice-SOS inputs to compiled-game soundness proofs. Consequently, no finite conditioned level certifies the entire optimal CHSH randomness tradeoff against quantum side information, although standard level two does. Away from the endpoint, we prove a sharp one-level cost throughout $α\in[13/10,3/2]$, using optimal-strategy kernels and exact Bernstein matrix positivity to certify a continuous interval. The results separate ordinary SOS degree from the resources keyed by single-question certificate structure.

Wasserstein Stability, Couplings Across Volumes, and the $1+1/d$ Moment Thresholds in the Edwards--Anderson Model

Authors: Mauris Chueng, Hexiang Wang, Keheng Zhu
Type: theory · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09680v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper rigorously analyzes the thermodynamic limit of the Edwards-Anderson Ising model under random disorder. It proves that the convergence of the system's pressure to a stable infinite-volume limit depends critically on the disorder couplings having a finite moment of order $1+1/d$. This threshold exponent is shown to be sharp, providing deep mathematical insight into the role of disorder fluctuations.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical statistical mechanics, the rigorous mathematical tools developed—especially the analysis of convergence thresholds based on moment conditions and coupling stability—are highly relevant to understanding critical phenomena in complex, disordered quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the stability and convergence of the quenched pressure in the Edwards-Anderson Ising model when the disorder couplings are spatially inhomogeneous.

Main result. It establishes that the existence of a universal-coupling limit for the pressure requires the disorder couplings to satisfy the moment condition $\mathbb{E}|J|^{1+1/d} < \infty$, and this exponent is proven to be optimal.

Method. The analysis combines concepts from probability theory, specifically using the 1-Wasserstein distance to quantify stability, and employing strong limit theorems (like Borel-Cantelli) to relate convergence in probability to almost sure convergence.

Model / system. The system is the nearest-neighbor Edwards-Anderson Ising model, characterized by random couplings $J_{x,i}$ on a lattice $\Lambda_L$. The study focuses on the quenched pressure, defined as the normalized logarithm of the partition function, $p^L(eta, J)$.

Key observables. The quenched pressure, $p_b^L(eta, J)$, and its convergence properties under different boundary conditions (free vs. periodic).

Important parameters / regimes. The spatial dimension $d$, the inverse temperature $eta$, and the critical moment exponent $1+1/d$.

Assumptions / limitations. The disorder $\{J_{x,i}\}$ is assumed to be i.i.d. initially, but the core results extend to spatially inhomogeneous disorder, requiring analysis via joint couplings across volumes.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes basic convergence results using the 1-Wasserstein distance for the free/periodic pressures. It then develops the universal-coupling criterion, culminating in the proof that the $1+1/d$ moment condition is necessary and sufficient for this strong convergence property.

Abstract

We study the quenched pressure of the nearest-neighbor Edwards--Anderson Ising model with free and periodic boundary conditions. First, we prove that the infinite-volume pressure is $βd$-Lipschitz in the coupling law for the $1$-Wasserstein distance, yielding quantitative thermodynamic limits for spatially inhomogeneous disorder. Second, for periodic volumes, we prove that almost-sure convergence under every joint coupling of the finite-volume disorder arrays is equivalent to complete convergence of the one-volume pressure laws. Third, we prove that $\mathbb{E}\vert{}J\vert{}^{1+1/d}<\infty$ guarantees this universal-coupling conclusion. We further show that this exponent is optimal among uniform power-moment assumptions: for every $1\leq q<1+1/d$, there is a centered symmetric law with finite $q$-th moment for which canonical nested volumes converge almost surely, whereas independently resampled volumes with the same fixed-volume marginals converge in probability but not almost surely. Finally, in dimension one, we prove that the first-moment condition is also necessary for a finite limiting pressure.

Accelerating A*-Based Algorithms for Decoding Quantum Low-Density Parity-Check Codes

Authors: Lamia Yous, Francisco Garcia Herrero, Mark F. Flanagan
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10056v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces a novel, hybrid decoding framework for QLDPC codes by combining the speed of Belief Propagation (BP) with the accuracy of the A*-based Tesseract decoder. A key component is a reliability-based gating mechanism that filters uncertain BP outputs, allowing Tesseract to focus only on the most challenging residual errors. This significantly reduces computational overhead while preserving high error correction performance.

Why it may be interesting. The development of faster, more robust decoding algorithms is crucial for scaling up quantum computers, making this work highly relevant to the theoretical understanding of fault-tolerant quantum computation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is developing an efficient decoder for Quantum Low-Density Parity-Check (QLDPC) codes to enable reliable, large-scale quantum computation.

Main result. The proposed hybrid BP-Tesseract decoding framework achieves substantial reductions in decoding complexity (up to 15x fewer expanded nodes) while maintaining the logical error rate of the stand-alone Tesseract decoder.

Method. A two-stage hybrid approach is used: Belief Propagation (BP) runs first to generate soft information and hard decisions; if BP fails to converge, a reliability-based gating mechanism filters the output, which then guides the A*-based Tesseract search on the residual error.

Model / system. The system involves QLDPC codes used for Quantum Error Correction (QEC), often constructed via the CSS framework. Decoding is performed on the Tanner graph structure associated with these codes.

Key observables. Decoding Complexity (number of expanded nodes), Logical Error Rate (LER), Syndrome ($s$), and soft information (per-qubit confidence levels).

Important parameters / regimes. Physical error rate ($p$), reliability threshold ($ au$), and the gating parameter ($p_{ ext{gated}}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The core assumption is that combining the speed of BP with the guaranteed accuracy of Tesseract, guided by a reliability filter, overcomes the computational bottlenecks of the standalone Tesseract decoder.

Figures summary. Figures compare the node expansion reduction factor, mean number of node expansions, and LER performance across the three decoder types (Tesseract, BP-Tesseract, and the proposed gated BP-Tesseract) for T1 and BB codes.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the need for efficient QEC decoders, reviews existing methods (BP and Tesseract) highlighting their weaknesses, and then proposes and validates the hybrid BP-Tesseract framework using a reliability-based gating mechanism.

Abstract

Quantum low-density parity-check (QLDPC) codes represent a promising approach for error correction in quantum computing. The recently proposed Tesseract decoder uses the A* search algorithm that guarantees finding the most likely error pattern. However, practical implementations of Tesseract often involve an extremely large graph, and the inherently sequential nature of the search results in high computational overhead and long runtime. To improve decoding efficiency, we propose a two-stage decoding framework. First, a belief propagation (BP) decoder efficiently processes the syndrome. This step generates hard decisions (a binary error vector) and soft information (per-qubit confidence levels). In non-convergent BP cases, a gating mechanism examines the BP decoder's output to identify and filter out qubits with oscillating confidence values. In these cases, the refined output serves as input to Tesseract, which then attempts to identify and correct any residual errors. Abstract

In this note, we show that the generalized Bures--Wasserstein distance induced by the generalized fidelity does not satisfy the data processing inequality in dimensions greater than two. In dimension two, although we do not settle the DPI in full generality, we derive two sufficient conditions guaranteeing it by using an explicit formula for the real part of the generalized fidelity. Furthermore, we find a necessary and sufficient condition under which the generalized fidelity reduces to the Uhlmann fidelity.

Induced random mixed states are symmetric Dirichlet mixtures

Authors: Brian T. Kirby, Alexander C. B. Greenwood, Sanjaya Lohani, Joseph M. Lukens
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09421v1

Summary. This paper proves a deep mathematical equivalence between random mixed quantum states generated by tracing out an environment and a statistical mixture called the Mai-Alquier distribution. This identification is highly valuable because it allows researchers to calculate complex quantum observables, such as purity moments and determinants, using simpler tools from the theory of Dirichlet distributions and Haar averages. It unifies the theoretical understanding of induced random mixed states.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful mathematical tool to simplify calculations in open quantum systems by mapping physical environmental tracing processes onto well-understood statistical ensembles (Dirichlet mixtures).

Main problem. The paper establishes an exact equivalence between random mixed states induced by tracing out an environment and a specific statistical mixture known as the Mai-Alquier (MA) distribution.

Main result. This equivalence allows complex calculations involving induced random mixed states (like purity moments and determinants) to be recast into manageable calculations involving Dirichlet moments and low-order Haar averages.

Abstract

We establish an exact equivalence in distribution between $D$-dimensional random mixed states induced by partial traces over $K$-dimensional environments and the Mai-Alquier distribution, a mixture of $K$ independent Haar-random pure states weighted by a symmetric Dirichlet distribution. This identification recasts a class of expectation values for induced ensembles into calculations involving Dirichlet moments and low-order Haar averages on a single-system state space. As applications, we recover exact purity moments up to fourth order, derive the mean Hilbert--Schmidt distance between independent induced ensembles with possibly different environment dimensions, and obtain exact average determinants. These results provide a unified and constructive perspective on induced random mixed states and on the evaluation of quantities such as purity moments, overlaps, and determinants.

Italian Business-to-Business Invoicing Data: A Network Analysis

Authors: Valerio Astuti, Daniele Piras

Summary. This paper analyzes the structure of the Italian B2B commercial network using massive invoicing data, modeling firms as nodes and transactions as weighted edges. It finds the network possesses a scale-free structure and characterizes how economic importance (centrality) is unevenly distributed among a few key firms. The findings offer quantitative insights into the underlying connectivity and vulnerabilities of the national production system.

Why it may be interesting. The application of complex network theory, which shares mathematical tools with statistical physics (e.g., power-law distributions, critical phenomena), to a real-world economic system provides a model for analyzing emergent collective behavior in non-physical systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper characterizes the structure of the Italian production network using comprehensive firm-to-firm commercial transaction data to derive insights into the Italian economy.

Main result. The network exhibits a scale-free structure, indicated by heavy-tailed distributions for buyer/seller counts and centrality measures. Furthermore, the tail exponents for buyer-side distributions are found to be lower than those for seller-side distributions.

Method. Network analysis techniques are employed, treating firms as nodes and invoices as weighted, directed edges. Key metrics analyzed include degree distributions, network centrality (Eigenvector, Katz, PageRank), and structural properties like assortativity.

Model / system. The system is the network of Italian Business-to-Business (B2B) commercial relationships, constructed from electronic invoicing data from the Italian Tax Office. Nodes are firms, and edges are weighted by the total monetary amount exchanged.

Key observables. Distribution of buyers/sellers per firm, network centrality measures, tail exponents of degree distributions, network density, and mean degree.

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis is based on the 2019 Italian electronic invoicing data, focusing on the B2B subset of transactions.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is static, and while the data coverage is comprehensive, the comparison of network metrics across different international datasets is cautioned against due to potential methodological variations.

Abstract

We present a comprehensive description of the network of Italian Business-to-Business commercial relationships, based on the universe of electronic invoices collected by the Italian Tax Office. The firm-to-firm detail of the data is exploited to describe the distribution of the numbers of buyers (customers) and sellers (suppliers) per firm, the centrality of each firm in the production network, and the average distance between firms. We characterize for the first time the firm-to-firm network describing the Italian production system, and present its geographic and sectoral breakdown. The analysis reveals heavy tailed distributions for the numbers of buyers and sellers per firm, implying a scale-free structure of the network. The distributions of centrality values display heavy tails as well, indicating a strong concentration of importance in a relatively small number of firms. We estimate the tail exponents for all these distributions, finding in all cases lower exponents for downstream (buyer-side) distributions than for the upstream (seller-side) ones.
