Summary. This work develops an 'information ladder' to quantify how well neutrino oscillation parameters can be determined from reactor antineutrino measurements at JUNO. By comparing Quantum, Flavor, and Event-level Fisher Information, the authors map out the information loss across the measurement chain. They conclude that while some parameters suffer degradation, the experiment remains highly sensitive enough to achieve sub-percent precision on key mass splittings.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous application of quantum information theory to particle physics measurements provides a powerful framework for assessing experimental potential, which is relevant to any field dealing with measurement limits in quantum systems.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theoretical framework by deriving the QFI matrix elements, then calculates the flavor-specific and event-level Fisher matrices, and finally compares these metrics to quantify parameter sensitivity and information loss for JUNO.

Abstract

Since neutrinos are quantum systems inherently, the precision with which oscillation parameters can be estimated ultimately depends on how much information about these parameters is encoded in the neutrino state and how efficiently that information can be extracted through measurement. In this work, we quantify how information encoded in reactor antineutrino states flows through the measurement process to the events observed at the detector, using quantum and classical Fisher information. We establish the information ladder for JUNO, revealing that the loss of precision across different information levels is strongly parameter dependent. We demonstrate that the JUNO configuration approaches the optimal statistical limit for the oscillation parameters of the solar sector, while information on $θ_{13}$ and $Δm_{31}^{2}$ is significantly degraded by the measurement strategy and detector effects. Despite this information loss, the remaining information is sufficient for JUNO to achieve sub-percent precision on $Δm_{31}^{2}$ within six years.

Summary. This work introduces a quantum Barankin bound, a tighter lower bound on estimation error than the standard QCRB, by utilizing a non-symmetric right-division framework. The authors provide analytic formulas for this bound specifically for Gaussian states. This offers a more powerful tool for determining the ultimate precision limits in quantum parameter estimation, particularly in few-shot regimes.

Why it may be interesting. The development of tighter, analytically tractable bounds for parameter estimation is crucial for understanding the fundamental limits of quantum measurements, especially in regimes where standard QCRB fails.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper proposes a quantum generalization of the Barankin bound as an alternative to the Quantum Cramér-Rao Bound (QCRB) for quantum parameter estimation.

Main result. They derive a novel, non-symmetric quantum Barankin bound using a right-division superoperator, which is shown to be generally tighter than previous symmetric bounds.

Method. The methodology involves extending classical estimation theory bounds to the quantum domain, deriving explicit analytic formulas for Gaussian states using matrix algebra and characteristic functions.

Model / system. The theory applies to quantum parameter estimation for a family of quantum states $\{\hat{ ho}_{ ilde{\lambda}}\}_{ ilde{\lambda} \in \Lambda}$, with specific analytic derivations provided for Gaussian states.

Key observables. Mean Squared Error (MSE) of estimators, Quantum Fisher Information (QFI), and the Barankin matrix.

Important parameters / regimes. The parameter set $\Lambda$ and the specific structure of the Gaussian states (defined by mean vectors and covariance matrices).

Assumptions / limitations. The derivation relies on the existence and properties of the right-division superoperator and the assumption that the relevant operators are trace class.

Paper structure. The paper develops the bound by first establishing the general framework using the right-division operator, comparing it to the symmetric version, and then specializing the results to derive analytic formulas for Gaussian states.

Abstract

We propose a quantum version of the Barankin bound as an alternative to the quantum Cramér-Rao bound for quantum parameter estimation. The quantum Barankin bound provides a lower bound on the mean squared error of estimators satisfying arbitrarily chosen bias constraints at arbitrarily chosen parameter points. In particular, unbiasedness over the entire parameter space can be imposed, yielding precision limits for globally unbiased quantum parameter estimation. In contrast to the recently derived quantum Barankin bound, which is based on a symmetric division superoperator, our bound is based on a nonsymmetric right-division superoperator. This formulation enables us to find analytic expressions for the Barankin matrix for Gaussian states, which allows for an efficient calculation of the bound. We demonstrate the usefulness of our results by applying them to various examples that exhibit the threshold effect in the few-shot regime, which is invisible to the standard quantum Cramér-Rao bound approach.

Tuning Charge Density Wave transitions through lattice strain in NbSe3

Summary. This work experimentally demonstrates that applying uniaxial lattice strain to NbSe3 significantly tunes its two distinct Charge Density Wave (CDW) transitions. By combining X-ray diffraction with transport measurements, the authors establish a quantitative, linear correlation between the material's structural parameters and the critical temperatures of the CDW states. This highlights the extreme sensitivity of correlated electronic phases to mechanical boundary conditions.

Why it may be interesting. The strong coupling between mechanical strain and electronic phase transitions (CDW) is a key area of condensed matter physics. Understanding how external mechanical fields can tune correlated electronic states provides insights applicable to designing novel electronic materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how applying mechanical lattice strain (tensile stress) tunes the Charge Density Wave (CDW) transitions in the quasi-one-dimensional material NbSe3.

Main result. The CDW critical temperatures ($T_{c1}, T_{c2}$) show strong, predictable linear correlations with the applied strain and resulting lattice parameters, suggesting the CDW state is highly sensitive to lattice geometry.

Method. The research combines in-situ X-ray diffraction (XRD) to measure lattice strain and elastic constants with transport measurements to track the temperature-dependent electrical resistance anomalies associated with the CDW transitions.

Model / system. The system is NbSe3, a prototypical quasi-1D conductor known for exhibiting multiple CDW states. The analysis models the coupling between structural distortions (lattice strain) and electronic instabilities (CDW formation) using elastic theory and electronic susceptibility calculations.

Key observables. CDW critical temperatures ($T_{c1}=145 ext{ K}, T_{c2}=59 ext{ K}$), electrical resistance changes ($\alpha_1, \alpha_2$), lattice parameters ($a, b, c, \alpha, eta, \gamma$), and elastic constants (Young's moduli, Poisson's coefficients).

Important parameters / regimes. Applied uniaxial tensile stresses ($\sigma_b, \sigma_c$), lattice strain ($\epsilon$), and the ratio of lattice parameters ($c/a$, $1/b$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the linear elastic regime for strain calculations and that the changes in conductivity are dominated by modifications to the band curvature rather than just the superconducting gap magnitude.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the evolution of the six lattice parameters versus applied forces, and show the temperature dependence of resistance curves under varying tensile stresses, mapping out the shifts in $T_c$ and resistance jumps.

Paper structure. The paper systematically presents mechanical characterization (XRD/elastic constants) followed by electronic characterization (transport measurements), establishing a quantitative link between the measured structural changes and the resulting shifts in the CDW transition temperatures and associated transport signatures.

Abstract

The Charge Density Wave (CDW) state is a perfect example of a combined structural and electronic state, both characterized by a periodic lattice distortion and an electronic modulation of condensed electrons, resulting from electron-phonon coupling. They are thus prone to be tuned by lattice strain. NbSe3 is a prototypical example of CDW states, with a chain-like structure displaying two CDWs at 145K and 59K, with wavevectors along and inclined with respect to the chain axis b. Here, we report on the evolution of the lattice structure and CDW properties in the quasi-one-dimensional charge-density-wave system NbSe3 under tensile stresses applied along and perpendicular to the chains axis b by a combination of X-ray diffraction and transport measurements. We find that the lattice structure show exotic Poissons coefficients and strongly anisotropic Youngs moduli while the evolution of the electrical resistance demonstrates shifts of both charge-density-wave critical temperatures that are strongly correlated with the lattice parameters. The two CDW transitions display different behaviours under applied stresses, and suggest that a modification of the band curvature leads to the observed transport signatures.

Why three? A two-level system with four mutually unbiased questions

Authors: Jonte R. Hance
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10078v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 3/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper explores the theoretical limits of measurement in quantum mechanics by examining the concept of mutually unbiased questions (MUBs). It shows that while a four-question system (hyperbit) can be mathematically constructed, it fails to satisfy several fundamental physical criteria—such as those governing dynamics or algebraic closure—that are inherent to the standard qubit model. This suggests that the structure of quantum theory strongly constrains the number and nature of possible measurements.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a deep theoretical constraint on the structure of quantum measurements, suggesting that physical reality might enforce specific algebraic symmetries (like those found in qubits) that are violated by mathematically possible, but physically inconsistent, extensions.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the maximum number of mutually unbiased questions (MUBs) a two-level system can support, contrasting the standard quantum mechanical result (three for a qubit) with potential extensions.

Main result. The algebraic structure dictates that the maximum number of unbiased questions is always odd (e.g., 3 or 5), suggesting that a hypothetical four-question system (hyperbit) lacks key physical properties of a true quantum system.

Method. The analysis combines algebraic constraints (Clifford algebras, anticommuting involutions) with geometric modeling (Bloch balls, 24-cell) and quantum information diagnostics (contextuality testing, Kirkwood-Dirac distributions).

Model / system. The primary model is the two-level system, extended to a hypothetical 'hyperbit' supporting four MUBs, which is mathematically realized as a four-dimensional Bloch ball. The analysis often embeds this into a two-qubit system for physical realization.

Key observables. Mutual Unbiased Questions (MUBs), preparation contextuality (tested via the hexagon subtheory), Kirkwood-Dirac imaginary parts, and the four properties (A-D) that distinguish qubits from the hyperbit.

Important parameters / regimes. The number of questions ($n=2, 3, 4, 5$), the contextuality threshold ($\lambda > 2/3$), and the algebraic structure of the underlying operators.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the validity of single-system postulates for the hyperbit, while the core argument relies on identifying which properties (like energy observability or algebraic closure) are violated when moving from $n=3$ to $n=4$.

Figures summary. Table I summarizes four key properties (A-D) that are compared across different systems (rebit, qubit, hyperbit, H-bit, O-bit), highlighting the failure of the hyperbit to satisfy all of them.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by establishing the algebraic condition for MUBs, deriving the structural limitations (odd number of questions), constructing the mathematical hyperbit, and then rigorously testing this model against known physical constraints (dynamics, closure, composability) using diagnostic tools.

Abstract

Two sharp yes/no questions are mutually unbiased if and only if their involutions anticommute. A set of unbiased questions is therefore a Clifford algebra, whose maximal size is odd, meaning four anticommuting questions typically imply a fifth: the two-level systems of real, complex and quaternionic quantum theory allow two, three and five questions, while four is skipped. Without this operator product restriction, a two-level system with four unbiased questions exists as the four-dimensional Bloch ball, the hyperbit. The single-system postulates that pick out the Jordan state spaces allow such a model, and only energy observability rules it out. We show what it lacks, and realise it inside two qubits: its Kirkwood-Dirac imaginary parts are hidden $\mathfrak{so}(4)$ generators, its composites allow entanglement but no interaction, and its 24-cell of states stays Spekkens preparation contextual down to depolarising strength $2/3$. In fermionic terms, the four questions are the Majorana operators of two modes while the fifth is parity: the four-question system is therefore the sector removed by parity superselection.

Summary. This work compares the thermodynamic irreversibility of a finite-particle quantum system (Bose-Hubbard model) against its classical field analog. By analyzing entropy generation during a heat-engine cycle, the authors show that retaining energy information beyond just the spatial record allows the quantum system to perform work with lower entropy cost. This quantum advantage, however, vanishes in the large particle number limit.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a rigorous, quantitative comparison of quantum vs. classical thermodynamics in the context of finite, non-equilibrium processes, directly addressing the role of quantum coherence in thermodynamic efficiency.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates whether the operational reduction of information in a finite quantum system, compared to its classical counterpart, leads to a less costly thermodynamic process regarding entropy generation.

Main result. The quantum system exhibits smaller entropy generation and achieves higher work efficiency than the classical system under matched thermal resources, although this advantage diminishes as the particle number increases toward the classical limit.

Method. The analysis uses a heat-engine cycle comparison, quantifying information loss via entropy gaps derived from comparing state representations constrained by the spatial record (R) versus the combined energy and record (E, R).

Model / system. The study compares a finite-particle Bose–Hubbard chain (quantum system) with its number-conserving classical-field counterpart. The Hamiltonians are matched by setting specific coupling constants.

Key observables. Entropy generation ($\Sigma$), work extracted, information gaps ($G_R, A_E, I_{E,R}$), and the comparison of $\Sigma_Q$ vs $\Sigma_{cl}$.

Important parameters / regimes. Total number of bosons ($Q$), number of sites ($L$), and the confinement parameter ($\Lambda$).

Assumptions / limitations. The comparison assumes matched thermal resources, and the advantage of the quantum system is shown to fade as $Q$ grows.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the matched quantum-classical construction, showing the three state representations ($ ho, ar{ ho}R, ar{ ho}$) and the corresponding entropy gaps, while supplementary figures confirm numerical stability and robustness.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the problem by defining the quantum and classical models, develops the theoretical framework using entropy decomposition identities ($G_R = A_E + I_{E,R}$), applies this to a heat-engine cycle comparison, and concludes by analyzing the resulting entropy costs and their dependence on particle number.

Abstract

Thermodynamics is useful because it lets us predict and control complex systems from a few accessible quantities when microscopic reconstruction is impractical. We ask whether this operational reduction can be less costly in a finite quantum system than in a matched classical one. We compare a few-boson Bose--Hubbard chain with its number-conserving classical-field counterpart under the same driving and the same coarse spatial record. The quantum system relaxes more in the observed particle distribution, yet the retained mean energy resolves more of the microscopic structure hidden by that record, leaving less information unusable and smaller entropy generation. We then close the process into a heat-engine cycle whose controls use only the measured mean energy and spatial record, not full state tomography; at matched thermal resources, the smaller entropy generation gives more work and higher efficiency. The advantage fades as the particle number grows toward the classical-field regime.

Summary. This paper compares two diagnostic tools—entanglement asymmetry and trace distance—to study symmetry restoration dynamics, particularly in the context of the Quantum Mpemba Effect. Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous theoretical framework for distinguishing between different types of quantum information encoded in symmetry measures versus energy-based measures, which is crucial for interpreting experimental results in open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates whether conventional energy-based diagnostics, such as the trace distance, accurately capture the dynamics of symmetry restoration observed via entanglement asymmetry in quantum many-body systems.

Main result. The diagnostic used depends critically on the system's symmetry: for charge symmetry, the trace distance effectively tracks entanglement asymmetry, but for permutation symmetry, a significant residual contribution causes the two diagnostics to diverge.

Method. The authors combine numerical simulations with an analytical decomposition of the trace distance into symmetry-coherence and residual contributions to compare its evolution against entanglement asymmetry.

Model / system. The study analyzes closed spin-1/2 quantum systems, employing Hamiltonians that can exhibit either charge symmetry or permutation symmetry. Initial states are engineered to break these symmetries, allowing for the study of relaxation dynamics.

Key observables. Entanglement asymmetry ($\Delta S_A$), Trace Distance ($\Delta(\hat{ ho}_A)(t)$), Symmetry-coherence, and Residual contributions.

Important parameters / regimes. The degree of initial symmetry breaking, controlled by parameters like the tilt angle $ heta$ or $\Delta heta$.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the problem by comparing two diagnostics for the Quantum Mpemba Effect. It then analyzes the dynamics for two distinct symmetry types (charge vs. permutation), deriving the necessary mathematical decompositions and presenting comparative results.

Abstract

Understanding symmetry restoration in isolated quantum many-body systems is an important problem in nonequilibrium many-body quantum physics. Recent studies have shown that the quantum Mpemba effect can be characterized through entanglement asymmetry, where states with stronger initial symmetry breaking restore symmetry faster. However, it remains unclear whether conventional energy-based measures, such as the trace distance, capture the same phenomenon. We investigate this question in closed spin-$1/2$ quantum systems with different symmetries by analyzing the dynamics of symmetry-breaking initial states. Combining numerical simulations with an analytical decomposition of the trace distance into symmetry-coherence and residual contributions, we identify the conditions under which trace distance tracks entanglement asymmetry and reproduces the Mpemba--like behavior observed in it. For charge symmetry, the residual contribution is negligible, making the trace distance effectively governed by symmetry-sector coherences. In contrast, for permutation symmetry, a significant residual contribution leads to qualitatively different relaxation dynamics. Our results establish when conventional energy-based diagnostics reliably capture symmetry-restoration dynamics and clarify the distinct physical information encoded by entanglement asymmetry and trace distance.

Summary. This theoretical paper rigorously analyzes quantum field quantization in rotating frames, comparing different mathematical descriptions (rigid vs. TT). The findings establish a consistent framework for understanding vacuum fluctuations in non-inertial settings.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the subtleties of defining vacuum states and thermalization in non-inertial frames, which is critical for understanding quantum field theory in curved or accelerating backgrounds relevant to quantum optics and open quantum systems.

Main problem. The paper provides a unified analysis of scalar-field quantization and detector response in relativistic rotating frames, comparing rigid and Trocheris-Takeno (TT) descriptions in Minkowski spacetime.

Main result. The authors show that while neither rotating time flow selects a global ground state, a circular Unruh-DeWitt detector in the Minkowski vacuum exhibits a stationary, non-thermal response, demonstrating the mutual consistency of these seemingly disparate effects.

Method. The analysis employs mode decomposition using Bogoliubov coefficients, compares different coordinate representations (rigid vs. TT), and calculates the detector response using Hadamard subtraction.

Model / system. The system involves a real scalar field quantized in the Minkowski vacuum, coupled to a two-level Unruh-DeWitt detector, analyzed under the influence of rotating coordinates.

Abstract

We provide a unified analysis of scalar-field quantization and detector response in relativistic rotating frames, comparing rigid and Trocheris-Takeno (TT) descriptions in unbounded Minkowski spacetime. We distinguish three questions that are often conflated: (i) how a fixed quantum state is represented in rotating coordinates, (ii) whether rotating time evolution selects a global ground state, and (iii) how a rotating detector responds to the vacuum. Coordinate-transformed rigid and TT modes span the same positive-frequency subspace as inertial modes: their Bogoliubov beta coefficients vanish and their mode sums reproduce the Minkowski Wightman function. Neither rotating time flow selects a global scalar ground state, although for different reasons: the rigid generator is not globally timelike and is unbounded below, whereas TT time translation is not a Killing symmetry. Nevertheless, a circular Unruh-DeWitt detector in the Minkowski vacuum has a stationary, nonzero response. We relate this response to negative corotating frequencies, evaluate it after an explicit Hadamard subtraction, and show that it is nonthermal. Thus vanishing Bogoliubov mixing, the absence of a symmetry-selected rotating vacuum, and detector excitation are mutually consistent.

Abstract

Understanding how thermal fluctuations modify or suppress quantum-enhanced sensing is a central problem in quantum metrology. Closely related questions arise in quantum thermodynamics, particularly regarding the relation between the work induced by a signal and the information acquired by a sensor. Here, we establish general relations among the sensitivity of thermal quantum states, as quantified by different quantum Fisher information metrics, their temperature dependence, and the work induced by a unitary signal. We further show that, at high temperature, all monotone QFI metrics coincide to leading order with a universal quantity that can be expressed as the variance of a simple observable and itself defines a lesser-known QFI metric. We illustrate these results using harmonic chains of bosonic modes relevant to trapped-ion platforms and quantum field-theoretic models.

Summary. This paper introduces a novel Block Tensor Train (Block-TT) framework to perform Quantum State Tomography (QST) for high-dimensional quantum systems. Abstract

A fault-tolerant quantum computer runs behind a telemetry stream logging syndromes, decoder actions, resets and timing separately from the answer. Can it reveal the logical input? For a distance-$d$ rotated surface-code memory on a fixed schedule of $T=Θ(d)$ rounds, under three stated hypotheses (sector-scalar honest backbone, transcript locality, Kotecky-Preiss smallness), the channel from logical qubit to transcript is $e^{-Θ(d)}$-close in diamond norm to one that ignores the input. A statement of this kind follows generically from correctability-privacy duality. Anisotropy does not. Each logical axis pays the distance of its own coset, so under amplitude damping the computational-basis label is governed by the code's $Z$-distance $d_Z\ge d_{\min}$ and not by the code distance. Two codes of quantum distance $1$ make the gap concrete. A phase-flip code's $X$-syndrome transcript is exactly input-independent under unobserved damping, while a repetition code leaks at first order. A matched converse identifies the records that do expose it, among them a lattice-surgery parity readout. On a 156-qubit superconducting processor our sufficient certificate misses by $21.5\times$, so the theorem cannot be invoked there. Measured directly, a $d_Z=1$ memory's record identifies its input with total variation $\ge 0.927$ under randomised, label-balanced acquisition. Holding the code fixed and varying the damping exposure reproduces the parameter-free law, with exponent $0.85\pm0.03$ against a predicted $0.86$. Randomized encoding returns the statistic to the floor at no two-qubit-gate cost. Fault tolerance does not grant transcript privacy. It relocates it, and only to the logical state, not to the circuit's identity.

Abstract

Modern quantum algorithms are increasingly formulated as coherent procedures for implementing polynomial transformations of operators and singular values. This perspective provides a powerful and unifying language for quantum algorithm design, connecting a wide range of distinct problems through five closely related key tools: block-encoding, qubitization, QSP, QSVT and GQSP. Block-encoding embeds non-unitary matrices into larger unitaries; qubitization converts block-encodings into structured operators; QSP, QSVT and GQSP enable polynomial transformations with near-optimal query complexity. Together, these techniques form a general toolkit for transforming matrix functions into implementable quantum circuits. This paper develops these techniques from first principles as a unified framework for constructing quantum algorithms. We apply this framework to representative applications to highlight design principles and demonstrate how distinct algorithms can be constructed from a unified sequence of operator transformations. A central contribution is a systematic decision workflow for selecting the appropriate approach according to the operator structure and the desired transformation polynomial. This perspective clarifies when direct GQSP or through qubitization, or Laurent expansion, or QSVT is most appropriate. We organize algorithmic design into an end-to-end pipeline: identifying the target matrix function, constructing an appropriate block-encoding, determining the relevant spectral domain, designing a polynomial or Laurent-polynomial approximation, synthesizing the phase factors, and translating the transformation into an executable quantum circuit. By applying this unified framework to example applications, we showcase a practical methodology for reasoning, designing, and implementing quantum algorithms based on polynomial transformations.
