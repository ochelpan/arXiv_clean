A First-Principles Multiscale Framework for Topological Superconductivity

Authors: Christopher L. Jacobs, Baishali Mandal, Taehwan Jung, Purna P. Paudel, Jason Kawasaki, Tudor D. Stanescu, Subhasish Mandal
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10381v1

Summary. This paper introduces a comprehensive, first-principles computational framework to study topological superconductivity in realistic materials. By integrating DFT, Wannier models, and BdG theory, the authors successfully predict TSC in Fe-based superconductors and heterostructures. This work provides a quantitative, materials-by-design pathway for engineering candidate platforms for Majorana physics.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on condensed matter, the development of a rigorous, multi-scale computational framework that links electronic structure (DFT) to topological invariants (Chern number) is highly relevant for modeling complex quantum phenomena in engineered quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core problem is developing a unified, materials-informed computational framework to theoretically predict and understand topological superconductivity (TSC) in real-world materials.

Main result. The framework successfully predicts multiple topological superconducting phases in Fe-based materials, highlighting FeSe/GaAs heterostructures as particularly promising platforms for realizing TSC.

Method. The approach combines Density Functional Theory (DFT) for electronic structure, Wannier-based low-energy Hamiltonians, Bogoliubov-de Gennes (BdG) theory, and Chern number calculations.

Model / system. The study applies this framework to bulk-like and monolayer FeTeSe, and to FeSe/GaAs heterostructures, investigating both intrinsic and proximity-induced TSC.

Key observables. Topological gaps, topological invariants (Chern number), and the presence of robust Majorana zero modes (MZMs).

Important parameters / regimes. Chemical potential ($\mu$), Zeeman field ($V_z$), and the presence of Rashba-active states and strong superconducting pairing.

Assumptions / limitations. The framework assumes that the low-energy physics can be captured by combining DFT results into effective Hamiltonians, though challenges remain in fully self-consistently treating unconventional pairing.

Figures summary. Figure 1(a) illustrates the complete multiscale workflow, connecting DFT calculations through Wannier-based models to the final topological invariant evaluations.

Paper structure. The paper follows a logical progression: first, establishing the need for a unified framework; second, detailing the combination of DFT, Wannierization, BdG theory, and topological analysis; and finally, applying this methodology to specific material systems like FeTeSe and FeSe/GaAs to derive design principles.

Abstract

A microscopic understanding of topological superconductivity (TSC) in real materials requires a materials-informed approach that integrates first-principles electronic structure, superconductivity, and topology within a unified computational framework. Here, we develop such an approach by combining density functional theory, Wannier-based low-energy Hamiltonians, Bogoliubov-de Gennes theory, and Matsubara Green's-function-based Chern number calculations performed directly on realistic multiorbital superconducting Hamiltonians. We apply this framework to bulk-like and monolayer FeTeSe and to FeSe/GaAs heterostructures, enabling a unified investigation of both intrinsic and proximity-induced topological superconductivity. We identify key electronic-structure ingredients that promote robust TSC, including Rashba-active states near the Fermi level, strong induced superconducting pairing, and substantial orbital hybridization between spin-orbit-active and superconducting sectors. Guided by these design principles, we predict multiple topological superconducting phases in Fe-based materials and demonstrate that FeSe/GaAs heterostructures are particularly promising, exhibiting topological transitions at experimentally accessible chemical potentials and low Zeeman fields. Complementing the theoretical predictions, we demonstrate the growth and structural characterization of FeSe/GaAs heterostructures, establishing the experimental feasibility of the proposed materials platform. Our results provide a quantitative route for engineering and screening candidate Majorana materials and heterostructures directly from realistic electronic structures and establish a foundation for future materials-by-design approaches to topological superconductivity.

Summary. This work analyzes the magnetoresistance in GaAs Corbino devices using hydrodynamic theories to probe electron transport. By fitting the observed quadratic magnetoresistance to models incorporating finite-slip boundary conditions, the authors confirm that the signal is dominated by bulk viscous flow. This establishes the Corbino geometry as a powerful tool for studying bulk transport properties in 2DEGs.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on solid-state transport, the rigorous application of continuum hydrodynamics to electron flow, including boundary physics (finite slip), provides a detailed example of non-equilibrium transport modeling relevant to open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the origin of positive magnetoresistance observed in high-mobility GaAs Corbino devices by analyzing hydrodynamic magnetotransport effects.

Main result. The observed magnetoresistance is predominantly governed by the bulk hydrodynamic response, allowing Corbino geometry to serve as a complementary bulk-sensitive probe of viscous electron flow.

Method. The analysis employs hydrodynamic theories of magnetotransport, specifically modeling the crossover between diffusive and viscous regimes using finite-slip boundary conditions.

Model / system. The physical system is a two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) in a GaAs Corbino geometry. The model incorporates hydrodynamic descriptions of current flow, accounting for momentum relaxation and viscous effects.

Key observables. Positive magnetoresistance exhibiting a quadratic dependence on the perpendicular magnetic field ($B^2$), and the temperature dependence of the magnetoresistance coefficient ($\alpha$).

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis distinguishes between the diffusive limit (momentum relaxation) and the hydrodynamic/Stokes limit (viscosity), and examines the quantitative modification due to finite-slip boundary conditions.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the applicability of hydrodynamic theories and successfully separates the contributions from the bulk response, finite-slip boundary effects, and contact voltages.

Figures summary. Figures show magnetoresistance ($R$ vs $B^2$) for varying Corbino disk radii and temperatures, illustrating the dependence of the quadratic magnetoresistance coefficient ($\alpha$) on both radius and temperature.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theoretical framework by deriving the general magnetoresistance expression incorporating finite-slip boundary conditions, analyzing the limiting cases (diffusive vs. hydrodynamic), and finally applying this model to fit multiple experimental data sets simultaneously to constrain physical parameters.

Abstract

We report the observation of positive magnetoresistance in high-mobility GaAs Corbino devices. Over a broad intermediate-temperature range, the resistance exhibits a quadratic dependence on perpendicular magnetic field. We analyze the data within hydrodynamic theories of magnetotransport in the Corbino geometry, which describe the crossover between the diffusive and viscous regimes, including finite-slip boundary conditions appropriate for current-penetrable contacts. The extracted relaxation rates are consistent with an approximately $T^2$ temperature dependence of the electron-electron scattering contribution. The extracted viscous relaxation parameters are consistent with those obtained from Hall-bar measurements. Comparison with theory indicates that the observed magnetoresistance is predominantly governed by the bulk hydrodynamic response, while finite slip modifies the Stokes--Ohm crossover quantitatively and the field-dependent boundary voltage provides a separate correction. These results show that Corbino magnetotransport can serve as a complementary bulk-sensitive probe of viscous electron flow.

Reconfigurable field-free spin Hall nano-oscillators enabled by crystallographic anisotropy in epitaxial Co/Pt

Summary. This work reports on the creation of highly functional spin Hall nano-oscillators (SHNOs) using epitaxial Co/Pt films. By leveraging the built-in crystallographic anisotropy, the devices achieve stable, high-frequency auto-oscillations without needing an external magnetic bias. Furthermore, the oscillation state can be nonvolatilely reconfigured, establishing a promising materials-based route for next-generation spintronic oscillators.

Why it may be interesting. While the core physics is in condensed matter spintronics, the concept of using intrinsic material symmetry (crystallographic anisotropy) to engineer a stable, bias-free operating point is analogous to engineering effective Hamiltonians or potentials in quantum systems, making it relevant for understanding engineered quantum control.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is to develop reconfigurable, field-free spin Hall nano-oscillators (SHNOs) to overcome the limitation of requiring a global external magnetic bias for operation.

Main result. The authors demonstrate that utilizing the intrinsic crystallographic anisotropy of epitaxial Co/Pt bilayers enables robust, field-free auto-oscillations above 10 GHz, with the oscillation state being nonvolatilely reconfigurable by the remanent magnetization.

Method. The study combines advanced experimental techniques, including Spin-Torque Ferromagnetic Resonance (STFMR) and micro-focused Brillouin Light Scattering ($\mu$-BLS), with micromagnetic simulations.

Model / system. The physical system is an epitaxial Co/Pt bilayer grown on MgO(110), patterned into nano-constrictions. The dynamics are governed by spin-torque effects interacting with the large, fixed anisotropy field derived from the crystal structure.

Key observables. Auto-oscillation frequency (>10 GHz), current-dependent microwave power spectral density, threshold current ($I_{th}$), and the degree of spectral synchronization between oscillation modes.

Important parameters / regimes. Crystallographic anisotropy field ($ ext{B}_{ ext{ani}} \approx 0.36 ext{ T}$), current angle relative to anisotropy axis ($\phi_I$), and the operating temperature/field (zero applied field).

Assumptions / limitations. The method relies on the assumption that the large crystallographic anisotropy is preserved after device fabrication, and that the auto-oscillation is spatially confined to the nanoconstriction region.

Figures summary. Figures show STFMR spectra confirming anisotropy fields, power spectral density demonstrating field-free auto-oscillations, and $\mu$-BLS maps illustrating the spatial confinement and the progressive merging (synchronization) of spectral branches as the current angle is tuned.

Paper structure. The paper details the experimental setup (device fabrication, measurement techniques like STFMR and $\mu$-BLS), presents the core findings regarding field-free oscillation enabled by anisotropy, and concludes by establishing this anisotropy as a general design principle for reconfigurable spintronic oscillators.

Abstract

Spin Hall nano-oscillators (SHNOs) are nanoscale microwave sources for wireless communication, neuromorphic computing and oscillator-based Ising machines, but conventional devices require a global magnetic bias. Here we replace this bias through crystallographic anisotropy in epitaxial Co/Pt. Growth of hcp Co with its c-axis in the film plane produces an anisotropy field of about 0.36 T and enables field-free auto-oscillations above 10 GHz in nanoconstriction SHNOs. The active current polarity is selected by the remanent magnetization, providing nonvolatile reconfiguration of the oscillation state. Micro-focused Brillouin light scattering confirms that the nonlinear response is confined to the nanoconstriction region. Lithographic control of the angle between the current and anisotropy axes tunes the excitation threshold and drives two spectral branches from separated modes to a dominant single branch, consistent with mutual synchronization. These results establish epitaxial crystallographic anisotropy as a route to reconfigurable field-free spintronic oscillators and oscillator networks.

Stable vortex soliton arrays in disclination-fractal systems

Summary. This paper investigates the stability of vortex solitons in nonlinear optical waveguides patterned with disclination-fractal structures. By comparing these fractal lattices to conventional defect structures, the authors prove that the fractal geometry provides a novel mechanism to stabilize these light field arrays. This has significant implications for designing advanced photonic devices capable of robust light field manipulation.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete example of how engineered geometric complexity (fractal geometry combined with defects) can stabilize nonlinear wave phenomena, offering insights applicable to designing robust photonic devices and understanding wave localization in complex media.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge addressed is achieving stable vortex solitons and vortex-soliton arrays within complex, geometrically structured nonlinear media.

Main result. The study demonstrates that vortex-soliton arrays in disclination-fractal configurations are significantly stable, while those in conventional disclination structures are unstable.

Method. The research employs linear stability analysis and direct numerical simulations of the nonlinear Schrödinger-like equation to track field evolution over long distances.

Model / system. The system models light propagation in waveguide arrays structured using disclination operations applied to fractal lattices (e.g., Sierpiński carpets). The dynamics are governed by a nonlinear Schrödinger equation incorporating diffraction, self-action, and geometric potential terms.

Key observables. Stability of the vortex-soliton array (measured by the growth rate of perturbations, Re(beta)), field mode maintenance, and phase coherence over propagation distance (z).

Important parameters / regimes. The structure type (disclination-fractal vs. conventional SSH), the topological phase (topologically trivial phase), and the nonlinearity strength are key parameters.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the nonlinear effect is sufficient to counteract diffraction-induced broadening, allowing for the localization of stable soliton states.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the construction of the fractal lattices, the comparison of spectral properties between fractal and conventional structures, and direct simulations showing stable propagation in the fractal case versus disintegration in the conventional case.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the physical model and the challenge of instability. It then analyzes the linear spectrum, identifies stable domain wall states in the topologically trivial phase, and finally uses nonlinear simulations and stability analysis to rigorously compare the stability of the fractal vs. conventional lattice structures.

Abstract

Vortex light fields carry orbital angular momentum and have attracted significant attention because of their wide applications in light field manipulations, optical communications, and quantum information processing. In nonlinear media, the balance between diffraction and self-action enables the formation of vortex solitons. However, achieving the stability of vortex solitons remains challenging due to radial and azimuthal modulation instabilities. Here, we report stable vortex solitons and vortex-soliton arrays in disclination-fractal configurations with different rotational symmetries, constructed by applying disclination operations to fractal lattice structures. The stable vortex-soliton arrays---composed of several vortex solitons---can exist along domain walls, resulting from the disclination operation applied to the topologically trivial phase. Their stability is verified through both linear stability analysis and direct simulations of perturbed propagation. For comparison, vortex-soliton arrays in conventional disclination configurations are found to be completely unstable, demonstrating the significance of the fractal configuration in stabilizing these nonlinear states. These findings reveal a new nonlinear excitation mechanism arising from the interplay between fractal geometry and disclination defects, thereby providing theoretical underpinnings for both the improved understanding of multi-field excitation phenomena in complex geometric systems and opening new avenues for the design of photonic devices based on fractal disclination structures.

Fixed-mesh based approach for modeling of superconducting magnetic bearings

Authors: Elias Paakkunainen, Bárbara Maria Oliveira Santos, Gabriel dos Santos, Timo Tarhasaari, Paavo Rasilo, Sebastian Schöps
Type: theory · Category: numerical methods · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09347v1

Summary. This paper introduces a fixed-mesh finite element method to simulate superconducting magnetic bearings, circumventing the computational bottleneck of remeshing required by traditional moving-boundary simulations. By incorporating motion effects into a time-dependent anisotropic material tensor, the model achieves high accuracy while drastically improving computational efficiency compared to reference methods.

Why it may be interesting. While the physics is classical electromagnetism, the core contribution is a novel, efficient numerical technique for time-dependent, moving-boundary problems in FEM, which has methodological relevance for complex simulations in quantum physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is accurately and efficiently modeling superconducting magnetic bearings (SMBs) while accounting for the relative motion between the superconducting components and the magnetic guideway.

Main result. The proposed fixed-mesh approach accurately predicts levitation forces, matching experimental results with high $R^2$ values, while being significantly more computationally efficient than traditional remeshing methods.

Method. The authors developed a fixed-mesh finite element method (FEM) that models motion by applying a coordinate transformation only to the air region, incorporating this effect into a time-dependent anisotropic material tensor.

Model / system. The system modeled is a superconducting magnetic bearing involving High-Temperature Superconductors (HTS) interacting with a magnetic guideway. The formulation uses the j-a mixed FEM approach, coupled with homogenization techniques for coated conductors.

Key observables. Levitation force ($f_x, f_y$), magnetic flux density ($|\vec{b}|$), and current density distributions ($j/j_c$).

Important parameters / regimes. The model incorporates material parameters like critical current density ($j_c$) and resistivity ($ ho$) which follow power-law characteristics.

Assumptions / limitations. The core assumption is that motion effects can be captured by modifying material properties (via the reluctivity tensor) within a fixed mesh, thereby avoiding the computational cost of remeshing at every time step.

Figures summary. Figures compare the predicted levitation force against experimental measurements and reference models across various motion states (vertical/horizontal translation). Other figures illustrate the geometry and the comparison of flux density and current distributions between fixed-mesh and remeshed simulations.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the physical problem, details the fixed-mesh methodology (including the coordinate transformation and anisotropic tensor), presents the coupled FEM formulation (j-a), validates the approach against verification problems (convergence, known measurements), and finally applies it to complex SMB simulations, highlighting computational gains.

Abstract

Numerical simulations of superconducting magnetic bearings (SMBs) are complicated by the relative motion between high temperature superconductors and the magnetic guideway, which commonly requires repeated updates of the geometry and mesh. We present a fixed-mesh based approach for modeling the motion induced effects in which a coordinate transformation is applied only to the air region between the moving regions. The transformation can be included in a time-dependent anisotropic material tensor, leaving the mesh during the simulation and the rest of the formulation unchanged. To provide numerical examples, the approach is implemented in 2D using the finite element method with the $j$-$a$ formulation and is combined with homogenization and circuit coupling for double-crossed loops of coated conductors. The open-source model implementations are made available. The proposed approach is verified and validated against existing modeling approaches and experimental results from the literature for SMBs. Two simulation examples are presented, and in both of them the levitation force predicted by the fixed-mesh model agrees with the experimental results, yielding $R^2$ values above 0.99. For a verification problem, it is shown that the fixed-mesh model converges towards the remeshed reference solution under mesh refinement as expected. For an SMB undergoing vertical and horizontal translations, the homogenized fixed-mesh model reproduces the lateral-force response of a fully resolved moving-geometry reference model. Additionally, the homogenized fixed-mesh model is computationally more efficient than the reference model, indicating a significant reduction in the number of degrees of freedom and the computation time. The results demonstrate accurate and computationally efficient simulation of translational motion with the fixed-mesh based approach.

Summary. This theoretical work calculates the intrinsic thermal Hall conductivity of phonons in graphene operating in the quantum Hall regime. It shows that while the direct phonon contribution is small, the valley-specific thermal Hall response mediated by electron-phonon coupling is predicted to be significant. This provides a pathway to experimentally probe valley physics using lattice vibrations in 2D Dirac materials.

Why it may be interesting. The work connects topological concepts (Hall viscosity, Berry curvature) from electronic transport to lattice dynamics (phonons), providing a theoretical framework for measuring emergent topological responses in condensed matter systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper calculates the intrinsic (valley) thermal Hall conductivity of phonons in graphene when bulk electron thermal transport is suppressed, specifically in the quantum Hall regime.

Main result. While the intrinsic phonon thermal Hall conductivity is predicted to be small, the valley thermal Hall transport induced by electron coupling is predicted to be comparable in size to the charge channel transport, suggesting a measurable valley response.

Method. The analysis uses effective field theory and linear response theory, deriving the phonon Hall viscosity by integrating out massive Dirac electrons coupled via geometric electron-phonon coupling.

Model / system. The system is graphene, modeled using a Dirac fermion formulation for electrons coupled to lattice distortions (phonons). The analysis is performed in the quantum Hall regime, treating the coupling perturbatively.

Key observables. Phonon (valley) thermal Hall conductivity ($\kappa_{ph}^H$, $\kappa_{ph}^{vH}$), Phonon Hall viscosity ($\eta_{ ext{em}}^H$), and the ratio $\kappa_{ph}^H / \kappa_e^H$.

Important parameters / regimes. Quantum Hall regime, low temperature ($T o 0$), and the magnitude of the electron-phonon coupling ($\partial_{ih}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes graphene can be approximated by its pristine state and relies on low-temperature approximations for the phonon dispersion relation.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the general framework for phonon Hall viscosity induced by electronic Hall effects, deriving the necessary effective actions, and finally calculating the specific intrinsic and valley contributions to the thermal Hall conductivity.

Abstract

We calculate the intrinsic contribution to the (valley) thermal Hall conductivity of phonons in (Semenoff-gapped) graphene in the quantum Hall regime where bulk electron thermal transport is suppressed. The (valley) thermal Hall transport of phonons considered here originates from a phonon (valley) Hall viscosity induced by electron (valley) Hall conductivity and viscosity via geometric electron-phonon coupling. The so generated phonon (valley) Hall viscosity is identified with the emergent (valley) Hall viscosity recently introduced in \textit{Phys. Lett. A} \textbf{595}, 132096 (2026). While our calculations refer to graphene, they may very well be generalized to other Dirac materials like group-VI transition metal dichalcogenides. We discuss the prospect of measuring the phonon thermal Hall conductivity in graphene-based heterostructures and estimate the corresponding valley response induced by electrons in the inversion symmetry broken phase.

Origin of Flat Bands and Role of Electron Correlation in Lutetium Hydrides

Authors: Anmol Lamichhane, Adam Denchfield, Hyeondeok Shin, Panchapakesan Ganesh, Russell J. Hemley, Hyowon Park
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09446v1

Summary. This theoretical study investigates the electronic structure of superconducting Lutetium hydrides, focusing on the origin of observed flat bands. Using advanced many-body techniques, the authors determine that the physics is governed not by the strength of electron correlation, but fundamentally by how the hydrogen orbitals are filled at specific crystallographic sites. This establishes orbital filling as the key determinant of electronic properties in these materials.

Why it may be interesting. The detailed analysis of correlation effects in a strongly correlated material system, using advanced many-body techniques like DMFT and cRPA, provides a model for understanding how orbital filling dictates electronic behavior, which is relevant to correlated electron physics in general.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to uncover the microscopic origin and correlation nature of puzzling flat-band regions observed in superconducting Lutetium hydrides ($ ext{LuH}_x$) where superconductivity and flat-band physics intersect.

Main result. The authors find that hydrogen orbital filling, rather than the magnitude of the Coulomb interaction, is the fundamental principle governing correlation strength and low-energy flat-band physics in these materials.

Method. The research employs a combination of Density Functional Theory (DFT), Dynamical Mean-Field Theory (DMFT), and the Constrained Random-Phase Approximation (cRPA) to analyze electronic structure and correlation effects.

Model / system. The system under investigation is the series of Lutetium hydrides ($ ext{LuH}_x$, $1.75 \le x \le 3$), which exhibit superconductivity under pressure and possess distinct tetrahedral and octahedral hydrogen sites.

Key observables. Screened on-site Coulomb interaction, correlation strength (mass enhancement, susceptibility), Density of States (DOS), flat-band features, and low-energy optical absorption peaks.

Important parameters / regimes. Hydrogen orbital filling, the relative energy levels of $ ext{H-s}$ vs. $ ext{Lu-d}$ states, and the degree of hybridization between the two sublattices.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that anti-site defects (H in octahedral sites) are responsible for generating the observed ARPES flat-band features, and that the system remains weakly correlated despite large interaction parameters.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate model structures, orbital-resolved DOS under different correlation treatments, and comparisons between calculated and experimental spectral features.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the electronic structure by first calculating correlation parameters, then identifying the role of anti-site defects in generating flat bands, and finally establishing hydrogen orbital filling as the primary organizing principle controlling correlation effects.

Abstract

Lutetium hydrides (LuH$_x$, $1.75 \leq x \leq 3$) form a diverse series of phases, several of which superconduct under pressure. Characterizing their electronic properties has remained challenging owing to a high propensity for hydrogen defect formation, and recent angle-resolved photoemission (ARPES) measurements reveal puzzling flat-band regions that position these materials as candidates where superconductivity and flat-band physics may intersect. Here, by combining density functional theory, dynamical mean-field theory, and the constrained random-phase approximation, we uncover the microscopic origin and correlation nature of these flat bands. Across all compositions, the screened on-site Coulomb interaction is larger for H-s states than for Lu-$d$ states due to compact hydrogen orbitals. Nevertheless, these systems remain weakly correlated metals: the nearly filled H-$s$ shell admits little charge fluctuation, so its large interaction acts as a static level shift rather than a source of correlation. Although hydrogen primarily occupies tetrahedral sites at $x=2$, we discover that anti-site defects--where hydrogens occupy slightly unfavorable octahedral sites--generate both the ARPES flat-band features and the low-energy optical absorption peak, attesting to the usual defective nature of such materials in experimental samples. We further find that correlation strength is governed primarily by hydrogen orbital filling at these sites rather than the interaction magnitude itself. Consequently, we identify hydrogen orbital filling as the fundamental organizing principle dictating correlation and low-energy flat-band physics in lutetium hydrides.

Phonon-Bottleneck-Governed Ultrafast Hot-Carrier Super-Diffusion in Transition Metal Dichalcogenides

Summary. This work investigates the ultrafast speed limits in 2D TMDCs by using specialized zero-bias terahertz sampling. The authors demonstrate that the rapid carrier response is not limited by conventional drift but by hot-carrier super-diffusion, which is ultimately governed by the intrinsic phonon bottleneck. This establishes phonon engineering as a key paradigm for designing faster, self-powered optoelectronic devices.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on solid-state electronics, the explicit identification of the phonon bottleneck as the rate-limiting step for non-equilibrium carrier dynamics provides a direct, measurable link between lattice vibrations and device performance, relevant to open quantum system modeling.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The operational speed of 2D TMDCs for optoelectronics is limited by low room-temperature mobilities and carrier transit delays.

Main result. The ultrafast response is governed by hot-carrier super-diffusion, which is quantitatively synchronized with the intrinsic optical-to-acoustic phonon scattering lifetime (phonon bottleneck).

Method. The study uses on-chip terahertz optoelectronic sampling at zero bias to measure intrinsic photoresponse, complemented by ultrafast pump-probe spectroscopy.

Model / system. The system involves 2D TMDCs ($ ext{MoS}_2$, $ ext{MoSe}_2$) interfaced with metal contacts, studied under zero-bias conditions to isolate intrinsic carrier dynamics from drift-limited transport.

Key observables. Ultrafast relaxation lifetimes (e.g., $48.5 ext{ ps}$ in $ ext{MoS}_2/ ext{Au}$), intrinsic $3 ext{-dB}$ bandwidths, and the quantitative correlation between electrical lifetime and phonon scattering lifetime.

Important parameters / regimes. Zero bias ($ ext{V}_{ ext{bias}} = 0 ext{ V}$), ultrafast timescales ($ ext{ps}$), and the phonon scattering rate.

Assumptions / limitations. The measurement setup successfully eliminates external parasitic delays and electric-field-driven drift by using Ohmic contacts and zero bias.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate time-resolved photocurrents, comparing Ohmic vs. Schottky contacts, showing position and bias independence of the fast decay component, and correlating optical phonon lifetimes with macroscopic electrical relaxation times.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the limitations of TMDCs, details the specialized zero-bias measurement platform, presents evidence ruling out drift-limited transport (via Ohmic/Schottky comparison), identifies hot-carrier super-diffusion, and finally links the macroscopic speed limit to the microscopic phonon bottleneck.

Abstract

Two-dimensional transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDCs) are promising for low-power optoelectronics, yet their operational speed is widely considered constrained by low room-temperature mobilities and carrier transit delays. Here, by combining on-chip terahertz optoelectronic sampling with thermally evaporated Ohmic contacts, we eliminate external parasitic delays and directly capture the intrinsic interfacial photoresponse in unencapsulated TMDCs under zero bias. The devices achieve ultrafast relaxation lifetimes of 48.5 ps in MoS2/Au and 14.2 ps in MoSe2/Ag, translating to intrinsic 3-dB bandwidths of 4.4 GHz and 7.5 GHz, respectively. Spatial scanning and bias-dependent measurements show that this response is position-independent and bias-immune, ruling out conventional drift-limited transport and identifying hot-carrier super-diffusion driven by an interfacial electron temperature gradient as the operative mechanism. Furthermore, ultrafast pump-probe spectroscopy reveals that the macroscopic response time is quantitatively synchronized with the microscopic optical-to-acoustic phonon scattering lifetime governed by the intrinsic phonon bottleneck. Our findings establish phonon engineering as a viable paradigm to tailor non-equilibrium optoelectronic dynamics, offering a blueprint for zero-bias, ultrafast, self-powered devices.
