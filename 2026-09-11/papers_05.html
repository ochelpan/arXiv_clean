From Certifying Rank $k$ Projectors To Non-Positivity, Entanglement, And Non-Hermitian Witnesses Through Traces Of Matrix Powers

Authors: H. F. Chau
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09557v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops a systematic framework to determine the properties of quantum states and operators by analyzing the traces of their matrix powers. It establishes that these traces can act as powerful mathematical witnesses to certify entanglement, non-positivity, and non-Hermiticity. The work is significant for quantum information theory as it provides robust, measurable diagnostic tools for characterizing quantum systems.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides fundamental mathematical tools—the trace of powers—to diagnose complex quantum properties (like entanglement or non-Hermiticity) using only measurable quantities, which is crucial for experimental quantum information science.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper systematically investigates necessary and sufficient conditions to constrain the eigenvalues of a Hermitian matrix using only the traces of its matrix powers.

Main result. These trace constraints can serve as powerful, general witnesses to certify properties like quantum state purity, entanglement, non-positivity, and non-Hermiticity.

Method. The authors derive polynomial constraints involving $ ext{Tr} H^j$ and develop specific witness criteria (e.g., UTNPW, EW) based on these trace identities.

Model / system. The analysis focuses on Hermitian matrices ($H$) and density matrices ($ ho$), which are central to quantum information theory. The methods are general but are applied to diagnose quantum state properties.

Key observables. Traces of matrix powers ($ ext{Tr} H^j$), purity ($ ext{Tr} ho^2$), and the values derived from various quantum witnesses.

Important parameters / regimes. The dimension of the matrix ($D$), the number of required trace constraints ($2p+1$), and the measurement uncertainty/rounding error.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis addresses practical limitations, including numerical stability and measurement uncertainty, suggesting that the required accuracy for certification is very high.

Figures summary. The notes mention tables summarizing the performance of the NPW and EW algorithms across different dimensions, showing success probabilities.

Paper structure. The paper builds from establishing general eigenvalue constraints using traces of powers, to deriving specific witnesses (NHW, UTNPW, EW), and concluding with a systematic analysis of the strengths, weaknesses, and limitations of these certification techniques.

Abstract

We all know that a density matrix $ρ$ is pure if and only if $\mathrm{Tr} \,ρ^2 = 1$. But is this the only way to prove the purity of $ρ$ using the trace of its powers? Here I systematically study the necessary and sufficient conditions of the more general question of guaranteeing that all eigenvalues of a Hermitian matrix belong to a specified set through its trace of powers. More importantly, these characterization results are automatically witnesses certifying a quantum state as entangled, a Hermitian operator has least one negative eigenvalue and a linear operator as non-Hermitian. I demonstrate the effectiveness of these witnesses, analyze their performance, and study their strength, weakness together with resource requirement through numerical simulation as well as analytical work. I also discuss briefly the effects of numerical stability, uncertainty in measurement and rounding errors on these problems.

From the Unknown to the Desired: Transforming Unknown Initial States in the Resource Theory of Work and Heat

Authors: Tanmoy Biswas, Andreas Winter
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09352v1

Summary. This paper develops a universal theory for performing thermodynamic state transformations when the initial quantum state is only partially known. By constructing protocols based on the charge-entropy normal form, the authors prove that optimal work extraction can be achieved asymptotically despite the lack of full state knowledge. This advances resource-theoretic thermodynamics beyond idealized, state-aware settings.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous, resource-theoretic framework for thermodynamics that explicitly handles the practical limitation of incomplete knowledge about the initial state, which is crucial for realistic quantum engine designs.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Developing a universal framework for thermodynamic state transformations (work and heat) when the initial quantum state is unknown, moving beyond state-dependent protocols.

Main result. A universal scheme is established showing that optimal work extraction can be achieved asymptotically even when the initial state is unknown, with a super-polynomially small error.

Method. The authors develop protocols based on the charge-entropy normal form, utilizing techniques like gentle tomography to estimate unknown state parameters while minimizing disturbance.

Model / system. The study analyzes quantum state transformations within the resource theory of work and heat, focusing on $n$ independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) copies of a state $ ho$ governed by mutually commuting conserved charges.

Key observables. Von Neumann entropy $S( ho)$ and the expectation values of conserved charges $ ext{Tr}( ho L_i)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The number of copies $n$, the error decay rate (super-polynomial), and the sublinear scaling of ancillary resources.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the existence of mutually commuting conserved charges and relies on coarse-grained measurements to estimate unknown state properties.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the conversion of an $n$-copy state to its charge–entropy normal form. Figure 2 shows the transformation between two states using an ancilla system.

Paper structure. The paper builds from establishing the charge-entropy normal form for states, to proving that states in the same class are asymptotically transformable, and finally detailing the protocols for estimating the necessary state parameters (entropy and charges) with minimal disturbance.

Abstract

We study thermodynamic state transformations in the resource theory of work and heat when the initial quantum state is unknown. Moving beyond state-dependent protocols, we develop a universal framework in which a single transformation applies to all admissible input states. For mutually commuting conserved charges, we construct charge-conserving protocols whose transformation error, quantified by trace distance, decays super-polynomially with the number of system copies. As an application, we demonstrate universal work extraction: asymptotically optimal work can be extracted without microscopic knowledge of the initial state, with a super-polynomially small error. Our results show that thermodynamic state transformations remain achievable under limited prior information and establish a universal framework for resource-theoretic thermodynamics beyond the state-aware setting.

Information Causality Characterizes the Set of Quantum Correlations in the Simplest Bell Scenario

Authors: Mariami Gachechiladze, Nikolai Miklin
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10508v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5

Summary. This paper uses the Information Causality principle to derive a precise characterization of quantum correlations in the simplest Bell test setting, recovering the known Tsirelson-Landau-Masanes criterion. Crucially, the authors prove that generalized IC is a strictly stronger constraint than Macroscopic Locality, providing a powerful tool for bounding quantum nonlocality.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on Bell tests, the rigorous connection between information-theoretic principles (IC) and fundamental physical constraints (like quantum nonlocality) provides a systematic theoretical framework applicable to understanding resource theories in quantum information.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates whether the physical principle of Information Causality (IC) is sufficient to derive the exact boundary for quantum correlations in the simplest bipartite Bell scenario, surpassing existing bounds like Uffink's inequality.

Main result. The authors prove that the generalized IC characterizes the quantum correlations in this scenario, yielding the Tsirelson-Landau-Masanes criterion. Furthermore, they establish that generalized IC is a strictly stronger principle than Macroscopic Locality (ML).

Method. The analysis combines the generalized IC formulation with a new communication protocol and involves taking limits (specifically, the vanishing channel capacity limit) to derive constraints on the correlators.

Model / system. The system is the simplest bipartite Bell scenario involving two binary measurements per party (Alice and Bob) and is analyzed through the lens of information-theoretic constraints imposed by a noisy communication channel.

Key observables. Correlators $\langle a_x b_y angle$, the Tsirelson-Landau-Masanes (TLM) criterion, and the generalized Information Causality (IC) constraint.

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis heavily relies on the limit of vanishing channel capacity ($\epsilon_c o 0$) and the structure of the communication channel parameters.

Assumptions / limitations. The derivation relies on specific parameterizations for the input distribution and the noisy channel, and the equivalence between the IC constraint and the TLM criterion is shown in the vanishing capacity limit.

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically illustrates the information causality setup with correlated inputs and a noisy channel output. The TLM criterion is presented in Equation (3).

Paper structure. The paper first establishes that generalized IC implies the TLM criterion in the simplest Bell scenario. It then compares IC to ML, demonstrating that ML is a weaker constraint by finding correlations that satisfy ML but violate IC. The core mathematical work involves deriving the constraint from the IC inequality via limiting procedures.

Abstract

Information causality (IC) was introduced as a physical principle constraining correlations in non-signaling theories. Whether it can recover the exact quantum correlation boundary, beyond Uffink's inequality, has remained an open question. Here, we combine its generalized formulation for correlated inputs with a new communication protocol to derive quantum Bell inequalities that exactly characterize the quantum correlations in the simplest bipartite Bell scenario, with two binary measurements. In particular, we derive the Tsirelson-Landau-Masanes criterion directly from IC. Thus, the generalized IC implies macroscopic locality, while we also present macroscopically local correlations that violate the generalized IC. This establishes that generalized IC is a strictly stronger principle in this scenario. Together with our earlier result that generalized IC implies a nontrivial communication complexity principle, these findings strengthen the role of information causality in explaining the limits of quantum nonlocality and provide a systematic route to deriving tighter bounds on the set of quantum correlations in more general Bell scenarios.

Robust Device-Independent Certification of Boolean-Phase Gates

Authors: Yunguang Han, Xingyuan Bu, Aleksandra Gočanin, Jiabing Yuan
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09836v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work presents a robust, device-independent method to certify the fidelity of Boolean-phase quantum gates. It achieves this by using a novel 'Boolean derivative' to translate gate-specific phase information into classical constraints suitable for Bell tests. This allows for the derivation of rigorous, quantifiable lower bounds on the gate's fidelity based purely on local measurement outcomes.

Why it may be interesting. The technique of translating gate-dependent quantum information into classical constraints via the Boolean derivative is a powerful tool for device-independent characterization, applicable to any gate whose structure can be mapped to local classical measurements.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops a robust, device-independent certification scheme for Boolean-phase gates, addressing the limitation that self-testing the output Choi state alone is insufficient.

Main result. They derive an explicit affine lower bound on the extracted-state squared fidelity for Boolean-phase gates, and provide a closed-form Choi-fidelity bound for an effective n-qubit channel using an independent-source network.

Method. The core method uses the Boolean derivative to convert gate-dependent phase information into classical signs, enabling the construction of a Bell test built from CHSH blocks using only local measurements.

Model / system. The system involves certifying Boolean-phase gates, defined by arbitrary Boolean functions $f: \{0, 1\}^n o \{0, 1\}$, which are computational-basis diagonal gates.

Key observables. The normalized Choi state $|\Psi_f angle$, the Bell expression $B_f$, and the extracted-state squared fidelity $F^2$.

Important parameters / regimes. The Bell deficit $\epsilon$, and the fidelity bound coefficients $c_Z$ and $c_{CCZ}$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the ability to combine an identity test and a gate-output test in an independent-source network using the same physical observables for reference.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the Boolean-derivative scoring for a Bell term, while Figure 2 depicts the independent-source network structure used for gate certification.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the problem of device-independent certification, then develops the Boolean derivative to build a classical Bell test. It extends this to an independent-source network to derive the fidelity bound, culminating in a specific joint analysis for the CCZ gate.

Abstract

Device-independent certification of a quantum gate requires the input and output tests to identify the same reference qubits. Self-testing the output Choi state alone does not guarantee this consistency. We develop a robust certification scheme for Boolean-phase gates, a broad family of computational-basis diagonal gates specified by Boolean functions. Boolean derivatives convert the target-dependent phase information into classical signs that can be evaluated from local measurement outcomes. This leads to Bell tests built from CHSH blocks using two binary measurements per party and no entangling measurements. At maximal violation, the tests self-test the normalized Choi state and the measured observables. Away from the maximum, they give an explicit affine lower bound on the extracted-state squared fidelity that is uniform over all Boolean functions and valid in arbitrary local dimensions. We then combine an identity test and a gate-output test in an independent-source network in which the reference devices use the same physical observables, obtaining a closed-form Choi-fidelity bound for an effective \(n\)-qubit channel. For CCZ, a joint six-party analysis gives a stronger robustness bound without changing the Bell expression or measurement settings. The construction shows how the algebraic structure of a gate can shift target-dependent information from quantum measurement design to classical processing of local outcomes.

SIM-GRAPH: A universal guide to symmetric interactions

Authors: R. C. Verstraten, C. Morais Smith
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09996v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces SIM-GRAPH, a novel method that leverages underlying symmetries in quantum Hamiltonians to drastically reduce the computational complexity of finding ground-state properties. By projecting the system onto a symmetry-reduced graph, it achieves exponential speedups over standard exact diagonalization for models like the TFIM. This makes the study of large, complex quantum magnetic systems computationally feasible.

Why it may be interesting. The technique provides a powerful, symmetry-based computational shortcut for strongly correlated models, offering insights into how symmetries constrain the low-energy physics of magnetic materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is efficiently calculating ground-state observables for interacting quantum many-body systems, which typically suffer from exponential scaling when using exact diagonalization.

Main result. The SIM-GRAPH method provides an exponential speedup by projecting interactions onto a symmetry-reduced graph, allowing for the study of large systems previously intractable.

Method. The method uses group theory to identify symmetries, reducing the system Hamiltonian by projecting interactions onto a smaller, symmetric subset of the lattice sites, resulting in a reduced Hamiltonian.

Model / system. The method is applied to interacting quantum spin systems, specifically using the Transverse Field Ising Model (TFIM) on various lattices, including 1D chains, 2D square lattices, and Sierpinski triangles.

Key observables. Ground-state observables, such as average magnetization ($\langle n_{\uparrow} angle$) and ground-state energy.

Important parameters / regimes. The size and geometry of the lattice (e.g., 3x3, 4x4, 1D chains); the symmetry group $S$ used for reduction.

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes that the ground-state observables are not significantly affected by spontaneous symmetry breaking, and that the lowest energy symmetric state is sufficient for accurate results.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the graph reduction process for lattices (e.g., 3x3 square lattice, 1D chain), comparing results from Exact Diagonalization (ED) against SIM-GRAPH variants ($ ext{SIM}1, ext{SIM}2.1, ext{SIM}2.4$) for magnetization.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the core SIM-GRAPH technique, details its extensions (SIM2 for superpositions, SIM3 for partial symmetries), and validates its performance across various geometries (1D, 2D, Sierpinski) by comparing results against ED benchmarks.

Abstract

Symmetry plays a central role throughout physics, from Fourier analysis on discrete lattices to the classification of elementary particles in the standard model. In finite quantum systems, symmetries are often discrete, such as reflection and rotational symmetries. Here, we present the SIM-GRAPH method (Symmetric Ising Models - Graph Reduction And Projected Hamiltonians), which uses such symmetries to efficiently calculate ground-state observables of interacting quantum systems. By projecting all interactions onto a symmetric subset of the system, the method effectively reduces the number of sites, thus providing an exponential speedup over standard exact diagonalization. Furthermore, we introduce two extensions that broaden the applicability of the method and allow for a controlled trade-off between computational cost and accuracy.

Summary. This work analyzes the scaling of the stabilizer Rényi entropy in the Dyck-Fredkin spin chain under a deformation parameter $t$. The key finding is that the SRE displays distinct scaling laws ($\Theta(N)$, $\Theta(\log N)$, or $\Theta(1)$) depending on $t$. This suggests that non-stabilizerness is a powerful diagnostic tool capable of revealing unique signatures of quantum criticality beyond what entanglement entropy can measure.

Why it may be interesting. The finding that SRE exhibits anomalous logarithmic scaling at $t=1$, while entanglement entropy does not, suggests that non-stabilizerness can reveal unique aspects of unconventional quantum criticality not captured by standard entanglement measures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the scaling behavior of the stabilizer Rényi entropy (SRE) as a quantitative measure of non-stabilizerness in quantum many-body systems.

Main result. The SRE exhibits distinct scaling behaviors ($\Theta(N)$, $\Theta(\log N)$, or $\Theta(1)$) depending on the deformation parameter $t$ of the Dyck-Fredkin chain, suggesting non-stabilizerness provides a unique probe for criticality.

Method. The authors use numerical exact finite-size calculations, employing the replica formulation of the SRE, to analyze the asymptotic scaling of the ground state entropy.

Model / system. The system analyzed is the spin-1/2 Dyck-Fredkin chain and its $t$-deformation, a local frustration-free model with unusual spectral-gap scaling.

Key observables. Stabilizer Rényi entropy ($\alpha$-SRE), entanglement entropy, and the spectral gap ($\Delta E$).

Important parameters / regimes. The deformation parameter $t$, which defines three distinct regimes: $t < 1$, $t = 1$, and $t > 1$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the assumption that the SRE provides a physically meaningful measure of non-stabilizerness that can distinguish critical points from entanglement entropy.

Figures summary. Figure 3 illustrates the 2-SRE scaling for the deformed chain, showing linear ($t<1$), logarithmic ($t=1$), and constant ($t>1$) behavior, contrasting with entanglement entropy scaling.

Paper structure. The paper introduces SRE as a measure of non-stabilizerness, details the numerical methods for calculating the entropy, and then presents the core results by analyzing the scaling of SRE across the three regimes defined by the parameter $t$.

Abstract

The stabilizer Rényi entropy is a quantitative measure of non-stabilizerness, or magic, and has typically been found to scale extensively with system size $N$ (i.e., $Θ(N)$) for a variety of many-body quantum states. In this note, we study the stabilizer Rényi entropy of the ground state of the spin-$\frac{1}{2}$ Dyck-Fredkin chain and its $t$-deformation, a local frustration-free model with unusual spectral-gap scaling. Exploiting the combinatorial structure, we carry out numerically exact finite-size calculations, which indicate asymptotic behavior depending on $t$: $Θ(N)$ for $t<1$, $Θ(\log N)$ at $t=1$, and $Θ(1)$ for $t>1$. The scaling at $t=1$ could be another manifestation of the unconventional criticality of the model, while the contrast with the behavior of the entanglement entropy suggests that non-stabilizerness might provide a new window into quantum many-body systems.

Noether Symmetries Generate Deterministic Energy-Harvesting Protocols

Summary. This theoretical paper establishes that the symmetry structure of a quantum source-harvester system dictates whether deterministic energy harvesting (DEH) is possible. By applying Noether's theorem, the authors prove that a single symmetry can generate an infinite family of source states, all of which perform the identical energy transfer. This provides a powerful, general framework for understanding energy extraction constrained by fundamental symmetries.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, general mathematical tool (Noether's theorem) to constrain non-equilibrium processes like energy harvesting, linking fundamental symmetries directly to thermodynamic constraints in open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper seeks to determine the general, symmetry-based conditions under which deterministic energy harvesting (DEH) is possible in source-harvester quantum dynamics.

Main result. The existence of a continuous Noether symmetry in the joint dynamics guarantees that an entire orbit of source states will implement the exact same deterministic energy harvesting transition.

Method. The authors utilize Noether's theorem, extending it to generalized probabilistic theories, to link continuous symmetries (conserved Noether charges) to the realization of deterministic energy transfer.

Model / system. The system is modeled by a composite Hamiltonian $H = H_h + H_s + H_{int}$, describing the interaction between a 'harvester' subsystem and a 'source' subsystem. Examples include Jaynes-Cummings and SU(2) models.

Key observables. Deterministic output state ($ ho_f^h$), source asymmetry ($I$), and the conserved Noether charge ($Q_\alpha$).

Important parameters / regimes. The symmetry generator $g$, the time evolution $ au$, and the resonance condition ($\Omega = \omega$) in the classical limit.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary theorems assume the existence of an exact Noether symmetry for the joint dynamics and exact invariance of the harvester boundary states.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the generation of DEH-capable source states via symmetry orbits (Figure 1) and show population dynamics confirming the coincidence of outcomes for different symmetry-rotated source states (Figure 2).

Paper structure. The paper builds from defining DEH and establishing the general principle using Noether's theorem (Theorem 1), providing concrete quantum examples (Jaynes-Cummings, SU(2)), generalizing the framework to classical and probabilistic theories, and finally deriving constraints on source asymmetry.

Abstract

We consider the general principles for when deterministic energy harvesting (DEH) is possible. DEH means absorbing energy from a fluctuating source without entropy being absorbed. We show that the symmetry structure of the source--harvester dynamics gives a general route beyond existing examples to identify DEH capable source states. Any continuous symmetry with a conserved Noether charge induces a source-side orbit of states that all implement the same deterministic harvester transition, provided the harvester boundary states are symmetry invariant. Consequently, one DEH capable source state with nonzero asymmetry between charge sectors can generate infinitely many simultaneously DEH capable source states. A Jaynes-Cummings model, a three-spin XX chain, and an SU(2) model illustrate the construction. We further extend Noether's theorem to generalised probabilistic theories and thereby generalise our main result. We further establish an asymmetry bound for harvesting, showing that source asymmetry cannot increase on average, while DEH saturates the bound exactly preserving asymmetry.

Abstract

The nature of the orbital-selective Mott transition (OSMT) remains elusive. This study shows that, by doping an orbitally degenerate spin-1 antiferromagnetic insulator in the Kanamori-Hubbard model, electronic modes emerge in the band gap in an orbital-selective manner, exhibiting momentum-shifted spin-mode dispersion relations. Electronic modes originating from conventional spin excitations, including the low-energy one, appear in the doped orbital, while those from inter-orbital spin excitations appear in the undoped orbital. These emergent modes constitute an essential feature of the OSMT.

Abstract

Summary. This paper proposes a generalized test using statistical correlations to distinguish quantum from classical time evolution, moving beyond traditional Bell inequalities. It shows that quantum vacuum fluctuations yield unique, time-independent correlations governed by the energy gap. These quantum signatures are distinct from the time-dependent, decaying correlations predicted by classical Hamiltonian systems.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a powerful, general theoretical tool—analyzing correlation patterns over time—to probe the quantum nature of physical processes in regimes (like cosmology) where standard non-locality tests are difficult to implement.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to generalize the use of statistical correlations as a Bell-type test to definitively distinguish between quantum and classical time evolution, especially in macroscopic settings where standard Bell tests fail.

Main result. Quantum vacuum fluctuations produce unique, generally time-independent correlations controlled by the energy gap, whereas classical systems exhibit time-dependent correlations that decay via dephasing, particularly near resonant poles.

Method. The authors compare statistical correlations derived from quantum Hamiltonian evolution versus classical Hamiltonian evolution, focusing on the behavior of correlation functions over time.

Model / system. The analysis uses closed systems undergoing Hamiltonian evolution, comparing quantum vacuum fluctuations to classical Hamiltonian systems, often illustrated using spin precession models.

Key observables. Statistical correlations of observables over time, energy gap ($\Delta E$), and the presence/absence of apparent poles in correlation functions.

Important parameters / regimes. The energy gap ($\Delta E$) between ground and excited states, and the time scale over which correlations are measured.

Assumptions / limitations. The test is designed to be model-independent, generalizing previous proposals, and it assumes the ability to measure correlations in systems where standard Bell tests are inapplicable.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the failure of standard Bell tests in certain physical regimes, then generalizing the concept to time evolution, and finally presenting the distinct signatures of quantum vacuum fluctuations versus classical dynamics.

Abstract

Violations of Bell's inequalities offer a definitive signal of non-classical (quantum) behavior in local deterministic systems. Yet, in many physical settings where quantum mechanics is expected to play an important role, one cannot construct a Bell-type test using the available observables. Secondly, we recover the Rydberg series without empirical asymptotic corrections, demonstrating access to precise spectral properties. Finally, we compute exact-exchange potentials for larger $π$-conjugated systems, establishing scaling capacity. Multipole splats extract new first-principles insights from established approximations and provide capacity for robust dataset generation for downstream processing and learning.

Summary. This paper proves the sharp Hausdorff-Young inequality on the quantum Euclidean space using advanced operator calculus techniques involving a Gabor transform-related flow. The result establishes sharp bounds for the Weyl transform and extends these findings to derive the sharp Young's Convolution Inequality in the noncommutative setting.

Why it may be interesting. While the core topic is harmonic analysis, the use of operator calculus on quantum spaces and the generalization of classical inequalities to noncommutative settings is highly relevant to quantum probability and quantum information theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to prove the sharp Hausdorff-Young inequality in a noncommutative setting, extending classical harmonic analysis results to quantum spaces.

Main result. The sharp Hausdorff-Young inequality, $\lVert U_ heta(f) Vert_{L^q(\mathbb{R}^2_ heta)} \le C_p \lVert f Vert_{L^p(\mathbb{R}^2)}$, is established, which also yields sharp constants for the Weyl transform and the Heisenberg group.

Method. The proof heavily relies on operator calculus, utilizing a novel operator-valued flow related to the Gabor transform and analyzing the monotonicity of associated functions via Fréchet differentiation.

Model / system. The analysis is conducted on the quantum Euclidean space, modeled by the von Neumann subalgebra $M_ heta = L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^2_ heta)$, which is related to the Weyl transform and noncommutative $L^p$ norms.

Key observables. The norms $\lVert U_ heta(f) Vert_{L^q(\mathbb{R}^2_ heta)}$ and $\lVert f Vert_{L^p(\mathbb{R}^2)}$, and the associated constants $C_p$.

Important parameters / regimes. The conjugate exponents $p$ and $q$ ($1/p + 1/q = 1$), and the deformation parameter $ heta$.

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes the applicability of operator calculus and requires $q>2$ for certain differentiability theorems to hold.

Paper structure. The paper develops the proof by first establishing the monotonicity of a function $L_A(f)$ using heat flow arguments, leading to the sharp inequality (Theorem 4.4). It then generalizes this to prove the sharp Young's Convolution Inequality (Theorem 5.3) by relating convolution to pointwise multiplication in the appropriate function space.

Abstract

We prove the sharp Hausdorff--Young inequality on the quantum Euclidean space. Meanwhile, our result implies the sharp Hausdorff-Young constants for the Weyl transform, as well as that for the Heisenberg group. The key ingredient is a novel operator-valued flow related to the Gabor transform. Our method is mainly based on operator calculus and hence generally applicable to similar problems. Finally, we give the definition of noncommutative convolution and include the partial results for the sharp Young's inequality.
