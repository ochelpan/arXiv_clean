Noise Limits on Fault-Tolerant Fermionic Quantum Computing

Authors: Owen Allison, Luke Coffman
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09467v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper calculates the noise threshold for fault-tolerant fermionic quantum computation using a specific non-Gaussian gate set. By analyzing the channel's structure via Uhlmann-Wootters concurrences, the authors establish a new, high upper bound of approximately 62% for the tolerable noise level. This result is crucial for designing robust quantum hardware based on fermionic physics.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous application of entanglement measures (concurrences) to diagnose the breakdown of non-Gaussianity in fermionic systems provides a powerful theoretical tool for assessing hardware viability in quantum computation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to determine the maximum noise level a quantum circuit can tolerate while remaining fault-tolerant for fermionic quantum computation.

Main result. The upper bound limit for fermionic quantum computing using the matchgate set is found to be approximately 62%, which is independent of circuit depth.

Method. The authors use Uhlmann-Wootters concurrences applied to the fermionic Choi state to test when the combined channel becomes 'convex Gaussian,' signaling a loss of non-Gaussian support.

Model / system. The system is fermionic quantum computing, which utilizes the matchgate with a non-Gaussian resource universal gate set. The analysis incorporates parity superselection and models noise using local depolarizing noise.

Key observables. Uhlmann-Wootters concurrences ($C_+(U, p)$ and $C_-(U, p)$) are used to diagnose the convex Gaussian nature of the noisy channel.

Important parameters / regimes. The noise probability $p$ is the key parameter, with the derived limit being $p = (8 - 2\sqrt{6})/5 \approx 62\%$.

Assumptions / limitations. The bound is not directly comparable to previous work due to differences in the noise model applied to the gate set.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the noise model (gate followed by local depolarizing noise) and show numerical results plotting diagnostic quantities ($\Delta_{\pm}$ and commutator sums) against the noise parameter $p$.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the problem of noise thresholds, details the mathematical framework using fermionic Choi states and parity projections, derives the necessary concurrence formulas, and finally solves for the critical noise parameter $p$.

Abstract

Determining the highest amount of noise that quantum circuits can handle is an interesting and crucial task in the development of fault-tolerant quantum computation. Previous work has constrained this upper limit for local depolarizing noise to $\approx 45~\%$ for circuits constructed using the universal Clifford with T gate set by finding the noise threshold where the gate set loses universality. In this work, we use a similar method with the matchgate with non-Gaussian resource universal gate set to find a limit of $p=(8 - 2 \sqrt{6})/5$ or $\approx 62~\%$ for fermionic quantum computing that is independent of circuit depth. To do this, we use Uhlmann-Wootters concurrences for a 4-mode fermionic Choi state representing a resourceful gate combined with local depolarizing noise to determine when the combined channel is convex Gaussian. These bounds are not directly comparable due to differences in noise model making the bound of $\approx 62~\%$ the best known for fermions.

Non-Multiplicativity of the Holevo Barycenter of Quantum Channels

Authors: Sayantan Chakraborty, Stefano Mancini, Leonardo Rossetti, Andreas Winter
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09373v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical paper proves that the Holevo barycenter of a quantum channel is generally not multiplicative under the tensor product. By constructing specific channels, the authors show that the barycenter of the product channel does not equal the tensor product of the individual barycenters. This result deepens our understanding of quantum channel capacities by demonstrating that simple algebraic rules fail even when related concepts like capacity additivity are studied.

Why it may be interesting. The findings challenge simple tensor product assumptions in quantum information theory, suggesting that complex resource measures like the Holevo barycenter exhibit non-trivial, context-dependent behavior when composing channels.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the multiplicativity problem for the Holevo barycenter of quantum channels, asking if the barycenter of a product channel equals the tensor product of the individual barycenters.

Main result. The Holevo barycenter is proven to be non-multiplicative under the tensor product of the channel with itself, and the entropy of the barycenter is neither universally subadditive nor universally superadditive.

Method. The authors use theoretical derivations comparing block weights derived from the product channel ($\Omega(N \otimes N)$) against the tensor product of the individual barycenters ($\Omega(N) \otimes \Omega(N)$).

Model / system. The study concerns general quantum channels $N: L(H) o L(K)$ acting on finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces, often analyzed using direct-sum constructions ($N = N_0 \oplus N_1 \oplus N_2$).

Key observables. Holevo Capacity ($\chi(N)$), Holevo Barycenter ($\Omega(N)$), Von Neumann Entropy ($H(\cdot)$), and block weights derived from channel compositions.

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis depends on the structure of the channels, particularly comparing cases where $\chi(N_0) = \chi(N_1)$ versus $\chi(N_0) = \chi(N_1) + \delta$.

Assumptions / limitations. All Hilbert spaces are assumed to be finite-dimensional. The entropy comparisons rely on specific structural assumptions about the block states.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the context by relating Holevo capacity additivity to barycenter tensorization. It then constructs specific channels and uses entropy and block weight comparisons to prove the non-multiplicativity of the barycenter and the lack of universal entropic ordering.

Abstract

The Holevo barycenter of a quantum channel is the unique output state obtained as the average output of any ensemble achieving the Holevo capacity. Given two quantum channels, the multiplicativity problem asks whether this barycenter tensorizes under parallel composition, namely whether the barycenter of the product channel coincides with the tensor product of the individual barycenters. This question is closely related to the additivity problem for the Holevo capacity: additivity implies tensorization of the Holevo barycenter, while tensorization alone is not sufficient for additivity. Although a construction is known that demonstrates the existence of channels with non-additive Holevo capacity, their corresponding Holevo barycenters still tensorize, leaving open whether multiplicativity might ultimately hold universally. Here, we answer this question in the negative by exhibiting channels for which the Holevo barycenter is not multiplicative under the tensor product of the channel with itself. Moreover, we show that the entropy of the Holevo barycenter is neither universally subadditive nor universally superadditive under tensor product.

On the Limits of Quantum Multiparty Simultaneous Communication

Authors: Pedro Montealegre, Ivan Rapaport, Jorge Valenzuela
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10289v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work analyzes the communication complexity of a multiparty coordination problem, showing that while public randomness offers a significant advantage, unentangled quantum communication does not surpass the power of classical private randomness in the bounded-error regime. The research establishes an exponential separation based on the number of players, providing deep insights into the role of shared randomness versus quantum superposition.

Why it may be interesting. The findings provide fundamental limits on how quantum information can simulate classical coordination mechanisms, which has implications for quantum cryptography and distributed quantum computation protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper establishes an exponential separation between the communication power of classical shared randomness (public coins) and unentangled quantum communication in a multiparty setting.

Main result. It shows that for the generalized Index Coordination problem, quantum communication without shared entanglement offers no asymptotic advantage over classical private randomness in the bounded-error regime, while public randomness provides a significant advantage.

Method. The authors employ techniques like the Parity Collapse method and information-pooling arguments, utilizing the Arithmetic Mean-Geometric Mean inequality to derive lower bounds on message size.

Model / system. The study uses the Simultaneous Message Passing (SMP) model generalized to $k$ players, comparing the resources of classical private randomness, classical public randomness, and unentangled quantum superposition.

Key observables. Maximum message length required by the players, measured in bits (public coins) or qubits (quantum communication).

Important parameters / regimes. The number of players ($k$), the input size ($n$), and the error parameter ($\epsilon$).

Assumptions / limitations. The quantum protocols considered lack shared entanglement or public coins, and the results hold for fixed error parameters $\epsilon$.

Figures summary. Not specified in the notes, but the paper relies on summarizing bounds in a table (Table 1) comparing message lengths across different models.

Paper structure. The paper first generalizes the Index Coordination problem to $k$ parties. It then derives lower bounds for the unambiguous and bounded-error regimes using techniques like the RAC inequality and AM-GM inequality, culminating in the explicit separation results.

Abstract

The Simultaneous Message Passing (SMP) model provides a fundamental framework for comparing classical and quantum communication. For two players, Gavinsky et al. (STOC 2006) established a separation underlying the incomparability of shared randomness and quantum communication: \textsc{Index Coordination} needs $O(\log n)$ public-coin bits but $Ω(n^{1/3})$ bounded-error qubits. In this work, we establish a multiparty exponential separation through $\operatorname{IC}_{k,n}$, a natural $k$-party generalization of \textsc{Index Coordination}. Public-coin protocols solve it unambiguously with maximum message length $O(\log n)$ bits. In contrast, quantum SMP protocols without shared entanglement or public coins require maximum message length $Ω(n^{1-1/k})$ qubits in the unambiguous regime and $Ω(n^{(k-1)/(k+1)})$ qubits in the bounded-error regime. A classical private-coin protocol matches the unambiguous bound, so quantum communication provides no asymptotic advantage over private randomness in this regime. For fixed error parameters, all constants are independent of $k$, establishing the exponential separation for every integer-valued function $k=k(n)\ge2$, without restricting its growth. Both quantum lower bounds become $Ω(n)$ when $k\ge c\log n$ for any fixed $c>0$, matching the full-input protocol and yielding tight linear complexity in both regimes. Our results demonstrate that quantum superposition cannot efficiently simulate the coordination afforded by public randomness, extending this separation to arbitrary $k$. To bound success probabilities for multiparty product states, we prove an exact factorization theorem for unambiguous quantum state identification, which may be of independent mathematical interest.

Private communication via zero-private-capacity quantum channels

Summary. This paper tackles the superactivation problem in quantum information theory by demonstrating that two quantum channels, each having zero private capacity, can jointly support private communication. The authors achieve this by constructing a specific joint channel that yields a positive secure rate. This result proves that secure communication capacity cannot be determined by analyzing the private capacity of the individual channels in isolation.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a deep theoretical result showing that local capacity measures are insufficient for characterizing secure quantum communication, which is a fundamental question in quantum information theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper resolves the 'private-capacity superactivation' problem: determining if two quantum channels, each with zero individual private capacity, can jointly enable private communication.

Main result. The joint use of a four-level channel and a qubit erasure channel, both having zero private capacity, was shown to achieve a positive private rate, demonstrating that channel private capacity alone is insufficient to determine secure communication capability.

Method. The analysis involves constructing specific quantum channels and applying information-theoretic bounds, utilizing concepts like Holevo information and classical coding over multiple uses to establish a positive lower bound on the achievable private rate.

Model / system. The model uses two independent, memoryless quantum channels: a four-level channel and a qubit erasure channel. The analysis is highly abstract, relying on mathematical constructions and formal proofs in Lean 4.

Key observables. Private bits per product use, Holevo information ($\chi$), and the achievable rate $P(E_{2,p} \otimes N)$.

Important parameters / regimes. Erasure probability $p$ (specifically $p=1/2$), and the positive rate exceeding $1.9030 imes 10^{-4}$ bits.

Assumptions / limitations. The proof relies on bounding the environment's leakage using a 'Classical coin bound' and assumes the applicability of the private coding theorem.

Figures summary. Figure 1 summarizes the logical dependencies of the formalized argument, while Table 1 provides a detailed correspondence between paper statements and their Lean declarations.

Paper structure. The paper develops the problem by first establishing the necessary information-theoretic bounds (using Holevo information and entropy curvature) and then constructing a concrete example (the joint channel use) to achieve a positive rate, culminating in a formalization within the Lean proof assistant.

Abstract

Private communication over a noisy quantum channel requires reliable transmission to the receiver and secrecy from the environment. Whether two channels with zero private capacity can jointly enable private communication is a longstanding open problem in quantum information theory. Here we resolve this problem by exhibiting a four-level channel and a qubit erasure channel with half erasure probability, each with zero private capacity, whose joint use achieves more than 0.0001903 private bits per product use. The encoding gives the receiver a linear information gain with at most quadratic environmental leakage, enabling privacy through a fixed joint measurement and classical coding. This superactivation, impossible for independent classical memoryless wiretap channels, shows that a channel's private capacity alone does not determine its value for secure communication. The initial activation example was identified through interactions with large language models, and the result has been formalized in Lean 4.

Protected domains and the cost of cooperation on weighted networks

Authors: Abhishek Chowdhury
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10463v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work analyzes the conditions for the spread of costly cooperation on weighted networks by modeling a donation game. It derives sharp mathematical thresholds for the benefit-to-cost ratio required for cooperation to invade. The methodology uses advanced techniques from non-equilibrium statistical mechanics to quantify the structural requirements for collective traits to persist against natural selection.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical framework for analyzing the emergence of cooperation via selection thresholds on complex networks connects concepts from statistical physics (non-equilibrium dynamics, critical phenomena) to evolutionary game theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the conditions under which costly cooperation can spread and persist within a population structured on a weighted network.

Main result. It determines sharp weak-selection thresholds for cooperation, finding specific ratios of benefit to cost ($b/c$) required for fixation on different network structures like cycles and paths.

Method. The analysis employs techniques from non-equilibrium statistical mechanics, including fluctuation response, first-passage time calculations, and optimization over network weights.

Model / system. The model is a donation game played on a finite, weighted, undirected graph where traits (cooperator/defector) are inherited via copying based on weighted contacts.

Key observables. Weak-selection threshold ($b/c$), fixation advantage ($\Delta$), and the minimal contact contrast required for a given threshold.

Important parameters / regimes. The selection strength ($\epsilon$), the benefit ($b$), the cost ($c$), and the network topology (e.g., $C_4, P_5, P_6$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is restricted to the source-first, averaged-payoff process, and comparisons are made between individual payoff dominance and population-level fixation advantage.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate small supports, showing the minimal promoter structures for cooperation on a four-cycle ($C_4$), five-site path ($P_5$), and six-site path ($P_6$).

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining the game and dynamics on weighted networks, to calculating fixation probabilities and susceptibility, and finally performing rigorous optimization to find the minimal structural requirements for sustained cooperation.

Abstract

A costly inherited trait can spread when its descendants create a favorable local environment. We ask which finite contact structures sustain such a collective competitor and what their advantage costs. Individuals play a donation game, conferring benefit $b$ at cost $c$. Reciprocal weighted contacts determine averaged payoffs and copying recipients, while sources are chosen globally according to payoff. Cooperation is favored when a uniformly introduced cooperator takes over more often than a defector in the complementary experiment. Exact fluctuation response and elimination of fast configurations identify the formation and competition of protected domains as the controlling processes. The sharp weak-selection threshold infima are $b/c=3$ on the four-cycle, $7$ on the five-site path, and $1$ on every fixed path with at least six sites. On the six-site path, a global bound forces near-barrier designs into this domain hierarchy. Polynomial relations among the contact ratios then control unrestricted optimization and imply eventual exact reflection symmetry. Reaching threshold $1+ε$ requires minimum contact contrast $656ε^{-2}[1+O(ε)]$ and neutral absorption time of order $ε^{-1}$. We determine their tradeoff and the associated design tolerance. Finite selection consumes the same margin. Within the controlled small-selection window, the maximal fixation advantage is of order $ε^{5/2}$ when the contrast exceeds its exact minimum by order $ε^{-1}$. Thus an improving invasion threshold can require increasing contact heterogeneity, precision and observation time.

Quantum Feature Engineering for Credit Default Prediction: When and Why IQP Circuits Help Linear Classifiers

Authors: Menachem Finkelstein, Diana Legziel Levy, Zohar Yakhini, Sarel Cohen
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10505v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

Summary. This work tests if quantum feature engineering, implemented via an IQP circuit, can boost credit default prediction accuracy beyond what is achievable with classical feature extraction methods like Kernel PCA. By appending 16 quantum features to a Logistic Regression model, the authors achieved a statistically significant performance gain, suggesting that quantum circuits can effectively map classical data into a richer feature space beneficial for linear classifiers.

Why it may be interesting. It explores the practical application of quantum feature mapping in a real-world, high-stakes classification problem, providing quantitative evidence for the potential utility of quantum circuits in enhancing classical machine learning models.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates whether quantum feature engineering using Instantaneous Quantum Polynomial-time (IQP) circuits can provide a statistically significant advantage in improving a linear classifier for credit default prediction over classical methods.

Main result. Appending 16 IQP features to a Logistic Regression model raised the F1 score by +0.055, significantly outperforming Kernel PCA (0.493) and the raw baseline (0.462), with the advantage surviving rigorous statistical correction.

Method. The authors encode classical financial features into rotation angles ($ heta_i$) and use an $n$-qubit IQP circuit (Hadamard $ ightarrow R_Z ightarrow ZZ$ ring) to generate $2n$ expectation values as quantum features, which are then used in a Logistic Regression model.

Model / system. The model is an $n$-qubit IQP circuit, which runs in constant depth and encodes feature correlations in a $2^n$-dimensional Hilbert space. The quantum features are appended to the original classical features for classification.

Key observables. F1 score, Accuracy, AUC, and the expectation values $\langle X_i angle$ and $\langle Y_i angle$ for each qubit.

Important parameters / regimes. Number of qubits ($n=8$), number of features (16 quantum features), and the feature budget constraint used for comparison.

Assumptions / limitations. The quantum advantage is demonstrated on a classical simulator, and the comparison is specifically limited to improving a linear classifier, suggesting a mechanism of external non-linearity injection.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the 8-qubit IQP feature extractor sequence: Hadamard superposition $ ightarrow R_Z$ data-encoding $ ightarrow ZZ$ ring entanglement, followed by X and Y basis measurements.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the problem (credit risk), details the quantum feature construction (IQP circuit), compares the resulting features against classical benchmarks (KPCA, linear transformations) using rigorous statistical testing (BH correction), and analyzes the mechanism of improvement (linearity).

Abstract

Credit default prediction is a tabular classification problem in which modest gains in F1 translate directly into reduced financial exposure. We ask whether Instantaneous Quantum Polynomial-time (IQP) circuits can produce features that improve a classifier over both its raw classical baseline and Kernel PCA - the strongest unsupervised classical non-linear alternative - at an equal feature budget. The dataset provides 23 financial attributes per client; for an n-qubit circuit we select n of them, encode each as a rotation angle, and read 2n expectation values back out as new features. The motivation for using a quantum circuit is computational: an n-qubit IQP circuit runs in constant depth and encodes feature correlations in a 2^n-dimensional Hilbert space, whereas classical simulation of its exact output statistics scales exponentially in n. Using the UCI Default of Credit Card Clients dataset and five-fold cross-validation, we find that appending 16 IQP features (n = 8 qubits) to a Logistic Regression model raises F1 from 0.462 to 0.517 (+0.055, p < 0.0001). Kernel PCA, the next-best method, reaches only 0.493 at the same feature count; the gap survives Benjamini-Hochberg correction across 12 tests (p = 0.00007). No other classifier - Random Forest, SVM, XGBoost, or k-NN - benefits, which points to a linear-expressivity mechanism rather than a generic improvement. We also show that how the 8 input features are chosen matters: Random Forest importance-guided selection reaches F1 = 0.523, while encoding maximally uncorrelated features drops it to 0.496, demonstrating that the circuit amplifies informative structure rather than creating it from scratch.

The authors identify a distinct structural feature in the local field factor G(q) at intermediate wave vectors. This feature is argued to be a direct signature of short-range order, providing a crucial benchmark for understanding electronic correlations beyond simple approximations.

Why it may be interesting. The study provides benchmark data on electronic correlations in a fundamental model, which is crucial for developing accurate theoretical inputs for many-body calculations relevant to correlated materials.

Main problem. The study aims to accurately determine the wave-vector-dependent static local field factor G(q) and the static density-density response function for the 3D electron gas, particularly in the strongly coupled (low-density) regime.

Method. The primary method involves using Diffusion Monte Carlo (DMC) simulations to calculate the response functions, which are then analyzed using theoretical parametrizations.

Model / system. The system modeled is the three-dimensional electron gas (HEG), a fundamental model in condensed matter physics used as a starting point for ab initio calculations of materials.

Abstract

The three-dimensional electron gas is a fundamental model in condensed matter physics and quantum chemistry, and the exchange-correlation energy derived from it serves as the starting point of \textit{ab initio} computations of materials. However, the wave-vector-dependent response, and hence the static local field factor $G(q)$, has remained without accurate ground-state benchmark in the strongly coupled regime for three decades. Using diffusion Monte Carlo, we calculate $G(q)$ and the static density-density response function across the liquid phase and find a pronounced structure in $G(q)$ at intermediate wave vectors, already visible at metallic densities and growing with increasing interaction strength. We identify it as a fingerprint of short-range order by showing that it is required to reproduce the static structure factor. Our parametrization, valid in the entire liquid phase, predicts a low-energy resonant mode inside the particle-hole continuum.

Small-Bias Quantum Approximate Counting via the Multiplicative Adversary Method

Authors: Albert Lin, Han-Hsuan Lin
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09804v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper derives tight, fine-grained lower bounds for the quantum query complexity of distinguishing between two Hamming weights using the Multiplicative Adversary Method. By relating this problem to the unique OR problem, the authors establish a complex lower bound that precisely characterizes the required number of quantum queries in the small-bias regime.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous application of the multiplicative adversary method to derive fine-grained lower bounds provides a powerful, query-by-query understanding of quantum distinguishability, which is relevant for understanding resource scaling in quantum algorithms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper studies the quantum query complexity required to distinguish between two specific Hamming weights, $|x|=M$ versus $|x|=M+\Delta$, in the small-bias regime.

Main result. It establishes a fine-grained lower bound for this quantum counting problem, showing the complexity scales with a maximum of two terms derived from the multiplicative adversary method.

Method. The primary technique is the Multiplicative Adversary Method (MADV), which is applied to analyze the progress of quantum distinguishability across different Hamming-weight layers.

Model / system. The model is a quantum query algorithm operating on an input space $\{0,1\}^N$, where the goal is to distinguish between two subsets defined by their Hamming weights. The analysis uses concepts from the eigenspace method for symmetric functions.

Key observables. The success probability margin $\zeta$, and the quantum query complexity $Q$ (or $T$).

Important parameters / regimes. Total bits $N$, the two weights $M$ and $M+\Delta$, and the bias parameter $\zeta$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the success probability is at least $1/2 + \zeta$, and often assumes $M+\Delta \le N-M$ for the primary derivation.

Paper structure. The paper builds the lower bound by first analyzing the unique OR problem using MADV, then reducing this to the two-weight counting problem, and finally combining these results to yield the overall complexity bound.

Abstract

We study the two-weight decision version of quantum approximate counting: given oracle access to $x\in\{0,1\}^N$, distinguish $|x|=M$ from $|x|=M+Δ$ with success probability $1/2+ζ$. Using the multiplicative adversary method, we prove $Ω\left(\max\left\{ζ\sqrt{(N-M)(M+Δ)}/Δ,\sqrt{ζN/Δ}\right\}\right)$. The same parameter dependence follows from the polynomial-method characterization of the two-layer symmetric function by Podder, Yao, and Ye. Our contribution is a multiplicative-adversary derivation that tracks the progress produced by individual oracle queries. For the first term, after complementing the input if necessary, we assume $M+Δ\le N-M$. We use the Hamming-layer subspaces from the eigenspace method of Ambainis, Spalek, and de Wolf and compose their adjacent-layer unitary maps to relate the two nonadjacent promise layers. After fixing the queried coordinate, the analysis block-diagonalizes into four-dimensional subspaces. An exact calculation of the one-query progress ratio gives the first lower bound. The same estimate also implies $\left\|(I-\widehatΠ_{\mathrm{bad}})\lvertΨ^T\rangle\right\|^2=O\left(T^2Δ^2/((N-M)(M+Δ))\right)$ for the coherent input superposition used in the adversary argument. For the second term, we prove directly using a three-eigenvalue multiplicative adversary that unique OR on $n$ bits with success probability $1/2+ζ$ requires $Ω(\sqrt{ζn})$ queries, and then reduce unique OR to the two-weight counting problem.

Accurate identification of twist and strain in moiré materials is therefore essential. In this work, we establish a general framework to extract the twist and strain configurations from moiré images with either atomic or moiré scale resolution. With only moiré-wavelength information, we show that there is a continuous family of possible twist and strain configurations, each one accounting for different orientations of the moiré pattern. To estimate the most likely twist-strain configuration, we discuss additional constraints and methods involving the minimum elastic energy and the electronic spectra. The minimum elastic energy, in particular, reflects that shear configurations become much more favorable as the strain increases. As an example of the developed methodology, we discuss the formation and identification of strained triangular moiré patterns. Our framework provides a comprehensive approach to identify the twist and strain configurations in systems with moiré-scale resolution.

Abstract

We formulate a quasiclassical theory of the Edelstein effect in superconductors that incorporates both intraband and interband contributions. To describe the interband contribution, which is absent from the conventional leading-order quasiclassical formulation, we derive augmented Eilenberger equations in the presence of antisymmetric spin-orbit coupling. The intraband contribution is evaluated using multiband Eilenberger equations. We apply these formulations to supercurrent-induced surface spin magnetization in $s$-wave Rashba superconductors and investigate its dependence on temperature, distance from the surface, spin-orbit coupling strength, and supercurrent. The intraband contribution originates from a supercurrent-induced asymmetry of quasiparticles with opposite momenta and spin polarizations, whereas the interband contribution arises from the anomalous-velocity term generated by the momentum derivative of the Rashba spin-orbit potential. In the helicity basis, this anomalous-velocity term is expressed in terms of the Berry connection associated with the momentum dependence of the Rashba eigenstates. The intraband contribution increases linearly with the spin-orbit coupling strength, whereas the interband contribution exhibits a nonmonotonic dependence and is maximized when the Rashba spin splitting is comparable to the superconducting gap. Moreover, within the clean $s$-wave Rashba model considered here, we find that the nonlinear dependence of magnetization on the supercurrent arises solely from the interband contribution. Thus, although the intraband contribution dominates the linear Edelstein effect, the nonlinear Edelstein effect can serve as a useful probe of the interband contribution originating from quantum geometry.

Abstract

We present an extensive molecular dynamics (MD) study of the dynamical properties of a binary hard-sphere fluid over a wide range of packing fractions, $φ\approx 0.357-0.582$. The self-diffusivity, $D$, and shear viscosity, $η$, are computed using an efficient implementation of the Einstein--Helfand method. The finite-size effects in $D$ scale as $1/N^α$, with $α$ increasing from approximately $1/3$ to $3/4$ with increasing $φ$, whereas those in $η$ are negligible except for $φ\gtrsim 0.554$, where they scale as $1/N$. The data are then extrapolated to the thermodynamic limit to obtain $D_{\infty}$ and $η_{\infty}$. Both coefficients show a super-Arrhenius dependence on $φ$ for dense states, accompanied by a breakdown of the Stokes--Einstein relation. Although both $D_{\infty}(φ)$ and $η_{\infty}(φ)$ data are well described by an exponential form, we demonstrate that these fits do not provide reliable estimates of the critical packing fraction, $φ_0$, owing to the substantial extrapolation required beyond the accessible equilibrium range. We find the commonly assumed proportionality between $η$ and the structural relaxation time, $τ_α$ , to not hold for this system. For $φ\gtrsim 0.570$, the van Hove self-correlation function $G_s(r, τ)$ exhibits a spatial exponential decay at intermediate times, $τ$, signaling dynamic heterogeneity. The characteristic decay length scales as $λ\sim τ^ν$, with $ν\approx 1/3$, in contrast to the conventional square-root scaling. We also investigate temporal heterogeneity through the four-point dynamic susceptibility, $χ_4(τ)$, and its peak time, $τ_4$. These findings provide rigorous benchmark MD data for computational studies of glassy dynamics and establish a reference for testing theoretical models in dense disordered systems.
