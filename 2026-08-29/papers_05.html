Efficient Quantum Simulations of Yang-Mills theory with Maximal-tree Gauge

Authors: Tianyin Li, Ying-Ying Li, Xiaoyang Wang, Hongxi Xing
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27267v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

Summary. This paper presents a quantum algorithmic framework to simulate Yang-Mills theories, specifically QCD, by employing the maximal-tree gauge to simplify the Hamiltonian. The time evolution is efficiently implemented using the Quantum Singular Value Transformation (QSVT). The key achievement is deriving polynomial complexity bounds, proving that such non-Abelian theories are efficiently simulable on quantum computers.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a concrete, resource-efficient quantum algorithm for simulating a cornerstone problem in high-energy physics (QCD), which is highly relevant for quantum simulation research.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Developing an efficient quantum algorithmic framework to simulate the dynamics of Yang-Mills theories, such as SU(3) QCD.

Main result. The authors provide rigorous complexity bounds demonstrating that non-Abelian Yang-Mills theories can be simulated efficiently on quantum computers with polynomial scaling in key parameters.

Method. The simulation uses the maximal-tree gauge to fix gauge redundancies, digitizes the resulting Hamiltonian in the field-amplitude basis, and implements the time evolution via the Quantum Singular Value Transformation (QSVT).

Model / system. The system is Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD), modeled by an SU(3) non-Abelian Yang-Mills theory. The dynamics are governed by a gauge-fixed Hamiltonian $H_{ ext{fix}} = H_{\Pi} + H_B + H_V$.

Key observables. Qubit count ($N_G$) and gate complexity ($\mu$) for the time evolution operator $U_{ ext{fix}}(t)$.

Important parameters / regimes. Inverse simulation precision ($1/\epsilon_s$), lattice volume ($\mathcal{V}$ or $V$), gauge coupling ($g$), and target energy scale ($E$).

Assumptions / limitations. The derivation assumes the global Gauss-law constraint subspace allows the necessary matrix $K$ to be positive definite and invertible; the Hamiltonian includes an $O(a_s)$ discretization error.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the maximal tree structure on a cubic lattice, and Figure 2 shows the quantum circuit realizing the time evolution operator $U_{ ext{fix}}(t)$ using QSVT.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the gauge-fixed Hamiltonian using the maximal-tree gauge, derives the necessary constraints (like Gauss's law), and then details the quantum implementation using QSVT. Finally, it derives the polynomial scaling bounds for the required qubits and gate complexity.

Abstract

We develop a quantum algorithmic framework for the efficient simulation of Yang--Mills theories, including the $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ gauge theory in Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD). The framework uses maximal-tree gauge in terms of gauge field variables that removes all local gauge redundancies. In the resulting gauge-fixed formulation and digitization in the field-amplitude basis, we show that Hamiltonian time evolution admits an efficient implementation based on quantum singular value transformation (QSVT). We derive upper bounds on the total number of qubits and gate complexity, finding polynomial scaling with the inverse simulation precision $1/\varepsilon_s$, lattice volume $\mathcal{V}$, gauge coupling $g$, and target energy scale $E$. Our results provide a rigorous complexity-theoretic demonstration that non-Abelian Yang--Mills theories can be simulated efficiently on quantum computers, paving the way toward first-principles quantum simulations of non-perturbative QCD dynamics.

Main result. The Quantum Support Vector Classifier (QSVC) achieved the highest recall (99.99%) in ideal simulations, demonstrating a signal-retention advantage over classical methods, which persists even under realistic hardware noise.

Method. A quantum kernel is constructed using a fully entangled ZZFeatureMap, which is then integrated into a standard Support Vector Machine (SVM) framework for classification.

Model / system. The system is particle-track classification using centroid-based features from the CLAS12 drift-chamber detector. The methodology involves simulating the quantum kernel computation on large datasets using multi-node HPC resources.

Key observables. Recall (99.99% ideal, 99.51% noisy), AUC (0.9985 ideal, 0.9671 noisy), and peak significance improvement.

Important parameters / regimes. Scales up to $4.0 imes 10^5$ test events; performance is quantified under a calibrated hardware noise model (FakeMumbaiV2).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that the quantum kernel's ability to capture non-linear correlations provides a distinct advantage in signal-retention, even when hardware noise degrades performance.

Figures summary. The notes suggest geometric analysis showing near-orthogonal inter-class states under ideal conditions, and performance curves comparing ideal vs. noisy metrics.

Paper structure. The paper systematically designs the QSVC workflow, benchmarks it against classical kernels (linear, RBF, etc.), and then rigorously tests its performance under both ideal simulation and realistic hardware noise models.

Abstract

We present a systematic design for large-scale quantum kernel classification, demonstrated through a quantum support vector classifier (QSVC) for particle-track classification using centroid-based CLAS12 drift-chamber features. Each event is encoded into a six-qubit state via a fully entangled ZZFeatureMap, whose fidelities define a quantum kernel within a standard SVM framework. By decoupling state preparation from kernel construction and distributing evaluation across a multi-node MPI-based HPC allocation, the approach scales to 1.0x10^5 training and 4.0x10^5 test events with an exactly constructed kernel matrix, to our knowledge more than an order of magnitude larger than prior high-energy-physics quantum-kernel studies. Benchmarked against linear, polynomial, RBF, and sigmoid SVM kernels and extremely randomized trees (ERT), the ideal QSVC achieves the highest recall (99.99%) among all models. Under a calibrated hardware noise model (FakeMumbaiV2, 500 training / 2,000 test events), AUC falls from 0.9985 to 0.9671 and peak significance improvement falls from 17.5 to ~3.5, yet recall remains at 99.51% -- indicating this signal-retention advantage is attenuated but not eliminated by circuit-level decoherence. Geometric analysis of the quantum embedding shows near-orthogonal inter-class states with coherent intra-class neighborhoods under ideal simulation; under noise this structure compresses toward the maximally mixed state while preserving its relative ordering. These results demonstrate a scalable, reproducible workflow for quantum kernel experimentation at HEP-relevant scale, quantifying the practical cost of realistic hardware noise on quantum-enhanced classification.

Optimal cloning of mixed states

Authors: Marco Fanizza, Dmitry Grinko, Thilo Scharnhorst, Jack Spilecki
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27298v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper rigorously solves the sample complexity problem for approximately cloning unknown mixed quantum states. By establishing matching upper and lower bounds using the PCT cloner and projector states, it determines that the required number of input copies scales as $\Theta(krd/\epsilon)$. This result sets a fundamental limit on quantum information processing tasks.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides fundamental, quantitative bounds on the information-theoretic limits of quantum state manipulation, which is crucial for designing quantum communication protocols and understanding the robustness of quantum information.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper determines the minimum number of input copies ($n$) required to approximate the cloning of $k$ additional copies of an unknown mixed quantum state $ ho$ to a specified fidelity ($1-\epsilon$).

Main result. The sample complexity for both mixed state cloning and approximate transposition is found to be $\Theta(krd/\epsilon)$, where $r$ is the rank of the state, $d$ is the dimension, and $\epsilon$ is the error tolerance.

Method. The authors establish the upper bound using a 'Purify-Clone-Trace' (PCT) cloner, which reduces the mixed state problem to the pure state case. The matching lower bound is proven by analyzing the special, hard-to-clone case of projector states.

Model / system. The study concerns the theoretical limits of quantum state cloning and transposition for mixed states $ ho$ in a $d$-dimensional Hilbert space. The analysis uses tools from representation theory, including Schur polynomials, to derive rigorous bounds.

Key observables. Fidelity ($F$), Sample Complexity ($n$), State Rank ($r$), and Dimension ($d$).

Important parameters / regimes. The required fidelity $1-\epsilon$, the number of copies to clone $k$, and the state rank $r$.

Assumptions / limitations. The lower bound proof relies on considering projector states ($ ho = P/r$) as the worst-case scenario for cloning.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the PCT cloner process: purifying the input state, applying the Werner cloner in the larger space, and tracing out auxiliary registers.

Paper structure. The paper first reviews the known optimal result for pure states. It then constructs the PCT cloner to establish an upper bound for mixed states. Finally, it proves the matching lower bound using projector states, thereby resolving the sample complexity.

Abstract

We consider the problem of approximate cloning of quantum states: given $n$ copies of an unknown state $ρ\in \mathbb{C}^{d \times d}$, prepare an $(n+k)$-copy state with high fidelity to $ρ^{\otimes (n+k)}$. Werner's pure state cloner is the optimal channel for the pure state case, and shows that $n = Θ(kd/\varepsilon)$ copies are necessary and sufficient to clone $k$ additional copies of an unknown pure state to fidelity $1-\varepsilon$. The random purification channel gives a straightforward extension of Werner's cloner to mixed state inputs: given $n$ copies of a mixed state, randomly purify your input, apply Werner's channel in the larger Hilbert space, and then trace out the auxiliary registers. This gives a mixed state cloner using $n = O(krd/\varepsilon)$ copies to clone rank-$r$ states. Can one do any better? We show that the answer is no: one must use $n = Ω(krd/\varepsilon)$ copies. We prove our lower bound by studying the special case of projector cloning, in which the input state $ρ$ is promised to be of the form $P/r$, where $P$ is a rank-$r$ orthogonal projector. As a further application of our techniques, we consider the closely related problem of approximate transposition of quantum states, where one seeks to convert $ρ^{\otimes n}$ to a $k$-copy state with high fidelity to $(ρ^T)^{\otimes k}$. Here, we again show $n = Θ(krd/\varepsilon)$ copies are necessary and sufficient for this task.

Randomness can be certified in energy-constrained semi-device-independent scenarios

Authors: Shashank Kumar Ranu, Lewis Wooltorton, Alastair A. Abbott, Omar Fawzi

Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

Summary. This paper establishes the theoretical possibility of generating certified randomness in energy-constrained semi-device-independent quantum cryptography. By adapting the NPA hierarchy to incorporate energy bounds, the authors derive SDP relaxations that provide lower bounds on the min-entropy even when the adversary has full quantum access. This resolves a long-standing open question in the field.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides rigorous, device-independent security guarantees for quantum random number generation under realistic physical constraints (energy), which is highly relevant for quantum optics and quantum information hardware.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To determine if certified randomness can be generated in energy-constrained semi-device-independent (SDI) quantum cryptography scenarios, especially when the adversary can share quantum entanglement.

Main result. The authors affirmatively answer the open question, proving that certified randomness generation is theoretically possible even against a fully quantum adversary, yielding positive lower bounds on the min-entropy.

Method. They adapt the Navascués-Pironio-Acín (NPA) hierarchy to construct semidefinite programming (SDP) relaxations of the guessing probability, allowing for dimension-independent security proofs.

Model / system. The model is a prepare-and-measure (PM) framework for quantum random number generation, constrained by a finite energy bound ($\omega$) on the prepared states.

Key observables. The primary observables are the extractable min-entropy ($H_{\min}$) and the correlator difference ($I_{ ext{corr}}$), which quantify the security and randomness rate.

Important parameters / regimes. The energy parameter ($\omega$) is crucial, as the certified rate remains positive for a range of small energy values.

Assumptions / limitations. The core assumption is the energy constraint on the prepared states. The analysis is strongest when assuming the adversary can share full quantum entanglement.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the energy-constrained PM scenario, showing the certified $H_{\min}$ vs. $\omega$ for different dimension assumptions, and demonstrating the robustness of $H_{\min}$ when the observed correlator is reduced.

Paper structure. The paper develops the SDP relaxation by adapting the NPA hierarchy to the energy-constrained setting, deriving structural constraints on the POVM elements, and finally solving the optimization problem to yield the certified lower bound on randomness.

Abstract

The prepare-and-measure framework based on energy constraints offers a practical middle ground between fully device-dependent and device-independent quantum cryptography. The only assumption on an otherwise uncharacterized prepare-and-measure device is that the energy of the prepared states is bounded. Existing security analyses of this framework assume that the preparation and measurement devices share at most classical correlations, and under this assumption certified lower bounds on the extractable randomness have been established. Recent work has shown that an adversary who pre-distributes entanglement between the devices can mount attacks that are strictly more powerful than those available when the devices share only classical correlations, reducing the extractable randomness below the previously certified rates. This leaves open the fundamental question of whether randomness can be certified at all in this scenario. We address this open question by constructing semidefinite programming relaxations of the guessing probability by adapting the Navascués-Pironio-Acín hierarchy to the energy-constrained setting where shared entanglement between the devices is permitted. Our relaxations yield certified lower bounds on the extractable randomness without enforcing any restrictions on the dimensions of the quantum state shared between the preparation and measurement devices. We show that these certified lower bounds are strictly positive for a range of energy values, thereby answering the open question affirmatively: certified randomness generation is theoretically possible in the energy-constrained SDI framework even in the presence of a fully quantum adversary.

Abstract

Dark matter halos, which host galaxies and galaxy clusters, have peculiar velocities far from thermal equilibrium. Characterizing the nonlinear and non-Gaussian features of this velocity distribution improves our understanding of the gravitational evolution of the cosmic web and supports related cosmological applications. We endeavor to establish a connection between the peculiar velocity distribution of halos and nonequilibrium statistical mechanics, with the objective of obtaining a model that is both concise and accurate for practical applications. We extracted halo samples from large N-body simulations and performed maximum-likelihood fits to the peculiar velocity distributions using a two-parameter Tsallis model, derived from non-extensive statistical mechanics. On the theoretical side, we reformulated the halo distribution in the superstatistics framework by means of a generalized Gram-Charlier expansion based on the gamma distribution. For halo peculiar velocities below 1000 km/s, the Tsallis model achieves 5 percent accuracy over z=0-2, with performance improving toward lower redshifts. Our results show that the halo velocity distribution becomes increasingly non-Gaussian and departs further from equilibrium over time. The best-fit parameters depend only weakly on mass, though low-mass halos exhibit slightly stronger non-Gaussianity. The two parameters, especially the velocity dispersion, offer promising probes of cosmological parameters. Theoretically, we find that in general the halo peculiar velocity distribution function is expressible as a superposition of a series of Tsallis distribution functions, while simulation results demonstrate that the zeroth-order approximation, namely a single Tsallis function, already achieves sufficient accuracy.

Abstract

The Koopman-von Neumann framework has been proposed to design quantum algorithms for non-linear dynamics. It maps a non-linear ordinary differential equation to a linear partial differential equation (PDE) governing a probability amplitude. Previous works represents this amplitude in the Hermite-function basis, equivalently as a bosonic state, and truncates the total Hermite degree to obtain a representation over $Θ(m\log N)$ qubits, where $N$ is the number of variables and $m$ the truncation order. Finally, we apply the framework to Bayesian inverse problems with Gaussian priors and observation noise, reducing posterior-state preparation to the preparation of a structured Hamiltonian's ground state.

Abstract

FeTe, as a representative parent material among iron-based superconductors, provides an ideal platform for exploring the interplay among orbital-selective correlations, magnetism, and unconventional superconductivity. However, a unified picture of the correlated electronic structure and magnetism of FeTe under strain remains to be fully clarified. Here, combining density functional theory plus dynamical mean-field theory and Heisenberg model analysis, we uncover an orbital-selective reconstruction of the correlated electronic structure and reveal a strain-driven trajectory from bicollinear to stripe antiferromagnetism (AFM) via an intermediate competing staggered $n$-mer AFM regime in FeTe. Moderate strain gives rise to a regime where more coherent quasiparticles coexist with suppressed local moments. Further strain drives FeTe into an incoherent correlated regime with robust local moments and Fe-$3d_{z^2}$-dominated low-energy states. These results establish a strain-driven trajectory across distinct magnetic and correlated electronic states in FeTe.
