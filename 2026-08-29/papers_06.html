Strong Converse Exponent of Quantum State Merging

Authors: Mario Berta, Hao-Chung Cheng, Roberto Rubboli, Marco Tomamichel
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27202v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical paper calculates the exact strong converse exponent for quantum state merging, a key task in quantum information theory. It establishes that this exponent is governed by optimized $\alpha$-$z$ conditional Rényi entropies, which differs from standard results. The findings provide rigorous bounds on the achievable rates for quantum information transfer protocols.

Why it may be interesting. The precise characterization of the strong converse exponent using specific conditional entropy forms provides deep insights into the fundamental limits of quantum information processing tasks.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper determines the strong converse exponent for the entanglement cost associated with quantum state merging.

Main result. The strong converse exponent is characterized by optimized $\alpha$-$z$ conditional Rényi entropies, contrasting with typical results governed by sandwiched conditional Rényi entropies.

Method. The analysis adapts the club-sandwiched converse method and utilizes techniques from Schatten-norm Hölder theory to establish asymptotic bounds on the fidelity decay.

Model / system. The study concerns the quantum state merging task, where a tripartite pure state $\psi_{ABR}$ is used to transfer information from Alice (A) to Bob, preserving coherence relative to an inaccessible reference system R.

Key observables. Entanglement Cost ($r$), Squared Fidelity ($F$), Purified Distance, and various conditional Rényi entropies ($H^{\uparrow}_{\alpha, \alpha/2}(A|B)_{\psi}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The exponent depends on the optimization parameter $\alpha \in [1/2, 1]$ and the rate $r$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces and focuses on the strong converse regime where the optimal fidelity decays exponentially.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the quantum state merging protocol. Table I summarizes the strong converse and error exponents for different smoothing types (Globally vs. Partially smoothed).

Paper structure. The paper develops the result by establishing upper and lower bounds on the fidelity decay rate, comparing the constraints imposed by global vs. partial smoothing, and deriving the final exponent using variational representations.

Abstract

We determine the strong converse exponent for the entanglement cost of quantum state merging, showing that it is characterized by the optimized $α$-$z$ conditional Rényi entropies with $z=α/2\in[1/2,1]$. This contrasts with the sandwiched conditional Rényi entropies that typically govern strong converse exponents in quantum information theory. As a consequence, we derive the strong converse exponent of the partially smoothed conditional min-entropy in purified distance. This exponent is governed by club-sandwiched conditional entropies, whereas global smoothing leads to a sandwiched expression.

The Capacity of Entanglement and Holographic Entropies at Finite Resources

Authors: Ning Bao, Jacob March
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.26377v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work refines the understanding of holographic entropy by replacing problematic, dimension-dependent bounds with the capacity of entanglement. It proves that this capacity provides a robust, dimension-free constraint on the entanglement structure of quantum states. This is crucial for understanding the fundamental limits of entropy estimation in quantum gravity settings.

Why it may be interesting. It provides rigorous, resource-aware bounds on entanglement entropy derived from gravity/holography, which informs the fundamental limits of quantum information processing in strongly coupled systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses ambiguities in applying holographic entropy formulas, like Ryu-Takayanagi, to finite resources by refining the theoretical bounds on entanglement entropy.

Main result. It establishes that the capacity of entanglement provides a dimension-free, saturated bound, and shows that the minimal surface fixes all one-shot Rényi entropies simultaneously for holographic states.

Method. The analysis involves replacing dimension-dependent factors with the capacity of entanglement (variance of modular energy) and using concentration inequalities derived from the modular Hamiltonian.

Model / system. The framework is based on the AdS/CFT correspondence, relating boundary von Neumann entropy to the area of minimal surfaces in the bulk geometry.

Key observables. Von Neumann entropy ($S_{vN}$), Rényi entropies ($S_\alpha$), and the capacity of entanglement ($C$).

Important parameters / regimes. The entanglement area, the central charge, and the small perturbation scale relative to $S_{vN}^2$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes local smoothness of the remainder in the Rényi spectrum and relies on the holographic input structure for concentration estimates.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by identifying the ambiguities in the RT formula, proposing the capacity of entanglement as a replacement bound, and then demonstrating how this leads to strong constraints on the state's one-shot entropy spectrum.

Abstract

The Ryu-Takayanagi formula equates the area of a minimal surface with the von Neumann entropy of a boundary subregion, and leaves two things about that identification open. The first is how sharply a geometry fixes an entropy. Fannes-Audenaert answers with a Hilbert-space dimension, which diverges as the cutoff is removed however close the two states are. We replace it with the capacity of entanglement, the variance of the modular energy, whose square root grows like the square root of the entangling area where the dimensional factor grows like the regulated volume. The bound is dimension-free and saturated, and it makes the ambiguity of the entropy subextensive for any perturbation whose capacity is small compared with $S_{vN}^2$ times the trace norm. The second is what the area means for a single state, since compression and dilution rates are defined only for many copies while a geometry describes one. When a single replica saddle dominates near $α= 1$, every smooth Rényi entropy at fixed $α> 1$ agrees with $S_{vN}$ to $\textit{O}(\sqrt{S_{vN}})$, as do the smooth min- and max-entropies. The minimal surface therefore fixes every one-shot entropy of the state at once, with large central charge playing the role of large copy number in the asymptotic equipartition property. As a consequence we bound how far outside the holographic entropy cone a holographic state can appear to fall, leaving estimation and certification open.

Summary. This paper addresses the critical gap in evaluating distributed quantum circuit partitioning by moving beyond simple entanglement cost metrics. Using an automated pipeline, the authors systematically test various algorithms on simulated DQC architectures across different network topologies. The findings prove that structural metrics, such as circuit depth and gate density, are crucial for accurately predicting real-world performance penalties in modular quantum processors.

Why it may be interesting. The focus on quantifying physical overheads like circuit depth and gate density in a distributed setting is highly relevant for understanding the practical limitations of scaling quantum computation beyond single, monolithic chips.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Current evaluations of Distributed Quantum Computing (DQC) circuit partitioning algorithms are insufficient because they focus too narrowly on total entanglement cost, ignoring crucial structural and temporal overheads from network constraints.

Main result. The study demonstrates that partitioning algorithms with similar entanglement costs can yield drastically different physical execution penalties, emphasizing that structure-aware metrics are essential for guiding DQC compiler design.

Method. The authors developed an automated, reproducible evaluation pipeline that applies established monolithic benchmarking metrics to partitioned distributed circuits across various quantum network topologies and standardized workloads.

Model / system. The system modeled is Distributed Quantum Computing (DQC), which networks modular Quantum Processing Units (QPUs). Circuits are represented in OpenQASM format and are mapped onto specified network topologies (e.g., linear, grid, fully connected).

Key observables. Inter-QPU communication bottlenecks, circuit depth inflation, gate density reduction, e-bit count, circuit depth ratio, and $\Delta$gate density.

Important parameters / regimes. Standardized workloads include QASMBench, Quantum Fourier Transform (QFT), and QAOA; topologies include 4-QPU linear, grid, and fully connected networks.

Assumptions / limitations. The evaluation assumes that intra-QPU connectivity is fully connected, and the analysis relies on applying monolithic metrics to distributed structures.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the evaluation pipeline, compare reproduced e-bit counts across benchmarks (QFT, QAOA, etc.), and show correlation matrices mapping input circuit properties against DQC partitioning costs and structural penalties.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the evaluation gap, details the automated pipeline for testing various partitioning algorithms (MLFM-R, FGP-rOEE, Pytket variants) across multiple topologies and workloads, presents empirical results comparing structural penalties, and concludes that comprehensive metrics are necessary for compiler design.

Abstract

Distributed Quantum Computing (DQC) addresses the physical scaling limitations of monolithic quantum processors by networking modular Quantum Processing Units (QPUs). Efficient execution of quantum algorithms on DQC architectures requires compiling them across QPUs while minimizing inter-QPU communication bottlenecks, primarily through circuit partitioning. However, current evaluations of state-of-the-art partitioning heuristics focus primarily on the total entanglement cost of the partitions, failing to capture the broader structural and temporal overheads introduced by distributed network constraints. This paper addresses this evaluation gap by applying established monolithic benchmarking metrics to partitioned distributed circuits to quantify the performance impact of network constraints. Using an open-source, automated evaluation pipeline, we systematically assess diverse partitioning algorithms across standardized workloads and quantum network topologies. Our empirical results reveal that partitioning algorithms with comparable entanglement costs can still introduce drastically different physical execution penalties. By exposing these hidden trade-offs, such as severe increases in circuit depth and substantial reductions in gate density, this study demonstrates that comprehensive circuit-level metrics are essential for guiding the future design of DQC compilers.

Universality and sharp thresholds for ellipsoid fitting

Authors: Frederic Koehler, Youngtak Sohn
Type: theory · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27372v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper rigorously establishes a sharp phase transition for the problem of fitting random data points with an ellipsoid in high dimensions. It determines a critical threshold $\alpha^\star(\kappa)$ that dictates whether an exact fit exists, revealing a universality property dependent only on the fourth moment of the data distribution. This resolves a long-standing conjecture for Gaussian data.

Why it may be interesting. The mathematical framework of phase transitions in high-dimensional geometry and optimization is highly relevant to understanding the generalization capabilities and phase transitions in complex machine learning models.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper establishes the existence and nature of a sharp phase transition for determining if a set of random vectors can be perfectly fitted by a positive definite ellipsoid.

Main result. A sharp threshold $\alpha^\star(\kappa)$ exists, separating the regime where a fit is possible from the regime where the minimum fitting error is strictly positive. For Gaussian data, this threshold is found to be $1/4$.

Method. The analysis uses tools from random matrix theory and convex optimization, framing the problem as a random Semidefinite Program (SDP) in both satisfiable (SAT) and unsatisfiable (UNSAT) regimes.

Model / system. The model consists of $n$ random vectors $\{x_i\} \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ with independent subgaussian coordinates, mean zero, and a common fourth moment $\kappa$. The problem is finding $R \succeq 0$ such that $x_i^ op R x_i = 1$ for all $i$.

Key observables. The ratio $\alpha = n/d^2$, the threshold $\alpha^\star(\kappa)$, and the optimal squared fitting error $\Gamma_X$.

Important parameters / regimes. The common fourth moment $\kappa$ of the coordinate distribution, and the dimension $d$ relative to the number of samples $n$.

Assumptions / limitations. The vectors are assumed to have independent coordinates satisfying subgaussian bounds, though later results extend universality to broader classes of distributions.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the phase diagram showing the theoretical threshold $\alpha^\star(\kappa)$ versus numerical estimates, and the average squared loss in the unsatisfiable regime.

Paper structure. The paper develops results by analyzing the SAT and UNSAT regimes separately, deriving the threshold $\alpha^\star(\kappa)$ based on the fourth moment $\kappa$, and proving universality for the asymptotic error in the failure regime.

Abstract

We establish a sharp phase transition for fitting random vectors by an ellipsoid. The random vectors have independent subgaussian coordinates with mean zero, variance one, and a common fourth moment, and the number of vectors is proportional to the square of the dimension. We identify an explicit satisfiability threshold such that, with high probability, a positive definite ellipsoid passes through every data point below the threshold, whereas no positive semidefinite fit exists above it. We also determine the optimal squared fitting error throughout the unsatisfiable regime. In particular, the threshold depends on the coordinate distributions only through their common fourth moment, revealing a fourth moment universality phenomenon. For standard Gaussian data the threshold is $1/4$, resolving the ellipsoid fitting conjecture.

Occupation-Driven Josephson Diode in a Symmetric Junction

Authors: Jianxiong Zhai, Zelei Zhang, Jiawei Yan

Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: 🔥 Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 4/5 · methods for driven-dissipative 3/5 · correlated / nonlocal dissipation 1/5 · driven-dissipative phase transition 1/5 · quantum measurements 1/5

Summary. This paper introduces a mechanism for achieving a Josephson diode effect in a symmetric junction by engineering the non-equilibrium occupation of quantum states via attached reservoirs. By analyzing the system using advanced Green's function techniques, the authors demonstrate that the diode asymmetry originates solely from the reservoir-induced occupation factor, not from intrinsic junction asymmetry. This provides a powerful tool for designing superconducting electronic components.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a sophisticated theoretical framework for engineering non-reciprocity in superconducting devices using reservoir engineering, which is highly relevant to superconducting quantum circuits and mesoscopic physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper proposes a novel mechanism for generating a Josephson diode effect in a symmetric junction, where the nonreciprocity arises purely from non-equilibrium occupation of current-carrying states rather than an inherent asymmetry in the Andreev spectrum.

Main result. The diode effect is shown to be entirely governed by a reservoir-controlled occupation factor ($P_\gamma$), which can be engineered to approach ideal rectification ($\eta_D o 1$) even when the underlying spectral current ($I_A$) is reciprocal.

Method. The analysis employs the Keldysh-Lindblad formalism, first in the simplified Quantum Zeno limit, and later extended to finite superconducting gaps and dissipation using contour-ordered Green's functions.

Model / system. The system is a double-quantum-dot junction coupled to superconducting leads and engineered reservoirs. The dynamics are governed by a Lindblad master equation, which is analyzed using Green's function techniques.

Key observables. Diode efficiency ($\eta_D$), critical currents ($I_c^+, |I_c^-|$), and the current factorization $I_{ss} = I_A P_\gamma$.

Important parameters / regimes. Reservoir coupling strength ($\kappa$), superconducting gap ($\Delta$), and local Coulomb repulsion ($U$).

Assumptions / limitations. The initial analysis relies on the Quantum Zeno limit ($\kappa \gg t, \Gamma$), but the results are shown to be robust and extend to finite $\kappa$ and finite $\Delta$.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate Zeno filtering separating bright/dark orbitals, the diode effect via occupation reweighting ($P_\gamma$), and the effect of Coulomb repulsion inducing a dark-pair resonance.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from the simplified Zeno limit analysis to deriving the full steady-state Green's functions using contour formalism, culminating in the characterization of the finite-gap diode efficiency.

Abstract

We propose a Josephson diode mechanism in which nonreciprocity arises not from a conventional asymmetric Andreev spectrum but from nonequilibrium occupation of the current-carrying states engineered by attached reservoirs. We realize this mechanism in a double-quantum-dot junction, where a phase-textured nonlocal reservoir acts as a quantum Zeno selector: rapid dissipation freezes out the bright state directly coupled to the jump operator $L$, while preserving an orthogonal dark Andreev channel whose supercurrent remains comparable to that of the lossless junction. In the infinite-gap limit, the steady-state current factorizes as $I_{\rm ss}=I_A P_γ$, so that even when the Andreev current $I_A$ is strictly reciprocal, the phase asymmetry of $P_γ$ alone can produce a Josephson diode effect through reservoir engineering. We further show that local Coulomb repulsion can drive the system toward a nearly ideal diode regime via a dark-pair resonance. Using Keldysh-Lindblad calculations, we demonstrate that our results remain robust for realistic junctions with a finite superconducting gap and dissipation.

Summary. This paper theoretically analyzes the Non-Hermitian Skin Effect in a chain of dipoles coupled via long-range radiative interactions within a chiral medium. This work advances the understanding of boundary effects in open, chiral, and long-range coupled quantum systems.

Why it may be interesting. Main problem. The study investigates the Non-Hermitian Skin Effect (NHSE) in a system characterized by long-range radiative coupling within a reciprocal chiral medium.

Main result. The system exhibits a complex skin mode profile composed of an exponential term from a complex-beta pole and a longer-range tail decaying as 1/[n(ln n)^2].

Method. The analysis involves comparing the open-boundary condition (OBC) spectrum to the Bloch spectrum using complex analysis techniques, particularly analyzing the complex-beta plane structure.

Model / system. The model is a straight chain of electric dipoles embedded in a passive reciprocal chiral medium, whose coupling is mediated by long-range radiative interactions.

Abstract

We find that long-range radiative coupling through a passive reciprocal chiral medium produces an unusual non-Hermitian skin effect in a chain of electric dipoles. Although the system is reciprocal, the chirality generates polarization-dependent phase accumulation and attenuation, leading to degenerate pairs of skin modes localized at opposite boundaries. We find that the skin mode profile consists of an exponential contribution from an isolated complex-$β$ pole and a longer-range tail proportional to $1/[n(\ln n)^2]$, where $n$ is the distance from the occupied boundary measured in lattice sites. Finite-chain calculations show that part of the OBC spectrum contracts toward the Bloch spectrum, whereas the number of boundary-localized modes remains extensive.

Abstract

Summary. This theoretical work investigates how breaking inversion symmetry in two-band superconductors stabilizes interband phase solitons (IBPS) in a ring geometry. The key mechanism is a magneto-electric coupling term that acts as a chiral bias, forcing the soliton to become the true ground state. This transition is predicted to manifest as a unique, field-odd signature in the measured current-flux response.

Why it may be interesting. The mechanism of stabilizing a topological excitation (the soliton) via a symmetry-breaking coupling term ($\Lambda$) is analogous to controlling quantum states in superconducting circuits or Josephson junctions, making it relevant to open quantum systems and topological physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study determines the conditions under which an Interband Phase Soliton (IBPS) in a two-band superconducting ring transitions from a metastable state to a true ground state.

Main result. Breaking inversion symmetry introduces a magneto-electric coupling term that biases the energy balance, allowing the soliton to become the true ground state, which is observable as a field-odd branch in current-flux measurements.

Method. The analysis involves minimizing a reduced Gibbs energy functional derived from the two-band Ginzburg-Landau theory, solving the Double Sine-Gordon equation, and analyzing the resulting phase diagram.

Model / system. The system is a two-band superconductor confined to a ring geometry. The physics is modeled using a GL functional augmented by a magneto-electric Lifshitz term, leading to a relative-phase degree of freedom ($\phi$) that supports soliton excitations.

Key observables. Field-odd soliton branches in the equilibrium current-flux response $I_n(\Phi_a)$; the ground state selection between uniform and solitonic configurations.

Important parameters / regimes. The magneto-electric coupling strength ($\Lambda$), the winding number ($n$), and the superconducting gap parameters ($g_1, g_2$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes a thin-walled ring geometry and that the order parameters are rigid, depending only on the azimuthal coordinate $\phi$.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the physical setup in a noncentrosymmetric ring, the double-sine-Gordon profile for the soliton, and phase diagrams showing the transition between uniform and solitonic ground states as the bias $\Lambda$ increases.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the physical model and the problem of metastability in centrosymmetric rings, to incorporating the symmetry-breaking magneto-electric term ($\Lambda$), deriving the energy minimization conditions, and finally predicting the observable signature in current-flux measurements.

Abstract

Two-band superconductors maintain a relative interband phase which can carry winding soliton excitations in a superconducting ring, supported by independent winding numbers in the two bands. In rings of superconductors obeying the inversion symmetry the interband phase solitons are metastable states, separated from the uniform ground state by the energy of screening currents. In this work we find that, breaking the inversion symmetry strongly enough, one can make the soliton a true ground state. In that case, a magneto-electric coupling, absent in centrosymmetric materials, contributes critically above a certain threshold, a relevant, free-energy term, odd with respect to the winding number, which biases the energy balance in favor of a particular winding sign. Once the bias outweighs the energy cost that originally made the soliton metastable, a phase soliton with a finite winding number becomes the ground state, with chirality set by the applied field. In current--flux measurements performed in equilibrium states, the effect is demonstrated by field-odd soliton branches, that replace the metastable ones existing in mesoscopic rings, built by two-component superconductors, realizing a magneto-electric analog of the Little--Parks fluxoid-branch physics in the interband relative-phase sector.

In the gapless normal wire segment, the two parity-dependent signals originate from separate energy resonances corresponding to the lack of generic zero-energy states. By contrast, in the topological wire, the pair of low energy levels with even and odd parity are nearly degenerate in energy, and therefore, the two parity branches remain within the same broader resonance region in the quantum-dot potential, and persist under independent variations of the dot potential and wire chemical potential. Our results show that the experimentally observed quantum capacitance response is distinguished not by a particular flux trace at one optimized point in parameter space, but by its persistence over a finite range of independently controlled parameters, including the quantum-dot potential. Abstract

High-frequency artificial phononic crystals offer a low-loss platform compatible with on-chip integration, yet realizing Chern phononic phases at GHz frequencies remains challenging. Here, we propose a magnon-induced phononic Chern insulator in a honeycomb phononic crystal hybridized with ferromagnetic islands at the hexagon centers. A circularly polarized Kittel mode couples to the surrounding phonons with a phase winding, which breaks time-reversal symmetry and opens a full Chern gap. In the large-detuning regime, this mechanism leads to an effective Haldane-type phononic model with magnon-induced complex hopping. By tuning the magnon-phonon interaction, the full hybrid system accesses Chern phases with tunable Chern numbers |C|=1 and |C|=2. The predicted gaps can exceed realistic phonon and magnon linewidths, enabling their observation in GHz acoustic devices. Our work establishes chiral magnon--phonon hybridization as a route to magnetically reconfigurable topological phononics.

Abstract

Magic-angle helical trilayer graphene---three graphene layers sequentially twisted in the same direction by $\sim1.8^\circ$---relaxes into a mosaic of domains that, at zero field, carry opposite valley-resolved Chern numbers, with boundaries hosting a network of gapless conducting modes. Charge transport through this network depends sensitively on how the modes connect and scatter, making well-quantized transport unlikely. Contrary to this expectation, we observe a field-induced Chern gap with Chern number $C=-6$ emanating from charge neutrality; in this gap, the Hall resistance is quantized to within $2\%$ of the expected value, $-h/6e^2$, at 4.6 K. We explain this behavior using both Hofstadter and orbital Zeeman calculations, which show that a moderate magnetic field drives a valley-selective topological transition. Above the transition, the total Chern number of the occupied states in each spin-valley flavor becomes identical across neighboring domains, and the domain-wall modes can become gapped. Though the central valence-band Chern numbers still differ between the two domain types, the observed quantized transport attests to a global gap.
