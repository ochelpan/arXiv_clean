Arithmetic Tuning of Dynamical Critical Exponents in Quasiperiodic Localization Transitions

Authors: Tian-Cheng Yi, Yi-Fan Liu, Enguo Guan, Wen-Long You
Type: theory · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.23990v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Frenkel-Kontorova 3/5 · non-equilibrium universality 3/5 · analog quantum simulation 2/5 · driven-dissipative phase transition 2/5

Summary. This paper demonstrates that the critical dynamics of localization transitions in quasiperiodic systems can be tuned simply by varying the incommensurate frequency ($\Omega$) derived from arithmetic sequences. While the correlation-length exponent remains fixed, the dynamical critical exponent varies systematically, suggesting that the arithmetic properties of the modulation parameter control the non-equilibrium quantum dynamics.

Why it may be interesting. The finding that the arithmetic structure of an irrational number can act as a control parameter for tuning dynamical critical behavior is highly relevant for understanding non-equilibrium quantum dynamics in engineered systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how the dynamical critical exponents of localization transitions in quasiperiodic systems can be tuned without altering the underlying microscopic Hamiltonian.

Main result. The correlation-length exponent remains constant ($ u \simeq 1$), while the dynamical critical exponent ($z$) varies systematically by changing the incommensurate frequency ($\Omega$) via the arithmetic structure of generalized Fibonacci sequences.

Method. The analysis employs advanced diagnostics including generalized fidelity susceptibility, localization-length scaling, and finite-size gap analysis applied to the single-particle Schrödinger equation.

Model / system. The system is a one-dimensional tight-binding chain of spinless fermions subjected to a quasiperiodic onsite potential, defined by $V_j = V \cos(2\pi\Omega j + \phi)$. The quasiperiodicity is controlled by $\Omega$, which is derived from generalized Fibonacci sequences parameterized by $(m, n)$.

Key observables. Correlation-length exponent ($ u$), Dynamical critical exponent ($z$), Generalized fidelity susceptibility ($\chi^{2r+2}$), and the low-energy energy gap ($\Delta$).

Important parameters / regimes. Hopping amplitude ($J$), Modulation amplitude ($V$), and the incommensurate frequency ($\Omega$) controlled by the arithmetic parameters $(m, n)$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the system is quasiperiodic (ruling out rational $\Omega$) and uses finite-size scaling theory to extract critical exponents.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate data collapse for $ u=1$ using $\ln \chi^2$ and $\xi$ vs $V$ for different modulation parameters, confirming the robustness of $ u$.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the physical model and the control mechanism using arithmetic sequences. It then applies scaling analyses using fidelity susceptibility and gap measurements to extract $ u$ and $z$, demonstrating the differential dependence of these exponents on the arithmetic parameter $\Omega$.

Abstract

The critical exponents and universality classes of localization transitions in quasiperiodic systems are of fundamental importance for understanding critical phenomena in aperiodic systems. Here we show that the dynamical critical behavior can be tuned without adding new terms or changing the form of the Hamiltonian, but solely by varying the incommensurate frequency of the quasiperiodic onsite potential. We construct a family of incommensurate frequencies from the limiting ratios of generalized Fibonacci sequences controlled by the parameters $(m,n)$, and use them to define the quasiperiodic onsite potential. By combining generalized fidelity susceptibility, localization-length scaling, and finite-size gap analysis, we find that the correlation-length exponent is insensitive to the choice of the incommensurate frequency and remains consistent with the correlation-length critical exponent, $ν\simeq 1$, in the localization transition of the standard Aubry--Andr'e--Harper model. In contrast, the dynamical exponent extracted from the low-energy gap scaling varies systematically with the incommensurate frequency. Our results show that changing the incommensurate frequency provides a simple way to tune dynamical critical scaling in deterministic aperiodic systems. Our results suggest instead that the arithmetic structure of an irrational number can serve as a control parameter for nonequilibrium quantum dynamics, enabling the tuning of dynamical critical behavior without changing the microscopic Hamiltonian or the physical spatial dimension.

Summary. This paper develops a minimal, thermodynamically consistent chemical oscillator model to study autonomous oscillations driven by non-equilibrium processes. Using advanced mathematical techniques like normal-form reduction, the authors analytically determine the Hopf bifurcation point. Crucially, they show that this dynamical transition is accompanied by sharp, quantifiable discontinuities in key thermodynamic observables, bridging non-equilibrium dynamics and thermodynamics.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous connection drawn between a non-equilibrium dynamical transition (Hopf bifurcation) and measurable thermodynamic quantities (discontinuities in free energy/work rate) provides a powerful theoretical tool for characterizing open, non-equilibrium systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To introduce and analyze a minimal, thermodynamically consistent chemical oscillator that exhibits autonomous oscillations under non-equilibrium driving.

Main result. The system undergoes a supercritical Hopf bifurcation at a critical parameter value, which is analytically linked to kink-like discontinuities in time-averaged thermodynamic observables like the semi-grand Gibbs free energy and non-conservative work rate.

Method. The analysis employs normal-form reduction near the Hopf bifurcation point, utilizing a controlled irreversible approximation to analytically determine the bifurcation structure and thermodynamic response.

Model / system. The model is a Chemical Reaction Network (CRN) involving five species, featuring reversible, uni- and bi-molecular reactions, and open to an environment via chemostatting. The dynamics are governed by the Reaction Rate Equation.

Key observables. Semi-grand Gibbs free energy, non-conservative work rate, Hopf bifurcation point, limit cycle radius, and angular frequency.

Important parameters / regimes. Chemostat concentration (control parameter), reaction rate constants, and the bifurcation parameter $[S]$.

Assumptions / limitations. The reactions are assumed to be elementary, allowing the use of Local Detailed Balance (LDB), and the analysis relies on normal-form reduction near the bifurcation point.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the schematic reaction network, the time evolution of thermodynamic quantities ($\langle G angle$ and $\langle \dot{W} angle$) across the Hopf bifurcation, and the convergence of analytical predictions for the bifurcation threshold.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the minimal CRN, derives the necessary thermodynamic balance equations, analyzes the dynamics using normal-form reduction near the Hopf bifurcation, and finally calculates the discontinuity of thermodynamic observables at the onset of oscillations.

Abstract

Inspired by the chemical reaction network considered as the smallest system featuring a Hopf bifurcation and its reversible extension, we introduce an even more minimal reaction network that is thermodynamically consistent and exhibits autonomous oscillations under nonequilibrium driving. The model combines three features that are rarely realized simultaneously in compact oscillator networks: a chemically plausible structure restricted to uni- and bimolecular reactions, reversibility of such reactions, and analytical tractability. The system consists of three internal species coupled to two chemostatted species and still undergoes a supercritical Hopf bifurcation when a chemostat concentration is varied. To analyze the dynamics and thermodynamics near the onset of oscillations, we employ the mathematical technique of normal-form reduction, which allows obtaining a controlled irreversible approximation that preserves the leading phase-space structure of the full reversible network while enabling explicit calculations. This framework provides analytical access to the bifurcation structure and the leading contributions governing thermodynamic observables. Within this setting, we use the onset of oscillations to characterize the thermodynamic response of the system. In particular, we offer an analytical description of key thermodynamic quantities such as the semi-grand Gibbs free energy and the non-conservative work rate, which exhibit a kink-like discontinuity at the Hopf bifurcation. We thus provide an analytical description of a phenomenon previously characterized only through numerical simulations of more complex networks.

Summary. This theoretical paper develops a geometric framework to explain how micro-swimmers can generate directed motion by exploiting thermal fluctuations when driven by time-varying external parameters. It mathematically links the resulting propulsion to the non-zero curvature of the parameter space and quantifies the associated energy dissipation using a Riemannian metric. This unifies concepts from stochastic mechanics, thermodynamics, and geometry in the context of active matter.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a powerful geometric framework linking non-equilibrium thermodynamics (entropy production) directly to the topology of the control parameter space (Berry curvature), which is a concept highly relevant in quantum pumping and quantum control.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To show how thermal fluctuations allow a micro-swimmer (scallop) to achieve directed propulsion, bypassing the constraints of Purcell's scallop theorem.

Main result. Directed geometric propulsion is achieved when the control parameters are cyclically modulated, leading to a non-zero Berry-Sinitsyn-Nemenman curvature and excess entropy production quantified by a Riemannian metric.

Method. The analysis uses stochastic thermodynamics, solving the Smoluchowski equation via perturbation theory and generalized inverse operators to calculate non-equilibrium work and entropy production.

Model / system. The system is a single-hinge scallop swimmer whose stochastic shape dynamics are governed by a Smoluchowski equation dependent on two time-varying control parameters ($ heta_1, heta_2$).

Key observables. Non-zero Berry-Sinitsyn-Nemenman curvature ($F_{12}$), Riemannian thermodynamic metric ($g_{ij}$), and excess entropy production ($\Sigma_{ex}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The slow-driving parameter $\epsilon$, and the control parameters $ heta(t)$ which are modulated along a closed loop in the parameter space $T^2$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes Stokes flow ($ ext{Re} \ll 1$), utilizes perturbation theory for slow driving, and relies on the generalized inverse operator for the Smoluchowski generator.

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically illustrates the scallop model and shows that cycling the control parameters along a closed loop in the parameter space induces the necessary geometric curvature and non-zero net displacement.

Paper structure. The paper develops the framework by first establishing the stochastic dynamics via the Smoluchowski equation, then applying perturbation theory to calculate non-equilibrium corrections, and finally deriving the geometric connection between excess entropy production and the Riemannian metric tensor.

Abstract

According to Purcell's scallop theorem, reciprocal single-degree-of-freedom shape deformations cannot achieve net propulsion in a viscous fluid. We show that this limitation is bypassed by thermal fluctuations in a two-parameter driven potential landscape. Formulating the stochastic shape dynamics via a Smoluchowski equation with position-dependent mobility $M_\mathrm{eff}(x)$, we utilize a generalized inverse operator to evaluate the slow-driving response. Cyclic modulation of the control parameters induces a non-zero Berry-Sinitsyn-Nemenman curvature $F_{12}(\bmθ)$, resulting in directed geometric propulsion. Simultaneously, the non-adiabatic excess dissipation is dictated by a Riemannian thermodynamic metric $g_{ij}(\bmθ)$. Our results provide a unified geometric foundation that bridges hydrodynamic friction, stochastic mechanics, and thermodynamic trade-offs in micro-swimmers.

Geometry-controlled correlated electric-field noise in enclosed ion traps from billiard return spectra

Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.24770v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: correlated / nonlocal dissipation 3/5 · Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5 · QC/QI experiment 1/5 · analog quantum simulation 1/5 · methods for driven-dissipative 1/5 · quantum measurements 1/5

Summary. This theoretical paper determines how the physical boundaries of an ion trap control the correlated electric-field noise acting on trapped ions. By employing techniques from spectral theory and billiards, the authors show that the enclosure geometry selectively enhances normal-field noise while suppressing tangential-field noise. This provides a fundamental understanding of how passive electrode design can be used to engineer the quantum noise environment for quantum computing applications.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, quantitative link between macroscopic electrostatics (geometry) and microscopic quantum noise processes (heating rates, Lindblad operators) in trapped ions, offering a new diagnostic tool for trap quality.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how the passive conducting geometry of an enclosed ion trap dictates the spatial structure and magnitude of correlated electric-field noise experienced by trapped ions.

Main result. The enclosure geometry imposes a mode-selective filtering effect, increasing normal-field noise while decreasing tangential-field noise, and provides an exact return-pair functional describing this noise.

Method. The analysis uses boundary-potential covariance, the Dirichlet Green function, and billiard unfolding techniques to construct the electric-field cross-spectral matrix and its associated Kossakowski generator.

Model / system. The system is trapped ions confined within an enclosed geometry, such as a parallel slab or a general curved cover. The noise source is modeled as a stochastic electric field fluctuating due to the surrounding passive conductors.

Key observables. Electric-field cross-spectral matrix, collective environmental noise eigenchannels, noise rank, and the ratio of normal-to-tangential heating rates ($\zeta(3)$ and $\eta(3)$ at specific limits).

Important parameters / regimes. The ratio of cover height to ion spacing ($h/d$), the frequency ($\omega$), and the ion number ($N$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the Born–Markov secular approximation and assumes fixed stochastic source statistics, focusing on the geometric reshaping of the noise operator.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the mode-selective filtering effect (normal vs. tangential heating ratios) and show the correlation between the electrostatic decay exponent and the shortest specular excess length across curved covers.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the general framework using boundary-potential covariance to deriving the exact return-pair functional for the parallel slab. It then applies this framework to show the collective ordering theorem for normal/tangential fields and extends the analysis to general curved covers, concluding with diagnostics on noise rank and gate exposure.

Abstract

We ask how passive conducting geometry determines the spatial structure of electric-field noise seen by trapped ions. From a boundary-potential covariance and the Dirichlet Green function we construct the $N$-ion electric-field cross-spectral matrix and its blockwise motional Kossakowski generator. In a parallel slab, billiard unfolding turns the electrostatic response into a return-depth measure and yields an exact return-pair functional for arbitrary stationary surface spectra. For every finite equal-height ion array, the passive cover increases the normal-field covariance matrix in the positive-semidefinite ordering and decreases the tangential-field covariance matrix in the same ordering: all collective normal-field coordinates acquire more absolute noise, while all collective tangential coordinates acquire less. At $h=2d$ the local-noise single-ion ratios are exactly $ζ(3)$ and $η(3)$. Diagonalizing the equal-frequency covariance identifies collective environmental noise eigenchannels and a geometry-dependent noise rank; within a degenerate frequency block these become the Lindblad jump channels. In a ten-ion example, closing the cover to $h=2d$ lowers the participation rank from 5.61 to 5.11 while increasing the leading channel's share from 23.7% to 28.6%; a primitive Mølmer-Sørensen calculation shows how the projected covariance sets the weak-heating gate exposure. Beyond parallel walls, specular paths emerge as large-$q_z$ saddles of the screened boundary operator. Across 16 curved covers, the fitted electrostatic decay exponent correlates at 0.9991 with the independently computed shortest specular excess length, while separate tests resolve focusing and competing saddles.

Jerky Motion of Active Granular Particles

Summary. This work investigates jerky motion in active granular particles caused by the combination of self-propulsion and dry friction. The authors demonstrate that this interaction forces the dynamics into a jerk-dominated regime, predicting a quadratic increase in speed over time. The findings are supported by theoretical modeling, numerical analysis, and physical experiments using active vibrobots.

Why it may be interesting. The emergence of higher-order, non-Markovian dynamics from simple frictional contacts is a general mechanism in non-equilibrium statistical physics, relevant for understanding energy dissipation and transport in complex active matter systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The standard Newtonian description is insufficient for systems exhibiting abrupt transitions between rest and motion, necessitating the inclusion of higher-order derivatives like jerk.

Main result. The interplay between activity and dry friction robustly generates jerk-dominated dynamics, leading to quadratic speed increase ($v \sim t^2$) rather than the linear growth expected classically.

Method. The study combines analytical derivations (using path integrals and Onsager-Machlup functionals) with numerical simulations and physical experiments using active vibrobots.

Model / system. The system involves self-propelled particles (active granular particles) subjected to an active force and dry friction, which introduces a threshold for motion.

Key observables. Particle speed scaling exponent ($\alpha$), displacement scaling ($\delta r \sim t^3$), and the probability of activation.

Important parameters / regimes. Dry friction threshold force ($\Delta F$), activity strength, vibration amplitude (low vs. high), and persistence time ($ au$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis often relies on approximations such as neglecting white noise or assuming the velocity after activation is independent of the activation point.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the mechanism of jerky motion due to dry friction, compare speed evolution in jerky vs. Newtonian regimes, and show data collapse confirming cubic scaling of displacement.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the theoretical need for higher-order dynamics, models the system using active Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes coupled with dry friction, derives scaling laws, and validates these predictions through both numerical simulations and physical experiments.

Abstract

Abrupt transitions between rest and motion can render the standard Newtonian description -- based on position, velocity, and acceleration -- incomplete, requiring higher-order derivatives such as the jerk, the third time derivative of position. Here, we show that the interplay between activity and dry friction gives rise to robust jerk-dominated dynamics in self-propelled particles: the particle speed increases quadratically with time under an active force, in contrast to the linear growth expected for conventional Newtonian dynamics. We demonstrate this behavior analytically, numerically, and experimentally using active vibrobots self-propelling on a vertically vibrating plate at low vibration amplitudes, where surface asperities generate dry friction and, thus, give rise to jerky motion when combined with activity. Our results establish dry friction as a simple mechanism for realizing higher-order dynamics in active matter and suggest that jerky dynamics may arise broadly in nonequilibrium systems with frictional contacts.

By leveraging entanglement-based photon sources and integrating Quantum Key Distribution (QKD), the system achieves unprecedented key rates and high clock synchronization precision. Abstract

We experimentally demonstrate an entanglement-based Quantum-Secure Time Transfer (QSTT) system in an emulated low Earth orbit satellite-to-ground channel using a type-0 Sagnac-based entangled-photon source. Our new QSTT system delivers a finite Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) key rate of approximately $3~$bits$~\text{s}^{-1}$ with a $10^{-10}$ security parameter, providing a $30$-fold increase in the QKD key rate compared to the state-of-the-art entanglement-based QKD system delivered by the Micius satellite. Beyond this high key rate outcome, novel to our QSTT system is a GPS-free clock synchronization, optimized use of QKD, embedded post-quantum security, and obfuscation of the system configuration via use of a pre-shared key. Collectively, these enhancements deliver the most efficient and secure deployment of QSTT to date, and point the way forward to high-accuracy ultra-secure time transfer in space.

Summary. This paper presents a rigorous, resource-efficient method to estimate the fidelity susceptibility ($\chi_F$), a key indicator of quantum phase transitions. It achieves this by reformulating $\chi_F$ as a moment reconstruction problem solvable via Krylov subspace techniques. By coupling this theory with randomized single-copy measurements (classical shadows), the authors provide a statistically certified lower bound on $\chi_F$ suitable for near-term quantum devices.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a crucial bridge between theoretical quantum diagnostics ($\chi_F$) and practical, resource-constrained quantum measurements (classical shadows), making it highly relevant for experimental quantum simulation and metrology.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of directly measuring the fidelity susceptibility ($\chi_F$), a key diagnostic for quantum phase transitions and metrology, on quantum hardware.

Main result. It develops a method to certify a lower bound for $\chi_F$ by approximating it using randomized single-copy measurements and Krylov subspace methods.

Method. The core method involves reformulating $\chi_F$ as a moment problem, approximating it via the Krylov subspace, and then estimating the required moments using classical shadow techniques.

Model / system. The theory applies to general quantum systems described by a Hamiltonian $H(\lambda)$ with a non-degenerate ground state, often exemplified by models like the 1D Transverse-Field Ising Model.

Key observables. Fidelity susceptibility ($\chi_F$), linear static susceptibilities ($\chi_O$), and response moments ($M_k$).

Important parameters / regimes. The convergence rate depends on the response condition number ($\kappa_\phi$), and the required measurement resources scale with the desired accuracy ($\epsilon$) and failure probability ($\delta$).

Assumptions / limitations. Assumes a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, a non-degenerate ground state, and a spectral gap ($\Delta > 0$). The final bound is an additive, rather than relative, guarantee.

Figures summary. Figure 1 compares the calculated $\chi^{(n)}_F$ approximations against the exact $\chi_F$ for the 1D TFIM, demonstrating geometric convergence as the Krylov order $n$ increases.

Paper structure. The paper builds from the definition of $\chi_F$ via resolvent methods, reduces it to a moment reconstruction problem, applies Krylov subspace theory to establish a monotone lower bound hierarchy, and finally uses randomized Pauli measurements (classical shadows) to estimate these moments and certify the bound.

Abstract

Fidelity susceptibility is a useful probe of quantum phase transitions and quantum metrology, but its direct evaluation on a quantum device is challenging. By integrating Krylov-subspace methods with a resolvent-based reformulation, we present a method for certifying fidelity susceptibility using randomized single-copy measurements across repeated ground-state preparations. The resulting approximations form monotone lower bounds to the exact fidelity susceptibility and converge geometrically. We also present applications to metrology and linear static susceptibilities.

Summary. This work theoretically investigates Dynamic Quantum Phase Transitions in the two-leg Creutz ladder with long-range hopping. By analyzing the dynamical free energy derived from an exact two-band model solution, the authors determine that the critical times are highly sensitive to the hopping decay exponent ($ u$) and the finite range ($D$). The findings suggest that for certain parameter regimes, the critical dynamics become extremely dense, indicating complex non-equilibrium behavior.

Why it may be interesting. The connection between long-range interactions, quasi-periodicity, and the emergence of dense critical dynamics is a key topic in non-equilibrium quantum many-body physics, relevant to understanding thermalization and quantum chaos.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates Dynamic Quantum Phase Transitions (DQPTs) that occur during quantum quenches in a specific lattice model.

Main result. The critical times where non-analyticities in the dynamical free energy appear depend on both the long-range hopping exponent (ν) and the finite range (D).

Method. The analysis involves calculating the Loschmidt amplitude and the dynamical free energy from an exact solution of a generic two-band model in momentum space.

Model / system. The system is the two-leg Creutz ladder incorporating long-range hopping, modeled as a spinless two-band free-fermion system. The Hamiltonian is analyzed under quantum quenches.

Key observables. Dynamical free energy f(t), Loschmidt amplitude Z(t), and the location of Yang-Lee-Fisher (YLF) zeros.

Important parameters / regimes. Long-range hopping exponent (ν), finite range (D), and quench parameters ($ heta, ilde{ heta}$).

Abstract

In this work, we investigate quantum quenches in the two-leg Creutz model with long-range hopping, where the hopping amplitudes decay with distance as a power law characterized by an exponent $ν$ and have a finite range $D$. We first obtain the exact solution of a generic two-band model in momentum space. This allows us to compute the Loschmidt amplitude and, consequently, the dynamical free energy $f(\texttt{t})$ of the two-band model. We also show how to determine the Yang-Lee Fisher (YLF) zeros by solving a nonlinear equation. We demonstrate that the two-leg Creutz model in momentum space is a special case of the generic two-band model. Using these results, we identify the non-analyticities in the dynamical free energy $f(\texttt{t})$ at critical times $\texttt{t}_{c}$. We find that the number of nontrivial critical times $N_{s}$ depends on both $ν$ and $D$. In particular, we show that for small $ν$ and large $D$ the critical times become increasingly dense, leading, in the appropriate regime, to non-analyticities at an increasingly dense set of times---similar to what was observed by Xavier and Hoyos [Phys. Rev. B, $\textbf{108}$, 214303 (2023)] in the Su-Schrieffer-Heeger (SSH) model with long-range hopping terms.

Summary. This paper introduces a novel hybrid quantum-classical algorithm to overcome the major hurdle of nonlinearity in quantum CFD simulations. By using tensor-based block encodings, the method avoids the exponential resource scaling associated with previous state-based approaches. This breakthrough demonstrates a scalable pathway for achieving quantum advantage in simulating complex, high-Reynolds number fluid dynamics.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on fluid dynamics, the core methodology—using tensor networks and quantum programming to manage complex, non-linear operators—is highly relevant to simulating open quantum systems or many-body dynamics where nonlinear couplings are present.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge addressed is the efficient and scalable implementation of nonlinear terms within Quantum Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) simulations.

Main result. The proposed hybrid quantum-classical tensor network algorithm significantly reduces the computational cost and memory footprint compared to fully classical tensor network solvers, establishing a scalable path toward quantum advantage in CFD.

Method. The authors propose a hybrid approach combining variational time-stepping with quantum tensor programming. Nonlinearity is handled by replacing state-based encodings with tensor-based block encodings, which are compiled into unitary gates.

Model / system. The study models Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD), specifically simulating turbulent flow fields governed by nonlinear Partial Differential Equations (PDEs). Examples include the Temporally Developing Jet (TDJ) and Taylor Green Vortex (TGV).

Key observables. Success probability of the nonlinear operation ($\alpha_{succ}$), computational cost, memory footprint, and the field representation error ($\epsilon_{\ell_2}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Reynolds numbers ($Re \in \{10, 20, ..., 1000\}$), bond dimension ($\chi$), and time step ($\Delta t$).

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes that the nonlinear terms can be effectively encoded into diagonal block encodings, which stabilizes the success probability against exponential decay with system size.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the hybrid algorithm workflow, the quantum circuit decomposition for a single time step (distinguishing linear and nonlinear operators), and comparisons of error scaling and computational costs across various Reynolds numbers.

Paper structure. The paper details the theoretical framework for handling nonlinear PDEs using a hybrid quantum-classical loop. It then applies this framework to simulate specific turbulent flow examples (TDJ, TGV), providing rigorous error and cost analyses.

Abstract

Nonlinear terms present a fundamental challenge for quantum computational fluid dynamics, as their implementation on inherently linear quantum hardware typically requires resource-intensive workarounds that limit scalability to large-scale simulations. We present a hybrid quantum-classical tensor network algorithm that addresses this bottleneck by combining variational time-stepping with quantum tensor programming to efficiently compile operators and time-dependent fields into quantum circuits. Within a probabilistic framework, we replace prior state-based nonlinear implementations with tensor-based block encodings, stabilizing success probabilities that otherwise decay exponentially with system size. Benchmarking on turbulent flow fields demonstrates that the algorithm maintains high success probabilities and moderate measurement overhead across increasing Reynolds numbers and grid resolutions. Abstract

We investigate the emergence of roton instability in self-bound quantum droplets interacting via a finite-range soft-core potential modeled by a Heaviside step interaction. Abstract

We investigate frustrated magnetism of a corner-connected square plaquette Heisenberg model with exchange interactions along the edges $(J_1)$, along inter-plaquette links $(J_2)$, and along square diagonals $(J_3)$. Using Luttinger--Tisza (LT) minimization of the Fourier interaction matrix $\mathcal J(\mathbf q)$ together with large-scale Monte Carlo (MC) simulations, we obtain a classical low-temperature magnetic phase diagram in the normalized plane $(J_1/|J_2|, J_3/|J_2|)$. The two methods play complementary roles: the LT analysis provides the zero-temperature candidate ordering wave vectors, while the MC simulations elucidate the resulting ordering tendencies under the hard-spin constraint at low but finite temperatures. Three regimes emerge at low temperatures, an antiferromagnetic phase (AF), a ferromagnetic phase (FM) and a degenerate spin state with \textit{up-up-down-down (uudd)} ordering (DS). For the frustrated degenerate spinstate, LT exhibits line-like minima along $q_x=\pm q_y$ in the $hk$-plane, revealing a highly degenerate spin configuration which violates the hard-spin constraint. The MC results uncover a state with quasi-two-dimensional ordering. The DS regime is intrinsically multi-$\mathbf{q}$: the ordered texture assembles itself from symmetry-related modes on the lines, producing a ``\textit{uudd}'' spin arrangement comprising of distinctive strong antiferromagnetic correlations on diagonals, ferromagnetic correlations on corner links, and highly suppressed correlations on the edges. The field-temperature phase diagram for a representative parameter point in the DS regime, determined by MC simulations, yields field induced distinct regions of negatively and positively correlated layers. These two regions are separated by a curve corresponding to negligible inter-layer correlations.
