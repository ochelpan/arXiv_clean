Summary. This paper introduces 'weak irreducibility' as a spectral criterion to differentiate true thermodynamic phase coexistence from pseudo-transitions in finite systems. This provides a deep spectral understanding of how finite-size effects round off phase transitions.

Why it may be interesting. The spectral analysis provides a powerful, non-perturbative tool to characterize the nature of phase transitions, linking spectral properties directly to thermodynamic phase boundaries, which is relevant for understanding quantum critical points and phase separation in many-body systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To establish a rigorous, operator-level criterion to distinguish genuine thermodynamic phase coexistence from mere avoided crossings (pseudo-transitions) in finite-size systems.

Main result. Genuine phase coexistence emerges as the singular limit where inter-sector connectivity vanishes, leading to a reducible thermodynamic manifold, while pseudo-transitions retain finite connectivity.

Method. The analysis uses the transfer-matrix formalism, decomposing the operator into sector imbalance and inter-sector connectivity components to analyze the spectral structure.

Model / system. The study applies to statistical mechanics systems undergoing first-order phase transitions, analyzed using restricted transfer operators for finite transverse width (L).

Key observables. Sector imbalance, spectral gap (connectivity), and the weak irreducibility of the transfer operator.

Paper structure. The paper develops the effective two-sector transfer-operator reduction, introducing imbalance and connectivity coordinates, and then applies this framework to distinguish between pseudo-transitions (finite connectivity) and true coexistence (vanishing connectivity).

Classification of Non-Hermitian Flat Bands

Summary. This paper introduces a comprehensive, projector-based classification system for non-Hermitian flat bands, distinguishing four classes based on the mathematical regularity of the system's biorthogonal projector. The core finding is that the breakdown of projector continuity leads to topological invariants mismatch ($C_R eq C_L$), which is directly correlated with an anomalous enhancement in resonant cross-orbital energy transfer.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a rigorous mathematical framework linking topological invariants (Chern numbers) to measurable transport phenomena (cross-orbital transfer) in non-Hermitian systems, which is highly relevant for understanding open quantum systems and engineered materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper establishes a projector-based classification scheme for non-Hermitian (NH) flat bands, extending known concepts from Hermitian systems.

Main result. The classification identifies four classes (NH-A, NH-C, NH-D, NH-E) based on the regularity of the biorthogonal projector, showing that topological mismatch ($C_R eq C_L$) in the NH-E class leads to anomalous resonant cross-orbital transfer.

Method. The methodology involves analyzing the mathematical regularity (analyticity, continuity, singularity) of the biorthogonal projector $P_{bi}(k)$ across the Brillouin zone and relating this regularity to topological invariants (Chern numbers) and transport properties.

Model / system. The study focuses on electronic systems exhibiting flat bands described by non-Hermitian Hamiltonians ($H eq H^\dagger$). The analysis compares the behavior of these bands across different mathematical regimes defined by the projector's properties.

Key observables. Right Chern number ($C_R$), Left Chern number ($C_L$), Biorthogonal Chern number ($C_{bi}$), and the cross-orbital transfer intensity $T_{B o A}(k)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The classification depends critically on the regularity of the biorthogonal projector $P_{bi}(k)$ at the band touching points.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is primarily restricted to rank-one flat bands, though generalizations to higher-rank manifolds are mentioned as future work.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the comparison between Hermitian and non-Hermitian classifications, showing band structures, projector magnitudes, and the scaling of maximum cross-orbital transfer intensity ($T_{ ext{max}}$) versus damping rate ($\eta$) for different classes.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the need for a projector-based classification for NH flat bands. It then details the four classes (NH-A to NH-E) based on projector regularity, linking each class to specific topological invariants and measurable physical responses like cross-orbital transfer scaling.

Abstract

Exact flat bands provide a versatile setting for correlated and topological phenomena, yet their properties are controlled not only by their dispersion but also by the structure of their Bloch projectors. Here, we establish a projector-based classification of non-Hermitian flat bands. In contrast to Hermitian flat bands, non-Hermiticity introduces a biorthogonal projector whose left-right pairing permits a distinct norm-pole singularity. We identify four classes: analytic NH-A, continuous but nonanalytic NH-C, bounded but discontinuous NH-D, and pole-singular NH-E. We show that continuity of the biorthogonal projector preserves the locking of the right, left, and biorthogonal Chern numbers, $C_R=C_L=C_{\rm bi}$, thereby realizing non-Hermitian critical topological flat bands. Once continuity is lost, this locking can break down; in particular, a mismatch $C_R\neq C_L$ can occur in the NH-E class. Finally, we show that this Chern number mismatch ensures to produce an anomalous enhancement of resonant cross-orbital transfer. Our results establish biorthogonal projector regularity as the organizing principle linking compact localized states, topology, and driven response in non-Hermitian flat bands.

Abstract

We study the equilibrium phase behavior of a fluid confined in a slit made from two patterned walls. Shearing the walls frustrates the fluid, due to a competition between capillary condensation and interface delocalization, forcing the formation of bridging phases with different pinning properties. This leads to an unusually rich phase diagram, displaying first-order and continuous phase transitions, depending sensitively on the slit width and shear. Generalized Kelvin equations determine the phase boundaries, while the bridging phases are characterized by large correlation lengths, predictions for which are tested using a microscopic density functional model.

Summary. This work proposes a current-driven mechanism to create magnetic hopfions, which are topologically knotted spin textures, starting from initial magnetic torons. By simulating the dynamics using the LLG equation, the authors show that the annihilation of an emergent monopole-antimonopole pair, induced by an electric current, converts the toron into a hopfion. This establishes a controllable, current-mediated route for generating complex topological states in chiral magnets.

Why it may be interesting. The study connects macroscopic current-driven dynamics (STT) to the creation of highly non-trivial topological excitations (Hopfions), providing a pathway to study knot theory in condensed matter systems, which is relevant to understanding complex magnetic memory or spintronic devices.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is to establish a controlled, deterministic mechanism for generating magnetic hopfions, which possess complex knotted topology, from simpler magnetic textures like torons.

Main result. The study demonstrates that applying an electric current drives the annihilation of an emergent monopole-antimonopole pair within a toron, successfully converting it into one or more hopfions.

Method. The dynamics are simulated by solving the Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert (LLG) equation, incorporating spin-transfer torque (STT) driven by an external current.

Model / system. The system is a chiral magnet modeled by a Hamiltonian including exchange, Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction (DMI), and Zeeman coupling. The dynamics are governed by the LLG equation under the influence of STT.

Key observables. Hopf number (H), which quantifies the knot topology; toron length (L); and the time evolution of the topological invariant.

Important parameters / regimes. The initial toron length (L) dictates the number of hopfions; the sign of the applied current controls the sign of the Hopf invariant; and the threshold current is sensitive to material parameters.

Assumptions / limitations. The mechanism relies on the competition between toron length shortening and radial expansion, and the initial state is assumed to be metastable.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the hopfion topology and toron structure, show the time evolution of toron dynamics under current for varying initial lengths, and plot the time evolution of the Hopf number to distinguish between trivial annihilation and hopfion generation.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theoretical framework using the LLG equation, simulates the evolution of torons under current, analyzes the dependence of the final topological state (Hopfion vs. trivial) on initial length and current strength, and concludes by mapping out an experimentally accessible creation pathway.

Abstract

Recent advances in three-dimensional magnetization imaging techniques have opened new avenues for exploring topological spin textures beyond domain walls and skyrmions. Among them, magnetic hopfions are particularly promising, as their knotted topology is expected to give rise to unconventional dynamics and responses; however, their controlled creation remains challenging. Here we propose a simple mechanism for generating hopfions from magnetic torons, three-dimensional textures hosting an emergent monopole-antimonopole pair. Using Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert simulations, we show that an electric current drives the annihilation of this pair, converting a toron into a hopfion. The initial toron length determines the number of generated hopfions, while the current direction selects the sign of the Hopf invariant. We further find that the threshold current depends sensitively on material parameters, indicating a close connection to skyrmion dynamics. Our results establish an experimentally accessible route to hopfion creation and reveal a pathway from monopole defects to knotted topological textures.

Summary. This paper investigates the magnetocaloric effect in the quasi-1D antiferromagnet BaCo2V2O8 by combining angle-resolved measurements with advanced tensor-network simulations. The research distinguishes between the field associated with the Ising critical point and a higher-field transverse response. It concludes that the MCE can be engineered by tailoring anisotropic Zeeman couplings, opening a route for magnetic cooling in quantum magnets.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a detailed study of magnetic phase transitions and thermodynamic responses in a highly anisotropic quantum magnet, which is relevant for understanding magnetic field control over quantum ground states.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To determine whether the anomalous magnetocaloric effect (MCE) in the quasi-1D antiferromagnet BaCo2V2O8 is governed by the intrinsic Ising critical field or by transverse spin fluctuations when the applied magnetic field is rotated.

Main result. The dominant MCE response persists away from the intrinsic Ising critical region, suggesting that magnetic cooling can be achieved by engineering anisotropic Zeeman-coupling configurations in quantum magnets.

Method. The study combines angle-resolved MCE measurements with tensor-network calculations (DMRG) on an effective XXZ spin chain model.

Model / system. The system is the quasi-one-dimensional Ising-like antiferromagnet BaCo2V2O8, modeled by an effective spin-1/2 XXZ chain Hamiltonian incorporating site-dependent Zeeman couplings due to the material's geometry and anisotropic g-tensor.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the crystal structure and geometry for different field directions, showing experimental MCE dips and angle-dependent magnetization/susceptibility data.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by establishing the physical model and experimental measurements, then using theoretical calculations (DMRG) to analyze the field dependence of various magnetic order parameters and thermodynamic quantities, culminating in a comparison between the theoretical and experimental findings regarding the MCE source.

Abstract

We investigate the transverse-field thermodynamics of the quasi-one-dimensional Ising-like antiferromagnet BaCo$_2$V$_2$O$_8$, whose tilted screw-chain geometry and anisotropic Landé $g$ tensor generate spatially modulated Zeeman couplings. Angle-resolved magnetocaloric-effect (MCE) measurements reveal a high-field temperature minimum near the transverse-field Ising critical field for $H\parallel[110]$ that persists and shifts only weakly upon field rotation. Tensor-network calculations show that the rotation-induced staggered transverse field rapidly lowers the Ising critical field and that the magnetic Grüneisen ratio changes sign near the high-field temperature minimum, consistent with experiment. Our results establish that a dominant MCE response can persist away from the Ising critical region, suggesting a route to magnetic cooling by tailoring anisotropic Zeeman-coupling configurations in quantum magnets.

Summary. This paper presents an exact autoregressive sampling algorithm for planar Ising spin glasses by creatively adapting the Kac--Ward theory. It overcomes the mathematical hurdles posed by boundary fields in conditional probability calculations using an auxiliary spin construction. The resulting method generates perfectly uncorrelated samples, offering a crucial, exact benchmark for validating approximate machine learning samplers in disordered systems.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical statistical mechanics, the development of an exact, non-Markovian sampling technique that provides a rigorous benchmark for neural samplers is highly relevant to developing robust generative models for complex physical systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is achieving exact sampling from the Boltzmann distribution of spin glasses, as standard MCMC methods suffer from critical slowing down, and modern neural samplers are only approximate.

Main result. The authors developed an exact autoregressive sampling algorithm that generates strictly i.i.d. samples with exact normalized likelihoods, providing a rigorous baseline for benchmarking machine learning samplers.

Method. The method adapts the Kac--Ward theory by introducing a planarity-preserving auxiliary spin construction to map the conditional partition functions, which are otherwise corrupted by boundary fields, into an extended zero-field Ising model solvable by a determinant formula.

Model / system. The system studied is the planar Ising spin glass, governed by the Hamiltonian E(s) = -sum J_ij s_i s_j. The analysis is applied to specific cases like the triangular AFM Ising model.

Key observables. Autocorrelation function (expected to be delta-correlated, $\delta_{t,0}$), Entropy (S), Magnetization (|m|), and Specific Heat (c_v).

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature (T), system size (N), and the coupling constants J_ij.

Assumptions / limitations. The method relies critically on the planarity of the underlying graph structure and the successful construction of the auxiliary spin to maintain solvability.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the sampling process using auxiliary spins, compare autocorrelation functions (Metropolis vs. exact sampler), and show temperature dependence of observables like entropy and specific heat.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the difficulty of exact sampling, introduces the Kac--Ward theory framework, details the novel auxiliary spin construction to handle boundary fields, derives the conditional probabilities via determinant ratios, and finally validates the method by calculating observables like entropy and comparing autocorrelation functions.

Abstract

Exact sampling from the Boltzmann distribution of spin glasses remains an outstanding challenge: Markov chain Monte Carlo methods suffer from critical slowing down and metastable trapping, while modern neural autoregressive samplers such as variational autoregressive networks are approximate and, in the absence of exact reference samples, cannot be rigorously benchmarked. Here we present an exact autoregressive sampling algorithm for planar Ising spin glasses based on the Kac--Ward theory. Under the chain-rule factorization, sequentially fixing spins induces boundary-localized external fields, which destroy the zero-field structure required for exact evaluation. By encoding these fields with a planarity-preserving auxiliary spin construction, the conditional partition functions are mapped to an extended zero-field Ising model and exactly evaluated using the Kac--Ward determinant formula. The method generates strictly independent and identically distributed samples with exact normalized likelihoods at a computational cost of $\mathcal{O}(N^{5/2})$ for $N$ spins, thereby providing an exact baseline for benchmarking neural autoregressive samplers.

Abstract

The design of disordered point patterns with desirable properties is an exciting and ongoing research endeavor, with applications ranging from materials to computer science. Our work paves the way for optimal sampling and coating design on curved manifolds, with many applications across physics, materials and computer science.

Abstract

Summary. This work rigorously benchmarks the GW approximation against exact diagonalization for the extended Hubbard model. It finds that the approximation fails to correctly predict the Mott gap, quantifying the necessary vertex correction ($\Gamma_{ ext{eff}}$) which varies significantly across the phase diagram. The results establish quantitative criteria for assessing the reliability of GW in strongly correlated electronic systems.

Why it may be interesting. The detailed analysis of correlation effects, gap opening, and the failure of simple approximations like GW provides crucial theoretical diagnostics for understanding strongly correlated quantum materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to quantify the reliability and error of the GW approximation for quasiparticle predictions in correlated materials, specifically addressing the role of vertex corrections.

Main result. The effective vertex correction ($\Gamma_{ ext{eff}}$) changes character across the phase diagram: it is $<1$ in the weak-coupling regime but grows large in the Mott regime, indicating that standard GW fails to capture the full gap opening.

Method. The authors benchmark GW theory against numerically exact results obtained via exact diagonalization (ED) of the extended Hubbard model on finite rings.

Model / system. The study uses the half-filled extended Hubbard model, described by a Hamiltonian including on-site ($U$) and nearest-neighbor ($V$) interactions, studied on finite rings.

Key observables. Effective vertex ($\Gamma_{ ext{eff}}$), quasiparticle gap ($\Delta$), and the comparison between ED and GW calculated gaps.

Important parameters / regimes. Interaction strengths ($U, V$), finite size ($N=6, 10$), and the crossover point $U^*$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis uses the static limit (COHSEX) for the self-energy and focuses on the spin-integrated density-channel functional.

Figures summary. Figures compare the exact ED gap versus the GW gap, show the dependence of $\Gamma_{ ext{eff}}$ on $U$, and illustrate how non-local interactions split the accidental crossover point.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the failure of GW in different regimes, first establishing the ED benchmark, then diagnosing the failure via $\Gamma_{ ext{eff}}$, and finally showing how non-local interactions complicate the picture.

Abstract

The $GW$ approximation is the standard tool for quasiparticle predictions in materials, yet its regime of validity in correlated systems remains poorly quantified, because \textit{ab initio} vertex corrections are computationally prohibitive. Using exact diagonalization of the half-filled extended Hubbard model on finite rings as a numerically exact reference, we construct the corresponding model-space $GW$ theory on the identical Hilbert space and quantify its error as a function of local ($U$) and non-local ($V$) interaction strength. We find that the vertex correction changes character across the phase diagram: in the weak-coupling regime the effective vertex $Γ_{\rm eff} < 1$, reflecting the suppression of RPA charge fluctuations by exact short-range correlations, whereas in the Mott regime $Γ_{\rm eff}$ grows monotonically (to $\sim 3$ for $N=6$, reflecting the local vertex required to open the Hubbard gap. Vertex corrections in the electron-hole (polarizability) channel are shown to \emph{worsen} the gap error, indicating that the Mott gap resides in the self-energy channel. For $V=0$, static $COHSEX$ is accidentally exact at a single crossover point $U^* \approx 3.5\,t$; finite $V$, through non-local Fock exchange, splits this point into a double-crossover window that collapses toward weak coupling. These results yield quantitative diagnostics for the reliability of $GW$ in correlated materials.

Summary. This theoretical paper analyzes Bell-CHSH correlations for a massless Majorana field confined to a double cone using advanced techniques from modular localization theory. By constructing the Bell functional using the field's one-particle scalar product, the authors demonstrate that the violation of the classical bound approaches the Tsirelson limit ($2\sqrt{2}$) as the localization profile becomes increasingly narrow. This links fundamental QFT structures to quantum information bounds.

Why it may be interesting. The connection between fundamental concepts in quantum field theory (modular localization, chiral fields) and quantum information theory (Bell inequalities) provides a novel physical realization for testing quantum non-locality.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the violation of Bell-CHSH inequalities using correlations derived from a chiral massless Majorana field localized in a double cone geometry.

Main result. Near-maximal Bell violation, approaching the Tsirelson bound of $2\sqrt{2}$, is achieved using smooth, rapidly decreasing Gaussian profiles in the modular momentum space.

Method. The analysis employs modular localization theory, deriving the one-particle scalar product in modular momentum space, and reducing the Bell test to a functional of this product.

Model / system. The system is a chiral massless Majorana field confined to a double cone region in 1+1 dimensions. The mathematical framework heavily utilizes concepts from modular operator theory (Tomita-Takesaki operator) applied to this field.

Important parameters / regimes. The Gaussian width parameter $\sigma$, which is taken to zero ($\sigma o 0^+$) to approach the maximal violation.

Assumptions / limitations. The construction requires the modular conjugation to be antiunitary with respect to the weighted scalar product, and the analysis is restricted to the one-particle level.

Abstract

We investigate Bell-CHSH correlations for a chiral massless Majorana field localized in a double cone. The conformal modular flow of the associated light-ray interval is converted into translations by a rapidity-like coordinate, and the one-particle scalar product is derived in modular momentum space. Particular attention is paid to the fact that this scalar product carries a Fermi-Dirac weight. Requiring the modular conjugation to be antiunitary with respect to that weighted scalar product fixes the Fourier-space realization of the modular objects. The resulting Tomita-Takesaki operator and its adjoint generate Alice and twisted-dual Bob directions for which all four crossed CAR orthogonality relations follow automatically from modular localization. The Bell problem then reduces to a simple one-function functional. For a Gaussian modular-momentum profile $h_σ(k)=\exp[-k^2/(2σ^2)]$ the Bell parameter is larger than the classical bound for a broad range of widths and approaches the Tsirelson value analytically, \[ \lim_{σ\to 0^+}{\it B}(σ)=2\sqrt2 \] Thus near-maximal Bell violation is obtained from an explicit sequence of smooth rapidly decreasing profiles concentrated around zero modular momentum, corresponding to the modular spectral value $λ=1$.
