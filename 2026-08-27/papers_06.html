Visualizing flat-band spatial renormalization in rhombohedral graphene superlattices

Authors: Peng-Cheng Pan, Shihao Zhang, Yang Zhang, Ji Huang, Ling-Hui Tong, Chen-Chen Xu, Yuan Tian, Li Zhang, Lijie Zhang, Yuanyuan Hu, Wen-Xiao Wang, Zhihui Qin, Long-Jing Yin

Summary. This work uses STM to visualize how the moiré potential in graphene/hBN superlattices causes a periodic, site-dependent renormalization of flat electronic bands. The key finding is that this spatial modulation only exists above a critical moiré period ($\sim 10 ext{ nm}$), establishing a direct, real-space link between the moiré potential and the emergence of topological order like the FQAHE.

Why it may be interesting. The direct visualization of how a geometric potential (the moiré pattern) dictates the emergence of topological order in a correlated system provides a concrete, experimentally verifiable mechanism for realizing exotic quantum states.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to clarify the microscopic role of the moiré potential in driving emergent correlated phases, such as the Fractional Quantum Anomalous Hall Effect (FQAHE), in rhombohedral graphene superlattices.

Main result. The authors visualize that the moiré potential causes a spatially periodic renormalization of flat bands, and this modulation vanishes below a critical moiré period ($\sim 10 ext{ nm}$), which coincides with the threshold for FQAHE.

Method. The research combines Scanning Tunneling Microscopy/Spectroscopy (STM/STS) to image real-space electronic structure with theoretical modeling to attribute the modulation to atomic corrugation-induced charge redistribution.

Model / system. The system studied is rhombohedral graphene (RG) superlattices formed with hexagonal boron nitride (hBN) substrates (e.g., R4G/hBN, R5G/hBN). The physics involves flat-band physics, correlated electron behavior, and the formation of topological states.

Key observables. Spatially resolved energy shifts of flat bands ($ ext{d}I/ ext{d}V$ spectra), the magnitude of the band splitting gap ($\Delta$), and the persistence of moiré-scale modulation.

Important parameters / regimes. Moiré period ($\lambda_m$), which shows a critical threshold around $10 ext{ nm}$; the filling level (full vs. partial); and the twist angle ($ heta$).

Assumptions / limitations. The mechanism driving the modulation is theoretically attributed to atomic corrugation inducing a spatially varying local chemical potential.

Figures summary. Figures show STM topography and spatially resolved $ ext{d}I/ ext{d}V$ spectra demonstrating site-dependent energy shifts. They illustrate the disappearance of this modulation when the moiré period is too small, and map the spatial reshaping of correlated states.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the problem by noting the importance of moiré-driven phases. It then uses STM/STS to observe the spatial renormalization of flat bands, demonstrating the dependence on the moiré period. Finally, it links this observed periodicity threshold to the theoretical requirement for topological order.

Abstract

Rhombohedral graphene/hBN moiré superlattices exhibit flat-band-driven emergent phases, including superconductivity and the fractional quantum anomalous Hall effect (FQAHE), yet the microscopic role of the moiré potential remains unclear. Here, using scanning tunneling microscopy, we visualize moiré-modulated spatial renormalization of flat bands in rhombohedral pentalayer and tetralayer graphene/hBN superlattices. We observe spatially hierarchical filling, manifested as periodic energy shifts of the flat bands at the moiré scale, leading to spatial reshaping of correlated states in the interacting regime. Remarkably, this modulation vanishes below a ~10 nm moiré period--the same threshold below which the FQAHE is absent. Theoretical modeling attributes this mechanism to atomic-corrugation-induced charge redistribution. Our work provides real-space visualization of moiré-engineered flat-band reconstruction, resolving a key link between moiré periodic potential and emergent topological order.

Summary. This research demonstrates how structured light, specifically optical vortices, can be used to write precise, controllable topological spin textures in chiral magnetic films. By controlling the beam's orbital and spin angular momentum, researchers can deterministically encode these quantum optical properties into the magnetic topology, opening a new pathway for quantum-controlled spintronic devices.

Why it may be interesting. This work bridges quantum optics (structured light, OAM) directly into condensed matter magnetism, providing a mechanism for non-invasive, quantum-number-controlled manipulation of magnetic topology, which is highly relevant for advanced spintronics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the deterministic and local writing of in-plane topological spin textures (like bimerons and antibimerons) within chiral ferromagnetic thin films using structured light, specifically optical vortices (OVs).

Main result. Structured light provides a versatile route for topology-selective writing, where the optical quantum numbers (OAM, SAM) can be directly encoded into the topological charge (Q) of the resulting magnetic texture.

Method. The study employs micromagnetic simulations to model the time evolution of magnetization dynamics driven by pulsed Laguerre-Gaussian optical vortex fields.

Model / system. The system is an ultrathin ferromagnetic film exhibiting chiral magnetic properties, governed by a free energy functional and simulated using the Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert equation. The driving force is a time-dependent magnetic field derived from the structured light.

Key observables. Topological charge (Q), magnetic texture morphology (bimerons/antibimerons), and the dependence of these on optical quantum numbers (m, p, polarization s).

Important parameters / regimes. Material parameters include $M_s$, $A$, $D$, and $K_x$; simulation parameters involve beam waist ($W$), pulse duration ($t_{ ext{pulse}}$), and OAM ($m$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the paraxial approximation for the OV field and assumes the dynamics are governed by the LLG equation under the influence of the structured light pulse.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the schematic of OV incidence on a chiral film, the spatial profiles of LG modes, and time-evolution maps showing the nucleation of single or clustered topological textures corresponding to specific input optical parameters.

Paper structure. The paper builds from defining the physical model and Hamiltonian, detailing the simulation protocol (three-stage writing process), presenting results for specific polarization/OAM combinations, and concluding with a general framework for topology-selective writing.

Abstract

Structured light offers a powerful means of controlling light-matter interactions through multiple tunable optical degrees of freedom. Using micromagnetic simulations, we investigate the nucleation of asymmetric bimerons and antibimerons by pulsed Laguerre-Gaussian optical vortices in chiral ferromagnetic thin films with $C_{nv}$ and $D_{2d}$ symmetries, respectively. For optical vortices with orbital angular momentum (OAM) $|m|=1$, circularly polarized beams deterministically nucleate a single bimeron or antibimeron via the interplay of spin angular momentum, OAM, and magnetic chirality, whereas linearly polarized beams produce textures whose topological charge directly follows the OAM ($Q=m$). Optical vortices with OAM $|m|>1$ nucleate clusters and other configurations composed of multiple spin textures, whose morphology and topological charge depend sensitively on the optical quantum numbers and pulse parameters. These findings reveal a route to topology-selective writing through the encoding of optical quantum numbers into distinct in-plane topological magnetic states.

Summary. This work introduces a novel, scattering-driven mechanism for the layer Hall effect in magnons, bypassing the need for topological band structures. By analyzing a nanowire on a magnetic film, the authors show that non-reciprocal dipolar scattering under a temperature gradient generates measurable transverse thermal Hall currents. This provides a robust, field-tunable pathway for realizing this effect in conventional magnetic materials.

Why it may be interesting. While the primary focus is condensed matter magnetism, the development of a scattering-theory approach to engineer directional transport (thermal/spin currents) based on symmetry breaking (non-reciprocity) is highly relevant to open quantum systems and engineered quantum transport phenomena.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To establish a fundamentally distinct mechanism for the layer Hall effect (LHE) in bosonic excitations, specifically magnons, that is driven by non-reciprocal dipolar scattering rather than geometric Berry curvature effects.

Main result. The study predicts a magnon LHE where a longitudinal temperature gradient drives opposite transverse thermal Hall currents, yielding a sizable Hall angle ($\sim 6^\circ$) in conventional magnetic heterostructures.

Method. The authors employ a microscopic scattering theory approach, deriving hybridization coefficients and analyzing the resulting magnon current densities under a temperature gradient.

Model / system. The system modeled is a hybrid chiral architecture: a magnetic nanowire placed atop a magnetic film, interacting via an interlayer dipolar coupling. The analysis focuses on the magnon excitations in this coupled system.

Key observables. Transverse thermal Hall currents ($ ilde{J}_z, J_z$), the Hall angle ($\sim 6^\circ$), and the dependence of currents on the external magnetic field orientation ($ heta$).

Important parameters / regimes. The non-reciprocal nature of the dipolar coupling ($g_{\kappa}(kz)$), the applied longitudinal temperature gradient ($ abla T$), and the external magnetic field strength ($\mu_0H_0$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the ballistic regime (micrometer length scales) and attributes the effect to the non-reciprocity of the dipolar coupling, thereby bypassing the need for topological engineering.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the physical setup: a magnetic nanowire on a magnetic film subjected to a longitudinal temperature gradient, with magnetization tilted by angles $ heta$ and $ ilde{ heta}$.

Paper structure. The paper builds from identifying the limitations of Berry curvature mechanisms to developing a scattering-theory framework based on non-reciprocal dipolar coupling, calculating the resulting transverse currents, and demonstrating the tunability and magnitude of the effect.

Abstract

The layer Hall effect has been exclusively attributed to layer-locked Berry curvature, posing a fundamental barrier to its realization in conventional magnets. Here we report a fundamentally distinct layer Hall effect for bosonic excitations, i.e., magnons, which originates solely from non-reciprocal dipolar scattering at heterointerfaces, thereby decoupling the phenomenon from geometric-phase mechanisms. Using a microscopic scattering theory, we demonstrate that a longitudinal temperature gradient drives opposite transverse thermal Hall currents in a nanowire atop a magnetic film, with the direction fully reconfigurable by the applied magnetic field. The effect yields a significant Hall angle of $\sim 6^{\circ}$ in conventional magnetic heterostructures, eliminating the need for topological engineering. Our findings establish a scattering-driven paradigm for layer Hall effect, extendable to ferrons and polar phonons, and predict a Hall response that is readily detectable in conventional magnetic heterostructures.

Intervalley Magnetotrions Tunable by Electric and Magnetic Fields in Buckled Two-Dimensional Materials

Summary. This work theoretically models intervalley magnetotrions in 2D materials like silicene and stanene under combined electric and magnetic fields. It establishes that the system exhibits an exact separation of center-of-mass and internal dynamics when carrier masses are equal. This allows for independent, field-tunable control over both the collective motion (Landau levels) and the binding energy of the trion complex.

Why it may be interesting. The independent tunability of collective (c.m.) and internal degrees of freedom by external fields provides a powerful platform for engineering quantum states, relevant to quantum optics and open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops a theoretical framework to study intervalley magnetotrions in buckled two-dimensional materials under external electric and magnetic fields.

Main result. It shows that the center-of-mass and internal motions of these trions can separate exactly under the condition of equal electron and hole effective masses. Furthermore, the trion binding energy is found to increase monotonically with both applied fields.

Method. The analysis uses the effective-mass approximation to formulate the three-particle Schrödinger equation, solving the center-of-mass motion via a harmonic oscillator Hamiltonian and the internal motion using the hyperspherical harmonics method.

Model / system. The system involves trions (three-particle bound states) in Xene monolayers (silicene, germanene, stanene), modeled with the Rytova–Keldysh interaction potential, and subjected to perpendicular electric and magnetic fields.

Key observables. Trion binding energy, quantized Landau surface energies, and the separation of center-of-mass and internal motion.

Important parameters / regimes. High-magnetic-field regime ($B \gtrsim 15 ext{ T}$), electric field ($E_\perp$), and the condition of equal effective masses ($m_{e1} = m_{e2} = m_h$).

Assumptions / limitations. The effective-mass approximation is employed, and the analysis is restricted to the high-magnetic-field regime.

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically illustrates equal-mass intervalley trions in Xene monolayers, detailing the conditions for mass equality and showing the associated band structures.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the theoretical framework for the three-particle system, then analyzes the center-of-mass motion leading to tunable Landau states, and finally investigates the internal dynamics using advanced quantum mechanical methods.

Abstract

We develop a theoretical framework for intervalley magnetotrions in buckled two-dimensional materials, including silicene, germanene, and stanene, subjected to perpendicular electric and magnetic fields. Within the effective-mass approximation, the three-particle Schrödinger equation is formulated with the Rytova--Keldysh interaction potential and analyzed in the high-magnetic-field regime. We demonstrate that intervalley trions with equal electron and hole effective masses constitute an exceptional case for which the center-of-mass and internal motions separate exactly. The center-of-mass motion is governed by a two-dimensional harmonic-oscillator Hamiltonian, leading to quantized Landau states whose energies form electrically tunable Landau surfaces controlled by the magnetic field and the electric-field dependence of the carrier effective masses. The internal motion is investigated by solving the three-body Schrödinger equation within the framework of the hyperspherical harmonics method. Numerical calculations reveal that the trion binding energy increases monotonically with both magnetic and electric fields owing to the combined effects of magnetic confinement and electric-field-induced enhancement of the effective masses. The strongest binding is obtained for silicene, followed by stanene and germanene. The present work provides a unified description of both the collective center-of-mass motion and the internal dynamics of magnetotrions in Xene monolayers, demonstrating that both degrees of freedom can be independently manipulated by external electric and magnetic fields.

Abstract

Summary. This paper analyzes a nonlocal reaction-diffusion model for population dynamics to study pattern formation influenced by the specific mathematical form of interaction kernels. It finds that the kernel choice dictates the system's resilience, demonstrating a 'Turing-before-Tipping' mechanism for some kernels but not others. This highlights how nonlocal interactions fundamentally alter the stability and pattern-forming capabilities of complex ecological systems.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on ecology, the mathematical framework of pattern formation, bifurcation analysis, and resilience mechanisms in non-equilibrium systems shares theoretical overlap with pattern formation in quantum many-body systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how the choice of nonlocal interaction kernel affects pattern formation and persistence in a population model incorporating nonlocal competition and facilitation.

Main result. The choice of kernel creates a dichotomy in pattern behavior; Gaussian-type kernels show robust 'Turing-before-Tipping' resilience, while hat-type kernels do not exhibit this behavior regarding the death rate.

Method. The study employs center manifold analysis and numerical continuation methods to analyze pattern-forming bifurcations and pattern stability far from onset.

Model / system. It uses a single-species reaction-diffusion model for population density, incorporating nonlocal facilitation and competition terms governed by various kernels (Gaussian, exponential, hat, etc.).

Key observables. Pattern formation/bifurcation, pattern persistence past the tipping point, and early warning signs for population extinction.

Important parameters / regimes. The death rate ($d$) and the relative spatial scale of competition ($\sigma_c$) to facilitation ($\sigma_f$) are key bifurcation parameters.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis fixes the facilitation scale ($\sigma_f = 1$), and the study compares kernels based on whether their Fourier transforms are non-negative (Gaussian-type) or take on negative values (Hat-type).

Figures summary. The notes reference figures illustrating example kernels in physical and Fourier space, and plots showing uniform solutions versus the death rate ($d$) for tipping (fold) and non-tipping (transcritical) bifurcations.

Paper structure. The paper analyzes the model via center manifold expansion to determine pattern-forming bifurcations, followed by numerical continuation methods to study pattern stability and the 'Turing-before-Tipping' phenomenon.

Abstract

Spatial patterns, such as those in dryland vegetation models, have historically been studied in systems of reaction-diffusion systems with pattern onset via a Turing bifurcation from a spatially uniform state. More recently, spatial patterns have been considered in models that incorporate spatially extended interactions via nonlocal interaction kernels. It remains largely underexplored if and how the choice of nonlocal interaction kernel contributes to differences in pattern formation and persistence, particularly in models that contain competition and facilitation. Here, we investigate spatial patterns in a reaction-diffusion model for a single species that includes nonlocal competition and facilitation processes; Gaussian, exponential, algebraic, hat, and smooth hat kernels are considered as specific examples. Via a center manifold analysis, and using the relative spatial scale of competition to facilitation and the death rate as bifurcation parameters, we identify that the choice of kernel has impacts on the pattern forming bifurcation. Bifurcations using the Gaussian, exponential, and algebraic kernels largely follow expectations of Turing patterns, but patterns in the hat and smooth hat kernels can form even when the scale of competition is less than that of facilitation. The dynamics of patterns far from onset are investigated via numerical continuation methods. The model produces the so-called "Turing-before-Tipping" phenomenon demonstrating that the arrangement into spatial patterns is an effective resilience mechanism against harsh conditions. Again, there is a kernel-dependent dichotomy in pattern behavior. Early warning signs for population extinction are observed with the Gaussian, exponential, and algebraic kernels, but not under the hat or smooth hat cases.

Abstract

Here, we introduce a materials platform for spectrally programmable spin transport based on a van der Waals heterostructure combining the antiferromagnetic semiconductor NiPS$_3$ with WSe$_2$. Summary. This work explores quantum transport in SSH chains containing topological domain walls, showing that their interaction creates a self-similar hierarchy of minibands. Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how the interaction of topological domain-wall states in Su-Schrieffer-Heeger (SSH) chains generates a self-similar, hierarchical structure in quantum transport properties.

Main result. The hybridization of domain-wall localized states leads to minibands whose structure can be recursively constructed, revealing a self-similar hierarchy of spectral gaps and resonances.

Model / system. The system is based on SSH chains containing multiple topological domain walls. The model is analyzed by treating the domain-wall states as emergent degrees of freedom that define an effective, recursively built lattice structure.

Abstract

We investigate the transport properties of Su-Schrieffer-Heeger (SSH) chains containing multiple topological domain walls and show that their interaction generates a hierarchy of emergent spectral structures. Each domain wall contributes a localized state inside the SSH gap, and the hybridization of these states produces minibands whose signatures are directly reflected in the transmission spectra. By combining domain-wall lattices with different domain-wall separations, we construct effective SSH structures within the miniband subspace. The resulting transmission spectra reproduce the characteristic features of conventional SSH chains, including gap formation, finite-size resonances, and the correspondence between transmission spectra and band structure. The construction can be applied recursively, generating successive generations of effective SSH structures. As a consequence, effective SSH spectra repeatedly emerge within progressively narrower energy intervals, producing a self-similar hierarchy of minibands and spectral gaps. To understand the origin of this hierarchy, we develop an effective renormalized description based on successive decimation. The effective parameters exhibit a hierarchy of interlaced singularities whose number increases at each iteration. These singularities partition the energy axis into progressively finer intervals and provide a natural interpretation of the repeated fragmentation of the spectrum. Our results show that topological domain-wall states can act as emergent degrees of freedom from which multiscale transport channels, effective couplings, and hierarchical spectral structures may be engineered. More generally, the framework introduced here establishes a connection between recursive topological constructions, effective Hamiltonians, and the emergence of self-similar spectra in one-dimensional systems.
