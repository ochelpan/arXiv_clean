Graphix: A software framework for Measurement-Based Quantum Computation

Authors: Mateo Uldemolins, Pranav Nair, Emlyn Graham, Shinichi Sunami, Thierry Martinez, Maxime Garnier

Summary. This paper introduces Graphix, a novel Python software framework designed to standardize and simplify the development of Measurement-Based Quantum Computation (MBQC) protocols. It provides a unified environment that handles the complex interplay between graph states, single-qubit measurements, and Pauli corrections. Graphix aims to accelerate research by offering a comprehensive, extensible infrastructure for the entire MBQC workflow.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on computation, the rigorous mathematical treatment of flow reconstruction (Causal, Generalised, Pauli) and the standardization of patterns provides deep insights into the underlying algebraic structure of quantum operations, which is relevant to understanding constraints in complex quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The field lacks dedicated, unified software tools to bridge the theoretical foundations of Measurement-Based Quantum Computation (MBQC) with practical, extensible research workflows.

Main result. The authors introduce Graphix, a comprehensive Python framework that provides a unified, modular environment for developing, simulating, and analyzing MBQC protocols.

Method. The framework is built around the measurement calculus, allowing for the native representation of arbitrary MBQC computations, including state preparation, entanglement, measurements, and Pauli corrections.

Model / system. The model is Measurement-Based Quantum Computation (MBQC), which utilizes a graph state as a resource and performs computation via single-qubit measurements followed by classical feed-forward Pauli corrections.

Key observables. The induced partial order ($\prec$) on measured nodes, and the resulting Pauli corrections ($X_s, Z_s$) applied to the resource state.

Important parameters / regimes. The structure of the open graph $\Gamma$, the measurement angles ($\lambda, \alpha$), and the specific Pauli correction strategies ($x, z$).

Assumptions / limitations. The core assumption for computation is that the transitive closure of the correction set must define a strict partial order; standardization procedures assume the input pattern is well-formed.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates an MBQC computation (Hadamard gate) showing the open graph, measurement maps, and resulting Pauli corrections (red arrows).

Paper structure. The paper reviews essential MBQC concepts (Section 2) using Graphix examples, followed by a detailed description of the library architecture and advanced formalisms like flow reconstruction (Sections 3 and beyond).

Abstract

Measurement-based quantum computing (MBQC) is a powerful model for quantum computation, but dedicated software tools that bridge its theoretical foundations with practical research workflows remain limited. We present Graphix, a software framework for MBQC written in Python that provides a unified environment for developing, integrating, and exploring measurement-based protocols and algorithms. Graphix establishes a modular, extensible, and user-friendly architecture for the compilation and simulation of quantum computations in the MBQC model with abstractions closely aligned with the theoretical formulations of MBQC. Through examples drawn from recent research on MBQC, we demonstrate Graphix's ability to reproduce and extend previous results. Graphix thus provides a software infrastructure for MBQC, supporting education and research while enabling collaborative development and accelerating the transition toward practical implementations.

Nearly Optimal Amplitude Estimation at any Depth

Authors: Jona Erle, Bálint Koczor

Summary. This paper introduces Windowed Least Squares Amplitude Estimation (WLSAE), a quantum algorithm designed to estimate an unknown amplitude with high precision. It achieves a near-optimal query-depth scaling that is remarkably uniform across all possible input amplitudes. This robustness makes it a strong candidate for practical quantum applications like overlap certification in the early fault-tolerant regime.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a significant algorithmic improvement for fundamental quantum estimation tasks, directly impacting the feasibility of quantum simulation and verification protocols that rely on accurate amplitude measurements.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of achieving near-optimal query-depth tradeoffs in Amplitude Estimation (AE) algorithms, particularly ensuring the quantum speedup persists uniformly across the entire range of possible amplitudes.

Main result. A novel algorithm, WLSAE, achieves the nearly optimal query-depth tradeoff $M^2N \in \widetilde{O}(\epsilon^{-2})$ uniformly for all amplitudes $a \in [0, 1]$, overcoming limitations of prior methods at the boundaries.

Method. The method develops Windowed Least Squares Amplitude Estimation (WLSAE) by sampling Grover circuit depths from a carefully constructed, admissible probability window $p$. The error is bounded using concentration inequalities like Bernstein's inequality.

Model / system. The core task is estimating the amplitude $a = \langle\psi| P |\psi angle$ (or the angle $\lambda = \arcsin(\sqrt{a})$) associated with a unitary evolution $U$ and a projector $P$. The measurement relies on outcomes from circuits involving the Grover operator $G$.

Key observables. The primary observable is the estimated amplitude $\hat{a}$ or angle $\hat{ heta}$, with the goal of bounding the additive error $|\hat{ heta} - \lambda| \le \epsilon$.

Important parameters / regimes. The key parameters are the target error $\epsilon$, the maximum circuit depth $M$, and the number of shots $N$. The performance is analyzed across the full range of amplitudes $a \in [0, 1]$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the existence of an admissible probability window $p$ and relies on bounding the deviation of a loss function derived from quantum measurements using concentration inequalities.

Figures summary. Figure 1 compares various window functions (Gaussian, Uniform, etc.) for WLSAE, showing performance relative to the Cramér–Rao bound. Figure 2 confirms the algorithm's amplitude-uniformity by showing constant error bounds across $\lambda \in [0, \pi/2]$ for a fixed query budget.

Paper structure. The paper first defines the problem and the WLSAE framework. It then derives rigorous bounds on the estimation error by analyzing the variance of the loss function across the depth window. Finally, it applies these bounds to establish the near-optimal query-depth scaling and demonstrates the method's practical applicability.

Abstract

We develop a class of amplitude estimation algorithms with tunable circuit depth $M$ and circuit repetitions $N$, requiring neither ancilla qubits nor controlled Grover operations. For additive error $ε$ in the Grover angle, they attain the nearly optimal query-depth tradeoff $M^2N\in\widetilde{O}(ε^{-2})$ uniformly over $λ\in[0,π/2]$, spanning the full range from classical sampling at $M=1$ to the Heisenberg limit at $M=Θ(ε^{-1})$. While prior depth-tunable work establishes comparable angle-accuracy guarantees only away from the boundaries $λ\to0$ or $π/2$ or at discrete depth tradeoffs, our guarantee extends to both boundaries, so the quantum speedup persists there rather than degrading to classical sampling. Numerical experiments confirm the predicted uniform angle accuracy and show low overhead in practice, making them strong candidates for practical amplitude estimation in the early fault-tolerant regime, with applications such as overlap certification, trial-state verification, and Monte Carlo methods.

Summary. This work analyzes how the spectral properties of random matrices change when the underlying correlations decay slowly (power-law). Using the Matrix-Dyson-Equation framework, the authors derive exact results for the bulk density deformation and pinpoint critical exponents that govern the transition between different spectral regimes.

Why it may be interesting. While the focus is on random matrices, the mathematical techniques used to analyze spectral transitions and critical exponents are highly relevant to understanding phase transitions in complex quantum many-body systems, such as those modeled by disordered Hamiltonians or interacting quantum gases.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the spectral behavior (bulk and edge) of Wigner-type random matrices whose underlying covariance structure exhibits long-range temporal correlations.

Main result. The bulk spectral density deforms from the semicircle law in a predictable manner, and the analysis establishes critical exponents ($\gamma_c=1/2$) governing the divergence of bulk moments for power-law correlations.

Method. The authors employ advanced techniques involving the Matrix-Dyson-Equation (MDE) framework, moment calculations, and the derivation of a scalar self-consistent equation for the Stieltjes transform.

Model / system. The model consists of symmetric $N imes N$ Wigner-type random matrices constructed from row-independent stationary Gaussian sequences, allowing for controlled correlation decay via AR(1) or power-law structures.

Key observables. Bulk spectral density $\varrho(x)$, the largest eigenvalue $\lambda_{\max}(S)$, and the fourth moment $\mu_4( ho)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The correlation decay parameter $ ho$ (for AR(1)) and the power-law exponent $\gamma$ (for $d au \sim t^{-\gamma}$), with critical points at $\gamma_c=1/2$ and $\gamma=1$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on verifying hypotheses (A)-(E) of the MDE framework, though limitations exist for strong correlations ($ ho \gtrsim 0.7$).

Figures summary. Figures compare empirical spectral histograms against theoretical predictions (e.g., for $ ho=0.5, 0.8$) and show the collapse of top eigenvalues onto a singular-value cascade for the noiseless Volterra ensemble.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first analyzing the AR(1) case, verifying MDE hypotheses and deriving the self-consistent equation. It then extends the analysis to power-law correlations, rigorously determining critical exponents ($\gamma_c$) and resolving the apparent conflict between smooth crossovers and discrete phase transitions.

Abstract

We study long-range correlated Wigner-type matrices built from row-independent stationary Gaussian sequences. For exponentially decaying (AR(1)) correlations, the bulk spectral density deforms from the semicircle law via an explicit combinatorial "hub" mechanism, yet we verify the flatness and decay hypotheses of the matrix-Dyson-equation framework (MDE), with numerical evidence supporting Tracy-Widom edge universality for every fixed $ρ<1$ of the exponential decay correlations; the degenerate limit $ρ\to1^-$ reduces to a symmetrized Volterra operator, connecting to the singular-value cascade identified in a companion BBP analysis. For power-law correlations $dt\sim t^{-γ}$, we identify $γ_c=1/2$ as the critical point for divergence of the bulk fourth-moment, while $γ=1$ marks the breakdown of the flatness condition governing the MDE edge analysis. We prove the fourth-moment transition exactly and find numerically that the self-consistent edge varies smoothly across $γ=1$, with no evidence of a kink or discontinuity.

Entropy Production Bounds the Accuracy of Computation in Markov Networks

Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: correlated / nonlocal dissipation 2/5 · driven-dissipative phase transition 2/5 · non-equilibrium universality 2/5 · Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5 · methods for driven-dissipative 1/5 · quantum measurements 1/5

Summary. This work derives a fundamental thermodynamic bound showing that the computational error in stochastic networks is limited by the entropy production rate and the system's memory capacity. By applying this to models of biochemical processes, the authors establish a quantitative framework for understanding the energetic costs associated with biological computation. It demonstrates that high accuracy requires either significant energy dissipation or the retention of long-lived memory states.

Why it may be interesting. The connection between non-equilibrium thermodynamics (entropy production) and information processing capacity provides a fundamental, quantitative constraint on how efficiently biological systems can compute.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper establishes a universal thermodynamic bound on the accuracy of computation performed by stochastic networks.

Main result. The lag error in computation is bounded by the product of the entropy production rate and a memory time ($\epsilon^2_{ ext{lag}} \le au_m \sigma$).

Method. The analysis decomposes the total error into representation and lag components, deriving the bound using entropy production rates and relative entropy decay.

Model / system. The study uses reversible continuous-time Markov networks to model biological information processing, including systems like ion channels, Lac repressors, and NPQ.

Key observables. Computational error ($\epsilon^2$), Entropy Production Rate ($\sigma$), and Memory Time ($ au_m$).

Important parameters / regimes. The bound is derived for the near-equilibrium limit, highlighting the trade-off between dissipation and memory storage.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on decomposing the error and using quadratic approximations for entropy production in some models, though the bound itself is shown to be general.

Figures summary. Figures compare the integrated lag error against the derived thermodynamic bound across various random input protocols for multiple biochemical models.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the error decomposition, derives the core thermodynamic bound ($\epsilon^2_{ ext{lag}} \le au_m \sigma$), and then applies this framework to test it across several distinct, complex biological Markov models.

Abstract

Biological and artificial networks compute by transforming time-dependent inputs into functional outputs. Because the internal state of a stochastic network relaxes on finite timescales, its output generally lags behind a changing environment, producing computational errors. We show that for reversible continuous-time Markov networks the error admits a universal thermodynamic bound. Decomposing the total error into representation and lag contributions, we derive an inequality relating the lag error to the entropy production rate and a memory time equal to the integrated equilibrium autocorrelation of the output observable. The bound implies that accurate dynamical computation requires either substantial dissipation or long-lived memory encoded in slowly relaxing modes. We demonstrate these principles in artificial Markov networks and in models of biochemical information processing. Our results establish a thermodynamic limit on information processing in stochastic networks and provide a quantitative framework for understanding the energetic costs of biological computation.

Summary. This theoretical paper analyzes the training loss of diffusion models, showing that the irreducible excess loss is mathematically equivalent to the integrated Fisher-Rao metric along the diffusion path. By deriving this from the Schrödinger bridge principle, it provides a deep geometric understanding of how the training objective captures the intrinsic information geometry of the data manifold.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on ML, the rigorous connection between information geometry (Fisher metric) and the fundamental path-space structure of stochastic processes is highly relevant to understanding the geometry of quantum state estimation and open quantum system dynamics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper quantifies the 'irreducible excess' in the training loss of Denoising Score Matching (DSM) by relating it to the intrinsic geometry of the diffusion process.

Main result. This excess loss is exactly equal to the integrated trace of the Fisher–Rao metric of the conditional endpoint family, providing a geometric interpretation of the training objective floor.

Method. The derivation utilizes the Schrödinger bridge variational principle and employs information-theoretic tools, specifically relating the loss to the rate of mutual information loss.

Model / system. The model is a general diffusion process used in diffusion models, connecting a clean data distribution ($P_0$) to a noisy state ($P_T$) via a corruption kernel.

Key observables. Loss Floor, Fisher–Rao metric ($g(x, t)$), Mutual Information $I(y; x_t)$, and the excess action $\Delta S$.

Important parameters / regimes. Diffusion time $T$, noise schedule parameters ($\sigma_t, \alpha_t$), and the Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR).

Assumptions / limitations. Mild regularity assumptions are required for the general corruption kernel, and the analysis contrasts second-order training geometry with third-order sampling errors.

Figures summary. Figures demonstrate the agreement between the calculated floor ($ rac{1}{2} R ext{MMSE} d\lambda$) and the entropy change ($\Delta h$) for Gaussian mixtures, and confirm the scaling of the floor divergence rate with the data rank $k$.

Paper structure. The paper builds from the general variational principle (Schrödinger bridge) to derive the geometric characterization of the loss floor via the Fisher metric, and then applies this to specific Gaussian cases to derive closed forms and test identities.

Abstract

Denoising score matching trains diffusion models by regressing onto a conditional score, although generation ultimately requires the marginal score. The two objectives share the same population minimizer, but the conditional target remains random at fixed noisy state and introduces an irreducible excess in the training loss. We isolate this excess and show that, for a general corruption kernel under mild regularity assumptions, it is exactly the trace of the Fisher--Rao metric of the conditional endpoint family, integrated along the diffusion trajectory. This gives an exact conditional-variance decomposition of the denoising objective and identifies the information geometry observed in diffusion latent spaces as an intrinsic component of the training loss. We derive the result from a Schr"odinger bridge variational principle, in which the ideal objective arises as excess path-space relative entropy. For corruption diffusions, the Fisher term is proportional to the rate at which the noisy state loses mutual information about the clean data, separating the loss floor into an information flow determined by the data and a weight determined by the corruption schedule and objective. In the Gaussian case, this yields a closed form for the floor, recovers reparametrization invariance of the continuous-time objective, and relates its high-SNR divergence to the information dimension of the data. Finally, we show that raw losses obtained with different noise ranges or weightings need not rank models consistently because they contain different additive floors, and contrast the second-order geometry seen by training with the third-order conditional statistics entering numerical sampling error.

Summary. This paper simulates the random sequential adsorption of DLA clusters on a lattice to study jamming states. It systematically analyzes how varying the diversity of cluster shapes—from fixed to highly polydisperse—affects the final jamming density and its fluctuations. The findings reveal that increased shape diversity leads to denser packing, providing quantitative scaling laws for these non-equilibrium deposition processes.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical statistical mechanics, the study of jamming transitions and the role of quenched disorder (shape diversity) in determining macroscopic properties has analogies in understanding complex energy landscapes or disordered quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how the diversity of cluster shapes affects the jamming properties when using Diffusion-Limited Aggregation (DLA) clusters for irreversible adsorption.

Main result. The jamming density decreases with cluster size as a power-law, and increasing shape polydispersity promotes denser packing. Furthermore, the fluctuation scaling of the jamming density differs significantly depending on whether the cluster shape pool is fixed or refreshed.

Method. The research employs large-scale Monte Carlo simulations to model the Random Sequential Adsorption (RSA) process using DLA clusters on a square lattice.

Model / system. The system is the irreversible adsorption of DLA clusters on a square lattice, analyzed through several model variants: fixed-shape, refreshing-shape, and polydisperse variants controlled by a pool size $S$.

Key observables. Jamming density ($p_j$), the scaling exponent $\alpha$ in $p_j(k) - p_\infty \sim k^{-\alpha}$, and the fluctuation of the jamming density ($\sigma(L)$).

Important parameters / regimes. Cluster size ($k$), system size ($L$), and the degree of shape diversity (controlled by $S$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the assumption that differences between model variants diminish at very large cluster sizes ($k o \infty$) due to the statistical self-similarity of DLA clusters.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate representative DLA clusters showing shape diversity and show the scaling behavior of the jamming density and its fluctuations ($\sigma(L)$) for different model variants.

Paper structure. The paper systematically compares results from multiple model variants (fixed vs. refreshing vs. polydisperse) to quantify the role of shape diversity on the jamming density and its fluctuations, establishing scaling laws for both observables.

Abstract

Motivated by the ubiquity of ramified fractal deposits in nature and engineered systems, we investigate the irreversible adsorption of diffusion-limited aggregation (DLA) clusters on a square lattice. We study the role of cluster shape diversity on jamming properties of the system by systematically controlling the number of distinct shapes used across and within realizations, encompassing both monodisperse and polydisperse model variants. Our large-scale simulations over a broad range of cluster sizes $2\leqslant k\leqslant4096$ show that the jamming density decreases with cluster size as a power-law $p_j(k)-p_j^\infty\sim k^{-α}$. Both $α$ and $p_j^\infty$ are found to depend on the degree of shape diversity, with $α$ ranging from $0.374(2)$ to $0.417(1)$. It is observed that increasing shape polydispersity promotes denser packing. Importantly, the fluctuations of the jamming density exhibit distinct scaling behavior: $σ(L)\sim1/L$ for a fixed pool of cluster shape(s), but remain $L$-independent when the pool of shape(s) is refreshed across different realizations. Furthermore, our results demonstrate that the differences between the model variants systematically diminish with increasing $k$ and are expected to vanish as $k\to\infty$ due to the statistical self-similarity of the DLA clusters.

Summary. This paper develops a perturbative theory for quantum tunneling in the thin-barrier limit, moving beyond the standard WKB approximation. It derives that the time-dependent tunneling probability depends critically on system symmetry, predicting quadratic growth for resonant cases and linear growth otherwise. This work provides essential theoretical tools for modeling energy leakage and coherent dynamics in engineered quantum barriers.

Why it may be interesting. The detailed analysis of time-dependent tunneling rates, especially the distinction between $t^2$ and linear growth, provides crucial theoretical benchmarks for understanding coherent energy transfer in engineered quantum systems like optical lattices or mesoscopic structures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper analyzes quantum tunneling when the tunneling action is small, specifically in the thin-barrier limit, contrasting with standard WKB methods.

Main result. The tunneling probability exhibits distinct time dependencies depending on the system's symmetry: quadratic growth ($\propto t^2$) in resonant/symmetric cases and linear growth in non-resonant/asymmetric cases.

Method. The analysis employs a perturbative expansion in the barrier strength ($\alpha$) and utilizes time-dependent Schrödinger equation solutions, often involving Green's functions or eigenbasis expansions.

Model / system. The system models a quantum particle tunneling through a potential barrier, which is idealized as a delta function barrier ($\alpha^{-1}\delta(x)$) in the thin-barrier limit. The Hamiltonian is solved using time-dependent methods.

Key observables. Tunneling probability ($P_R(t)$) and the time-dependent tunneling wavefunction $\psi(t, x>a)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The barrier strength $\alpha$ (control parameter), and the energy mismatch $\Delta E(a)^0 = E_a^0 - E_s^0$ which dictates the early-time rate.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on perturbative approximations in $\alpha$ and uses specific limits (e.g., early-time approximations, quadratic order in $\alpha$) to simplify the complex time evolution.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the potential barriers (finite vs. delta function) and show the lowest energy wavefunctions for both zero and small non-zero barrier strengths.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theory by first establishing the thin-barrier limit equivalence, then solving the $\alpha=0$ case, and finally introducing the barrier strength perturbatively to derive the time-dependent tunneling probability in various regimes.

Abstract

The usual WKB analysis for quantum tunneling applies when the tunneling action is large, as it is for tall, wide potential barriers. In contrast we analyze tunneling when the action is small, as it is for tunneling across a tall, thin barrier. We develop a perturbative analysis where the control parameter is the inverse of the area under the potential barrier and apply our technique to several examples in $1+1$ dimensions. In resonant situations for bound particles we find that the tunneling probability grows with time as $\propto t^2$, while in non-resonant situations it grows linearly with time. We evaluate not only the tunneling probability but also the time-dependent tunneling wavefunction for a particle that escapes to infinity, {\it i.e.} from a quasi-bound state to the continuum.

Abstract

Our results demonstrate that multiple-scattering effects alone can generate emergent effective spin-orbit interactions and qualitatively modify the edge spectrum of disordered Kagome systems.

Abstract

Determining whether a mixed quantum state is uniquely determined among all states by its k-body marginals (k-UDA) is a fundamental problem in quantum system certification. We develop a range-based approach to this problem by analyzing the structure of the range of the global state. For three-qubit states, we show that states with GHZ-SLOCC-free ranges are 2-UDA at ranks one, three, and four. We derive a necessary and sufficient range criterion for rank-two 2-UDA states and reduce it to a finite quadratic-form test. To cover the remaining range configurations, we formulate an exact range-restricted semidefinite programming criterion and extend it to arbitrary finite-dimensional tripartite states. We also show that every three-qubit state of rank at least five is not 2-UDA, and further extend high-rank obstructions to multipartite systems. For a channel-based multipartite family, we characterize exactly when a state is $(n-1)$-UDA and show that lower-order marginals never suffice. Finally, we apply these results to the certification of genuine multipartite entanglement.

Abstract

The state frame potential is a standard diagnostic of how closely a quantum state ensemble approximates Haar randomness. In this work, we study the problem of estimating the state frame potential of order $t$ to within additive error $\varepsilon$ under three progressively weaker access models: (i) query access to a multi-state-preparation oracle, (ii) general sample access, and (iii) single-copy sample access. In the query model, we establish a near-optimal query complexity of $\widetildeΘ(\sqrt{t}/\varepsilon)$, yielding a quadratic improvement in the dependence on $t$ over the previous best result of Nakata, Takeuchi, Kliesch, and Darmawan (PRX Quantum 2025). In the general sample model, we establish the optimal sample complexity $Θ(t/\varepsilon^2)$. In the single-copy sample model, we present a store-and-estimate approach whose sample complexity depends on the Rényi entropy of the ensemble weights. As an application, we use the single-copy algorithm to assess the randomness of projected state ensembles, where the entropy term becomes the observational Rényi entropy associated with measuring one subsystem.
