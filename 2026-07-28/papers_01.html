Summary. This paper proves that the transmission probability for Klein tunneling through an asymmetric barrier is symmetric. However, using time-dependent simulations, the authors show this asymmetry is not in the transmitted current but rather in the generation of negative-energy states within the barrier region. Therefore, directional control in this regime must be sought by analyzing pair production rather than just particle flux.

Why it may be interesting. The finding that asymptotic transport is robust while internal dynamics are sensitive provides a nuanced view of how symmetry breaking can be 'hidden' from simple current measurements, which is relevant for understanding non-equilibrium quantum transport in mesoscopic systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates whether Klein tunneling through a spatially asymmetric potential barrier maintains left-right symmetry for Dirac fermions, contrasting with expectations of asymmetry.

Main result. Transmission probability remains symmetric ($T_L = T_R$), but the asymmetry manifests in the interior dynamics as a directionally dependent population of negative-energy states (pair creation).

Method. The analysis combines time-independent scattering matrix arguments with time-dependent Wigner-function simulations in phase space.

Model / system. The system is modeled by the relativistic Dirac equation describing quantum particle scattering off a one-dimensional, spatially asymmetric potential barrier $M(x)$.

Key observables. Transmission probability ($T$), Reflection probability ($R$), and the population of under-barrier negative-energy states.

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis is restricted to asymptotic leads carrying exactly one propagating channel per direction.

Assumptions / limitations. The stationary state analysis assumes current conservation, while the time-dependent part relies on Wigner function propagation in phase space.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the tunneling probability, reflection probability, and negative energy population for both left and right incidence across an asymmetric barrier, comparing sharp vs. smooth edges.

Paper structure. The paper first uses time-independent scattering theory to prove $T_L = T_R$. It then employs time-dependent Wigner-function simulations to locate the source of asymmetry in the interior negative-energy population, concluding that pair production, not current, carries the directional information.

Abstract

We prove that the transmission probability for the Klein tunneling through a spatially asymmetric barrier is the same for left and right incidence whenever each asymptotic lead carries a single propagating channel per direction. Time-dependent Wigner-function simulations confirm this and locate the missing directionality in the barrier's interior, where a sharp edge generates several times more under-barrier negative-energy population than a smooth one. Directional control in the Klein regime therefore resides in pair production rather than in the transmitted current.

Abstract

We study the non-reciprocal properties of the iron oxide $α$-Fe$_2$O$_3$ (hematite) in the canted easy-plane antiferromagnetic phase, specifically in the GHz to THz frequency range. First, using the the microscopic spin Hamiltonian, we obtain the correct classical ground state where the canting is induced by the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interactions (DMI). The magnon spectrum is simulated using linear spin wave theory. We then compute the polarizability and the sub-gap optical conductivities using linear response. We find that the conductivity tensor contains frequency peaks at the zero momentum magnon gaps of order $0.1~$meV which can be tuned by the DMI and on-site anisotropic spin interactions. Furthermore, we show that the canting-induced net magnetic moment $\mathbf{m}$ represents a measure for the effective time-reversal-symmetry breaking and non-reciprocity of the system: a finite $\mathbf{m}$ results in a non-zero Hall conductivity. Finally, we discuss the prospective application of hematite in non-reciprocal circulator design, by computing the non-reciprocal circulator transmission amplitude using the conductivities as input.

Abstract

Coherent errors in stabilizer codes are often correlated across qubits and QEC cycles. Having a general analytical treatment of such noise would thus be extremely valuable. We derive here the exact logical channel induced by repeated QEC cycles under correlated coherent $Z$ noise, and develop a broadly general cumulant-expansion framework that yields a tractable expression for the noise-averaged logical infidelity. Crucially, this expression is non-perturbative in the noise, and applies to arbitrary stabilizer codes and correlation structures. It reveals a feature with no analogue in standard stochastic Pauli error models: the induced channel depends on which stabilizer eigenspace is chosen as the codespace. Exploiting this, we introduce protected stabilizer eigenspace (PROSE) encoding, an error-suppression strategy that selects the optimal codespace. We show that this eigenspace can be efficiently identified in many relevant situations. Further, when combined with logical Pauli twirling, PROSE matches or outperforms standard error suppression techniques (dynamical decoupling, Pauli twirling of physical qubits). We also show that noise correlations, usually assumed to be harmful to QEC, can instead be a resource: with the right encoding, even positive correlations reduce the logical infidelity below the uncorrelated baseline. Our results offer a new, broadly applicable lens on correlated coherent noise in stabilizer codes.

Abstract

Abstract

Abstract

We study bath-induced thermalization in the many-body localized XXZ spin chain subjected to a quasiperiodic magnetic field. Our results suggest that the standard avalanche framework requires revision to account for the short-range correlations of quasiperiodic potentials.
