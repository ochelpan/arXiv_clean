Self-Healing Diffusion Monte Carlo applied to a simple fermionic model: A critical assessment of the method

Authors: Michel Caffarel, Manon Pinar, Anthony Scemama
Type: theory · Category: numerical methods · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01301v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: methods for driven-dissipative 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work provides a critical assessment of the Self-Healing Diffusion Monte Carlo (SHDMC) method when applied to fermionic systems. The authors demonstrate that the standard implementation fails to converge to the correct fixed-node energy due to systematic node deterioration. They propose and validate modifications, such as emphasizing the nodal region or using localized basis sets, which are necessary for achieving accurate results.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous analysis of convergence failure and the proposal of specific algorithmic fixes (like local basis sets) are highly relevant to developing robust numerical techniques for strongly correlated quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper critically assesses the Self-Healing Diffusion Monte Carlo (SHDMC) method for improving the nodal surface of fermionic wavefunctions.

Main result. Standard SHDMC fails for the tested fermionic model, causing the fixed-node energy to increase monotonically; however, modifying the nodal update criterion or using localized basis sets can partially restore convergence.

Method. The authors employ an iterative fixed-node calculation within the SHDMC framework, comparing standard criteria against modified local criteria and localized basis sets.

Model / system. The study uses a one-dimensional model with periodic boundary conditions, incorporating an inversion symmetry to mimic the nodal structure of fermionic wavefunctions.

Key observables. Fixed-node energy ($E_{FN}$), nodal convergence (node position $ heta_N$), and the stability index ($\eta$).

Important parameters / regimes. The degree of nodal constraint ($\epsilon$) and the choice of basis set (Fourier vs. localized).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that the nodal structure can be iteratively optimized using the SHDMC procedure, which is shown to be conditionally true.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the monotonic increase of $E_{FN}$ under standard SHDMC, and show that modified criteria or localized bases allow the node position to converge toward the correct physical value.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the failure of standard SHDMC, then proposing and testing a modification focusing on the nodal region, and finally demonstrating improved convergence using localized basis sets.

Abstract

We investigate the Self-Healing Diffusion Monte Carlo (SHDMC) method using a one-dimensional model with periodic boundary conditions. An inversion symmetry is introduced to mimic the antisymmetry property of fermionic wavefunctions, with the bosonic and fermionic sectors being modeled by the even and odd eigenstates, respectively. As in realistic fermionic systems, the nodal structure is only partially constrained by symmetry, making this model a non-trivial testbed for nodal optimization algorithms such as SHDMC. We show that the nodal evolution under SHDMC iterations can be cast into a dynamical system exhibiting both attractive and repulsive fixed points. In the standard formulation of SHDMC applied to this model, the fixed-node energy is found to increase upon iteration, and the node converges to a wrong value, indicating that SHDMC does not always converge to the correct solution. We further show that this problem is partially cured by modifying the nodal update criterion to give more importance to the nodal region. Achieving convergence in the general case very likely requires the use of a localized basis set, as is the case for this model.

Symmetry-Reduced Variational Quantum Simulation of the $U(5)\rightarrow SU(3)$ Quantum Phase Transition in the Interacting Boson Model

Summary. This work uses a symmetry-reduced Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) to study the quantum phase transition in the Interacting Boson Model. By analyzing finite-size scaling of order parameters, the authors demonstrate that the VQE approach can efficiently and accurately simulate this transition, confirming theoretical predictions for the critical point.

Why it may be interesting. The application of quantum simulation techniques (VQE) to model a well-established nuclear structure model (IBM) provides a concrete testbed for quantum algorithms in condensed matter/nuclear physics, particularly for studying phase transitions.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the quantum phase transition from the spherical $U(5)$ limit to the deformed $SU(3)$ limit within the Interacting Boson Model (IBM).

Main result. The symmetry-reduced Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) successfully simulates the QPT, reproducing structural observables and approaching the analytically predicted critical point $\xi_c = 8/17$.

Method. The study employs a symmetry-preserving, minimum-qubit VQE framework combined with finite-size scaling and thermodynamic-limit extrapolation techniques.

Model / system. The system is the Interacting Boson Model (IBM), which describes the low-lying collective states of even-even nuclei. The Hamiltonian is parameterized by control variables $\xi$ and $\chi$.

Key observables. Normalized d-boson occupation ($\rho_d$) used as the primary order parameter, ground-state energy ($E_0$), and quadrupole collectivity ($ ext{CQ}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Control parameters $\xi$ and $\chi$; the critical point $\xi_c = 8/17 \approx 0.470588$.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that symmetry reduction based on rotational invariance is valid for minimizing qubits while preserving the finite-size physics of the QPT.

Figures summary. Figures compare VQE results against exact diagonalization benchmarks, showing convergence of finite-size indicators toward the analytic critical point $\xi_c$.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the IBM Hamiltonian, develops a symmetry-reduced VQE workflow, benchmarks this method against exact diagonalization results, and analyzes the finite-size scaling of order parameters to extrapolate the true thermodynamic critical point.

Abstract

The spherical-to-deformed quantum phase transition of the Interacting Boson Model (IBM) is investigated using the variational quantum eigensolver (VQE). The $U(5)$--$SU(3)$ transitional Hamiltonian is studied with $χ=-\sqrt{7}/2$. We develop a symmetry-preserving, minimum-qubit VQE framework for collective nuclear models, achieving a substantial reduction in qubit requirements without compromising the finite-size quantum-phase-transition physics. The transition is characterized through the normalized $d$-boson occupation and ground-state energy derivatives. Finite-size results are found to approach the analytic critical point $ξ_c=8/17\simeq0.470588$, with an independent order-parameter extrapolation yielding $ξ_\infty=0.46986(61)$. The VQE reproduces ground-state energies and structural observables to numerical precision. These results demonstrate the potential of symmetry-reduced VQE for efficient quantum simulations of collective nuclear dynamics and quantum phase transitions.

Verifiable quantum advantage in extremely low depth

Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

Summary. This paper constructs a sampling problem that proves quantum advantage using quantum circuits with remarkably low depth ($\log \log \lambda$ or constant). It achieves this by adapting existing lattice-based proofs of quantumness, requiring only shallow circuit execution and sampling, which is a significant step toward practical quantum cryptography demonstrations.

Why it may be interesting. This work pushes the boundary of quantum complexity theory by demonstrating that quantum advantage can be shown using extremely shallow circuits, which is highly relevant for near-term quantum hardware feasibility.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To construct a sampling problem that is solvable by extremely shallow quantum circuits, computationally hard for classical algorithms based on lattice assumptions, and efficiently verifiable.

Main result. The authors provide constructions showing that quantum advantage can be demonstrated using circuits of depth $O(\log \log \lambda)$ or even constant depth ($QAC^0$), without requiring mid-circuit measurements.

Method. The work compiles existing lattice-based proofs of quantumness (like those from Arabadjieva et al.) into low-depth quantum circuits, framing the problem as a challenge-response protocol.

Model / system. The model involves quantum circuits operating on qubits, specifically analyzing the capabilities of shallow circuits defined by $QNC^0[\log \log]$ or $QAC^0$ gate sets. The underlying hardness relies on the Learning with Errors (LWE) problem.

Key observables. Acceptance probability of the quantum sampler, the depth of the required quantum circuit, and the computational advantage over classical samplers.

Important parameters / regimes. Circuit depth ($\log \log \lambda$ or constant), security parameter ($\lambda$), and the lattice knowledge assumption strength ($ ext{LK-}1/4$).

Assumptions / limitations. The construction relies on non-standard assumptions, including a strengthened variant of the adaptive-hardcore-bit property of LWE and the $ ext{LK-}1/4$ assumption.

Figures summary. The notes refer to a table comparing various quantum advantage protocols based on depth, measurements, and fan-out/fan-in, indicating the current work improves these metrics.

Paper structure. The paper builds upon previous work (BCMVV, AGGM) by focusing on reducing the circuit depth. It establishes the necessary quantum sampling problem and proves the existence of polynomial-size quantum circuits achieving the desired low depth while maintaining classical hardness.

Abstract

We give a sampling problem that is solvable by shallow quantum circuits, hard for polynomial-time classical algorithms under lattice-based assumptions, and efficiently verifiable by a classical computer. The quantum sampler admits two implementations: one uses log-logarithmic-depth quantum circuits with one- and two-qubit gates, i.e., $\mathsf{QNC}^0[\log\log]$ circuits, while the other uses constant-depth quantum circuits with unbounded fan-in gates, i.e., $\mathsf{QAC}^0$ circuits. Our construction can be seen as compiling the Learning with Errors (LWE)-based single-round proof of quantumness of Arabadjieva et al. (2025) to very low depth. The price paid for this compilation is the reliance on less standard, though well-motivated, assumptions: in addition to the lattice knowledge assumption used by Arabadjieva et al. (2025), we require a strengthened variant of the adaptive-hardcore-bit property of LWE, for which we provide supporting evidence. Unlike previous low-depth proofs of quantumness, the quantum computation here requires no mid-circuit measurements or feed-forward: it consists only of running a shallow circuit and sampling from its output distribution. This shows that shallow quantum circuits have sufficient structure to solve certain classically hard tasks whose solutions can be verified efficiently.

Summary. This paper investigates using hybrid quantum-classical neural networks to solve the CVRP, aiming to replace large, parameter-heavy classical modules with compact Quantum Neural Networks (QNNs). The authors successfully show that this replacement significantly reduces model size while maintaining competitive solution quality compared to classical baselines, suggesting a viable compression strategy for neural combinatorial optimization.

Model / system. The problem is modeled using an arc-based integer programming formulation for CVRP. The proposed model is a hybrid network where the classical encoder's feed-forward block is replaced by a variational QNN circuit utilizing rotations and CNOT gates.

Key observables. Objective value (route length), average relative deviation ($r_m$), and total evaluation time ($t$) when solving CVRP instances of size $n \in \{10, 20, 50, 100\}$.

Important parameters / regimes. The QNN uses a small configuration ($q=4$ qubits, $L_q=2$ layers), and performance is tested across varying instance sizes ($n$).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary limitation noted is that the performance gap between the hybrid model and the classical baseline grows for larger instances, and the comparison does not prove quantum advantage over classical heuristics.

Abstract

This work studies hybrid quantum-classical neural networks for learning routing heuristics. Specifically, this paper asks whether small quantum neural networks can replace parameter-heavy modules inside a competitive attention-based routing model while maintaining solution quality. For the capacitated vehicle routing problem, encoder feed-forward replacement emerges as the most promising design: it reduces the number of model parameters by 56.6% while keeping the hybrid model close to the classical neural baseline at small and medium instance sizes, although the gap grows for larger instances. This work also compares to classical routing algorithms, which remain highly competitive and often superior on the fixed Euclidean test sets. Our results therefore do not indicate quantum advantage or solver dominance, but identify encoder feed-forward replacement as a viable hybrid-module compression strategy for neural combinatorial optimization.

Summary. This theoretical paper analyzes the memory capacity of one-way quantum automata under symmetry constraints using advanced operator algebra. It develops a framework that quantifies memory by analyzing the rank of associated Hankel matrices and decomposes the system's dynamics into components related to symmetry. The key finding is that noncommutativity imposes a quantifiable, one-state penalty on the worst-case computational cost.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous connection drawn between algebraic structure (commutativity) and computational cost (state cost) provides a powerful, general framework for understanding resource limitations in quantum computation models.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to develop an operator-algebraic theory to characterize the 'behavioral memory' of one-way quantum automata when subjected to compact symmetry constraints.

Main result. It establishes a sharp dichotomy where noncommutativity in the invariant algebra raises the worst-case classical cost by exactly one state, and it relates memory capacity to structural properties like the commutant's center and multiplicity blocks.

Method. The authors employ operator-algebraic techniques, defining behavior via the Hilbert-Schmidt pairing and analyzing the rank of the acceptance Hankel matrix, $eta(A)$.

Model / system. The model is the one-way quantum automaton, whose evolution and memory are captured by an acceptance function $f_A(w)$ and analyzed through its associated operator algebra.

Key observables. Behavioral Memory ($eta(A)$), Operational Strict-Cutpoint Memory ($ ext{scPFA},_R(A, au)$), and the rank of the acceptance Hankel matrix.

Important parameters / regimes. The structure of the symmetry commutant, the dimension of the invariant algebra, and the state cost associated with commutativity vs. noncommutativity.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis focuses on the scope of strict-cutpoint language recognition by real PFAs, and the theory distinguishes between different comparison classes of quantum channels.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by defining the memory measure using Hankel matrices, deriving an exact formula for the linear memory dimension via isotypic decomposition, and then analyzing the structural cost implications based on the algebraic properties (commutativity vs. noncommutativity) of the symmetry-constrained invariant algebra.

Abstract

Under compact symmetry, observable behavior reduces to an invariant operator algebra, but its dimension is not yet classical memory: some coordinates are dynamically frozen, some invisible to threshold tests, and some already classical. We develop an operator-algebraic theory that separates these effects through three filters. For one automaton, behavior is the Hilbert--Schmidt pairing between prefix-reachable states and suffix-observable effects, whose rank equals the real Hankel rank without controllability or observability assumptions. Maximizing this invariant over a symmetry-constrained dynamical class gives a structural capacity controlled by the symmetry commutant: its center stores isotypic populations frozen by reversible dynamics, its traceless multiplicity blocks carry movable noncommutative coordinates, dissipation removes the unary spectral loss inside those blocks, and covariant mobility releases relative populations subject to component conservation. Operational realization then determines which surviving coordinates force probabilistic states. For a fixed nontrivial invariant readout, full mobility gives an exact dichotomy in worst-case state cost: a commutative invariant algebra costs exactly its dimension, whereas a noncommutative multiplicity block raises the unrestricted cost by exactly one state. Thus noncommutativity has a one-state worst-case classical price. The known four-letter quadratic-plus-one law at trivial symmetry is the fully mobile endpoint of this principle. Schur--Weyl duality further shows that different preserved symmetries on the same tensor-power Hilbert space can change the worst memory scale from polynomial to exponential, while fixed-weight modules give an exact Catalan law at half filling, with structural capacity equal to the Catalan count minus its central-sector correction.

Abstract

For mathematicians: In this first in a series of two papers, we give a mathematically rigorous classification of (sufficiently nice) $c=1$ vertex operator algebras (VOAs) and vertex operator superalgebras (VOSAs). We confirm the lore that any such VO(S)A is either a lattice VO(S)A $V_L$ associated to a rank-1 integral lattice $L$, or can be obtained as an orbifold thereof, i.e. a $G$-invariant subalgebra $V_L^G$ for some finite group $G$ of automorphisms. Summary. This paper provides a deep structural analysis of Quantum Secret Sharing codes featuring a 'helper' party. It characterizes these codes, particularly the 'blind helper' type, by deriving necessary and sufficient conditions based on stabilizer theory. The work proves that for single-qubit secrets, recovery is always achievable using LOCC from the helper, advancing the theory of quantum information security.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical characterization of information access structures (like helper codes) is fundamental to quantum cryptography and secure quantum computation protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper analyzes the general structure of Quantum Secret Sharing (QSS) codes, specifically focusing on the unique constraints imposed by the 'helper' access structure.

Main result. It fully characterizes blind helper stabilizer codes for single qubits, showing that recovery is always possible via one-way LOCC from the helper to the target party.

Method. The analysis employs stabilizer formalism, deriving necessary and sufficient conditions for helper codes, and comparing structural constraints derived from information theory and quantum error correction principles.

Model / system. The model is pure QSS schemes where a secret is encoded into a subspace of a multipartite system. The focus is on stabilizer codes, which are defined by commuting Pauli operators on the shared qubits.

Key observables. The possibility of recovery via Local Operations and Classical Communication (LOCC), and the structure of the logical operators supporting the secret.

Abstract

Helper quantum secret sharing is a form of secret sharing defined by its unique access structure. One special fixed party, called the helper, can work together with any other party to fully decode the secret. A blind helper is one who can provide this assistance while not holding any local information about the encoded secret. In this work, we analyze the general structure of QSS helper codes and present new code constructions. We fully characterize the structure of blind helper stabilizer codes and show that for the encoding of a single qubit, recovery is always possible using one-way local operations and classical communication (LOCC) from the helper to the targeted party. Furthermore, we demonstrate how such codes also allow for the helper to target larger subsets of parties by one-way LOCC, enabling them to be authorized to recover the secret. Finally, when each party only holds a qubit, we identify the general form of all helper codes (including non-stabilizer codes) and find that LOCC recovery is only possible in special cases.

Concentration of additive functionals of Stratonovich-type

Authors: Rick Bebon, Aljaž Godec, Angelika Rohde
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01581v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops a rigorous, non-asymptotic theory for the concentration of additive functionals of Stratonovich-type, which are crucial for analyzing thermodynamic quantities along fluctuating paths. By employing a novel symmetrization technique, the authors overcome mathematical hurdles associated with non-self-adjoint generators. The results provide powerful, explicit concentration bounds, particularly highlighting a distinct sub-Gaussian behavior under detailed balance.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical treatment of path functionals like work or entropy production is central to modern non-equilibrium statistical mechanics, providing tools for quantifying fluctuations beyond simple averages.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper tackles the challenging problem of proving non-asymptotic concentration inequalities for additive functionals of Stratonovich-type ($\mathcal{J}_t$) in the context of stochastic thermodynamics.

Main result. They successfully prove concentration inequalities for $\mathcal{J}_t$ for general geometrically ergodic diffusion processes, notably showing that under detailed balance, the concentration is sub-Gaussian with a variance independent of the spectral gap.

Method. The authors overcome the difficulty posed by the non-self-adjoint nature of the tilted generator by working with a symmetrized Dirichlet form and bounding the semigroup norm via the quadratic form.

Model / system. The analysis applies to general geometrically ergodic diffusion processes ($X_s$) describing fluctuating paths in non-equilibrium statistical mechanics. The comparison is drawn between Stratonovich-type functionals (currents) and classical Lebesgue-type functionals.

Key observables. Additive functionals $\mathcal{J}_t = rac{1}{t}\int_0^t U(X_s)\circ dX_s$, and their concentration bounds.

Important parameters / regimes. The time $t$, the function $U$, and the properties of the underlying diffusion process (e.g., geometric ergodicity, detailed balance).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis requires working with a symmetrized Dirichlet form and assumes the underlying process is a geometrically ergodic diffusion process.

Paper structure. The paper contrasts the mathematical difficulty of Stratonovich functionals versus Lebesgue functionals, develops a novel symmetrization technique for the generator, proves the general concentration inequality, and finally establishes the distinct sub-Gaussian behavior under detailed balance.

Abstract

Additive functionals $\overline{J}_t=\frac{1}{t}\int_0^tU(X_s)\circ dX_s$ of Stratonovich-type recently attracted much attention in the context of inference of thermodynamic properties of complex systems from observations $U$ of individual fluctuating paths $(X_s)_{0\le s\le t}$, whereby $X_0$ is initiated from some general measure. Concentration results on $\overline{J}_t$, albeit desirable, are virtually nonexistent. They turn out to be significantly more challenging to prove than for classical Lebesgue-type functionals $\overlineρ_t=\frac{1}{t}\int_0^t V(X_s)ds$ because the tilt deforms the full second-order structure of the Feynman-Kac generator instead of contributing an additive potential. This renders the generator generally non-self-adjoint even under detailed balance. We overcome this by working with a symmetrized Dirichlet form with a new effective potential that now couples the observable to the non-equilibrium character of the dynamics. We prove concentration inequalities for $\overline{J}_t$ for any bounded, sufficiently smooth vector-valued function $U$ of a general geometrically ergodic diffusion process $X_s$, including explicit sub-gamma and Bernstein-type inequalities, and we obtain explicit upper bounds on ${\rm Var}(\overline{J}_t)$. Strikingly, under detailed balance the concentration of $\overline{J}_t$ is distinctively sub-Gaussian at all times and all deviations, with a variance proxy fixed by the noise alone and independent of the spectral gap, which has no analog for $\overlineρ_t$.

DF-SQD: Deterministic Fields for Sampling-Based Quantum Diagonalization

Summary. This paper introduces DF-SQD, a novel hybrid quantum-classical algorithm designed to improve the sampling-based diagonalization of molecular Hamiltonians. By deriving deterministic auxiliary-field circuits, DF-SQD efficiently proposes quantum states, leading to superior energy accuracy and reduced resource requirements compared to prior methods on benchmark molecules like $ ext{N}_2$ and $ ext{Fe}_2 ext{S}_2$.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the resource scaling challenge in quantum chemistry simulations, providing a concrete, hybrid algorithmic improvement for accessing high-accuracy electronic structure data on near-term quantum hardware.

Model / system. The work applies to quantum chemistry calculations, specifically simulating the $ ext{N}_2$ molecule and the $ ext{Fe}_2 ext{S}_2$ cluster, utilizing active-space Hamiltonians and mapping them onto qubit systems.

Abstract

Sampling-based quantum diagonalization method exploits Quantum-centric supercomputing platforms to sample bitstrings for Hamiltonian projection on a quantum computer, and then classically diagonalize the Hamiltonian to estimate the eigenvalues and eigenvectors. In current quantum devices an algorithm is useful when shallow quantum circuits with error mitigation support can discover better results while having either a proof of convergence or some method to explain trust in experiment. In this paper, we introduce DF-SQD, a hybrid algorithm that derives deterministic auxiliary-field circuits from selected double-factorization leaves of the two-electron tensor. Abstract

We establish the general form of the evolution equation and study scattering and tunneling in real quantum mechanics. We prove an analogue of Stone's theorem for a real Kahler space and derive the corresponding general evolution equation, whose evolution operator is both orthogonal and symplectic. We formulate scattering theory in terms of real wave operators and the associated S-matrix. For the class of potentials considered, we show that the differential scattering cross section is equivalent to that obtained in standard complex quantum mechanics. We also show that the tunneling probability in real quantum mechanics is identical to that in complex quantum mechanics.

Abstract

Multimodal evaluations cannot say whether a vision-language model misread an image or misreasoned about it, because every existing method for separating the two places a second model in the loop. We introduce the render ceiling, a model-free reference for benchmarks built by rendering known objects: inverting the frozen cameras and re-solving cross-view correspondence recovers exactly the answer the images support. We prove the ceiling fails only through an enumerable set of projection coincidences and certify that set empty on 2,160 rendered crystal structures, so every point of a model's deficit belongs to the model. Across fourteen vision-language models, supplying exact geometry as text lifts every model yet closes under half the gap for thirteen, while a supervised vision model with no language component reads the same images at 0.8952, above every vision-language model. The instrument exposes extraction-stage fabrication that downstream accuracy would misattribute to reasoning, yields camera-placement rules for benchmark builders, and transfers to any benchmark with an invertible forward rendering.
