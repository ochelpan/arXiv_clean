Structure-Aware Placement and Routing of Multi-Controlled Toffoli on Bivariate Bicycle Code Architectures

Authors: Anik Basu Bhaumik, Suman Dutta, Siyi Wang, Anupam Chattopadhyay
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00852v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work tackles the resource overhead of implementing complex MCT gates on BB-code quantum computers. The authors introduce a novel, structure-aware placement strategy that groups interacting circuit components to minimize inter-module communication. This approach significantly reduces routing overhead and provides quantitative estimates of circuit failure probabilities for fault-tolerant quantum computation.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on quantum computation, the optimization techniques—specifically the graph-based placement heuristics and resource estimation—are relevant to designing complex physical implementations of quantum circuits.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the major optimization challenge of efficiently mapping high-level Multi-Controlled Toffoli (MCT) gates onto fault-tolerant quantum architectures based on Bivariate Bicycle (BB) codes.

Main result. A targeted placement strategy exploiting the binary-tree structure of MCT decompositions reduces inter-module instruction counts by up to 16.02%, and grid-based layouts offer significant routing improvements over linear ones.

Method. The authors propose a structure-aware placement strategy combined with direct |CCZ> state injection to map optimal MCT decompositions onto the BB-code architecture, minimizing inter-module routing overhead.

Model / system. The system is a fault-tolerant quantum computer utilizing Bivariate Bicycle (BB) codes, which are a type of qLDPC code. The workload involves implementing MCT gates, requiring the management of magic-state factories for non-Clifford operations.

Key observables. Inter-module instruction count (routing overhead), aggregate execution errors (E), and circuit failure probabilities (P_fail).

Important parameters / regimes. The grouping factor (k), the physical error rate (p), and the topology (grid vs. linear).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the availability of optimal-Toffoli-depth MCT decompositions and relies on the Qiskit community's bicycle_numerics error estimator.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the comparison of routing performance between linear and grid factory placements, and show the calculated aggregate error and circuit failure probabilities as a function of circuit size (n) and physical error rate (p).

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the difficulty of mapping MCT gates onto BB-code architectures. It then introduces the targeted placement strategy, detailing how grouping subtrees reduces routing overhead. Finally, it validates the approach by analyzing aggregate error and failure probabilities under different topological constraints.

Abstract

The multi-controlled Toffoli (MCT) gate is a fundamental primitive in quantum circuit design, with applications in quantum arithmetic, cryptanalysis, and algorithmic implementations. Being a high-level logical operation, the efficient decomposition of MCT gates into lower-level netlists has remained a major optimization challenge for decades. While emerging quantum error-correcting codes such as bivariate bicycle (BB) codes drastically reduce fault-tolerance overhead, realizing non-Clifford circuits on modular BB-code architectures introduces complex compilation bottlenecks governed by inter-module routing, factory density, and layout. Consequently, the mapping of MCT gates onto BB-code architectures remains relatively unexplored. In this paper, we overcome these challenges by mapping optimal-Toffoli-depth MCT decompositions (Dutta et al., PRA, 2025) onto BB-code-based fault-tolerant architectures via direct $\lvert \mathrm{CCZ} \rangle$ state injection from an external magic state factory. We introduce a targeted placement strategy that exploits the binary-tree structure of MCT decompositions to co-locate interacting subtrees. This approach reduces inter-module instruction counts by up to $\mathbf{16.02}\%$ compared to a naive sequential first-fit placement. We also evaluate the impact of factory placement across different topologies, demonstrating that grid-based layouts yield up to a $\mathbf{23.7}\%$ reduction in inter-module instructions relative to linear architectures (Yoder et al., arXiv, 2025). Finally, we validate the practical viability of our compiled circuits by analyzing aggregate execution errors and logical failure probabilities using the bicycle-ISA error estimator bicycle_numerics provided by the Qiskit community, https://github.com/qiskit-community/bicycle-architecture-compiler.

The Fermionic Cohomology Problem on the Full Fock Space Is $\mathrm{QMA}_1$-Complete

Authors: Yibin Wang
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00694v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

Summary. This paper proves that determining the fermionic cohomology on the entire Fock space is $\mathrm{QMA}_1$-complete. It achieves this by mapping the physical problem onto a quantum computational hardness problem using advanced techniques involving differential operators and number-penalized lifts. This result establishes a fundamental computational barrier for solving zero-energy state problems in fermionic quantum systems.

Why it may be interesting. This work connects abstract algebraic topology (cohomology) in quantum many-body systems directly to the computational complexity class $\mathrm{QMA}_1$, providing a powerful tool for classifying the hardness of ground-state problems in quantum physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the computational complexity of determining if the fermionic cohomology is non-zero on the entire, full Fock space.

Main result. The global fermionic cohomology problem on the full Fock space is proven to be $\mathrm{QMA}_1$-complete.

Method. The proof involves constructing a reduction from known $\mathrm{QMA}_1$-hard problems by lifting the problem structure to the full Fock space using number-penalized techniques and analyzing the resulting Laplacian operator.

Model / system. The system involves fermionic differential operators ($D$) acting on the full Fock space ($\mathcal{F}n$), which are related to the zero-energy states of an associated supersymmetric Hamiltonian. The structure is built upon marker differentials and data block encoding.

Key observables. The Betti number ($b_p$), which equals the zero-energy degeneracy of the Laplacian ($\Delta_p$), and the existence of non-zero cohomology classes.

Important parameters / regimes. The complexity class $\mathrm{QMA}_1$, the maximum number of modes a monomial acts on (e.g., $\le 41$), and the gap ($\delta$) in the Hamiltonian spectrum.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the exact verification convention of $\mathrm{QMA}_1$ and the ability to construct specific, sparse, and well-conditioned residual relations ($eR_ullet$).

Figures summary. The notes do not describe figures, but reference theorems and lemmas that establish the mathematical machinery for the complexity proof.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining the physical problem (cohomology) to establishing the complexity class (QMA1) by first analyzing the specified-degree case, and finally generalizing the result to the full-Fock space using number-penalized lifts and explicit encoding schemes.

Abstract

Fermionic cohomology characterizes the zero-energy states of the supersymmetric Hamiltonian associated with a fermionic differential. Previous work showed that the problem restricted to a particle-number sector specified with the input is $\mathrm{QMA}_1$-hard and belongs to $\mathrm{QMA}$. We study the global problem, in which no sector is specified and cohomology may occur anywhere in the full Fock space. This formulation directly matches the whole-space ground-state question: a specified-degree NO instance may still have zero-energy states in another sector, whereas the global NO promise excludes them across all sectors and their superpositions. We prove that this full-Fock problem is $\mathrm{QMA}_1$-complete for differentials given as exact lists of local monomials, even when each monomial acts on at most $41$ modes. As a companion result, we prove $\mathrm{QMA}_1$-completeness for the specified-degree problem with $30$-mode terms whose hard instances admit a one-dimensional block-chain realization.

Uniform Hiding of Haar Block Transpose Gram Matrices

Authors: Hongru Zhao
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01008v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical paper establishes a strong, quantifiable bound on the total variation distance between the probability distribution generated by a complex interferometer (Haar-derived) and a simple Gaussian reference distribution. By employing advanced random matrix theory and telescoping arguments, the authors show that this difference decays rapidly with the size of the system, providing a critical component for hardness arguments in quantum computation.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous, quantifiable statement about the hardness of distinguishing the true quantum process (Haar-derived) from a classical Gaussian model, which is crucial for quantum security proofs.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to prove a uniform total variation comparison (hiding) between the probability law of a complex symmetric transpose Gram matrix derived from a Haar interferometer and its corresponding complex Gaussian reference law.

Main result. The total variation distance between the two probability laws is bounded by an explicit error term, $\delta_{M,N}$, which is quadratic in the number of selected output modes ($N$) and inversely proportional to the interferometer size ($M$).

Method. The proof combines advanced random matrix theory techniques, including centered circular orthogonal ensemble (COE) score analysis, rectangular relative entropy bounds, and a telescoping argument across ambient dimensions.

Model / system. The model is Gaussian Boson Sampling (GBS) implemented with squeezed vacuum states entering a passive interferometer, where the probability is governed by the squared hafnian of a complex symmetric product.

Key observables. The probability distribution governed by the transpose Gram product ($ ext{Y Y}^T$), and the total variation distance ($ ext{dTV}$) between the Haar-derived law and the Gaussian law.

Important parameters / regimes. N (selected output modes), K (squeezed inputs), M (physical mode count). The result is uniform for $N \le K \le M$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the pure, zero-displacement setting and specifically compares the ordinary transpose ($ ext{Y Y}^T$) rather than the Hermitian product ($ ext{Y Y}^*$).

Figures summary. Figure 1 summarizes the proof architecture combining ambient recursion, COE scores, and rectangular comparison. Other figures illustrate the Haar lift, Markov contraction, and density score evaluation steps.

Paper structure. The paper builds the result by first establishing regularity and finite bounds for event paths (Lemma III.3), then deriving a one-step bound on the total variation distance ($ ext{dTV}(\mu_{m+1,K}, \mu_{m,K})$) via a Taylor decomposition (Proposition III.4), and finally telescoping this one-step bound to achieve the overall final bound.

Abstract

Gaussian boson sampling with equally squeezed active inputs assigns collision free probabilities through a complex symmetric transpose Gram matrix formed from a rectangular block of a Haar interferometer. We prove a finite total variation comparison with the corresponding complex Gaussian transpose Gram law. The error bound is explicit, quadratic in the number of selected output modes, inversely proportional to the interferometer size, and uniform in the number of squeezed active inputs. The proof combines a centered circular orthogonal ensemble score analysis in the dense regime with a rectangular relative entropy bound. This result supplies a random matrix replacement component of Gaussian boson sampling hardness arguments.

Summary. This paper presents a method to retrieve an input optical signal that has been encoded and propagated through a complex, coupled waveguide array. The retrieval mechanism relies on introducing a precise, engineered phase shift at the output section (decoder). Simulations confirm that this approach allows for high-fidelity signal reconstruction, suggesting applications in ultrafast coherent information processing.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses coherent control and signal processing in structured photonic media, which has strong overlap with quantum optics and open quantum system dynamics, particularly regarding time-reversal concepts.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core challenge is retrieving a phase-encrypted input optical signal with high fidelity after it has undergone complex, coherent propagation through an array of coupled waveguides.

Main result. The proposed scheme successfully demonstrates the retrieval of the input broadband signal using a localized phase-shifting mechanism in the decoder section of the waveguide array.

Method. The retrieval is achieved by applying a specific, engineered phase shift ($\pi$ shift in odd waveguides) at the decoder entrance, which theoretically causes a coherent inversion of the propagation evolution.

Model / system. The system utilizes an array of coupled dielectric waveguides, modeled by a discrete Schrödinger-type equation describing the evolution of the electric field components along the propagation direction (z).

Key observables. Global fidelity ($F_{gl}$) and phase fidelity ($F_{ph}$) are used to quantify the success of signal retrieval, alongside cross-sectional electric field distributions.

Important parameters / regimes. Key parameters include coupling strengths ($J_\ell$), dielectric permittivities ($\epsilon_{r1}, \epsilon_{sr}$), and the pulse characteristics ($\lambda = 1.55 \mu ext{m}$, 18 fs duration).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the ability to implement precise, engineered phase shifts at the decoder and assumes the weak-coupling regime for the waveguide array.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the schematic setup of the coder and decoder, the evolution of the electric field magnitude through the system, and plots showing the fidelity versus propagation distance.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the physical problem in coupled waveguides, develops the theoretical framework using coupled-mode equations, proposes the phase-shifting retrieval concept, and validates the scheme using full-wave electromagnetic simulations.

Abstract

Optical waveguides are key elements for high fidelity, long distance optical communications. Coupled waveguide arrays allow for higher information density, steerting the propagation direction, and for encoding information. However, due to the mixing of relative phases for short pulses containing multiple waveguide-mode frequencies, a process for retrieving an encoded input state once these signals undergo coherent propagation remains elusive. A concept is presented to extract with high fidelity the phase-encrypted input signal from spatio-temporal propagated states. As a realization, an array of coupled waveguides is suggested with the retrieval mechanism being realized by local phase shifts that comply with the identified retrieval concept. Three dimensional full-wave electromagnetic simulations for broadband optical signals in coupled dielectric waveguides confirm the validity of the scheme and the high fidelity of information retrieval pointing to potential applications, for instance in ultrafast coherent coding and decoding of information imprinted on pulse sequences.

Summary. This theoretical study analyzes Majorana bound states in a superconductor-semiconductor nanowire coupled to an optical cavity. By solving the coupled electron-photon Hamiltonian, the authors show that the cavity interaction modifies the topological phase diagram, enabling the topological state to be accessed at lower external magnetic fields than previously thought possible.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly combines concepts from cavity QED (light-matter coupling) with topological superconductivity, which is a major area of research in AMO physics and quantum optics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper theoretically investigates how coupling Majorana bound states (MBSs) in a superconductor-semiconductor heterostructure to a single-mode photonic cavity modifies the system's topological phase diagram.

Main result. Cavity coupling shifts the phase boundary to smaller Zeeman energies, and even weak coupling allows entering the topological phase at lower critical Zeeman energies than the uncoupled case.

Method. The authors derived an effective light-matter Hamiltonian and solved the coupled electron-photon system using exact diagonalization, high-frequency expansion, and mean-field decoupling.

Model / system. The model is a hybrid platform: a 1D quantum wire tunnel-coupled to an s-wave superconductor, all embedded within a single-mode photonic cavity. The coupling enters the effective superconducting pairing term.

Key observables. Topological phase boundary, gap closing, effective superconducting pairing strength.

Important parameters / regimes. Light-matter coupling strength (g), Zeeman energy ($V_Z$), number of photons (n).

Assumptions / limitations. The photonic vector potential is aligned along the tunneling direction, and the analysis uses approximations for the photon number (e.g., dark cavity limit or semiclassical limit).

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the physical setup (wire-superconductor-cavity). Figure 2(a) shows the topological phase diagram dependence on $V_Z/\Delta_{eff}$ and $g$ for the dark cavity limit.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the coupled Hamiltonian, derives the effective wire-cavity Hamiltonian, and then applies various mathematical techniques (exact diagonalization, etc.) to calculate the phase diagram and analyze the impact of coupling strength.

Abstract

We theoretically study a hybrid superconductor-semiconductor platform hosting Majorana bound states coupled to a single mode photonic cavity. Starting with a one-dimensional wire coupled to a bulk $s$-wave superconductor embedded in a photonic cavity, we derive an effective light-matter Hamiltonian for such a platform. Assuming that the photonic vector potential is aligned along the tunneling between a wire and a superconductor, we find that the cavity coupling enters only in the effective superconducting pairing term. By solving the coupled electron-photon Hamiltonian using different approaches, such as exact diagonalization in case of zero or large number of photons, high-frequency expansion, and mean-field decoupling, we find that the phase boundary between the topological trivial phases is shifted to smaller values of the Zeeman energy compared to the uncoupled case. Cavity embedding has the strongest effect on the phase diagram in the semiclassical regime, corresponding to a large number of photons. In all cases, we find that at large values of the light-matter coupling strength the effective superconducting pairing is suppressed, driving the system into the gapless phase. We demonstrate that even small light-matter coupling strength allows for entering the topological phase at values of the Zeeman energy compared to the uncoupled platform.

Summary. This work reports on using Aharonov-Bohm interferometry in graphene to directly probe the spin structure of fractional quantum Hall edge states. By coupling the target FQH edge to a spin-polarized probe, the researchers distinguish between spin-unpolarized and spin-polarized regimes of the $\nu=2/3$ state. Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to develop a spin-sensitive probe to characterize the spin-singlet topological order present in fractional quantum Hall (FQH) edge states, particularly the $\nu=2/3$ state.

Main result. The authors successfully identified the spin-unpolarized and spin-polarized regimes of the $\nu=2/3$ edge state using Aharonov-Bohm interferometry, confirming the existence of spin-charge separation effects.

Method. The primary method involves using a p-n junction to couple a target FQH edge state to a probe integer QH state, forming an Aharonov-Bohm interferometer whose interference pattern is spin-dependent.

Model / system. The system is graphene exhibiting FQH edge states. The theoretical analysis models the edge using boson fields and investigates the transition between $SU(2)$-symmetric (unpolarized) and broken symmetry (polarized) regimes.

Abstract

The edge modes of spin-singlet fractional quantum Hall (FQH) phases are manifestations of multicomponent topological order and SU(2) spin symmetry. An archetype is the spin-unpolarized state at $ν$=2/3, whose edge is expected to host spatially coexisting yet counter-propagating charge and neutral spin modes. Despite efforts, its edge properties, including spin coherence and spin-charge separation, have remained elusive owing to the difficulty of resolving spins in FQH edge transport. Here, we develop a spin-sensitive probe of graphene FQH edges by using a p-n junction to interface a target FQH state with a probe integer quantum Hall (QH) state of opposite polarity. An Aharonov-Bohm (AB) interferometer forms along the interface, when the target and probe edge channels carry the same spin. We identify the spin-unpolarized and polarized edges of the $ν$=2/3 states at lower and higher magnetic fields, respectively, through spin-dependent interference. A novel multiparticle AB interference between two electrons of opposite spin emerges from spin-charge separation and recombination on the unpolarized edge, featuring a spin swap. Our results establish edge transport as a probe of spin-singlet topological orders, with implications for parafermion platforms in graphene-superconductor hybrids.

Abstract

Summary. This theoretical work investigates how to engineer multiple chiral Majorana modes in magnetic topological insulator heterostructures for topological quantum computing. By modeling the system using an extended 3D BHZ Hamiltonian, the authors show that magnetic effects and superconducting proximity can generate these robust channels. Method. The study employs theoretical modeling using an extended 3D Bernevig-Hughes-Zhang (BHZ) Hamiltonian within the Bogoliubov-de Gennes framework.

Abstract

Achieving robust topological superconductivity with multiple Majorana channels is a key step for scalable topological quantum computing. To this end, we investigate magnetically doped three-dimensional topological insulator heterostructures proximitized by an s-wave superconductor within a fully three-dimensional extended Bernevig-Hughes-Zhang framework that explicitly accounts for vertical confinement. We show that magnetic exchange coupling, orbital mixing, and bulk band inversion cooperate to generate effective equal-spin p-wave pairing channels supporting multiple chiral Majorana modes. The number of Majorana channels is determined by the confined modes in the vertical direction and the high-Chern-number phases of the normal state, which allows us to derive an analytical criterion for the emergence of the multiple-Majorana topological superconducting phases. The chiral Majorana modes remain robust against strong disorder and moderate symmetry-breaking perturbations. We also demonstrate that a superconducting phase difference defining a vertical Josephson junction acts as a tunable parameter that controls the hybridization, minigap, and effective multiplicity of the low energy Majorana channels. Our results establish magnetic three-dimensional topological- insulator heterostructures as a promising platform for engineering multiple chiral Majorana modes.

Abstract

Recent real-space imaging in quantum paraelectric SrTiO$_3$ [Nature 656, 54 (2026)] reveals that local polar textures do not continuously grow upon cooling, but reach a maximum intensity at intermediate temperatures around 60-65~K and weaken again toward the quantum paraelectric ground state. Such a reentrant-like weakening of local polar textures challenges the conventional paradigm in which ordering tendencies generally strengthen as thermal fluctuations are suppressed. Here we employ a self-consistent phase-field theory showing that this anomalous behavior naturally arises from the interplay between intrinsic polar and antiferrodistortiv (AFD) fluctuations. We demonstrate that flexoelectric-like coupling strongly hybridizes the polar and AFD modes. As the uncoupled polar and AFD modes cross near 52~K, their hybridization is maximized, driving the lower hybridized branch to develop a minimum on a finite-wave-vector shell. This finite-$q$ softening triggers a Brazovskii-type instability, strongly enhancing polarization correlations and producing nanoscale polar textures. Away from the crossing temperature, the two modes become increasingly detuned, weakening their hybridization and the associated finite-$q$ softening. These results reaveal the origin of the formation of polar nanoregions in SrTiO$_3$, naturally explaining the unexpected confinement to an intermediate-temperature window and providing a mechanism beyond the quantum-fluctuation-based interpretation suggested by experiment. Furthermore, we predict an unconventional reentrant-like sequence in weakly strained SrTiO$_3$, evolving from ferroelectric to paraelectric, polar-nanoregion, and eventually paraelectric regimes upon heating from zero temperature.

Summary. This work investigates superconducting proximity effects in semiconductor/superconductor hybrids by measuring quantum oscillations in a buried InAs quantum well. By analyzing the SdH oscillations under various superconducting films, the authors determine the intrinsic normal-state parameters of the semiconductor. This technique overcomes the ambiguity caused by the superconducting metal shunt, providing crucial data for understanding interface physics.

Why it may be interesting. The methodology of using quantum oscillations to probe parameters inaccessible via standard tunneling spectroscopy in superconducting hybrids provides a powerful, complementary diagnostic tool for studying superconducting interfaces and proximity effects.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is accurately characterizing the electronic parameters of a semiconductor quantum well when it is buried beneath a superconducting metal film, as the metal shorts the electrical transport.

Main result. The study successfully determines the normal-state parameters (density, mass, g-factor) of the buried semiconductor using quantum oscillations, showing that the superconducting metal induces a distinct interface subband without altering the core parameters of the well.

Method. The analysis relies on measuring Shubnikov-de Haas (SdH) oscillations in the presence of superconducting films, complemented by a Dingle analysis that explicitly accounts for the shunt resistance.

Model / system. The system is a superconductor-semiconductor hybrid, specifically an InAs quantum well placed beneath various superconducting metals (Al, Sn, Nb, etc.). The physics involves analyzing the superconducting proximity effect and the resulting interface subband formation.

Key observables. Shubnikov-de Haas oscillation frequencies, oscillation amplitudes (analyzed via Dingle fitting), cyclotron masses, and g-factors.

Important parameters / regimes. Quantum lifetime ($ au_q$), superconducting gap ($\Delta_0$), quasiparticle weight ($Z$), and the coupling strength ($\Gamma$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the SdH oscillations of the buried well remain measurable despite the shunt, and that the measured parameters can be separated from the superconducting renormalization effects.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the schematic of the buried well and the metal-induced interface subband, and show the SdH oscillation envelopes for both the fundamental and interface branches fitted using Dingle analysis.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the problem of shunt-induced ambiguity, presents the measurement technique using multiple superconducting films, analyzes the resulting quantum oscillations to extract parameters, and concludes by comparing these findings to predictions from tunneling spectroscopy.

Abstract

Superconductor-semiconductor hybrids host emergent states of matter and offer a platform for new qubits, but the superconducting metal shunts electrical transport, which rules out conventional semiconductor characterization and leaves the hybrid parameters to speculation. Here we determine density, mass, $g$-factor, mobility and subband occupation beneath the superconductor, from Shubnikov-de Haas oscillations of a buried InAs quantum well under Al, Sn, V, Nb, Ta and Re films, with a Dingle analysis that accounts for the shunt. Every metal adds an interface subband whose occupation falls into one of two classes, whereas the mass and $g$-factor of the buried well are unchanged to within 10\%. Within the uncertainty set by the transport mobility, no film shortens the quantum lifetime of the buried well, and Al and Sn lengthen it. Quantum lifetimes bound the hybridization of the interface subband to 2-4~meV. These measurements supply the normal-state parameters that tunnelling spectroscopy renormalizes but cannot measure.
