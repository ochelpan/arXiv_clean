Anti-higher-order topological insulators

Authors: Cheng-Ming Miao, Yu-Hao Wan, Ying-Tao Zhang, Qing-Feng Sun
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00780v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 2/5

Summary. This paper introduces anti-higher-order topological insulators (anti-HOTIs) as the dual counterpart to conventional topological insulators. By analyzing a bilayer Chern insulator model, the authors demonstrate that anti-HOTIs exhibit a unique state distribution where edge states exist but corner states are suppressed. This work establishes anti-topology as a new theoretical framework for topological materials research.

Why it may be interesting. The concept of anti-topology provides a new framework for classifying topological phases, which is highly relevant to understanding boundary physics in condensed matter systems, potentially leading to novel material designs.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the need to explore the dual counterpart of topological states, introducing the concept of anti-topological states and anti-higher-order topological insulators (anti-HOTIs).

Main result. The authors establish anti-topology as a new paradigm, demonstrating that anti-HOTIs host states along most boundaries but are characteristically absent at the corners, unlike conventional HOTIs.

Method. The study uses theoretical modeling based on bilayer Chern insulator Hamiltonians and analyzes the topological invariant defined by the charges enclosed by the Band Inversion Surfaces (BIS).

Model / system. The system is modeled using a bilayer Hamiltonian applied to a Chern insulator, incorporating components derived from a modified Qi–Wu–Zhang (QWZ) model. The analysis is performed on nanoribbons and nanoflakes.

Key observables. The topological invariant is the set of topological charges enclosed by the BIS; key observables include the gap magnitude and the spatial distribution of edge/anti-corner states.

Important parameters / regimes. Key parameters include the Chern numbers enclosed by the BIS at high-symmetry points ($\Gamma, X, Y, M$), and the system parameters $B$ and $J$ in the phase diagram analysis.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the system preserves inversion symmetry, which helps pin the topological charges at specific high-symmetry points in the Brillouin Zone.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the schematic difference between HOT (corner states) and anti-HOT (anti-corner states), show band structures of nanoribbons at varying angles, and present phase diagrams mapping the anti-HOT phase based on enclosed topological charges.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the concept of anti-topology via duality, develops the model using a bilayer Hamiltonian, calculates the topological invariant using BIS analysis, and finally demonstrates the anti-HOT phase through numerical simulations and phase diagram construction.

Abstract

Duality is a fundamental concept in physics that connects complementary opposites like particles and holes. Similarly, while topological states in topological insulators localize at boundaries, the existence and nature of their dual counterparts remain unexplored. Here, we introduce anti-topological states as the dual of topological states, exemplified by anti-higher-order topological insulators. Unlike higher-order topological insulators, where states localize at corners, anti-higher-order topological insulators host states along edges but absent at corners, realizing an inverted distribution of states. We demonstrate this phenomenon in a bilayer Chern insulator with opposite Chern numbers, where the band inversion surfaces enclose distinct high-symmetry points. The topological invariant distinguishing these phases is given by the topological charges enclosed by band inversion surfaces. This work establishes anti-topology as a new paradigm, opening a chapter in topological research.

Large Exchange Magnetostriction in a Kagome Antiferromagnet at Room Temperature

Authors: Jie Du, Liang Yao, Hang Li, Xiaodong Zhou, Yuan Yao, Xuekui Xi, Yong-Chang Lau, Wenhong Wang

Summary. This work reports the observation of large, highly reversible magnetostriction in the kagome antiferromagnet YMn6Sn6 at room temperature. The mechanism is determined to originate from the competition between different magnetic exchange interactions, which drive the system from a helical to a collinear magnetic state. This establishes kagome helimagnets as a promising, low-dissipation platform for magnetomechanical applications.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on condensed matter magnetism, the concept of field-induced, reversible lattice strain driven by competing quantum interactions (exchange coupling) provides a model for understanding controllable, low-dissipation coupling mechanisms relevant to quantum sensing or actuation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The research addresses the need for high-performance magnetostrictive materials, specifically by systematically exploring large magnetostriction in antiferromagnets, which are often overlooked compared to ferromagnets.

Main result. The kagome antiferromagnet YMn6Sn6 exhibits large, nearly hysteresis-free magnetostriction (exceeding 400 ppm) at room temperature, which is attributed to the competition between intralayer and interlayer exchange interactions.

Method. The study combines extensive experimental measurements (magnetostriction, XRD, STEM) with first-principles theoretical calculations (DFT) to model the magnetoelastic coupling.

Model / system. The physical system is the kagome helimagnet YMn6Sn6, characterized by competing magnetic exchange interactions (intralayer FM vs. interlayer coupling) that stabilize a noncollinear helical magnetic ground state.

Key observables. Large anisotropic and volume magnetostriction ($\sim 300-400$ ppm), field-induced magnetic phase transitions (helical to collinear), and the degree of magnetostrictive reversibility.

Important parameters / regimes. Room temperature (300 K), applied magnetic field up to 9 T, and the ratio/competition strength between intralayer and interlayer exchange interactions.

Assumptions / limitations. The large magnetostriction is primarily driven by exchange-interaction-mediated spin structure reconstruction rather than conventional Spin-Orbit Coupling (SOC) effects.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the crystal structure, XRD patterns, STEM images confirming homogeneity, and detailed plots showing anisotropic strain responses ($\lambda_{\parallel}$ vs. $\lambda_{\perp}$) correlated with magnetic field-induced phase transitions.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the technological need, presents structural characterization, details the magnetic phase transitions under field, quantifies the anisotropic magnetostriction, and finally uses DFT calculations to prove the exchange-driven nature of the magnetoelastic coupling.

Abstract

The pursuit of high-performance magnetostrictive materials is crucial for advancing technologies in sensing, actuation, and microelectromechanical systems. Although pronounced magnetostrictive effects have been observed in a few ferromagnets, a systematic exploration of magnetostriction across a broader range of antiferromagnets remains limited. Here, we report the observation of large magnetostriction in the kagome antiferromagnet YMn6Sn6. Under a magnetic field, the system undergoes a field-induced evolution from a helical magnetic ground state toward a collinear field-polarized state, accompanied by a large anisotropic lattice strain and a substantial volume magnetostriction exceeding 400 ppm at room temperature. Remarkably, the magnetostrictive response remains nearly fully reversible up to 9 T with nearly hysteresis-free behavior, effectively minimizing the energy dissipation commonly associated with domain-wall pinning. Combined experimental measurements and theoretical calculations reveal that the large, nearly hysteresis-free magnetostriction originates from the competition between intralayer ferromagnetic and interlayer exchange interactions. This exchange-driven magnetoelastic coupling further gives rise to a strongly direction-dependent lattice distortion pathway. This work establishes kagome helimagnets as a tunable platform for low-dissipation magnetoelastic functionalities and responsive magnetomechanical applications.

Superconducting diode effect from field-induced $s+if$ pairing in Ising superconductors

Authors: Alex Levchenko, Maxim Khodas
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01476v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 2/5

Summary. This theoretical work analyzes the superconducting diode effect in Ising superconductors due to field-induced s+if pairing. By developing a two-component Ginzburg-Landau theory, the authors demonstrate that the SDE is governed by the coupling between singlet and triplet channels. This effect provides a novel transport measurement to detect the presence of hidden triplet pairing states.

Why it may be interesting. The strong coupling between magnetic fields, spin-orbit coupling, and superconducting symmetry breaking provides a rich platform for studying emergent quantum phenomena in condensed matter systems, relevant to understanding topological superconductivity.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the superconducting diode effect (SDE) arising from field-induced s+if pairing in Ising superconductors.

Main result. The SDE is shown to be a transport probe for the hidden triplet pairing channel, with the diode response strength controlled by the ratio of Zeeman to spin-orbit energies ($\Delta Z/\Delta ext{so}$).

Method. The authors construct and analyze a symmetry-constrained two-component Ginzburg-Landau theory for coupled singlet and triplet order parameters, deriving coefficients from a microscopic model.

Model / system. The system is an Ising superconductor (e.g., monolayer TMDs) subjected to an in-plane magnetic field, which induces an s+if condensate character via triplet Cooper pairs.

Key observables. Superconducting Diode Effect ($\eta_{ ext{SDE}}$), Lifshitz invariants, and the superconducting order parameters ($\psi$ and $\mathbf{d}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Zeeman energy ($\Delta Z$), spin-orbit energy ($\Delta ext{so}$), and the ratio $\Delta Z/\Delta ext{so}$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the GL theory framework and the assumption that the triplet sector can be integrated out to yield an effective single-component theory.

Figures summary. Figure 3 illustrates the temperature and field dependence of the diode efficiency, showing the triplet channel extends the diode regime beyond the singlet-only critical field.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the physical mechanism (SOC leading to triplet pairing and Lifshitz invariants) to constructing the full GL theory, deriving effective coefficients, and finally analyzing the resulting SDE behavior under varying field strengths.

Abstract

The in-plane critical field of Ising superconductors exceeds the Pauli limit by an order of magnitude because the Ising spin-orbit coupling locks the electron spins out of the basal plane. The same locking converts an in-plane Zeeman field into a source of equal-spin triplet Cooper pairs, so that the field-driven condensate acquires an $s+if$ character. We show that this conversion channel also generates Lifshitz invariants, the odd-in-momentum terms of the Ginzburg-Landau expansion responsible for the superconducting diode effect. When the basal mirror symmetry of the monolayer is lifted by a substrate or a gate, the field-induced triplets couple linearly to the Cooper-pair momentum, and an intrinsic diode response develops whose strength is set by the ratio of the Zeeman and spin-orbit energies rather than by the small ratio of the spin-orbit and Fermi energies familiar from parity-mixing mechanisms. We construct the symmetry-constrained two-component Ginzburg-Landau theory of the coupled singlet and triplet order parameters and derive all of its coefficients from the microscopic model of an Ising superconductor; the complete functional, including all gradient and quartic terms, is generated by a single pair-breaking function of temperature, field, and Cooper-pair momentum. An attractive triplet channel reshapes the diode response: at weak fields it suppresses the efficiency through destructive interference between the direct and the collective-mode conversion paths, while at strong fields it extends the diode regime well beyond the singlet-only critical field, with the maximal efficiency reached along the triplet-enhanced phase boundary. The diode effect thereby serves as a transport probe of a hidden triplet pairing channel and of the field-induced $s+if$ state.

Summary. This work extends Floquet scattering theory to analyze quantum transport in topological semimetals like nodal-ring and multi-Weyl systems under periodic driving. By solving the full boundary problem, the authors prove that while evanescent modes are required for mathematical consistency, only propagating modes dictate observable transport properties. This establishes a unified framework for understanding Floquet-induced phenomena like Fano resonances in these complex materials.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical treatment of boundary conditions in the presence of time-periodic driving and complex band structures (Weyl/Nodal-Ring) provides a powerful, general framework for analyzing open quantum systems dynamics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops a comprehensive Floquet scattering theory to analyze quantum transport in periodically driven nodal-ring and multi-Weyl semimetals.

Main result. It establishes that while evanescent modes are mathematically necessary for boundary matching, they do not contribute to measurable transport quantities like transmission or shot noise, which are determined entirely by propagating channels.

Method. The authors solve the full boundary-value problem using Floquet scattering theory, constructing the scattering matrix by matching wavefunctions and their derivatives at potential interfaces.

Model / system. The study focuses on Nodal-Ring Semimetals (NRSs) and multi-Weyl Semimetals (mWSMs) subjected to a time-periodic driving potential. The analysis involves solving the time-dependent Schrödinger equation in the Floquet basis.

Key observables. Transmission, reflection, pumped shot noise, and Floquet-induced Fano resonance features.

Important parameters / regimes. The driving frequency ($\omega$), the potential strength ($V_0, V_1$), and the topological nature of the semimetals (NRSs, mWSMs).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the zero-temperature limit for shot noise calculations and relies on the mathematical necessity of including evanescent modes for consistency in nonlinear dispersion regimes.

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically illustrates the scattering geometry for a quasiparticle incident on a time-periodic potential well in a double-Weyl mWSM, dividing the space into incident, well, and transmitted regions.

Paper structure. The paper builds from developing the full Floquet scattering formalism for anisotropic topological semimetals, deriving the scattering matrix by solving boundary conditions across interfaces, and finally analyzing the resulting transport coefficients and Fano resonance signatures.

Abstract

We develop a comprehensive Floquet scattering theory for quantum transport in periodically driven nodal-ring semimetals (NRSs) and multi-Weyl semimetals (mWSMs), extending our earlier study reported in Annalen der Physik 535, 2200460 (2023), in which evanescent modes were neglected, to a complete formalism that includes both propagating and evanescent channels. By solving the full boundary-value problem, we obtain the complete set of scattering states and show that, although evanescent modes are indispensable for satisfying the matching conditions at the potential interfaces, they carry zero net probability current and do not contribute to any observable transport quantity. We identify a previously unexplored transport regime in NRSs in which two propagating channels coexist and participate in coherent scattering, producing multi-channel quantum interference and Floquet-induced Fano resonances. The transmission, reflection, pumped shot noise, and the associated Fano resonance features are determined entirely by the propagating channels, and the resonance energies coincide with those of the corresponding quasi-bound states of the static potential well. Our results establish a unified framework for Floquet transport in anisotropic topological semimetals, and confirm that the approximation of neglecting evanescent modes in our earlier work is justified for all measurable transport properties.

Summary. This work investigates the high-density behavior of interlayer excitons in MoSe2/WSe2 heterobilayers using PLE spectroscopy. By tuning the excitation energy, the researchers drive the system into a nonlinear regime, observing PL saturation and a systematic blueshift in the emission energy. This blueshift confirms the presence of a net repulsive exciton-exciton interaction, establishing PLE as a key diagnostic tool for these quantum materials.

Why it may be interesting. The use of PLE spectroscopy to map out the nonlinear response of excitons in a solid-state van der Waals heterostructure provides a powerful experimental tool for studying many-body physics in quantum materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to probe the nonlinear, high-density interactions of dipolar interlayer excitons (IXs) within MoSe2/WSe2 heterobilayers.

Main result. The IX emission exhibits a systematic blueshift proportional to the increasing IX population, confirming a net repulsive exciton-exciton interaction dominated by dipole-dipole repulsion.

Method. Excitation-energy-dependent photoluminescence excitation (PLE) spectroscopy and power-dependent PL measurements are used to selectively vary and probe the high-density regime of the excitons.

Model / system. The physical system is a van der Waals heterobilayer of MoSe2/WSe2, which forms interlayer excitons due to type-II band alignment, giving rise to an intrinsic out-of-plane dipole moment.

Key observables. Interlayer exciton photoluminescence (PL) energy shift (blueshift), saturation of PL response, and apparent broadening of intralayer resonances in PLE spectra.

Important parameters / regimes. Low temperatures (4 K vs 75 K), varying excitation power, and the density regime achieved through resonant excitation.

Assumptions / limitations. The observed blueshift is attributed to repulsive exciton-exciton interaction, and the PLE measurements are sensitive enough to probe nonlinear population dynamics.

Figures summary. Figures summarize the structure, band alignment, temperature dependence (Varshni fit), magnetic field effects (Zeeman shifts), and the dependence of IX emission energy on excitation energy and power.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the physical system and methods (PLE, power dependence) to access the nonlinear regime, presents evidence of nonlinear dynamics (saturation, broadening), and quantifies the interaction strength via the systematic blueshift of the IX emission.

Abstract

Interlayer excitons in transition-metal dichalcogenide heterobilayers possess intrinsic out-of-plane dipole moments, providing a platform for investigating exciton-exciton interactions at high densities. Here, we use excitation-energy-dependent photoluminescence excitation (PLE) spectroscopy to probe the nonlinear response of dipolar interlayer excitons in chemical vapor deposition-grown MoSe$_2$/WSe$_2$ heterobilayers. By tuning the excitation energy across intralayer exciton resonances at fixed excitation power, we selectively vary the population injected into the interlayer exciton states. Resonant excitation drives the system into a nonlinear regime, leading to saturation of the interlayer exciton photoluminescence and an apparent broadening of the intralayer $1s$ resonances in the PLE spectra. At the same time, the interlayer exciton emission exhibits a pronounced blueshift, reaching approximately 2.5 meV at 4 K and 1 meV at 75 K. The blueshift increases systematically with the interlayer exciton population and is consistent with a net repulsive exciton-exciton interaction, with contributions from dipole-dipole repulsion in the density regime investigated. Our results establish PLE as a sensitive approach for accessing the nonlinear, high-density regime of interlayer excitons and probing their interactions in van der Waals heterostructures.

Exchange striction determines how fast antiferromagnetic insulators demagnetize

Authors: Aleksandr Buzdakov, Ravi Kaushik, Nikolai Khokhlov, Sergey Artyukhin, Alexey Kimel
Type: both · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01069v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: correlated / nonlocal dissipation 1/5 · driven-dissipative phase transition 1/5 · non-equilibrium universality 1/5

Summary. This research establishes that the speed at which antiferromagnetic insulators demagnetize is controlled by the coupling between lattice vibrations (phonons) and spin excitations (magnons). By calculating the exchange striction tensor, the authors provide a predictive, computable parameter that successfully explains the vast difference in demagnetization rates observed between $ ext{Cr}_2 ext{O}_3$ and $ ext{FeBO}_3$.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a concrete, computable material parameter ($\partial J / \partial u$) that links fundamental lattice physics (phonons/displacements) directly to ultrafast magnetic dynamics, which is highly relevant for understanding energy dissipation and non-equilibrium quantum dynamics in solids.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to determine the fundamental physical mechanism that sets the speed limit for demagnetization in antiferromagnetic insulators.

Main result. The demagnetization rate is governed by energy flow from the lattice into the spins, quantified by the exchange striction tensor ($\partial J / \partial u$). This mechanism explains the large difference in demagnetization speeds between $ ext{Cr}_2 ext{O}_3$ and $ ext{FeBO}_3$.

Method. The study combines first-principles calculations (DFT+U) to compute exchange striction parameters with atomistic spin-lattice dynamics (SLD) simulations to model the time evolution of the magnetic order parameter.

Model / system. The system involves antiferromagnetic insulators ($ ext{Cr}_2 ext{O}_3$, $ ext{FeBO}_3$) modeled using spin operators and phonon operators. The Hamiltonian is expanded to include an interaction term driven by the dependence of the exchange coupling ($J$) on atomic displacements ($\mathbf{u}$), leading to a one-phonon-two-magnon process.

Key observables. Demagnetization time ($ au$) measured via time-resolved Second-Harmonic Generation (SHG) contrast; the exchange-striction tensor ($\partial J / \partial u$).

Important parameters / regimes. The ratio of decay rates ($ au_{ ext{DM},1p1m}^{-1} / au_{ ext{ex},1p2m}^{-1}$); the magnitude of the exchange coupling sensitivity to displacement.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on perturbative expansions in atomic displacements and assumes that the one-phonon-two-magnon (1p2m) channel dominates over the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya (DM)-driven 1p1m channel.

Figures summary. Figures compare crystal structures and magnetic paths of $ ext{Cr}_2 ext{O}_3$ and $ ext{FeBO}_3$, showing the overlap between phonon dispersions and the two-magnon continuum, which dictates the coupling strength.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from identifying the experimental challenge (fast demagnetization) to proposing the mechanism (lattice coupling via exchange striction). It details the theoretical derivation of the coupling vertex (1p2m process) and concludes by applying this framework to compare two specific materials, quantifying the rate difference.

Abstract

Antiferromagnets combine terahertz spin dynamics with insensitivity to stray fields, and how quickly their order can be manipulated sets the speed limit on device operation. Femtosecond optical pulses demagnetize antiferromagnetic insulators on timescales that span picoseconds to nanoseconds across compounds, and no material parameter is known that accounts for the spread or predicts where a new compound will fall. In a compensated antiferromagnet, no angular momentum needs to leave the spin system, so the rate is set by energy flow from the lattice into the spins. Time-resolved second-harmonic generation experiments show that Cr2O3 demagnetizes within 2 ps once the lattice is driven above the Neel temperature, two orders of magnitude faster than the structurally similar FeBO3. First-principles calculations trace the disparity to exchange striction: short Cr-Cr contacts make the exchange coupling tenfold more sensitive to atomic displacements and widen the phase space for phonon decay into magnon pairs. Spin-lattice simulations with ab initio parameters reproduce the order of magnitude of the measured ratio. The derivative of the exchange coupling with respect to the ionic displacement thus emerges as a computable parameter that predicts how fast an insulating antiferromagnet can be demagnetized. The results advance our understanding of ultrafast control in insulating antiferromagnets, and suggest a practical pathway to screen candidate materials for thermally assisted antiferromagnetic memory before synthesis.

Singular Weak-Field Thermodynamics of 2D Superconductors

Authors: Guopeng Xu, Chunli Huang
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01602v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5 · analog quantum simulation 1/5 · quantum measurements 1/5

Summary. This theoretical work analyzes the thermodynamics of 2D superconductors in a magnetic field, finding that the critical field for vortex formation depends on the sample size. By comparing weak and strong screening limits, the authors derive specific scaling laws for this critical field and establish a firm microscopic foundation for the weak-field ground state.

Why it may be interesting. The study of size-dependent critical fields and the non-commutativity of limits is highly relevant to understanding finite-size effects in quantum materials, which is a key area in modern condensed matter theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the weak-field thermodynamics of two-dimensional (2D) superconductors, specifically addressing the non-commutativity of the weak-field and thermodynamic limits.

Main result. The lower critical field required to nucleate the first vortex is size-dependent, scaling differently in weak ($\propto \mathcal{A}^{-1}\ln(\mathcal{A})$) and strong ($\propto \mathcal{A}^{-1/2}$) screening regimes.

Method. The analysis uses microscopic models, solving for the ground state energy of both vortex-free and single-vortex states by incorporating screening effects via coupling to 3D Maxwell equations.

Model / system. The system is a 2D superconductor (e.g., atomically thin TMDs) modeled using a Hamiltonian involving Landau levels and attractive contact interactions. The study compares the energy density in the presence of an external magnetic field ($B$) versus the thermodynamic limit ($A o \infty$).

Key observables. Lower critical field $B_v(\mathcal{A})$, energy density ($E/A$), and the vortex orbital moment ($m_v$).

Important parameters / regimes. System area ($\mathcal{A}$), external magnetic field ($B$), screening regimes (weak vs. strong), and the ratio of characteristic length scales ($\xi, \Lambda, R$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on treating the problem non-perturbatively using Landau quantization and making approximations in momentum space for calculating vortex self-energies.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the ground state energy dependence on field and area, showing non-analytic behavior at zero field and comparing energy densities for uniform vs. single-vortex states.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by establishing the physical difference between 2D and 3D superconductors, deriving the energy functional for vortex states, analyzing the scaling laws for the first-vortex field in different screening regimes, and concluding with the thermodynamic limit behavior.

Abstract

In a bulk 3D type-II superconductor, the lower critical field at which an isolated vortex becomes thermodynamically favorable is a size-independent material property. We show that the situation is different in 2D superconductors: the larger the superconductor, the weaker the field needed to create its first vortex. The lower critical field in 2D is always size-dependent. For a disk of area $\mathcal A$, the lower critical field $B_v(\mathcal A)$ scales as $\mathcal A^{-1}\ln(\mathcal A/\mathcal A_0)$ in the weak-screening regime and as $\mathcal A^{-1/2}$ in the strong-screening regime. We derive these results from an analytically tractable microscopic model that admits many-body wavefunctions for both the uniform and singly quantized vortex states in a magnetic field, and incorporate screening by coupling their long-distance 2D supercurrents to 3D Maxwell equations. These results motivate organizing the weak-field ground-state of a 2D superconductor in the $(1/\mathcal A,B)$ plane. The origin represents the zero-field thermodynamic limit and it is singular. Approaching the origin along the $B$ axis leads to an increasingly dilute vortex lattice, whereas approaching along the $1/\mathcal A$ axis yields the uniform vortex-free state. Our theory shows that every trajectory carrying fixed finite flux ultimately approaches the vortex-free state in the thermodynamic limit and provides a firm microscopic foundation for the weak-field thermodynamics of 2D superconductors.

Main problem. The paper investigates the chaotic dynamics of large one-dimensional lattices of nonlinear maps as the extent of spatial coupling is increased.

Model / system. The system is a Coupled Map Lattice (CML) defined by a 1D lattice of nonlinear maps, coupled via a spatially extended interaction term. The dynamics are governed by $\vec{u}(n+1) = A_c(\epsilon, \xi_d)\vec{f}(\vec{u}(n))$.

Abstract

The chaotic dynamics of a large one-dimensional lattice of nonlinear maps with a spatial coupling of increasing extent is explored using the covariant Lyapunov vectors (CLVs). A theoretical approach is developed that predicts the spectral variation of the CLVs and the spectrum of Lyapunov exponents. The theoretical approach is independent of the details of the mapping function and requires only a description of the lattice coupling. The general structure of the spatial power spectrum of the CLVs is determined using only the eigenvectors of the coupling matrix. This yields a decomposition of the CLVs into two regimes. The first regime, corresponding to the largest Lyapunov exponents, contains highly entangled CLVs with significant contributions from large scale spatial structures and aligns with the sorted eigenvectors, which occur in pairs with wavenumbers that increase linearly with their index. The second regime contains significant mixing of length scales with increasing index. The index separating these two regimes is approximately equal to the fractal dimension of the dynamics.

Abstract

The physical origin of the suppression of superconductivity with hole doping in overdoped cuprates remains unclear. We measure the electronic specific heat of microgram-scale Tl$_2$Ba$_2$CuO$_6$ crystals and find sharp superconducting anomalies persisting far into the overdoped regime, with $Δγ(T_{\rm c})/γ_n\approx0.6$ for $T_{\rm c}=14$-$25$K. A weak-coupling BCS-like framework incorporating the known Fermi surface and cation disorder quantitatively reproduces the observed anomaly and its weak doping dependence. We conclude that the observed $T_{\rm c}(p)$ cannot be driven predominantly by increasing disorder and is instead consistent with a smoothly decreasing pairing strength.
