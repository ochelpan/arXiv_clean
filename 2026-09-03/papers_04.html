Towards Natural Gas Contract Selection via Quantum-Guided Independent Set Reduction

Authors: Vivek Dixit, Vaibhaw Kumar, Kentaro Ohno, Alberto Maldonado Romo, Larry Bowden
Type: both · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00881v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: QC/QI experiment 2/5

Summary. This work proposes a quantum-classical hybrid solver to tackle the computationally hard Maximum Independent Set (MIS) problem, using natural gas contract selection as an industrial motivation. The method iteratively simplifies the problem using classical graph theory before applying QAOA on the remaining kernel using current quantum hardware. It demonstrates that this hybrid approach effectively handles large, real-world graph structures.

Why it may be interesting. The methodology of combining deterministic classical graph reduction with quantum optimization (QAOA) to tackle large, structured optimization problems is highly relevant for developing scalable quantum algorithms for complex physical constraints.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the NP-complete problem of finding the Maximum Independent Set (MIS) on a graph, framed initially by selecting compatible natural gas transportation contracts.

Main result. The developed quantum-classical hybrid solver achieved strong performance on benchmark instances, reporting an average approximation ratio of 0.996 on QOBLIB and successfully reducing industrially motivated graphs.

Method. It employs a hybrid quantum-classical framework that combines iterative classical graph reduction techniques with quantum optimization using the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA).

Model / system. The problem is formulated as an MIS problem, which is mapped to a Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) model and subsequently to an Ising Hamiltonian for execution on NISQ quantum hardware.

Key observables. Average approximation ratio (e.g., 0.996), ability to recover optimal solutions, and circuit metrics like two-qubit depth and CZ count.

Important parameters / regimes. NISQ hardware constraints, graph sizes up to 900 contracts, and QAOA parameters ($p$, $k_{swap}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The quantum workload is assumed to scale with qubit count rather than problem size, and the classical reduction process is guaranteed to preserve at least one maximum independent set.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the overall hybrid quantum-classical workflow, showing the alternation between classical reduction and hardware-aware QAOA execution.

Paper structure. The paper details the formulation of the MIS problem as a QUBO, describes the hybrid solver pipeline involving classical reduction rules and quantum optimization via QAOA, presents results on standard benchmarks (QOBLIB), and discusses hardware-aware circuit synthesis for superconducting processors.

Abstract

Selecting mutually compatible natural gas transportation contracts is a practically important optimization task in which operators must choose from many candidate agreements subject to temporal, infrastructural, and flow-related constraints. As the number of candidates grows, the resulting search space becomes difficult to explore exhaustively. We study a pairwise abstraction of this task, formulated as a Maximum Clique problem on a contract-compatibility graph, or equivalently as a Maximum Independent Set (MIS) problem on the complement graph. Building on recent work, this paper studies a quantum-classical framework for solving large-scale MIS instances within the limitations of noisy quantum hardware. The approach combines iterative classical graph reduction with quantum-guided optimization to progressively simplify the search space while maintaining high solution quality. This enables large candidate spaces to be reduced to smaller subproblems that are more suitable for execution on current quantum computers. We evaluate the approach on fifteen benchmark instances from the Quantum Optimization Benchmarking Library (QOBLIB), obtaining an average approximation ratio of 0.996 and recovering optimal solutions for fourteen instances, including graphs with up to 186 vertices. We further evaluate the algorithm on six synthetic pairwise contract-compatibility graphs containing up to 900 contracts, where the proposed method achieves an average approximation ratio of 0.989 and obtains optimal solutions in four cases. These experiments demonstrate the ability of the hybrid MIS solver to reduce industrially motivated graphs. The pairwise abstraction serves as the first step of a two-stage screening procedure that narrows the candidate contracts to a smaller set of mutually compatible ones, which can then be verified against pipeline-capacity constraints.

Towards unsupervised representation learning for quantum data: quantum models with inference and generation

Summary. This paper establishes a rigorous mathematical framework for unsupervised representation learning when the data consists of quantum states rather than classical measurements. It addresses the fundamental challenge posed by the lack of a standard factorization rule for quantum states. The work characterizes the resulting 'ambiguous states' and shows that physically realizable quantum correlations are constrained by entanglement criteria like the PPT condition.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a rigorous mathematical structure for applying modern ML techniques (like variational inference) to quantum data, which is crucial for developing quantum machine learning algorithms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops a mathematical framework to generalize unsupervised representation learning, concepts central to modern machine learning, to the quantum domain.

Main result. It characterizes the necessary structure of quantum models with inference and generation, showing that non-trivial models must involve non-linear maps, and links the existence of such models to the Positive Partial Transpose (PPT) criterion.

Method. The authors define quantum models using joint quantum states over visible and latent systems and employ State-over-Time (SOT) maps to generalize the factorization properties of classical probability distributions.

Model / system. The system involves quantum states (density operators) defined over visible and latent subsystems, which are assumed to be finite-dimensional. The core mathematical tools are SOT maps and the concept of 'ambiguous states' arising from factorization constraints.

Key observables. Ambiguous states, joint quantum states, and the positive-partial-transpose (PPT) criterion.

Important parameters / regimes. The structure of the SOT maps (e.g., Leifer-Spekkens construction) and the resulting correlation type (e.g., PPT states).

Assumptions / limitations. The framework assumes the existence of a suitable notion of factorisation (via SOT maps) to mimic the classical chain rule, and later proves that non-trivial models require non-linear maps.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the generic setup of classical representation learning, showing the relationship between inference, generation, and the latent/visible spaces.

Paper structure. The paper first contrasts the quantum challenge with classical ML, then develops the SOT map framework, analyzes specific SOT map types (Leifer-Spekkens, etc.), establishes a 'no-go' result requiring non-linearity, and finally characterizes ambiguous states in relation to PPT criteria.

Abstract

With quantum sensors, simulators and networks emerging, a future of quantum technology may produce quantum states as data---that is, coherently rather than as classical measurement records---thus motivating the study of suitable quantum generalisations of modern machine learning, including the automated, unsupervised extraction of useful representations. Two ingredients are central to the latter: inference, mapping observations to latent representations, and generation, mapping latent states back to synthetic data. Both are related to each other and to joint distributions for training models by the chain-rule of classical probability theory. The fact that quantum states however lack such universal, standard factorisation property thus poses a challenge. Here we develop a conceptual and mathematical framework for unsupervised representation learning from quantum data. Models are joint quantum states over visible and latent systems; state-over-time maps provide a notion of factorisation into a marginal state and inference (generation) channel; models with inference (generation) are ambiguous states---states for which such factorisation obtains---subject to a further consistency condition on extended inference maps as data extension. These stipulations are restrictive: we show that non-trivial models must feature non-linear such maps to the extended space. For three representative state-over-time maps, we completely characterise the ambiguous states, uncovering a hierarchy tied to the positive-partial-transpose (PPT) criterion from entanglement theory. Notably, the Leifer-Spekkens construction supports inference and generation exactly for model classes of PPT states, thus allowing genuinely quantum visible-latent correlations. We also formulate quantum counterparts of exact and approximate inference training, explore weaker notions of data extension and sketch a future research programme.

Abstract

Transport in certain systems fails when diffusion cannot replenish or remove material from a boundary rapidly enough, causing the boundary concentration to reach a critical minimum or maximum. Motivated by experiments in electrochemical systems that demonstrate this diffusion-limited failure of charge transport, we determine dynamic current protocols that maximize charge transfer subject to a prescribed concentration constraint. The optimal protocol has a "bang-ride" structure: the maximum feasible current is used until the boundary concentration reaches its critical value, after which the current is progressively reduced to maintain that value. For semi-infinite domains, we derive the optimal currents analytically and obtain system-independent upper bounds on their improvement over constant-current operation. We also extend the framework to finite and multilayer domains and show that the same formulation applies to both charging and discharging.

Summary. This paper reformulates higher-gradient gravity by deriving its field equations from the Second Law of Thermodynamics applied to a self-gravitating fluid. This thermodynamic approach successfully bypasses the Ostrogradsky instability inherent in standard Lagrangian formulations. The resulting theory is ghost-free and provides novel physical identities, linking gravity to fundamental thermodynamic principles.

Why it may be interesting. The use of entropy principles and gradient flow dynamics to stabilize a classical field theory is a powerful analogy to non-equilibrium statistical mechanics, which is central to understanding open quantum systems and dissipation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Higher-gradient modifications of Newtonian gravity, while removing point-mass singularities, suffer from the Ostrogradsky instability when treated via standard Lagrangian dynamics.

Main result. The field equations can be derived from the Second Law of Thermodynamics applied to a self-gravitating fluid, yielding a ghost-free, first-order relaxation equation that naturally incorporates Newton's law and dissipation-free couplings.

Method. The authors derive the field equations by treating the potential as a thermodynamic variable and enforcing the local entropy balance ($\sigma_s \ge 0$), leading to a gradient flow description.

Model / system. The model is a modification of Newtonian gravity involving higher-gradient terms, analyzed through the lens of non-equilibrium thermodynamics applied to a self-gravitating fluid.

Key observables. The energy current, pressure tensor, and the stability condition derived from the entropy density (concavity).

Important parameters / regimes. The amplitude sum rule $\sum \alpha_i = -1$ constrains the internal length scale below $4 imes 10^{-5}$ m.

Assumptions / limitations. The derivation assumes the system is in mechanical equilibrium ($\Pi = 0$) for simplified results, and the dynamics are governed by a first-order relaxation equation rather than a standard action principle.

Paper structure. The paper first identifies the instability issue in higher-gradient gravity. It then reformulates the dynamics using the Second Law of Thermodynamics, showing the resulting equations are equivalent to the stationary limit of a first-order relaxation equation. Finally, it analyzes the constraints imposed by this thermodynamic structure on the theory's parameters.

Abstract

Higher-gradient modifications of Newtonian gravity remove the point-mass singularity, but their Lagrangian dynamics is haunted by the Ostrogradsky instability. We show that the same field equations follow from the Second Law of thermodynamics applied to a self-gravitating fluid with a weakly nonlocal state space, and that on this route the instability is absent by construction: the potential obeys a first-order relaxation equation that is a gradient flow, whose Lyapunov function is the entropy density, and whose relaxation spectrum is negative definite at every wavenumber precisely when the entropy is concave. The derivation also yields the pressure tensor and the energy current, an exact identity that reproduces Newton's force law without further input, and a dissipation-free cross-coupling to the bulk viscous pressure that no variational principle can produce. Regularity constrains the Yukawa-type corrections by an amplitude sum rule, $\sum_iα_i=-1$, so that laboratory tests of the inverse-square law bound the largest internal length below $4\times10^{-5}$~m, and identify the complex-root regime as a classically consistent Lee--Wick-like sector.

Summary. This paper analyzes a 1D fractional U(1) Ginzburg-Landau model to study its phase transition behavior. Using stochastic dynamics, the authors show that while the correlation scaling mimics the BKT transition, the system lacks the characteristic jump in the helicity modulus seen in 2D systems. This highlights how long-range interactions fundamentally alter the nature of phase transitions.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a theoretical framework for understanding how long-range interactions (fractional Laplacian) modify classic critical phenomena like the BKT transition, offering insights applicable to systems with non-local dynamics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the nature of phase transitions, specifically BKT-like correlation scaling, in a one-dimensional fractional U(1) Ginzburg-Landau model.

Main result. The transition exhibits BKT-like behavior in correlation scaling, evidenced by finite-size scaling collapse, but it notably lacks the universal jump in the helicity modulus characteristic of the 2D XY model.

Method. The authors employ stochastic Gross-Pitaevskii dynamics to sample the equilibrium Gibbs measure and analyze various correlation functions and twist responses.

Model / system. The system is a one-dimensional complex field model governed by a fractional U(1) Ginzburg-Landau Hamiltonian, featuring a nonlocal quadratic term with Fourier multiplier |k|^sigma, focusing on the marginal case sigma=1.

Key observables. Correlation function C(r), Binder ratios, effective exponents, and spectral twist responses.

Important parameters / regimes. The critical exponent sigma, the temperature T, and the system size L, with a focus on the marginal case sigma=1.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on defining a spectral twist response due to the nonlocal operator, and it contrasts its findings with the known universal jump of the 2D XY model.

Figures summary. Not specified, but the analysis involves showing size-independent ratios at low T and collapse using a BKT-type variable.

Abstract

We study a one-dimensional fractional $U(1)$ Ginzburg--Landau model whose quadratic part has Fourier multiplier $|k|^σ$, focusing on the marginal case $σ=1$. This dispersion yields logarithmic spin-wave fluctuations, suggesting BKT-like behavior despite the one-dimensional setting. We sample the equilibrium Gibbs measure using stochastic Gross--Pitaevskii dynamics and analyze correlation functions, dimensionless ratios, effective exponents, and twist responses. The correlation function shows a low-temperature algebraic branch with a temperature-dependent exponent, while the high-temperature regime exhibits a nonlocal-kernel-induced tail consistent with $C(r)\sim r^{-2}$. The correlation and Binder ratios are nearly size independent at low temperature and collapse with the BKT-type variable $(T-T_{\rm BKT})(\log L)^2$; finite-size effects set in around $T\simeq0.35\text{--}0.4$, consistent with $T_{\rm BKT}\simeq0.35$. Unlike the two-dimensional XY model, twist responses do not yield a finite helicity modulus: the ordinary linear-response quantity grows with system size, whereas the cusp twist response scales as $L^{-η(T)}$, like the squared zero-mode order parameter. Thus, the transition is BKT-like in correlation scaling, but lacks a universal helicity-modulus jump.

Abstract

We analytically characterize entanglement generation by two paradigmatic coherently controlled quantum processes, the quantum switch and time-flip. Retaining rather than measuring or discarding the control, we treat the control and target as the bipartite system and assume pure product inputs, so that any output entanglement reflects the entangling capability of the process. For the switch of qubit unitaries, we derive exact expressions for entanglement, together with a geometric characterization and a coherence-entanglement conservation relation. We then extend our analysis to binary random unitary, Pauli, amplitude damping, and generalized amplitude damping channels. We prove that switch of channels commuting under composition cannot entangle a separable input. Yet maximal entanglement is possible even with dissipation, e.g., an entanglement-breaking damping channel switched with a bit-flip can transform a product input into a maximally entangled state. For two generalized amplitude damping channels, a common stationary state prevents entanglement, while different stationary populations can enable it. We present an analogous study for the time-flip of unitary and binary random unitary channels. Our findings provide a systematic analysis of how much entanglement can be generated by coherent control of channel order or input-output direction.

Summary. This paper develops a general theory to compare the performance of various quantum Otto engines by factoring the power into a work term and an implementation-specific time scale. It demonstrates that the maximum operating speed is fundamentally limited by the thermalization times of the hot and cold baths, regardless of the specific cycle used.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, general framework for comparing non-equilibrium quantum processes, showing how fundamental thermodynamic limits constrain the speed achievable by different physical implementations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to systematically compare the operational speed (power output) of different quantum realizations of the Otto engine, despite them achieving the same thermodynamic efficiency.

Main result. The power of any quantum Otto engine can be factorized into a characteristic work (dependent on internal frequencies) and an implementation-specific characteristic time. The maximum speed is universally limited by the sum of the hot and cold bath thermalization times.

Method. The analysis uses a factorization approach ($P = W/T^*$) to separate thermodynamic and dynamical aspects, comparing four distinct dynamical realizations (e.g., four-stroke, always-on, Floquet-modulated).

Model / system. The system is a quantum heat engine, modeled using qubits coupled to thermal baths, analyzed via master equations and Hamiltonian dynamics for various cycle implementations.

Key observables. Power output ($P$), characteristic work ($W$), characteristic time ($T^*$), and efficiency ($\eta$).

Important parameters / regimes. Coupling strengths ($ au, \Omega$), internal frequencies ($\omega$), and dimensionless ratios characterizing the cycle dynamics ($s_{LT}, s_F, s_\pi$).

Assumptions / limitations. The core assumption is the factorization $P = W/T^*$, allowing the comparison to focus solely on the characteristic time $T^*$. Various approximations are used depending on the cycle (e.g., slow modulation, fast oscillation limit).

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the four-stroke model and the Floquet-inspired model for a single qubit, and the two-qubit realization, comparing power scaling in different regimes.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the general framework by factorizing power, then analyzes specific dynamical realizations (four-stroke, Floquet, always-on, on-off) by deriving their respective characteristic times and asymptotic scaling laws.

Abstract

A general thermal machine is characterized by distinct time or energy scales: temperature, coupling to the heat baths, internal free dynamics and interactions, and external driving. We address the question: what parameters determine the speed of a given engine, and how can they be used to reliably compare different types of engine? Specifically, we consider four realizations of the Otto engine, each operating with the same Otto efficiency, and aim to characterize and compare their power outputs. The main insight of this paper is that, irrespective of their very different dynamical implementations, the power of each engine can be factorized into a common characteristic work and an implementation-specific characteristic time. The characteristic work depends only on the internal frequencies of the machine and the temperatures, whereas the characteristic time depends on the coupling strength to the baths and on the implementation-specific driving. This observation allows us to compare the power of different engines through their characteristic times and thereby reduce the relevant parameter space to parameters that capture the dynamical aspects of engine performance. Despite different dynamical implementations, this framework reveals simple common features. In all cases, the maximum operating speed is limited by a common timescale given by the sum of the characteristic thermalization times of the hot and cold baths. Moreover, the four engines fall into two distinct asymptotic classes: implementations in which driving and thermalization occur simultaneously exhibit quadratic scaling, whereas those in which work extraction and thermalization alternate exhibit linear scaling. These results expose general dynamical features that determine the operational speed of quantum Otto engines.

Abstract

Point defects have been successfully utilized in various quantum technologies, serving as quantum qubits for quantum computation, single-photon emitters for quantum communication, and nanoscale sensors for quantum metrology. However, their further development faces key challenges, particularly in discovering and exploring suitable defect-host systems that meet the necessary criteria for quantum applications. Here, using polarization-resolved Raman spectroscopy and terahertz absorption spectroscopy, we discover three distinct chromium-vacancy-induced excitations in the uniaxial antiferromagnetic insulator, Cr$_2$O$_3$. These vacancy-induced excitations have an energy scale of a few tens of millielectronvolts and are twofold degenerate, and the lowest one at 64 $cm^{-1}$ is sharp and sensitive to the external magnetic field along the easy-axis direction, particularly close to the spin-flop regime around 6T, where the mode softens from 64 to 27$cm^{-1}$. Based on the defect supercell first-principles calculations, we interpret the mode at 64 $cm^{-1}$ as a local magnetic excitation of the local moment within the chromium vacancy state. Our results establish that the magnetic defect states in Cr$_2$O$_3$ have potential for quantum applications.

Abstract

Quantum secret sharing (QSS) is a process in which the state of a quantum system is partitioned into multiple shares, allowing only specific subsets of shareholders to reconstruct the state, while others gain no information about its identity. In this work, we propose a variant of QSS, called ``helper QSS,'' which designates a special shareholder whose participation with any other group of parties is sufficient for recovering the state. We present different families of helper codes that function for an arbitrary number of parties and system sizes. As an application, we introduce the idea of a programmable access structure in QSS, which allows for a third-party programmer to independently choose the access structure of the code even after the shares are delivered to all the shareholders. This choice is made blindly, meaning that the programmer has no information about the secret being encoded, and it is implemented using the nonlocal process of quantum steering.
