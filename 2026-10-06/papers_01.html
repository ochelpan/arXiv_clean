Summary. This paper introduces a novel, efficient perfect-sampling algorithm to calculate the Magic Rényi Entropy ($ ext{M}_2 ext{RE}$), a resource measure quantifying fermionic non-Gaussianity. By operating directly on Matrix Product States, the method achieves polynomial scaling ($\mathcal{O}(nD^3)$) with system size. This allows researchers to quantitatively probe higher-order quantum correlations in fermionic systems like the XXZ chain, which are inaccessible to standard covariance-based measures.

Why it may be interesting. The development of a polynomial-time sampling technique for higher-order correlation measures like $ ext{M}_2 ext{RE}$ is crucial for simulating complex quantum many-body physics that goes beyond simple quadratic Hamiltonians.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is developing an efficient computational method to calculate the second-order Magic Rényi Entropy ($ ext{M}_2 ext{RE}$) for fermionic non-Gaussianity, as exact evaluation scales exponentially with system size.

Main result. The authors developed a perfect-sampling method that calculates $ ext{M}_2 ext{RE}$ in polynomial time ($\mathcal{O}(nD^3)$), successfully demonstrating that this measure captures higher-order correlations inaccessible to simpler covariance-based metrics.

Method. The method employs a perfect-sampling technique using a bounded estimator and a recursive sweep to sample directly from Matrix Product States (MPSs), avoiding the explicit construction of replicated tensor networks.

Model / system. The study focuses on many-body fermionic systems, benchmarked on the XXZ chain of spinful fermions, which are represented using a right-canonical MPS formalism.

Key observables. Fermionic non-Gaussianity, quantified by the $ ext{M}_2 ext{RE} = -\ln u_2$, and the purity $ u_2$ of the convolved state.

Important parameters / regimes. System size ($L$), MPS bond dimension ($D$), and the anisotropy parameter ($\Delta$) of the XXZ chain.

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes the input state is represented by a right-canonical MPS and utilizes a bounded estimator to control sampling fluctuations.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the MRE scaling for the XXZ chain, showing agreement with known analytical results for $\Delta=-1$ and saturation behavior for $\Delta<-1$. Other figures detail the structure of the sampling sweep using boundary matrices.

Paper structure. The paper develops the perfect-sampling technique for $ ext{M}_2 ext{RE}$ calculation, benchmarks it on the XXZ chain to confirm polynomial scaling and physical accuracy, and analyzes the saturation of the non-Gaussianity measure in different regimes.

Abstract

Fermionic non-Gaussianity is a key resource for universal quantum computation, and its behavior in quantum many-body systems has attracted growing interest. Recently, a convolution-based measure, which we call the magic Rényi entropy (MRE), has been proposed as a resource measure of non-Gaussianity, but its evaluation is limited to small systems due to the computational cost growing exponentially with system size. In this work, we develop a perfect-sampling method to calculate the second-order MRE from matrix product states (MPSs). Our method uses a bounded estimator to control sampling fluctuations and a recursive sweep to draw the samples directly from the input MPS, which allows us to generate each sample with $\mathcal O(nD^3)$ time, where $n$ and $D$ denote the number of modes and MPS bond dimension, respectively. We benchmark our method on the XXZ chain with up to $128$ sites, demonstrating that the MRE captures intricate higher-order correlations at large scales, which are inaccessible to covariance-based measures. These results provide a computational tool that quantitatively evaluates many-body fermionic non-Gaussianity by accounting for higher-order correlations.

Abstract

Non-Clifford gates are essential for avoiding classical simulability under the Gottesman-Knill theorem. An error-corrected circuit with non-Clifford gates will therefore be an important element for realizing useful fault-tolerant quantum algorithms. However, accurately understanding the fault-tolerant performance of such circuits faces challenges due to the classical hardness of simulating these circuits. We avoid simulating logical measurements and develop exact and efficient classical methods for benchmarking circuits composed of X, CNOT, and diagonal gates in the Clifford hierarchy, including state and gate teleportation gadgets with feedforward. For circuits with third-level diagonal gates, our benchmarking algorithm efficiently samples syndrome statistics and estimates the output logical fidelity and expectation values of certain Clifford observables, including all Pauli observables. The evaluation of syndrome expectation values also extends to circuits with fourth-level diagonal gates. Our algorithm scales polynomially in the number of physical qubits n and circuit depth T, independent of the number of logical qubits. The benchmarking applications include IQP sampling and magic state preparation/cultivation. We demonstrate the practical utility of our simulation algorithms by using them to optimize high-rate magic state distillation factories based on three copies of a [[27,3,3]] tricycle code.

Summary. This work analyzes the 'infrared memory' left in a fermionic system by a localized, time-varying defect (Majorana shutter). By examining multiple observables—including entanglement entropy and quasiparticle number—the authors demonstrate that the low-frequency physics is governed by distinct coefficients, proving that global and local measures of quantum memory are not mathematically equivalent.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a rigorous framework for distinguishing between different types of quantum memory effects—global vs. local—in strongly correlated, non-equilibrium fermionic systems, which is crucial for understanding quantum quenches.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the 'infrared memory' imprinted on a quantum system by a local, time-dependent scattering defect modeled as a Majorana shutter.

Main result. The analysis establishes that global infrared divergences (like in quasiparticle number) and local spatial entropy divergences are controlled by distinct, though related, low-frequency scattering data, showing they are not interchangeable.

Method. The authors use analytical techniques involving the scattering matrix, Bogoliubov kernels, and lattice simulations to probe the system's response to time-dependent perturbations.

Model / system. The system is a 1D fermionic chain described by massless Majorana fields, perturbed by a smooth, finite-width coupling acting as the 'Majorana shutter' defect.

Key observables. Overlap of Gaussian vacua (Anderson orthogonality), post-quench quasiparticle number (logarithmic growth), and entanglement entropy (central-charge description).

Important parameters / regimes. The finite width ($a$) and the switching time ($ au$) define the memory parameter ($\epsilon_{ ext{mem}} = a/ au$), and the scattering angle ($ heta_\lambda$) controls the zero-frequency scattering matrix.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis often relies on the zero-memory approximation ($\epsilon_{ ext{mem}} \ll 1$) and treats the system using continuum field theory methods.

Figures summary. The notes do not provide a summary of figures, but they detail the quantitative results for various observables like the overlap exponent, the logarithmic coefficient, and the entropy scaling.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the universality of the zero-frequency scattering matrix, then analyzing the time-dependent Bogoliubov kernel, and finally comparing the resulting divergences across global (quasiparticle number) and local (entropy) observables.

Abstract

We study a finite-width Majorana shutter as a local, time-dependent scattering defect and determine which features of its infrared memory are insensitive to the microscopic spatial profile. For a smooth shutter, the zero-frequency scattering matrix is fixed by the integrated coupling, while finite-width corrections vanish in the long-wavelength limit. A time-dependent switch then produces an infrared Bogoliubov kernel whose singular part is controlled by the change in this scattering matrix. We examine the many-body response through three observables. The overlap of the pre- and post-shutter Gaussian vacua exhibits Anderson orthogonality, with an exponent set by the scattering angle. A lattice calculation of the post-quench quasiparticle number resolves its logarithmic infrared growth, reaching the predicted coefficient in the slow-switch test and reproducing the expected dependence on the scattering-angle change. When the entanglement cut passes through the shutter, the entropy follows the effective-central-charge description of a conformal defect, with parameter-free agreement at the percent level, improving for the wider shutter. When the shutter lies inside the interval, its contribution remains a subleading finite-size correction. A local bilinear correlator approaches the expected Majorana power law and shows no stable logarithmic amplitude. These results show that the same low-frequency scattering data control distinct global and spatial observables, while their infrared coefficients are not interchangeable. The remaining limits are also made explicit: the finite-memory correction to the continuum kernel and the controlled continuum limit of large-interval spatial entropy.

Summary. This paper analyzes the period shift of the Kohn oscillation in a Bose-Einstein condensate trapped in a harmonic potential modified by gain and loss (PT-symmetry). The authors show that interactions cause the oscillation period to deviate from the non-interacting value, predicting the shift scales quadratically with gain at small strengths. They use advanced numerical methods to track these periodic orbits up to resonance points.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses non-Hermitian physics in the context of ultracold atoms, showing how gain/loss terms fundamentally alter coherent dynamics like the Kohn oscillation, which is crucial for understanding open quantum systems in atomic traps.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how interatomic interactions modify the period of the Kohn oscillation in a Bose-Einstein condensate confined in a harmonic trap subjected to a parity-time-symmetric gain-loss potential.

Main result. The period shift is found to be non-zero and proportional to the square of the gain strength ($\gamma^2$) at small gains, and the analysis determines scaling exponents for the critical gain where resonance occurs.

Method. The research employs the Gross-Pitaevskii equation, utilizing moment models, Ehrenfest theorems, and advanced numerical techniques like pseudo-arclength continuation to track periodic orbits.

Model / system. The system is a Bose-Einstein condensate (BEC) governed by a GP equation incorporating a harmonic trap, repulsive interactions ($g|\psi|^2$), and a gain-loss term ($\propto \gamma x$), making the potential PT-symmetric.

Key observables. Period of oscillation ($T$), cloud width, center of mass position ($X(t)$), and the critical gain ($\gamma_c$) where resonance occurs.

Important parameters / regimes. Interaction strength ($g$), gain strength ($\gamma$), trap frequency ($\omega$), and the initial ground-state width ($\sigma_0$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on approximations such as fixed profile shape in the moment model, and the use of continuation methods near resonance points.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the time evolution of density and COM position for $\gamma=0$, show the period $T$ vs. $\gamma$ confirming quadratic growth, and compare results from different numerical domains/tapering schemes.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from the linear (non-interacting) case to the interacting case, deriving analytical approximations for small gain, developing moment equations, and finally using advanced numerical continuation techniques to map out the periodic orbits up to critical resonance points.

Abstract

The center of mass of a Bose--Einstein condensate in a harmonic trap oscillates at the trap frequency independently of the interactions. Without interactions a parity-time-symmetric gain-loss gradient leaves this frequency unchanged, and a cloud of the ground-state width is translated rigidly while the atom number is periodically modulated. With interactions the protection is lost, because the gain and the loss change the number of atoms; the modulated atom number drives the width of the cloud, and the width acts back on the center of mass. At small gain the period grows quadratically with the gain strength, with a coefficient that in the Thomas--Fermi regime is set by the size of the cloud alone. The family of periodic orbits continued from the Kohn oscillation can be followed up to a critical gain, where it comes into resonance with highly excited states of the trap, and over the computed families this critical gain decreases with the amplitude of the oscillation and with the size of the cloud. A five-variable moment model reproduces the period and the width of the cloud along the orbits nearly up to the critical gain.

Abstract
