Persistence of kinks in the NLS equation with competing nonlinearities

Authors: P. G. Kevrekidis, D. E. Pelinovsky
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.02541v1

Summary. This work analyzes the persistence and stability of kink solutions in a cubic-quintic NLS equation subjected to an external potential. Using advanced mathematical techniques like Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction, the authors establish criteria based on an effective potential to predict if a kink will be stable or unstable. The results confirm that the pinning mechanism is robust and provides detailed insights into the spectral properties of the perturbed system.

Why it may be interesting. The study of nonlinear wave dynamics, particularly the stability and pinning of topological defects like kinks, is highly relevant to nonlinear optics, Bose-Einstein condensates (BECs), and nonlinear wave propagation in various physical systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how kinks (solitary wave solutions) in the Nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation persist when translational invariance is broken by an external potential.

Main result. The persistence of kinks is governed by the roots of an effective potential, and the stability of the pinned state is determined by the sign of the derivative of this effective potential at the root.

Method. The analysis employs the Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction method, perturbation theory to analyze eigenvalue splitting, and comparison with numerical continuation and direct dynamical simulations.

Model / system. The model is the NLS equation with competing focusing cubic and defocusing quintic nonlinearities, perturbed by an external potential V(x). Stationary solutions are sought in the form of kinks.

Key observables. Effective potential V_eff(s), the sign of V'eff(s_0), and the eigenvalues mu+ and lambda characterizing the stability.

Important parameters / regimes. The small perturbation parameter epsilon (ε) controlling the strength of the external potential V(x).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the validity of the Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction and asymptotic expansions for the eigenvalues, though some long-term stability aspects remain open.

Figures summary. Figure 6 illustrates the dynamical evolution of an unstable kink, showing it emitting radiation and subsequently settling near a stable pinned state.

Paper structure. The paper follows a structure moving from the analytical derivation of the pinning mechanism via V_eff, to the spectral stability analysis determining unstable eigenvalues, and concluding with numerical corroboration of these analytical predictions.

Abstract

External potentials break the translational invariance of nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equations and generally convert a continuous family of standing wave solutions into isolated pinned states. We study this mechanism for the prototypical case of a kink in the NLS equation with competing focusing cubic and defocusing quintic nonlinearities. The Lyapunov--Schmidt reduction method is used to prove that the location $s_0$ of the pinned kinks is selected near roots of an effective potential $V_{\rm eff}(s)$. We then determine how the translational zero eigenvalue of the Jacobian operator splits under the perturbation and prove that the sign of $V_{\rm eff}'(s_0)$ distinguishes eigendirections of positive and negative energy. We further show that if the Jacobian operator admits a negative eigenvalue, then the stability problem contains a real unstable eigenvalue. The derivation of the asymptotic expansion for the unstable eigenvalue is nonstandard because the zero eigenvalue is embedded in the continuous spectrum of the linearized operator. Finally, we show that the persistence of kinks lacks parity symmetry even if the external potential is odd. Numerical continuation, spectral computations with domains adapted to the slowly decaying eigenfunctions, and direct dynamical simulations of the time evolution corroborate the analytical predictions for different representative examples.

Summary. This work develops a continuum PDE model to describe how bacterial clusters form and move under directed forces like chemotaxis. By generalizing previous models, the authors provide analytical solutions for the cluster's speed and map out stability regimes, showing excellent agreement between discrete simulations and the derived PDE theory.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on biology, the mathematical framework—deriving complex nonlinear PDEs from discrete stochastic processes and analyzing traveling wave solutions—is highly relevant to non-equilibrium statistical mechanics and pattern formation in complex systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper models the aggregation of bacteria into clusters when subjected to directed motion, such as chemotaxis or fluid transport.

Main result. The authors derived a fourth-order nonlinear PDE that accurately describes the cluster density profile and group velocity, identifying distinct regimes for cluster existence and speed.

Method. The continuum limit was derived from stochastic Agent-Based Models (ABMs) using Taylor expansion, leading to a generalized nonlinear PDE solved via a traveling pulse ansatz.

Model / system. The system models bacterial aggregation using two variants: Model I (intrinsic directed motion $v$) and Model II (implicit directional bias $r$). The dynamics are captured by a fourth-order PDE.

Key observables. Cluster density profile, group velocity (speed of the travelling cluster), and stability regimes (e.g., bistability or 'gap' regions).

Important parameters / regimes. Directed motion parameters ($v$ or $r$), aggregation strength ($b$), and diffusion coefficients ($\alpha, eta, \gamma$ in the PDE).

Assumptions / limitations. The derivation relies on the assumption of a small bin spacing ($h o 0$) for the PDE limit, and the analysis uses traveling wave ansatz solutions.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the transition of the cluster state as velocity increases (localized $ o$ homogeneous) and show bifurcation diagrams revealing 'gap' and bistability windows for the two models.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by establishing the ABM foundation, deriving the continuum PDE limit, and then analyzing the resulting PDE using traveling wave solutions to determine the cluster speed and stability boundaries for both motion models.

Abstract

We model directed motion of cells that aggregate into a cluster in the presence of chemotaxis or fluid transport. Starting from a stochastic agent-based model (ABM) of Galante \& Levy (2013), Chavy-Waddy \& Kolokolnikov (2016), we consider two model variants, with (I) directed motion of individuals, or (II) implicit individual bias in one direction. In both cases, we derive fourth order nonlinear PDEs representing the continuum limits of the models as a generalization of Chavy-Waddy \& Kolokolnikov (2016). An exact analytic solution then describes cluster density profile as well as the group velocity, and we identify the regimes where the travelling cluster exists. Excellent agreement is observed between the discrete models and the exact solutions of the PDEs in both models, including the existence and speed of the travelling cluster.

Summary. This theoretical study analyzes how doping the moiré superlattice of twisted bilayer graphene (tBG) modifies the optical fingerprint of a coupled WSe2 monolayer. The research shows that doping generates a periodic potential that strongly influences the exciton's energy levels, leading to observable satellite resonances. This establishes the doped tBG as a powerful tool for optically engineering excitonic states in adjacent 2D semiconductors.

Why it may be interesting. The work directly addresses how external tuning (electrostatic doping) can engineer the optical properties of strongly correlated excitonic states in a moiré heterostructure, which is a key area in correlated materials physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how electrostatic doping within the moiré superlattice of twisted bilayer graphene (tBG) controls the optical absorption spectrum of a coupled WSe2 monolayer.

Main result. Electrostatic doping induces pronounced modifications in the excitonic absorption spectrum, notably causing the emergence of multiple satellite resonances due to the interplay between exciton degrees of freedom and doping-induced hybridization.

Method. The authors employed a detailed theoretical model solving the exciton Hamiltonian, incorporating the spatially modulated electrostatic potential derived from the doped moiré superlattice.

Model / system. The system is a WSe2 monolayer stacked on tBG, forming a moiré superlattice. The exciton is governed by a Hamiltonian that includes terms for kinetic energy, Coulomb interaction, and the periodic potential induced by the doped tBG.

Key observables. Excitonic absorption spectrum, multiple satellite resonances, energy shift ($\Delta E_m$), and characteristic filling factor ($\delta u$).

Important parameters / regimes. Twist angles below the magic angle ($ heta_t < 1.1^\circ$), moiré filling factor ($ u$), and carrier density ($\approx 4 imes 10^{12} ext{ cm}^{-2}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The model uses the screened Coulomb interaction and treats the moiré potential as an effective mean-field periodic potential, neglecting fine-structure effects like Berry curvature contributions.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the schematic of the heterostructure, the mechanism of potential generation via doping, and the resulting spectral features and energy shifts as a function of doping/filling factor.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the physical system and the Hamiltonian, details the theoretical methodology for calculating the exciton response to the moiré potential, presents the key results regarding spectral modifications, and concludes by establishing the tunability of excitonic states.

Abstract

We theoretically investigate the optical response of the WSe2 monolayer vertically stacked on twisted bilayer graphene (tBG) under electrostatic doping. In this heterostructure, the doped moiré superlattice of tBG generates a spatially modulated electrostatic potential that couples to the electron and hole constituents of the WSe2 exciton through the screened Coulomb interaction, thereby providing a microscopic mechanism for exciton coupling to the charge-density landscape of the moiré Fermi sea. Focusing on twist angles below the magic angle (1.1°), where electronic correlations are strongly enhanced, we demonstrate that electrostatic doping induces pronounced modifications of the excitonic absorption spectrum, including the emergence of multiple satellite resonances. We show that these spectral features originate from the interplay between the exciton center-of-mass and internal degrees of freedom, together with doping-induced hybridization between Rydberg exciton states of different orbital symmetry. The calculated spectra are in good agreement with previously reported experimental observations and theoretical predictions. Our results establish electrostatic doping in tBG as an effective means of engineering excitonic states in adjacent semiconducting monolayers and provide a pathway toward novel optical sensor devices.

Summary. This theoretical work explores enhancing superconductivity in a Fermi-Bose mixture by coupling electrons to a dilute Bose gas. By analyzing density fluctuations using RPA, the authors demonstrate that the resulting attraction can lead to an algebraic enhancement of $T_c$ rather than the typical exponential BCS dependence. This suggests a powerful mechanism for designing novel superconducting materials.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a concrete theoretical pathway to engineer high-$T_c$ superconductors by leveraging quantum critical fluctuations from bosonic baths, which is highly relevant to modern condensed matter research.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates mechanisms to enhance superconducting transition temperatures ($T_c$) by coupling fermions (electrons) to a bosonic gas (e.g., excitons or dilute bosons).

Main result. The coupling can lead to an effective attraction that results in an algebraic dependence for $T_c$ ($T_c \propto g$), significantly surpassing the exponential dependence found in standard BCS theory.

Method. The analysis employs the Random Phase Approximation (RPA) and utilizes renormalization group (RG) techniques to analyze the flow of pairing channels and calculate the fermion self-energy.

Model / system. The model is a two-dimensional system consisting of a metal (fermions) coupled to an uncondensed Bose gas with repulsive Bose-Fermi interactions. The analysis covers both deeply degenerate and non-degenerate temperature regimes.

Key observables. Superconducting transition temperature ($T_c$), fermion self-energy ($\Sigma(i\omega)$), and the effective pairing strength ($g$).

Important parameters / regimes. Boson density (acts as a bottleneck), interaction coupling constant ($\lambda$), and the ratio of energy scales ($\Lambda$ vs $\Lambda'$).

Assumptions / limitations. The use of RPA, the validity of the T-matrix approximation for the boson self-energy, and the assumption that long-range density fluctuations drive the pairing enhancement.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the model system: dense electrons interacting with dilute excitons.

Paper structure. The paper builds from establishing the general model Hamiltonian and the standard BCS comparison, moves through detailed calculations of the boson polarization function ($\Pi_b$) in different temperature regimes, and culminates in deriving the enhanced $T_c$ via the self-energy calculation.

Abstract

We couple a metal to an uncondensed bose gas with repulsive bose-fermi interactions in two dimensions. Within the random phase approximation, we show that the density fluctuations of the bosons, which mediate the effective fermion-fermion attraction, become long ranged on appropriate tuning of interactions. This leads to parametrically enhanced superconducting temperatures $T_c \propto g$ as opposed to $T_c \propto \exp(-1/g)$ for the BCS case. The dilute bose gas density acts as the primary bottleneck to the enhancement. We discuss the conceptual difficulties of implementing this idea in electron-exciton mixtures.

Summary. This work tackles the difficult inverse problem of determining the constitutive force driving phase transitions from noisy Allen-Cahn simulation movies. It introduces noise-adjusted moment equations and a specialized transformer (SCCT) to correct for various sources of measurement bias. The results demonstrate that the method accurately recovers the underlying physics, even distinguishing forces based on fine-scale interface details.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical phase transitions, the rigorous treatment of noise, bias correction, and inverse problem solving using moment methods and advanced regularization techniques is highly relevant to reconstructing quantum dynamics from noisy measurements.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenging inverse problem of inferring the unknown constitutive force governing a phase-field evolution from noisy, time-series image data.

Main result. The developed noise-adjusted moment equations and the SCCT successfully infer the force, showing that accurate estimation of the underlying equation structure is more critical than increasing regularization flexibility.

Method. The methodology involves developing observation-aware moment equations to correct for nonlinear observation noise, instrument coupling, and temporal biases, which are then used in a constrained Bernstein inverse for force reconstruction.

Model / system. The system is governed by the Allen-Cahn dynamics, which describes the evolution of an order parameter $u$ as an $L^2$ gradient flow of an energy functional. The force $G(u)$ is parameterized within an admissible family.

Key observables. Noisy phase observations ($y_{n,i}$), topological invariants ($b_0, b_1, b_2$), and the inferred constitutive force $G(u)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The surface tension ($\sigma$) sets the energy scale, and the force $G(u)$ is parameterized by five coefficients ($c_j$).

Assumptions / limitations. The noise is assumed to be i.i.d. Gaussian for bias correction, and the method requires separating the force inference from the external energy scale.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the method hierarchy, detailing how Hermite polynomials and paired views correct distinct noise effects, while the observation source dictates the temporal balance choice.

Paper structure. The paper builds logically: Section 2 establishes the identifiability of the force; Section 3 develops the noise-adjusted moment equations and the constrained inverse; and Section 4 validates the entire pipeline using synthetic and physical test cases.

Abstract

Inferring an Allen-Cahn constitutive force from images requires more than an accurate optimizer: nonlinear observation noise alters the mean of force features, shared noise couples instruments to the response, and a temporal balance borrowed from another solver can remain biased even on clean data. We develop observation-aware, noise-adjusted moment equations that address these effects before inversion. The surface-tension-calibrated constitutive transformer (SCCT) couples the resulting moments to a positive Bernstein inverse and can adapt its prior and precision from images and moments. A conditional stability bound separates moment residuals from prior error. Synthetic tests show that correcting the estimating equation matters more than increasing regularizer flexibility; the benefit of learned adaptation is smaller and depends on the training comparison. Forces inferred from planar movies predict unseen planar and three-dimensional evolutions. A ring-cage breakup shows why diffuse-interface accuracy distinguishes forces even when their topological predictions agree, while external surface tension sets the energy scale separately.

Summary. This work reports on magnetotransport measurements in twisted MoTe2, revealing that fractional hole-filling states behave as generalized Wigner crystals. The key finding is the stark contrast in how the resistance responds to a perpendicular magnetic field at different fillings, suggesting that the underlying charge configuration dictates the magnetic lattice and transport properties.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides experimental evidence of distinct correlated insulating/charge-ordered states whose magnetic response is highly sensitive to the specific fractional filling, offering a playground for studying magnetic control in correlated electronic systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the magnetotransport response of fractional hole-filling states in a twisted MoTe2 homobilayer, focusing on the interplay between magnetism and charge order.

Main result. The system exhibits distinct, contrasting magnetic responses at different fractional fillings ($ u = -2/3$ vs. $ u = -1/3$), which are interpreted as signatures of generalized Wigner crystals with different underlying magnetic lattices.

Method. The research combines low-temperature magnetotransport measurements (measuring $R_{XX}$ and $R_{XY}$) with theoretical modeling using Hartree-Fock and exact-diagonalization techniques.

Model / system. The physical platform is a twisted MoTe2 homobilayer, which forms a moiré superlattice. The low-energy physics is modeled by carriers on an effective triangular lattice, exhibiting correlated electron behavior at fractional fillings.

Key observables. Longitudinal and transverse resistance ($R_{XX}, R_{XY}$), resistance peaks at fractional fillings ($ u = -2/3, -1/3, -1/2$), and the shift of these peaks with perpendicular magnetic field ($H$).

Important parameters / regimes. Fractional filling factors ($ u$), perpendicular magnetic field ($H$), and perpendicular displacement field ($D$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the system is in the layer-polarized regime ($|D| > D_C$) and interprets the observed phenomena through the lens of generalized Wigner crystal formation.

Figures summary. Figures show the evolution of resistance peaks with $H$ at different fillings, demonstrating opposite magnetic responses at $ u = -2/3$ and $ u = -1/3$, and quadratic shifts/anisotropy at $ u = -1/2$.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by presenting experimental magnetotransport data across various fillings, contrasting the magnetic field responses, and then using theoretical calculations (Hartree-Fock/exact diagonalization) to explain the observed differences based on the underlying charge configuration and magnetic lattice structure.

Abstract

We report a magnetotransport study of fractional hole-filling states in the layer-polarized regime of a twisted MoTe$_2$ homobilayer. A perpendicular magnetic field suppresses the resistance peak at the two-thirds filling while enhancing the one-third peak by several orders of magnitude. Both remain near their commensurate densities. We argue that these commensurate states are generalized Wigner crystals whose distinct charge configurations define different magnetic lattices, leading to contrasting responses to spin polarization. Near half filling, we observe a qualitatively different state whose resistance maximum shifts approximately quadratically with field and exhibits pronounced transport anisotropy consistent with a stripe phase. These results reveal a strong dependence of magnetotransport on fractional filling and open a route to magnetic control of transport in moiré charge-ordered states.

Summary. This work presents a comprehensive computational search for novel binary kagome superconductors that possess intrinsic topological electronic states. By combining stability filtering, electron-phonon coupling calculations, and topological analysis, the authors predict several high-$T_c$ candidates, notably $ ext{Ti}_3 ext{Si}$, making these materials promising platforms for topological superconductivity.

Abstract

Topological superconductivity has attracted broad interest because of its connection to Majorana fermions and quantum computation. Kagome materials provide a natural setting in which interesting electronic structures, topological surface states, and superconductivity may occur in nearby energy windows. Here we carry out a systematic first-principles search for binary kagome superconducting candidates with topological electronic states across several structural families $A_mB_n$ ($m$:$n$=3:1; 3:2; 1:1). The screening combines formation-energy and phonon-stability filtering, magnetic-ground-state searches, electron-phonon-coupling calculations, and topological electronic-structure analysis. We identify 286 nonmagnetic dynamically stable candidates for electron-phonon-coupling analysis, among which 84 have estimated transition temperatures above 3 K and seven exceed the $\sim 9$ K reference scale of existing ambient-pressure kagome superconductors. In addition, several systems are predicted to host abundant topological surface states near the Fermi level. Ti$_3$Si, with the largest estimated $T_\text{c}=15.2$ K in this dataset, shows nodal-line-derived drumhead surface states and selected nodes that survive spin-orbit coupling near $E_F$. Combining superconductivity with nontrivial topological electronic structures, the binary kagome compounds predicted here represent compact and chemically tunable candidate platforms for topological superconductivity and for exploring kagome-related quantum phenomena.

Summary. This work demonstrates that high-T$_c$ bilayer nickelates can host a topological superconducting phase due to the interplay between layer-asymmetry and spin-orbit coupling. By modeling the system with a two-orbital Hamiltonian, the authors predict the existence of topological invariants and Majorana boundary modes. This establishes nickelates as a promising platform for realizing and tuning topological superconductivity.

Why it may be interesting. The explicit connection between structural asymmetry (substrate interaction) and the emergence of topological superconductivity in a strongly correlated material like nickelates is highly relevant for understanding unconventional pairing mechanisms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates whether the interlayer pairing mechanism in high-T$_c$ bilayer nickelates can support an intrinsic time-reversal-invariant topological superconducting phase.

Main result. The combination of a modest layer-asymmetric potential and layer-staggered Rashba SOC drives the system into a topological superconducting phase characterized by a nonzero mirror winding number and Majorana boundary modes.

Method. The analysis uses a two-orbital tight-binding model derived from first-principles calculations, analyzing the Bogoliubov-de Gennes Hamiltonian structure.

Model / system. The system is modeled as substrate-supported bilayer nickelate thin films, incorporating layer-asymmetric potentials and layer-staggered Rashba spin-orbit coupling into an effective two-orbital Hamiltonian.

Key observables. Topological superconducting phase (Class DIII), nonzero mirror winding number, Majorana Kramers pair at the [11] edge, and bulk gap nodes.

Important parameters / regimes. Layer-asymmetric potential strength (V) and Rashba SOC strength (lambda).

Assumptions / limitations. The model neglects the orbital dependence of the substrate-induced terms, and the substrate breaks the mirror symmetry via an interface electric dipole.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the physical setup on the substrate, the pairing structure on the Fermi surface, and the band structure showing the effect of SOC.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the physical model using first-principles derived parameters, analyzes the resulting Hamiltonian structure, calculates the topological invariants (winding number), and concludes by discussing experimental realization.

Abstract

The recent discovery of high-T$_c$ superconductivity in bilayer nickelates has highlighted the crucial role of interlayer coupling and motivated proposals for dominant interlayer pairing. Whether such pairing can support topological superconductivity, however, remains unexplored. Here, we demonstrate that substrate-supported bilayer nickelate thin films can host an intrinsic time-reversal-invariant topological superconducting phase protected by diagonal mirror symmetry. Using a two-orbital model derived from first-principles calculations, we show that a modest layer-asymmetric potential and layer-staggered Rashba spin-orbit coupling drive the system into this topological phase over a broad parameter range with a dominant interlayer pairing. The nontrivial topology arises from a reversal of the pairing sign on one of the spin-split bonding Fermi surfaces and is characterized by a nonzero mirror winding number along the diagonal direction, as well as a Majorana Kramers pair at the [11] edge. The phase also features bulk gap nodes located slightly away from the diagonal directions, featuring chiral charge and zero-energy flat bands. We discuss experimental routes for realizing and tuning this phase. Our results establish high-T$_c$ bilayer nickelates as a promising platform for topological superconductivity, where tunable topological transitions and Majorana boundary modes can provide probes of unconventional pairing.

Abstract

We investigate collective charge excitations in three-dimensional vortex nodal-ring semimetals (VNRs), whose pseudospin texture winds around the nodal ring and differs qualitatively from that of the conventional $\mathcal{PT}$-symmetric nodal-ring semimetal (PTNR). While plasmon modes in PTNRs have been studied in a companion work, arXiv:2609.35373. Here, we focus on how a VNR's pseudospin texture changes the response compared with the former. Within a low-energy two-band description and the random-phase approximation, we analyse the density response, density-of-states, Drude weights, dielectric function, and plasmon modes at low doping. We consider the untilted system together with in-plane and axial band-tilts. Although the VNR and PTNR have distinct Hamiltonians, pseudospin textures, and Berry-curvature distributions, their linearised energy dispersions coincide. The vortex texture, however, introduces a toroidal-angle dependence in the band-overlap factor and quantum metric, thereby modifying the charge response. We examine how these effects enter the dielectric screening and collective modes for different wavevector orientations. Our results clarify how pseudospin texture, beyond the band dispersion alone, influences collective charge dynamics in three-dimensional nodal-ring semimetals.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Studying fracture in large-scale structures dominated by self-weight is unfeasible due to immense scale, necessitating a benchtop test that bypasses specialized centrifuge facilities.

Main result. The proposed self-weight test successfully validates a continuum fracture theory by showing that crack nucleation and propagation occur precisely where the material strength criterion is violated under actual gravity loading.

Method. The study combines a simple benchtop experiment on a cantilever beam with advanced Finite Element Analysis (FEA) based on minimizing total energy, $E = P + G_c H^2$.

Model / system. The physical system is a cantilever beam with a V-notch, loaded only by its self-weight. The analysis uses a macroscopic continuum fracture theory that accounts for potential energy and surface energy.

Key observables. Crack nucleation and propagation location, support force vs. displacement load-displacement curves, and the region where the strength criterion $F(S) \geq 0$ is violated.

Important parameters / regimes. Length scale hierarchy ($\ell \ll h < l < H = D < L$), material properties (density, elasticity, fracture toughness $G_c$), and the gravitational body force $b = - ho_0 g e_2$.

Assumptions / limitations. The material is assumed to be macroscopically homogeneous, isotropic, and linear elastic brittle. The analysis relies on the validity of the continuum fracture energy minimization framework.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the schematic of the test setup, stress field violation contours showing where fracture initiates, and load-displacement curves comparing experimental measurements to theoretical predictions.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem, details the novel self-weight benchtop test, applies the theory to 3D-printable mortar and ice specimens, and validates the theory by comparing FEA results with experimental load-displacement measurements.

Abstract

Fracture in large-scale structures --- from failing concrete dams to calving glaciers --- is dominated by self-weight. Yet, owing to their immense scale, directly studying when and where cracks nucleate and propagate in these systems under gravity is unfeasible. To date, experimental investigations have largely relied on mimicking self-weight in scaled-down specimens via centrifugal forces, necessitating specialized centrifuge facilities. In this Letter, we introduce a simple benchtop test that bypasses the need for this infrastructure. The method utilizes a cantilever beam containing a downward-pointing V-notch, loaded solely by its self-weight. Through appropriate selection of the notch geometry and placement and the beam dimensions, the setup forces a crack to nucleate at the notch tip and propagate through the specimen in a measurable manner. We demonstrate the utility of this self-weight fracture test on a 3D-printable mortar and analyze the results using the fracture theory recently introduced in (\citeauthor{LPKFG2026}, 2026). \emph{Inter alia}, the findings provide a first validation of the theory for fracture driven by body forces.
