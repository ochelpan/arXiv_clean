Orbital-Rotation Shadow Tomography Reduces the Classical Cost of Quantum-Classical Auxiliary-Field Quantum Monte Carlo

Authors: Luning Zhao, Joshua Goings, Evgeny Epifanovsky, Martin Roetteler
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.03103v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces orbital-rotation shadows to drastically reduce the classical computational cost associated with Quantum-Classical Auxiliary-Field Quantum Monte Carlo (QC-AFQMC). By developing efficient estimators for key observables like overlaps, the authors make the simulation of strongly correlated electronic structure problems, such as those in $\pi$-conjugated molecules, significantly more feasible on classical hardware coupled with quantum measurements.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete, scalable computational pathway to simulate complex quantum many-body systems that are otherwise intractable due to classical overhead, directly impacting the feasibility of quantum simulation in chemistry and materials science.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the classical post-processing bottleneck in Quantum-Classical Auxiliary-Field Quantum Monte Carlo (QC-AFQMC) simulations for strongly correlated electronic structure problems.

Main result. They demonstrate that using orbital-rotation shadows significantly reduces the computational cost of calculating necessary overlaps and energy estimators, achieving polynomial scaling ($O(N^3)$ to $O(N^4)$) compared to previous exponential costs.

Method. The core method involves deriving efficient estimators for quantum overlaps and local energies by implementing particle-number-preserving fermionic shadows (orbital-rotation shadows) and combining them with advanced trace evaluation techniques.

Model / system. The work applies to strongly correlated electronic structure problems in molecules and materials, specifically using the framework of QC-AFQMC to evaluate properties of the quantum trial state $|\Psi_T angle$ relative to a target determinant $|\phi angle$.

Key observables. Overlaps ($\langle\Psi_T|\phi angle$), force biases, and local energies, which are crucial for the AFQMC propagation.

Important parameters / regimes. The scaling analysis depends on system size ($N$), the number of ancillary modes ($\eta$), and the desired error tolerance ($\epsilon$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the properties of fermionic shadows, the ability to efficiently evaluate traces using Grassmann integration, and the assumption that the orbital-rotation shadow technique correctly captures the necessary quantum information.

Figures summary. Not specified in the notes, but the analysis involves deriving complex mathematical expressions for expectation values and variances related to Gaussian random matrices.

Paper structure. The paper builds from identifying the QC-AFQMC bottleneck, introducing orbital-rotation shadows as a solution, deriving specific unbiased estimators for overlaps and derivatives, and finally demonstrating the feasibility and performance projections for large molecules.

Abstract

Quantum-classical auxiliary-field quantum Monte Carlo (QC-AFQMC) is a promising route to using quantum computers for strongly correlated electronic structure problems, but its scalability is limited by a classical post-processing bottleneck: the measured quantum trial state must be used to evaluate overlaps, force biases, and local energies for many walkers throughout the AFQMC propagation. Existing matchgate-shadow approaches avoid the exponential cost of generic classical shadows, but their post-processing remains expensive for large systems. In this work, we show that particle-number-preserving fermionic shadows, implemented as orbital-rotation shadows, are able to drastically reduce QC-AFQMC post-processing costs. We derive efficient estimators for overlaps and their derivatives, giving $O(N^3)$ scaling for overlaps and force biases and $O(N^4)$ scaling for local energies per shadow and walker and with $O(N)$ Cholesky vector. For fixed normalized target determinants, we also prove that the overlap estimator is unbiased with variance upper bounded by $O(η)$, where $η$ is the particle number, yielding a sufficient budget of $O(η/ε^2)$ ideal independent measurements for additive overlap error $ε$. We combine these estimators with a novel virtual-correlation-energy treatment and a GPU implementation and demonstrate QC-AFQMC using orbital-rotation shadows. Performance projections for $π$-conjugated molecules up to pentacene with a (22e, 22o) active space and 356 total orbitals show that the full classical post-processing workflow can be completed in slightly more than two days on 8$\times$A100 graphics processing units (GPUs). These results significantly reduce classical post-processing costs for QC-AFQMC and motivate further improvements in preparation and measurement of the trial state on quantum hardware.

Parity-Time Symmetry of Plasma Waves

Authors: Hong Qin
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.03433v1

Summary. This work establishes PT symmetry as a fundamental structural principle governing plasma waves in magnetized, collisionless plasmas. By proving that the underlying kinetic Hamiltonian is PT-symmetric, the authors link spontaneous symmetry breaking to the onset of kinetic instabilities. This framework offers new tools for predicting plasma instabilities and understanding spectral topology.

Why it may be interesting. The application of PT-symmetry concepts, typically from quantum mechanics, to classical plasma wave dynamics provides a powerful structural principle for understanding stability and spectral topology in complex, non-Hermitian physical systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the parity-time (PT) symmetry of the susceptibility tensor and the underlying kinetic system in a homogeneous, collisionless, magnetized plasma.

Main result. The susceptibility exhibits PT symmetry, which is shown to be a reduced-response-space manifestation of the full Vlasov–Maxwell Hamiltonian being PT-symmetric. Spontaneous PT-symmetry breaking is fundamentally linked to kinetic instabilities occurring via Krein collisions.

Method. The analysis involves identifying parity (P) and time reversal (T) operators on the electromagnetic kinetic eigenmode space and applying the framework of PT-symmetric quantum mechanics to plasma response functions.

Model / system. The system is a homogeneous collisionless magnetized plasma with a stationary gyrotropic equilibrium. The theory is built upon the linearized Vlasov–Maxwell and Vlasov–Poisson systems.

Key observables. Susceptibility tensor $\chi(\omega, \mathbf{k})$, eigenfrequencies ($\omega$), and the Hamiltonian operator $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{VM}}$.

Important parameters / regimes. Plasma frequency ($\omega_{ps}$), cyclotron frequency ($\Omega_s$), and the wave vector $\mathbf{k}$.

Assumptions / limitations. The plasma is assumed to be collisionless, and the equilibrium is stationary and gyrotropic.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the geometry of the wave vector and the reflection operator $R$. Other figures schematically show the movement of eigenfrequencies across the real axis during explicit PT-symmetry breaking.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the PT symmetry of the susceptibility tensor $\chi$ (Section III), and then extends this analysis to prove the PT symmetry of the full Hamiltonian operator $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{VM}}$ of the Vlasov–Maxwell system (Section IV).

Abstract

The susceptibility tensor of a homogeneous collisionless magnetized plasma possesses parity-time (PT) symmetry. For a stationary gyrotropic equilibrium, with the wave vector $\mathbf{k}$ and the equilibrium magnetic field $\mathbf{B}_0$ in the $x$--$z$ plane, the kinetic response satisfies $χ(ω^*,\mathbf{k})=Rχ(ω,\mathbf{k})^*R$, where $R=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,1)$. We show that the susceptibility PT-symmetry is a reduced-response-space manifestation of PT symmetry of the underpinning kinetic system. We identify parity $P$ and time reversal $T$ on the electromagnetic kinetic eigenmode space and show that the Hamiltonian operator $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{VM}}$ of the linearized Vlasov--Maxwell system is PT-symmetric, satisfying $PT\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{VM}}(PT)^{-1}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{VM}}$. The PT symmetry of the homogeneous magnetized Vlasov--Poisson eigenproblem is also established. Many kinetic instabilities associated with nonmonotonic distributions, rings, loss cones, and resonant energetic-particle populations are fundamentally spontaneous PT-symmetry breaking, which is achieved through and only through Krein collisions. Three examples demonstrate distinct utilities of this analysis. Definite-sign cyclotron residues protect thermal Bernstein modes from spontaneous PT-symmetry breaking. The nonmonotonic Dory--Guest--Harris distribution permits sign-indefinite residues and Krein collisions, for which we derive an analytical instability threshold and growth rate. The recently discovered mode-pole resonance instability provides a distinct realization of spontaneous PT-symmetry breaking and illustrates how PT symmetry can guide the discovery of kinetic instabilities with applications to fusion energy. These results establish PT symmetry as a structural principle of plasma waves and instabilities and open new directions for studying spectral topology in kinetic plasmas.

Summary. This paper presents polynomial-time algorithms for determining if a high-order tensor belongs to the convex set defining quantum separability or the nuclear norm ball. It achieves this by framing the problem as a multiprover game and developing sophisticated classical and quantum techniques involving spectral compression. The results provide significant algorithmic advances for quantum information theory.

Why it may be interesting. The development of polynomial-time quantum algorithms for fundamental tasks like separability testing is crucial for advancing quantum characterization and resource theories in quantum information.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops efficient algorithms to solve multilinear optimization problems related to weak membership in the nuclear norm unit ball and multipartite quantum separability.

Main result. It achieves polynomial-time classical algorithms and a quantum algorithm requiring only polylogarithmic copies to test quantum separability in the Frobenius norm.

Method. The approach combines viewing tensor optimization as a cooperative multiprover game with recursive spectral compression techniques, leading to both classical and quantum algorithmic implementations.

Model / system. The focus is on high-order tensors $M$ whose factors are constrained by convex sets $K_j$, specifically relating to quantum states $ ho$ in the context of multipartite quantum separability.

Key observables. The primary observables are the distance to the separable set, $ ext{dist}(M, C)$, and the maximum value of the multilinear expectation $ ext{Opt}(M)$.

Important parameters / regimes. Key parameters include the number of subsystems $k$, the local dimension $d$, the approximation accuracy $\epsilon$, and the number of copies used in the quantum protocol.

Assumptions / limitations. The quantum algorithm relies on the ability to implement specific filtering channels and utilizes the concept of coherent measurements across multiple copies of the state.

Figures summary. Not specified in the provided notes.

Paper structure. The paper is structured by first outlining the general multilinear optimization problem, then detailing the classical algorithm using spectral compression, and finally presenting the quantum algorithm leveraging copies and dimensionality reduction techniques.

Abstract

We study a multilinear optimization problem for tensors with bounded Frobenius norm, where each of the $k$ local factors of dimension $d$ is chosen from a convex set. We give a deterministic algorithm running in time $d^{O(k)}$ for constant additive approximations. As applications, we obtain the first polynomial-time algorithms at constant accuracy for weak membership in the nuclear-norm unit ball of high-order tensors and for multipartite quantum separability in Frobenius norm. Our approach views tensor optimization as a cooperative multiprover game and combines this perspective with a recursive spectral compression procedure. We then show that related ideas admit a quantum implementation when the input state is given through copies, rather than an explicit classical description. In these settings we obtain an algorithm to solve Frobenius-norm separability testing using $\text{poly}(k)$ copies and $\text{poly}(k\log d)$ time.

Abstract

A quantum orthogonal polynomial transform (QOPT) is an algorithmic primitive that maps a superposition of standard basis states $\sum_k α_{k} \ket{k}$ coherently to a basis of normalized univariate polynomials orthogonal with respect to a probability measure $μ(x)$. We provide new discrete and continuous QOPTs for polynomial families in the Askey scheme extending the results for the quantum Hermite transform (Jain et al., STOC'26). Our efficient QOPT algorithms require time $O(\text{polylog}(N, 1/ε))$, where $N$ is the grid size and $ε$ is the error, for the discrete Charlier, Meixner and Krawtchouk transforms and for the integer-order Laguerre transform in the continuous setting. The efficient QOPTs are obtained by uncovering the links between Askey scheme polynomials and Gaussian quantum optical gates and developing a compilation framework for $SU(2)$ and $SU(1,1)$ optical gates on Cartesian grids extending the framework developed by Iyer et al. (arXiv:2602.15180). Further, we reduce the continuous Jacobi transform to the discrete Hahn transform and provide an $O\!\left(N\operatorname{polylog}((N+α+β+1)/ε)\right)$-time Hahn transform, a quadratic speedup over the naive implementation. This is based on a more efficient compilation of the Clebsch--Gordan transform for coupling $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ representations with spins $(j_{1}, j_{2})$. Finally, we develop a new framework for Laguerre transforms for all orders $ν>0$ by fast-forwarding the corresponding radial oscillator using a 3-term chirp decomposition and an efficient algorithm for the Quantum Hankel Transform on a logarithmic grid.

Quantum Simulation on Riemannian Manifolds

Authors: Dylan Herman, Jacob Watkins, Guneykan Ozgul, Jiayu Shen, Brandon Augustino, Junhyung Lyle Kim, Shouvanik Chakrabarti
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.03673v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops quantum algorithms to simulate quantum dynamics governed by the Schrödinger equation on Riemannian manifolds. It introduces two primary techniques—a global spectral method and a local, chart-based coherent simulation—to handle the complex geometry. The work demonstrates the feasibility of these methods for simulating physical models ranging from constrained optimization problems to nonlinear field theories.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the computational challenge of simulating quantum dynamics on curved spaces, which is crucial for modeling physical systems whose configuration space is inherently Riemannian (e.g., particle motion on curved surfaces or field theories on curved backgrounds).

Detailed structure

Main problem. Developing efficient quantum algorithms to simulate the time evolution governed by the Schrödinger equation on a Riemannian manifold.

Main result. The authors present two main algorithmic approaches: a global spectral method for specific manifolds and a coherent simulation of local spectral methods applicable to any compact manifold.

Method. The methods involve spectral decomposition using the Laplace-Beltrami operator's eigenbasis (global method) or decomposing the manifold into overlapping local charts and coherently recombining local simulations (coherent-chart method).

Model / system. The system is described by the Riemannian Schrödinger equation, $i\partial_t\psi = H_g\psi$, where the Hamiltonian includes the Laplace-Beltrami operator ($\Delta_g$) derived from the manifold metric $g$. Applications include optimization on curved spaces and simulating field theories like the nonlinear sigma model.

Key observables. The time evolution of the quantum state $\psi(t)$, and convergence/error guarantees related to the spectral cutoff used in the simulation.

Important parameters / regimes. The choice of manifold (e.g., sphere, torus, simplex), the metric tensor $g$, and the spectral cutoff parameter.

Assumptions / limitations. The global method requires an efficient transform to the eigenbasis, while the coherent-chart method relies on deriving conditions for polynomial spectral cutoffs in the continuum limit.

Paper structure. The paper is structured around presenting and analyzing two distinct quantum simulation algorithms: the global spectral method and the coherent-chart method. It then applies these methods to specific physical problems, namely quantum optimization (e.g., Quantum Hamiltonian Descent) and physical field theory simulations.

Abstract

We investigate algorithms for the quantum simulation of the Schrödinger equation on a Riemannian manifold, where the kinetic operator is defined by the Laplace--Beltrami operator corresponding to the metric. Our first algorithms are based on a global spectral method based on the identification of an efficient transform to the eigenbasis of the Laplace--Beltrami operator. We use this method to provide explicit, efficient, quantum simulation algorithms for the Riemannian Schrödinger equation on tori and spheres with their standard metrics, simplices with the Wright--Fisher metric, truncated positive orthants and their invertible affine images with the log-barrier Hessian metric, and $\ell_p$ balls with a metric induced by the Duffy map. Our second algorithm is based on a coherent simulation of local spectral methods on multiple charts, and is in principle applicable to any compact manifold. We first analyze this algorithm in the continuum and derive conditions under which a polynomial spectral cutoff suffices. We also provide a discretization analysis of a polynomial spectral cutoff for tensor-products of constant-dimensional manifolds. Finally, we consider applications of these methods to optimization and physical simulation. For optimization, we provide results including a generalization and convergence analysis of Quantum Hamiltonian Descent for geodesically convex functions that leads to explicit algorithms on the sphere and simplex, and a Riemannian generalization of the Real-Space Adiabatic Algorithm. For physical simulation, we show that our algorithms can simulate certain spatially discretized field theories, including a variant of the nonlinear sigma model.

Quantum utility routing in continuous-variable QKD networks with trusted and untrusted relays

Summary. This paper introduces Quantum Utility Routing (QUR), a novel weighted framework designed to optimize path selection in CV-QKD networks. QUR balances the need for high secret key rates against the cost of network resources, accounting for both trusted and untrusted relays. The results show that QUR provides a superior trade-off compared to existing routing methods, demonstrating a practical approach to designing robust quantum communication infrastructure.

Why it may be interesting. This work is highly relevant as it applies advanced graph theory and optimization techniques to the practical engineering challenges of quantum network deployment, specifically optimizing resource allocation alongside quantum security guarantees.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of developing an optimal routing strategy for Continuous-Variable Quantum Key Distribution (CV-QKD) networks that must balance cryptographic key generation rates, network resource usage, and the varying trust levels of intermediate relays.

Abstract

Continuous-variable quantum key distribution (CV-QKD) networks require routing strategies that account simultaneously for secret key generation, relay trust and finite network resources. We introduce Quantum Utility Routing (QUR), a weighted routing framework for networks combining trusted point-to-point links with untrusted CV Bell measurement relays. Transition level composable finite size secret key rates (SKRs) are incorporated directly into the route search, allowing QUR to balance bottleneck SKR against route geometry and resource use. We consider graph optimized QUR (QUR$_{\mathrm{go}}$) and a fixed weight implementation (QUR$_{\mathrm{fw}}$) that uses machine learning to avoid repeated graph specific weight optimization, and benchmark both against A and Max--Min routing. At the default network configuration, QUR$_{\mathrm{go}}$ and QUR$_{\mathrm{fw}}$ improve the mean path SKR relative to A while requiring substantially fewer network resources than Max--Min. The framework also maintains secure connectivity over a broad range of trusted node availability by exploiting untrusted infrastructure as measurement relays. These results demonstrate that routing in CV-QKD networks can be treated as a configurable trade off between cryptographic performance and network resource consumption.

Summary. This paper constructs an infinite family of Quantum Low-Density Parity-Check (QLDPC) codes that provably approach the quantum Gilbert-Varshamov bound. By addressing the challenge of code degeneracy, the authors show that these codes can match the performance of random stabilizer codes across various quantum channels. This advances the practical design of high-rate, good-distance quantum error-correcting codes.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides concrete, constructive methods for designing quantum error-correcting codes (QEC) that approach fundamental theoretical limits (like the GV bound), which is central to fault-tolerant quantum computation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To prove that Quantum Low-Density Parity-Check (QLDPC) codes can achieve the quantum Gilbert-Varshamov (GV) bound, and to show that random QLDPC ensembles match the performance of random stabilizer codes on various quantum channels.

Main result. The authors construct an ensemble of QLDPC codes such that a random code approaches the quantum GV bound with high probability, matching the performance of random stabilizer codes for channels like the erasure and depolarizing channels.

Method. The construction involves combining low-degeneracy QLDPC codes (obtained via the Alon, Edmonds, and Luby distance amplification procedure) with random inner codes via concatenation, viewed as a quantum analogue of Thommesen's construction.

Model / system. The work focuses on quantum stabilizer codes, specifically QLDPC codes defined by row-sparse parity-check matrices. The analysis is conducted using the linear-algebraic formulation of stabilizer spaces.

Important parameters / regimes. The alphabet size $p$, the degeneracy parameter $\eta$, and the error tolerance $\epsilon$.

Assumptions / limitations. The construction relies on the ability to control the degeneracy of the QLDPC codes using the AEL procedure, which is necessary because QLDPC codes are inherently degenerate.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the random model schema: Base Code $ ightarrow$ "AEL" Code $ ightarrow$ Randomly Concatenated Code.

Paper structure. The paper develops by first establishing the need to control degeneracy, then detailing the AEL construction to achieve low-degeneracy outer codes approaching the quantum Singleton bound, and finally combining this with random inner codes to prove the overall approach to the GV bound.

Abstract

We show that for any $R,ε>0$ and any prime $p\geq 2$, there exists an infinite family of $p$-ary quantum low-density parity-check (QLDPC) codes, rate $R$, checks of weight $O_ε(1)$, and normalized distance at least $δ_{\mathrm{GV}}(p,R)-ε$. Here, $δ_{\mathrm{GV}}(p,R)$ denotes the quantum Gilbert-Varshamov (GV) bound for $p$-ary stabilizer codes. In fact, we construct an ensemble of such QLDPC codes, such that a random code from this ensemble is close to the GV bound with high probability. Moreover, this ensemble matches the performance of random stabilizer codes on several quantum channels. Specifically, it approaches the quantum capacity of the erasure channel and the hashing bound for memoryless Pauli channels, including the depolarizing channel. A significant challenge in working with QLDPC codes is that they are necessarily \emph{degenerate}, i.e., contain many low-weight stabilizers. A key contribution of our work is a construction of QLDPC codes with quantitative control on their degeneracy. These codes are obtained by combining known constructions of asymptotically good QLDPC codes with the expander-based distance amplification procedure of Alon, Edmonds, and Luby [FOCS'95]. Our random ensemble is constructed by starting with these low-degeneracy QLDPC codes near the quantum Singleton bound and concatenating each coordinate with a random inner code. This can be viewed as a quantum analogue of Thommesen's construction, and as an LDPC version of a result of Ouyang, with an appropriately designed outer code.

Abstract

Collective modes of the superconducting condensate encode fundamental information on the pairing interaction, yet their direct detection remains experimentally challenging. In multiband superconductors, the Leggett mode, i.e., a coherent oscillation of the relative phase between two condensates, is Raman-active and couples to light only nonlinearly in ordinary situations. Here we show theoretically that terahertz two-dimensional coherent spectroscopy (2DCS) provides a direct fingerprint of the Leggett mode (with frequency $ω_L$) through sideband emission at the detection frequency $ω_t = ω_L\pmΩ$ ($Ω$ is the central frequency of the incident pulses) in the nonlinear 2D spectrum. The key insight is that the finite duration of the THz pulses is the physical mechanism that makes the mode accessible: the broad spectral content of a finite pulse provides the nonlinear frequency combinations needed to resonantly excite the mode at ωL without any prior knowledge of its value or tuning of external parameters. We demonstrate robustness of this signature against temperature and quasiparticle relaxation, and show that sweeping $Ω$ allows unambiguous identification of the Leggett mode.
