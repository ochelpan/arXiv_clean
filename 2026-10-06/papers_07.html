A graph-theoretic analysis of non-generic free-fermion solvability by Krylov decompositions

Authors: Jannis Ruh, Samuel J. Elman, Adrian Chapman
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.03557v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work develops a graph-theoretic framework to classify quantum spin models that are exactly solvable via free-fermion mappings. By constructing 'Krylov currents' based on the frustration graph, the authors systematically generate a family of non-generically solvable Hamiltonians. This provides a powerful, systematic route for finding and characterizing complex, exactly solvable many-body systems.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous graph-theoretic approach to classifying exactly solvable models provides powerful tools for understanding quantum many-body physics, especially in regimes where simple mean-field or generic approximations fail.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper characterizes the structural conditions under which quantum spin models, particularly those related to the FFD chain, admit free-fermion solutions, distinguishing between generic and non-generic solvability.

Main result. The authors identify 'path support' as a signature of non-generic solvability and provide a systematic method using 'Krylov currents' to generate an entire family of non-generically solvable, exactly solvable Hamiltonians.

Method. The analysis combines graph theory (using the frustration graph) with advanced algebraic techniques, including the construction of Krylov currents and the use of path-product expansions.

Model / system. The primary model is the Free-fermion-in-disguise (FFD) chain, a 1D spin-chain Hamiltonian involving Pauli operators. The study extends this to non-generic solvable extensions $H_{NG}[a, b]$ by adding terms derived from Krylov currents.

Key observables. Path support, energy gap ($\Delta$), and the structure of the generated Lie algebra ($\mathfrak{so}(d)$).

Important parameters / regimes. Coupling strengths (which determine generic vs. non-generic solvability), and the graph structure parameters like $\alpha(G)$ (number of consecutive disjunct triangles).

Assumptions / limitations. The necessity of the path-product representation for free-fermion solvability is conjectured, and the analysis relies on specific anticommutation relations derived from the graph structure.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the frustration graph of the FFD chain, the construction of Krylov currents from induced paths, and numerical results showing the dependence of the energy gap on the current strength.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by defining the FFD model and its graph structure, introducing Krylov currents as the mechanism for non-generic extensions, proving the path-product expansion for these currents, and finally establishing the algebraic structure generated by these currents.

Abstract

Exactly solvable models are often identified from structural features of their interactions. For quantum spin Hamiltonians, these are captured by the frustration graph of anticommuting Pauli terms. The frustration graph characterizes broad families of spin models that map to free fermions for arbitrary, or generic, values of their coupling strengths. Nevertheless, there exist non-generically solvable models that admit free-fermion solutions only at finely tuned couplings. In this work, we characterize a family of such models in terms of graph structures. Given an arbitrary instance of Fendley's free-fermions-in-disguise (FFD) chain, we construct a set of operators, called Krylov currents, that are exactly quadratic in its effective fermionic modes and thus extend it to a linear family of free-fermion solvable Hamiltonians. As the Krylov currents are nonlinear in the FFD Hamiltonian terms, the elements of this generated family are non-generically solvable, and they accordingly fail the known conditions for generic solvability. We prove that every Krylov current admits an expansion in terms of induced paths of the frustration graph and that they generate the full special-orthogonal algebra of Gaussian transformations. Our results identify path support as a signature of non-generic solvability, and they provide a systematic route to finding exactly solvable many-body Hamiltonians.

BARC codes: general polynomial framework for coherent-state superposition codes

Summary. This paper introduces BARC codes, a new class of quantum error-correcting codes for bosonic systems, by framing the error-correcting constraints within an algebraic geometry framework. The authors use polynomial systems to systematically construct these codes, providing explicit examples like ellipsoidal and hexagonal codes. This offers a powerful, general method for designing robust quantum codes against realistic photon loss errors.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, abstract algebraic toolset to systematically discover and analyze quantum error-correcting codes for bosonic systems, which is highly relevant to quantum optics and quantum information theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to develop a systematic, general algebraic framework for constructing novel bosonic quantum error-correcting codes.

Main result. They introduce Bosonic Algebraically-Restricted Constellation (BARC) codes, defined by polynomial constraints derived from treating the Knill-Laflamme conditions as algebraic equations.

Method. The methodology combines tools from algebraic geometry and commutative algebra (polynomial systems, ideals) to solve moment equations that characterize the code states.

Model / system. The system involves bosonic quantum states represented by superpositions of coherent states in an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space, designed to correct for photon-gain and photon-loss errors.

Key observables. Minimum geometric separation between constellation points, and entanglement fidelity when benchmarking against established codes.

Important parameters / regimes. The degree of the polynomial constraints, the noise type (e.g., pure-loss noise), and the constellation weight.

Assumptions / limitations. The initial explicit constructions are limited to the single-mode, uniform-weight case, and the derivation involves approximating exact matrix elements by discarding certain overlap terms.

Figures summary. The notes reference tables detailing specific parameters ($\alpha^\star_{C}$, $ar{n}^\star_{C}$, $\Delta^\star_{C}$, $s^\star_{C}$) for various benchmarked code families (Hex3, Ellipse, QSC, QCC).

Paper structure. The paper establishes the general algebraic framework (BARC codes) by relating error correction to polynomial constraints, derives necessary moment equations, and then benchmarks the constructed codes against known families using fidelity metrics.

Abstract

We introduce an algebraic framework for constructing a new class of quantum error-correcting codes, bosonic algebraically-restricted constellation (BARC) codes. The code states are finite superpositions of coherent-state constellations constrained by symmetries of solution sets to multivariate complex polynomial systems. The constraints are associated with photon-gain and photon-loss errors, thereby imposing approximate Knill--Laflamme conditions for such errors. For equally-weighted constellation points, we derive upper bounds on the minimum geometric separation between points, indicating how noise-resilient the code is. In the single-mode case, we characterize degree-two polynomial solution sets using an orthogonal group symmetry to obtain explicit ellipsoidal and hexagonal BARC codes. We benchmark representative instances of these families against spherical and cubature codes using the entanglement fidelity with optimal recovery under pure-loss noise.

Breaking the cubic barrier for the inverse-free Solovay-Kitaev algorithm

Authors: Adam Bouland, Victor Shyaka, Chenyi Zhang
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.03655v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper presents significant theoretical improvements to the Solovay-Kitaev algorithm for quantum computation when the available gate set lacks inverses. By developing sophisticated recursive approximation techniques and analyzing directed unitaries, the authors demonstrate an IFSK exponent below the established cubic barrier of 3. This work shows that current compilation methods can be significantly more efficient than previously thought, advancing the field of quantum circuit synthesis.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly advances the theoretical understanding of quantum computation complexity, providing concrete algorithmic improvements that challenge established information-theoretic limits for gate compilation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to improve the exponent of the inverse-free Solovay-Kitaev (IFSK) algorithm, which compiles quantum unitaries using gate sets that lack inverses.

Main result. The authors achieve an IFSK exponent for a qubit of at most $\approx 2.988$, surpassing the known information-theoretic bound of 3, and also show an exponent of $\le 2.138$ for a specific Pauli-supplemented gate set.

Method. The methods involve constructing highly accurate directed unitaries and employing recursive approximation schemes, building upon techniques like 'Pauli golf' and fixed-point iteration analysis.

Model / system. The system is a single qubit ($ ext{SU}(2)$), and the problem is compiling an arbitrary target unitary $U$ to a specified accuracy $\epsilon$ using a given, potentially non-inverse-closed, universal gate set $G$.

Key observables. The primary observable is the exponent $b$ in the complexity $O(\log_b(1/\epsilon))$ of the compilation algorithm.

Important parameters / regimes. The key parameters are the target accuracy $\epsilon$, and the exponent itself, which is compared against the information-theoretic bound of 3.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the existence and computability of fixed points derived from error-bounding functions, and the applicability of recursive bounds for sufficiently small $\epsilon$.

Figures summary. The notes mention Figure 1 illustrating convergence rates, comparing doubly-exponential convergence (Kuperberg's algorithm) to the single-exponential scaling achieved by the new algorithms.

Paper structure. The paper develops algorithms (FullCompile and DirectedUnitary) that recursively approximate the target unitary. The core mathematical machinery involves establishing error bounds using lemmas (e.g., Lemma 4.7, Lemma 4.8) derived from analyzing the structure of the approximation sequence $P_{24}$ and solving for necessary parameters via fixed-point iteration.

Abstract

The Solovay-Kitaev algorithm states that it is possible to efficiently compile unitaries to accuracy $ε$ in $\text{polylog}(1/ε)$ time using any universal gate set. While Solovay and Kitaev's initial work addressed inverse-closed gate sets, recently Bouland and Giurgicǎ-Tiron showed it is possible to efficiently compile arbitrary gate sets without inverses. However, the exponent of their compilation algorithm is high. For example the inverse-free exponent for a qubit is over $8.62$, while information theoretically it is known that an inverse-free exponent of $3$ is possible in any dimension by a result of Oszmaniec, Sawicki, and Horodecki. This leads to a natural question: can one improve the exponent in Bouland and Giurgicǎ-Tiron's algorithm? And are there any cases where the inverse-free exponent is below 3, thus surpassing existing information-theoretic arguments? To answer this question, we first show that a special case of the inverse-free compiling problem -- namely Pauli gates supplemented by an irrational rotation of a qubit - admits a highly efficient compilation algorithm with an exponent at most $\approx 2.138$. Our algorithm fuses ideas from prior algorithms of Kuperberg and Sardharwalla, Cubitt, Harrow and Linden to construct an efficient Pauli golf'' compilation routine. We then extend these ideas to the general inverse-free case for a qubit, where we give a compiling algorithm with exponent at most $2.988$. This result uses a novel form of approximateaiming'' of unitary corrections to improve the compilation efficiency. Our results give two examples of quantum compiling algorithms surpassing information-theoretic arguments, and demonstrate that there are many efficiency gains possible for inverse-free compilation.

Compiling Together: High-Throughput Distributed Quantum Computing via Multi-Compilation

Authors: Yipei Liu, Sen Zhang, Zebo Yang, Lei Yang
Type: both · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.03260v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper tackles the throughput bottleneck in Distributed Quantum Computing by proposing 'multi-compilation,' which runs several logically equivalent circuit compilations simultaneously. By optimizing the joint selection and allocation of these candidates under limited entanglement resources, the authors significantly boost computational throughput and improve result fidelity. The solution is formulated as an NP-hard optimization problem solved using advanced techniques like MILP.

Why it may be interesting. The work directly addresses resource management and scaling limitations in distributed quantum architectures, which is crucial for realizing fault-tolerant quantum computation across modular hardware platforms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is maximizing the throughput of quantum computation on a Distributed Quantum Computing (DQC) network limited by the finite, rate-constrained generation of entanglement (Bell pairs) across communication links.

Main result. By employing multi-compilation—running logically equivalent circuits concurrently—the method significantly boosts throughput (up to 4.5x in simulation) and improves output fidelity compared to using a single compilation.

Method. The problem is formulated as Candidate-Constrained Max-Shot Allocation (CMA), an NP-hard optimization problem, solved using dynamic programming, MILP, and a greedy heuristic.

Model / system. The model is a DQC network connecting multiple Quantum Processing Units (QPUs) via communication links, where the resource bottleneck is the rate at which Bell pairs can be generated on these links.

Key observables. Throughput (shots/s), output fidelity, and Bell-pair utilization across inter-QPU links.

Important parameters / regimes. Bell-pair generation rate ($R_e$) per link, number of QPUs (up to 36), and circuit size (up to 144 qubits).

Assumptions / limitations. The model assumes a per-link communication constraint, and the allocation is modeled using a discretized rate model (integer units of shots/s).

Figures summary. Figures demonstrate that multi-compilation increases fidelity significantly (e.g., 93.3% improvement for TFIM-20) and reduces the time needed to reach a target fidelity by up to 10x compared to single-compilation baselines.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the DQC throughput bottleneck, formulates the CMA optimization problem, details the NP-hardness proof, and then presents and compares solutions using dynamic programming, MILP, and greedy heuristics, validating results on simulations and hardware.

Abstract

Quantum computing is a promising paradigm for problems that are challenging for classical machines, but realizing that promise requires far more qubits than a single processor can offer. Distributed quantum computing (DQC) scales out by connecting multiple quantum processing units (QPUs), at the cost of making entanglement the scarce resource: every remote gate consumes a Bell pair, and inter-QPU links generate Bell pairs at finite rates orders of magnitude slower than local gates. Since quantum programs are executed repeatedly, this rate bounds how fast shots complete and thus how fast results are obtained. Existing DQC compilers emit a single implementation per circuit, so throughput is capped by its busiest link while other links stay idle. We observe that alternative compilations of the same circuit are logically equivalent yet stress different links; executing them concurrently and pooling their samples converts idle Bell pairs into additional shots. We formulate the joint selection of compilations and allocation of shots under per-link Bell-pair capacities as Candidate-Constrained Max-Shot Allocation (CMA), prove it NP-hard, and solve it with a dynamic program and its approximate variant AppDP, a compact MILP, and a greedy heuristic Effi. In simulation on six-QPU networks, multi-compilation raises throughput by 2-4.5 over the single compilation and correspondingly improves output fidelity under equal Bell-pair budgets. On hardware, it increases measured fidelity by up to 93% and reaches the single-compilation fidelity in up to 10 less time. Scaling to 36 QPUs and 144-qubit circuits, MILP improves throughput over the single compilation by 76.8% on average across 48 configurations while Effi allocates in milliseconds.

Summary. This paper establishes a rigorous, distance-independent criterion for the universality of the Clifford+T gate set in quantum computing. By defining 'projective distance measures' based on four axioms, the authors prove that the gate set can approximate any unitary operation for any distance metric satisfying these properties. This provides a fundamental theoretical underpinning for quantum circuit synthesis.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a rigorous, abstract mathematical framework for understanding when a small set of quantum gates is sufficient to simulate any quantum operation, independent of the specific metric used to measure error.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To determine the necessary and sufficient properties of a distance measure required to prove the distance-independent universality of the Clifford+T gate set for quantum computation.

Main result. The universality theorem holds for any distance measure satisfying the axioms of a 'projective distance measure,' formalizing the proof in Lean.

Method. The authors define projective distance measures using four axioms and prove that the gate set $\{C(X), H, T\}$ is universal with respect to any such measure by decomposing the approximation into sequential steps.

Model / system. The system is quantum computation, focusing on approximating any arbitrary unitary matrix $U \in U(2^n)$ using the Clifford+T gate set.

Key observables. Distance measures (Hilbert-Schmidt, Trace, Operator-norm, Frobenius) and the approximation error $\epsilon$.

Important parameters / regimes. The structure of the distance measure (e.g., satisfying Projective Invariance) and the gate set $\{C(X), H, T\}$.

Assumptions / limitations. The necessary structure for universality is captured entirely by the definition of a projective distance measure.

Paper structure. The paper first defines projective distance measures via four axioms. It then proves that the universality theorem holds for any such measure, relying on decomposing the approximation into steps involving $R_z( heta)$ gates, which are subsequently shown to be approximable by circuits built from $\{H, S, G_1\}$.

Abstract

A well-known theorem states that the Clifford+T gate set is universal for quantum computing. The theorem combines approximation using a distance measure on unitary matrices with equality up to global phase. Previous proofs combine these two notions for specific distance measures, but do not identify the properties that govern how they work together. Which properties are sufficient to state and prove the theorem? We answer this question by defining projective distance measures using four axioms. We prove that the universality theorem holds for every distance measure satisfying these axioms. Thus, our formulation of the theorem is independent of any particular choice of distance measure. We show that the Hilbert-Schmidt distance is already a projective distance measure. We also develop a general construction that transforms a large class of distance measures into projective distance measures and apply it to obtain projective versions of the operator-norm distance, the Frobenius distance, and the trace distance. Finally, we formalize the proof of the universality theorem in Lean.

Entanglement Requires Fluctuations at Conformal Interfaces

Authors: Yuya Kusuki, Kosei Suzuyama, Yin Tang
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.02309v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work establishes a theoretical bound relating the universal logarithmic coefficient of entanglement capacity ($c_{cap}$) to that of entanglement entropy ($c_{eff}$) at conformal interfaces. By proving this bound in several physical contexts, including free CFTs and holographic models, the authors demonstrate that capacity provides a distinct, measurable diagnostic tool for characterizing quantum correlations across boundaries.

Why it may be interesting. The connection between entanglement measures like entropy and capacity is fundamental to understanding quantum correlations across boundaries, which has implications for quantum information flow and boundary physics in condensed matter systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the relationship between entanglement entropy ($S_A$) and the capacity of entanglement ($C_A$) at quantum interfaces, specifically testing the conjecture that $c_{eff} \le c_{cap}$.

Main result. The conjectured bound $c_{eff} \le c_{cap}$ is proven in free CFTs and holographic models, and numerical calculations confirm that capacity provides independent data distinct from entropy.

Method. The authors employ theoretical proofs using defect perturbation theory, replica trick calculations, and analyzing scaling laws derived from DMRG simulations on lattice models.

Model / system. The study focuses on 2D Conformal Field Theories (CFTs) connected by various interfaces, including free boson/fermion CFTs, holographic interface branes, and interacting systems like the tricritical Ising/Ising interface.

Key observables. Entanglement Entropy ($S_A$) and Capacity of Entanglement ($C_A$), characterized by their universal logarithmic coefficients ($c_{eff}$ and $c_{cap}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The interface coupling strengths, the size of the system ($L$), and the central charges of the constituent CFTs.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the applicability of CFT techniques, the use of replica geometry, and the validity of the holographic correspondence in certain limits.

Figures summary. Figures compare lattice coefficients ($c_{eff}/c$ and $c_{cap}/c$) against exact free-fermion curves, and show how capacity slopes can overtake entropy slopes at interfaces.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the conjecture in free field theories and holography, to providing rigorous proofs using defect perturbation theory, and finally validating the results through extensive numerical calculations on complex interacting lattice models.

Abstract

We conjecture that the universal logarithmic coefficient for capacity of entanglement $c_{\mathrm{cap}}$ is bounded below by the corresponding entropy coefficient $c_{\mathrm{eff}}$ at conformal interfaces. We prove the bound in free boson and fermion CFTs and in holographic models with interface branes, and establish it using defect perturbation theory. Numerical calculations at interacting interfaces between the tricritical Ising and Ising CFTs further support the bound. Despite this constraint, capacity provides independent interface data. We construct interfaces with identical entropy and energy transmission coefficients but different capacity coefficients, and show that capacity can even increase as entropy decreases.

Abstract

Hybrid quantum classical neural networks integrate parameterized quantum circuits (PQCs) with established deep learning architectures, but their performance depends strongly on the choice of quantum circuit architecture, a choice that remains largely manual. Most existing approaches rely on hand-designed or fixed circuit ansätze, requiring circuit structure, gate composition, and qubit connectivity to be specified in advance with no guarantee that they suit the task. This limitation is especially acute in image classification, where quantum circuits must transform features extracted by classical networks while remaining compact enough for practical training, requirements that generic, task-agnostic ansätze are unlikely to satisfy simultaneously. We extend EXAQC, an evolutionary framework for automated quantum circuit discovery, to image classification. EXAQC evolves PQCs as intermediate processing modules while retaining classical feature-extraction and prediction layers. On MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and CIFAR-10, EXAQC achieves 98.42%, 90.62%, and 85.47% accuracy, respectively, while using comparable gate counts to other quantum architecture-search methods. Encoding choice also matters: rotation-based encodings (RX, RY, U3) outperform amplitude encoding by 22-25 points on CIFAR-10. These results demonstrate that automated circuit discovery yields compact quantum modules that can replace larger classical components in vision architectures while retaining competitive accuracy.

Paper structure. The paper builds from establishing the error basis for D(G) (including irrep operators) to analyzing local error detection (flux/charge checks) and culminates in a detailed, systematic protocol for recovering errors supported on extended structures called 'ribbons.'

Abstract

We study exact recovery for non-Abelian topological surface codes based on the quantum double $D(G)$ of any finite group $G$. We determine an orthogonal error basis, that is comprised of group multiplication and irreducible representation operators. Whenever the errors are supported on closed loops on the lattice or dual lattice, there is a redundancy in the basis, due to stabilizers. To remedy this, we introduce a gauge-fixing that results in a complete and orthogonal error basis on the code space. Assuming as input predetermined, neutral correctable error clusters, we construct charge and flux transfer circuits that move the errors onto ancillas that then get projected out. This is an exact and deterministic recovery protocol, applicable to surface codes for any finite, in particular non-Abelian, group $G$.

Abstract

Polynomial transformations of data encoded in quantum operations are a building block of quantum algorithms, and their query complexity measures how often those operations are used. For multiple variables, existing methods either realize restricted polynomial families or implement monomials separately at a query cost exponential in the number of variables. We present a quantum circuit that implements every multivariate trigonometric polynomial up to an explicit normalization. The circuit processes one variable coherently over the frequency components of the remaining variables, allowing all components to share the same signal queries. Each variable is queried exactly as many times as its degree in the polynomial, and we prove that this query complexity is optimal. We further extend the construction to commuting unitaries. The resulting circuit applies the multivariate polynomial simultaneously across their shared eigenbasis, using the minimum numbers of forward and inverse queries to each unitary. We also study trainable rotations with fixed preparation and a local readout of the last input. At query depth at least two, independent uniform initialization gives every active readout gradient a variance lower bound set by its squared branch weight and the inverse square root of the depth, uniformly over data states and dimensions. For supervised squared loss, retaining sample correlations gives an initialization gradient-variance bound in terms of targets and input spectra. This also bounds the expected loss decrease after the first exact gradient step with a specified step size. These results provide a scalable and practical solution for developing multivariate quantum algorithms and quantum learning models.

Abstract

Marriott and Watrous showed that quantum Merlin--Arthur games admit generic error reduction without increasing witness size [Computational Complexity, 2005]. In this work, we show that this state-size-preserving amplification property does not hold for polynomial-time quantum computation with quantum advice. In particular, we present decision problems for which even a vanishing additive error reduction requires longer advice. More precisely, for every polynomially bounded advice length $m(n)\geq n^4$ and every error bound $\varepsilon(n)$ that stays below $1/2$ by at least an inverse polynomial, there is a positive function $δ$ with $δ(n)=O\bigl(\min\{(\log m/m)^{1/4},\ \sqrt{\log m/m}\,/(1/2-\varepsilon(n))\}\bigr)$ such that $\mathsf{BQP}_{\varepsilon}/\mathsf{q}m \subsetneq \mathsf{BQP}_{\varepsilon + δ}/\mathsf{q}m$; for constant $\varepsilon$ the gap is $O(\sqrt{\log m/m})$. Here, $\mathsf{BQP}_\varepsilon/\mathsf{q}m$ is the class of languages recognizable with error at most $\varepsilon(n)$ by a polynomial-time quantum algorithm with an $m(n)$-qubit advice state that only depends on the input length $n$. We show this by proving a stronger separation $\mathsf{P}_{\varepsilon+δ}/\mathsf{r} m \not\subset \mathsf{BQP}_{\varepsilon}/\mathsf{q} m$, where $\mathsf{P}_{\varepsilon}/\mathsf{r}m$ is the class of languages recognizable with error at most $\varepsilon(n)$ by a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm with an $m(n)$-bit advice string sampled from a distribution that depends only on $n$.
