High-Rate Quantum Codes with Proven Distance and Low-Weight Measurements

Authors: Kishor Bharti, Tobias Haug, Runzhou Tao, Kevin Ye
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.02621v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops a rigorous theory for a specific family of quantum error-correcting codes defined on rectangular grids. It mathematically proves the exact distance of these codes and, crucially, maps out the achievable trade-off between the encoding rate and the required measurement weight. This provides concrete, constructive blueprints for building resource-efficient quantum memory.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides concrete, mathematically verifiable constructions for QEC codes, which is foundational for building fault-tolerant quantum computation architectures, linking abstract algebraic structures to physical resource constraints.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To find a useful finite construction for quantum error correction that explicitly relates the protected capacity (encoding rate), the minimum support of an undetectable logical Pauli error (distance), and the weight of the measured operators (measurement weight).

Main result. The authors derive explicit formulas for a family of subsystem codes on rectangular grids, proving the dressed distance is exactly $2L$ (twice the number of dimensions) and establishing a concrete, constructive tradeoff between encoding rate and measurement weight.

Method. The analysis uses algebraic techniques involving quotient spaces over F2, formal verification via Lean code snippets, and finite-rate optimization to map out the rate-weight frontier.

Model / system. The study focuses on a family of quantum subsystem codes defined on rectangular coordinate grids of any dimension, with even side lengths of at least four. These codes are of the Calderbank–Shor–Steane (CSS) type.

Key observables. Encoding Rate ($K/N$), Dressed Distance ($D$), Measurement Weight ($w$), and the number of protected logical qubits ($K$).

Important parameters / regimes. The side lengths $q_1, \dots, q_L$ (must be even and $\ge 4$), the total data qubits $N$, and the budget constraint (e.g., $N \le 10,000$).

Assumptions / limitations. The derived parameters are for a specific family of codes, and the analysis relies on formal proofs regarding the full-center equality and the structure of the gauge group.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the supports of gauge operators and logical operators in small examples (e.g., $4 imes 4$ code), and the main results are summarized in tables showing the rate-weight frontiers for fixed distances (D=16, 32, 64).

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by defining the code structure on rectangular grids, deriving the exact distance using quotient space arguments, and finally performing a finite, explicit enumeration to map the optimal rate-weight trade-off for fixed distances.

Abstract

We study a family of quantum subsystem codes defined on rectangular coordinate grids of any dimension, with even side lengths of at least four. The directly measured operators act along coordinate lines. For every member of the family, we derive the number of protected logical qubits and prove its dressed distance without a numerical search. To illustrate the resulting design choices, we impose a budget of at most 10,000 data qubits and determine the complete tradeoff between encoding rate and measurement weight within this family at distances 16, 32, and 64. At distance 16, one code protects 4,096 logical qubits among 10,000 data qubits using weight-ten measurements. At distances 32 and 64, codes with 7,776 and 9,216 data qubits protect 1,024 and 256 logical qubits, respectively, using weight-six measurements. We include and explain Lean code snippets throughout the paper to guide readers through the formal verification of our subsystem-code construction and its dressed and bare distances.

Lie Algebraic Uncertainty Relations

Authors: Xiaoli Hu, Naihuan Jing, Jian Zhang, Yunlong Xiao
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.03661v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work develops a novel, algebraic method to determine the minimum quantum uncertainty for sets of incompatible observables that generate a Lie algebra. By reformulating uncertainty using covariance matrices and the Lie algebra structure, the authors derive optimal bounds independent of the specific quantum state. This connects fundamental quantum limitations directly to representation theory, offering insights into quantum information processing limits.

Why it may be interesting. By connecting fundamental quantum uncertainty to the deep mathematical structure of Lie algebras, it provides a powerful, general framework for understanding intrinsic limits on quantum information processing.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to find optimal, state-independent bounds for quantum uncertainty relations, particularly for observables whose generators form a Lie algebra.

Main result. The optimal uncertainty bound is determined entirely by the algebraic structure of the Lie algebra and the representation it acts upon, replacing state optimization with direct algebraic calculation.

Method. It introduces a covariance-based formulation of uncertainty and leverages the structure theory of Lie algebras (e.g., Gram matrices, root systems) to derive these bounds.

Model / system. The analysis is performed on quantum observables whose commutation relations define a Lie algebra ($\mathfrak{g}$), applicable to both Hermitian and non-Hermitian operators.

Key observables. Lie algebraic uncertainty ($U_\psi(X)$), Casimir eigenvalues ($\langle C_2 angle$), and weight space values.

Important parameters / regimes. The structure constants of the Lie algebra, the representation labels ($\vec{\lambda}$), and the Killing form inner product.

Assumptions / limitations. The operators under consideration must form a basis of a Lie algebra, and the analysis relies heavily on the structure theory of semisimple Lie algebras.

Figures summary. The paper provides tables listing the optimal Lie algebraic uncertainty relations for various classical and exceptional simple Lie algebras in different representations.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from introducing the covariance-based uncertainty measure to deriving the optimal bound using the inverse Gram matrix. It then proves basis invariance and establishes the constant uncertainty within weight spaces, culminating in general theorems for the full irreducible representation and semisimple algebras.

Abstract

Uncertainty relations express the fundamental incompatibility of quantum observables, yet optimal state-independent bounds are known analytically in only a few cases. Here we introduce a covariance-based formulation of quantum uncertainty that yields an infinite family of relations for both Hermitian observables and non-Hermitian operators. For operators forming a basis of a Lie algebra, we derive the optimal bound in closed form from the algebra and its representation, replacing optimization over quantum states with a direct algebraic calculation. Within each weight space, uncertainty takes a fixed value, turning the inequality into a conservation law. These results show how commutation relations and their realization on a quantum state jointly determine the minimum quantum uncertainty associated with incompatible operators. By connecting uncertainty to symmetry and representation theory, the framework offers a route to uncovering intrinsic limits on quantum information processing through its underlying Lie algebra.

Nonlocal, nonlinear electrostatics: like-charge attraction and asymmetric solvation

Authors: V. Stepanyan, Y. Sh. Mamasakhlisov, A. E. Allahverdyan
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.02849v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 2/5

Summary. This paper develops a theory for nonlinear electrostatics to explain anomalous interactions in molecular systems. It shows that nonlocal effects can cause like-charge attraction and break the expected symmetry of solvation based on charge sign. The work uses mean-field theory to analyze these complex, non-local interactions.

Why it may be interesting. The focus on nonlinear, nonlocal electrostatics and symmetry breaking in solvation provides a rich theoretical framework applicable to complex molecular interactions, relevant for understanding environmental effects on quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates nonlinear electrostatics to understand phenomena like like-charge attraction and solvation structure asymmetry, which are missed by standard local theories.

Main result. The research demonstrates that like charges can attract due to nonlocal polarization effects, and that the solvation structure can violate effective C-symmetry depending on the external charge sign.

Method. The authors employ a mean-field approach, solving self-consistent equations derived from the Coulomb coupling, and comparing results with nonlinear Poisson-Boltzmann frameworks.

Model / system. The system models an equilibrium ensemble of linear molecules with rigid charge distributions interacting via a mean-field Coulomb potential. The analysis extends to comparing results with asymmetric electrolytes.

Key observables. Mean-field potential $\phi(x)$, free energy ($F$), entropy differences, and the average dipole moment ($ar{P}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The nonlocality scale ($\ell \sim \ell_{ ext{mol}}$), the dielectric permittivity ($\epsilon$), and the charge sign.

Assumptions / limitations. The theory relies on the mean-field approximation, which is an upper bound, and assumes the rigid-charge distribution model (neglecting induced dipoles).

Paper structure. The paper details the mean-field approach, discusses the violation of effective C-symmetry, and compares the findings with established nonlinear Poisson-Boltzmann equations.

Abstract

We study an equilibrium system, where linear molecules with a rigid charge distribution interact via the mean-field generated by the Coulomb coupling. We focus on the nonlinear regime, which is valid close to external charges immersed in the system. The finite molecular size in this regime leads to a nonlocal response at short distances. First, we show that the mean-field potential is non-monotonic, i.e., like charges can attract each other. Second, we show that the polarization induced by an external charge (and hence its solvation structure) depends on the sign of this charge, violating the effective C-symmetry of the macroscopic electrodynamics. We identify several scenarios of this effect, characterize it with the free energy and entropy differences, and compare it with analogous effects in asymmetric electrolytes described by nonlinear Poisson-Boltzmann equations.

On The Complexity of Redundancy-Free Quantum Hamiltonians

Authors: Matthew B. Hastings, Alexander Schmidhuber
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.03697v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work analyzes the computational difficulty associated with preparing quantum states governed by 'redundancy-free Hamiltonians.' It establishes that while the problem is tractable for low inverse temperatures ($eta$), it becomes NP-hard for large $eta$. The research provides theoretical tools to understand the complexity boundaries in quantum simulation and quantum information processing.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a rigorous framework for understanding the computational limits of quantum simulation protocols (like HDQI) by linking Hamiltonian structure directly to complexity classes (NP-hard, QMA-complete).

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper characterizes the computational hardness of preparing quantum states, specifically the thermofield double state, associated with a specialized class of Hamiltonians called 'redundancy-free Hamiltonians.'

Main result. The complexity of state preparation is shown to be easy at quadratically lower temperatures than for general Hamiltonians, while estimating the ground state energy is proven to be QMA-complete.

Method. The analysis employs techniques from statistical mechanics (polymer expansion) and quantum complexity theory, establishing bounds and hardness results based on the structure of the Hamiltonian's anticommutation graph.

Model / system. The focus is on 'redundancy-free Hamiltonians,' which are sums of products of Paulis. The complexity is analyzed using the structure defined by the Hamiltonian's anticommutation graph, leading to the concept of an 'anticommutation glass.'

Key observables. The partition function $ ext{Tr}(e^{-eta H})$ and the fidelity of state preparation are key observables.

Important parameters / regimes. The complexity is governed by the parameter $\kappa = eta^2 d$, where $d$ is the degree of the anticommutation graph, defining regimes of 'easy' vs. 'hard' computation.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis often simplifies by focusing on the Pauli case and relies on convergence conditions for the polymer expansion to hold for small $\kappa$.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by defining the redundancy-free Hamiltonians, analyzing the partition function via polymer expansions, establishing the complexity regimes ($\kappa$), and concluding with hardness results for state preparation and ground state energy estimation.

Abstract

We consider a class of redudancy-free Hamiltonians, which are those where the trace vanishes for any product of Hamiltonian terms in which at least one term appears an odd number of times. This includes Hamiltonians which are sums of products of Paulis with no relations between them. These Hamiltonians arise naturally in Hamiltonian Decoded Quantum Interferometry, which separates the hardness of preparing quantum states into two steps: a classical decoding step, and a step preparing the thermofield double state of such a redundancy-free Hamiltonian. We focus on the hardness of these Hamiltonians, both for intrinsic interest and to understand the complexity of the state preparation step in Hamiltonian DQI. We present several results showing that the problem becomes easy at quadratically lower temperature than for a general Hamiltonian, with the complexity at given inverse temperature $β$ depending on $β^2 d$, where $d$ is the degree of the anticommutation graph. We give an efficient classical algorithm for small $β^2 d$ to approximate the partition function, while for large $β^2 d$ we show that approximating the partition function is NP-hard. To this end, we introduce what we call an anticommutation glass, where frustration arises purely from anticommutation relations. We give a subexponential time quantum algorithm to prepare the thermofield state for small $β^2 d$, and present some results toward a polynomial time algorithm based on the Feiguin-Klich Hamiltonian. As a result of possible independent interest, we show that this Hamiltonian leads to a polynomial time algorithm for preparing thermofield double states of general Hamiltonians on an interaction graph of degree $d$ for $β\lesssim 1/d$. Finally, we show that estimating the ground state energy of redundancy-free Hamiltonians is QMA-complete.

Projective symmetry group classification of fermionic $Z_2$ spin liquids on the dipolar-octupolar pyrochlore magnets

Authors: Rohit Deb, Sambuddha Sanyal
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.02409v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work classifies $Z_2$ quantum spin liquids on pyrochlore magnets by treating the magnetic moments as fermionic partons. By incorporating the specific dipolar-octupolar symmetry, the authors find that the resulting gapless spinon spectra correctly account for the fractionalized thermodynamics observed in real materials. This provides a detailed, symmetry-enforced theoretical framework for understanding frustrated magnetism.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous application of symmetry constraints (PSG) to classify exotic quantum ground states, and the explicit derivation of fermionic excitations, provides a powerful theoretical tool for understanding strongly correlated magnetic materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To systematically construct and classify all symmetry-preserving $Z_2$ quantum spin liquid (QSL) states on the dipolar-octupolar (DO) pyrochlore lattice.

Main result. The distinct DO symmetry action selects a physically different set of symmetry-allowed mean-field channels, leading to a fermionic-parton account for gapless, fractionalized thermodynamics observed in materials like Nd2ScNbO7.

Method. The classification uses the Projective Symmetry Group (PSG) formalism applied to fermionic parton representations, followed by self-consistent mean-field energy minimization.

Model / system. The system is the pyrochlore lattice, modeled by an effective pseudospin-1/2 DO Hamiltonian that reduces to an anisotropic XYZ model. The analysis maps the spin-spin interaction into quadratic fermionic mean-field terms.

Key observables. Spinon band structures, low-temperature specific heat (power-law exponent), and the dynamical spin structure factor.

Important parameters / regimes. The anisotropic exchange couplings ($ ilde{J}_x, ilde{J}_z$) defining the phase diagram, and the chemical potential ($\chi_1$) governing unit cell periodicity.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on mean-field approximations, and the distinct DO symmetry action is crucial, differentiating the results from simpler spin-1/2 or bosonic models.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the pyrochlore lattice structure, sublattices, and the extended unit cell used for mean-field calculations.

Paper structure. The paper systematically covers the Hamiltonian definition, the PSG/symmetry classification framework, the derivation of the fermionic mean-field ansatz, and finally, the calculation of key observables and phase diagrams.

Abstract

We construct and classify $Z_2$ quantum spin liquids (QSLs) of fermionic partons on the dipolar-octupolar (DO) pyrochlore lattice. The DO pseudospin shares the space-group and time-reversal algebra of the effective spin-1/2 Kramers doublet, its projective symmetry group (PSG) yields the same 48 symmetric $Z_2$ classes as the generic fermionic classification. We show, the distinct DO symmetry action, encoded in a set of sublattice-independent operators, selects a physically different set of symmetry-allowed mean-field channels, so that the resulting ansatze, spinon spectra, and observables differ from both the effective spin-1/2 case and the bosonic DO classification. Decomposing the anisotropic XYZ exchange model into all symmetric singlet and triplet hopping and pairing channels and minimizing the mean-field energy self-consistently, we obtain phase diagrams in the ($\tilde{J_x}, \tilde{J_z}$) plane in which only a few $Z_2$ states, several supporting gapless or flat-band spinon spectra, are stabilized. For these states we compute the spinon band structures, the low-temperature specific heat, and the dynamical spin structure factor, and we argue that the power-law specific-heat exponent is a diagnostic of the spinon nodal structure. A near-linear-in-temperature specific heat together with broad continua in the structure factor gives a fermionic-parton account of the gapless, fractionalized thermodynamics reported in DO materials such as $Nd_2ScNbO_7$, complementing the bosonic quantum-spin-ice picture of the Ce-based compounds. The work extends the fermionic-parton classification of DO pyrochlore QSLs beyond the $U(1)$ sector, where $Z_2$ topological order is realized equally naturally by fermionic and bosonic spinons.

Quantum estimation, channel orders, and private capacity

Authors: Christoph Hirche
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.03706v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops a comprehensive framework to analyze the private capacity of quantum channels by connecting it to the measured $\chi^2$ divergence derived from binary estimation. It establishes a rigorous hierarchy of channel orderings and provides sharp, quantitative bounds for capacity and antidegradability, explaining when and why these information measures are equivalent or distinct.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides deep structural insights into quantum channel capacity, linking concrete estimation tasks (like MMSE) to abstract concepts like antidegradability, which is crucial for understanding information leakage in quantum communication protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to characterize the private capacity of quantum channels and establish structural conditions for when zero private capacity occurs, especially when this condition does not imply antidegradability.

Main result. The authors establish a hierarchy connecting complete measured-$\chi^2$ ordering to quantum $\chi^2$ ordering, providing sharp thresholds for private capacity and antidegradability for various noisy channels.

Method. The work heavily utilizes measured $\chi^2$ divergence derived from binary estimation tasks, connecting these measurements to fundamental quantum information measures like private capacity and relative entropy.

Model / system. The analysis focuses on general quantum channels ($\Lambda$) acting on qubits and qutrits, involving an input, a receiver, and an environment. Specific models include Werner-Holevo channels and Pauli half-erasure channels.

Key observables. Private Capacity $P(N)$, Minimum Mean-Square Error (MMSE), measured $\chi^2$ divergence ($\chi^2_m$), and various channel orderings ($\preceq$).

Important parameters / regimes. Blocklength ($n$), the degree of the channel (e.g., qutrit extension), and the error parameters ($p$ in erasure channels).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on connecting operational tasks (like binary estimation) to theoretical channel properties, and in some parts, assumes specific structural forms for the channels (e.g., for the simulator construction).

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the use of measured $\chi^2$ divergence in binary estimation at both the receiver and the environment.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first connecting private capacity to channel orders using measured $\chi^2$ (Chunk 1). It then develops the mathematical framework by relating $\chi^2_m$ to MMSE and establishing the hierarchy of orderings (Chunk 2). Finally, it applies these tools to analyze specific channel properties like antidegradability and capacity bounds using qutrit and general channel constructions (Chunks 3 & 4).

Abstract

Recent examples have shown that zero private capacity need not imply antidegradability and that two channels with zero private capacity can nevertheless transmit private information together. Existing entropic channel orders compare what a receiver and its environment can learn and thereby bound capacities. We connect these orders to the recent constructions through binary estimation, whose minimum mean-square error is governed by measured $χ^2$ divergence. A new integral representation shows how measured $χ^2$ on a qubit extension recovers quantum $χ^2$. It lets us pass from complete measured-$χ^2$ ordering to complete quantum-$χ^2$, relative-entropy, and less-noisy ordering. Zhu and Wang used a signed lift to show that their qutrit channel has zero private capacity. We show that Hermitian-smoothed measured-$χ^2$ ordering characterizes when such lifts exist, even with a quantum reference. Environmental dominance in binary estimation at every blocklength yields a finite-code reliability--secrecy bound. These results explain why complete comparison prevents activation with antidegradable helpers whereas regularized comparison alone does not. Building on that qutrit example, we establish this stability for a range of noisy Werner--Holevo channels, determine sharp private-capacity and antidegradability thresholds, and compute exact complementary capacities in a nondegradable range. By contrast, we extend Pauli half-erasure activation to all $1/2\le p<1$ and establish the same range for a new four-level family. Reference-assisted measured-$χ^2$ witnesses show why these activating constructions lack complete comparison.

Quantum Fire with Delegated Cloning

Authors: Rohit Chatterjee, Ananta Mukherjee, Vir Pathak, Supartha Podder
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.03593v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops 'delegatable quantum fire,' a cryptographic primitive that restricts the ability to clone quantum states to only those possessing a specific key. It provides two constructions, one using a classical key and another using reusable quantum keys called 'torches.' The work advances quantum cryptography by ensuring that cloning power can be precisely controlled and limited.

Why it may be interesting. While highly theoretical, the concepts of controlled quantum state manipulation and key-based access control are fundamental to quantum cryptography, which underpins future quantum networking and secure computation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the need to restrict the ability to clone quantum states (quantum fire) to only authorized parties by introducing key-based delegation mechanisms.

Main result. Two constructions are presented: one using a classical secret key and another, 'torch-fire,' using reusable quantum keys ('torches') that limit cloning to the number of available keys.

Method. The work is developed in the classical-oracle model, building upon existing quantum cryptographic primitives like one-shot signatures and quantum fire.

Model / system. The model is based on quantum information theory, involving quantum states called 'flames' and 'torches,' and relies on the framework of quantum oracles.

Key observables. The ability to clone a flame state, the number of cloning generations allowed, and the global non-proliferation of cloning capability.

Important parameters / regimes. The number of available torches ($m$), the number of cloning generations ($O(\log \lambda)$), and the error bound in circuit approximations ($\leq 2\sqrt{d}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The constructions are relative to classical oracles, and the analysis relies on the 'delayed collapsing property' of the underlying schemes.

Figures summary. Not specified.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the problem of delegating quantum fire, then presents two constructions (classical key and torch-fire) addressing key-controlled cloning. Later sections detail the circuit implementation of these concepts, including the 'Fire.TagO' circuit and the approximation of general quantum queries.

Abstract

Quantum fire is a recently introduced cryptographic primitive consisting of efficiently preparable quantum states, called \emph{flames}, that admit efficient cloning but resist efficient telegraphing, namely reconstruction via classical communication without preshared entanglement. In all prior constructions of quantum fire, cloning is a public operation that requires no separate key and every holder of a flame state can clone it. For applications to access control, however, an issuer may wish to delegate cloning to designated quantum servers while withholding this capability from other flame holders. To address this, we introduce \emph{delegatable quantum fire}, in which cloning requires a separate key. We give two constructions in the classical-oracle model. Our first construction uses a classical secret key which enables cloning, and any user with the entire key may clone successfully. Our second construction, which we call \emph{torch-fire}, uses quantum cloning keys, called \emph{torches}, which enable cloning while remaining unaffected in the process but cannot otherwise be split or delegated to enable additional cloning. An efficient adversary given $m$ torches cannot, except with negligible probability, enable more than $m$ noncommunicating parties to each clone a fresh, independently issued challenge flame. The adversary may jointly process its resources before separating the parties and distribute arbitrarily entangled registers among them. Both constructions are in the oracle model, relying on public classical oracles that allow queries in quantum superposition.

Quantum transmission in 1D disordered stealthy hyperuniform Kronig--Penney-like models

Summary. This theoretical work analyzes quantum transport in 1D disordered systems exhibiting stealthy hyperuniformity. By developing a high-order perturbation theory, the authors show that the SHU condition causes the leading localization effects to vanish up to the third order. This demonstrates that structural order can parametrically enhance quantum transmission, leading to a much weaker, fourth-order dependence on disorder strength.

Why it may be interesting. The systematic, high-order perturbative calculation provides deep insight into how specific geometric constraints (hyperuniformity) can fundamentally alter the scaling of quantum localization, which is a core topic in condensed matter theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates quantum transmission and localization in one-dimensional disordered systems characterized by 'stealthy hyperuniform (SHU)' disorder.

Main result. The SHU condition causes the leading attenuation of the Lyapunov exponent to vanish up to the third order in the scattering strength, shifting the leading localization dependence to the fourth order ($O(W^4)$).

Method. The authors employ advanced perturbative quantum field theory techniques, utilizing Feynman diagrams to calculate the Lyapunov exponent and inverse lifetime in the thermodynamic limit.

Model / system. The model is a Kronig-Penney-like system describing quantum particle propagation through a continuum of identical point scatterers in 1D, governed by a delta-function potential.

Key observables. Lyapunov exponent ($\lambda(k)$), transmission $T(E)$, inverse lifetime ($1/ au(k)$), and the structure factor $S(k)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The scattering strength ($W$), the wave vector ($k$), and the hyperuniformity cutoff ($K$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on taking the thermodynamic limit ($N o \infty$) and making specific approximations for summing over momenta in the perturbative expansion.

Figures summary. Figure 3 illustrates Feynman diagrams for calculating scattering coefficients up to the fourth order, distinguishing between different types of loop summations (e.g., 1-loop, 2-loop).

Paper structure. The paper systematically develops the perturbative calculation of the Lyapunov exponent and inverse lifetime, first for free boundary conditions, and then adapts the formalism for periodic boundary conditions, culminating in the analysis of the SHU regime.

Abstract

Disordered stealthy hyperuniform (SHU) systems are emerging as platforms for controlling classical and quantum transport due to vanishing single-scattering contributions in the stealthy regime. Here, to our knowledge, we present the first perturbation theory of one-dimensional quantum transport through a continuum of identical point scatterers with SHU positional disorder. Our perturbation theory indicates the exact cancellation of all the lower-order terms due to the Fourier-space stealthy condition $S(q)=0$, and the further suppression of the lowest non-vanishing fourth order in the Lyapunov exponent $λ(k)$ due to the real-space hyperuniform constraint $\lim\limits_{D\to\infty}σ_D^2=c<\infty$. Our findings presents universal implications of the general wave-propagation phenomena in disordered SHU systems.

Rate-Optimal Quantum Discrete Simulation Optimization

Authors: Mingjie Hu, Jian-Qiang Hu
Type: both · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.03360v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops a theoretical framework to determine the quantum advantage in discrete simulation optimization under a fixed budget. By establishing a minimax lower bound, the authors propose the QUEST algorithm, which is proven to achieve this optimal rate. The work provides strong theoretical and empirical evidence for quantum speedup in complex simulation estimation tasks.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on optimization, the development of the quantum simulation oracle and the analysis of fundamental quantum lower bounds for estimation tasks are highly relevant to understanding resource scaling in quantum information processing.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates whether quantum computing can accelerate the convergence rate of discrete simulation optimization problems under a fixed simulation budget.

Main result. The proposed algorithm, QUEST, achieves an expected optimality gap of order O((|X| log T)/T), matching the established quantum minimax lower bound up to logarithmic factors.

Method. The authors establish a minimax lower bound by reducing the problem to a sequential quantum phase-testing problem, and then propose an adaptive quantum algorithm combining quantum Monte Carlo with sequential elimination.

Model / system. The problem is modeled using a quantum simulation oracle, which acts as the quantum analogue for estimating the expected performance of a discrete decision variable $x$ in a stochastic system.

Key observables. Expected optimality gap, which measures the difference between the optimal decision's mean performance and the algorithm's recommended decision's mean performance.

Important parameters / regimes. Fixed simulation budget ($T$), the number of decisions ($|X|$), and the convergence rate of the expected optimality gap.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the centered simulation output is $\sigma$-sub-Gaussian, and the performance is measured under a fixed-budget setting.

Figures summary. Numerical experiments show that QUEST significantly reduces the expected optimality gap compared to classical Monte Carlo estimation (MC-EST) across varying problem sizes and distributions.

Paper structure. The paper first formulates the problem and defines the quantum oracle, then establishes the minimax lower bounds, proposes the QUEST algorithm, proves its rate optimality, and concludes with numerical validation.

Abstract

Quantum computing has developed rapidly in recent years and has shown potential advantages over classical computing in various domains. In this paper, we study whether quantum computing can accelerate the convergence rate of discrete simulation optimization in the fixed-budget setting. We first formulate the quantum discrete simulation optimization problem by defining the quantum simulation oracle, the algorithmic performance measure, and the corresponding optimality guarantee. Based on this formulation, we establish a minimax lower bound on the expected optimality gap for any quantum algorithm. The lower bound analysis reduces the discrete simulation optimization problem to a sequential quantum phase-testing problem, and the resulting lower-bound argument may be of independent interest. By comparing the quantum lower bound with its classical counterpart, we show that the optimal convergence rate in the quantum setting exhibits a quadratic improvement in its dependence on the simulation budget, at the cost of a worse dependence on the number of decisions. We then propose the first fixed-budget quantum algorithm, called \textsc{QUEST}, for discrete simulation optimization. The algorithm combines a quantum Monte Carlo subroutine with a sequential elimination framework and uses a slow-measurement mechanism to help preserve quantum superposition during the optimization process. We prove that \textsc{QUEST} achieves the optimal convergence rate up to logarithmic factors. Numerical experiments further show that \textsc{QUEST} outperforms its classical counterpart, which provides empirical evidence for the quantum advantage in improving the convergence rate of discrete simulation optimization algorithms.

Summary. This paper presents a theoretical blueprint for building a scalable Quantum Random Access Memory (QRAM) that operates passively, meaning its energy consumption scales sublinearly with the memory size. By evolving a fixed, local Hamiltonian, the authors achieve a significantly reduced energy cost ($O(\log N)$) and runtime ($O(\log^2 N)$) compared to conventional active QRAM implementations. Why it may be interesting. The focus on Hamiltonian engineering to achieve sublinear energy scaling ($O(\log N)$) for a fundamental quantum primitive like QRAM is highly relevant to open quantum systems and quantum hardware design.

Abstract

Counterdiabatic driving provides a universal route to fast quantum control, but its standard formulation presupposes a Hamiltonian generator. We develop a Hamiltonian-free theory of the adiabatic gauge potential (AGP) for parameter-dependent unitary operators $U_λ$. Exact and regularized versions of the AGP follow from a Hermitian, banded linear system at a cost comparable to the Lanczos construction in the Hermitian case, requiring neither diagonalization nor a matrix logarithm. We demonstrate rapid convergence of the truncated Krylov approximation for the kicked top and for an integrable brickwork circuit built from unitary $\check{R}$ matrices, a setting in which no local effective Hamiltonian is available. Our results extend shortcuts to adiabaticity, and AGP-based diagnostics of chaos, to Floquet systems and quantum circuits.

Abstract

We establish lower bounds for Hamiltonian property testing with access to the time-evolution operator but not its inverse. Each experiment may query the time-evolution operator multiple times, and distances between Hamiltonians are measured in the normalized Frobenius norm. In this model, we show that testing whether a Hamiltonian is $k$-local or $\varepsilon$-far from every $k$-local Hamiltonian requires $Ω(1/\varepsilon^2)$ total evolution time, matching the upper bound of Kallaugher and Liang (TQC'25). We also prove that testing whether an unknown Hamiltonian equals a target Hamiltonian or is $\varepsilon$-far from it requires $Ω(1/\varepsilon^2)$ total evolution time, matching the upper bound of Sinha and Tong (2025). These are the first lower bounds for natural problems in Hamiltonian learning and testing that rule out Heisenberg-limited scaling of $1/\varepsilon$. As a third result, we show that amplitude estimation to precision $\varepsilon$ requires $Ω(1/\varepsilon^2)$ total time evolution, recovering the result of Tang and Wright (QIP'26) in the continuous-time query model. All three results follow from the hardness of distinguishing the zero Hamiltonian from a suitably chosen ensemble of random Hamiltonians. We establish this hardness by adapting the continuous-time adversary method to forward Hamiltonian evolution.
