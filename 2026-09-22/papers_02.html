A memory-information window in finite-duration quantum control

Authors: Doyeol, Ahn
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21380v1

Summary. This paper develops a theoretical framework to probe environmental memory in quantum systems using coherent control. By analyzing the process map's dependence on noise correlation time ($ au_c$) versus gate duration ($t_g$), the authors identify a 'memory-information window.' This window shows that optimal noise characterization occurs when the environmental and control timescales are comparable.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete, measurable principle—the timescale-matching principle—for designing quantum experiments to characterize complex, realistic noise environments beyond simple Markovian assumptions.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how the finite correlation time of environmental noise (non-Markovianity) can be experimentally detected using coherent quantum control protocols.

Main result. The ability to extract information about the noise correlation time is maximized when the environmental and control timescales are comparable, defining a 'memory-information window.'

Method. The authors use exact stochastic trajectory calculations and analyze the parameter identifiability of the quantum process map by examining the Jacobian matrix of the process evolution.

Model / system. The system involves quantum control operations (e.g., single-qubit rotations, two-qubit gates) subjected to non-Markovian environmental noise, modeled using the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process.

Key observables. Process map elements, Pauli-transfer matrix (PTM) elements, and the smallest singular value of the process Jacobian.

Important parameters / regimes. Noise variance ($\sigma^2$), correlation time ($ au_c$), and gate duration ($t_g$), analyzed via the dimensionless ratio $ au_c/t_g$.

Assumptions / limitations. The noise is modeled as Gaussian noise with a finite correlation time, and the analysis relies on the concept of independent parameter identifiability.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the time-ordered dressing of stochastic interactions, showing the memory-information window, and comparing information observability for different control types (commuting vs. noncommuting).

Paper structure. The paper develops the theoretical framework for noise-induced process evolution, analyzes the short-memory and long-memory limits, and uses stochastic simulations to pinpoint the optimal timescale matching for information extraction.

Abstract

Environmental noise is a major source of decoherence and errors in quantum systems. In a non-Markovian environment, the noise has a finite correlation time, and the system can retain information about its previous interactions with the environment. Here, we investigate how such environmental memory can be observed through a finite-duration coherent quantum operation. Coherent control dynamically reshapes the stochastic coupling operator and thereby changes how two-time noise correlations affect the quantum process. For Ornstein-Uhlenbeck noise, we show analytically that two different limits reduce the information on the correlation time. In the short-memory limit, the leading dynamics depend on the integrated-noise combination σ/τc, where σ is the noise variance and τc is the correlation time, making these parameters locally degenerate. In the long-memory limit, the stochastic field becomes quasi-static during the operation, and sensitivity to the correlation time is suppressed. Exact stochastic simulations for a driven single-qubit Xπ rotation and a two-qubit exchange gate show that independent correlation-time information becomes largest between these limits, when the environmental and control timescales are comparable. Noncommuting controls enhance this information, whereas commuting controls do not. Simulations at different gate durations tg further show that the information window approximately follows the dimensionless correlation ratio τc/tg. These results provide a timescale-matching principle for probing finite-correlation non-Markovian noise with coherent quantum control.

Summary. This paper tackles the computational bottleneck in quantum game theory by developing tensor-contraction methods to handle large joint Hilbert spaces. It introduces the MEFPIA algorithm, a novel fixed-point iteration technique, which successfully finds quantum game equilibria with superior convergence compared to established methods. This provides a powerful tool for analyzing complex decision-making processes in quantum multi-agent systems.

Why it may be interesting. While the core topic is game theory, the reliance on density matrices, entropy, and fixed-point iteration connects to concepts in open quantum systems and non-equilibrium dynamics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge addressed is the computational difficulty of finding equilibrium strategies in multi-agent quantum games due to the exponential growth of the joint Hilbert space dimension.

Main result. The proposed Matrix Exponential Fixed-Point Iteration with Annealing (MEFPIA) algorithm is shown to be a promising and efficient numerical method for searching for these equilibrium points, achieving lower error in fewer steps than existing methods.

Method. The authors derive tensor-contraction expressions for payoff functions and gradients to circumvent explicit construction of the full joint density matrix, and then apply the MEFPIA algorithm.

Model / system. The study focuses on the Extended Gutoski-Watrous (EGW) game, where players' quantum strategies are modeled by local density matrices ($ ho$). The system is defined on a joint Hilbert space $H = igotimes_{i=1}^N H(i)$.

Key observables. Payoff functions, gradients, relative payoff error, and convergence rate.

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature ($T$) used in the annealing process, and the local dimensions of the players' Hilbert spaces.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on simplifying the calculation of traces and gradients using tensor contraction rules to manage computational complexity.

Paper structure. The paper builds from the theoretical framework of quantum game theory, details the computational simplification using tensor contractions, proposes the MEFPIA algorithm, and compares its performance against the MMWU algorithm.

Abstract

In recent years, quantum game theory has gained significant attention as a framework for studying decision-making in multi-agent systems using quantum principles. However, computing equilibrium strategies is challenging because the dimension of the joint Hilbert space grows as the product of the players' local dimensions. In this paper, we consider an extended Gutoski-Watrous (EGW) game in which each player's quantum strategy is represented by a local density matrix. We derive tensor-contraction expressions for the payoff functions and their gradients, thereby avoiding the explicit construction of the full joint density matrix and its computationally expensive multiplication by the payoff operators. Building on the resulting effective Hamiltonians, we propose the Matrix Exponential Fixed-Point Iteration with Annealing (MEFPIA) algorithm to search for equilibrium points in EGW games. We compare MEFPIA with the Matrix Multiplicative Weights Update (MMWU) algorithm in terms of convergence. For the tested instances and parameter settings, both algorithms approach the same strategy profiles and payoffs, while MEFPIA achieves lower relative error in fewer iterations. These results indicate that MEFPIA is a promising numerical method for equilibrium search in multi-agent quantum games. Our findings provide important insights into the quantum game theory's potential for addressing complex decision-making processes, as well as opening up new paths for future research and exploration in multi-agent quantum systems.

Summary. This paper develops an optimal control strategy for enhancing temperature sensing using single NV centers coupled to hydrogels. By implementing QSP-inspired coherent control sequences, the authors demonstrate a significant gain in measurement sensitivity (up to 60% in Fisher information) compared to standard Ramsey methods. This shows that optimizing the measurement sequence, rather than just the physical setup, is crucial for high-performance quantum sensing in practical settings.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the optimization of quantum sensing protocols in realistic, resource-constrained environments (like biosensing), providing quantitative gains by optimizing the measurement sequence itself.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to improve the sensitivity of temperature measurements using single Nitrogen-Vacancy (NV) centers in hydrogels, particularly when the measurement is limited by the duty cycle.

Main result. The authors achieved a reproducible 25–27% per-shot sensitivity gain over optimized Ramsey interrogation, corresponding to a 57–60% gain in Fisher information.

Method. They employed quantum control techniques, specifically QSP-inspired coherent control, applied to a pulsed single-NV measurement sequence.

Model / system. The system involves single NV centers coupled to stimulus-responsive hydrogels, which transduce temperature changes into magnetic field shifts. The physics is modeled using a sigmoidal volume-phase-transition model and simulated via Lindblad master equations.

Key observables. Per-shot sensitivity gain, Fisher information gain, and the rate gain governed by the measurement duty cycle.

Important parameters / regimes. The advantage is most pronounced when the dephasing time ($T_2$) is short compared with the measurement overhead or dose-limited waiting time.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes a point-dipole magnetic field and that the primary decoherence mechanism is dephasing ($\sigma_z$).

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the schematic of the sensor (hydrogel states), the comparison of signal envelopes (calibrated vs. uncalibrated), and global gain maps showing the dependence on control depth and time.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theoretical framework by modeling the temperature-to-field transduction, comparing standard Ramsey interrogation against optimized coherent control protocols, and deriving the resulting sensitivity gains.

Abstract

Stimulus-responsive hydrogels convert temperature changes into magnetic-field shifts detectable by nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers, enabling nanoscale thermometry in soft and biological environments. Existing hydrogel-nanodiamond demonstrations rely on NV ensembles, whose high photon throughput is accompanied by gradient-induced inhomogeneous broadening, while idealized single-NV projections assume high-fluence fluorescence/ODMR readout. Here we study a pulsed single-NV route for the same class of sensors and ask whether decoherence-aware coherent control can improve thermometric performance over optimized Ramsey interrogation at equal detected-photon budget. Using a sigmoidal volume-phase-transition model, dipolar magnetic transduction, and Lindblad master-equation simulations, we find a reproducible 25-27% per-shot sensitivity gain over optimized Ramsey, i.e., a 57-60% gain in Fisher information (1.57-1.60). The same gain carries over to the photon-normalized Fisher information. The rate gain is governed by the measurement duty cycle, the fraction of the experimental cycle spent accumulating signal rather than initializing, reading out or waiting, and becomes largest when the overhead or optical-dose constraint dominates the cycle time. The optimized trajectories reveal a response-shaping mechanism in which phase accumulation is concentrated near the end of the sequence, and a closed-form depth-two solution reproduces the numerical optimum and exhibits that mechanism analytically. The advantage is most pronounced when the dephasing time is short compared with the measurement overhead or dose-limited waiting time, which is the operating regime targeted by hydrogel-transduced single-spin biosensing.

Single-atom-based asynchronous photonic interconnect for scalable modular quantum computing

Authors: Jérémy Raskop, Nadav Kandel, Yaniv Amichy, Yaron Jarach, Tal Kanonich, Andrei Militaru, Johannes Fink, Barak Dayan
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21961v1

Summary. This paper proposes a memory-assisted quantum interconnect using a trapped atom in a cavity to link modular quantum processors. By allowing photons to arrive sequentially rather than simultaneously, the protocol achieves a linear scaling of the entanglement rate, dramatically improving scalability over existing quadratic-scaling methods. This breakthrough removes a major bottleneck for building large-scale quantum computers.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses a core bottleneck in quantum networking by providing a theoretically derived, high-rate protocol that circumvents the quadratic scaling limitations inherent in standard linear-optics entanglement distribution.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Scaling quantum computation requires robust quantum interconnects to distribute entanglement between modular quantum processing units (QPUs).

Main result. The proposed memory-assisted interconnect achieves an entanglement rate scaling linearly ($\propto p$) with photon probability, offering orders-of-magnitude improvement over the quadratic scaling ($\propto p^2$) of conventional linear-optics methods.

Method. The method utilizes a memory-assisted Bell-State Measurement (BSM) mediated by a single trapped atom in a cavity, allowing for sequential photon detection rather than simultaneous arrival.

Model / system. The system involves single trapped neutral atoms (e.g., Rb) coupled to a high-finesse cavity, implementing photon-atom controlled-Z (CZ) gates. The analysis models the rate by accounting for sequential photon interactions and decoherence.

Key observables. Entanglement rate ($\Gamma$), infidelity ($\delta_{tot}$), and the scaling dependence on photon delivery probability ($p$).

Important parameters / regimes. Photon delivery probability ($p$), cavity cooperativity ($C$), coherence time ($ar{T}^*$), and the total infidelity budget ($\delta$).

Assumptions / limitations. The protocol assumes the memory coherence time is long enough to allow for sequential photon arrivals, and the analysis models the total infidelity as a sum of added noise and memory errors.

Figures summary. Figures compare entanglement rate scaling ($\propto p^2$ vs. $\propto p$), illustrate the sequential vs. simultaneous arrival process, and show trade-offs between rate and pump power/noise.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the limitation of quadratic scaling in standard interconnects. It then details the memory-assisted protocol using sequential CZ gates. The latter sections focus on rigorous rate derivations, modeling the total infidelity budget, and optimizing operational parameters based on noise trade-offs.

Abstract

Scaling quantum computation beyond the capacity of a single quantum processing unit requires quantum interconnects between modular processors. Optical photons are natural carriers for distributing entanglement between these processors. Most loss-resilient protocols use photonic Bell-state measurements based on the linear-optics type-II fusion gate. The resulting entanglement rate scales quadratically with each processor's typically low photon-delivery probability. Here we analyze a memory-assisted quantum interconnect using a near-deterministic, robust photon--atom controlled-$Z$ gate via a single atom trapped in a high-finesse cavity. Detecting and measuring a photon from one processor heralds entanglement between that processor and the atom. This entanglement is preserved while the process repeats with the second processor until the second photon is detected and measured. Reading out the atomic qubit finalizes the entanglement between the processors. As the entanglement is mediated by the atom, the photons from both processors do not need to be indistinguishable, removing a major source of infidelity. Furthermore, by removing the simultaneous photon-arrival requirement, the protocol allows the entanglement rate to scale linearly rather than quadratically with photon-arrival probability over a wide parameter range. We derive entanglement rates under realistic parameters, accounting for decay of the atom's entanglement with the first processor and for decoherence caused by unheralded photon interactions. The nanosecond-scale of the gate and read-out operations leads to orders-of-magnitude entanglement-rate gain over linear optics, removing a key bottleneck in modular quantum computing.

Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21585v1

Summary. This paper provides a rigorous, exact calculation of DeWitt kernels for quantum cosmology by implementing the DeWitt boundary condition in the Lorentzian path integral. It establishes general construction rules and derives explicit kernel forms for various cosmological models, confirming the consistency between different mathematical approaches for defining quantum propagators in constrained systems.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on cosmology, the rigorous mathematical techniques for defining propagators via path integrals over gauge degrees of freedom (the lapse $N$) and handling boundary conditions (like the Dirichlet condition) are highly relevant to open quantum systems and quantum field theory in curved spacetime.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the technical challenge of correctly implementing the DeWitt boundary condition within the Lorentzian path integral formulation of minisuper-space quantum cosmology.

Main result. The authors provide general definitions and exact construction rules for DeWitt kernels, yielding explicit, closed-form expressions for these kernels in various cosmological backgrounds (e.g., de Sitter space).

Method. The methodology involves using the Batalin–Fradkin–Vilkovisky (BFV) path integral approach, analyzing lapse integrals over different contours (positive half-line vs. whole real line), and imposing the DeWitt condition as a boundary constraint.

Model / system. The physical system is minisuper-space quantum cosmology, governed by a constraint operator derived from the metric $ds^2 = \sigma^2 (-N^2 q dt^2 + q d\Omega^2)$. The dynamics are described by the Wheeler–DeWitt equation.

Key observables. The DeWitt kernels, specifically the positive-lapse kernel ($G^+$) and the whole-real-line kernel ($G_R$), are the primary observables.

Important parameters / regimes. The cosmological constant ($\Lambda$), the scale factor variable ($q$), and the boundary condition parameter ($\alpha$) are key parameters.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is restricted to a single self-adjoint constraint operator with the flat measure $dN$, neglecting full gauge fixing and Faddeev–Popov measures.

Paper structure. The paper develops the general framework for constructing DeWitt kernels, derives specific forms for the positive-lapse and whole-real-line kernels, and then applies these general results to calculate exact kernels for specific cosmological models like the closed, flat, and open de Sitter universes.

Abstract

The DeWitt boundary condition can be implemented in the Lorentzian path integral of minisuperspace quantum cosmology by summing only over non-singular paths. We give a general definition of the resulting DeWitt kernels, together with explicit rules for constructing them, and evaluate them exactly. The lapse integral over the positive half-line yields the Dirichlet Green function of the Wheeler--DeWitt operator, whereas the integral over the whole real line yields the Dirichlet group-averaging kernel; both vanish when either endpoint lies at the singularity. Since the minisuperspace actions are at most quadratic, all lapse integrals are performed in closed form without saddle-point approximations. We obtain the kernels for the closed universe without cosmological constant, where the whole-real-line kernel vanishes identically, for the flat de Sitter universe, and, in terms of Airy functions, for the closed and open de Sitter universes. Throughout, the DeWitt condition is imposed as the boundary condition at the singularity, which selects a self-adjoint constraint operator on the half-line and fixes the kernels uniquely, including their normalization.

Abstract

Efficient bidirectional microwave optical photon conversion is a key capability for scaling superconducting quantum processors into distributed networks. However, achieving the necessary conversion efficiency requires filling a critical knowledge gap in understanding the loss mechanisms of electro optic materials. Here, we characterize the microwave properties of single crystal bulk LiNbO3 and AlN over a broad range of powers, down to single photon levels, and spanning from millikelvin temperatures to above 1K. Summary. This work explores phase separation in active matter composed of inertial rotors driven by external torques. By analyzing the system's pressure and order parameters, the authors demonstrate that inertia is key to driving the system into a coexistence region between a dense hexatic solid and a dilute liquid. This provides a quantitative understanding of non-equilibrium phase transitions in active colloidal systems.

Main problem. The paper investigates the emergence of phase separation in a system of active, inertial rotors subjected to a constant applied torque, which breaks chiral symmetry.

Abstract

We uncover an emergent phase separation in a system of inertial dumbbells put into persistent rotation by a constant applied torque, breaking chiral symmetry, while interacting with each other through repulsive hard-core interactions. We numerically determine the phase diagram of the model, showing that there exists a binodal and a spinodal region where the system separates into a dense liquid and an ordered phase, which we identify as hexatic. An analysis of the system pressure unveils that the mechanism underlying the phase separation is heavily controlled by inertia. Locally injected kinetic energy is steadily stored into the system in a way that depends on density and that, at large inertia, results in a hot dilute and cool dense state, leading to a generically unstable homogeneous phase. The interplay between dumbbells' arrangement into clusters and chiral forces produces a spontaneous rigid rotation of the clusters around their centers of mass.

Abstract

Abstract

Entanglement and magic are resources that reveal complementary aspects of quantum many-body systems. Their interplay is captured by nonlocal magic, the magic that survives arbitrary local changes of basis. Yet their markedly different dynamical behavior leaves open how this irreducible component of magic spreads. Here we connect nonlocal magic to the capacity of entanglement, a tractable quantity measuring fluctuations of the entanglement Hamiltonian. This connection enables analytically controlled predictions across a wide range of many-body dynamics, from chaotic to integrable systems, which we investigate also using large-scale numerical simulations. In chaotic systems, nonlocal magic exhibits a transient buildup, with logarithmic growth in time followed by decay to a size-independent value. For integrable systems, we develop a quasiparticle picture in quantitative agreement with numerics, showing that the same initial growth instead leads to saturation at a value logarithmic in subsystem size. These contrasting behaviors have an operational consequence for entanglement embezzlement: the strongest scramblers embezzle only transiently, whereas free-fermionic dynamics can sustain universal embezzlement in the steady state.
