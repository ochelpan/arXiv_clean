Transcript-Bound Combiners for Downgrade-Resilient Hybrid Post-Quantum Key Establishment: Definition, Proof, and Embedded-Device Cost

Authors: Bhanwar Gupta, Sanjeev Rana
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21273v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops a cryptographic primitive, the Transcript-Bound Combiner, to secure hybrid Post-Quantum Key Establishment protocols. It solves the critical issue of downgrade attacks by cryptographically binding the session key to the entire handshake transcript. The solution is shown to be highly efficient for embedded devices while providing strong, provable security guarantees.

Why it may be interesting. not directly relevant

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the vulnerability of standalone hybrid Post-Quantum Key Establishment (PQC) combiners to downgrade attacks because they fail to authenticate the negotiation transcript.

Main result. By modifying the key schedule to bind the derived session key to a hash of the entire handshake transcript, the combiner achieves local downgrade resilience, resisting active attackers who attempt to strip the post-quantum component.

Method. The authors propose a game-based definition of downgrade resilience and provide a two-sided separation proof showing that transcript binding prevents downgrade attacks with negligible probability.

Model / system. The model is a cryptographic key schedule combiner that merges a post-quantum KEM (like ML-KEM) with a classical Diffie-Hellman primitive within a hybrid key establishment framework.

Key observables. The derived session key ($K$) and the integrity of the negotiation transcript ($ au$).

Important parameters / regimes. The computational overhead of transcript binding is quantified as adding about 11.8% to handshake computation but only 1.5% to radio-inclusive energy on Cortex-M4 devices.

Assumptions / limitations. The security analysis relies on the random-oracle model, and the cost model is calibrated using specific Cortex-M4 measurements.

Figures summary. Figure D1 shows that the overhead of transcript-binding remains stable (near 12%) regardless of the number of advertised cryptographic suites.

Paper structure. The paper defines the problem, proposes the transcript-bound key schedule (Equation 2), proves the two-sided separation theorem, and analyzes the concrete implementation cost using a calibrated model.

Abstract

Hybrid key establishment runs a post-quantum key-encapsulation mechanism (KEM) alongside a classical Diffie-Hellman primitive, so that the session key stays secure while either component resists attack. This design is now standardized in the Transport Layer Security protocol, Secure Shell, and the Internet Key Exchange, with the standardized module-lattice KEM (ML-KEM) as the post-quantum component. A hybrid KEM secures the derived key, but not the integrity of the negotiation that selects which primitives are used. Full protocols authenticate that negotiation through a handshake transcript; a hybrid KEM deployed as a standalone drop-in primitive, or inside a minimal handshake without transcript authentication, inherits no such guarantee, and an active attacker can strip the post-quantum option. We ask what the key schedule alone must contain to make downgrade resilience a local property of the combiner. We give a game-based definition at the combiner layer and prove a two-sided separation: a combiner that ignores the transcript is downgraded with certainty, whereas one that binds the session key and the confirmation tag to a hash of the transcript blocks every such attempt, up to a term negligible for a 256-bit transcript hash. We also give an explicit strongest-link security bound. Using a calibrated cost model composed from published Cortex-M4 measurements, transcript binding adds one hash per party - about 11.8% of handshake computation but only 1.5% of radio-inclusive energy - and adds no messages or bytes on the wire. Every reported number is produced by a released harness that passes a 30-check validation gate.

Unconditional quantum advantage from a two-round CHSH problem in one dimension

Authors: Yonghae Lee, Jeonghyeon Shin, Soojoon Lee
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21237v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper constructs a specific quantum game, the two-round 1D CHSH problem, to demonstrate an unconditional quantum advantage. It shows that a shallow quantum circuit can solve this problem optimally, while any classical circuit attempting to match this performance must exhibit super-polynomial circuit depth. This highlights that quantum advantage does not require perfect success probability or complex connectivity.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a concrete, physically realizable example demonstrating quantum advantage using only local interactions (1D geometry) and shallow circuits, which is highly relevant for near-term quantum hardware.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper establishes an unconditional quantum advantage by analyzing a relation problem derived from the two-round CHSH game in one dimension (2R1D-CHSH).

Main result. It proves that the quantum circuit, restricted to a shallow depth and one dimension, achieves the optimal CHSH success probability, while any classical circuit achieving a comparable success probability requires super-polynomial depth ($\Omega(\log N)$).

Method. The analysis compares the quantum success probability against the average success probability of randomized classical circuits, using techniques involving circuit depth bounding and temporal separation of inputs.

Model / system. The model is the 2R1D-CHSH problem, implemented on a quantum circuit of $2N$ qubits using only adjacent two-qubit gates with a depth of at most eight.

Key observables. Quantum success probability ($\omega_q( ext{CHSH}) = (2+\sqrt{2})/4$) and the required depth of the classical circuit ($\Omega(\log N)$).

Important parameters / regimes. The separation holds for any fixed $0 \le \delta < (\sqrt{2}-1)/4$. The size parameter is $N$.

Assumptions / limitations. The quantum circuit is restricted to a one-dimensional geometry, and the classical circuit is assumed to have fixed wiring and bounded gate fan-in.

Figures summary. Not specified in the notes.

Paper structure. The paper defines the CHSH game and its generalization, formally defines the 2R1D-CHSH problem, constructs the shallow quantum circuit, bounds the classical performance, and finally combines these results to prove the depth separation.

Abstract

We introduce a relation problem constructed from the Clauser--Horne--Shimony--Holt (CHSH) game, which we call the two-round one-dimensional CHSH problem. Its two-round structure ensures that the CHSH questions are supplied only after the relevant Pauli-frame data have been fixed, thereby ruling out a simple classical strategy that solves the corresponding problem perfectly when all inputs are supplied simultaneously. We construct a quantum circuit on $2N$ qubits that uses only adjacent two-qubit gates, has operational depth at most eight, and achieves the optimal quantum success probability of CHSH, which is strictly smaller than one. We prove that, for every fixed $0\leqδ<(\sqrt{2}-1)/4$, any randomized classical circuit with fixed wiring and bounded gate fan-in that achieves an average success probability of at least $(2+\sqrt{2})/4-δ$ requires depth $Ω(\log N)$ after the questions of the second round are supplied. This yields an unconditional separation even though the quantum circuit is restricted to a one-dimensional geometry, whereas the classical circuit has no geometric locality restriction. The result shows that perfect quantum success is not necessary for unconditional quantum advantage with shallow circuits.

Weighted Berry curvature and global geometry of mixed quantum states

Authors: Dominik Kuczyński, Erik Sjöqvist
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21355v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper rigorously examines the geometric properties of the Quantum Geometric Tensor for mixed quantum states. The authors demonstrate that the imaginary part of this tensor fails to define a globally consistent curvature, as its surface integral is path-dependent and not quantized. This implies that the concept of a topological invariant, like a Chern number, cannot be straightforwardly assigned to the mixed-state geometric phase using this tensor.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the topological aspects of quantum geometry for mixed states, which is crucial for understanding decoherence and geometric phases in open quantum systems, linking fundamental geometry to physical observables.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the geometric interpretation of the imaginary part of the Quantum Geometric Tensor (QGT) for mixed quantum states, specifically questioning if it corresponds to a well-defined curvature for a global U(1) line bundle.

Main result. The imaginary part of the QGT is generally not a closed form, and its surface integral is found to be surface-dependent and not quantized in integer multiples of $2\pi$, thus failing to define a first Chern number for mixed states.

Method. The authors employ mathematical analysis, comparing the proposed weighted curvature integral with the Stokes' theorem relation, and use an explicit two-level system model to test the quantization condition.

Model / system. The analysis focuses on mixed quantum states described by a density operator $ ho$, using a two-level system Hamiltonian $H = \mathbf{b} \cdot \sigma$ whose parameters vary over a parameter space (like $S^2$).

Key observables. Quantum Geometric Tensor (QGT), its imaginary part $\Omega$, and the resulting surface integral $ rac{1}{2\pi} \oint_S \Omega$.

Important parameters / regimes. The eigenvalues $\lambda_n$ of the density operator, and the path/surface $C$ and $S$ used for integration.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the existence of a connection whose curvature should yield a topological invariant (like a Chern number), which is tested against the mixed-state QGT.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the QGT for mixed states, then analyzes the mathematical closure condition ($d\Omega=0$) for the imaginary part $\Omega$. It proceeds to test this condition using a two-level system example, comparing surface integrals over different charts to demonstrate the lack of path/surface independence required for a topological invariant.

Abstract

We examine the geometric interpretation of the quantum geometric tensor proposed in [Phys. Rev. B {\bf 110}, 035404 (2024)] for mixed quantum states, focusing on its imaginary part, which is proportional to a weighted sum of the Berry curvatures of the eigenstates of the density operator. While the real part naturally decomposes into Fisher--Rao and weighted Fubini--Study contributions, we show that the imaginary part does not, in general, coincide with the curvature of a connection on a globally defined U(1) line bundle whose holonomy yields a mixed-state geometric phase. Using a two-level system as an explicit example, we demonstrate that its surface integral depends on the choice of surface bounded by the same closed path, with ambiguities that are not integer multiples of $2π$.

Weighted Quantum Signal Processing: Low-Depth Polynomial Approximation with Applications to Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks

Authors: Rohit Sarma Sarkar, Rupayan Bhattacharjee, Elias F. Combarro, Michele Grossi, Lirandë Pira, Carmen G. Almudéver, Sergi Abadal, Eduard Alarcon
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21567v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces Weighted Quantum Signal Processing (WQSP) as an advanced quantum framework to overcome the parameter and depth limitations of standard QSP for polynomial approximation. By strategically weighting the quantum circuit layers, WQSP achieves significant parameter compression while maintaining high approximation fidelity. This efficient method is successfully applied to create quantum-enhanced, parameter-efficient activation functions for Kolmogorov–Arnold Networks.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on polynomial approximation, the development of WQSP represents a novel, structured quantum circuit primitive that could be adapted to encode complex Hamiltonians or quantum state preparation protocols in quantum simulation or quantum machine learning algorithms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Standard Quantum Signal Processing (QSP) is limited by circuit depth and parity constraints when approximating high-degree polynomials. The goal is to develop a more efficient quantum framework for this task.

Main result. Weighted Quantum Signal Processing (WQSP) provides a compact, flexible, and parameter-efficient alternative to QSP, achieving comparable approximation quality with significantly fewer trainable parameters and shallower circuits.

Method. The authors introduce WQSP, an extension of QSP that incorporates a weight function into the central rotation operator, allowing for structured control over the circuit's expressivity.

Model / system. The work is based on quantum circuits used for polynomial approximation, specifically applied to parameterizing learnable activation functions within Kolmogorov–Arnold Networks (KANs).

Key observables. Approximation error (RMSE), number of trainable parameters, and circuit depth.

Important parameters / regimes. The choice of integer weights ($w_j$) is crucial, as it dictates the parameter reduction, achieving exponential reductions in parameters for polynomial realization.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis often assumes the target function can be decomposed into even and odd parts for parity constraints, and the initial focus is on real-valued functions.

Figures summary. Figures compare the Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) versus the number of parameters for WQSP-KANs versus QSP-KANs, demonstrating WQSP's superior parameter efficiency while maintaining accuracy.

Paper structure. The paper builds from the limitations of QSP, introduces WQSP as an improvement, analyzes its performance in the integer weight regime, and culminates in applying the framework to parameterize KANs for multivariate function approximation.

Abstract

Quantum Signal Processing is a powerful quantum framework for generating and approximating univariate polynomials. However, QSP is often limited by circuit-depth bottlenecks and parity constraints on the class of realizable polynomials. In this work, we introduce Weighted Quantum Signal Processing, an extension of QSP in which a weight function is assigned to the central rotation operator. This formulation provides a deeper understanding of QSP, which emerges as the special case of WQSP with unit weights. The choice of weights determines the structure and expressive capabilities of WQSP circuits. When the weights are natural numbers greater than one, WQSP reduces to a pruned version of QSP, revealing parameter redundancies in the standard framework. Through appropriate selection of integer weights, WQSP achieves linear-to-exponential reductions in the number of parameters required to realize arbitrary bounded univariate polynomials while preserving approximation quality. For generic weights, we establish corresponding approximation error bounds and show that, in many cases, the approximation is exact. We analyze WQSP from both a deterministic perspective, where polynomial generation is formulated as the solution of a linear system, and a quantum machine learning perspective, where WQSP serves as a structured and expressive quantum learning model. We further employ this learning framework to parameterize learnable activation functions in Kolmogorov--Arnold Networks for multivariate function approximation. Our results show that WQSP provides a compact, flexible, and theoretically grounded framework for realizing arbitrary univariate polynomials while requiring significantly fewer trainable parameters than conventional QSP. This yields expressive and parameter-efficient neural architectures, highlighting the potential of WQSP as a scalable primitive for quantum-enhanced machine learning.

Singular high-harmonic transport above a quantum threshold

Authors: Yugo Onishi, Su-Yang Xu, Liang Fu
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.20990v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: 🔥 non-equilibrium universality 4/5 · Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 2/5 · methods for driven-dissipative 2/5 · driven-dissipative phase transition 1/5

Summary. This theoretical work analyzes the strong-field transport in band insulators exhibiting a quantum power threshold. It shows that the resulting singular current-voltage relation generates high-harmonic radiation whose amplitude decays sub-exponentially. The authors derive universal scaling laws governing these harmonics, offering a pathway for THz generation in solids.

Why it may be interesting. The study of non-equilibrium, strong-field quantum transport, particularly the emergence of universal scaling laws for high harmonics, connects fundamental quantum tunneling physics to observable THz generation phenomena.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the nonperturbative, strong-field transport in band insulators, specifically characterizing the singular high-harmonic generation resulting from a quantum power threshold.

Main result. The current exhibits a singular DC-voltage relation due to Landau-Zener tunneling, leading to high-harmonic generation whose amplitude decays sub-exponentially with harmonic order, following a derived scaling law.

Method. The analysis uses the quantum master equation in the steady state, followed by Fourier analysis and the saddle-point approximation to derive asymptotic scaling laws for the harmonic components.

Model / system. The model is a small-gap insulator, initially treated as a 1D system governed by a massive Dirac Hamiltonian. Transport is driven by interband tunneling, characterized by a quantum power threshold $w_Q$.

Key observables. DC current-voltage relation $J(E)$, and the high-harmonic current amplitudes $J_n$ at frequency $n\omega$.

Important parameters / regimes. The quantum power threshold $w_Q = \Delta^2/\hbar$, and the characteristic field scale $E_c = \pi\Delta^2 / \hbar v e$.

Assumptions / limitations. Approximations include the relaxation time approximation (RTA) and the use of the saddle-point method for evaluating Fourier integrals, assuming the main contribution comes from the singularity near $E=0$.

Figures summary. Figures compare the calculated $J-V$ relation to numerical solutions, contrast the sub-exponential decay of harmonics from singular transport against super-exponential decay from analytic models, and illustrate the collapse of harmonic amplitudes onto a universal scaling function.

Paper structure. The paper builds from establishing the singular DC current-voltage relation via Landau-Zener tunneling in a 1D model, then extends this to AC transport by analyzing the Fourier components, deriving universal scaling laws, and comparing these results to other transport regimes.

Abstract

We show that Landau-Zener tunneling in a small-gap insulator produces a singular DC current--voltage relation, and correspondingly, strong high-harmonic generation in AC transport. Remarkably, high-harmonic responses decrease subexponentially with harmonic order, which is parametrically slower than in ordinary conductors and PN diodes. We further derive a scaling law for the current amplitude at frequency $nω$ produced by the applied electric field $E$ at fundamental frequency $ω$. Our work offers a promising route to THz generation based on frequency multiplication in solids.

Summary. This perspective reviews the evolution of optical frequency combs, detailing the shift from bulk lasers to integrated microresonators. It emphasizes the breakthrough concept of Topological OFCs, which utilize arrays of coupled resonators to generate nested combs protected by topological edge states. This advances the field toward more robust and scalable photonic light sources.

Why it may be interesting. This work sits at the intersection of quantum optics and condensed matter physics, specifically bridging nonlinear wave dynamics (solitons) with topological protection, which is highly relevant for developing robust quantum light sources.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the development of advanced, miniaturized light sources by exploring the generation of Topological Optical Frequency Combs (T-OFCs). It seeks to advance OFC technology beyond conventional microresonators by incorporating topological photonics principles.

Main result. The key result is the theoretical and experimental demonstration of T-OFCs generated in arrays of coupled microresonators, which exhibit nested spectral structures due to topological edge states.

Method. The work combines nonlinear wave physics (soliton dynamics, Kerr nonlinearity) with topological concepts (topological invariants, edge states) in integrated photonic platforms.

Model / system. The physical systems include arrays of coupled microresonators (e.g., SiN, LiNbO3) engineered to mimic topological structures, allowing light propagation in robust edge channels.

Key observables. Nested spectral comb structure, unidirectional photonic modes, and the existence of topologically protected edge states.

Important parameters / regimes. High-Q factor, Kerr nonlinearity ($\chi^{(3)}$), array coupling strength, and topological invariants.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that coupling resonators into arrays can induce topological phases, and that nonlinear effects (solitons) can manifest within these topologically protected edge states.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the technological roadmap: progressing from bulky mode-locked lasers to single-resonator microcombs, culminating in the array-based topological OFCs.

Paper structure. The paper provides a historical perspective on OFCs, detailing the evolution from early lasers to microcombs, and then focusing heavily on the theoretical framework and recent experimental breakthroughs involving topological photonics to realize T-OFCs.

Abstract

Optical frequency combs (OFCs) are revolutionary light sources characterized by discrete and equally spaced spectral lines, and they have found widespread applications in metrology, spectroscopy, and communications. In the early stages, OFCs were realized using mode-locked lasers. With advancements in the fabrication of high-quality factor ($Q$) microresonators and the increasing demand for miniaturized and integrable photonic chips, microresonator-based OFCs, commonly referred to as microcombs, have been developed. Although early studies of microcombs primarily focused on single microresonators or a few resonators, a significant breakthrough occurred in 2021 when it was theoretically predicted that light propagating in the topological edge channel of an array of ring resonators could generate nested frequency combs known as topological OFCs. Since then, the field of topological OFCs has progressed rapidly, with experimental observations made in 2024. This Perspective will introduce the history of OFCs, placing particular emphasis on the emergence and development of topological OFCs, as well as exploring the research challenges and opportunities associated with them.

Summary. This work develops a microscopic theory for how stripe-forming magnetic systems achieve global orientational order after a sudden quench. It demonstrates that this ordering process is governed by the dynamics of topological defects, particularly T-junctions, which move and eliminate over time. The key finding is the quantitative link between tracking these defects in real space and observing corresponding changes in the system's structure factor in reciprocal space.

Why it may be interesting. The connection drawn between the dynamics of topological defects (T-junctions) in real space and the measurable signatures in the structure factor (reciprocal space) provides a powerful, generalizable diagnostic tool for studying non-equilibrium ordering in complex many-body systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the non-equilibrium process by which a two-dimensional stripe-forming magnetic system develops global orientational order following a quench.

Main result. Global order is shown to be mediated by the motion and elimination of topological defects, specifically T-junctions, which provides a microscopic, defect-based description of the ordering process.

Method. The study uses Monte Carlo simulations to track the real-space evolution of spin configurations and compares these defect dynamics to signatures observed in the structure factor in reciprocal space.

Model / system. The system is a 2D stripe-forming magnetic model described by an Ising Hamiltonian incorporating nearest-neighbor exchange and long-range dipolar interactions. The evolution is studied by quenching the system into the stripe-ordered regime.

Key observables. T-junctions, corners, I-junctions (defects), the T-junction population dynamics, and the structure factor in reciprocal space.

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature (T), relative strength of interactions (delta), and system size. The dynamics are characterized by distinct regimes (formation, growth, equilibration).

Assumptions / limitations. The dipolar interaction is approximated using a finite cut-off radius (Rc), and the analysis relies on comparing real-space defect elimination rates with reciprocal-space structure factor changes.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate stripe pattern evolution after a quench, showing defect formation and subsequent elimination leading to global order. Another figure compares defect elimination rates measured in real space versus reciprocal space.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the physical model, details the simulation methodology (quenching, defect tracking), analyzes the time evolution through distinct dynamical regimes based on defect counts, and finally confirms the microscopic mechanism by showing the correspondence between real-space defect decay and reciprocal-space structure factor sharpening.

Abstract

We investigate the nonequilibrium development of orientational order in a two-dimensional stripe-forming magnetic system with competing interactions. Following a quench into the stripe-ordered regime, horizontal and vertical stripes organize into orientational regions(super-domains) separated by characteristic defects. We show that the evolution towards a globally oriented stripe state is mediated by the motion and elimination of these defects, with T-junctions providing a direct microscopic measure of super-domain reorganization. The T-junction population reveals distinct dynamical regimes associated with super-domain formation, growth, and equilibration. Temperature, system size, and equilibrium stripe width modify the characteristic time scales of this evolution, while thermal fluctuations produce a finite defect population at higher temperatures. We further show that the defect dynamics observed in real space are reflected in the structure factor, providing a reciprocal-space signature of defect elimination. These results establish a microscopic defect-based description of orientational ordering in stripe-forming magnetic systems.

Summary. This work uses advanced STM spectroscopy to study electron transport in quantum-confined Josephson junctions. The researchers find that the transport is significantly mediated by a collective excitation mode intrinsic to the junction structure. Abstract

Quantum confinement can strongly modify transport through Josephson junctions. Here, we study local tunneling transport through nanoscale Josephson junction stacks in the Coulomb blockade regime, where the metallic layers exhibit strong vertical quantum confinement. We find that quasiparticle transport is strongly enhanced by a collective excitation mode intrinsic to the junction and localized in the isolated metallic overlayer. We quantify both the collective-mode energy and the Coulomb gap and show that both exhibit strong layer-dependent modulation, consistent with the modulation of the underlying quantum well states. We further investigate how the collective excitation responds to various perturbations, including mechanical motion and an applied magnetic field. Our results suggest that this collective mode is sensitive to quasiparticles near the Fermi level and may therefore provide an indirect probe of the superconducting state.

Summary. This work introduces Bend Contour Electron Tomography (BCET), a novel computational framework to quantitatively map strain and topography in freestanding quantum thin films using wide-field TEM images. A characteristic corrugated surface morphology of Nb films on Cu is identified, with mesoscopic features comparable to the London penetration depth and coherence length, and its impact on superconducting radio frequency cavity performance metrics is examined. Magnetic field enhancement factors and superheating field suppression factors are calculated within the London model for a representative corrugated geometry. These results demonstrate that roughness trends derived from buffered chemical polished and electropolished Nb cavities do not capture the impact of nanoscale surface morphology on SRF Nb thin film performance, as even surfaces that possess low average roughness can contain geometric features that generate substantial local field enhancement and significantly suppress the Bean Livingston barrier. The influence of surface roughness on impurity diffusion is also investigated, which highlights the roles of increased surface area and local geometric confinement in modifying near surface impurity distributions. Tracking the effect of impurity diffusion on the evolution of magnetic field enhancement, we show that geometrically confined impurity distributions can mitigate nanoscale magnetic field enhancement substantially.

Abstract

Orbital transport in light transition metals has emerged as a promising route toward angular-momentum electronics, with Hanle magnetoresistance (HMR) providing a direct electrical probe of the orbital Hall effect (OHE) in non-magnetic conductors. Here we report magnetoresistance signatures consistent with orbital HMR in epitaxial SrVO$*3$ (SVO), a narrow-band $d^1$ oxide grown on (001)-oriented (LaAlO$*3$)$*{0.3}$(Sr$*2$TaAlO$*6$)$*{0.7}$ substrates. In films of different thickness and longitudinal resistivity, field-dependent measurements reveal a reproducible positive, even-in-field, and approximately quadratic $Δρ*{x-y}$ response, as expected for HMR. Angular measurements characterize the corresponding magnetoresistance anisotropy and reveal an additional non-Hanle contribution. Density functional theory (DFT) calculations yield an intrinsic orbital Hall conductivity $σ*{\mathrm{OH}}^{\mathrm{DFT}} = 390,(\hbar/e),Ω^{-1}\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ and a spin Hall conductivity $σ_{\mathrm{SH}}^{\mathrm{DFT}} = -12,(\hbar/e),Ω^{-1}\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$ at the Fermi level, corresponding to $|σ_{\mathrm{OH}}^{\mathrm{DFT}}/σ_{\mathrm{SH}}^{\mathrm{DFT}}| \approx 33$, thus indicating a predominantly orbital response. Using the diffusive HMR framework with $λ_{\mathrm{OD}} = 2$ nm as a reference value, the 20.8 nm film gives conservative saturation-limit lower-bound (LB) estimates $θ_{\mathrm{OH,LB}} = 0.0144 \pm 0.0004$ and $σ_{\mathrm{OH,LB}} = (460 \pm 11),(\hbar/e),Ω^{-1}\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$, comparable to the DFT value. Across the series, lower-bound estimates obtained with the same $λ_{\mathrm{OD}}$ increase overall with longitudinal conductivity. These results suggest that narrow-band $d^1$ metallic oxides are a promising platform for orbital transport.
