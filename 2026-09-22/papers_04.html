Bootstrapping Disordered Quantum Systems

Authors: Yaprak Önder, Michael G. Scheer, Minjae Cho, Eslam Khalaf
Type: theory · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.20916v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces a novel bootstrap framework to rigorously bound ground-state properties of quantum systems with quenched disorder, exemplified by the 1D RTFIM. By extending the operator algebra to include disorder variables, it derives bounds that do not require sampling disorder realizations. The resulting 'bootstrap gap' serves as a powerful diagnostic tool for mapping out the system's phase diagram, revealing critical behavior and Griffiths regimes.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous, non-perturbative nature of the bounds and the diagnostic power of the bootstrap gap provide powerful theoretical tools to map out complex phase diagrams in disordered quantum matter, which is highly relevant to understanding localization and critical phenomena.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops a rigorous bootstrap framework to obtain two-sided bounds on disorder-averaged ground-state observables for quantum systems subjected to quenched disorder.

Main result. The method successfully applies to the 1D RTFIM, yielding a 'bootstrap gap' diagnostic that characterizes the phase diagram, showing distinct behavior near Griffiths regions and critical points.

Method. It extends the operator algebra to include classical disorder variables and uses positivity constraints (including two-replica conditions) within an algebraic quantum mechanics framework to derive rigorous bounds without needing to sample disorder realizations.

Model / system. The primary model studied is the one-dimensional Random Transverse Field Ising Model (RTFIM), characterized by a Hamiltonian involving random couplings and fields ($\xi_j$). The method is claimed to be generalizable beyond this specific model.

Key observables. Disorder-averaged ground-state energy density, short-range spin-spin correlators, the variance of the energy density, and the 'bootstrap gap' ($\Delta E_0$).

Important parameters / regimes. Disorder strength ($W$), magnetic field ($h$), and the disorder distribution type ($\mathbb{Z}_2$, Uniform, Gaussian).

Assumptions / limitations. The method relies on enforcing positivity constraints on finite subsets of disorder operators and operators, and its application to the thermodynamic limit is a key feature.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the bootstrap gap ($\Delta E_0$) for different disorder types, mapping out phase boundaries, and analyze the convergence of the gap with increasing Krylov depth ($k$) using exponential and power-law fits.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the bootstrap framework, details its application to the RTFIM Hamiltonian using Majorana operators, derives bounds for various observables (including variance via two-replica methods), and analyzes the resulting bootstrap gap's behavior across different quantum phases.

Abstract

We present a bootstrap framework that yields rigorous two-sided bounds on ground-state observables of quantum systems with quenched disorder. The key idea is to extend the operator algebra to include classical disorder variables and to encode the disorder distribution through a set of moments. The method directly bounds disorder-averaged expectation values for finite or infinite lattice systems and for arbitrary disorder distributions. In contrast to conventional approaches, we do not need to sample disorder realizations, which enables us to make thorough use of symmetries that hold only on average rather than for each disorder realization. Additionally, we show that multi-replica operators can be used to access disorder fluctuations such as the variance of an observable. We apply the method to the one-dimensional random transverse-field Ising model (RTFIM) with discrete, uniform, and Gaussian disorder, obtaining tight bounds on the disorder-averaged ground-state energy density and short-range spin-spin correlators, as well as bounds on the disorder variance of the energy density. Additionally, we show that the difference between the upper and lower bounds on the disorder-averaged ground-state energy density, which we term the bootstrap gap, can be used to map out the phase diagram of the RTFIM. The bootstrap gap is small and flat within the paramagnetic and ferromagnetic phases at weak disorder, grows upon entering the Griffiths regions, and peaks along the critical line.

Commutator Geometry and Information Preservation in the Quantum Switch

Authors: Xu Chen, Xue Ma
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21310v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical work investigates the difference in information preservation when quantum channels are applied via a 'quantum switch' versus a fixed sequence. By using commutator geometry, the authors derive quantitative bounds showing how the switch affects fidelity and Quantum Fisher Information. The findings connect fundamental algebraic structures to the robustness of quantum information encoding against noise.

Why it may be interesting. It provides rigorous mathematical tools (commutator geometry) to quantify the advantage or disadvantage of indefinite causal order in noisy quantum processes, which is highly relevant for quantum communication protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper compares how information preservation (fidelity, QFI) differs when two commuting quantum channels are applied in a quantum switch versus a fixed sequential order.

Main result. For product inputs, the switch preserves fidelity at least as well as the fixed order; however, the gap is quantified by commutator identities, showing complex dependencies on the input state and channel structure.

Method. The analysis uses operator sum descriptions, commutator identities, and root fidelity to derive exact mathematical relationships between the output states of the two operational orders.

Model / system. The model involves two commuting quantum channels acting on a joint system, analyzed using concepts from quantum error correction (e.g., Pauli codes) and quantum metrology.

Key observables. Fidelity gap ($F_{FO} - F_{SW}$), Quantum Fisher Information (QFI), distinguishability, and commutator components ($Q_{ij}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Input state ($ ho$), channel parameters (e.g., error probabilities for Pauli X/Y channels), and the structure of the joint code.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is restricted to two commuting channels and often assumes specific product or pure encoded state families for deriving exact formulas.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the fidelity gap ($\Delta P$) for Pauli X and Y channels as a function of input orientation and control angle, showing distinct gain/loss regimes.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by defining the comparison between switch and fixed order, deriving commutator identities, establishing monotonicity for product states, and finally connecting the fidelity gap to QFI gain for pure encoded families.

Abstract

Two commuting channels yield the same composite channel in either fixed order, yet their quantum switch can alter information preservation. We study how this effect depends on the input state, retaining the joint output of the control and target. Exact Kraus commutator identities determine overlap and squared fidelity gaps. Fixed order preserves product inputs at least as well as the switch in fidelity. The reverse inequality holds for pure states of two qubits whose control Schmidt basis can be chosen to coincide with the order basis. A commutator matrix determines the target basis that maximizes the squared fidelity gap at fixed entanglement within this aligned family. For Pauli $X$ and $Y$ channels with equal error probabilities, the switch replaces a logical phase flip with an operator that stabilizes a joint code. At fixed noise, the switch preserves at least as much distinguishability and quantum Fisher information as fixed order for every state family in this code. For the pure encoded family studied here, exact formulas connect the phase information gain to the squared fidelity gap and quantify the reduction in information about the noise probability. Optimizing the probes yields equal maximal quantum Fisher information for noise estimation under both joint channels. These results connect commutator geometry and logical error structure to state preservation and the retention of encoded information.

From Trainability Diagnostics to Optimization Claims: Boundaries and Controls in Variational Quantum Optimization

Authors: Pilsung Kang
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21243v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work investigates the gap between knowing if a quantum circuit is 'trainable' and actually achieving good optimization results. By developing step-level diagnostics based on Hamiltonian term decomposition, the authors show that while these diagnostics characterize the gradient structure, they are insufficient on their own to prove optimization success. True performance requires careful matching of update norms and search budgets.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a deep theoretical understanding of the limitations of current diagnostic tools in VQA, suggesting that structural metrics must be paired with rigorous control matching to claim optimization success.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the 'trainability–optimization gap' in Variational Quantum Algorithms (VQAs), where gradient signal availability does not guarantee successful energy minimization.

Main result. Gradient-structure diagnostics can characterize trainability and update geometry, but they cannot serve as standalone evidence of optimization benefit; true benefit requires controls matched on update norm and search budget.

Method. The authors introduce step-level diagnostics by treating coefficient-weighted Hamiltonian-term gradients as task-like components, deriving a bridge between termwise organization, directional activity, and first-order descent.

Model / system. The study uses Variational Quantum Algorithms (VQAs) applied to transverse-field Ising model instances, employing both hardware-efficient and Hamiltonian variational ansatzes.

Key observables. Step-level diagnostics ($U_{ ext{step}}$, $Q_{ ext{step}}$), first-order descent, final energy, and various correlation coefficients ($ ext{corr}(\Delta E_{ ext{pred}}, \Delta E_{ ext{act}})$).

Important parameters / regimes. Update norm, search budget, and the structure of the Hamiltonian decomposition into Pauli terms.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on establishing an exact bridge between gradient components and assumes that optimization benefit requires matched controls on update norm and search budget.

Figures summary. Figure 1 provides an overview summarizing the step-level bridge, its resolution into standard first-order geometry, and the main conclusions regarding association tests and matched controls.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by defining the step-level diagnostic framework, deriving the mathematical bridge, testing various optimization rules (vanilla, PCGrad, LSO-PCGrad) on Ising models, and concluding with rigorous analysis showing the limitations of structure-based diagnostics.

Abstract

Barren plateau diagnostics characterize whether gradient signal remains available for training, but surviving signal need not translate into successful optimization. We study this trainability--optimization gap at the level of optimizer steps. Treating coefficient-weighted Hamiltonian-term gradients as task-like components, we introduce step-level diagnostics and derive an exact bridge between signed termwise organization, directional activity, and first-order descent. Resolving this bridge into standard first-order geometry shows that the apparent organization--activity factors are not independent optimization axes and that, at fixed state and update norm, the raw gradient maximizes first-order descent of the summed objective. We compare vanilla gradient descent, a deterministic Hamiltonian-term PCGrad variant, and probe-gated LSO-PCGrad on transverse-field Ising model instances with hardware-efficient and Hamiltonian variational ansatzes, together with matched controls for update norm and probe budget. Blind projection can improve an organization diagnostic while worsening final energy and first-order predictability. After conditioning on standard first-order geometry, residual term-space composition shows no reproducible material incremental association with realized descent, while optimizer-relative update norm shows positive material associations in some settings without cross-regime reproducibility. Matched controls provide no resolved final-energy benefit attributable to the projected direction, and the improvement of LSO-PCGrad is more consistent with probe-based search and step-norm adaptation than with Hamiltonian-term projection itself. These results show that gradient-structure diagnostics can characterize trainability and update geometry without serving as standalone evidence of optimization benefit, which requires controls matched on update norm and search budget.

Asymmetric quantum cloning on orthogonal orbits and four-mode fermionic states

Authors: Piotr Ćwikliński, Michał Studziński
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.22047v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper rigorously determines the achievable fidelity region for asymmetric quantum cloning when the input states are constrained to specific orbits of the orthogonal group. By employing advanced algebraic techniques involving the Brauer algebra, the authors derive explicit optimal cloning channels. The results are then applied to the physical system of four-mode fermionic states, establishing fundamental limits on quantum state duplication for these systems.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides deep connections between quantum information theory (cloning bounds) and representation theory (Brauer algebra, orthogonal groups), which is highly relevant for understanding fundamental limits in quantum communication protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the achievable fidelity region for asymmetric quantum cloning processes ($1 o 2$) applied to pure states residing on orbits generated by the orthogonal group $O(d)$. It specifically applies these general results to four-mode fermionic states.

Main result. The full region of achievable average single-copy fidelities is determined by solving a spectral problem derived from the Brauer algebra. The optimal cloner for the joint two-copy fidelity is shown to be different from the one optimal for single-copy fidelities.

Method. The authors reduce the optimization over all CPTP maps to a finite-dimensional spectral problem using orthogonal covariance and the Brauer algebra. They characterize the achievable region using a support function derived from the Uhlmann–Jozsa fidelity.

Model / system. The primary model is the quantum cloning process ($\Phi: ho_\psi o ho_{AB}(\psi)$) acting on states $\psi$ belonging to orbits $O(d)_q$ generated by the orthogonal group $O(d)$. The specific application involves four-mode fermionic states in the even-parity sector.

Key observables. Average single-copy fidelities ($F^A, F^B$), the two-copy moment ($\mathcal{M}^{(d)}_2(q)$), and the support function $h_{O(d)}^q(a, b)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The dimension $d \ge 3$, the orbit parameter $q(\psi) = |\psi^T \psi|^2 \in [0, 1]$, and the fidelity parameters $a$ and $b$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the input state $\psi$ is drawn from the invariant measure $\mu_q$ on the orbit $O(d)_q$, and the optimization is performed over CPTP maps.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the operational setup for asymmetric one-to-two cloning. Figure 2 illustrates pairing diagrams in the Brauer algebra $B_3(d)$ related to the cloning process.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the general framework for asymmetric cloning on $O(d)$ orbits, determining the fidelity region via spectral methods. It then applies these general results to the specific case of four-mode fermionic states, utilizing triality to connect the two analyses.

Abstract

In this work, we study asymmetric $(1\to2)$ quantum cloning for pure states belonging to orbits generated by the orthogonal group. For every dimension $(d\geq3)$, we determine the full region of achievable pairs of average single-copy fidelities. Using orthogonal covariance and the Brauer algebra, we reduce the optimization over all CPTP maps to a finite-dimensional spectral problem and construct explicit optimal cloning channels. We then apply the general results to four-mode fermionic states in the even-parity sector. Using triality, we identify the fixed-concurrence fermionic families with the orthogonal orbits considered in the first part of the paper. In particular, for pure Gaussian states we show that the symmetric locally optimal cloner is unique. We also compare local and global cloning and find that the channel optimal for the joint two-copy fidelity is different from the one optimal for the single-copy fidelities. Finally, we show that the locally optimal cloner cannot be implemented using only operations which preserve fermionic Gaussian states.

Asymptotics for Frequency Redundancies in Quantum Machine Learning Models

Authors: Felix Paul, Bhilahari Jeevanesan, Peter Jung
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21916v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work mathematically analyzes the frequency redundancy spectrum in Quantum Fourier Models (QFMs) by studying how the eigenvalues of the data re-uploading Hamiltonians shape this spectrum. The authors prove that for many structured eigenvalue choices, the redundancy profile converges to a Gaussian distribution as the model depth increases. This highlights that the choice of encoding strategy is crucial for controlling the model's spectral bias.

Why it may be interesting. The universal Gaussian convergence suggests that the structure of the encoding Hamiltonian dictates the spectral properties of the model, providing theoretical constraints on designing QFMs for specific tasks.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how the frequency redundancy spectrum in Quantum Fourier Models (QFMs) is determined by the choice of eigenvalues in the data re-uploading Hamiltonians.

Main result. The frequency redundancy profile ($r_\omega$) generically converges to a Gaussian profile as the number of layers increases, a feature linked to the central limit theorem for random walks.

Method. The analysis employs generating functions and Cauchy's integral formula to calculate the redundancy, followed by asymptotic evaluation techniques like Laplace's method for large numbers of layers.

Model / system. The model is a Quantum Fourier Model (QFM) built using Parametrized Quantum Circuits (PQCs) with a layered, data re-uploading structure, characterized by diagonal encoding Hamiltonians $H^{(\ell)}$ with eigenvalues $\Lambda^{(\ell)}$.

Key observables. Frequency redundancy ($r_\omega$), which measures the number of ways a specific frequency $\omega$ can be constructed from the eigenvalues.

Important parameters / regimes. Number of layers ($L$), dimension of the Hilbert space ($d$), and the specific structure of the eigenvalues (e.g., arithmetic progressions, fixed values).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the encoding Hamiltonians are diagonal and that the feature vector $x$ is one-dimensional for simplicity.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the QFM circuit structure, showing the transition of the redundancy profile from a simple function (like a triangle) to a Gaussian shape as the number of layers increases.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the QFM structure and the redundancy definition using generating functions. It then derives and analyzes the asymptotic behavior of $r_\omega$ for structured eigenvalue choices (arithmetic, fixed), demonstrating the universal Gaussian limit.

Abstract

The redundancy distribution of the frequency spectrum has been shown in the literature to impact the expressivity and trainability of Quantum Fourier Models (QFMs). In this work, we address the question of how this redundancy spectrum is shaped by the choice of eigenvalues of the data re-uploading Hamiltonians, using a simple mathematical formalism based on generating functions. We derive exact and asymptotic redundancy profiles for several structured eigenvalue choices identical for every layer, including arithmetic progressions and single-qubit Pauli encodings, and show that both approach a Gaussian profile as the number of layers grows. We then show that this Gaussian limit is not specific to these constructions but is a generic feature of QFMs built from integer eigenvalues that are identical in every layer. These results can be tied to the central limit theorem for random walks. These results clarify why generic or unstructured encoding choices give rise to a redundancy bias that favours low frequencies, highlighting the importance of well-thought-out encoding strategies when constructing a QFM.

Bootstrapping Classical Systems with Quenched Disorder

Authors: Minjae Cho, Yaprak Önder, Eslam Khalaf, Michael G. Scheer
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.20927v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work introduces a powerful bootstrap framework using linear and semidefinite programming to derive rigorous bounds on observables in disordered classical statistical systems. By applying this to models like the random Ising model, the authors successfully analyze complex phenomena such as the Griffiths region. It provides a mathematically rigorous approach to tackling the notoriously difficult problem of quenched disorder.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical techniques developed (LP/SDP bounds) provide powerful, non-perturbative tools to analyze complex, disordered many-body systems, which is a major challenge in modern statistical physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops a bootstrap method to derive rigorous mathematical bounds on disorder-averaged observables in classical statistical systems subjected to quenched disorder.

Main result. The method successfully applies to models like the random site-diluted Ising model and the disordered contact process, yielding bounds that can be analyzed for critical behavior, such as identifying the Griffiths region.

Method. The core method involves reformulating the constraints imposed by the disorder average into a sequence of linear programming (LP) and semidefinite programming (SDP) problems.

Model / system. The study focuses on classical statistical systems with quenched disorder, exemplified by the two-dimensional random site-diluted Ising model and the one-dimensional disordered contact process. The physics is governed by disorder-averaged Hamiltonians and probability measures.

Key observables. Disorder-averaged magnetization, nearest-neighbor correlators, two-replica correlators, and probabilities $P_1, P_2, P_3$ exhibiting kinks.

Important parameters / regimes. The disorder concentration parameter ($p$), temperature/inverse temperature ($eta$), and lattice size ($L$) are crucial parameters defining the phase diagram and scaling behavior.

Assumptions / limitations. The method relies on the positivity of probability measures, the existence of disorder averages, and the ability to formulate constraints as linear/semidefinite inequalities.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the transition of probabilities across 'kinks' in the phase diagram and compare bootstrap bounds (with and without specific inequalities) against expected theoretical values.

Paper structure. The paper builds the framework by defining the mathematical constraints (positivity, equations of motion) and then applying this framework to specific models (RSIM, DCP) to derive bounds and analyze critical phenomena like the Griffiths region.

Abstract

We introduce a bootstrap method for deriving bounds on disorder-averaged observables in classical statistical systems with quenched disorder. The three main ingredients of the bootstrap are the positivity of ensembles of probability measures, disorder-averaged equations of motion, and explicit expectation values of random coupling variables. Together, these ingredients lead to a linear programming problem over the space of disorder averages. By further employing the positivity of the two-replica Gram matrix, we also formulate a semidefinite programming problem that produces bounds on two-replica correlators. We demonstrate the method using two examples: the two-dimensional random site-diluted Ising model and the one-dimensional disordered contact process. For the latter example, the bootstrap bounds exhibit a region of multiple kinks that we relate to the Griffiths region, whose finite-size scaling analysis yields a typical correlation length exponent in good numerical agreement with the known value.

Dual-Sublattice Ferromagnetism Driven by Cooperative Double Exchange and Superexchange at NdNiO$_3$/CaMnO$_3$ Interfaces

Type: experiment · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21002v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper investigates how to induce ferromagnetism at the interface of $ ext{NdNiO}_3/ ext{CaMnO}_3$ superlattices. Using advanced X-ray spectroscopies, the authors prove that interfacial valence reconstruction creates the necessary conditions—specifically, $ ext{Mn}^{3+}$ and $ ext{Ni}^{2+}$—to activate cooperative double exchange and superexchange. This coupling mechanism successfully engineers a robust, dual-sublattice ferromagnetism.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a clear, experimentally validated blueprint for designing magnetic properties in artificial quantum materials by engineering the local electronic structure at interfaces, which is highly relevant to correlated electron physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to understand how to engineer emergent ferromagnetism at the interface between correlated oxides by controlling the electronic structure.

Main result. The research demonstrates that interfacial valence reconstruction, specifically enhanced $ ext{Mn}^{3+}$ and $ ext{Ni}^{2+}$ character, activates cooperative double exchange and superexchange pathways, leading to dual-sublattice ferromagnetism.

Method. The study employs depth-resolved Standing-Wave X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (SW-XPS) to map valence states and X-ray Magnetic Circular Dichroism (XMCD) to measure magnetic responses.

Model / system. The physical system is an epitaxial superlattice of $ ext{NdNiO}_3/ ext{CaMnO}_3$. The mechanism relies on the interplay between charge transfer, valence state reconstruction, and magnetic exchange interactions (double exchange and superexchange) at the heterointerface.

Key observables. Interfacial $ ext{Mn}^{3+}$ and $ ext{Ni}^{2+}$ enhancement (via SW-XPS); Ferromagnetic response from both Mn and Ni sublattices (via XMCD).

Important parameters / regimes. Low temperature ($20 ext{ K}$); Specific incidence angles for depth profiling; The ratio of $ ext{Mn}^{3+}/ ext{Mn}^{4+}$ and $ ext{Ni}^{2+}/ ext{Total Ni}$.

Assumptions / limitations. The ability to distinguish interfacial vs. layer-interior electronic states using the standing-wave electric field profile is crucial for the analysis.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the SW-XPS geometry and depth-selective measurements, showing valence profiles (Mn and Ni) and the resulting XMCD signals confirming magnetic coupling.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the structural and chemical composition using RHEED/HAXPES, then uses SW-XPS to map the necessary interfacial valence reconstruction ($ ext{Mn}^{3+}$ and $ ext{Ni}^{2+}$), and finally uses XMCD to confirm the resulting dual-sublattice ferromagnetism driven by the identified exchange mechanisms.

Abstract

Engineering emergent ferromagnetism at correlated-oxide interfaces offers a powerful route to creating collective states that do not exist in the parent materials. Here, we show that interfacial valence reconstruction in NdNiO$_3$/CaMnO$_3$ superlattices generates dual-sublattice ferromagnetism involving both Mn and Ni. Depth-resolved standing-wave X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy reveals enhanced Mn$^{3+}$ character on the CaMnO$_3$ side of the interface and enhanced Ni$^{2+}$ character on the NdNiO$_3$ side, establishing the configurations required for Mn$^{4+}$-O-Mn$^{3+}$ double exchange and Ni$^{2+}$-O-Mn$^{4+}$ superexchange, respectively. At low temperature, element-specific XMCD reveals ferromagnetic responses from both sublattices, with the Ni response concentrated predominantly in the Ni$^{2+}$-derived spectral component that SW-XPS independently shows to be enhanced at the interface. Together, these results show that cooperative double exchange within CaMnO$_3$ and superexchange across the interface couple the Mn and Ni sublattices, providing a general strategy for engineering interfacial ferromagnetism in correlated oxides.

Entanglement entropy in holographic CFTs with generic boundaries

Authors: Fabio Ori, Peng-Xiang Hao
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21370v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work extends the holographic description of entanglement entropy to CFTs with boundaries of generic spacetime signature. It demonstrates that achieving consistency requires complexifying the dual bulk spacetime, forcing relevant geometric objects like extremal surfaces to possess complex coordinates. This complexification successfully reproduces known results from boundary quantum field theory calculations.

Why it may be interesting. The necessity of complexifying the bulk geometry to match boundary quantum field theory results provides a powerful, non-trivial constraint on the holographic duality, suggesting that the mathematical structure of the dual spacetime must be generalized beyond simple Lorentzian signatures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of calculating entanglement entropy in holographic Conformal Field Theories (CFTs) when the boundary conditions are generic, particularly those leading to spacelike boundaries.

Main result. To maintain consistency with CFT predictions for generic boundaries, the dual bulk spacetime geometry, including the Ryu-Takayanagi surfaces and branes, must be extended into complex coordinates.

Method. The authors employ the holographic duality (AdS/CFT) framework, calculating entanglement entropy via the area of extremal surfaces (RT formula) and comparing this result to predictions derived from the boundary CFT (e.g., twist operator correlators).

Model / system. The model involves holographic CFTs dual to gravitational theories, specifically analyzing entanglement entropy in static and time-dependent setups with boundaries of generic spacetime signature, including free Gaussian fermions as a numerical check.

Key observables. Entanglement Entropy ($S_A$), which is calculated as $\min( ext{Re } S_{ ext{con}}, ext{Re } S_{ ext{dis}})$, and the complex geodesic length ($D_{ ext{disc}}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The ratio of region size ($L$) to the brane location ($t_0$), and the boundary cutoff $\epsilon$.

Assumptions / limitations. The standard real Lorentzian AdS/BCFT prescription fails for general causal structures; the selection of the dominant saddle point requires minimizing the real part of the area.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the difference between real (timelike) and complex (spacelike) anchoring points for RT surfaces, and plot the regularized entropy showing a phase transition matching Gaussian free fermion results.

Paper structure. The paper develops the formalism by first noting the failure of real bulk descriptions for spacelike boundaries, then deriving the necessary complex extensions for the RT surfaces and branes, and finally confirming the results by matching the complex geodesic length to known boundary CFT calculations.

Abstract

Entanglement entropy in holographic conformal field theories with boundaries of generic spacetime signature cannot be described entirely within the standard holographic duality to a real bulk spacetime. We show, both in static and time-dependent setups, that an exact match with the conformal field theory predictions in two boundary dimensions entails an extension of the dual spacetime to complex coordinates. As a consequence, relevant bulk objects such as Ryu-Takayanagi surfaces and branes are in general complex, yet anchored on a real asymptotic boundary preserving the physical meaning of field-theoretical quantities. We also provide a third numerical check of the holographic prediction with a two-dimensional system of free Gaussian fermions exhibiting the same asymptotic regimes across different entanglement phases.

From sparse quantum-computing data to atomistic simulation with universal machine-learning interatomic potentials

Authors: Tuan Minh Do, Yuichiro Yoshida, Kenji Ishihara, Wataru Mizukami
Type: theory · Category: numerical methods · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21536v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces a novel methodology to enhance machine-learning interatomic potentials (uMLIPs) by fine-tuning them with sparse, high-accuracy energy data obtained from quantum computing. The framework is successfully demonstrated across three chemically diverse systems, showing that quantum data can significantly improve the prediction of complex, finite-temperature properties like reaction barriers and adsorption thermodynamics.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the computational bottleneck of simulating complex quantum phenomena by creating a robust bridge between highly accurate but sparse quantum calculations and computationally feasible, large-scale molecular dynamics simulations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core problem is developing a practical framework to incorporate sparse, high-accuracy electronic structure data from quantum computing into large-scale, finite-temperature atomistic simulations.

Main result. The proposed fine-tuning framework successfully transfers quantum-level accuracy to improve predictions of complex, finite-temperature properties for diverse chemical systems.

Method. The authors fine-tune a pretrained universal Machine-Learning Interatomic Potential (uMLIP) using limited reference energies derived from quantum-computing-based electronic structure calculations.

Model / system. The framework is tested on three systems: the Menshutkin reaction, water adsorption in the HKUST-1 MOF, and CO hopping on an IrPdPtRhRu high-entropy-alloy nanoparticle.

Key observables. Transition-state energies, adsorption thermodynamics ($\Delta H_s$, $\Delta G$), and free-energy profiles (PMF) for chemical reactions and adsorption processes.

Important parameters / regimes. Finite temperature (e.g., 295 K, 300 K), small number of reference configurations (e.g., 14 for HKUST-1), and the use of enhanced-sampling molecular dynamics.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that refining a pretrained uMLIP with sparse, high-fidelity quantum data is sufficient to accurately model complex, thermally sampled configurations.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the workflow: quantum-classical calculations provide sparse reference energies to fine-tune a uMLIP, which is then used for finite-temperature sampling. Other figures compare calculated relative energies against high-level reference data.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the workflow, applies it to three distinct chemical problems (Menshutkin, HKUST-1, HEA), details the fine-tuning process, and presents quantitative results demonstrating improved accuracy in predicting finite-temperature observables.

Abstract

We propose a framework for incorporating quantum-computing-based electronic-structure calculations into universal machine-learning interatomic potentials (uMLIPs). Rather than constructing an interatomic potential from scratch, we refine a pretrained DFT-based uMLIP using a small set of accurate reference energies obtained from quantum computing. We demonstrate the approach for three chemically distinct applications: the Menshutkin reaction, water adsorption in the metal-organic framework HKUST-1, and CO hopping on a high-entropy-alloy nanoparticle. For the Menshutkin reaction, fine-tuning on gas-phase configurations improves the transition-state energy inside a carbon nanotube but not the product energy. For water adsorption in HKUST-1, fine-tuning with only 14 reference configurations brings adsorption thermodynamics obtained from millions of configurations sampled by Widom insertion into closer agreement with reference values. For CO hopping on an IrPdPtRhRu nanoparticle, the preference for on-top over bridge adsorption is recovered in the finite-temperature free-energy profile obtained from enhanced-sampling molecular dynamics, even though the reference data contain only energies. These results demonstrate that the proposed framework provides a practical route for incorporating quantum-computing calculations into realistic atomistic simulations and that quantum-computing reference data can improve pretrained uMLIPs.

Hybrid quantum-classical attention for histopathology-based molecular profiling in data-limited cancers

Abstract

For ``ideal anyons,'' the Berry phase associated with a closed loop of an anyon around another is robust, that is, independent of the size or the shape of the loop, and directly yields the braid statistics. That is not the case for the fractional quantum Hall (FQH) quasiparticles (QPs), which are charged and have finite size. We consider here how the Berry phase depends on the shape of the QP, which, unlike its charge, is not a topological property and varies along the path in response to the local potential. We show that the Berry phase $Θ$ associated with the loop of a fractionally charged QP around another contains three distinct contributions: $Θ=Θ_{\rm AB}+Θ_{\rm shape}+Θ_{\rm braid}$. The Aharonov-Bohm phase $Θ_{\rm AB}$ is dominant, being proportional to the area of the loop, and the shape-dependent term $Θ_{\rm shape}$, identified in this work, can be larger than the order-one contribution from the braid statistics $Θ_{\rm braid}$. A precise determination of the braid statistics is challenging because it can be swamped by practically undetectable uncertainties in its trajectory and shape. We discuss these results in the context of the interference experiments. We also note that the fractional phase jumps in these experiments can be understood without assuming the existence of QPs with sharply quantized fractional charges at the edges of the FQH system, wherein these phase jumps are a direct measure of the fractionally quantized vorticity of the QPs in the bulk of the fractional quantum Hall state.

Abstract

In this paper, we resolve the quantum KKL conjecture of Montanaro and Osborne \cite{MO2010} for quantum Boolean functions on the $n$-qubit hypercube. More precisely, for every self-adjoint unitary $T$, we bound the largest $L_2$-influence from below by a constant multiple of $\mathrm{Var}(T)\log(n)/n$. The proof heavily depends on upper and lower commutator estimates on the Hilbert space.
