Quantum Loads and the Generalized Telegrapher's Problem

Authors: E. Collin, A. Delattre
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21463v1

Summary. This work develops a quantum theoretical framework to describe microwave signal propagation in waveguides by mapping classical transmission line theory onto quantum scattering matrices. Using a generalized flux formalism and Hodge decomposition, the authors classify wave dynamics into gradient and curl components. This provides a universal tool for analyzing quantum loads and is directly applicable to microwave-based quantum information transfer.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical framework connecting classical electromagnetism (waveguides) to quantum scattering theory provides powerful tools for modeling quantum information transfer in solid-state microwave circuits.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper maps classical microwave transmission line theory onto a quantum scattering matrix description to analyze quantum loads.

Main result. It establishes a universal theoretical framework that classifies traveling waves into gradient-type and curl-type charge-currents, shedding light on broken gauge symmetries in TE modes.

Method. The authors use a generalized flux formalism and apply a Hodge-like decomposition to the charge-current vector living on the confining electrodes.

Model / system. The system involves quantum signals traveling through microwave waveguides (Cartesian and cylindrical geometries) in a cryogenic environment, analyzed via input-output quantum optics theory.

Key observables. The load is characterized by complex coefficients $Z_{ ext{load}}(\alpha)$ for each propagating mode $\alpha$.

Important parameters / regimes. Waveguide geometries (Cartesian, cylindrical), mode types (TEM, TM, TE), and effective electrode width ($w_{ ext{eff}}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis extends beyond conventional TEM transport to include TM and TE modes, requiring the use of a generalized flux field.

Figures summary. Figures schematically relate classical transmission lines to quantum scattering matrices and illustrate electrode geometries for different guides.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from mapping classical theory to quantum scattering, deriving the charge-current vector via generalized flux, applying Hodge decomposition, and finally classifying wave types and analyzing modal parameters for various geometries.

Abstract

We report on the mapping of classical microwave transmission line theory onto the quantum scattering matrix description. By means of a generalized flux formalism a la Devoret, we derive a charge-current vector living on the confining electrodes, to which a Hodge-like decomposition is applied. The presented theory holds equally well for all types of waves traveling within Cartesian and cylindrical guide geometries (the usual Transverse Electric Magnetic, but also Transverse Magnetic and Transverse Electric ones). We then demonstrate how a generic load decomposes into a series of complex coefficients $Z_{load}(α)$, for each mode $α$ propagating along the line. This decomposition leads to a particular classification of traveling waves: gradient-type and curl-type charge-currents. This distinction shines a new light on a broken gauge symmetry exhibited by a class of Transverse Electric modes. Our results provide a universal theoretical framework, which finds a direct usage in microwave-based quantum information transfer.

Semiclassical Arrhenius law for quantum-thermal escape rates

Authors: Luca Salasnich, Cesare Vianello

Summary. This paper develops advanced semiclassical theories to accurately calculate the quantum-thermal escape rate from a metastable potential barrier. It resolves a known divergence in continuum rate calculations at low temperatures by incorporating the discrete nature of the quantum spectrum. The resulting uniform formula provides a highly accurate description of the rate across all temperature regimes.

Why it may be interesting. This work is highly relevant to AMO physics and open quantum systems because calculating escape rates is fundamental to understanding tunneling and decay processes in quantum devices (e.g., Josephson junctions, molecular dynamics). The rigorous treatment of the low-temperature crossover is a key theoretical challenge.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the calculation of the quantum-thermal escape rate from a metastable potential well, specifically resolving the divergence encountered when using continuum approximations at low temperatures.

Main result. A uniform semiclassical formula is derived that remains accurate over eleven orders of magnitude, successfully recovering the standard WKB decay rate at zero temperature while maintaining physical consistency across temperature regimes.

Method. The authors employ semiclassical methods, comparing continuum approximations (like Affleck's formula) with more rigorous approaches involving discrete quasi-bound states and uniform transmission probabilities derived from complex scaling techniques.

Model / system. The model is a quantum particle in a one-dimensional potential $U(x)$ featuring a metastable well and a barrier. The analysis uses harmonic approximations for the well and parabolic approximations for the barrier, applied to potentials like cubic or quintic forms.

Key observables. Escape rate ($\Gamma$), resonance widths ($\hbar\Gamma_n$), and the barrier height ($U_b$).

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature ($T$) or inverse temperature ($eta$), well frequency ($\omega_a$), barrier frequency ($\omega_b$), and the crossover temperature ($T_0$).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption being tested is the validity of continuum vs. discrete treatments; the paper assumes the applicability of semiclassical quantization rules (Bohr-Sommerfeld) and complex scaling methods.

Figures summary. The notes mention comparing rates derived from different formulas (uniform, discretized, Affleck's) and referencing tables comparing resonance positions and widths for potentials like the cubic case.

Paper structure. The structure moves from establishing the general rate formula (resonance + continuum) to analyzing the failure of continuum treatments at low T, developing discrete and uniform semiclassical corrections, and finally benchmarking these results against exact complex scaling calculations.

Abstract

The quantum-thermal escape rate from a metastable well in the absence of dissipation is customarily written as a Boltzmann average of the Hill-Wheeler flux over a continuum of energies. However, this continuum treatment diverges exponentially at low temperature. The divergence originates in a mismatch between a quantum partition function and a classical flux integral; retaining the discrete nature of the quasibound spectrum removes it exactly, and the resulting semiclassical Arrhenius law is obtained in closed form on both sides of the crossover temperature. Evaluating a uniform Kemble transmission probability on Bohr-Sommerfeld levels further recovers the standard WKB decay rate of the lowest resonance at zero temperature. Benchmarked against the resonances of cubic and quintic potentials, obtained by complex scaling, this uniform semiclassical result stays within $9\%$ of the exact rate over eleven orders of magnitude.

The charge dependent hard-sphere model: Polycrystals as low-energy configurations

Authors: Leonard Kreutz, Timo Ziereis
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21508v1

Summary. This work analyzes how rigid polycrystalline structures form as the low-energy ground state in a model of interacting charged hard spheres. Using $\Gamma$-convergence, the authors derive a continuum theory showing that the energy concentrates at grain boundaries. The resulting surface energy is anisotropic and depends critically on the misorientation mismatch, providing a detailed theoretical description of solid-solid interfaces.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical condensed matter, the rigorous mathematical machinery of $\Gamma$-convergence and the study of low-energy configurations in complex, constrained systems are highly relevant to understanding phase transitions and ground states in quantum many-body systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the emergence of rigid polycrystalline structures as low-energy configurations in atomistic ionic particle systems.

Main result. The limiting energy density at grain boundaries is anisotropic and depends on the relative misorientation and translation misfit, showing a 'brittle surface energy' due to the rigid potentials.

Method. The analysis employs $\Gamma$-convergence to derive a continuum theory from the discrete atomistic energy functional, describing the system via piecewise constant fields encoding local orientation.

Model / system. The system is an atomistic ionic particle model with charges $\pm 1$, governed by a pair-potential Hamiltonian involving hard-sphere interactions for both attraction (opposite charges) and repulsion (same charges).

Key observables. Limiting energy density ($\phi$), surface energy, and the function $u_{\epsilon}$ describing local lattice orientation.

Important parameters / regimes. The scaling parameter $\epsilon$ (related to $1/\sqrt{N}$), relative misorientation angle, and translation misfit vector.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that interpolating grain boundary layers are energetically unfavorable, leading to a 'brittle set-up' dictated by the rigid potentials.

Figures summary. Figure 1 shows the attractive and repulsive potentials; Figure 2 illustrates a minimizer configuration; Figure 3 shows the local orientation function $u_{\epsilon}$; and Figure 4 schematically depicts boundary regions.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the problem using the Hamiltonian, applies $\Gamma$-convergence to derive the continuum limit, proves key inequalities (Theorem 2.5) through complex energy estimations involving competitor constructions, and concludes by characterizing the anisotropic surface energy.

Abstract

We investigate the emergence of rigid polycrystalline structures in atomistic ionic particle systems as low-energy configurations. The interaction between particles of opposite charge is modeled by hard spheres that interact when they are tangential. The interaction between particles of same charge is modeled as a hard repulsion that forces a minimal distance between them. The atomistic energy is frame invariant, and no underlying reference lattice is assumed on the ionic configurations. The asymptotic behavior of configurations with finite surface energy scaling is identified by means of $Γ$-convergence. The related continuum theory is described by piecewise constant fields that encode the local orientation of the configuration. The limiting energy is local and concentrates at grain boundaries, which correspond to the boundaries of the regions where the underlying configuration has a constant orientation. The limiting energy density is anisotropic and depends on the relative misorientation of the two grains, their translation misfit, and the normal to their interface. Furthermore, we perform a fine analysis of surface energies for solid-solid and solid-vacuum phase transitions and determine energetically favorable orientation mismatches. This relies on a structure result for our grain boundaries, which shows that, due to the rigid setup, interpolating layers near the grain interface are energetically not favorable.

Abstract

Global Entanglement Quantification via Classical Shadows

Summary. This paper introduces the use of the classical shadows technique to estimate the generalized global entanglement, $E_G^{(n)}$, which quantifies multipartite entanglement. By leveraging classical shadows, the authors overcome the severe measurement overhead associated with directly estimating all necessary Pauli observables. The results demonstrate that this method offers a clear, resource-efficient advantage over traditional estimation techniques for characterizing entanglement in large quantum systems.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a crucial resource-efficient tool for characterizing complex quantum correlations, which is vital for benchmarking quantum hardware and understanding entanglement scaling in many-body quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core challenge is quantifying multipartite entanglement, a difficult task for which no universal measure exists.

Main result. The classical shadows technique provides a significant and measurable advantage over direct estimation methods for quantifying global entanglement, especially for large systems with limited measurement resources.

Method. The authors propose using the classical shadows protocol to estimate the expectation values of Pauli observables required for the generalized global entanglement measure, $E_G^{(n)}$.

Model / system. The study analyzes general quantum states ($ ho$) across $n$-qubit partitions, simulating results for well-known entangled states like GHZ, W, and EPR states, as well as random states.

Key observables. Generalized global entanglement ($E_G^{(n)}$), linear entropy of reduced density matrices ($ ext{SL}( ho_{\ell})$), and expectation values of Pauli observables.

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis explores scaling with system size ($N$), the number of qubits in the partition ($n$), and measurement resource ratios ($r$).

Assumptions / limitations. The classical shadows estimator is only unbiased in the asymptotic limit, and direct estimation suffers from severe scaling issues.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the faster convergence of shadow estimations towards the exact entanglement value compared to grouping methods, particularly as system size or required observables increase.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the entanglement measure, details the classical shadows technique for estimating required observables, compares its performance against grouping methods (QWC, FC) through simulations on various entangled states, and concludes with a demonstration of its feasibility in resource-limited regimes.

Abstract

Detecting and characterizing quantum correlations are tasks of great relevance in quantum information. More specifically, quantifying the amount of multipartite entanglement is a known difficult task, even for pure states. To this end, several entanglement measures have been proposed, although there is currently no universal manner to do so. In this work, we propose the classical shadows technique to measure the generalized global entanglement introduced in Phys. Rev. A 74, 022314. This particular multipartite entanglement quantifier $E_G^{(n)}$ relies on the linear entropies of all $n$-qubit partitions of a state. As the linear entropy can be written in terms of a complete set of observables of the subsystem, we employ classical shadows to estimate many observables with fewer measurements. We simulate the quantification of $E_G^{(n)}$ for well-known entangled states and for random states, comparing shadow estimations and grouping techniques. Our results show a clear advantage of classical shadows over direct estimation approaches, indicating that it can be a useful tool to quantify entanglement without the need to reconstruct the density operator of the whole system.

A Functorial Theory of Defects in Abelian Chern-Simons Theory

Authors: Daniel Galviz

Summary. This paper develops a comprehensive, functorial mathematical theory for Abelian Chern-Simons field theories, treating them as Topological Quantum Field Theories (TQFTs). It classifies the theory and its defects using a mathematical invariant called the finite quadratic module. This work provides a rigorous framework for understanding topological order in condensed matter systems.

Why it may be interesting. The deep connection between Chern-Simons theory, topological order, and categorical structures provides powerful mathematical tools for understanding the ground state degeneracy and robust properties of strongly correlated electron systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To provide a unified, functorial description of Abelian Chern-Simons theories, organizing previously separate aspects like closed-manifold partition functions, BF theory, Turaev-Viro realizations, symmetries, and defect structures.

Main result. The theory is rigorously classified by the finite quadratic module $( ext{GK}, q_K)$, which governs the bulk theory, defects, and symmetries. The framework successfully relates defect structures to categorical data, providing a concrete model for topological orders.

Method. The authors utilize the framework of Categorical TQFTs, employing the Alterfold construction and analyzing the theory via its classification invariant, the finite quadratic module.

Model / system. The primary model is Abelian Chern-Simons theory, which serves as an effective topological field theory for topological phases of matter, such as those found in fractional quantum Hall states.

Key observables. The theory is governed by the invariant $( ext{GK}, q_K)$; defects are classified by specific categorical data within the associated pointed modular category $ ext{C}( ext{GK}, q_K)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The classification depends on the lattice $ ext{GK}$ and the quadratic form $q_K$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on establishing functorial relationships between different mathematical realizations (e.g., Turaev-Viro vs. Alterfold) and assumes the validity of the categorical framework for defect extension.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the functorial TQFT structure using the quadratic module invariant, then detailing the classification of defects (boundaries, domain walls) via categorical theorems, and finally comparing this structure with known lattice models (like the toric code) and addressing dualities.

Abstract

Recent work has constructed Abelian Chern-Simons theories as categorical TQFTs, allowing us to naturally incorporate categorical defects and construct defect extensions of Abelian Chern-Simons TQFTs. We first identify the Turaev-Viro realizations of Abelian Chern-Simons theory in the center and doubled pointed modular cases, clarifying the distinction between single bulk realizations and canonical doubled ones. Alternatively, the Alterfold construction supplies the associated topological boundaries, domain walls, and condensation sectors, establishing an explicit Alterfold/Chern-Simons dictionary. We show that the finite quadratic module is the invariant controlling the bulk theory, its topological symmetries, orientation-reversal invariance, and defects. We further show that multicomponent Abelian BF theory arises as the extended TQFT of an off-diagonal Abelian Chern-Simons theory, placing it naturally within the same extended framework. Finally, we demonstrate that recently proposed Abelian Chern-Simons dualities do not define a genuine TQFT duality. These results provide a concrete model for defects in Abelian topological orders and suggest a route toward the non-Abelian case.

Abstract

We consider the 4.8.8 color code in a brickwork layout on the square lattice with nearest-neighbor gates. We identify periodic superdense circuits with twelve CNOT layers that appear to preserve the full distance of these codes. Circuit-level simulations targeting superconducting architectures demonstrate favorable scaling of 4.8.8 color codes at plausible physical noise rates, including both for planar codes and their toroidal cousins under periodic boundary conditions.

Summary. This paper introduces a novel Renormalization Group approach for random matrix models used to study quantum gravity. By employing a partial matrix-valued intermediate field in the large-N limit, the authors construct a symmetry-preserving RG flow. This leads to a well-defined continuum limit exhibiting a critical fixed point whose exponent matches known results from double-scaling theory.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on quantum gravity, the rigorous application of RG techniques, especially the background-field method, provides advanced theoretical tools for analyzing critical phenomena and continuum limits in complex many-body systems, which has parallels in strongly correlated electron models.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is developing a reliable Renormalization Group (RG) flow for discrete gravity models that successfully preserves the underlying symmetry group, such as U(N) or O(N).

Main result. The authors construct a relational RG flow that preserves symmetry and yields an infrared theory described by a 3D non-local Euclidean field theory with a non-trivial Wilson-Fisher-like fixed point.

Method. The method involves introducing a partial matrix-valued intermediate field and utilizing the large-N limit, where measure concentration allows this field to act as a self-consistent background field.

Model / system. The study focuses on random matrix models, which are used to model the random geometry of two-dimensional quantum Euclidean spacetime. The analysis relates these models to discrete gravity partition functions.

Key observables. Critical exponents, the Wilson-Fisher fixed point structure, and the double-scaling exponent.

Important parameters / regimes. The large-$N$ limit, the continuum limit, and the coupling constants defining the random matrix interactions.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the symmetry-preserving RG challenge, develops the novel background-field approach using the intermediate field, derives the resulting continuum theory, and concludes by discussing extensions to other discrete gravity models.

Abstract

The construction of a reliable renormalization group (RG) flow for discrete gravity models that preserves their underlying symmetry group, typically $U(N)$ or $O(N)$, remains an open problem. For random matrix models, which are the focus of this paper, this symmetry is intrinsically tied to the interactions encoding the random geometry of two-dimensional quantum Euclidean spacetime. We develop a novel approach based on the introduction of a partial matrix-valued intermediate field. In the large-$N$ limit, measure concentration strongly suppresses fluctuations of its singular values, allowing it to play the role of a self-consistent background field. This provides the basis for a relational RG in which the notion of scale is dynamically induced by the effective Gaussian measure in the basis where the intermediate field is diagonal. Our construction preserves the symmetry of the original model and admits a well-defined continuum limit. We show that the resulting infrared theory is described by a three-dimensional non-local Euclidean field theory with a non-trivial Wilson-Fisher-like fixed point and a single relevant direction. Remarkably, the associated critical exponent exactly matches the standard double-scaling exponent. We finally discuss extensions of the background-field approach to other discrete gravity models, such as random tensor models, and to different symmetry groups, as well as connections with more formal RG frameworks and information geometry.

Uhlmann Geometry of Fixed-Rank Density Matrices: A Fiber-Bundle Approach

Authors: Xu-Yang Hou, Hao Guo
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.21875v1

Summary. This paper develops a geometric theory for mixed quantum states by formulating Uhlmann's phase on the manifold of fixed-rank density matrices. It constructs this space as a principal fiber bundle, yielding a unique connection form. The key finding is that this framework predicts non-trivial, quantized topological invariants, linking the geometry of mixed states to measurable phenomena like the quantum Hall effect.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful geometric tool to analyze mixed states, moving beyond the limitations of full-rank assumptions. The connection to topological invariants suggests that geometric phases in mixed systems can carry quantized, measurable information.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper extends Uhlmann's geometric phase theory from full-rank to the physically relevant case of fixed-rank density matrices ($ ext{rank}( ho) = k < N$). It aims to provide a robust geometric diagnostic for mixed-state quantum matter.

Main result. The fixed-rank state space forms the base of a principal $U(k)$-bundle, allowing the Uhlmann connection to be uniquely determined by a Sylvester equation. This framework predicts non-trivial, quantized topological invariants like the second Chern number for these mixed states.

Method. The authors employ differential geometry, constructing the theory on the manifold of rank-$k$ density matrices by defining a principal fiber bundle structure over this base manifold. They derive the connection form using techniques related to minimal purifications and the Bures metric.

Model / system. The system is described by density matrices $ ho$ whose rank is fixed at $k < N$. This models physical scenarios such as reduced states of subsystems or states confined to a decoherence-free subspace.

Key observables. Uhlmann phase, Chern numbers (especially the second Chern number), Bures distance, and the Uhlmann connection form $\omega$.

Important parameters / regimes. The fixed rank $k$ relative to the Hilbert space dimension $N$, and the degeneracy of the spectral weights.

Assumptions / limitations. The theory assumes the existence of minimal purifications $W$ that define the principal $U(k)$-bundle structure over the fixed-rank stratum.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the principal $U(k)$-bundle structure over the fixed-rank stratum, derives the Uhlmann connection via the Sylvester equation, and calculates topological invariants (like the Chern number) by relating the geometry to known physical responses (e.g., quantum Hall effect).

Abstract

For full-rank density matrices the state space is contractible, the Uhlmann bundle is topologically trivial, and the holonomy admits no quantized invariants; although the Uhlmann phase is genuinely geometric, this topological poverty has kept it from serving as a robust physical diagnostic of mixed-state matter. Mixed states of fixed rank below the Hilbert-space dimension, however, are ubiquitous: reduced states of subsystems, states in an invariant sector, and states in a decoherence-free subspace all have a support smaller than the Hilbert space that can vary with parameters. Their geometry is far richer: the support carries genuine curvature, non-Abelian holonomy, and Chern topology. We formulate Uhlmann's theory directly on the manifold of rank-$k$ density matrices: minimal purifications make the fixed-rank stratum the base of a principal $U(k)$-bundle whose Uhlmann connection is uniquely determined by a Sylvester equation, and in an eigenframe this connection takes a closed form reducing to the Berry, Wilczek--Zee, and faithful Uhlmann connections at $k=1$, at equal weights, and at $k=N$. The fixed-rank bundle inherits the topology of the Grassmannian, and non-factorizable higher Chern topology requires failure of the global eigenline splitting, which in the present spectral setting requires degeneracy, minimally realized by a rank-2 Yang monopole whose quantized second Chern number links the geometry to the four-dimensional quantum Hall response. Solvable models, from a genuinely non-Abelian holonomy to a decoherence-free Lindblad orbit, illustrate the content: the supporting subspace carries the topology, the spectral weights shape the transport, and the Uhlmann phase provides a direct geometric signature of these mixed-state phase transitions.

In the decoupled-layer limit, the lightly hole-doped ground state is typically an incommensurate spin-stripe state. However, with sufficiently strong interlayer coupling, stripe order is destabilized relative to a commensurate Néel-ordered metal. The resulting state exhibits a reconstructed Fermi surface with hole pockets centered at $(\pmπ/2,\pmπ/2)$ that are similar in character to those seen in experiments. Our results illustrate the qualitatively new physics that can arise from interlayer coupling in multilayer cuprates.

Selection rules for pinned and quasipinned occupation numbers with degeneracy

Summary. This theoretical paper rigorously proves selection rules governing the occupation numbers of fermionic states when these numbers are constrained by generalized Pauli principles. It extends previous results to handle degeneracy without assumptions and provides quantitative bounds for 'quasipinning' scenarios. This work is vital for developing accurate theoretical descriptions of strongly correlated electron systems.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical treatment of selection rules in the presence of degeneracy and approximations (quasipinning) is crucial for accurately modeling strongly correlated electron systems where simple mean-field approaches fail.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper establishes selection rules for occupation numbers when these numbers saturate generalized Pauli constraints in fermionic systems.

Main result. It proves the selection rule for every generalized Pauli constraint, even in the degenerate case, and extends the rule to quasipinned states with quantifiable error bounds.

Method. The proof combines algebraic techniques related to moment maps, compactness arguments, and analysis of the polytope defined by occupation numbers.

Model / system. The system involves fermionic states ($\Psi$) whose properties are characterized by the one-body reduced density matrix ($\gamma\Psi$) and its eigenvalues, the natural occupation numbers ($\lambda_\Psi$). The constraints define a polytope ($\Delta_{d,N}$).

Key observables. Natural occupation numbers ($\lambda_j$), the one-body reduced density matrix ($\gamma\Psi$), and the deviation function $f(\Psi)$ from the constraint manifold.

Important parameters / regimes. The degree of degeneracy, the specific generalized Pauli constraint $D$, and the small deviation parameter $f(\Psi)$ for quasipinned states.

Assumptions / limitations. The rule fails for ordering constraints ($\lambda_j \ge \lambda_{j+1}$), and the proof for the general case relies on advanced geometric properties of moment maps.

Paper structure. The paper develops from the known non-degenerate case to the general degenerate case, proving the selection rule for all generalized Pauli constraints, and then analyzing the implications for quasipinned states.

Abstract

When the natural occupation numbers of a fermionic state saturate a generalized Pauli constraint, the state obeys a selection rule: only those configurations of natural orbitals that saturate the constraint themselves contribute. Pinning thus singles out an active space. The selection rule was proved for non-degenerate occupation numbers; for degenerate ones it was conjectured, and proved for one saturated constraint under an unverified assumption. Here, it is proved for every generalized Pauli constraint, with no assumption, and also in the spin-adapted setting. The rule can only fail for the ordering constraints $λ_j\geλ_{j+1}$. Several saturated constraints are served by one basis whenever their common zero face contains a non-degenerate point. In practice, occupation numbers are quasipinned rather than pinned, and I show what the rule then becomes.
