Summary. This paper introduces and reviews the Truncated Wigner Approximation (TWA) as an efficient method to simulate complex, dissipative many-body spin systems. By mapping the density matrix onto a continuous phase space, it derives solvable stochastic differential equations governing system evolution. This allows for calculating key quantum observables like correlations and thermal states in systems ranging from magnetism to light emission.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, computationally tractable tool (TWA) to study open quantum dynamics in spin systems, bridging the gap between exact quantum descriptions and classical stochastic simulations, which is highly relevant for quantum optics and magnetism.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is accurately simulating the complex, non-equilibrium dynamics of interacting and/or dissipative open spin systems.

Main result. The authors successfully derive a computationally inexpensive stochastic differential equation (SDE) framework for describing these dynamics using the Truncated Wigner Approximation (TWA). This method allows calculation of expectation values, correlations, and thermal states.

Method. The paper employs the Wigner-Moyal mapping to represent the density matrix in continuous phase space. The resulting evolution equation is approximated into a Fokker-Planck equation equivalent to Ito SDEs for the spin variables.

Model / system. The focus is on open spin systems, modeled using a continuous SU(2) representation on an enlarged Bloch sphere. The dynamics are governed by mapping the Lindblad master equation onto phase-space evolution equations.

Key observables. Expectation values of operators, multi-time correlations, spectra, thermal states, and ground state energies.

Important parameters / regimes. The approximation quality depends on neglecting higher-order derivatives in the Fokker-Planck expansion; the method is benchmarked against exactly solvable models.

Assumptions / limitations. Key assumptions include using controlled approximations to map the master equation to SDEs, and exploiting gauge freedom in the continuous phase space representation. It assumes that the path integral formulation can be rigorously mapped onto the curved phase space.

Figures summary. Not specified by notes; the mathematical derivations focus on explicit formulas relating Pauli matrices to derivatives of the phase-point operator $\hat{\Delta}$ with respect to spherical coordinates $( heta, \phi)$.

Paper structure. The paper reviews the TWA framework, details the mapping from the Lindblad equation to SDEs via Wigner function evolution, discusses mathematical tools for this mapping (derivatives of phase-point operators), and benchmarks the method on various physical problems.

Abstract

We review the truncated Wigner approximation (TWA) for spins as a computationally inexpensive numerical approximation method to describe interacting and / or dissipative many-body spin systems. Using the Wigner-Moyal mapping from Hilbert space to a suitable phase space, the many-body density matrix is represented by a c-number distribution, the Wigner function. The gauge freedom in continuous phase space can be exploited to find positive Wigner functions for a large class of spin states, including entangled ones. Employing different sets of correspondence rules, we derive equations of motion for the Wigner function, which, applying controlled approximations, can be mapped to stochastic differential equations. This allows a computationally inexpensive simulation of expectation values. Using a phase-space analog of the quantum regression theorem also multi-time correlations and spectra can be obtained. To illustrate the potential of the method, we benchmark the TWA for spins with some exactly solvable problems of interacting, dissipative spin systems, and then discuss its application to collective processes, such as the superradiant emission of light. Extending the TWA to imaginary time furthermore provides a tool to approximately calculate thermal and ground states of spin Hamiltonians. Finally, we show that the TWA stochastic equations can equivalently be derived within a path-integral approach, provided that the operator products in the dissipator are rigorously mapped onto the curved phase space.

Abstract

We show that this phenomenon can be realized using ultracold bosonic atoms in optical cavities.

Abstract

We study the two-step Floquet dynamics of the chiral $J_1$-$J_2$-$K$ Heisenberg model on the $4\times4$ torus, alternating its non-chiral Heisenberg part $H_{\rm AF}$ and its chiral plaquette part $H_K$, at the parameter point where the static model (recovered in the infinite frequency limit) hosts a quasi-degenerate, spectrally isolated chiral-spin-liquid (CSL) topological doublet. The one-period propagator is computed exactly in all momentum/rotation symmetry sectors. It is shown that, decreasing the frequency, the topological doublet survives the drive far beyond the frequency $ω_{\mathrm{res}}\simeq 11.5 J_1$ at which folded states first cross it in quasienergy: the time-averaged energy of the Floquet eigenstates, which orders the folded Floquet spectrum, shows that the doublet remains the isolated bottom of the spectrum down to $ω\simeq6J_1$, while stroboscopic time evolution over thousands of periods shows no heating for $ω\gtrsim ω_{\mathrm{res}}$ and only slow absorption below. The quasienergy resonances that occur in the folded regime are invisible in the average energy, identifying them as parametrically weak avoided crossings. We argue that this mechanism -- stability controlled by local energy scales rather than by the extensive many-body bandwidth -- is precisely the one expected to survive in the thermodynamic limit, where a prethermal Floquet CSL should persist for $ω$ above a threshold set by local scales, with heating times exponentially long in $ω/J_1$. Consistently, the optimal $D=3$ chiral PEPS of the static problem still describes the driven doublet deep in the folded regime, with an essentially unchanged local tensor.

Summary. This paper theoretically investigates how low-frequency microwave pumping can induce resonant excitations in ferromagnetic magnons within thin films like YIG. Using Floquet theory and perturbation methods, the authors predict specific resonance conditions and calculate the critical driving amplitudes required to excite these modes. The findings offer a pathway for targeted control of quantum magnetic states using external electromagnetic fields.

Why it may be interesting. This work is highly relevant as it uses advanced techniques from periodically driven quantum systems (Floquet theory) to model collective excitations in magnetic materials, providing a theoretical framework for controlling magnon dynamics using external fields.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study analyzes resonant excitations of ferromagnetic magnons driven by low-frequency microwave pumping, focusing on parametric resonances occurring when driving frequencies are below the natural magnon energy.

Main result. The theory predicts regions of parametric resonance and determines threshold amplitudes for excitation, showing a characteristic power-law dependence on damping. These results are compared with micromagnetic simulations.

Method. The primary methods involve applying Floquet theory to analyze time-periodic systems, utilizing degenerate perturbation theory in the resulting quasi-energy spectrum, and comparing these analytical predictions against full micromagnetic simulations solving the LLG equation.

Model / system. The system modeled is thin films of ferro- or ferri-magnetic materials like YIG. The dynamics are governed by an effective spin-$S$ Heisenberg model Hamiltonian, analyzed using linear spin wave approximations and bosonic operators.

Key observables. Quasi-energies ($\epsilon$), resonance thresholds ($h_{th}$), instability regions ($ ext{Im}(\epsilon)>0$), and magnon density increase above the threshold.

Important parameters / regimes. Damping constant ($\alpha$), microwave driving amplitude ($h_1$), and driving frequency ($\omega_D$). Resonance conditions are linked to $\omega_D = 2\omega_k/n$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the linear spin wave approximation, the uniform-mode approximation, and perturbative expansions for small or intermediate driving amplitudes; higher-order magnon-magnon scattering is neglected in the analytic models.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate analytical quasi-energies vs. numerical solutions showing resonance structure (Fig 1), stability charts mapping instability regions ($ ext{Im}(\epsilon)>0$) vs. drive parameters (Fig 2, Fig 4), and simulation results comparing magnon dispersion below and above the predicted threshold (Fig 5).

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the Hamiltonian and linear spin wave framework to applying Floquet theory for time-periodic driving. It then develops analytical predictions for resonance thresholds using perturbation theory, addresses strong driving fields via gauge transformations involving Bessel functions, and concludes by comparing these findings with full micromagnetic simulations.

Abstract

We analyse resonant excitations of ferromagnetic magnons via microwave pumping using Floquet theory. Special focus is put on driving frequencies that are below the corresponding magnon energy, which can be excited in large parameter regions via parametric resonances. We develop a theoretical framework that analytically predicts the regions of resonances and resonance thresholds in thin films of ferro- and ferri-magnetic materials like YIG as a function of damping, amplitude and frequency. Resonance regions are separated by exceptional points of the quasi-energies and the results are compared with micromagnetic simulations. The corresponding threshold amplitudes can be estimated from a characteristic powerlaw with damping, leading to the possibility of targeted exciatations at selected wavenumbers using low frequency drive.

High-frequency dual-channel lock-in detection via rapidly oscillating driving

Summary. This paper proposes and demonstrates a novel dual-channel lock-in detection protocol for measuring weak, high-frequency AC signals using quantum probes like NV centers in diamond. By applying rapidly oscillating driving fields, the system's dynamics are effectively modulated to allow simultaneous measurement of both signal amplitude and initial phase from two simple population measurements. This significantly enhances noise resistance and extends sensing capabilities into previously inaccessible high-frequency regimes.

Why it may be interesting. This work is highly relevant to AMO physics as it pushes quantum sensing into higher frequency regimes by engineering effective Hamiltonians through time-dependent driving fields. The use of solid-state qubits (NV centers) for this purpose provides a concrete platform for realizing advanced quantum metrology protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is developing a general protocol for dual-channel lock-in detection of high-frequency AC signals, overcoming limitations faced by conventional methods in the high-frequency regime.

Main result. The proposed quantum protocol successfully extends lock-in detection to high frequencies by using rapidly oscillating driving fields, enabling simultaneous estimation of both signal amplitude and initial phase from only two population measurements.

Method. The method involves deriving an effective Hamiltonian via the Magnus expansion after applying a rapidly oscillating driving field. This allows measuring in-phase ($I_{ ext{sig}}$) and quadrature ($Q_{ ext{sig}}$) components separately by tuning the driving field's phase.

Model / system. The protocol is demonstrated using nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers in diamond as a two-level quantum probe. The system dynamics are governed by an effective Hamiltonian derived from the time evolution under both the target signal and the rapidly oscillating drive fields.

Key observables. Measurable quantities include the state populations ($P_0, P_1$), which determine $I_{ ext{sig}}$ and $Q_{ ext{sig}}$. Performance is quantified by Root Mean Squared Errors (RMSEs) for amplitude/phase and Signal-to-Noise Improvement Ratio (SNIR).

Important parameters / regimes. Key parameters include the target signal amplitude ($A$), initial phase ($\varphi$), driving field strength ($B$), and frequency ($\omega$). The protocol works even when background noise power is much larger than the signal power.

Assumptions / limitations. The derivation relies on the Magnus expansion approximation, which is assumed valid for short evolution times relative to the total measurement time. The analysis also considers various noise models (white, 1/f, harmonic).

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically illustrates the protocol steps using initialization, target signal application, and microwave driving fields. Figure 3 compares performance metrics like RMSEs and SNIR across different noise types (White, 1/f, Harmonic) under varying noise intensities.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the general theoretical framework for dual-channel lock-in detection using rapidly oscillating drives. It then details the implementation on NV centers in diamond, deriving the measurement scheme and analyzing performance metrics like SNR improvement against various noise sources.

Abstract

Here we propose a general protocol for dual-channel lock-in detection of high-frequency ac signals. We find that the effect of a high-frequency target signal can be modulated through the application of rapidly oscillating driving fields. Based on this mechanism, we develop a quantum dual-channel lock-in detection protocol for high-frequency signals, which not only extends the accessible frequency range of quantum sensing but also enables the simultaneous estimation of the signal amplitude and initial phase. Furthermore, we present a feasible implementation scheme of the protocol based on nitrogen-vacancy centers in diamond. Numerical simulations demonstrate that the proposed protocol can effectively filter out background noise and significantly improve the signal-to-noise ratio. Our results provide a promising approach for realizing noise-resistant detection of weak signals in the high-frequency regime.

Summary. This paper introduces an exact mathematical mapping showing that the dynamics of a $p$-body interacting Ising model can be simplified to an equivalent two-body model with a reduced interaction range. This 'order-range' duality provides a systematic method for constructing and studying solvable, dual-unitary Floquet quantum systems.

Why it may be interesting. The explicit construction of dual-unitary models and the systematic reduction of high-order interactions into simpler, lower-dimensional effective Hamiltonians provide powerful tools for analyzing complex quantum many-body physics relevant to non-equilibrium statistical mechanics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study focuses on understanding non-equilibrium dynamics of interacting quantum many-body systems, specifically extending concepts like dual unitarity to models with high-order $p$-body interactions.

Main result. The paper establishes an exact Floquet interaction order-range mapping, showing that the evolution of a $p$-body model can be mapped onto an equivalent two-body Ising model with maximum range $p-1$.

Method. The authors use algebraic proofs involving Pauli matrices and induction to derive the mapping theorems, followed by analyzing spectral statistics and entanglement dynamics.

Model / system. The system is a periodically kicked one-dimensional spin-$1/2$ Ising model featuring contiguous $p$-body interactions. The analysis focuses on the Floquet evolution operator derived from this Hamiltonian.

Key observables. Entanglement dynamics (e.g., $\alpha$-Rényi entropies) and spectral statistics (Poisson vs. Wigner-Dyson distributions).

Important parameters / regimes. Interaction strengths ($J=n\pi/4$), kicking strength ($b=\pi/4$), and the system size parity ($L$ even or odd).

Assumptions / limitations. The mapping relies on specific tuning of interaction strengths (e.g., $J = n\pi/4$) and assumes periodic boundary conditions.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate level spacing distributions for $p=3$, showing Poisson for even $L$ and Wigner-Dyson for odd $L$. Figure 3 shows the time evolution of 2-$ ext{Rényi}$ entanglement entropy for $p=3$ and $p=4$ under different initial state types.

Paper structure. The paper systematically develops proofs (Lemma 2, Theorem 1) establishing the order-range mapping from $p$-body to $(p-1)$-range 2-body interactions. It then analyzes the consequences of this mapping based on the parity of system length $L$, concluding with entanglement dynamics calculations.

Abstract

We introduce and study a periodically kicked version of a one-dimensional spin-$1/2$ Ising model with contiguous $p$-body interactions. At specific interaction strengths of the model, we establish an exact Floquet interaction $\textit{order-range}$ mapping: the Floquet evolution operator of the $p$-body Ising model is exactly mapped to a two body Ising model with interactions having maximum range $p-1$ and exactly determined couplings. Upon further tuning the kicking strength, the mapped model becomes dual-unitary, thus providing an explicit family of $p$-body dual unitary models. We then determine exact entanglement dynamics of the $p$-body model for a class of solvable initial states at all times. We further illustrate the nontrivial structures that can emerge from the $\textit{order-range}$ mapping using a minimal example of $p=3$ and generalize it for all odd $p$. These findings provide a systematic route to constructing and studying $p$-body dual-unitary Floquet models.
