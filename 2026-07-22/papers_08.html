Summary. This paper introduces the Stochastic Pauli-path Simulator (SPPS), a novel computational framework designed to enable large-scale classical simulation of quantum circuits used in optimization. By using path sampling and automatic differentiation, SPPS provides unbiased stochastic gradient estimates necessary for variational quantum algorithms. This advances the utility of Pauli-based methods beyond simple forward state estimation into practical quantum optimization.

Main result. The proposed Stochastic Pauli-path Simulator (SPPS) yields unbiased gradient estimates with provable convergence guarantees, successfully extending Pauli-based simulation from simple forward estimation to complex, large-scale quantum optimization.

Method. SPPS uses path sampling across the full Pauli-path space during Heisenberg evolution and employs importance reweighting combined with Path Automatic Differentiation (PAD) to derive unbiased stochastic gradient estimators for variational quantum algorithms.

Abstract

Pauli-based simulators offer a promising route to large-scale classical simulation of quantum circuits in the low-magic regime. Yet their applicability remains largely limited to forward simulation, making them inadequate for optimization-driven quantum tasks such as variational state preparation and parameter initialization. Existing approaches either lack native support for gradient-based optimization or suffer from severe gradient bias. Here we propose the stochastic Pauli-path simulator (SPPS), a computational framework for large-scale quantum optimization that enables unbiased stochastic gradient estimation via Pauli-path sampling across optimization iterations. Our theoretical analysis shows that the proposed simulator yields unbiased gradient estimates and admits provable convergence guarantees. We systematically evaluate our proposal, including quantum eigensolver benchmarks with up to 100 qubits and quantum neural network benchmarks with up to 40 qubits. Across these tasks, SPPS faithfully tracks optimization dynamics, converges within minutes, and broadens the role of Pauli-based simulation from forward estimation to large-scale quantum optimization.

Abstract

Refractory high-entropy alloys have emerged as promising candidates for high-temperature applications due to their exceptional mechanical properties. Understanding the thermodynamic mechanisms underlying chemical ordering in these complex systems is critical for optimizing their performance. These findings provide fundamental insights into the interplay between compositions, chemical ordering, and mechanical performance, offering guidance for the design of refractory high-entropy alloys.

Summary. This paper introduces the Universal Parent Hamiltonian Adiabatic Warm Start (UPHAWS) protocol to solve the problem of initializing quantum simulations near quantum phase transitions. Abstract

Computing the ground state properties of quantum systems is an important potential application of quantum computing. The success probability of quantum phase estimation approaches to ground state problems is proportional to the overlap of the input state with the ground state. Local ansatz state approaches suffer from the orthogonality catastrophe, whereas adiabatic state preparation (ASP) can prepare good approximations with a cost growing with the inverse square of the minimum spectral gap along the adiabatic path. When the initial and target Hamiltonians lie in quantum phases separated by a first order phase transition, the minimum spectral gap along the adiabatic path becomes exponentially small as a function of system size. We pursue a solution to this problem based on choosing an initial Hamiltonian for adiabatic state preparation whose ground state lies in the same quantum phase as the target ground state. We develop a protocol for universal adiabatic warm starts with universal parent Hamiltonians (UPHAWS) that can initialize ASP in any state whose preparation circuit is known. We use the Feynman--Kitaev clock Hamiltonian as a universal parent Hamiltonian, for preparation circuits with and without mid circuit measurement. We benchmark the framework on a $\mathbb{Z}_2$-symmetric matrix product state (MPS) family interpolating to a target GHZ Hamiltonian, and on the linear $H_6$ chain under symmetric bond stretching. For the $H_6$ system a bond-dimension-$4$ matrix product state warm-start increases the minimum gap on the adiabatic path by a factor of two relative to the Hartree-Fock initialization. To perform these classical benchmark simulations we develop a momentum-space truncation of the adiabatic Hamiltonian that may be of independent interest.

Abstract

We present the first unified analytical description of the bound, scattering, and Gamow (metastable) resonance states of the one-dimensional symmetric Woods--Saxon potential within the framework of Dunkl quantum mechanics, establishing a single analytical framework that consistently describes all physically relevant spectral regimes. The Dunkl deformation introduces a reflection-sector-dependent inverse-square interaction that precludes a direct analytical treatment of the corresponding Schrödinger equation. By employing a Pekeris-type approximation, the problem is transformed into a hypergeometric differential equation, enabling the derivation of analytical wave functions, bound-state quantization conditions, scattering amplitudes, and Gamow resonance solutions within a unified formalism. The results reveal that the Dunkl deformation fundamentally modifies the system's spectral properties by generating a reflection-sector-dependent splitting of the bound-state spectrum, along with quantitative changes in the reflection and transmission probabilities. The narrow transmission peaks are shown to originate from long-lived Gamow resonance states, whose complex energies, decay widths, and lifetimes are determined through analytic continuation into the complex-energy plane. In the limit of vanishing Dunkl deformation, the conventional Woods--Saxon results are fully recovered, confirming the consistency of the proposed formalism. The proposed analytical framework provides a versatile approach for investigating finite-range potentials in Dunkl quantum mechanics and opens the way to systematic studies of reflection-deformed quantum systems beyond the Woods--Saxon interaction.

Abstract

Abstract

Quantum Hamiltonian descent (QHD) formulates continuous optimization as time-dependent quantum dynamics, where a kinetic term drives exploration and a potential term encodes the objective function. Digital implementations of QHD require encoding the search space into qubits, and this choice can shift the dominant cost among logical qubits, circuit depth, non-Clifford rotations, and potential synthesis. In this work, we present an encoding-aware resource analysis comparing one-hot and binary amplitude encodings for QHD. We derive gate-count scalings, construct and validate circuits against classical \Sch-equation solvers, and estimate Clifford+$R_z$ and fault-tolerant Clifford+$T$ resources on benchmark optimization problems. These results suggest that exploiting the analytic structure of the target function to compile the potential evolution in QHD more efficiently is needed for further resource reductions.

Abstract

We consider a formal model of quantum circuit description languages (QCDLs) in which semantically meaningful programs correspond to computable unitary matrices. We show that any semantically universal QCDL -- that is, any QCDL able to describe all computable unitary matrices, which in turn form the set of matrices we can meaningfully represent on digital hardware -- cannot have a semi-decidable set of semantically meaningful descriptions. In particular, no such language admits a compiler that reliably recognizes all valid program descriptions. This result stands in contrast to classical programming languages. While compilation in languages such as C or C++ may itself involve non-terminating computations, the set of semantically meaningful programs remains recursively enumerable, since successful compilation provides a witness of validity. The essential difference lies in the nature of the semantic domains: classical languages describe partial recursive functions, whereas QCDLs describe total unitary operators. Our analysis establishes a fundamental limitation of quantum circuit description languages and highlights a structural distinction between classical and quantum models of computation at the level of formal language theory.

It establishes stability criteria for both stationary and traveling wave solutions based on the system's parameters like damping and parametric gain. The findings detail how fractional diffraction fundamentally alters soliton existence, stability boundaries, and interaction dynamics.

Why it may be interesting. This work extends nonlinear physics into fractional calculus, which is crucial for modeling wave propagation in media where dispersion/diffraction are non-local or anomalous. The interplay between parametric driving, loss, and fractional effects provides a rich platform for studying dissipative quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the existence, stability, and dynamics of optical solitons within a complex nonlinear wave equation incorporating fractional diffraction, cubic nonlinearity, linear loss, and parametric driving.

Main result. The analysis reveals two families of quiescent solitons ($\psi_+$ and $\psi_-$) with distinct stabilities; furthermore, moving solitons exhibit stable regimes that are highly dependent on the fractional diffraction exponent ($\alpha$) and velocity ($V$).

Method. Stability is assessed by linearizing the governing equation around soliton solutions and solving the resulting eigenvalue problem using numerical techniques like the Fourier collocation method.

Model / system. The system is modeled by a one-dimensional nonlinear Schrödinger equation featuring a Riesz-fractional diffraction operator, cubic self-focusing nonlinearity, damping ($\gamma$), and parametric gain ($h$). This model is suggested to be realizable in laser cavities.

Key observables. Soliton stability (eigenvalues $ ext{Re}(\delta)$), soliton profiles ($\psi^+$ vs $\psi^-$), collision outcomes (quasi-elasticity, repulsion), and the norm/momentum of the solitons.

Important parameters / regimes. Fractional diffraction exponent ($\alpha$, $1 < \alpha \le 2$), parametric drive amplitude ($h$), loss coefficient ($\gamma$), and soliton velocity ($V$).

Assumptions / limitations. The stability analysis relies on linearizing perturbations around found stationary or traveling solutions, and the interaction studies often focus on the lossless limit ($\gamma=0$).

Figures summary. Figures illustrate existence/stability diagrams for quiescent solitons vs. parameters (Fig. 1), profile variations with velocity and $\alpha$ (Fig. 5), time evolution of stability regimes (Fig. 6), and detailed collision/pursuit interactions between pairs of stable traveling solitons (Fig. 7).

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the governing equation in different limits ($\gamma=0$, $V=0$), determines soliton existence via numerical methods, performs rigorous stability analysis for both quiescent and moving states, and concludes by characterizing complex interaction dynamics.

Abstract

We systematically investigate the existence, stability, and dynamics of optical solitons in the framework of the one-dimensional nonlinear Schrödinger equation with the Riesz-fractional diffraction operator, cubic self-focusing, and linear loss, balanced by a linear parametric drive. The model, which can be realized in a laser cavity, produces standing and moving solitons, the latter ones existing below a critical velocity. One of the soliton species is stable in a wide range of parameters, while others are unstable. The fractional diffraction significantly alters the existence conditions and stability thresholds of the solitons. Collision between moving solitons are considered too. The results essentially expand the variety of nonlinear modes in media with fractional diffraction.

Summary. The paper investigates the fundamental physics behind Chiral-Induced Spin Selectivity (CISS) using a DNA-based model simulated with the Lindblad master equation. It introduces 'mobility polarization' as a key diagnostic, demonstrating that spin-independent dissipation is necessary to stabilize the spin selectivity effect into a measurable steady state. This provides a general theoretical framework for understanding spin filtering in chiral molecular conductors.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete theoretical mechanism for CISS based on open quantum system dynamics (dissipation), which is highly relevant to understanding charge transport in nanoscale molecular electronics and bio-hybrid devices.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The microscopic origin of Chiral-Induced Spin Selectivity (CISS) remains highly debated, particularly regarding the necessity of spin-dependent versus spin-independent dissipation.

Main result. The study establishes that spin-independent dissipation is crucial for locking transient spin asymmetries into a steady polarization, revealing a generic 'one enhanced, one suppressed' mechanism universal to helical chiral structures.

Method. The authors use the Lindblad master equation formalism applied to a donor-double-stranded DNA-acceptor model to calculate spin-resolved transport properties.

Model / system. The system is modeled as a Donor–double-stranded DNA–Acceptor (D–dsDNA–A) chain, governed by an extended Hamiltonian including helical Spin-Orbit Coupling (SOC).

Key observables. Spin-resolved mean-square displacement ($ ext{MSD}$), mobility polarization ($P_v = rac{D_\uparrow - D_\downarrow}{D_\uparrow + D_\downarrow}$), and the leading-time fraction ($f$).

Important parameters / regimes. Dissipation rates ($\Gamma, \Gamma_H$), helical SOC strength ($t_{SO}$), molecular length ($N$), and resonance energy windows ($\epsilon_D$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on modeling dissipation via Lindblad terms and distinguishing between dynamical mobility polarization ($p_v$) and steady-state spin accumulation.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the dependence of $P_v$ on dissipation strength ($\Gamma$), showing optimal regimes, and demonstrate how the leading-time fraction ($f$) transitions from zero to unity as dissipation increases.

Paper structure. The paper develops a diagnostic tool ($P_v$) using MSD analysis within the Lindblad framework. It first shows that pure electronic models fail to yield steady polarization; subsequent inclusion of spin-independent dissipation locks this asymmetry, leading to the general 'one enhanced, one suppressed' pattern.

Abstract

Despite extensive experiments, chiral-induced spin selectivity (CISS) remains debated. Using a donor-double-stranded DNA-acceptor model and Lindblad equation, we define spin mobility polarization via spin-resolved mean-square displacement and fix relaxation on a turnover plateau. Spin-independent relaxation locks the transient asymmetry from helical spin-orbit coupling (SOC) into a steady polarization. Weak SOC, expected to have little effect, boosts one spin but suppresses the other instead, in a "one enhanced, one suppressed" pattern. Flipping the helix exchanges them. The work defines mobility polarization as a probe and gives a generic dissipation-driven mechanism for CISS.

Summary. This paper explores controlling the Non-Hermitian Skin Effect (NHSE) in honeycomb lattices by introducing Pseudomagnetic Fields (PMFs). The authors demonstrate that PMFs, generated via strain or gain/loss modulation, effectively suppress the NHSE by confining bulk states rather than localizing them at boundaries. This establishes PMFs as a powerful and general tool for engineering wave dynamics in artificial metamaterials.

Why it may be interesting. This work is highly relevant to open quantum systems because it provides concrete theoretical models—using engineered non-Hermiticity (gain/loss) or geometric strain—to actively manipulate topological edge states, which is a key area in modern condensed matter theory and photonics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates methods to control the Non-Hermitian Skin Effect (NHSE) using engineered Pseudomagnetic Fields (PMFs). The goal is to show that PMFs provide a robust, symmetry-preserving mechanism for suppressing this localization.

Main result. In both strain-induced and gain/loss-modulated scenarios, PMFs efficiently suppress the NHSE by driving localized skin modes into the bulk spectrum. This control mechanism is shown to be versatile and insensitive to specific boundary terminations.

Method. The analysis uses tight-binding models on a honeycomb lattice, analyzing spectral properties (like topological area) under Periodic Boundary Conditions (PBC) versus Open Boundary Conditions (OBC). The suppression is diagnosed by observing the contraction of the complex energy spectrum.

Model / system. The system is modeled as a non-Hermitian honeycomb lattice Hamiltonian. PMFs are generated either through monotonically increasing strain or via spatially modulated gain and loss terms, which induce an effective pseudo-gauge field.

Key observables. Suppression of NHSE (skin modes moving into the bulk), reduction of the skin topological area, and contraction of the complex-energy spectrum under PBC.

Important parameters / regimes. Strain magnitude ($\alpha$), modulation parameter ($eta$), lattice hopping amplitudes ($t$, $\delta_x$, $\delta_y$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis often relies on treating the system in the long-wavelength limit, allowing for a locally translationally invariant description (momentum space).

Figures summary. Figures illustrate PMF realization via strain and gain/loss; they compare band structures under different conditions; and show spatial wave function intensity profiles demonstrating bulk localization induced by the PMF.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the NHSE problem, details two distinct mechanisms for generating time-reversal symmetric PMFs (strain and gain/loss), analyzes the spectral consequences of these fields on the honeycomb lattice Hamiltonian, and concludes by establishing PMFs as a general control tool for wave localization.

Abstract

Magnetic suppression of the non-Hermitian skin effect (NHSE) offers a viable route for its control. While the NHSE has been realized in various classical-wave platforms, only pseudomagnetic fields (PMFs), which preserve time-reversal symmetry, can be engineered in such systems; however, their interplay with the NHSE remains underexplored. Here, we investigate this interplay in a non-Hermitian honeycomb lattice by considering two distinct mechanisms for generating PMFs: monotonically increasing strain and spatially modulated gain and loss. We show that in both scenarios, PMFs can efficiently suppress the NHSE by driving skin modes into the bulk, accompanied by a reduction of the skin topological area and a contraction of the complex-energy spectrum under periodic boundary conditions. This mechanism is insensitive to boundary details and holds for various edge terminations, including zigzag, bearded, armchair, and twig edges. Our results establish PMFs as a versatile and effective means to control the NHSE, and point toward feasible implementations in a broad range of artificial platforms, including photonic and acoustic metamaterials as well as topolectrical circuits.
