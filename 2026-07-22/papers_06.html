Summary. This work provides a rigorous derivation of Richards' equation from a mesoscopic kinetic theory describing water flow in soil. It uses the Chapman-Enskog expansion to bridge the gap between microscopic pore dynamics and macroscopic continuum laws. Furthermore, it applies spectral analysis to derive advanced permeability models for structured media directly from connectivity properties.

Why it may be interesting. Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper derives Richards' equation for unsaturated soil water flow by rigorously separating two limits—spatial and temporal—that are often conflated in standard macroscopic theories.

Main result. It successfully derives the continuum balance (Richards' equation) from a mesoscopic kinetic description by applying a Chapman-Enskog reduction, providing first-principles derivations for permeability models like dual-permeability.

Method. The core methodology involves performing a Chapman-Enskog expansion on a Continuum Kinetic Equation (CKE), which itself is obtained via a gradient expansion (spatial limit) of the pore-filling distribution dynamics.

Model / system. The system models unsaturated soil water flow, governed by the evolution of the pore-filling distribution $g(r, x, t)$ within Representative Elementary Volumes (REVs). The analysis extends to bimodal distributions, allowing for spectral decomposition and domain reduction techniques.

Key observables. Hydraulic Conductivity ($K$), Water Content ($ heta$), Retention Curve, Inter-domain Exchange Coefficient ($\Gamma_w$).

Important parameters / regimes. Damköhler Number (Da), which controls the temporal separation; pore size distribution characteristics (e.g., bimodal gap).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumptions are that the system can be reduced via a small Damköhler number expansion ($Da \ll 1$) and, in advanced sections, that spectral gaps exist to justify domain decomposition.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the derivation path from CKE $ o$ Richards' equation; spectral analysis shows eigenvalue gaps ($\lambda_1$) confirming the validity of two-domain reductions by showing rate ratios approaching unity as separation increases.

Paper structure. The paper follows a multi-stage reduction: first, establishing the CKE via spatial limit; second, applying CE expansion in time to yield Richards' equation and identifying $K$; finally, using spectral theory on bimodal systems to derive inter-domain exchange coefficients from first principles.

Abstract

Richards' equation for unsaturated water flow is derived from the kinetic theory of the pore-filling distribution g(r,x,t) of a companion paper. It separates two limits that macroscopic theory usually conflates. The spatial limit is purely kinematic: contracting the representative elementary volume (REV) to a point yields the closed continuum kinetic equation (CKE) d_t g + div F = C[g], with F a pore-resolved pre-closure flux and C[g] the occupancy-gated redistribution operator. The dynamics lies in the temporal limit, the subject of this paper: a Chapman-Enskog (CE) reduction of the CKE controlled by the Damkohler number Da (redistribution time over forcing time), the structural analogue of the passage from Boltzmann to Navier-Stokes. The linearized redistribution operator J is self-adjoint and negative semidefinite in the mass inner product, with a one-dimensional kernel fixing a single invariant (water) and hence one macroscopic equation; the CE hierarchy inverts the same J at every order, only the source changing. Four results follow: the equilibrium step defines the retention curve; the linearized water budget plus the inter-REV source set the first-order equation; its solvability is mass conservation, i.e. Richards' equation; and the response function gives the macroscopic flux, identifying the conductivity K as a first-order transport coefficient, the counterpart of viscosity. Mean-field reduction recovers the standard integral formula; the serial-path correction gives Mualem's heterogeneity penalty. A bimodal pore-size distribution opens a gap in the relaxation spectrum; projecting onto the bands and applying CE within each, with cross-band relaxation surviving at O(1), derives the dual- and multiple-permeability models from first principles, the exchange coefficient set by inter-band connectivity. When Da is not small the expansion breaks down and the full CKE is needed.

Summary. This paper rigorously analyzes the mathematical properties—specifically the Hausdorff dimension and survival tail—of computational histories that run forever despite being almost surely terminating. It achieves this by developing powerful bounds on source-power sums using matrix theory, demonstrating how subtle differences in local rules can lead to vastly different global nontermination behaviors.

Why it may be interesting. While highly abstract, the rigorous mathematical framework developed for bounding complexity and dimension in infinite random processes has parallels with analyzing long-time behavior or rare events in non-equilibrium statistical mechanics systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper studies the survival tail, Kolmogorov complexity, and Hausdorff dimension of exceptional random tapes for randomized algorithms that are guaranteed to terminate almost surely.

Main result. It establishes bounds on source-power sums ($Z_{\Lambda}^T(s)$) using matrix commutation properties, showing that nontermination dimensions can differ drastically even when ordinary kernels match.

Method. The analysis employs advanced tools from information theory and probability, including Rényi entropy sums, powered action matrices, and dimension calculations derived from source-power bounds.

Model / system. The model is a randomized computation process defined by a source law $P$ on the tape $\omega$, focusing on the set of tapes where the repair process never stops ($ au(\omega) = \infty$).

Key observables. Survival tail ($P\{ au \geq T\}$), Source-power sum ($Z_{\Lambda}^T(s)$), Effective dimension ($ ext{Dimeff},_P(\omega)$), and Hausdorff dimension.

Important parameters / regimes. The exponent $s$ (where $s=1$ controls termination) and structural parameters related to the underlying computation graph or source law are critical.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis requires specific structural conditions, such as powered action matrices commuting for certain values of $s$, and relies on definitions involving 'source-power domination'.

Figures summary. Not specified in the notes; the focus is on mathematical theorems and derived bounds.

Paper structure. The paper develops results by first defining the survival tail and source-power sums, then using matrix commutation properties (Theorem 2.3) to bound these sums for various $s$. It applies these general tools to specific problems like k-SAT and provides exact likelihood ratio identities for finite runs.

Abstract

A randomized algorithm may terminate almost surely even though exceptional random tapes make it run forever. This paper studies the survival tail, the Kolmogorov complexity of one such tape, and the Hausdorff dimension of all of them. For each $s>0$ at which the powered repair matrices commute, the main theorem bounds $\sum_wP[w]^s$ over surviving prefixes $w$, uniformly over deterministic nonanticipating selectors. The case $s=1$ controls termination; the full family gives weak-source and dimension bounds. The source powers contain information absent even from the ordinary repair kernel and the complete stopping-time law. Under one common finite tape source, two overlapping disagreement-repair rules on a four-vertex path have the same ordinary kernels and the same stopping-time law for every selector, yet their nontermination dimensions can be arbitrarily close to zero and one. At one common source-power level, the same dominated tape source makes one rule run forever but gives the other an exponential stopping tail. The separation is caused by action labels that produce the same state transition and are therefore invisible at power one. For bounded-dependence $k$-SAT, conditional block min-entropy above the trace-growth threshold gives exponential termination, and the effective dimension of an individual infinite run is bounded by the trace growth induced by the clauses repaired infinitely often. Tree formulas asymptotically attain the maximum-degree dimension and global source bounds, while clique formulas attain the graph-specific one-step threshold in the stated regime. An exact backward likelihood identity complements these setwise results with tail and coding bounds for each run.

Summary. The paper develops a theoretical framework to detect the Unruh effect by measuring acceleration-dependent effects on an atom. It optimizes measurement parameters—including probe time and initial state—to minimize the uncertainty in estimating the acceleration factor. Why it may be interesting. This work provides a detailed roadmap for experimental verification of fundamental QFT predictions (Unruh effect) using controllable quantum systems like trapped atoms, combining advanced concepts from open quantum systems and metrology.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the quantum metrological detection of the Unruh effect, which involves verifying acceleration-dependent modifications predicted by quantum field theory for uniformly accelerated observers.

Main result. The minimal uncertainty in estimating the acceleration factor can be significantly reduced by optimizing the individual probe interaction time and utilizing atoms with larger transition dipole moments. The optimal initial state depends critically on whether the acceleration is small or large.

Method. Quantum metrology principles are applied to bound the estimation uncertainty using the Fisher information, requiring optimization over the probe evolution time, initial atomic state, and measurement basis.

Model / system. The system involves a two-level probe atom coupled to the vacuum electromagnetic field while undergoing uniform acceleration. The dynamics are modeled using an effective Hamiltonian derived from the multipolar coupling scheme.

Key observables. Minimal estimation uncertainty ($ ext{Var}(X)$) of the acceleration factor ($X = a/(c\omega_0)$), and the Fisher information ($F_X$).

Important parameters / regimes. Acceleration factor $X$, individual probe evolution time $ au$, total probe time $T$, initial atomic state parameters ($\cos heta, \sin heta$), and transition dipole moments.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the electromagnetic field is initially in a vacuum state (inertial observer frame) and utilizes weak coupling approximations for the density matrix evolution.

Paper structure. Abstract

For a uniformly accelerated two-level probe atom, the acceleration-dependent factor is encoded in the atomic state during the atom-field evolution. If this factor can be estimated through a large number of measurements with an uncertainty smaller than its magnitude, the Unruh effect can be experimentally verified. In this work, we show that the minimal uncertainty is inversely proportional to the square root of the total probe time. For a fixed total probe time, we minimize the estimation uncertainty by optimizing the atom-field interaction time, the initial atomic state, and the measurement basis in each probe. Results show that the uncertainty decreases as the evolution time of each individual probe becomes shorter. The optimal measurement basis is determined by the initial atomic state, while the optimal initial state depends on both the acceleration factor and the evolution time of each probe. For a fixed evolution time, we analyze the estimation uncertainty versus the acceleration factor and identify the optimal factor and minimal uncertainty for each initial state. The initial excited state outperforms other configurations in the small-acceleration regime, whereas the ground state can become advantageous for sufficiently large acceleration and long evolution time of each probe. Finally, we estimate the minimal total probe time required for testing the Unruh effect and demonstrate that it can be significantly reduced by using probe atoms with larger transition dipole moments.

Main problem. The study investigates the statistical mechanics of a three-body Hopfield model defined on sparse random graphs with finite mean connectivity.

Main result. Three-body interactions induce a discontinuous retrieval transition, leading to a spin-glass-like trapping mechanism at low temperatures that is distinct from conventional two-body models.

Method. The analysis employs the replica-symmetric (RS) functional framework combined with Population Dynamics (PD) for self-consistent equation solving, complemented by finite-size Monte Carlo simulations.

Model / system. The model is a three-body Hopfield network described by a Hamiltonian involving three-spin couplings ($J_{ijk}$) on sparse random graphs. The system's behavior is characterized by the interplay between many-body interactions and graph disorder.

Key observables. Local-field distribution $W_\xi(h)$, retrieval order parameter, spin-glass overlap $q$, and characteristic storage load $\alpha$.

Important parameters / regimes. Mean connectivity ($c=O(1)$), number of patterns ($P$), normalized load ($\alpha = P/c$), and temperature/inverse temperature ($eta$).

Abstract

A three-body Hopfield model defined on sparse random graphs with finite mean connectivity is studied using a replica-symmetric (RS) analysis. By extending the functional replica framework for conventional two-body finite-connectivity Hopfield models, self-consistent equations for the local-field distribution are derived and solved numerically by population dynamics. In contrast to two-body sparse Hopfield models, three-body interactions induce a discontinuous retrieval transition, coexistence of paramagnetic and retrieval solutions, and a distinct spinodal structure. Starting the RS population dynamics from an uninformative finite-amplitude field distribution leads to a spin-glass-like fixed point at low temperatures rather than to the retrieval state. This trapping already occurs for a single embedded pattern, where conventional cross-talk noise among stored patterns is absent, indicating that it originates from the combination of sparse connectivity and three-body interactions. Within the RS description, increasing the number of embedded patterns drives a crossing between the retrieval and glassy free-energy branches, making the glassy branch thermodynamically stable at high load. Finite-size Monte Carlo simulations using population annealing support the RS description of the retrieval branch and reproduce the trapping behavior under cooling, while deviations from the RS spin-glass branch point to replica-symmetry breaking in the low-temperature glassy regime. The characteristic storage load scales linearly with the mean connectivity, reflecting the sparse number of couplings. These results clarify how many-body discontinuity and sparse graph disorder simultaneously influence the accessibility and capacity of associative memory.

Summary. This paper develops a statistical approach to model adverse selection, treating market quality deterioration as a dynamic truncation of the underlying quality distribution. It determines critical conditions based on buyer payment rates and initial quality variance required to prevent the market from collapsing entirely. The findings offer insights into how interventions can stabilize imperfect markets.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper analyzes adverse selection in markets driven by asymmetric information, which causes market deterioration by eliminating high-quality goods.

Main result. It establishes conditions (related to the payment rate $\xi$ and minimum quality floor $q_0$) necessary to prevent complete market collapse, showing that variance and price premium jointly determine the speed of deterioration.

Method. The analysis models the process as a dynamic truncation of the underlying quality distribution, using recursion relations derived from conditional probability distributions.

Model / system. The system is an economic model where buyers set an upper bound ($b_t = \xi ar{q}_t$) on payment, causing sellers with quality $q > b_t$ to exit. The market state evolves by retaining the conditional distribution of quality.

Key observables. Mean market quality ($ar{q}_t$), retained-market variance ($\sigma^2_{t+1}$), and the price premium ($a_t$).

Important parameters / regimes. The payment rate $\xi$, the underlying quality variance parameter ($s^2$), and the minimum quality floor ($q_0$).

Abstract

The adverse-selection mechanism in markets explains how asymmetric information between buyers and sellers can drive high-quality goods out of the market, thereby causing market deterioration. In its simplest formulation, only the mean quality is used to describe the market, and this is insufficient to determine how fast the market deteriorates or how the quality distribution evolves. To resolve the two problems, we describe the adverse selection as a dynamic truncation of the quality distribution: buyers set an upper bound proportional to the mean quality by a rate $ξ$ that is larger than unity, and sellers whose quality exceeds this upper bound reject an offer and exit the market. The retained market is then characterized by the conditional distribution obtained after this truncation, and the corresponding evolution process is iterated until market quality reaches a stable state. This statistical approach gives three results. (i) We identify a mechanism for preventing complete adverse selection, defined as the process where the quality of the market is driven down to the minimum quality floor. (ii) A larger quality variance or a smaller price premium, defined as the amount by which the payment upper bound exceeds the current mean quality, raises the upper bound on the deterioration in mean quality. (iii) A maximum-entropy benchmark shows numerically how quality variance and the payment rate jointly determine market deterioration and the final stable quality platform. This approach also clarifies how market interventions can slow adverse selection: they may raise buyers' payment rate, reduce quality variance, or increase the minimum quality floor.

Summary. The paper presents a novel multilevel quantum algorithm to estimate observables derived from parabolic Partial Differential Equations. Method. The core technique involves using a multilevel estimator based on encoding the fine–coarse cancellation within a shifted Ritz–Schur factorization to avoid direct block encoding of high-resolution operators.

Model / system. The model is the heat equation ($\partial_t u + L u = 0$) solved via finite element discretization, leading to a semi-discrete linear ODE system $\dot{\mathbf{y}}_h = -L_h \mathbf{y}_h$. The analysis covers both direct semigroup estimation and multilevel correction.

Key observables. Linear and quadratic observables of the solution operator, such as regional average heat content or local mass flux ($\chi=0, 1, 2$).

Abstract

Can a quantum PDE algorithm avoid the polynomial cost of resolving a fine spatial mesh? For standard fixed-order discretizations, direct classical methods require work polynomial in $h^{-1}$, or equivalently in the number of spatial degrees of freedom $N_h=Θ(h^{-d})$. Direct quantum implementations of a parabolic semigroup still have coherent complexity $\widetilde{\mathcal O}(\sqrt{T}/h)$, and gradient-dependent observables such as heat flux and dissipation introduce additional mesh dependence. Decay of the solution norm will further suppress the postselection probability for preparing a normalized final state. We develop a multilevel quantum algorithm that estimates linear and quadratic observables $directly$ and places the fine--coarse cancellation inside the circuit before measurement. A contour-based LCU reconstructs each target-time correction from a coherent family of shifted resolvent differences. Rather than block encoding the fine and coarse inverses separately, we encode their difference through a shifted Ritz--Schur factorization, exposing its $\mathcal O(h_\ell^2)$ two-grid normalization. For Fourier hierarchies, the corresponding SELECT oracle consists of a quantum Fourier or sine transform, a spectral-band selector, and reversible diagonal arithmetic. We also give a non-Fourier realization based on energy-orthogonal dyadic midpoint details in one dimension, together with structured tensor-product extensions under fixed-rank coefficient and access assumptions. For readouts with derivative order $0\leχ\le2$, optimized amplitude estimation removes $all$ polynomial dependence on the finest mesh size. Under the stated access assumptions, both linear and quadratic observables can be estimated with complexity $\widetilde{\mathcal O}(1+(Tε)^{-1})$, with only polylogarithmic dependence on $h^{-1}$.

Summary. This work develops a symmetry-based microscopic theory to explain superconductivity in nickelates. It finds that La5Ni3O11 exhibits a two-gap superconducting state, attributing the lower $T_c$ compared to related materials to changes in orbital hopping ratios. The methodology combines advanced electronic structure calculations with rigorous group-theoretical analysis of pairing symmetries.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on solid-state physics, the use of symmetry constraints and mean-field approaches to determine pairing instabilities is relevant for understanding emergent order parameters in complex quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To develop a unified microscopic theory explaining the difference in superconducting pairing mechanisms between nickelate superconductors like La5Ni3O11 and pressurized La3Ni2O7.

Main result. The superconductivity in La5Ni3O11 is characterized by a two-gap nature, involving leading interlayer pairing ($d_{z^2}$) and subleading intralayer pairing ($d_{x^2-y^2}$), with the reduction of Tc attributed to the hopping parameter ratio $|t_{\perp}^z/t_{\parallel}^{x}|$.

Method. The study employs a symmetry-based approach combined with advanced many-body techniques, including DFT+DMFT and analysis via the Bogoliubov-de Gennes Hamiltonian.

Model / system. The system is based on nickelate superconductors (e.g., La5Ni3O11), modeled using an effective quasiparticle Hamiltonian derived from strong correlation physics involving Ni-$e_g$ orbitals.

Key observables. Superconducting transition temperature ($T_c$), superconducting gap structure ($\Delta_{sz^2}$ vs $\Delta_{sx^2+y^2}$), and the hopping parameter ratio $|t_{\perp}^z/t_{\parallel}^{x}|$.

Important parameters / regimes. $T_c \approx 64$ K for La5Ni3O11; $T_c = 80$ K for pressurized La3Ni2O7.

Assumptions / limitations. The low-energy physics is dominated by Ni-$e_g$ orbitals, and the pairing mechanism can be analyzed using symmetry constraints on the gap function.

Paper structure. The paper systematically develops a theoretical framework starting from DFT+DMFT calculations to analyze the low-energy physics, determining the superconducting state via minimizing the free energy functional derived from the BdG Hamiltonian.

Abstract

Recent experiments report high-temperature superconductivity in the hybrid nickelate $\mathrm{La}_5\mathrm{Ni}_3\mathrm{O}_{11}$, which is composed of alternating stacks of bilayer $\mathrm{La}_3\mathrm{Ni}_2\mathrm{O}_7$ and monolayer $\mathrm{La}_2\mathrm{NiO}_4$. However, the superconducting transition temperature $T_c \approx 64~\mathrm{K}$ for $\mathrm{La}_5\mathrm{Ni}_3\mathrm{O}_{11}$ is remarkably lower than the $80~\mathrm{K}$ observed for pressurized $\mathrm{La}_3\mathrm{Ni}_2\mathrm{O}_7$. Thus, an unified microscopic theory is required to address the difference in the pairing mechanisms between these systems. Here, we develop a phenomenological symmetry-based approach to systematically analyze the low-energy physics in $\mathrm{La}_5\mathrm{Ni}_3\mathrm{O}_{11}$, which is obtained by a charge self-consistent density functional theory plus dynamical mean-field theory method. We show that the superconductivity in $\mathrm{La}_5\mathrm{Ni}_3\mathrm{O}_{11}$ exhibits a two-gap nature, consisting of a leading interlayer pairing between the $d_{z^2}$ orbitals and a subleading intralayer pairing between the $d_{x^2-y^2}$ orbitals. The reduction of $T_c$ can be attributed to the diminished contribution of the interlayer pairing, as reflected by the hopping parameter ratio $|t_{\perp}^z/t_{\parallel}^{x}|$. Base on this unified picture, we discuss the possible pairing mechanism and the role of $γ$ pocket for the superconductivity in the bilayer NiO$_2$ planes of nickelate superconductors.

Abstract

Materials with a kagome lattice host exotic quantum phenomena driven by the interplay between band topology, spin-orbit coupling, magnetism, and electronic correlations. While magnetism of kagome materials has been widely investigated, their unconventional superconductivity (SC) remains largely unexplored due to the limited availability of suitable materials. Here, we report evidence of unconventional SC in the ScIr$_{2-x}$Si$_{x}$ family by combining muon-spin spectroscopy measurements with band-structure calculations. The parent ScIr$_2$ undergoes a structural phase transition from a high-$T$ cubic- to a low-$T$ rhombohedral phase, while the Ir kagome layer remains, albeit slightly distorted. Although the structural transition is suppressed by Si substitution, the superconducting pairing of ScIr$_{2-x}$Si$_{x}$ remains well described by a two-gap model. Since at least one of the gaps has nodes, this indicates an unconventional SC. Its unconventional nature can be explained by the distinct flat bands occurring near the Fermi level, leading to strong electronic correlations in the ScIr$_{2-x}$Si$_{x}$ family. Moreover, the low-$T$ phase of ScIr$_2$ exhibits an Ir chiral chain; therefore, it can be classified as a topological chiral crystal. Overall, the unusual properties of the ScIr$_{2-x}$Si$_{x}$ family make it an interesting, albeit rare, system for studying the interplay between unconventional SC, flat bands, and chirality.

Abstract

We introduce approximate expressions for the thermodynamics of the two-dimensional Ising model in an external magnetic field. The external field breaks the system's symmetry, which complicates the exact calculation of the free energy. To address this, we write the model in its quaternion representation to apply a small angle approximation with respect to a reference frame. The approximation simplifies the transfer matrix to a centrosymmetric matrix for which the largest eigenvalue is known and depends on the external field strength. We therefore write an approximate expression for the system's free energy and derive the magnetization, susceptibility, internal energy, and specific heat. These results are compared with numerical simulations performed with the BKL algorithm. We find that the approximate expressions and numerical simulations agree in regions where the approximation is valid.
