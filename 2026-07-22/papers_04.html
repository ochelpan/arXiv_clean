Summary. This paper presents a comprehensive theoretical framework for optimizing quantum measurement reduction by estimating observable expectation values. It tackles the problem using a combination of Second-Order Cone Programming (SOCP) for continuous allocation and advanced global optimization techniques for dictionary design. The key contribution is providing mathematically certified lower bounds on the required shot count, making resource estimation rigorous and verifiable.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides rigorous, mathematically verifiable tools to determine the minimum required experimental resources (shots) needed to estimate quantum observables, moving beyond heuristic optimization methods common in quantum simulation and characterization.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core problem is optimizing quantum measurement reduction, which requires solving two coupled global optimization layers: an inner continuous shot allocation problem and an outer nonconvex dictionary design problem.

Main result. The authors develop a framework that solves the inner layer certifiably using Second-Order Cone Programming (SOCP) and uses a robust optimizer (RANGE) for the outer layer, achieving significant compression of measurement dictionaries while maintaining certified optimality bounds.

Method. The methodology combines convex optimization (SOCP) for shot allocation with adaptive global optimization (RANGE) for dictionary design. Crucially, it establishes verifiable lower-bound certificates via the conic dual and incorporates robust analysis using covariance sandwiching for finite-sample guarantees.

Model / system. The theory applies to estimating expectation values $\langle H angle$ of observables $H$ in quantum systems. The measurement process is modeled by defining a dictionary of available contexts, each associated with a visible subspace and an estimated covariance model.

Key observables. Minimum leading shot cost ($\Phi$), expected value $\langle H angle$, certified lower/upper bounds ($L, U$), and the required number of shots ($N_\ell$).

Important parameters / regimes. Shot costs ($c_\ell$), measurement context definitions ($ ext{V}\ell$), covariance matrices ($\Gamma\ell$), and confidence parameters ($\delta$ for finite-sample bounds).

Assumptions / limitations. The framework assumes the existence of a measurable dictionary of contexts. Robustness theorems address uncertainty in estimating the covariance matrices from pilot data.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates measurement reduction as a combination of geometric constraints (visible subspaces) and optimization, with the dual providing a certificate bounding all possible schedules.

Paper structure. The paper builds by first defining the two-layer optimization problem. It then solves the inner layer via SOCP, establishing the primal/dual relationship. Subsequent sections introduce robust analysis for finite samples using covariance bounds (Theorem 3 & 4), culminating in a certified repair procedure to ensure mathematical validity.

Abstract

Quantum-measurement reduction contains two distinct global-optimization layers: a continuous problem of splitting an observable and allocating shots within a fixed measurement dictionary, and a nonconvex outer problem of designing the dictionary and calibrating its data-driven uncertainty model. We solve the inner layer globally and certifiably as a second-order cone program (SOCP), and use RANGE, a robust adaptive nature-inspired global optimizer, for the combinatorial and statistical outer layer. For any declared set of contexts, per-shot costs, score functions, and covariance model, the SOCP returns the minimum leading shot cost among unbiased linear stratified estimators. The conic dual supplies an independently checkable lower-bound witness; after feasibility repair, an external verifier recomputes $L \le Φ\le U$ from stored data without trusting the optimizer. Pilot measurements yield simultaneous finite-sample covariance brackets, and the dual becomes a pricing oracle for omitted contexts. Discrete RANGE searches covering sub-dictionaries, Pareto compression fronts, and candidate contexts; continuous RANGE performs an explicitly empirical, coverage-constrained calibration of covariance-radius models, while rigorous certificates retain the proved finite-sample radius. RANGE compresses molecular context dictionaries by 4.3-6.1x at 0.2-2.1% certified-frontier excess. Standard strategies are exactly optimal for H2 yet leave factors of 2.1-7.7 in shots within their own settings by H2O. Adding fully commuting contexts lowers the certified optimum by up to 56%; on 29-35-qubit production f-element Hamiltonians under a declared Hartree-Fock-proxy covariance model, the capped-dictionary enlargement saves 31-70% of the shots, and transformations reducing block-encoding cost need not reduce sampling cost.

Summary. This paper rigorously tests the operational robustness of Sample-based Quantum Diagonalization (SQD) for calculating molecular energies using real quantum hardware. By systematically varying initial conditions, qubit layouts, and sample sizes, the authors show that the self-consistent recovery loop effectively mitigates many expected sources of noise and error. Why it may be interesting. This work is highly relevant as it directly tests the viability of quantum algorithms for chemistry on near-term, noisy hardware by quantifying how much classical post-processing can salvage results from imperfect quantum measurements.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study assesses the hardware robustness of Sample-based Quantum Diagonalization (SQD), a hybrid quantum-classical method used for estimating molecular ground-state energies.

Main result. SQD demonstrates genuine deployment robustness, as errors from classical input perturbations and variations in qubit layout diminish significantly after only a few self-consistent recovery iterations. Furthermore, accuracy saturates at moderate shot budgets rather than improving indefinitely with more samples.

Method. The authors analyze SQD by subjecting it to three probes: perturbing initial CCSD amplitudes (Q1), varying hardware choices like qubit layouts and noise mitigation (Q2), and scaling the QPU shot budget (Q3).

Model / system. The calculations model electronic structure for small molecules ($ ext{BeH}_2$, $ ext{H}_2 ext{O}$, $ ext{N}_2$) using the Local Unitary Cluster Jastrow (LUCJ) ansatz, initialized by CCSD amplitudes. The work is performed on IBM Heron hardware.

Key observables. The primary observable is the final recovered energy error ($\Delta E = | ext{E}_{ ext{SQD}} - ext{ECASCI}|$) after a fixed number of recovery iterations ($T=5$).

Important parameters / regimes. Key parameters include the CCSD amplitude perturbations (e.g., zeroing $t_2$), qubit layout types (naive, randomized, zigzag), noise mitigation status, and the QPU shot budget ($ ext{Nshots}$).

Paper structure. Abstract

Sample-based Quantum Diagonalization (SQD) is a hybrid quantum-classical method that replaces variational optimization with a self-consistent recovery loop over QPU samples. Although SQD is considered robust to noisy samples and imperfect classical inputs, its robustness across practical deployment choices has not been systematically analyzed. As a result, shot budgets, qubit layouts, noise mitigation strategies, and the coupled-cluster singles and doubles (CCSD) amplitudes that initialize the ansatz are often chosen without clear empirical guidance. We analyze SQD robustness on IBM Heron hardware across these dimensions. Structured CCSD-amplitude perturbations, including complete zeroing, produce only modest energy shifts from the clean baseline. Differences across layouts and noise-mitigation settings are large in the first recovery iteration but narrow within a few iterations. Accuracy saturates at moderate shot budgets, while very large budgets slightly worsen recovered energies, likely because working-set selection limits the value of additional samples. These results identify where SQD provides genuine deployment robustness and where its limits remain.

Abstract

Quantum machine learning on real noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) hardware has remained largely confined to binary or few-class tasks, limited by the cost of on-hardware training and the underuse of large devices at inference. We present a unified framework that classifies ten-class MNIST end-to-end on a $127$-qubit IBM Eagle processor, with three central contributions. First, a two-phase protocol decouples a gradient-based classical optimization of the encoder and readout from a gradient-free optimization of the quantum parameters, removing the parameter-shift gradient cost that makes on-hardware training impractical. Second, we introduce Quantum Multi-Programming to a trained quantum classifier for the first time, packing multiple circuit copies onto one device to deliver parallel inference at no mean-accuracy cost while cutting quantum-processor job submissions proportionally. Third, a controlled comparison shows that on-hardware fine-tuning yields no measurable accuracy gain, motivating a practical NISQ workflow: train on a classical simulator and reserve the hardware for inference only. Benchmarked against a matched-capacity classical network, the quantum module shows no per-parameter accuracy advantage at this scale; we therefore frame the work as a feasibility-and-workflow demonstration for multi-class quantum image classification on current hardware.

Summary. This paper investigates the relationship between local unitary equivalence and Bargmann invariants for bipartite quantum states. While the invariants fully characterize two-qubit states ($n=2$), the authors prove that this fails for larger systems ($n \ge 3$) by constructing counterexamples. The result highlights that while global unitaries can relate states, local unitaries impose stricter constraints not captured solely by these specific invariant measures.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides fundamental theoretical boundaries on how much information (invariants) is needed to classify quantum states under local operations, a core concept in characterizing entanglement and resource theories relevant to quantum computation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The central problem is determining when the local unitary equivalence of a bipartite quantum state ($ ho_{AB}$) is completely characterized by its local unitary Bargmann invariants, especially for systems larger than two qubits ($n \ge 3$).

Main result. The authors disprove the conjecture that local unitary orbits are fully determined by these invariants for $n \ge 3$, providing explicit counterexamples. However, they show that if the state is pure, equivalence is guaranteed.

Method. The proof involves constructing specific counterexamples using rank-one projections to define pairs of states ($ ho_{AB}, \sigma_{AB}$) that are related by a global unitary transformation but fail to be locally unitarily equivalent.

Model / system. The study focuses on bipartite quantum states $ ho_{AB}$ defined on the Hilbert space $\mathbb{C}^n \otimes \mathbb{C}^n$ (two-qudit systems). The analysis revolves around the action of the local unitary group ($U \otimes V$).

Key observables. Local unitary equivalence, and the values derived from the local unitary Bargmann invariants ($ ext{Tr}( ho_{j_1}\cdots ho_{j_m})$).

Important parameters / regimes. $n$ (the dimension of the subsystems), with special attention paid to the transition between $n=2$ and $n \ge 3$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on comparing states related by a global unitary transformation ($W$) versus those related only by local unitaries.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the known result for $n=2$ (complete determination by invariants). It then proceeds to construct counterexamples in Theorem 2.6 for $n \ge 3$, demonstrating the failure of the conjecture, before concluding with Proposition 2.9 which restores equivalence for pure states.

Abstract

In this paper, we study the local unitary equivalence of quantum states on $\mathbb{C}^n\otimes\mathbb{C}^n$, which is an important notion in quantum information theory. For the case $n=2$, the local unitary orbits of two-qubit states are completely determined by their local unitary Bargmann invariants. We show that the local unitary Bargmann invariants do not form a complete set of invariants of local unitary orbits for $n\geqslant 3$, which negatively answers a problem proposed by L. Zhang and B. Xie.

Summary. The paper introduces an entanglement measure hierarchy, $E_k$, derived from extending the Positive Partial Transpose (PPT) condition using $k$-symmetric extensions. This framework provides a quantifiable resource yardstick that strictly refines existing measures and successfully detects bound entangled states where simpler methods fail. By leveraging SDP duality, it offers both theoretical rigor and practical computational scalability.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful theoretical tool for quantifying entanglement resources in a systematically improvable manner, directly addressing fundamental open problems regarding bound entanglement that are crucial for understanding quantum correlations beyond simple separability criteria.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is to develop a systematically improvable resource-theoretic measure for quantifying quantum entanglement that overcomes the limitations of existing measures, such as PPT-robustness, especially concerning bound entangled states.

Main result. The authors introduce a hierarchy of measures, $E_k$, based on $k$-symmetric PPT extensions. This hierarchy is proven to be faithful and strictly refines previous bounds, converging exactly to the true separability measure as $k o \infty$.

Method. The measures are defined using Semidefinite Programming (SDP) by characterizing the set of 'free states' ($F_k$) via $k$-symmetric PPT extensions. A dual formulation utilizing witness operators provides a computationally efficient alternative to solving the full primal SDP.

Model / system. The framework applies to bipartite quantum systems ($ ho_{AB}$) and involves extending the state space using $k$ copies of one subsystem ($H_A \otimes H_{B_1} \otimes \cdots \otimes H_{B_k}$). The analysis is tested on standard states like Werner, Horodecki, and UPB states.

Key observables. The entanglement measure $E_k( ho)$, the set of free states $F_k$, and the limit measure $E_{SEP}( ho)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The integer parameter $k$ controls the level of extension, ranging from $k=1$ (PPT-robustness) up to $\infty$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the equivalence between primal and dual SDP formulations for computational tractability. The framework assumes LOCC operations are captured by the set of $k$-free operations $O_k$.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the strict monotonicity ($E_1 < E_2 < E_3$) of the measure hierarchy for Werner states and demonstrate that bound entangled states yield non-zero values for $E_k$ when $k \ge 2$, while $E_1=0$.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the concept via $k$-symmetric PPT extensions, defines the measure $E_k$ using both primal and dual SDP formulations, proves its desirable properties (convexity, monotonicity), establishes the convergence to the separability measure, and finally validates the framework computationally on various entangled state examples.

Abstract

We introduce a hierarchy of entanglement measures Ek based on k-symmetric PPT extensions. Each Ek, defined via a minimal eigenvalue shift and computed by semidefinite programming, is faithful, convex, and monotone under free operations. The hierarchy strictly refines PPT-robustness at k = 1, detects bound entanglement at k = 2, and converges exactly to the separability measure as k -> infinity. Numerical experiments on Horodecki, Werner, UPB, and random states demonstrate practical scalability. Our framework unifies computational efficiency with operational fidelity in a single tunable family -- a combination previously believed to be fundamentally incompatible in entanglement quantification. It supplies, for the first time, a systematically improvable resource-theoretic yardstick that accounts for all entangled states, including the bound entangled ones that have long resisted quantitative treatment.

Summary. The paper experimentally benchmarks composite quantum gates using chiral-interference circuits to test error resilience. Abstract

We construct and experimentally implement compact gate-native circuits that simulate the state-transfer interference underlying three- and four-level chiral-resolution protocols. Both models are encoded in a two-qubit register, with the enantiomer-dependent sign of one of the couplings simulated by a conditional-phase operation in the four-level circuit and by the sign of a final rotation in the three-level circuit. On an IBM quantum processor, the two circuits produce the expected enantiomer-dependent output states with probabilities of nearly $98\%$. We then use these circuits as physically motivated, phase-sensitive benchmarks for composite quantum gates. We introduce rotation-angle error to the single-qubit operations and replace them by several composite gates, including B5, SK1, BB1, H5s, and X5. The comparison demonstrates that single-gate robustness does not translate to equivalent whole-circuit robustness. In particular, variable-rotation sequences do not preserve the required relative phases, making them unsuitable for error correction in circuits. By contrast, the H5s/X5 sequences maintain high target-state populations for relative errors as large as $50\%$, whereas elementary rotations reach the same threshold only for approximately $8\%$. The three-level circuit exhibits a similar enhancement and additionally reveals an error-cancellation symmetry whose protection under composite replacement is exact only when the relevant full propagators satisfy an inverse relation.

Abstract
