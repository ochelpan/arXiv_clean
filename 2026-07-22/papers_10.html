Summary. This paper develops and validates a theoretical model describing quasiparticle heating losses in superconducting microwave resonators. By analyzing the resulting nonlinear changes in resonance response, the authors identify a unique 'oval-shaped distortion' in the complex plane as a definitive experimental signature for diagnosing when QPH dominates energy dissipation.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete, measurable spectroscopic tool (the oval distortion) to diagnose fundamental non-equilibrium energy loss mechanisms (QPH) in superconducting circuits, which is crucial for designing high-performance quantum devices.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the nonlinear behavior of superconducting microwave resonators caused by quasiparticle heating (QPH) effects.

Main result. A distinctive oval-shaped distortion in the resonance circle plotted in the complex plane serves as a practical and reliable experimental signature for identifying the QPH-dominated loss regime.

Method. The authors combine theoretical modeling, modifying Rothwarf-Taylor equations to create a macroscopic QPH model, with systematic experimental measurements using VNA sweeps of readout power and bath temperature.

Model / system. The system is a Niobium (Nb) superconducting half-wavelength coplanar waveguide (CPW) resonator. The theory models the quasiparticle density evolution by modifying Rothwarf-Taylor equations to link the quality factor ($Q$) directly to the microwave readout power ($P_r$).

Key observables. The primary observables are the $S_{21}$ transmission parameter trajectory in the Argand complex plane, and the extracted internal quality factor ($Q_i/Q_c$) as a function of readout power.

Important parameters / regimes. Key regimes include the low-power thermal saturation limit and the high-power universal scaling regime ($u \propto P_r^{1/3}$), alongside bath temperature ($T_b$) and readout power ($P_r$).

Assumptions / limitations. The model assumes that QPH is the dominant source of dissipative non-linearity in the investigated parameter space, allowing for a simplified analysis based on modified Rothwarf-Taylor equations.

Figures summary. Figures show numerical solutions of the governing quartic equation and experimental $S_{21}$ loci in the Argand plane. These figures illustrate the characteristic oval distortion developing as QPH losses become dominant over other loss mechanisms.

Paper structure. The paper establishes a theoretical model by modifying Rothwarf-Taylor equations to link $Q$ to power via quasiparticle heating. It then analyzes this theory using numerical solutions and validates it through systematic experimental measurements of the resonance response across varying temperatures and powers, culminating in the identification of the diagnostic signature.

Abstract

We investigate the nonlinear behavior of a superconducting microwave resonator subjected to a dissipative mechanism where the associated quality factor (Q factor) decreases with increasing dissipated power, leading to a dissipative feedback effect. By modifying the Rothwarf-Taylor equations, we establish a macroscopic quasiparticle heating (QPH) model that directly links the quality factor to the microwave readout power. The key finding is the identification of a distinctive oval-shaped distortion in the resonance circle in the complex plane. This distortion serves as a practical experimental signature for identifying the readout power regime in which QPH dominates the loss, under conditions where other nonlinear mechanisms are sufficiently weak. To validate the model, we design and fabricate a niobium (Nb) half-wavelength coplanar waveguide (CPW) resonator and conduct systematic bath temperature and readout power sweeps. The model provides a well fit to the observed oval-shaped resonance circle distortion across a wide range of operating conditions, confirming the QPH mechanism as the primary source of the dissipative non-linearity in the parameter space investigated.

Summary. This paper regularizes the singular geometry of disclinations in graphene using a Yukawa potential, creating a smooth, finite-core defect structure. It calculates that this regularization leads to scale-dependent topological charge and a smoothly varying spinorial holonomy for Dirac quasiparticles. This provides a physically realistic extension beyond idealized models for studying geometric phases in 2D materials.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete mathematical framework for handling singular topological defects in condensed matter systems, offering insights into how finite-size effects modify global geometric phases relevant to quantum transport.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study addresses the singularity inherent in ideal conical geometries used to model topological defects (disclinations) by introducing a smooth, finite-core regularization.

Main result. The Yukawa regularization successfully replaces the singular curvature with a distributed source, leading to scale-dependent topological charge and a smoothly varying, radius-dependent spinorial holonomy for quasiparticles.

Method. The authors solve differential equations derived from geometric considerations (Poisson-like equation) using a modified Helmholtz kernel to model the curvature distribution.

Model / system. The system models graphene-like materials containing topological disclinations. The physics is described by massless Dirac quasiparticles propagating in a curved background metric determined by the Yukawa regularization.

Key observables. Scale-dependent topological charge, radius-dependent holonomy (geometric phase), and the conformal factor $\Omega(r)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The screening length ($\ell = \mu^{-1}$) controls the size of the finite core; $\Lambda$ characterizes the total integrated curvature.

Assumptions / limitations. Ideal conical defects are insufficient due to singularities, necessitating a Yukawa-type regularization that preserves asymptotic topology.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the normalized enclosed topological charge $Q( ho)/\Lambda$ versus radial coordinate $ ho$, showing a monotonic increase from zero at the core to the total value $\Lambda$ asymptotically.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the need for regularization, deriving the smooth metric components using the Yukawa kernel, and finally analyzing the resulting dynamics of Dirac quasiparticles in this modified background.

Abstract

Conical geometries provide the standard description of disclinations, but they concentrate the curvature at a singular apex. We introduce a Yukawa-type regularization that replaces this singularity by a smooth curvature distribution while preserving the asymptotic topology of the defect. Exact expressions are obtained for the conformal factor, curvature, and enclosed topological charge. The resulting geometry exhibits a scale-dependent topological charge and a corresponding radius-dependent holonomy, establishing a direct connection between distributed curvature and geometric phases. We further investigate the dynamics of massless Dirac quasiparticles in this background and show that the regularized core modifies the spin connection while preserving the asymptotic topological signature of the defect. These results provide a finite-core extension of the conventional conical description and offer a natural framework for studying geometric and topological effects in graphene-like systems.

Tunable Superconductivity Mediated by Heavy-Electron Plasmons: Band-Structure and Quantum-Geometric Engineering

Summary. This work develops a theory for high-temperature superconductivity using plasmons as the pairing glue, moving beyond traditional phonon mechanisms. It introduces the concept of 'flat-band mediators,' where flat bands generate collective excitations that enhance $T_c$ in an adjacent itinerant electron system. The findings suggest promising material platforms like multilayer graphene exhibiting coexisting light and heavy electronic sectors.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on superconductivity, the reliance on 'quantum geometry' and collective excitations mediated by band structure features (like flat bands) provides theoretical tools applicable to understanding strongly correlated phases driven by geometric constraints or topological effects in quantum materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Establishing fundamental principles for achieving high superconducting critical temperatures ($T_c$) by engineering pairing mechanisms beyond conventional phonon mediation.

Main result. The paper establishes a 'flat-band mediator paradigm,' showing that flat bands can serve as tunable mediators whose collective charge excitations set the superconducting energy scale, potentially boosting $T_c$ significantly above 20 K when combined with phonons.

Method. Theoretical calculations utilizing Eliashberg theory and Random Phase Approximation (RPA) to model electron-plasmon interactions in two-carrier and flat-band systems.

Model / system. The study models two-carrier systems (light/heavy electrons) and insulating systems with flat bands. The pairing mechanism is mediated by plasmons, whose dispersion and coupling are governed by the heavy-electron quantum metric or interband transitions.

Key observables. $T_c$ (critical temperature), Plasmon energy scale ($\Omega_p$), Pairing strength ($\lambda$).

Important parameters / regimes. Carrier densities ($n_l, n_h$), effective masses ($m_l, m_h$), quantum geometry parameters ($\zeta$), and phonon coupling constant ($\lambda_{ph}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis uses the Random Phase Approximation (RPA) and linearized isotropic Eliashberg equations; neglecting vertex corrections beyond Migdal's theorem is a key approximation.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate schematic comparisons between phonon-mediated and plasmon-mediated superconductivity, showing $T_c$ dependence on carrier densities, and detailing the quantum geometry effects in flat-band systems.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing general principles (plasmon mediation) to analyzing specific metallic two-carrier models, then advancing to the more complex insulating flat-band scenario, culminating in a platform proposal based on coexisting light/heavy electrons.

Abstract

Conventional superconductivity derives its pairing glue from lattice vibrations, tying its characteristic scales to chemistry and atomic masses. Plasmons$-$the collective oscillations of electrons$-$can instead be reshaped through electronic structure engineering, but the principles governing optimal plasmon-mediated pairing remain unclear. Here, we establish such principles for two-carrier systems in which heavy-electron plasmons mediate the pairing of light electrons. Within the random-phase approximation and Eliashberg theory, we calculate the optimal $T_c$ of minimal metallic models and show that it is controlled by a competition between the plasmon energy scale and retardation-driven suppression of the repulsion, yielding optimal carrier densities and band masses. While the plasmon channel alone reaches only $T_c\sim$ 0.1 K, a moderate phonon attraction cooperates with it, boosting $T_c$ by two orders of magnitude to above 20 K. However, the band flattening needed for slow metallic plasmons also favors the development of competing orders. We therefore consider an insulating system in which coherent interband transitions between flat bands generate gapped interband plasmons without free carriers. The heavy-band quantum metric governs the dispersion and electron-plasmon pairing strength of the interband plasmon, while the quantum geometry of the light band suppresses static screening and enhances the net attraction. Because layer separation rapidly weakens pairing, we propose systems with coexisting light and heavy electrons living in different mirror-symmetry sectors of the same layer as promising platforms. Our results establish a new role for flat-band systems in superconductivity: rather than hosting the paired electrons themselves, they can serve as a tunable pairing mediator whose collective charge excitations set the superconducting energy scale beyond their narrow bandwidth.

Abstract

We investigate two-color, two dimensional spatially localized light modes in a resonant Kerr third-harmonic generation model. Using computational tools, we identify two distinct families of localized states. Unlike the single-component 2D nonlinear Schrödinger equation and previously studied non-resonant two-color systems, the dynamics are not dictated by a universal critical power, but depend on the distribution of power between the harmonics. The "fundamental-dominated" family acts as a "dynamical separatrix" between simultaneous collapse and joint diffraction, whereas the "third-harmonic-dominated" family does not. We further identify resonant collapse events accompanied by strong oscillations of the third harmonic, revealing a collapse mechanism absent from standard Kerr self-focusing.

Summary. This paper presents a novel method for determining the periodicity of atomic step arrays on vicinal silicon surfaces using raw Scanning Tunneling Microscopy data. By applying Difference-of-Gaussians filtering and analyzing the Fourier transform's characteristic peak suppression, the authors achieved high precision in measuring the step period. This technique allows for accurate characterization of surface crystallography even when traditional measurement methods fail due to instrumental artifacts.

Why it may be interesting. While not directly quantum information theory, this work represents a highly advanced application of signal processing (Fourier analysis) to characterize nanoscale physical structures, which shares mathematical rigor with analyzing correlation functions in condensed matter systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The reliable determination of periodicity for multiatomic steps on high-Miller-index vicinal Si surfaces is difficult due to surface tilt, narrow terraces, and instrumental distortions.

Main result. By analyzing the Fourier transform of differential maps derived from raw STM topography data, the authors determined a precise period of 5.99 nm for triple-step arrays, suggesting a local $ ext{Si}(8 8 11)$ orientation on nominally $(5 5 7)$-oriented wafers.

Method. The analysis uses Scanning Tunneling Microscopy (STM) data processed via the Difference-of-Gaussians (DoG) approach to generate differential maps. Periodicity is extracted by analyzing the period-dependent suppression of specific Fourier peaks in the FFT pattern.

Model / system. The physical system is vicinal single-crystal silicon wafers ($ ext{Si}(h h m)$), specifically focusing on periodic triple-step staircases formed on $ ext{Si}(5 5 7)$. The analysis models the adatom concentration function averaged over the surface.

Key observables. The intensity and pattern of Fourier peaks (specifically, the suppression of specific $m$-th order peaks) in the differential map's FFT.

Important parameters / regimes. Periodicity ($L$), interatomic distance ($b=0.333$ nm), and the number of $ ext{Si}(1 1 1)$ unit cells involved in the structure (e.g., $7 imes 7$).

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes that the periodic structures are commensurable with the underlying crystal lattice, allowing for Fourier analysis to reveal periodicity-dependent suppression effects.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the DoG processing of topography images and show model calculations where the expected period ($L=16b, 17b, 18b$) dictates which specific Fourier peaks are suppressed in the resulting FFT pattern.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the difficulty of traditional measurements on stepped surfaces, introduces the DoG processing technique to mitigate artifacts, and then uses theoretical modeling (based on adatom concentration) combined with experimental observation of peak suppression to derive the precise local surface orientation and periodicity.

Abstract

Reliable determination of the periodicity of multiatomic steps on high-Miller-index vicinal surfaces is often complicated by (i) the presence of relatively narrow terraces tilted at large angles relative to the scanning plane, and (ii) unavoidable distortions in the lateral direction resulting from creep and improper calibration of a piezo scanner. We argue that the period of the triple-step arrays on vicinal Si(5\,5\,6) and Si(5 5 7) single-crystal wafers can be determined from raw scanning tunneling microscopy data, without preliminary corrections, with precision approaching the interatomic distance. We demonstrate that the intensity of the Fourier peaks of the differential maps, derived from raw topography images by the difference-of-Gaussians approach, strongly depends on the period of the ordered triple-step arrays. This suppression of the ninth Fourier peak indicates that the regular array of triple steps has a period of $18b=5.99$ nm in projection onto the Si(1 1 1) terrace plane, where $b=0.333$ nm is the distance between atomic rows of the $1\times 1$ lattice in the $[\bar{1}\,\bar{1}\,2]$ direction. This means that the nominally (5\,5\,7)-oriented Si wafers may correspond locally to the Si(8 8 11) orientation. The method can be applied to the precise analysis of other vicinal surfaces with narrow and wide terraces containing an integer number of Si(1 1 1)$7\times 7$ unit cells.

Summary. This paper introduces ah-SCDFT, a general theoretical framework that incorporates crucial anharmonic lattice vibrations into first-principles calculations for superconductivity. Abstract

First-principles studies of superconductivity often neglect anharmonic effects (AHE), despite their crucial role in achieving quantitative accuracy in many materials. To bridge this gap, we introduce a general computational approach, termed anharmonic superconducting density functional theory (ah-SCDFT) which systematically incorporates anharmonic corrections into standard SCDFT. This approach allows for high-fidelity predictions of superconducting properties with only a modest increase in computational cost for a limited number of superconducting calculation convergence steps. We demonstrate the effectiveness and reliability of ah-SCDFT by applying it to the prototypical superconductor MgB2, accurately reproducing its superconducting behavior under both ambient conditions and applied pressure in excellent agreement with experiment. Our results establish ah-SCDFT as a powerful, efficient, and broadly applicable approach for quantitatively reliable studies of superconductivity and a promising tool for the prediction of new superconducting materials.

Abstract

We develop a physics-constrained machine-learning framework that decodes nanobubble configurations from density-of-states (DOS) spectra. For spatially separated nanobubbles, previous full quantum-transport calculations established that the multi-bubble DOS is numerically equivalent to the normalized sum of the constituent single-bubble spectra. We encode this validated additive relation in a compact neural decomposition model. Abstract

Hydrogen-rich superconductors have garnered considerable interest following the discovery of hot superconductivity in high pressure H$_3$S, reviving prospects for room temperature superconductors under high-pressures. Using H$_3$Se as a reference system, we investigate the vibrational and superconducting properties of D$_3$Se in the Im\bar{3}m phase across 60-200 GPa by combining first-principles calculations with the stochastic self-consistent harmonic approximation to treat ionic quantum and anharmonic effects. These effects introduce significant renormalization to the phonon spectra and stabilize the lattice down to at least 70 GPa, well below the harmonic prediction of >110 GPa. Ultimately, the phonon renormalizations alter the electron-phonon coupling, introducing a decrease in the superconducting critical temperature by about 3-16 K across the studied pressure range with respect to standard calculations. This dramatic reduction demonstrates that anharmonicity fundamentally governs the isotope effect on this system. The stark discrepancy between anharmonic and harmonic descriptions underscores the need for targeted experimental efforts to resolve the origin of the persistent theory-experiment discrepancy in compressed hydrides.

Abstract

We reexamine the crystal structure, electronic structure, lattice dynamics, phonon dispersion, electron-phonon coupling, and superconducting properties of MgHCu3 perovskite using the PBEsol and PBE functionals. This perovskite phase was recently proposed to exhibit superconductivity with the critical superconducting temperature, TC, of 42 K, which falls slightly over the classical 40 K limit for the phonon driven superconductivity. We show that although the crystal and electronic structure of this hypothetical compound are quite robust with respect to the k point mesh and functional used, yet the phonons and phonon related properties are extremely sensitive to the density of the grid chosen as well as functional used for calculations. Correspondingly, the values of the critical superconducting temperature calculated here for different Gaussian broadenings vary in a broad range of ca. 10 to 31 K and they do not exceed the classical limit. We suggest that the properties of this and many other high TC hydrides claimed should be thoroughly scrutinized using a variety of functionals, and benchmarked with experiment, to provide more reliable values of TC.
