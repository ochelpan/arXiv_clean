Model Fractional Quantum Hall States on Lattices: Exact Parent Hamiltonians and Routes to Realization

Authors: Guangyue Ji, Jinjie Zhang
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.25585v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 3/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops a systematic method to construct exact parent Hamiltonians for Fractional Quantum Hall states on lattices. By comparing these exact models to those derived from simple onsite interactions, the authors demonstrate that while Laughlin states are robustly stabilized, the Moore-Read state is more susceptible to spectral mixing. This framework offers a theoretical roadmap for realizing complex topological phases in engineered quantum systems.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous, systematic theoretical tool to bridge the gap between idealized continuum FQH physics and the constraints of realistic, lattice-based experimental platforms, which is crucial for guiding experimental realization.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to develop a unified theoretical framework connecting lattice Fractional Quantum Hall (FQH) model states, their exact parent Hamiltonians, and experimentally accessible interactions.

Main result. The study successfully shows that short-range density interactions stabilize Laughlin states at $ u=1/3$ and $1/4$, while the Moore-Read triplet at $ u=1$ is more sensitive to interband mixing than the Laughlin states.

Method. The authors systematically construct Hermitian parent Hamiltonians by sampling continuum Lowest Landau Level (LLL) model states onto a lattice, using biorthogonal duals to ensure the resulting lattice operators are Hermitian.

Model / system. The system models FQH phases (Laughlin and Moore-Read states) realized on lattices, using parent Hamiltonians derived from the continuum LLL structure. The analysis compares these exact parent Hamiltonians to those generated by simple projected onsite interactions.

Key observables. Overlap measures ($O_{regular}, O_{min}$), spectral isolation ratio ($r$), and the gap structure ($\Delta_{iso}, \delta_{low}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Filling fractions ($ u=1/3, 1/4, 1$), flux densities ($\phi$), and interaction strengths ($U/J$).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that the lattice model states can be accurately represented by sampling the continuum LLL, and the use of the Hermitian $\mu$-based Hamiltonian is preferred over the $ au$-based one.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the construction of parent Hamiltonians and show the spectra for various FQH states ($ u=1/3, 1/4, 1$) under different interaction regimes, demonstrating spectral robustness for Laughlin states versus the sensitivity of the Moore-Read state.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the general framework for constructing lattice parent Hamiltonians using continuum LLL zero modes. It then applies this framework to benchmark Laughlin and Moore-Read states, comparing the results from the exact parent Hamiltonian against those derived from simple projected onsite interactions across varying interaction strengths and flux densities.

Abstract

Recent advances in engineered quantum platforms have enabled the realization of bosonic Laughlin states at $ν=1/2$ and brought non-Abelian topological phases within experimental reach. A central theoretical challenge is to develop a unified framework connecting lattice fractional quantum Hall (FQH) model states, exact parent Hamiltonians, and experimentally accessible interactions. We systematically construct Hermitian parent Hamiltonians for which continuum lowest Landau level (LLL) model states sampled on lattice sites are exact zero modes. Using these model manifolds as quantitative references, we find that, within the projected lattice LLL at the flux densities studied, short-range density interactions stabilize Laughlin ground-state manifolds at $ν=1/3$ and $1/4$, while two-body onsite repulsion supports a Moore--Read triplet at $ν=1$. Full multiband calculations reveal a sharp contrast: the Laughlin manifolds remain robust against interband mixing on the studied tori, whereas the Moore--Read triplet becomes less spectrally isolated and eventually undergoes a level crossing with competing states near the interband scale. Our framework provides a theoretical foundation for using lattice model states to guide the search for experimentally accessible Abelian and non-Abelian FQH states.

On the Computational Complexity of Guided Berry Phase Estimation

Authors: Gabriel Waite
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.25929v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 3/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work establishes the computational complexity of estimating the Berry phase for parameterized quantum systems, proving it is BQP-complete for 2-local interactions. By developing advanced simulation techniques, the authors also show a critical complexity transition, proving that 1-local Hamiltonians allow for efficient, polynomial-time computation of the Berry phase.

Why it may be interesting. It provides rigorous complexity results for physically relevant quantities (Berry phases) in quantum simulation, directly impacting the theoretical understanding of what quantum computers can efficiently calculate regarding geometric phases.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper determines the computational complexity of deciding the Berry phase for parameterized local qubit Hamiltonians.

Main result. It proves that this problem is BQP-complete for 2-local Hamiltonians, while establishing that the problem for 1-local Hamiltonians can be solved in polynomial time (P).

Method. The techniques involve extending the Schrieffer-Wolff transformation to parameterized families and utilizing the perturbative gadget reduction framework to control simulation errors.

Model / system. The study focuses on parameterised 2-local qubit Hamiltonians, specifically on square or triangular lattices, and extends to weighted Heisenberg interactions.

Key observables. The Berry phase ($\vartheta$), which is the primary observable whose computational complexity is analyzed.

Important parameters / regimes. The locality of interactions (1-local vs. 2-local), the required precision ($\epsilon_{ ext{alg}}$), and the initial overlap ($\delta_0$) of the guiding state.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on constructing simulator Hamiltonians that accurately approximate the target Hamiltonian up to second order, and the hardness proof requires the existence of a guiding state with sufficient overlap.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the locality of the Hamiltonian relative to known complexity results, and schematics detail the logical flow connecting uniform simulation, Schrieffer-Wolff analysis, and gadget reductions.

Paper structure. The paper builds from establishing uniform simulation techniques for parameterized Hamiltonians, extending these to the Schrieffer-Wolff analysis, applying gadget reductions to control errors, and culminating in the hardness proof (BQP-completeness) and the polynomial-time algorithm for 1-local cases.

Abstract

We prove that deciding the Berry phase for parameterised 2-local qubit Hamiltonians is BQP-complete when presented with a classical description of a guiding state, promised to overlap with the ground state of the system. Our results extend to systems with weighted Heisenberg interactions and when restricted to a 2D square or triangular lattice geometry. The techniques we develop leverage the Schrieffer--Wolff transformation, typically used in the construction of perturbative gadget reductions for local Hamiltonian problems, extending it to parameterised families of Hamiltonians. We demonstrate that there exists a choice of parameterised simulator Hamiltonians whose Berry phase well-approximates that of a parameterised target family. Using the perturbative gadget reduction framework of Oliveira and Terhal and of Schuch and Verstraete, we adapt the arguments to parameterised interactions and demonstrate the error bounds in the resulting simulation can be controlled. Additionally, we provide an explicit proof that families of 1-local Hamiltonians have a Berry phase that can be efficiently computed to inverse-polynomial precision. This establishes a complexity transition between 1-local and 2-local Hamiltonian families.

Summary. This work analyzes the Convective-Viscous Cahn-Hilliard equation, focusing on the challenging case where the mobility depends on the order parameter. By restricting the mobility dependence to specific reciprocal forms, the authors successfully derive exact analytical solutions for the traveling wave fronts. This provides a significant theoretical advance over previous approximations, offering deeper insight into non-equilibrium phase separation dynamics.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical phase transitions, the mathematical techniques for solving nonlinear PDEs with complex, state-dependent coefficients are highly relevant to modeling non-equilibrium dynamics in quantum many-body systems, such as domain wall motion or quench dynamics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the complex dynamics of phase transitions modeled by the Cahn-Hilliard equation when the mobility coefficient depends non-trivially on the order parameter.

Main result. Exact traveling wave solutions are obtained for specific reciprocal linear and quadratic dependencies of the mobility on the order parameter, providing analytical insights where numerical methods previously were required.

Method. The authors employ the traveling wave ansatz ($z = x - vt$) and solve the resulting nonlinear partial differential equation by assuming a specific functional form for the derivative ($\partial u / \partial z$).

Model / system. The system is governed by the Convective-Viscous Cahn-Hilliard equation, which models phase separation and transitions using an order parameter $w$. The complexity arises from the mobility term $ar{M}(w)$ depending on $w$.

Key observables. Front velocity ($v$), the solution profile $u(x-vt)$, and the constraints on system parameters ($\kappa, v$).

Important parameters / regimes. The mobility dependence parameters ($ heta, \xi$) and the ratio of convective to diffusive terms ($\alpha$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is restricted to specific functional forms for mobility (reciprocal dependencies) to achieve exact analytical solutions, and it assumes the solution approaches constant values at infinity.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the dependence of two specific mobility forms ($M_c$ and $M_s$) on a parameter $ au$ within the physically relevant range of the order parameter $u$.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the governing equation and the difficulty of non-constant mobility. It then focuses on deriving and solving the traveling wave equation using specific mobility forms, leading to detailed analysis in appendices regarding parameter constraints and solution forms.

Abstract

Generally the mobility in the Cahn-Hilliard equation may depend on the order parameter; this was evident already from the original derivation. However, explicit introduction of such dependence results in tremendous calculation difficulties, so the constant mobility approximation was commonly used. On the other hand, in several cases this dependence appeared to be crucial, so more realistic expressions were applied, usually positive powers or polynomials; still, for such dependencies only approximate and/or numerical solution exist. We consider 'reciprocal' linear and quadratic dependencies of mobility on the order parameter; for these dependencies exact traveling wave solutions are obtained. Even more, in a limited interval of the parameters reciprocal quadratic mobilities are rather good approximations to polynomial mobilities.

Abstract

We theoretically investigate the role played by non-Markovianity in the charging of a quantum battery. We focus on the case of a Micromaser, where the energy is stored in a resonant cavity mode and memory effects are introduced and controlled by coupling this mode to a memory ancilla, described as a two-level system, that in turn sequentially interacts with a stream of two-level chargers through pairwise exchange interactions leading to a partial swap. Through numerical analysis, we show that non-Markovian memory effects act as a resource that enhances both the charging capacity and the stability of this device with respect to the Markovian counterpart. We conclude our study showing that the proposed scheme can be implemented in state-of-the-art solid-state platforms for circuit quantum electrodynamics.

Summary. This paper develops a statistical method to analyze diffusion dynamics on large networks by studying the statistical properties of the network Laplacian's eigenspectrum, bypassing the need to calculate all eigenvalues. Using advanced inequalities and asymptotic analysis, the authors derive how network structure changes affect the probability distribution of eigenvalues. This provides a powerful, computationally efficient diagnostic tool for characterizing transport processes in complex systems.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical diffusion, the mathematical techniques—analyzing spectral properties statistically in the large $N$ limit—are highly relevant to understanding spectral gaps and transport in complex quantum systems or quantum networks.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to analyze the statistical properties of the eigenspectrum of the network Laplacian to understand diffusion dynamics efficiently, especially for very large networks.

Main result. The analysis derives scaled formulas for the derivatives of the maximum probability of finding eigenvalues above or below a threshold, showing how network weights influence the diffusion modes.

Method. The authors use statistical tools like the Gershgorin circle theorem, Cantelli’s inequality, and asymptotic analysis ($N o \infty$) to characterize the spectrum without computing all eigenvalues.

Model / system. The model is linear diffusion on a network, mathematically described by the evolution equation d(x)/dt = -D L x, where L is the network Laplacian matrix.

Key observables. Mean ($\mu_\lambda$) and variance ($\sigma^2_\lambda$) of the eigenspectrum; $P_{rmax}(\lambda \ge k)$ and $P_{rmax}(\lambda \le k)$ (maximum probabilities of finding eigenvalues above or below a threshold k).

Important parameters / regimes. Network weights ($w_{ij}$); the number of nodes ($N$) in the limit $N o \infty$; the condition $\mu = 0.2929 a_{max}$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes non-negative, constant edge weights ($w_{ij} \ge 0$) and relies on approximations derived from inequalities (Cantelli's) and limits ($N o \infty$).

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the Gershgorin discs, the probability bound condition, and the scaled derivatives of $P_{rmax}$ with respect to edge weights ($w_{ij}$) and the threshold ($k$).

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the statistical framework using Gershgorin discs, then applying Cantelli's inequality to bound probabilities, and finally deriving the scaled derivatives of these probabilities with respect to network parameters in the large $N$ limit.

Abstract

Network diffusion is a well-known model for studying how a quantity in a network propagates to reach a steady state, where its value becomes uniformly distributed across all nodes. Previous studies of network diffusion have mainly focused on the second-smallest eigenvalue of the network Laplacian to determine the timescale associated with the slowest decaying mode. However, this study investigates the eigenspectrum of the network Laplacian in terms of its statistical properties. This method is useful for studying the statistical properties of network diffusion without calculating all eigenvalues, which can be useful for large networks. Additionally, this study introduces a condition under which the system is less diffusive and investigates how changes in the network structure influence the overall diffusion dynamics.

Intersection Exponents of Simple Random Walks in Two and Three Dimensions

Authors: Qiyuan Shi, Runsheng Liu, Xinyi Li, Ming Li, Youjin Deng
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.25968v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: non-equilibrium universality 2/5 · analog quantum simulation 1/5 · methods for driven-dissipative 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper calculates the intersection exponents ($\xi$) that describe how the probability of multiple independent random walks avoiding each other decays in 2D and 3D. Using Monte Carlo simulations, the authors extend the analysis to complex group configurations and continuous parameters. The results provide crucial numerical data for theoretical studies of non-intersecting paths in statistical mechanics.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical random walks, the mathematical techniques used to determine scaling exponents and analyze multi-particle avoidance probabilities are foundational to understanding critical phenomena and correlation decay in complex many-body quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper determines the intersection exponents ($\xi$) governing the decay of the probability that several independent random walks avoid one another in two and three dimensions.

Main result. The authors provide numerical values for $\xi$ in 3D for various group configurations and extend the analysis to continuous moment orders ($\lambda$), confirming theoretical predictions in 2D.

Method. The primary method involves Monte Carlo (MC) simulations to estimate the non-intersection probability ($P_N$) and fitting this probability to a scaling ansatz $P_N \sim N^{-\xi/2}$.

Model / system. The system models simple random walks on a lattice in $d=2$ and $d=3$. The analysis generalizes to considering multiple groups of walks where intersections are forbidden between groups but allowed within them.

Key observables. The non-intersection probability $P_N$ and the associated intersection exponent $\xi$.

Important parameters / regimes. The number of random walks in different groups ($k, m$), the continuous moment parameter $\lambda$, and the spatial dimension ($d=2, 3$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the assumption that the non-intersection probability decays algebraically, $P_N \sim N^{-\xi/2}$, and involves finite-M bias corrections when estimating continuous parameters.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the calculated non-intersection probability $P_N$ for various group sizes and the resulting exponents $\xi(k, \lambda)/2$ as a function of $\lambda$ for $k=1, 2, 3$.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the random-walk model and numerical methods (Section II), followed by the presentation of results for 2D and 3D intersection exponents (Section III).

Abstract

The probability that several independent random walks avoid one another decays algebraically with the number of steps $N\to\infty$ in two and three dimensions, $P_N\sim N^{-ξ/2}$, where the intersection exponent $ξ$ depends on the number of random walks. More generally, one may consider several groups of independent random walks, with intersections allowed within each group and forbidden between different groups. We first study intersection exponents in two dimensions, where our results agree with the known exact formulas and test the numerical approach. In three dimensions, where no general exact expression is known, we determine $ξ$ for a range of cases. For two groups containing $k$ and $m$ random walks, respectively, we obtain the exponents $ξ(k,m)$ for $k=1,2$ and $m=1,2,\ldots,7$. We further extend $m$ to a continuous parameter $λ$, allowing us to obtain numerical values for $ξ(k,λ)$ for $k=1,2,3$ and $0.25\leqλ\leq3$. Away from the smallest moment orders, these functions increase with $λ$ with decreasing slopes, as expected from the strict concavity of Brownian intersection exponents. We also investigate three-group configurations for several representative cases. The resulting integer and continuous exponents supply numerical values for analytical studies of non-intersecting random paths.

Summary. This theoretical work critiques experimental claims of observing momentum-dependent spin polarization in CrSb using Andreev reflection. By applying symmetry arguments, the authors show that the measured ASP is forbidden by symmetry in the diffusive regime and for most measured directions in the ballistic regime. This suggests the original measurements may stem from flawed definitions or experimental artifacts rather than genuine physical effects.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous application of symmetry principles to interpret complex transport measurements in magnetic topological materials is highly relevant for understanding spin-charge coupling in condensed matter systems.

Main result. The authors demonstrate that the claimed ASP is forbidden by symmetry in the diffusive regime for all measured directions and is forbidden in the ballistic regime for two of the three measured orientations.

Method. The analysis uses symmetry constraints and theoretical derivations for both the diffusive and ballistic regimes of Andreev reflection to calculate the expected ASP.

Model / system. The study focuses on the altermagnet CrSb, analyzing spin transport properties across a point contact interface.

Key observables. Andreev Spin Polarization (ASP) measured via point-contact Andreev reflection (PCAR).

Important parameters / regimes. Crystallographic orientations ((0001), (-1-120), (10-10)), diffusive vs. ballistic regimes.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on symmetry properties (e.g., C2^perp||Mn) and the theoretical framework of Andreev reflection in magnetic materials.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by analyzing the theoretical constraints imposed by symmetry on the ASP in both the diffusive and ballistic limits, contrasting these findings with the original claims.

Abstract

Zhang et al. [Phys. Rev. Lett. 137, 016701 (2026)] report momentum-dependent spin polarization in the altermagnet CrSb from point-contact Andreev reflection along the (0001), (-1-120), and (10-10) directions. We show that the Andreev spin polarization they claim to measure is forbidden by symmetry: it vanishes identically in the diffusive regime for all directions in CrSb, and in the ballistic regime for two of the three orientations measured. It is allowed only along one direction, where the calculated band structure gives ~50%. The large, nearly isotropic values reported follow from an incorrect definition of ballistic Andreev spin polarization that evaluates the spin asymmetry at fixed three-dimensional momentum; the Andreev selection rule is local only in the momentum component parallel to the interface. Moreover, the ballistic regime is not demonstrated, and the fitting procedure is flawed.

Summary. This paper theoretically examines the electrostatic pressure between two charged walls containing counterions, focusing on how modulating the surface charge density affects the force. Using the Poisson-Boltzmann framework and exact solutions to the 2D Liouville equation, the authors demonstrate that modulation can cause the pressure to increase or decrease depending on the separation distance. This provides detailed insights into the non-uniform effects of surface chemistry on confined plasmas.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical electrostatics, the use of advanced mathematical techniques like the 2D Liouville equation and the analysis of non-uniform boundary conditions are relevant to understanding boundary effects in quantum field theories or confined quantum systems.

Main problem. The study investigates the thermal equilibrium and effective force (pressure) between two parallel walls containing counterions, specifically analyzing the effect of surface charge modulations.

Main result. The analysis shows that surface charge modulation can both increase (at small distances) and decrease (at large distances) the pressure between the walls compared to uniformly charged surfaces.

Method. The authors derive exact solutions for the electrostatic potential using the general solution of the two-dimensional Liouville equation, analyzing the system within the Poisson-Boltzmann mean-field theory.

Model / system. The system models a classical plasma of identical point charges (counterions) confined between two parallel walls separated by distance d. The walls possess position-dependent surface charge densities, leading to a 2D electrostatic problem.

Key observables. Effective force (pressure) between the walls, counterion density profile, and the electrostatic potential $\psi(x, y)$.

Important parameters / regimes. Bjerrum length ($\ell_B$), Gouy-Chapman length ($\mu$), and the coupling constant ($\Xi$). The analysis focuses on the weak coupling regime ($\Xi \ll 1$).

Assumptions / limitations. The exact solutions are restricted to models where the surface charge density is generated inversely from the calculated potential, and the analysis assumes the dielectric constant of the walls matches the medium.

Paper structure. The paper builds upon previous work by extending exact solutions for a single EDL to two parallel EDLs, utilizing the general solution of the 2D Liouville equation to derive expressions for the pressure and charge density.

Abstract

We study the thermal equilibrium of a classical system of identical point charges moving between two walls with parallel surfaces at a distance $d$, charged symmetrically with position-dependent surface charge densities. Specifically, we study the effect of surface charge modulation on the effective force (pressure) between the walls in the Poisson-Boltzmann limit. Based on Monte Carlo simulations at high temperatures, it is predicted that surface charge modulation reduces the pressure between the walls compared to uniformly charged wall surfaces with the same average surface charge density. We restrict ourselves to surface charge modulations in only one direction and, using a general solution of the two-dimensional Liouville equation, we construct exact solutions for the electrostatic potential. The surface charge density on the walls is generated inversely from this potential, which means that our exact results apply to a limited set of models with a specific variation of the surface charge density with distance $d$. Explicit analytical results show that surface charge modulation can both increase (small distances $d$) and decrease (large $d$) the pressure between the walls.

Lattice in Line: Optimized DMRG ordering for complex lattice geometries

Authors: Roman Rausch
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.25384v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 2/5 · methods for driven-dissipative 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper tackles the critical preprocessing step for applying DMRG to 2D/3D lattices: finding the optimal site ordering. The authors demonstrate that using advanced SAT/CP solvers to optimize for cutwidth ($C$) and interaction range ($R$) is superior to minimizing graph bandwidth ($B$). This methodology provides a powerful, systematic tool for simulating complex magnetic materials.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on lattice ordering, the underlying techniques—using advanced constraint solvers (SAT/CP) to solve hard combinatorial optimization problems—are highly relevant for mapping complex quantum Hamiltonians onto tractable computational structures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of finding an optimal linear ordering of sites on complex 2D or 3D lattices to efficiently apply the Density-Matrix Renormalization Group (DMRG) algorithm.

Main result. Optimizing the ordering by targeting the cutwidth ($C$) and then the average interaction range ($R$) lexicographically yields the best ground-state energies, significantly outperforming bandwidth-only optimizations.

Method. The authors propose a systematic, staged optimization approach utilizing Boolean Satisfiability (SAT) and Constraint-Programming (CP) solvers (specifically CP-SAT) to solve the NP-hard lattice compilation problem.

Model / system. The work applies to lattice geometries (e.g., pyrochlore, fullerene, kagome) relevant to frustrated magnetism, modeled using Hamiltonians like the SU(2) Heisenberg model.

Key observables. Graph Bandwidth ($B$), Cutwidth ($C$), Average Interaction Range ($R$), and the resulting ground-state energy.

Important parameters / regimes. The optimal ordering is determined by minimizing $(C, R)$ lexicographically, with $B$ being less critical.

Assumptions / limitations. Heuristic algorithms (like RCM) are unreliable for achieving true optimal orderings; the SAT/CP approach provides rigorous bounds.

Figures summary. Figures compare optimal vs. heuristic orderings for rings, showing $C$ vs. $B$ planes, and comparing $R$ values derived from cutwidth-optimal versus bandwidth-optimal orderings.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem, analyzes proxy loss functions ($B, C, R$), compares optimization techniques (heuristics vs. QUBO vs. SAT/CP), and systematically presents results for various complex lattice geometries.

Abstract

The density-matrix renormalization group (DMRG) is a one-dimensional tensor-network technique, but it is not limited to one-dimensional systems: it can be applied to periodic 2D and 3D clusters and molecules, provided their sites are first enumerated along a line; a step one may call "lattice compilation". This paper discusses three proxy loss functions for finding this optimal enumeration: the graph bandwidth $B$ (maximum interaction range), the cutwidth $C$ (maximum number of bonds crossing a cut), and the average interaction range $R$. Constructing the Hamiltonian MPO (matrix-product operator) for a large set of clusters that are of interest in frustrated magnetism, I find that $C$ determines the peak SU(2) Heisenberg MPO bond dimension and $R$ the average one. Targeting $(C,R)$ lexicographically yields the best energies. Targeting $B$ indirectly reduces $C$, but not as efficiently as targeting $C$ directly. Otherwise, the value of $B$ itself is largely irrelevant in the sense that good DMRG energies can have large $B$. To perform the optimization, classic heuristics (e.g. reverse Cuthill--McKee) prove unreliable even for small clusters, and I find that a QUBO formulation improves them only marginally. Instead I propose a staged optimization built on Boolean satisfiability (SAT) and constraint-programming (CP) solvers, chiefly CP-SAT of Google's OR-Tools, a hybrid of CP propagation and SAT clause learning. This approach yields significantly better orderings together with rigorous bounds. The corresponding Lattice in Line code is available at https://github.com/spinflip/lattice_in_line and was designed with extensive use of the Fable 5 large language model.

Abstract

We introduce and develop Functional Dimensional Regularization (FDR), a novel functional renormalization group (RG) framework that extends the principles of dimensional regularization beyond the perturbative $\varepsilon$-expansion. The key insight is to define the FDR beta functions, valid in any continuous dimension $d$ and characterized by threshold functions, as a sum over all critical dimensions of the corresponding scheme-independent beta functions computed in DR. We demonstrate that this formalism exhibits all the hallmarks of a fully-fledged functional RG. Within the local potential approximation, we perform a general fixed point analysis, recovering the $\varepsilon$-expansion for all multi-critical models and establishing a direct connection to functional scaling solutions, spike plots, and eigen-perturbation spectra. Extending the framework to the second order of the derivative expansion, we compute the critical exponents for the Ising universality class in $d=3$, providing estimates for the anomalous dimension $η$ and RG spectrum that converge rapidly and show favorable agreement with state-of-the-art results. We present a detailed comparison with non-perturbative and proper-time RG approaches, highlighting structural similarities and key differences. In particular, we introduce a ``distillation'' procedure that allows one to systematically derive the FDR flow from any other one-loop-exact RG scheme. Our results establish FDR as a self-contained and competitive RG framework that combines the technical simplicity of dimensional regularization with the versatility of the functional approach.

Abstract

Probability backflow is investigated for charged spin-$1/2$ particles in a uniform magnetic field within the lowest-Landau-level approximation. While single-mode Landau states exhibit only weak and non-robust backflow, it is shown that two-mode interference gives rise to negative probability flux in both the Schrödinger and Pauli formulations. In the Pauli case, orbital and spin contributions to the probability current are explicitly separated, revealing an additional interference mechanism. The external magnetic field is found to act as a tunable control parameter, simultaneously controlling the spatial overlap of orbitals and the spin dynamics. In the two-mode regime, a resonance between orbital mismatch and spin precession leads to an enhancement of backflow. Finite spatial resolution is shown not to suppress the effect within realistic coarse-graining. These results establish magnetic-field-controlled quantum backflow in Landau systems as an accessible interference phenomenon.
