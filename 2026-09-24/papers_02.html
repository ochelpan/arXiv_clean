Summary. This paper presents a tensor network framework to classically simulate complex dynamic quantum circuits that include mid-circuit measurements and classical feedback. It introduces and compares two simulation strategies—single-path stochastic sampling and multi-path ensemble tracking—to manage the exponential growth of computational branches. The resulting framework offers a scalable tool for analyzing quantum algorithms that rely on adaptive measurements.

Why it may be interesting. The ability to simulate complex, adaptive quantum processes like those found in quantum error correction or state preparation, which are crucial for understanding open quantum systems dynamics, is highly valuable.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is developing a scalable classical simulation method for dynamic quantum circuits, which suffer from exponentially growing execution branches due to mid-circuit measurements.

Main result. The authors provide a practical and scalable framework by comparing single-path stochastic and multi-path ensemble simulation strategies, showing the trade-offs between accuracy and computational cost.

Method. The method extends a DMRG-based tensor network approach to incorporate dynamic circuit operations, handling branching via stochastic sampling or weighted ensemble propagation.

Model / system. The model is the dynamic quantum circuit, involving unitary operations, mid-circuit measurements yielding classical outcomes, and classically controlled operations acting on both a quantum and a classical register.

Key observables. Fidelity (global and partial), computational runtime, and peak memory usage.

Important parameters / regimes. Bond dimension ($\chi$), circuit depth, and the number of active measurement branches (for multi-path).

Assumptions / limitations. The multi-path approach assumes that truncating low-probability branches (top-M selection) is an acceptable approximation.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem, details the tensor network extension of DMRG for dynamic circuits, compares the single-path and multi-path simulation strategies, and benchmarks these methods on standard and random circuits.

Abstract

Dynamic circuits, which incorporate mid-circuit measurements and classically controlled operations, extend the expressive power of quantum programs and are central to applications such as quantum error correction, state preparation, and distributed quantum algorithms. However, their classical simulation is challenging due to the exponential growth of execution branches induced by mid-circuit measurements. In this work, we develop a tensor network approach for simulating dynamic circuits by extending a DMRG-based method for quantum circuit simulation to support dynamic circuit operations. To address the exponential proliferation of execution branches, we introduce and compare two strategies: a single-path stochastic approach that samples individual measurement outcomes, and a multi-path approach that maintains an ensemble of branches. We benchmark these methods on standard dynamic circuits, including the teleportation protocol and GHZ state preparation, as well as on random dynamic circuits. Our analysis explores the trade-offs between simulation accuracy and computational efficiency. These results provide a practical and scalable framework for the classical simulation of dynamic circuits.

Summary. This work experimentally benchmarks Maximum-Likelihood Amplitude Estimation (MLAE) for Monte Carlo integration on trapped-ion quantum computers. Main result. While the algorithm shows near-Heisenberg scaling in the noiseless limit, on current trapped-ion hardware, the error saturates at a noise floor, and the super-classical scaling advantage does not survive.

Method. A Maximum-Likelihood Amplitude Estimation (MLAE) pipeline, combining controlled-Ry state preparation, Grover amplification, and maximum-likelihood inference, was benchmarked.

Model / system. The experiments were conducted on IonQ trapped-ion processors (Forte-1 and Aria-1 noise models), using a benchmark integral estimation problem.

Key observables. Estimation error ($\epsilon$), error scaling exponent ($\alpha$), total oracle calls, and device time.

Important parameters / regimes. Amplification depth ($m$), number of qubits (5, 9, 19), and the comparison between hardware performance and vendor noise models.

Paper structure. The paper systematically benchmarks the MLAE pipeline across multiple hardware settings and schedules, first establishing the noiseless limit, and then analyzing the performance degradation and saturation on real quantum hardware.

Abstract

Quantum Amplitude Estimation promises to accelerate Monte Carlo integration by replacing the classical $\varepsilon \propto N^{-1/2}$ sampling law with the Heisenberg-limited $\varepsilon \propto N^{-1}$ scaling, but whether any part of that advantage survives on present-day hardware is an empirical question. We report a systematic experimental benchmark of a Maximum-Likelihood Amplitude Estimation QMC pipeline on IonQ trapped-ion processors, comprising 60 hardware trials on IonQ Forte-1 across five settings(5 & 9 qubits, 100-200 shots per depth, linear & exponential amplification schedules, ~113 device-hours), together with 100 trials on IonQ Aria-1 & Forte-1 noise models at 5, 9 & 19 qubits. The pipeline combines controlled-$R_y$ rotational state preparation, Grover-based amplitude amplification and maximum-likelihood inference and is benchmarked against $b_{\max}^{-1}\!\int_0^{b_{\max}}\!\sin^2\!x\,\mathrm{d}x$ with $b_{\max}=π/5$ ,given results emerge. First, the noiseless limit it attains $\varepsilon \propto N^{-0.88}$, close to the Heisenberg-scaling reference, reducing the error 54-74x over a 133x increase in oracle calls. Second, scaling does not survive on hardware. Every noisy backend saturates at an error floor of (2-5)x 10^{-2}, with fitted exponents of 0.04-0.15 on Forte-1 linear schedules and $α\le\!0$ on the vendor noise models: beyond the optimal shallow depth, however, deeper amplification provides no sustained accuracy gain and instead returns to the noise floor. Forte-1 hardware also outperformed IonQ's own Aria-1 noise model at every depth $m \ge 2$, indicating that vendor noise models are bad predictors of MLAE accuracy. The practical implication for UQ is that on current devices the useful operating point is a shallow, schedule-tuned amplification depth chosen so that the deepest circuit lands near p~1/2 not the deepest schedule the coherence budget allows.

Key observables. Gapless spin-liquid phase, gapped non-Abelian phase (with chiral Majorana edge modes), and the required bond-directional exchange interactions ($J_x, J_y, J_z$).

Important parameters / regimes. Out-of-plane magnetic flux ($\Phi_A$) and the semiconductor Fermi energy ($E_F$), which control interference and enforce bond directionality via emergent Nambu-exchange symmetry.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the physical architecture and the target Hamiltonian. Finally, it discusses the resulting quantum phases and experimental feasibility.

Abstract

We propose a realization of the Kitaev honeycomb Hamiltonian -- an archetypal spin-liquid model -- in a superconducting metamaterial. The architecture consists of Cooper-pair boxes coupled through depleted semiconductor--superconductor heterostructures that do not require spin--orbit coupling. The Cooper-pair boxes encode effective spin degrees of freedom, while normal and anomalous virtual propagation through the heterostructures mediate bond-directional interactions. Two key control parameters are an out-of-plane magnetic flux and the semiconductor Fermi energy. The former controls interference and distinguishes the bond directions, while tuning the latter close to the bottom of the band gives rise to an emergent Nambu-exchange symmetry that enforces the required bond directionality. Through numerical calculations, we identify an operating regime with controlled corrections, with associated energy and length scales within experimental reach. These results establish a route toward equilibrium quantum spin liquids in engineered superconducting networks.

Summary. This paper theoretically analyzes how isolated, coupled quantum systems, such as qubits, can exhibit thermalization and dephasing despite evolving unitarily. By deriving and solving a weak-coupling master equation, the authors show that non-linear coupling terms are necessary to drive the system towards a thermal state, while linear coupling alone is insufficient. The findings have implications for understanding energy relaxation in quantum circuits.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses non-equilibrium dynamics in isolated quantum systems, providing crucial insights into how thermalization can occur purely through internal interactions without coupling to an external bath, which is vital for understanding quantum thermalization.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the phenomenon of thermalization and dephasing within an isolated quantum system, contrasting this behavior with the standard definition of unitary evolution.

Main result. The system's ability to thermalize depends critically on the coupling mechanism: linear coupling fails to achieve thermal equilibrium, whereas non-linearity (three-wave mixing) ensures a thermal distribution determined by the initial total energy.

Method. The analysis employs a weak-coupling master equation approach, deriving time evolution equations for the density matrix elements of a subsystem coupled to the rest of the system.

Model / system. The model consists of an isolated set of $N$ coupled qubits (or weakly anharmonic oscillators). The dynamics are analyzed by treating one qubit as the system and the remaining qubits as an effective bath, considering both linear and cubic (three-wave mixing) coupling terms.

Key observables. Populations ($ ho_{gg}, ho_{ee}$), coherence ($ ho_{ge}$), and the final steady-state temperature ($eta$).

Important parameters / regimes. Weak coupling limit, initial total energy ($\Delta E$), and the presence/absence of non-linearity.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on deriving a weak-coupling master equation and making approximations regarding the coupling strength and the nature of the bath interaction.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the setup comparing global unitary evolution to subsystem analysis, show time traces of populations for WAOs under three-wave mixing, and display the decay of off-diagonal elements.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the theoretical framework using a weak-coupling master equation for a subsystem. It then analyzes the results for linear coupling (showing no thermalization) and contrasts this with the non-linear case (showing thermalization). Finally, it presents potential experimental realizations.

Abstract

A system evolving unitarily does not dephase or thermalize by definition. Yet, if one considers a part of a unitary system, situation is different. Here we analyze an isolated set of coupled qubits taking one qubit at a time as a subsystem, and writing self-consistently a weak-coupling master equation for it. Weakly anharmonic oscillators and ideal qubits (two-level systems) demonstrate dephasing, i.e. the diagonal elements of the density matrix reach a steady-state in the long-time limit, and the off-diagonal elements of ideal qubits decay exponentially. For the linear coupling between the qubits, only the degenerate ones interact, and the system does not reach thermal distribution. However, non-linearity, in our analysis in form of three-wave mixing, ensures a thermal distribution in the long time limit, where the temperature is determined uniquely by the energy in the initial non-thermal state. Finally, we present a potential experimental scheme based on a superconducting quantum circuit.

Abstract

We characterise the approach to nonequilibrium steady state in nested resetting processes by deriving accumulation times for the system, which quantify the effective first-passage time for the local establishment of steady state. For equal resetting rates, we obtain closed-form expressions showing that relaxation propagates as a wavefront with a delay that increases linearly along the resetting chain. We then extend this analysis to heterogeneous resetting rates, deriving exact steady-state distributions, spatial moments and accumulation times for both degenerate and non-degenerate resetting rates. We show that relaxation to steady state is ultimately governed by the minimum resetting rate in the system and, in degenerate systems, its multiplicity. These results provide a comprehensive analytical description of first-passage to nonequilibrium steady state in hierarchically coupled stochastic resetting systems, with potential applications to relaxation and transport processes in biological systems.

Main problem. The paper derives bounds on the thermodynamic cost (entropy production) associated with communication channels and defines a thermodynamic efficiency metric.

Main result. The thermodynamic efficiency is bounded by unity, and maximizing it favors a biased input that switches rarely, leading to spontaneous symmetry breaking in simple models.

Method. The analysis uses information theory concepts (mutual information, capacity) combined with non-equilibrium thermodynamics, decomposing entropy production into adiabatic and non-adiabatic parts.

Model / system. The study applies to a general class of communication channels relevant to both artificial and natural systems, exemplified by the Binary Symmetric Channel (BSC) and a minimal cellular sensing model.

Key observables. Mutual Information ($I$), Channel Capacity ($C$), Entropy Production ($\Sigma$), and Thermodynamic Efficiency ($\eta = I/\Sigma$).

Important parameters / regimes. Input bias parameter ($b$), channel error rate ($\epsilon$), and the ratio of transition to idle entropy production ($r$).

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the dependence of efficiency ($\eta$) on input bias ($b$) and show the non-trivial symmetry breaking transition in the BSC case.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the fundamental thermodynamic bound ($k_b I \le \Sigma_s$), defines efficiency, and then applies this framework to specific models like the BSC, demonstrating how optimization leads to biased, symmetry-breaking inputs.

Abstract

We identify a broad class of communication channels that captures common physical constraints in both artificial and natural systems and derive bounds on their thermodynamic cost. We find that the entropy production per channel use is bounded from below by the input-output mutual information, and their ratio -mutual information divided by entropy production- defines the thermodynamic efficiency. Unlike previous studies of energy-constrained communication channels, our analysis shows that thermodynamic costs must be assigned not only to the input symbols themselves, but also to transitions between successive symbols. As a result, maximizing thermodynamic efficiency favors a biased input that switches only rarely, rather than the capacity-achieving input. For the binary symmetric channel, this preference emerges through a pitchfork bifurcation that spontaneously breaks the symmetry of the channel. A minimal model of cellular sensing exhibits the same phenomenon.

Abstract

We study the equilibrium statistical mechanics of one-dimensional classical Machian fractons: particles whose dynamics conserves a global dipole moment and whose Hamiltonian couples momentum differences through a position-dependent pair-inertia kernel. Compactly supported interaction kernels have a divergence in the Gibbs partition function, and have been shown to break ergodicity and symmetry by forming non-equilibrium steady states with particle clusters, evading the Hohenberg-Mermin-Wagner-Coleman theorem. In this paper, we consider kernels with non-compact support and study their ergodic properties. For exponentially decaying kernels, graph-Laplacian and matrix-tree bounds provide an extensive free energy suggesting that a putative statistical mechanical description is valid. Similarly, uniform non-local kernels have a super-extensive free energy and require a Kac rescaling. A generalized Hohenberg--Mermin--Wagner--Coleman argument, supported by finite-size scaling, implies symmetry-breaking density order parameter vanishes at all wave vectors melting the long-range translation-breaking density order of compact kernels. To study the resulting equilibrium ensemble, we construct a nonreversible event-chain Monte Carlo (ECMC) algorithm that samples the coupled position-momentum phase space while preserving the dipole moment and total momentum. The ECMC sampling is shown to quantitatively match long time-averaged quantites in Hamiltonian dynamics. The equilibrium liquid exhibits preferred short-range clustering and strongly non-Gaussian single-particle momentum tails associated with the correlated nature of positions and momenta.
