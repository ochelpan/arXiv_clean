Crystal Electric Field Analysis on the Magnetic Properties of Ferromagnetic CeRu$_2$Ge$_2$ Single Crystal

Authors: Shovan Dan, Suman Nandi, Gourav Dwari, Bishal Baran Maity, Bhagyashree A Chalke, Ruta Kulkarni, P. D. Babu, Arumugam Thamizhavel
Type: both · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.26587v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper presents a comprehensive study of the magnetic and transport properties of the ferromagnetic material CeRu2Ge2. By applying Crystal Electric Field (CEF) theory to experimental data from magnetization, resistivity, and specific heat, the authors successfully explain the observed magnetic anisotropy. The work highlights that the magnetic easy axis is dictated by the CEF ground state, providing a detailed picture of local moment magnetism in this correlated material.

Why it may be interesting. The detailed analysis of magnetic anisotropy and the successful application of CEF theory to explain the magnetic ground state in a heavy fermion ferromagnet provides insights into how crystal symmetry dictates magnetic ordering in correlated electron systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To conduct a detailed anisotropic study on the magnetic and transport properties of the ferromagnetic single crystal CeRu2Ge2 and explain the observed phenomena using the Crystal Electric Field (CEF) framework.

Main result. The magnetic anisotropy and overall magnetic behavior are quantitatively explained by the CEF model, showing the easy axis aligns along the [001] direction, consistent with the CEF ground state.

Method. The study combines multiple experimental techniques (magnetization, resistivity, specific heat) with theoretical analysis using the point-charge Crystal Electric Field (CEF) model.

Model / system. The physical system is the CeRu2Ge2 single crystal, which is analyzed using the CEF Hamiltonian, which is fitted to experimental data to determine the electronic energy level scheme.

Key observables. Ferromagnetic transition temperature (Tc = 7.5 K), positive magnetoresistance, magnon excitation gap, magnetic susceptibility anisotropy ([100] vs [001]), and CEF energy levels (three doublets).

Important parameters / regimes. CEF parameters (e.g., B2^0 = -25 K), molecular field constants (lambda_x, lambda_z), and the magnon gap energy (Delta).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the point-charge CEF model, and the interpretation of large negative molecular field constants as anisotropic exchange rather than direct antiferromagnetic exchange.

Figures summary. Figures show XRD patterns, magnetization vs. T/B curves along different axes, resistivity vs. T curves with magnon-gap fits, and MR vs. B plots.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from structural characterization and basic magnetic/transport measurements to detailed CEF analysis using specific heat (Schottky effect) and susceptibility data, culminating in a full explanation of the magnetic anisotropy.

Abstract

We report a detailed anisotropic studies on the magnetic and transport properties of a CeRu$_2$Ge$_2$ single crystal. A clear ferromagnetic transition is observed at $T_{\rm C} = 7.5$~K in the magnetic susceptibility, electrical resistivity and heat capacity measurements. The magnetoresistance (MR) is positive at low temperature within the magnetically ordered state. We confirmed the presence of a magnon excitation gap in the resistivity, MR and the low temperature heat capacity data. The pronounced anisotropy observed in the magnetic susceptibility and magnetization along the [100] and [001] directions is quantitatively explained within the crystal electric field (CEF) framework using the point-charge model. The analysis reveals a CEF level scheme comprising three doublets, with a ground state and two excited states at 491 and 671~K, that was further confirmed from the Schottky effect in the magnetic part of the heat capacity data. Unlike most Kondo lattice ferromagnets of Ce compounds, where the magnetic easy axis deviates from the expected CEF ground state, CeRu$_2$Ge$_2$ exhibits magnetization, which is fully consistent with the CEF derived ground state wave function, with the easy axis of magnetization aligned along the [001] direction.

Double Descent and Malign Overfitting in Diffusion Models

Authors: Raphaël Urfin, Tony Bonnaire, Giulio Biroli, Marc Mézard
Type: theory · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.26392v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper investigates why diffusion models exhibit 'malign overfitting'—a failure mode worse than expected—despite being fundamentally regression-like. By analyzing the bias and variance of the score estimator, the authors show that the model memorizes training data rather than learning the true underlying distribution. They conclude that while overparameterization is powerful, it requires explicit regularization to prevent catastrophic generalization failure.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a deep theoretical understanding of generalization failure in complex generative models, drawing parallels between statistical physics concepts (like phase transitions or critical points) and machine learning generalization boundaries.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the paradox of 'malign overfitting' observed in diffusion models, contrasting it with the expected 'benign overfitting' from standard deep learning regression.

Main result. Overfitting in diffusion models is malignant because the implicit regularization drives the model to memorize the empirical score rather than the true underlying score, which is mitigated only by explicit regularization.

Method. The analysis combines theoretical derivations using random-features models (Gaussian Equivalence Principle) with empirical studies on U-Nets, employing bias-variance decomposition to pinpoint the failure mechanism.

Model / system. The system studied is the training of diffusion models (like DDPM-style U-Nets) to estimate the score function of an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process. The theory is rigorously tested using Random Feature Neural Networks (RFNNs).

Key observables. Test loss ($L_{ ext{test}}$), Empirical test loss ($L_{ ext{emp}}^{ ext{test}}$), Training loss ($L_{n,m}^{ ext{train}}$), Bias, and Variance.

Important parameters / regimes. Model size ($p$), sample size ($n$), noise realizations ($m$), and regularization strength ($\lambda$).

Assumptions / limitations. The theoretical analysis often relies on the high-dimensional limit ($p, n, d \gg 1$) and specific asymptotic approximations.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the double descent curve, showing the divergence between $L_{ ext{test}}$ and $L_{ ext{emp}}^{ ext{test}}$ in the overparameterized regime, and demonstrating that regularization restores optimal performance.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the paradox, develops theoretical tools (RFNN analysis), diagnoses the mechanism via bias/variance decomposition, and concludes by showing that regularization is necessary to restore beneficial overparameterization.

Abstract

Conventional wisdom in deep learning holds that overparameterization---having more parameters $p$ than training samples $n$---is benign: larger models generalize better and, even without regularization, interpolating models generalize well, the test error following a double-descent curve. One might expect the same benign overfitting for diffusion models, whose training reduces to regression, i.e. to minimizing a quadratic score-matching loss. Yet the opposite is observed: overfitting here is catastrophic, driving the model into a memorization regime. We resolve this paradox by combining experiments on U-Nets trained on CelebA with a random-features model for which we derive closed-form learning curves. We show that with a fixed number $m$ of noise realizations per training sample, an interpolation peak does occur, but at $p\sim nm$ rather than at $p\sim n$ as in standard regression. The rise of the test loss, however, sets in much earlier, at $p\sim n$, independently of $m$. This overfitting is malign because, although the implicit regularization of training is fully at work, it drives the model toward the empirical score, which memorizes the training set, rather than toward the true score. A bias-variance decomposition pinpoints the mechanism: the bias of the score estimator starts to grow at $p\sim n$; past the peak the variance decays, as in regression, whereas the bias keeps growing and both saturate at a large value. Since diffusion models are trained with $m\gg1$, the peak is pushed to very large model sizes, and therefore sit on the rising branch that precedes it, where malign overfitting is already in play. Nevertheless, overparameterization remains beneficial when paired with regularization: in the random-features theory and in U-Net experiments, optimally regularized large models---via a ridge penalty or early stopping, respectively---outperform any unregularized models.

Error Correction Properties of Covariant Bosonic Encodings

Authors: Frederic St-Amand, Jean-Philippe Burelle, Baptiste Royer
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.26660v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

Summary. This paper develops a rigorous, representation-theoretic framework to analyze bosonic quantum error-correcting codes. By viewing the QEC matrix as a group representation morphism, the authors systematically analyze how these codes protect against noise like photon loss. This provides a powerful theoretical tool for designing hardware-efficient fault-tolerant quantum memories.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, abstract mathematical tool (representation theory) to analyze physical error correction codes in bosonic systems, which is highly relevant for quantum optics and superconducting circuit implementations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to develop a general framework for constructing and analyzing the error-correction properties of covariant bosonic codes.

Main result. The analysis shows that the Quantum Error Correction (QEC) matrix can be viewed as a morphism of group representations, allowing for the systematic study of error protection for various bosonic codes.

Method. The authors employ a representation-theoretic framework, defining codewords via a covariant encoding that acts as a morphism between logical and physical group representations.

Model / system. The system involves bosonic modes, where logical information is encoded in the infinite-dimensional Hilbert space. The analysis focuses on error protection against amplitude damping channels (photon loss) using finite subgroups of SU(2).

Key observables. The QEC matrix ($Q_{kl}$) and the near-optimal fidelity ($ ilde{F}$) are key diagnostics used to quantify the code's robustness.

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis involves parameters related to the size of the group representations ($d_L, N$) and the order of the error operators.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is the applicability of the representation-theoretic view of QEC, and the analysis relies on using the computationally tractable near-optimal fidelity ($ ilde{F}$) instead of the optimal fidelity ($F_{ ext{opt}}$).

Paper structure. The paper builds from preliminaries on Representation Theory and Covariant Encoding (Section II) to establish the general framework connecting the QEC matrix to group representation morphisms (Section III). Appendices detail proofs regarding the action of group elements on single-photon states and the structure of the QEC matrix.

Abstract

Bosonic codes offer a promising approach towards hardware-efficient fault tolerance, with many leading examples such as the cat-code and the GKP code organized by an underlying symmetry. We build on a representation-theoretic framework of quantum error correction to formalize the construction and analysis of multimode symmetric bosonic codes, focusing on finite groups. Here, a physical, logical representation and an initial seed state together define the codewords via a covariant encoding. A central observation is that QEC matrices can be viewed as morphisms of group representations, so that Schur's lemma forces entire irreducible representations blocks to vanish. We recover several known single- and two-mode bosonic codes and build and analyze the error protection capabilities of three codes based on finite subgroups of SU(2). We analyze the impact of reducibility of the logical representation and optimize the seed state using the near-optimal fidelity.

Error Suppression in Distributed Quantum Computing with Heterogeneous-Distance Lattice Surgery

Authors: Daniel Dilley, Anastashia Jebraeilli, Rayat Roy, Shobhit Gupta, Alvin Gonzales, Zain Saleem
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.26784v1

Summary. This paper proposes a resource-efficient method called heterogeneous-distance lattice surgery for fault-tolerant quantum computing across noisy links. By selectively enlarging the ancilla at the inter-QPU boundary while keeping data patches smaller, the authors significantly reduce physical qubit overhead compared to standard methods. Simulations show this approach maintains excellent error suppression even as link noise increases.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the scaling bottleneck in quantum computing by providing a resource-efficient error correction scheme for noisy, modular interconnects, which is crucial for realizing large-scale quantum processors.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is achieving fault-tolerant quantum operations across inter-QPU links, which are significantly noisier than local gates in distributed quantum computing architectures.

Main result. The proposed heterogeneous-distance lattice surgery (8-DAM) architecture maintains low logical error rates at high link noise while significantly reducing the physical qubit overhead compared to uniform-distance implementations.

Method. The authors introduce a novel lattice surgery protocol using an enlarged central ancilla and traveling stabilizers to suppress hook errors during merge/split operations between unequal-distance data patches.

Model / system. The model uses the rotated surface code implemented across modular Quantum Processing Units (QPUs) connected by noisy inter-QPU links. The core technique is the 8-DAM architecture, where the central ancilla is enlarged while data patches retain a smaller, fixed distance $d$.

Key observables. Logical-readout error rates, physical qubit overhead (qubit count), and the comparison of error suppression factors against uniform-distance lattice surgery.

Important parameters / regimes. Local depolarizing noise ($ ext{p}{ ext{local}}$), inter-QPU link noise ($ ext{p}$), and the specific distance parameters used in the heterogeneous construction (e.g., $3 ext{--}7 ext{--}3$).}

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that local QPU operations are fault-tolerant if the distance is high enough, and it specifically excludes modeling link-generation failures, leakage, or correlated link noise.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the geometry of the rotated surface code patches, the merge configurations for lattice surgery, and the resulting 8-DAM patch architecture, along with error rate comparisons for single and dual logical CNOT operations.

Paper structure. The paper reviews background on rotated surface codes and lattice surgery, presents the heterogeneous 8-DAM constructions and simulation results, and concludes by discussing the resource-efficient implications for fault-tolerant distributed quantum computing.

Abstract

Distributed quantum computing requires fault-tolerant operations across inter-QPU links that can be substantially noisier than local gates. Uniformly increasing code distance provides additional protection but also enlarges data patches used for local storage and computation. Here, we introduce distributed heterogeneous-distance lattice surgery using an eight-data-patch ancilla-mediated (8-DAM) architecture, which will be useful for near-term quantum devices with less qubit overhead. In this architecture, the central ancilla spanning the inter-QPU boundary is enlarged while the data patches retain distance $d$. The protocol uses traveling stabilizers to suppress hook errors during merge and split operations between these unequal-distance patches. Circuit-level simulations of rotated surface codes at fixed local depolarizing noise show that logical-readout error rates depend only weakly on link noise. The resulting advantage over conventional lattice surgery grows as link errors increase. Comparisons with uniform distance implementations demonstrate comparable logical error suppression with reduced physical-qubit overhead. We also demonstrate how 8-DAM layouts support two simultaneous distributed logical CNOT operations between four logical data qubits using a single enlarged ancilla. At higher link noise, this construction yields lower logical error rates and more stability than two independent distributed logical CNOTs. These results support selective ancilla enlargement as a resource-efficient approach to fault-tolerant distributed quantum computing.

Exploring Asymmetric QEC Code Concatenation

Authors: Sayam Sethi, Maxwell Poster, Aditi Awasthi, Willers Yang, Joshua Viszlai, Jonathan Mark Baker
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.25251v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper investigates how to improve concatenated quantum error-correcting codes by mitigating the inherent asymmetry between X and Z error channels. The authors propose and test six specific 'deformation' strategies applied to the building block code. They demonstrate that careful deformation can balance the X and Z logical error rates while improving the overall error suppression capability.

Why it may be interesting. The work directly addresses resource optimization in quantum error correction by providing concrete, structured methods (deformations) to mitigate systematic biases in logical error rates, which is crucial for scaling quantum computation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Concatenated Quantum Error-Correcting Codes (QECC) suffer from logical error rate (LER) skewness between X and Z observables due to the inherent asymmetry of the building block code.

Main result. One proposed deformation strategy successfully achieves near-identical X and Z LERs while simultaneously reducing the total LER in a logical-memory experiment.

Method. The authors formalize the space of Clifford-deformations (Hadamard deformations) and compare six concrete strategies using a concatenated maximum-likelihood soft-information decoder.

Model / system. The study focuses on concatenating the [[4, 1, 2]] Iceberg code. The analysis uses a phenomenological noise model encompassing both unbiased (depolarizing) and biased noise channels.

Key observables. Logical Error Rates ($ ext{LER}_X$ and $ ext{LER}_Z$), Total LER, and Code Capacity.

Important parameters / regimes. Concatenation level ($l$), physical error rate ($p$), and the specific deformation strategy employed.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the maximum-likelihood decoder applied level-by-level, and the search space for optimal deformations grows doubly exponentially.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the Iceberg code structure, the six deformation strategies, and the resulting LER performance under unbiased and biased noise across multiple concatenation levels.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem of X/Z LER imbalance in concatenated codes, formalizes the deformation space, proposes and compares six specific deformation strategies, and evaluates their performance using simulation under various noise models.

Abstract

Concatenated quantum error-correcting codes have recently gained popularity because they improve the effective distance of a joint code without requiring the discovery of new codes with desirable parameters. A common building block for such studies is the $[[4,1,2]]$ Iceberg code, the smallest error-detecting code and therefore the one with the lowest resource overhead. However, it has an asymmetric number of $X$ and $Z$ checks, and concatenating it produces skewed logical error rates (LERs) between the $X$ and $Z$ observables. This asymmetry can be mitigated by Clifford-deforming the individual blocks of the concatenation, but the space of possible Hadamard deformations grows doubly exponentially with the number of concatenation levels. We formalize this deformation space and investigate it by proposing six concrete deformation strategies, which we compare under a code capacity (with and without bias) noise model using a concatenated maximum-likelihood soft-information decoder. Among the strategies we study, one one of them achieves near-identical $X$ and $Z$ LERs in a logical-memory experiment while simultaneously reducing the total LER (the sum of the $X$ and $Z$ error rates).

Abstract

We construct quantum locally testable codes (LTCs) with constant rate, distance, soundness and locality under a variant of a product expansion conjecture of Bafna and Vyas about Reed-Solomon codes. In particular, we use the high-dimensional expansion framework of Dinur, Lin and Vidick for constructing quantum LTCs, instantiated with the non-Abelian cubical complexes of Rungtanapirom, Stix and Vdovina. Our code is obtained by equipping the complex with carefully chosen Reed-Solomon local codes whose symmetries are compatible with those of the complex.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing a core result (1-round quantum coloring of cycles) to generalizing this to all LCLs. The mathematical development involves constructing the necessary quantum operators via iterative methods and proving their convergence and positivity.

Abstract

We present a distributed quantum algorithm that $3$-colors cycles in $O(1)$ rounds, with high probability. It follows that all locally checkable labeling problems (LCLs) that have round complexity $O(\log^* n)$ in the classical LOCAL model can be solved in $O(1)$ rounds in the quantum-LOCAL model, with high probability; this includes problems such as maximal independent set and maximal matching in bounded-degree graphs. This presents the first natural examples of graph problems with an asymptotic distributed quantum advantage for the LOCAL model; all prior examples that separate LOCAL and quantum-LOCAL are artificial problems constructed merely for the sake of demonstrating quantum advantage.

Quantum Broadcast Channels with Mutually Confidential Messages

Authors: Paula Belzig, Sukanya Ghosal, Farzin Salek, Graeme Smith
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.26750v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work derives the inner bound for the confidential classical capacity of quantum broadcast channels, ensuring that two transmitted messages remain secret from each other. Using advanced information theory, the authors prove that for certain channels, the achievable confidential rates are strictly lower than what standard quantum capacity measures predict. This highlights the crucial role of mutual secrecy constraints in quantum communication theory.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous characterization of confidential capacity regions and the explicit separation from standard quantum capacity bounds provide deep insights into the fundamental security limits of quantum communication protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper studies the fundamental limits of transmitting two independent, mutually confidential classical messages over a quantum broadcast channel.

Main result. It establishes an inner bound on the confidential classical capacity region, and demonstrates that for certain channels, this achievable rate region lies outside the unassisted quantum-capacity region.

Method. The authors employ advanced information-theoretic techniques, including establishing Marton-type inner bounds, using likelihood-selected coding schemes, and applying complex multi-letter capacity theorems.

Model / system. The system modeled is a Quantum Broadcast Channel (CQ channel) designed to transmit two messages ($M_1, M_2$) such that each message remains secret from the other receiver, even when the other receiver knows its own message.

Key observables. Achievable rate pairs $(R_1, R_2)$, average error probability ($P_e^{(i)}$), and conditional strong-secrecy parameters ($\Delta_{1,n}, \Delta_{2,n}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis depends on the block length $n o \infty$ limit, and the specific structure of the quantum channel (e.g., Blackwell or Platypus channels).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the existence of finite auxiliary variables and the use of normalized likelihood weights for encoding.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the confidential-message model for cq channels, showing the two secrecy conditions. Figure 2 illustrates the likelihood selection process for fixed indices.

Paper structure. The paper develops the inner bound by first establishing the classical Marton-type bound, then providing a concrete classical-quantum realization using likelihood-selected coding, and finally comparing this confidential classical capacity to the unassisted quantum capacity.

Abstract

We study the transmission of two independent confidential classical messages over a quantum broadcast channel, one for each receiver. Each message must remain secret from the other receiver, including when that receiver knows its own message. For classical inputs and quantum outputs, we establish the classical Marton-type inner bound under average reliability and conditional strong secrecy. The encoder selects pairs of codewords from independently generated codebooks using normalized likelihood weights. We prove reliability through a change-of-distribution argument. Our main technical result is a bipartite classical-quantum resolvability theorem that accounts for the dependence created by pair selection and establishes secrecy for the same encoder. We also obtain a multi-letter capacity characterization and extend it to arbitrary quantum inputs under secrecy against the other receiver, together with the Stinespring environment. We recover confidential capacity regions for deterministic classical and degraded classical-quantum channels, with an explicit evaluation for the classical Blackwell channel. For coherent isometric extensions of injective deterministic classical broadcast channels, we show that the confidential classical capacity region equals that of the corresponding classical channel. We then compare confidential classical communication with quantum transmission. For the coherent isometric extension of the Blackwell channel, we determine the unassisted quantum-capacity region and show that some achievable confidential classical rate pairs lie outside it. The Platypus channel provides another example of this separation.

Summary. This paper introduces an advanced Schrödingerization algorithm to solve quantum linear systems by transforming the problem into an inhomogeneous convection equation. By employing Duhamel's principle and Fourier projection, the authors achieve an optimal query complexity of $O(\kappa_A \log(1/\epsilon))$. This represents a significant algorithmic improvement for quantum computation tasks involving linear system inversion.

Why it may be interesting. The use of Schrödingerization to solve linear systems is a powerful technique for quantum simulation, offering a potentially superior scaling compared to older methods by avoiding separate LCU steps.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops a novel, efficient quantum algorithm to solve quantum linear systems of equations (QLSPs), aiming to prepare a state proportional to the solution $x = A^{-1}b$.

Main result. The proposed Schrödingerization algorithm achieves a query complexity of $O(\kappa_A \log(1/\epsilon))$ queries, matching the standard worst-case scaling for solving QLSPs.

Method. It reformulates the problem using Duhamel's principle to create an inhomogeneous convection equation, which is then solved via a two-dimensional Schrödingerization process involving Fourier projection and an auxiliary variable.

Model / system. The core model is the quantum linear system $Ax=b$, where $A$ is an invertible matrix. The method is tested on various systems, including positive-definite and indefinite matrices.

Key observables. Solution error ($\ell_2$ state error $\le \epsilon$), recovery probability ($P_p$), and the condition number ($\kappa_A$).

Abstract

We investigate an optimal scheme for the detection of single microwave photons by a Josephson junction through the analysis of its switching times distribution. The proposed analysis is of support for the decision about the existence of the photon field, which is important in the case of rare events. We assume that the cavity and the transmission line are ideal (each photon absorbed to the cavity gives a current pulse as the output of the transmission line) and the photon source is periodic. The employed methodology consists in comparing the switching probabilities of a Josephson junction exposed to a train of current pulses, simulating a weak photon field, with that of the same device in absence of pulses. In both cases, thermal noise can induce thermal activated switchings. The investigation of the unbalance in the number of switching events in the two cases, gives an estimate of the efficiency of the detection. Furthermore, in the assumption of escapes described by Kramers model, it is possible to provide a relationship between the properties of the photons field, the quantum efficiency of the detection process, and the Josephson junctions switching features at finite temperatures.

Abstract

The phenomenon of bias-driven circular charge and spin currents in a ring nanojunction is investigated in the presence of electron-electron (e-e) and electron-phonon (e-ph) interactions within a tight-binding framework based on the non-equilibrium Green's function formalism. The Lang-Firsov transformation is employed to map the interacting system onto an effective electronic model, which is subsequently treated within the Hartree-Fock mean-field scheme. By exploring the interplay among e-e interaction, e-ph coupling, and electrode-ring interface sensitivity, several intriguing features emerge in both circular charge and spin currents that, to the best of our knowledge, have not been reported previously. In addition to bias-driven circular currents, charge and spin-dependent junction currents through the nanojunction are also analyzed. Selective spin transport is achieved, leading to a high degree of spin polarization. All four current components, two associated with circular currents and two with transport currents, are systematically inspected over a wide range of parameter regimes to assess the sensitivity of the results to the relevant system parameters. Our findings provide useful insights into charge and spin transport phenomena in interacting nanojunctions with single- and multi-loop geometries.
