Scaling of qubit coherence in quantum dot based Majorana chains: Rabi and Ramsey oscillations of Majorana qubits formed by four and six quantum dots

Authors: Haining Pan, Sankar Das Sarma

Summary. This theoretical study analyzes the coherence scaling of Majorana qubits built on quantum dot chains by examining Rabi and Ramsey oscillations for varying chain lengths. The authors find that while longer chains might improve quantum memory, the coherence time for qubit manipulation is not universally enhanced by increasing the number of dots due to complex disorder-induced leakage channels. The results highlight that coherence performance is critically dependent on precise parameter tuning rather than just system size.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the fundamental challenge of scaling quantum hardware: demonstrating that increasing physical size (more dots) translates into improved coherence for practical quantum computation protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how the coherence time (Rabi and Ramsey) of Majorana-based qubits scales as the number of quantum dots ($N$) increases in artificial Kitaev chains.

Main result. Increasing the chain length ($N$) does not guarantee better qubit performance; while longer chains might be better for quantum memory, the coherence enhancement is highly sensitive to disorder and specific manipulation protocols.

Method. The authors employ theoretical modeling, deriving effective Hamiltonians and calculating coherence decay rates by analyzing leakage channels in both the pristine and disordered limits.

Model / system. The system is a double $N$-site quantum-dot artificial Kitaev chain, designed to host Majorana zero modes. The physics is governed by a generalized Hamiltonian incorporating site energies, tunneling terms, and disorder.

Key observables. Rabi coherence time ($T_{2, ext{Rabi}}$) and Ramsey coherence time ($T_{2, ext{Ramsey}}$) oscillations, and the associated leakage probabilities ($P_{ ext{leak}}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Chain length ($N$), superconducting gaps, detuning ($\epsilon$), and the strength of disorder fluctuations.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis compares results for pristine limits versus realistic disorder, and the derivation of dephasing envelopes relies on Gaussian disorder assumptions.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate Rabi and Ramsey oscillations for $N=2$ and $N=3$ qubits in the pristine limit, comparing numerical and analytical results, and showing leakage probabilities.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining the physical system and Hamiltonian, deriving the effective $2 imes 2$ block Hamiltonians for parity sectors, calculating the decay mechanisms for Rabi and Ramsey sequences, and finally analyzing the scaling effects of disorder.

Abstract

We present a theoretical study of the Rabi and Ramsey coherence time of Majorana-based qubits formed by double $N$-site quantum-dot artificial Kitaev chains, following the recent experimental realization arXiv:2607.09511. We generalize the qubit to two chains of arbitrary length and compare the Rabi and Ramsey oscillations of the four-dot and six-dot qubits, simulated with realistic disorder for three sets of parameters: small and large superconducting gaps, and the experimental parameters. We find that, in the pristine limit, the Rabi oscillation is independent of the chain length, whereas the Ramsey oscillation of the six-dot qubit suffers from larger leakage, because the third site suppresses the unwanted energy splitting away from the sweet spot but at the same time makes the deliberate splitting difficult to achieve, which requires a larger detuning that could excite the bulk. In reality with disorder, neither coherence time improves universally with the chain length. The Rabi coherence is set by the competition of three dephasing channels, and the additional dots pay off only if the fluctuation of the interchain tunneling is suppressed; the Ramsey coherence is set by the fluctuation of the energy splitting during the $σ_z$ rotation, which for the four-dot qubit is fixed by the sweet-spot disorder alone and does not depend on the mean splitting, whereas for the six-dot qubit the detuning that generates the splitting adds a fluctuation growing with the splitting itself, so the additional dots pay off only for small splittings. This implies that a longer chain is thus a better quantum memory but not necessarily a better qubit under manipulation. Although topological protection is expected in a longer Kitaev chain qubit, observing it in the current Rabi and Ramsey measurement protocols remains an experimental challenge.

Through numerical simulation and stability analysis, the authors demonstrate that even simple systems can exhibit rich spatiotemporal chaos due to nonlinear interactions.

Main problem. The paper investigates the complex routes to chaos and pattern formation in a mass-conserving two-species reaction-diffusion system.

Main result. The system can exhibit rich spatiotemporal behavior, including period-doubling cascades and low-dimensional spatiotemporal chaos, even when primary bifurcations are restricted to the Cahn-Hilliard type.

Method. The analysis combines linear stability analysis (identifying Cahn-Hilliard and Allen-Cahn-Hopf instabilities) with numerical continuation and direct time simulations to map out bifurcation structures.

Model / system. The model is a mass-conserving reaction-diffusion system for two species, governed by coupled partial differential equations with a specific reaction term $f(u, v)$. Mass conservation ($\partial ho / \partial t = abla^2 \eta$) is a defining feature.

Key observables. Order parameter $M(t)$, Normalized Peak Asymmetry ($\Phi$), Normalized Flip Ratio ($\mathcal{F}$), and the identification of different instability types (CH, ACH).

Important parameters / regimes. Diffusion constants ($d_u, d_v$), reaction constants ($a_1, a_2, \alpha$), and the total mean density $ar{ ho}$.

Figures summary. Stability diagrams map out the conditions for instability based on system invariants, showing regions for Cahn-Hilliard and Allen-Cahn-Hopf instabilities.

Paper structure. The paper revisits the system by first analyzing linear stability to identify potential instabilities (CH, ACH). It then investigates the nonlinear dynamics, focusing on the case where only CH instabilities occur, leading to the study of secondary bifurcations and the emergence of chaos.

Abstract

Mass-conserving reaction-diffusion systems with two species correspond to a seemingly simple case where pattern formation occurs under the influence of a conservation law. Here, we first revisit their linear stability behavior and point out that generically two instabilities can occur: a stationary large-scale mass-conserving (Cahn-Hilliard) instability and an instability that combines features of a stationary large-scale non-mass-conserving (Allen-Cahn) instability and a oscillatory large-scale mass-conserving (conserved-Hopf) instability. We term it an Allen-Cahn-Hopf instability. Second, we investigate the nonlinear dynamics for a specific model related to the formation of cell polarization where only a Cahn-Hilliard instability can occur, i.e., all primary bifurcations are stationary. We analyze how secondary and further bifurcations subsequently give rise to various oscillatory states. The emerging rich spectrum of spatiotemporal behavior includes several period-doubling cascades related to different forms of spatial and temporal symmetry breaking. Beside regular states, three types of low-dimensional spatiotemporal chaos occur and involve transitions like fusion and an outer crises. Our results demonstrate the importance of nonlinear interactions in the dynamics of mass-conserving reaction-diffusion systems, and show that even a simple two-species system with primary bifurcations of Cahn-Hilliard type can show complex spatiotemporal behavior.

Summary. This theoretical study investigates whether electron-electron correlations are the primary cause of the Chirality Induced Spin Selectivity (CISS) effect in molecular junctions. Using the NEGF method with Hubbard atoms, the authors found that while correlations are important, they alone cannot account for the large spin polarization factors seen in experiments. The results suggest that other mechanisms, beyond simple correlation effects, must be at play.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses spin transport in nanoscale systems using advanced many-body techniques (NEGF, SBA), which is highly relevant to open quantum systems and molecular spintronics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to determine the specific role of electronic correlation effects in explaining the Chirality Induced Spin Selectivity (CISS) effect in molecular junctions.

Main result. The calculations found very little spin polarization, suggesting that while electron-electron interactions are important for spin selectivity, they cannot be solely responsible for the high polarization factors observed experimentally.

Method. The Non-Equilibrium Green's Function (NEGF) approach is employed, treating correlation effects using the self-consistent second Born approximation.

Model / system. The physical system modeled is a chain of Hubbard atoms arranged in a helical geometry, sandwiched between two leads, one of which is magnetic.

Key observables. Spin polarization (SP) factor, total currents for different spin directions, and the distribution of spin populations on the chain atoms.

Important parameters / regimes. The ratio of the Hubbard constant to the hopping parameter ($U/t$), and the strength of spin-orbit coupling (SOC).

Assumptions / limitations. The interaction with the leads is treated within the Wide Band Approximation, and correlation effects are systematically included up to the second Born approximation.

Figures summary. Not specified.

Paper structure. The paper systematically applies the NEGF formalism to model spin transport through a chiral molecular junction, comparing results for L and D enantiomers while varying the correlation strength ($U/t$).

Abstract

The Non-Equilibrium Green's Function (NEGF) approach is applied to a chain of Hubbard atoms in a helical geometry sandwiched between two leads, one of which is magnetic, to assess what role the electronic correlation effects within the molecule may play in explaining the Chirality Induced Spin Selectivity (CISS) effect. The correlation effects are treated at the level of the self-consistent second Born approximation. Two chiralities, Laevorotatory (L) and Dextrorotatory (D), of the helix, are discussed. Further, we account for spin-orbit coupling (SOC) within the chains using a simplified model of Kane and Mele that exploits next-nearest neighbour interactions intrinsic to a curved geometry. The interaction with the leads is considered within the Wide Band Approximation. We consider the ratio, U/t, of the Hubbard constant U to the hopping t between atomic sites along the chain of up to two, but found very little spin polarisation in our calculations. Even though the spin polarisation factor defined as a relative difference between the largest and smallest total current is found for both enantiomers practically the same, the orientation of the tip's polarisation required to reach the largest and smallest currents for the two enantiomers differed. It is also found that the qualitative agreement with observations can only be achieved if we account, self-consistently, for the second order of the perturbation theory. The distribution of the spin populations on the chain atoms for different U/t ratios is also discussed. This study concludes that even though accounting for electron-electron interactions are important in understanding spin selectivity within molecular junctions, they cannot be made solely responsible for the high spin polarisation factors observed in experiments.

Abstract

Artificial-intelligence (AI) agents are beginning to enter experimental laboratories, automating experiments and accelerating scientific discovery. Herein, we introduce an AI-driven workflow in which an AI agent performs multi-step, multi-day quantum transport measurements end-to-end. Summary. This work demonstrates that in AuPt superlattices, absorbed optical energy rapidly localizes into Pt due to its high electronic heat capacity, creating an interface stress imbalance. Abstract

Controlling the spatial distribution of absorbed optical energy is central to nanoscale photothermal chemistry, plasmonics, and ultrafast materials control. Here, we show that a metallic AuPt superlattice concentrates electronic energy in Pt within a few hundred femtoseconds, regardless of the initial energy distribution between the two constituents. Ultrafast X-ray diffraction follows this energy redistribution through the amplitude of a coherent 570 GHz superlattice phonon driven by the stress imbalance at the AuPt interfaces. Despite the nearly homogeneous absorption at 400 nm, the observed lattice motion is identical to that produced by 800 nm excitation, which is absorbed predominantly in Pt. This dissipation driven localization of energy arises from the large electronic heat capacity of Pt and rapid electronic transport through the superlattice, providing a route to femtosecond control of nanoscale energy distributions.

Observation of the magnetic spin Hall effect in a ferromagnet

Authors: Nicholas Davey-García, Jone Mencos, Luciano Bravo, Inge Groen, Luis E. Hueso, Andreas Berger, Fèlix Casanova

Summary. This work successfully identifies the Magnetic Spin Hall Effect (MSHE) in conventional ferromagnets using a lateral spin-valve geometry. By measuring the spin signal's dependence on magnetization reversal, the authors confirmed the MSHE's unique time-reversal-symmetry-odd character. This establishes the MSHE as a sizable, magnetically switchable spin-charge conversion mechanism comparable to established heavy metal effects.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on condensed matter magnetism, the ability to electrically isolate and quantify a symmetry-protected spin-charge conversion mechanism (MSHE) is highly relevant to understanding spin-orbit torque physics and spintronics, which often interfaces with quantum control concepts.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal was to experimentally identify the Magnetic Spin Hall Effect (MSHE) in conventional ferromagnets, as its detection had been previously elusive.

Main result. The MSHE was unequivocally identified by observing that the associated spin signal reverses sign upon magnetization reversal, confirming its time-reversal-symmetry-odd character. The measured magnetic spin Hall angle was found to be $(3.8 \pm 0.6)\%$.

Method. The study utilized a non-local lateral spin-valve (LSV) geometry to electrically measure spin signals, comparing the response to magnetization reversal against conventional spin Hall effects.

Model / system. The experiment uses perpendicularly magnetized (PMA) Co-based multilayer thin films integrated into a lateral spin-valve structure, allowing for precise control and measurement of spin transport phenomena.

Key observables. Non-local resistance ($\Delta R_{NL}$), MSHE/MISHE signals, and the magnetic spin Hall angle ($ heta_{MSH}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Magnetic spin Hall angle $ heta_{\mathrm{MSH}} = (3.8 \pm 0.6)\%$; spin diffusion length $\lambda_{PMA}^{\perp} = 0.31 \pm 0.06$ nm.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the use of LSV architectures to isolate the effect and relies on theoretical models to decouple the TRS-odd (MSHE) from the TRS-even (SHE) components.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate schematics comparing SHE and MSHE mechanisms, and show non-local spin signal measurements ($\Delta R_{NL}$) demonstrating the sign reversal characteristic of the MSHE upon magnetization reversal.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the theoretical difference between TRS-even (SHE) and TRS-odd (MSHE) components, details the fabrication and measurement setup using LSV, and presents the key results showing the distinct magnetic switching behavior of the MSHE signal.

Abstract

The conventional spin Hall effect generates spin currents whose flow direction, spin polarization, and driving electric field are mutually perpendicular. Magnetic order lifts this symmetry restriction and enables additional time-reversal-symmetry-odd components of the spin-conductivity tensor, giving rise to the magnetic spin Hall effect (MSHE). These components also couple the generated spin polarization to the magnetic order, providing a degree of control absent in the conventional spin Hall effect. Although the MSHE has been observed in antiferromagnets, its experimental identification in conventional ferromagnets has remained elusive. Here, using a non-local lateral spin-valve geometry, we electrically identify the MSHE and its reciprocal effect in a perpendicularly magnetized Co-based multilayer. Reversal of the multilayer magnetization reverses the MSHE and magnetic inverse spin Hall signals, revealing their time-reversal-symmetry-odd character and magnetization control. By contrast, the conventional spin Hall and inverse spin Hall signals measured in the same devices remain unchanged under magnetization reversal, consistent with their time-reversal-symmetry-even character. We obtain a magnetic spin Hall angle of $θ_{\mathrm{MSH}} = (3.8 \pm 0.6)\%$, comparable in magnitude to the spin Hall angle of heavy metals commonly used in spintronic devices, such as Pt. These results establish the MSHE as a sizable, magnetically switchable spin-charge interconversion mechanism in conventional ferromagnets.

Summary. This theoretical work calculates the magnetoelectric conductivity in nodal semimetals, focusing on the difference between dipole-like and monopole-like Berry curvature sources. Method. The calculations employ the semiclassical Boltzmann formalism within the relaxation-time approximation, evaluating contributions from Drude, BC, OMM, anomalous-Hall, and Lorentz-force terms.

Model / system. The study analyzes two primary models: the two-band Vortex Nodal-Ring (VNR) semimetal and the three-band Hopf Semimetal (HSM). Comparisons are drawn with Weyl semimetals (monopole sources) and other nodal systems.

Important parameters / regimes. Cubic order in the magnetic field ($B^3$), and the specific planar-Hall field configurations.

Assumptions / limitations. Calculations are performed using the semiclassical Boltzmann formalism and assume static, uniform electric and magnetic fields.

Abstract

We compute the linear magnetoelectric conductivity of a two-band vortex nodal-ring (VNR) semimetal and a three-band Hopf semimetal (HSM) within the semiclassical Boltzmann formalism in the relaxation-time approximation. We consider three inequivalent planar-Hall configurations, in which the electric ($\boldsymbol E$) and magnetic ($\boldsymbol B$) fields are oriented differently with respect to the nodal-ring plane and the BC-dipole axis. Unlike Weyl and multifold semimetals, where Berry-curvature (BC) flux originates from monopole-like sources, both systems host dipole-like BC sources and vanishing Chern numbers. Working consistently to cubic order in the magnetic field, we evaluate the Drude, BC, orbital-magnetic-moment (OMM), anomalous-Hall, and Lorentz-force contributions to the conductivity. In both systems, the dipolar BC and OMM generate odd powers of $|\boldsymbol B|$ in in-plane response channels that are symmetry-forbidden in systems with monopole-like BC sources. For the HSM, the internode-scattering contribution to the conductivity vanishes identically. By comparing the VNR with a $\mathcal{PT}$-broken gapped nodal ring and the HSM with a pseudospin-1 triple-point semimetal, we demonstrate that the dipolar nature of the BC gives rise to distinctive features in magnetoelectric response that are absent in semimetals with monopole-like BC flux.

Summary. This work develops a theoretical framework to electrically switch the Néel order in centrosymmetric altermagnets, overcoming the symmetry-imposed prohibition on linear current effects. Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge addressed is achieving deterministic electrical switching of the Néel order in centrosymmetric altermagnets, a process hindered because linear current-induced spin polarization is forbidden by inversion symmetry.

Main result. The authors establish a nonlinear electrical write-read scheme by showing that the interplay between the nonrelativistic staggered spin response and a SOC-induced uniform transverse component enables deterministic $180^\circ$ reversal of the Néel order.

Method. The study combines symmetry analysis using spin group theory to constrain nonlinear spin polarization, quantum kinetic calculations (density matrix evolution), and macro-spin simulations using the Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert equation.

Model / system. The research focuses on centrosymmetric altermagnets, using a minimal tight-binding model for $ ext{FeSb}_2$ as a concrete example. The physics is governed by the interplay between nonrelativistic spin-split bands and finite spin-orbit coupling (SOC).

Key observables. Néel vector reversal ($180^\circ$ switching), anomalous Hall response (for state readout), and the coexistence/interaction of uniform and staggered nonlinear spin polarization components.

Important parameters / regimes. The mechanism is critically dependent on the presence of finite SOC, which generates the necessary transverse component, and the electric field orientation, which controls the switching direction.

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically illustrates the mechanism: the nonrelativistic limit yields a torque-inactive, collinear response, while finite SOC introduces a transverse component, leading to a non-zero torque capable of driving reversal.

Abstract

Deterministic electrical switching of the Néel order remains challenging in centrosymmetric altermagnets, where linear current-induced spin polarization is forbidden by inversion symmetry. Here, we establish a nonlinear route to Néel order control. Spin group symmetry shows that, in the nonrelativistic limit, the nonlinear spin polarization is constrained along the Néel vector, with a staggered response allowed in $8$ of the $10$ nontrivial spin-Laue groups. Although this Néel spin polarization is torque-inactive by itself, finite spin-orbit coupling can generate an additional uniform component transverse to the Néel vector. Using a minimal model for the $d$-wave altermagnet FeSb$_2$, we demonstrate the coexistence of the nonrelativistic staggered response with an SOC-induced uniform transverse component. Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert equation-based macro-spin simulations reveal that the transverse component alone produces oscillatory dynamics, whereas its cooperative action with the staggered response enables deterministic $180^\circ$ reversal. We further show that changing the electric field orientation reverses the staggered spin response and selects the switching direction, while the opposite Néel states can be distinguished by their anomalous Hall response. Our results establish a nonlinear electrical write-read scheme for centrosymmetric altermagnets.

Main result. Bulk superconductivity is robustly stabilized within the rhombohedral R-3c phase, which is linked to strong electron-phonon coupling; this superconductivity is lost when the material undergoes a structural reconstruction to a lower-symmetry phase at higher pressures.

Method. The study combines high-pressure experimental techniques (X-ray diffraction, transport, susceptibility) with advanced theoretical calculations (DFT, DFPT) to map the structural and electronic phase diagram.

Model / system. The system is Rhenium trioxide (ReO3), an oxide perovskite, whose structural evolution (Pm-3m $ ightarrow$ Im-3 $ ightarrow$ R-3c $ ightarrow$ mP16-P2/c) and superconducting properties are tracked as pressure increases up to 80 GPa.

Key observables. Superconducting critical temperature ($T_c$), structural phases (e.g., $Rar{3}c$), electron-phonon coupling ($\lambda$), and lattice parameters/angles.

Important parameters / regimes. Pressure (up to 80 GPa), $T_c$ (max $\sim 17.5$ K), and the octahedral rotation angle ($ heta$).

Assumptions / limitations. The superconducting state is primarily governed by electron-phonon coupling, and structural transitions dictate the electronic properties.

Figures summary. Figures summarize the pressure dependence of $T_c$, resistance ratio, unit cell volume, and Raman modes across the different structural phases of ReO3.

Paper structure. The paper systematically maps the structural phase transitions of ReO3 under pressure, identifying the stable superconducting phase ($Rar{3}c$) and using DFT calculations to explain the enhancement of electron-phonon coupling within this specific lattice geometry.

Abstract

Understanding how lattice geometry enables superconductivity in oxides remains a central challenge. Here, we report a systematic study of ReO3 up to 80 GPa. Synchrotron X-ray diffraction, Raman spectroscopy, electrical transport, dc magnetic susceptibility, and first-principles calculations establish a sequence of pressure-induced structural transitions, from cubic Pm-3m to Im-3 followed by the emergence of a rhombohedral R-3c phase accompanied by bulk superconductivity with a maximum Tc, onset ~17.5 K. DC magnetic susceptibility and trapped-flux magnetization measurements demonstrate that bulk superconductivity is confined to the pressure range where R-3c phase is dominant. Density functional theory calculations show strong electron-phonon coupling in the hR24-R-3c structure, with substantial contributions from both low-frequency Re vibrations and high-frequency oxygen-related phonon modes, yielding a calculated Tc comparable with the experiment. Upon further compression above ~35-40 GPa, powder X-ray diffraction results indicate a symmetry-lowering structural transition. Whereas the experimental diffraction patterns can be best described by a rhombohedral-derived R32-like average distortion with effective enlargement of the crystallographic unit cell, enthalpy calculations identify a lower-symmetry mP16-P2/c structure driven by phonon instability of the R-3c phase. This reconstructed higher-coordination phase has a reduced density of states at the Fermi level, weaker electron-phonon coupling, and a much lower calculated Tc, providing a microscopic explanation for the loss of bulk superconductivity in the higher-pressure phase. These results show that bulk superconductivity is stabilized within the rhombohedral structure, where pressure-induced lattice reconstruction supports enhanced electron-phonon coupling through cooperative Re-O lattice dynamics.
