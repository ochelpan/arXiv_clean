Topological Recursion and Quantum Path Signatures

Authors: Inês Aniceto, Thomas Cass, Samuel Crew
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.02861v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5

Summary. This work develops a sophisticated mathematical machinery using a non-commutative Laplace transform to relate path space observables (quantum path signatures) to algebraic equations from random matrix theory. It successfully derives the full genus expansion of these loop equations, providing deep structural insights into quantum field theories governed by such spectral relations.

Why it may be interesting. While highly abstract, the techniques developed—relating geometric/path integrals (signatures) to formal power series and spectral curves—are powerful tools for analyzing quantum field theories in dimensions where standard methods fail.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper establishes a rigorous mathematical framework connecting path space analysis (quantum path signatures) to algebraic structures derived from random matrix theory via a non-commutative Laplace transform.

Main result. They successfully derive the genus-$g$ loop equations for the quantum path signature, confirming consistency across multiple computational methods up to genus one and providing a systematic expansion in $1/N$.

Method. The core method involves introducing and utilizing a non-commutative Laplace transform that maps convolution products on path space functionals (signatures) to algebraic products of formal power series.

Model / system. The analysis centers on the quantum path signature, derived from random unitary matrix-valued paths $U(\gamma)$, which is related to Wilson loops in gauge theory and planar limits of matrix models.

Key observables. Quantum Path Signature ($S$), Planar Wilson Line Observable ($W$), Genus-$g$ corrections ($W_g$), and the non-commutative resolvent ($R_\mu$).

Important parameters / regimes. The large-$N$ limit (planar limit), genus $g$, and path geometry $\gamma(t)$ are key parameters.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes factorial decay of the signature moments for the existence of the planar limit, and it notes that the full solution theory for interacting models remains open due to non-closure.

Figures summary. Not specified in detail, but the structure implies showing recursive relations (e.g., Eq. 66) and comparing results from different computational approaches (discretized loops vs. combinatorial forms).

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first defining the non-commutative Laplace transform to map path convolution to algebraic products; it then applies this to derive the planar loop equation; subsequently, it extends this framework to systematically calculate genus-$g$ corrections ($W_g$) via integral equations.

Abstract

We introduce a non-commutative Laplace transform between functionals on path space and formal series in a tensor algebra, under which a natural convolution of path functionals becomes an algebraic product of series. Applying it to a random unitary matrix-valued path development - the quantum path signature - we show that the governing planar loop equations take a non-commutative spectral form. We then extend the loop equations to a $1/N$ genus expansion, organised by topological recursion, and obtain a hierarchy of integral equations on path space for the corrections.

CNOT-Distance is NP-complete under all-to-all connectivity

Authors: Antonio Acuaviva, Arturo Acuaviva, Pablo Acuaviva
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.03825v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper proves that calculating the minimum number of CNOT gates required to implement a given linear transformation (CNOT-Distance) is an NP-complete problem. The authors achieve this by constructing specific matrices whose gate count exactly matches the size of the minimum vertex cover of a graph. This result establishes a strong theoretical lower bound on the complexity of quantum circuit synthesis.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides fundamental computational hardness results for quantum circuit synthesis, which is crucial for understanding the limits of implementing desired unitary operations using physical hardware constraints.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The problem addressed is determining if a given invertible linear transformation $A \in GL(N, 2)$ can be implemented using at most $K$ CNOT gates ($ ext{CNOT-Distance}$).

Main result. The authors prove that the $ ext{CNOT-Distance}$ problem is NP-complete under all-to-all connectivity.

Method. The proof relies on a reduction from Minimum Vertex Cover (an NP-complete problem) and involves analyzing the structure of CNOT circuits by mapping them to XOR directed acyclic graphs.

Model / system. The model is quantum circuit synthesis using only CNOT gates acting on fixed labeled wires, represented mathematically by linear transformations over $\mathbb{F}_2$.

Key observables. $\ell_{ ext{CNOT}}(A)$ (minimum gate count), $ au(G)$ (minimum vertex cover size).

Important parameters / regimes. $N$ (number of wires/qubits), $K$ (gate budget).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes all-to-all connectivity between any pair of distinct wires.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the NP-completeness by constructing a specific matrix $A_G$ derived from an arbitrary graph $G$, showing that $\ell_{ ext{CNOT}}(A_G)$ exactly encodes the minimum vertex cover size, thus linking circuit complexity to graph theory.

Abstract

Given $A\in\operatorname{GL}(N,2)$ and an integer $K$, we ask whether $A$ can be implemented by at most $K$ CNOT gates on fixed labelled wires with all-to-all connectivity. We prove that this problem is NP-complete. From a finite simple graph $G=(V,E)$, we construct an upper-unitriangular matrix $A_G\in\operatorname{GL}(2|V|+|E|+1,2)$ satisfying $\ell_{\mathrm{CNOT}}(A_G)=2|V|+2|E|+τ(G)$, where $τ(G)$ is the minimum vertex-cover size. Each target matrix has $O(N)$ nonzero entries and row Hamming weight at most four. The lower bound unfolds an arbitrary CNOT circuit into an XOR directed acyclic graph and applies projection--contraction operations, allowing cancellation and unrestricted reuse of intermediate parities. For this family, the optimum is unchanged by any finite number of clean or borrowed ancillary wires that must be restored. A polynomial-time decoder further yields NP-hardness of approximation within every fixed additive constant and, through an L-reduction from Minimum Vertex Cover on cubic graphs, APX-hardness of the associated CNOT-circuit optimisation problem.

Factorization of Exclusive-Sum-Of-Products Expressions with Rectangle Covering to Reduce Quantum Circuit Cost

Authors: Audrey Hou, Lucia Zhang, Ali Al-Bayaty, Marek Perkowski
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.03188v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper presents novel algorithms that factor complex logic expressions (ESOP) by mapping them onto 2D matrices and using rectangle covering techniques. This factorization allows for the creation of equivalent, but significantly simpler, quantum circuits. The resulting cost reductions in both classical gate counts and quantum circuit metrics are substantial, making it a key tool for optimizing quantum computation resource estimates.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on circuit minimization rather than physical Hamiltonian simulation, the techniques developed for efficient logical decomposition and resource estimation are fundamental to designing scalable quantum algorithms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is optimizing quantum circuit costs associated with implementing complex logic functions represented as Exclusive-Sum-Of-Products (ESOP) expressions.

Main result. The proposed factorization algorithms significantly reduce both the classical literal count and the quantum Maslov cost of realizing these circuits, achieving reductions between 20% and 95%.

Method. The method involves representing the ESOP expression as a 2D matrix and applying specialized 'Rectangle Covering methods' (Disjoint and Even-Odd) to find an equivalent, factored form.

Model / system. The model is abstract quantum circuit realization for AND/EXOR logic gates. Costs are measured using the Maslov cost for both quantum oracle and automaton circuits, which are built upon generalized n-bit Toffoli gates.

Key observables. Literal count (L(E)), Maslov cost for quantum automata (A(E)), and Maslov cost for quantum oracles (O(E)).

Important parameters / regimes. The complexity of the ESOP expression, which must be FPRM with cube degrees at most 2 for direct matrix representation.

Assumptions / limitations. For quantum oracle circuits, intermediate variables in factored forms require 'restoring' via mirroring gates to maintain equivalence with the original circuit structure.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate gate realizations (CNOT, Toffoli), comparisons of costs before and after factorization using matrix representations, and demonstrations of the disjoint rectangle covering process on these matrices.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem of ESOP cost optimization, details the matrix representation and the two rectangle covering algorithms (Disjoint and Even-Odd), presents comparative results across multiple benchmarks for literal count, automata cost, and oracle cost, and concludes with an analysis comparing algorithmic efficiency.

Abstract

The implementation of quantum circuits is currently very expensive, especially due to the usage of large Toffoli gates. Therefore, it is critical to optimize circuit costs by factoring expressions as they become more complex. In the proposed algorithms to factor ESOP expressions, each product term is converted into a cell in a 2D matrix, and optimal factored AND/EXOR solutions are determined using Disjoint and Even-Odd Rectangle covering methods. Two Python programs implementing these algorithms were tested and evaluated using well-known benchmarks. The results showed that both the literal counts used in classical logic circuits as well as the Maslov cost used in quantum circuits was reduced by 20%-95% depending on expression size.

Harvest: Resource-Aware Quantum Compilation for Magic State Protocols

Authors: Jannik Pflieger, Aleksandra Świerkowska, Emmanouil Giortamis, Pramod Bhatotia

Summary. This paper introduces Harvest, a novel compiler designed for fault-tolerant quantum computation utilizing lattice surgery. It solves the critical problem where scheduling, placement, and magic-state resource management cannot be optimized in isolation due to shared physical constraints. By co-optimizing these factors, Harvest achieves substantial speedups and dramatically improves hardware resource utilization.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on compilation rather than fundamental physics, the resource management challenges—optimizing spatial layout alongside temporal execution constraints—are highly relevant to designing physical implementations of quantum circuits using superconducting or trapped-ion architectures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core problem is developing a quantum compiler that can co-optimize placement, routing, scheduling, and magic-state supply for lattice-surgery operations in fault-tolerant quantum computation.

Main result. Harvest achieves significant performance gains on benchmarks, showing an average speedup of 4.83x (up to 17.8x) over sequential execution while reclaiming substantial unused physical resources.

Method. The authors propose HARVEST, a resource-aware compilation approach that uses a patch-level intermediate representation (H-IR) to jointly optimize scheduling with circuit-aware placement and congestion-aware routing.

Model / system. The system modeled is a fault-tolerant quantum processor using topological codes, where operations are executed via 'lattice surgery' on a 2D grid of physical patches that can hold data, magic states, or act as routes.

Key observables. Average speedup (e.g., 4.83x), schedule length improvement (up to 1.35x), and the percentage of reclaimed unused resources (e.g., up to 72.0% magic-state patches).

Important parameters / regimes. The optimization must jointly consider data dependencies, physical resource conflicts (ports/routes), and consumable non-Clifford resources (magic states).

Assumptions / limitations. Independent optimization of placement, routing, scheduling, or magic-state supply is insufficient because operations contend for shared physical resources like ports and routes.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the patch-level execution model showing data, routing, and magic-state patches; comparisons show speedup gains over naive serial execution by integrating resource awareness into scheduling.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the limitations of existing compilers, proposes the HARVEST framework with its H-IR, details the co-optimization pipeline (placement $ ightarrow$ scheduling $ ightarrow$ pruning), and validates it against standard quantum benchmark suites.

Abstract

Fault-tolerant quantum processors based on topological codes execute programs through lattice surgery, where operations must be mapped, routed, and supplied with magic states across a 2D grid of physical patches. Non-Clifford operations require these magic states, produced either by distillation factories or by cultivation, each trading footprint against preparation latency, and delivering a magic state to the data patches that consume it requires routing through the same shared layout as every other operation. Yet placement, routing, scheduling, and magic-state supply cannot be optimized in isolation: two operations with no circuit-level dependency can still contend for the same ports, routes, or magic-state terminals once placed, so a compiler that decouples instruction scheduling from magic-state generation, or hard-codes a single generation protocol, is forced to trade execution time against layout footprint instead of co-optimizing both across protocols. We present Harvest, a resource-aware compilation approach for lattice-surgery that co-optimizes magic-state consumption with circuit-aware placement and congestion-aware routing under a protocol-agnostic resource model, then reclaims unused layout footprint after scheduling. Across standard benchmark suites (QAOA, QFT, QASMBench), Harvest achieves an average speedup of $4.83\times$ (up to $17.8\times$) over sequential execution, improves schedule length by up to $1.35\times$ through circuit-aware placement, and reclaims up to $72.0\%$ of unused magic-state patches and $33.9\%$ of unused routing patches.

Abstract

Summary. This paper develops and applies an adaptation of the Iterative Linear Quadratic Regulator (iLQR) tailored specifically to the Lie-group structure of SU(N). By incorporating this geometric constraint into the optimization process, the authors demonstrate improved convergence speed for quantum gate synthesis compared to standard methods. The findings highlight the value of group theory in enhancing optimal control algorithms for multi-qubit systems.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous mathematical framework for optimizing quantum controls by respecting the underlying group structure (SU(N)), which is crucial for developing scalable, physically constrained gate synthesis protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is designing complex multi-qubit quantum gates using Quantum Optimal Control (QOC) theory.

Main result. Incorporating the Lie-group geometry into the iLQR formulation accelerates convergence compared to Euclidean methods in unconstrained settings, though it increases sensitivity to initialization under constraints.

Method. The authors adapt the Iterative Linear Quadratic Regulator (iLQR) by formulating updates and cost functions directly on the Special Unitary group SU(N), comparing this Lie-group approach against standard Euclidean iLQR.

Model / system. The system involves quantum dynamics evolving on the special unitary group SU(N), where control is achieved by applying time-dependent Hamiltonian terms (drive fields) to synthesize target gates.

Key observables. Convergence speed, gate fidelity, and the resulting infidelities of synthesized gates.

Important parameters / regimes. The comparison hinges on two regimes: an idealized unconstrained setting versus a constrained setting limited to 2-local Hamiltonian terms.

Paper structure. The paper builds mathematically by deriving necessary Taylor expansions on Lie groups, detailing the iLQR update equations for SU(N), and then presenting numerical results comparing the convergence of the Lie-group vs. Euclidean formulations under varying constraints.

Abstract

In quantum optimal control theory, gradient-based trajectory optimization techniques have proven versatile in designing multi-qubit quantum gates. Furthermore, incorporating the underlying Lie-group structure can accelerate the optimization process. In this work, we adapt the Lie-group formulation of the iterative linear quadratic regulator (iLQR) to the special unitary group SU(N) and apply it to quantum gate synthesis, systematically comparing it against the standard Euclidean iLQR formulation across multiple two- to five-qubit gates. We find that in the idealized, unconstrained setting, where all Lie-algebra basis elements are available as drive Hamiltonian terms, the Lie-group formulation converges faster than the Euclidean iLQR formulation. If drive terms are constrained to 2-local Hamiltonian terms, the Lie-group variant converges faster in early optimization iterations, but exhibits greater sensitivity to initialization and a stronger tendency towards local minima. These results demonstrate that incorporating Lie-group geometry into iLQR substantially improves convergence and highlight important next steps for improvements in constrained control settings.

Optimal and Deterministic Quantum Search on the Simplex of Complete Graphs

Authors: Kiyoji Huang Fujiwara, Yujia Shi, Thomas G. Wong
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.03777v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper tackles the problem of achieving optimal and deterministic quantum search on the weighted simplex of complete graphs. The authors provide novel analytical proofs demonstrating that specific bridge weight conditions ($w=M$) guarantee both asymptotic optimality ($\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{N})$) and determinism for the quantum walk algorithm. It is significant because it rigorously characterizes the relationship between graph structure, edge weights, and search complexity.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides rigorous theoretical bounds on quantum search complexity ($\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{N})$) for a complex network structure, offering deep insights into how local structural properties (like weights) dictate global algorithmic efficiency in quantum computation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is to analytically prove optimal and deterministic quantum search algorithms for a specific graph structure: the simplex of complete graphs.

Main result. The authors provide the first analytical proof that optimal quantum search occurs when the bridge weight equals $M$, and they establish conditions ($\sqrt{M} \leq w \leq M$) under which the search is both asymptotically optimal and deterministic.

Method. The research combines analytical proofs using continuous-time quantum walk Hamiltonians with numerical demonstrations across various parameter regimes (bridge weights).

Model / system. The system is modeled as a weighted simplex of complete graphs, containing $N = M(M+1)$ vertices. The search dynamics are governed by a time evolution operator derived from a specific Hamiltonian involving the adjacency matrix and bridge weights ($w$).

Key observables. Search runtime (compared to $\sqrt{N}$), success probability, and the probability distribution across basis states $|a angle$ (marked vertex) and neighboring vertices.

Important parameters / regimes. The weight of the bridges ($w$) relative to $M$, specifically comparing regimes like $w=M$, $w=\sqrt{M}$, and $\sqrt{M} \ll w \ll M$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis focuses on the single-marked vertex case, and it explicitly shows that graph connectivity measures (like algebraic connectivity) are unreliable indicators of fast search performance in this family.

Figures summary. Figures summarize search results across different weights $w$, showing runtime vs. weight scaling; Figure 3 details probability overlaps and runtime for specific weight scalings ($w=M^\alpha$).

Paper structure. The paper progresses by establishing the physical model, analyzing the quantum walk Hamiltonian in reduced subspaces using perturbation theory, deriving optimal rates for various weight regimes, and finally comparing search performance against graph connectivity metrics.

Abstract

The simplex of complete graphs, also known as the first-order truncated simplex lattice, is a network of $M+1$ identical complete graphs, each with $M$ vertices, such that each clique contains an edge or bridge to every other clique. It contains $N = M(M+1)$ vertices, and previous asymptotic results using a continuous-time quantum walk to search this graph for a single marked vertex have either numerically demonstrated an optimal runtime of $O(\sqrt{N})$, or analytically proved a deterministic success probability of 1, but not both, even when the bridges are weighted. In this paper, we give the first analytical proof of optimal quantum search on this graph, proving that it occurs when the weight of the bridges equals $M$. In addition, we numerically show that the optimal runtime is achieved more broadly whenever the weight is at least $\sqrt{M}$. Furthermore, the algorithm is also deterministic when the weight scales between $\sqrt{M}$ and $M$, and this is the first example of quantum search on the simplex of complete graphs that is both asymptotically optimal and deterministic. In addition, for weights where the algorithm is nondeterministic, we give a way to find the marked vertex by inspecting neighboring vertices. Finally, while it is known that connectivity is not a reliable indicator of fast quantum search when comparing different graph families, we show that it is also unreliable within the graph family of weighted simplex of complete graphs.

Abstract

Quantum algorithms based on linear-system approaches for solving differential equations demand qubit and precision resources beyond near-term capabilities. To address these challenges, this work proposes a physics-informed quantum machine learning (PIQML) framework with hard constraint embedding, specifically designed for NISQ era. Within this framework, parameterized quantum circuits serve as machine learning models, where the input variable is encoded into a high-dimensional feature space via a Fourier feature map. Subsequently, to eliminate approximation errors in critical physical conditions, the solution is constructed through a rigorously designed function mapper that analytically enforces initial conditions as hard constraints. Crucially, we compute derivatives with respect to the input variable using the parameter-shift rule---a quantum native gradient evaluation technique that avoids classical discretization. Unlike generic loss functions that target abstract data patterns, our loss function focuses on the differential equation residual and reference data. This design ensures that the trained model not only approximates the data but also intrinsically satisfies the physical constraint expressed by the DE itself. Our method is validated on several differential equations, including highly oscillatory ones, demonstrating its capability to tackle challenging nonlinear dynamics. Results demonstrate that our quantum model successfully learns the solution, showing close agreement with a high-precision classical numerical benchmark.

Summary. This paper rigorously proves that low-depth quantum circuits possess computational capabilities fundamentally beyond those achievable by modern large language models (Transformers and DLMs). Model / system. The models compared are low-depth quantum circuits ($ ext{QNC}_0$) and classical neural network architectures, specifically decoder-only Transformers and Diffusion Language Models. The analysis focuses on their computational capacity under shallow depth constraints.

Abstract

Modern large language models - transformers and diffusion language models - are built around two canonical algorithmic tasks: prediction and generation. We prove unconditional separations between low-depth quantum computation and the corresponding bounded-resource classical language-model architectures in both regimes. Concretely, we exhibit the following: 1. Distributional separation. We give a distribution that is sampleable by $\textsf{QNC}^0$ circuits (i.e., a family of constant-depth quantum circuits consisting of bounded fan-in gates) that no constant-round diffusion language model ($\textsf{DLM}$) with shallow scheduling and denoising can sample within constant distance, even when allowed sublinear chain-of-thought and output-token revision/remasking events, the very features modern $\textsf{DLM}$s rely on. 2. Functional separation. We exhibit a function computable in $\land \circ \textsf{QNC}^0[\log\log n]$ (i.e., a family of O$(\log\log n)$-depth $\textsf{QNC}^0$ circuits, where $n$ is the input length, followed by a single classical $\mathsf{AND}$ gate) such that any constant-depth decoder-only transformer computing the function must be large: it would have to have width $n^{Ω(1)}$. Together, our work initiates the study of quantum advantage in the era of large language models.

Abstract

The superconducting diode effect (SDE) is a fascinating nonreciprocal phenomenon where the critical current is different for opposite current directions. It is widely believed that realizing SDE requires breaking both inversion symmetry (IS) and time-reversal symmetry (TRS), which are usually achieved via heterostructure engineering and applying external magnetic fields. Abstract

We explore the possibility that the two-phonon pairing mechanism proposed by Ngai can explain superconductivity in doped strontium titanate (STO). The two-phonon deformation potential is evaluated using two distinct theoretical estimates based on first-principles calculations and two empirical estimates based on experimental data, yielding very large values of the same order of magnitude. We derive the effective electron-electron interaction mediated by two-phonon exchange. Crucial to our computations is the strong dispersion of the involved soft phonons. Because the scale of the two-phonon interaction is much larger than the Fermi energy, the Migdal theorem does not apply. We obtain a one-loop Eliashberg equation that can be solved in the weak-coupling limit. We find that despite the significant electron-phonon matrix element, phase space considerations considerably reduce the effective coupling. A two-phonon mechanism can not be excluded based on the $T_c$ magnitude, but its value is dominated by the high-energy-frequency physics and is weakly affected by the soft-phonon behavior. The theory predicts a $T_c$ that monotonously increases with doping, which is not in accordance with the experiment. We identify the conditions for a two-phonon mechanism to be effective in other materials and discuss possible candidates.
