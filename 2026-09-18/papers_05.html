Comparing magic state cultivation methods using matrix product states

Authors: Tom Hartweg, Asier Piñeiro Orioli
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.19116v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: methods for driven-dissipative 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work compares multiple methods for preparing essential magic states for fault-tolerant quantum computation using surface codes. By employing advanced simulation techniques like MPS, the authors demonstrate a new cultivation scheme that significantly reduces resource overhead while maintaining high fidelity. The findings provide crucial benchmarks for optimizing quantum error correction protocols against physical noise and resource constraints.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous comparison of simulation techniques (MPS vs. CAMPS vs. Cliﬀt) to determine the most efficient path for resource estimation in fault-tolerant quantum computation is highly relevant for theoretical quantum information scientists.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper compares various methods for cultivating high-fidelity magic states required for fault-tolerant quantum computation, specifically addressing the performance limitations of non-Clifford circuits.

Main result. The proposed new cultivation scheme achieves a significantly lower expected space-time cost ($\sim 1.3 imes$) compared to existing protocols while maintaining a target logical error rate of $10^{-9}$.

Method. The authors use Matrix Product States (MPS) and Clifford-augmented MPS (CAMPS) to perform exact simulations of different fold-transversal cultivation schemes on surface codes.

Model / system. The study focuses on quantum error correction (QEC) using surface codes (regular and rotated) at distances $d=3$ and $d=5$. The process involves state preparation and subsequent cultivation stages.

Key observables. Logical error rate, expected space-time cost, and the multiplicative factor increase in expected attempts due to physical losses.

Important parameters / regimes. Code distance ($d=5$), target error rate ($10^{-9}$), and physical loss probabilities ($p_{ ext{loss}}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes losses are modeled by independent loss channels uncorrelated with depolarizing noise, and that MPS/CAMPS can accurately capture the required entanglement structure.

Figures summary. Figures compare the performance (error rate vs. attempts) of various schemes for $|S angle$ and $|T angle$ states at $d=3$ and $d=5$. Other figures detail gadget-level structures and space-time overhead breakdowns for different code types.

Paper structure. The paper systematically compares multiple cultivation schemes ($ ext{Sahay et al.}$ vs. proposed) across different code types ($ ext{Reg}$ vs. $ ext{Rot}$) and distances ($d=3, 5$), analyzing performance metrics like error rate and space-time cost, and finally quantifying the impact of physical losses.

Abstract

Magic state cultivation prepares high-fidelity magic states at low expected space-time costs; however, the exact performance of some schemes is unsettled due to the difficulty in simulating non-Clifford circuits. Here, we use matrix-product states (MPS) based methods to compute the exact performance of two types of fold-transversal cultivation schemes: (i) the Sahay et al method based on the regular surface code S gate, and (ii) a method we propose based on a partially fault-tolerant fold-transversal S gate. We show that for the former protocol at $d=5$, the $|T\rangle$ output reaches similar logical error rates to the $|S\rangle$ output, traditionally used as a cheap full Clifford proxy. This contrasts with the $\sim10\times$ discrepancy reported for the $d=5$ colour-code scheme of Gidney et al. We also find that our new $d=5$ scheme has $\sim1.3\times$ lower expected space-time cost while still reaching $10^{-9}$ logical error rate. We show that MPS and Clifford-augmented MPS (CAMPS) perform on par with or even better than the recently introduced near-Clifford simulator Clifft on the hardest $d=5$ regular surface code scheme. Additionally, to speed up simulation, we propose a new pre-screening method based on simple Pauli propagation, lowering by up to three orders of magnitude the required number of exact simulations, and use several simulator-agnostic sampling methods such as subset sampling.

Diagnosing and Restoring the Degraded Fault Distance of Magic State Cultivation

Authors: Tim Chan, Armands Strikis, Zhu Sun, Zhenyu Cai
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.17706v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper diagnoses why T-state magic state cultivation protocols suffer from a degraded fault distance compared to S-state protocols due to specific Pauli hook errors. The authors develop an advanced analytical framework and propose error-detecting 'flags' that successfully restore the fault tolerance, significantly lowering the logical error rate.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on abstract QEC, the rigorous error analysis and the development of detection flags are highly relevant to understanding noise resilience in any physical quantum system, including those studied in AMO physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the discrepancy in fault tolerance between T-state and S-state magic state cultivation protocols, where T-state cultivation exhibits a degraded fault distance.

Main result. By designing specific 'flags' to detect malignant Pauli hook errors, the pre-escape logical error rate for T-state cultivation can be significantly improved from $O(p^3)$ to $O(p^5)$.

Method. The authors employ analytical derivations, deriving general formulas and an $O(n^3)$-time algorithm to calculate the acceptance probability of logical mixed states afflicted with Clifford errors.

Model / system. The work analyzes quantum error correction circuits, specifically Magic State Cultivation (MSC) protocols using color codes (e.g., hexagonal color codes) to generate logical T and S states.

Key observables. Logical error rate (LER), fault distance, and the probability of observing a trivial syndrome ($pr(s=0)$).

Important parameters / regimes. Noise level ($p$), physical qubit count ($n$), and the required fault distance (e.g., distance-3 or -5).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes circuit-level depolarizing noise and relies on approximating the sum over undetectable fault configurations by considering only low-fault-count terms.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the comparison of logical error probabilities for T vs. S states at different distances, and show the circuit structure for the double-check mechanism and the improved flagged circuits.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first identifying the T-S discrepancy, then analytically deriving the error propagation mechanisms involving Pauli hook errors and Clifford errors. It introduces an algorithm for calculating syndrome probabilities and concludes by designing and analyzing error-detecting flags to restore the fault distance.

Abstract

T-state cultivation is a resource-efficient protocol producing logical T states but recent benchmarks show that its logical error rate is considerably higher than intended i.e. than S-state cultivation, which is the analogous protocol for producing logical S states. In this paper, we explain this T-S discrepancy by analytically showing, under circuit-level depolarising noise, that distance-3 (-5) T-state cultivation has fault distance 2 (3) due to Pauli hook errors that propagate to coherent Clifford errors after its final double-check circuit; such errors remain Pauli in S-state cultivation, which consequently retains fault distance 3 (5). As part of our analysis we derive a general formula, and an $\mathcal O(n^3)$-time algorithm for fixed logical-qubit count, for the acceptance probability of a logical mixed state afflicted with a Clifford error, where $n$ is the physical qubit count. We then design flags that detect the malignant hook errors in cultivation, improving the pre-escape logical error rate from $\mathcal O(p^3)$ to $\mathcal O(p^5)$. At noise level $p =10^{-3}$, this is a 4.9$\times$ improvement, costing a 1.36$\times$ increase in attempts per accepted shot.

Exact and fast series expansions for quantum models with long-range interactions

Authors: Antonia Duft, Patrick Adelhardt, Jan Alexander Koziol, Andreas A. Buchheit, Kai Phillip Schmidt
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18761v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces the graph zeta method, a deterministic computational framework designed to calculate high-order series expansions for quantum models with long-range interactions. It replaces slow Monte Carlo simulations by systematically evaluating lattice sums using graph zeta functions and tensor network techniques. This allows for fast, high-precision, and momentum-resolved modeling of complex quantum magnetic systems, with successful application to analyzing experimental data.

Why it may be interesting. The development of a deterministic, high-precision tool for calculating lattice sums in the presence of long-range interactions is crucial for theorists studying quantum magnetism and strongly correlated systems where traditional methods fail due to computational cost or statistical noise.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the need for an exact and fast computational method to calculate high-order series expansions for quantum models characterized by long-range interactions.

Main result. The proposed graph zeta method replaces slow, noisy Monte Carlo evaluations with a deterministic, high-precision calculation, enabling fast, momentum-resolved modeling of quantum matter.

Method. The core method is the 'graph zeta method,' which reformulates graph-embedding sums as graph zeta functions. It decomposes these sums based on treewidth, using Epstein zeta functions for low-treewidth blocks and tensor-network elimination for higher-treewidth blocks.

Model / system. The method is benchmarked on the transverse-field Ising model (TFIM) with power-law interactions in 1D, 2D, and 3D. It is also applied to the quasi-two-dimensional triangular-lattice antiferromagnet KTmSe2.

Key observables. High-resolution excitation spectra across the entire Brillouin zone, ground-state energy density, and series coefficients for various physical quantities.

Important parameters / regimes. The interaction decay law (power-law), the lattice dimensionality (1D, 2D, 3D), and the perturbation parameter $\lambda$.

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes the applicability of linked-cluster expansions and relies on the mathematical decomposition of graph-embedding sums via treewidth.

Figures summary. Not specified, but the notes indicate the method enables obtaining the full momentum-dependent series in a single calculation, which is key for spectral analysis.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the graph zeta method as a deterministic alternative to MC for linked-cluster expansions. It details the mathematical framework involving graph zeta functions and treewidth decomposition, benchmarks this method on the TFIM, and concludes with an application to analyze experimental data from a specific magnetic material.

Abstract

Over the past decade, high-order series expansions based on linked-cluster methods have become an important tool for studying low-energy properties of gapped quantum systems with long-range interactions. We introduce a deterministic framework that removes a central computational bottleneck of this method. Our graph zeta method replaces the costly and statistically noisy Monte Carlo (MC) evaluation of high-dimensional lattice sums by a systematic, high-precision computation that delivers series coefficients within minutes on standard desktop hardware. The full momentum-dependent series is obtained in a single calculation, enabling high-resolution excitation spectra throughout the Brillouin zone. Building on the companion paper [1], the method reformulates graph-embedding sums as graph zeta functions and decomposes them into blocks classified by their treewidth tw. Low-treewidth blocks (tw$\leq2$) admit closed expressions based on Epstein zeta functions, while higher-treewidth blocks (tw$>2$) are evaluated using tensor-network bucket elimination. We benchmark the approach for transverse-field Ising models with power-law interactions in 1d, 2d, and 3d, reproducing previous MC results at a fraction of the computational cost while enabling substantially denser parameter sampling. An open-source implementation makes the method directly applicable to general interactions and large parameter scans. As an application, we compare microscopic interaction models for the stacked quasi-2d transverse-field Ising triangular-lattice antiferromagnet KTmSe$_2$ and find that a model including dipolar interactions best describes existing experimental data. The graph zeta method thus turns high-order linked-cluster expansions into a practical and deterministic tool for fast quantitative momentum-resolved modeling of short- and long-range quantum matter.

Feasibility Ordering of Entanglement-Source Placement for Qubit Channels

Authors: Samuel Marquez Gonzalez
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18803v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: correlated / nonlocal dissipation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper proves a fundamental relationship between the entanglement properties of parallel versus sequential compositions of noisy qubit channels. Specifically, it shows that if the parallel combination is entanglement-assisted, the sequential composition must be entanglement-breaking. This establishes the optimality of midpoint placement for entanglement sources in the feasibility sense.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides rigorous mathematical tools (like factorization criteria and regularization) to compare different physical arrangements of quantum resources (entanglement sources) in open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the feasibility ordering of placing an entanglement source when considering two noisy qubit channels ($\Lambda_1 \otimes \Lambda_2$ vs. $\Lambda_2 \circ \Lambda_1$).

Main result. It proves that if the parallel composition of two qubit channels is entanglement-assisted (EA), then the corresponding sequential composition must be entanglement-breaking (EB), establishing midpoint optimality in the feasibility sense.

Method. The proof utilizes the transpose-factorization criterion, extending results from strictly positive channels to arbitrary channels via depolarizing regularization and quantum Sinkhorn scaling.

Model / system. The system involves two noisy qubit channels ($\Lambda_1, \Lambda_2$) acting on qubits, analyzed through concepts like entanglement breaking (EB) and entanglement assisting (EA).

Key observables. Entanglement-Breaking (EB) and Entanglement-Assisted (EA) properties of channel compositions ($\Lambda_1 \otimes \Lambda_2$ and $\Lambda_2 \circ \Lambda_1$).

Important parameters / regimes. The channels $\Lambda_1$ and $\Lambda_2$, and the concept of strict positivity for initial factorization steps.

Assumptions / limitations. The extension of the factorization criterion from strictly positive channels to arbitrary channels relies on depolarizing regularization.

Paper structure. The paper develops the argument by first establishing the necessary transpose factorization for strictly positive channels using Quantum Sinkhorn scaling, and then extending this result to arbitrary channels using regularization techniques to prove the main implication.

Abstract

This work studies the placement of an entanglement source along a communication line formed by two noisy qubit channels. Recent work argued, on analytical and numerical grounds, that midpoint placement should be at least as favorable as endpoint placement. Here it is shown that, for arbitrary qubit channels, if a sequential composition can preserve entanglement, then the corresponding parallel action cannot annihilate all entanglement. The proof uses the transpose-factorization criterion introduced in that recent work. Quantum Sinkhorn scaling converts every strictly positive qubit channel into a unital representative, and the special normal form of unital qubit channels then yields the required factorization of the transposed map through the original channel. Depolarizing regularization and the closedness of the set of entanglement-breaking channels extend the result to arbitrary channels. Consequently, $Λ_1 \otimes Λ_2 \in \mathrm{EA}$ implies $Λ_2 \circ Λ_1 \in \mathrm{EB}$, and, by exchanging the two channels, the same holds for the opposite composition order. This proves the recent conjecture that midpoint placement is optimal for all qubit channels, in the feasibility sense in which that optimality was originally defined.

Orbital-Induced Peierls Transitions: How Orbitals Orchestrate Lattice Instability

Authors: T. Mizokawa, S. V. Streltsov
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18614v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This review explores how orbital degrees of freedom can drive lattice instabilities, such as the Peierls transition, even in higher-dimensional materials. It details two key mechanisms: orbital anisotropy leading to effective 1D behavior, and the Jahn-Teller effect lifting band degeneracies. The work is crucial for understanding structural transitions in transition metal oxides based on orbital interactions.

Why it may be interesting. The coupling between orbital physics, lattice instability (Peierls), and electronic correlations is a cornerstone of strongly correlated electron physics, relevant to understanding Mott insulators and metal-insulator transitions.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how orbital degrees of freedom can induce the Peierls transition (lattice instability) in higher dimensions, moving beyond the traditional one-dimensional context.

Main result. Orbital anisotropy allows the Peierls instability to manifest in higher dimensions, often synergistically coupled with the Jahn-Teller effect in edge-sharing transition metal oxide structures.

Method. The analysis uses theoretical concepts like the generalized susceptibility ($\chi_0(\vec{q})$) and compares energy gains from electronic structure changes (e.g., molecular orbital formation) against elastic energy loss.

Model / system. The focus is on transition metal compounds, particularly those with partially filled $t_{2g}$ orbitals, often exhibiting edge-sharing octahedral geometries (e.g., in spinels).

Key observables. Gap opening at specific wavevectors, divergence of the generalized susceptibility, and band structure evolution under Jahn-Teller distortions.

Important parameters / regimes. Orbital filling ($d^n$), lattice geometry (edge-sharing vs. face-sharing), and the strength of orbital anisotropy.

Assumptions / limitations. The review primarily focuses on $d$ orbitals and notes the difficulty in cleanly separating the energy contributions from the band Jahn-Teller effect versus the Peierls mechanism.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the Peierls transition in 1D chains, compare orbital sharing in different octahedral geometries, and show $t_{2g}$ band dispersions under structural distortions in spinel structures.

Paper structure. The review progresses from the traditional 1D Peierls concept to mechanisms allowing instability in higher dimensions, detailing the role of orbital anisotropy and Jahn-Teller coupling in specific crystal structures.

Abstract

The Peierls transition is typically regarded as a phenomenon inherent to one-dimensional (1D) materials. However, orbital degrees of freedom can induce this instability even in higher dimensions. Two mechanisms are primarily responsible. First, the anisotropic shape of $p$ and $d$ orbitals can lead to effective "1D-zation" of the electronic spectrum. Second, orbital degrees of freedom can lift band degeneracy by shifting bands relative to each other via the local or band Jahn-Teller effect, thereby affecting the nesting of the Fermi surface. The orbital-induced Peierls effect is most commonly observed when ligand octahedra surrounding transition metals share edges, and less frequently in face-sharing geometries. In this review, we discuss the underlying physical mechanisms, the materials in which this phenomenon occurs, the characteristics of the high-temperature undistorted phase, and the role of local effects such as the formation of molecular orbitals.

Perfect state transfer under matrix powers: parity and spectral arithmetic

Authors: Xingkun Song
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18018v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper provides a rigorous mathematical classification of when Perfect State Transfer (PST) occurs for powers of a matrix $H$, often the adjacency matrix of a graph. It establishes that PST existence frequently follows a parity rule based on the spectral properties of $H$. The methodology combines spectral theory with number theory to precisely determine the allowed exponents $k$ for quantum state transfer.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides deep mathematical tools for analyzing quantum walks and quantum information transfer on graphs, which is highly relevant to quantum network theory and quantum computation models.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper classifies the exponents $k$ for which a real symmetric matrix $H$ (often an adjacency matrix) has Perfect State Transfer (PST) between two distinct vertices $a$ and $b$ when considering the matrix power $H^k$. It analyzes the interplay between spectral properties and arithmetic phase conditions governing this transfer.

Main result. The existence of PST often follows a parity rule: if the spectral supports are commensurable, PST occurs if $k$ is odd (related to $H$) or if $k$ is even (related to $H^2$). Specific graph families like hypercubes and paths are classified, showing PST is highly constrained.

Method. The analysis relies heavily on spectral theory, using spectral decomposition, spectral idempotents, and number-theoretic tools like the greatest common divisor (gcd) and 2-adic valuations ($ u_2$) to analyze phase compatibility conditions.

Model / system. The system is modeled by a real symmetric matrix $H$, typically the adjacency matrix $A(G)$ of a graph $G$. The dynamics are governed by the evolution operator $\exp(-itH^k)$, where $H^k$ is the $k$-th power of the matrix.

Key observables. Perfect State Transfer (PST) existence, minimum transfer time (related to $ ext{gcd}$ of spectral differences), and the parity of the exponent $k$.

Important parameters / regimes. The structure of the graph $G$ (e.g., path $P_7$, hypercubes), the vertex pair $(a, b)$, and the spectral properties of $H$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis often assumes that the supported eigenvalues are integer multiples of a common positive number ($c\mathbb{Z}$) or that the matrix is rational symmetric.

Figures summary. Not specified.

Paper structure. The paper develops from general functional calculus criteria to specific theorems establishing parity dichotomies (Theorem 1.1) for PST exponents, followed by specialized analyses for rational matrices and detailed classifications for specific graph families like paths and Johnson graphs.

Abstract

For a real symmetric matrix $H$ and distinct vertices $a,b$, we classify exponents $k$ for which $H^k$ has perfect state transfer (PST) from $a$ to $b$. If their supported eigenvalues are integer multiples of a common positive number, every odd exponent reduces to $H$ and every positive even exponent reduces to $H^2$. We determine the minimum transfer times using a greatest common divisor of supported spectral differences. For rational symmetric matrices, symmetry of the source vertex support about zero implies the odd-power equivalence without a commensurability assumption; this includes all bipartite graphs. If the source vertex supports zero, PST under one positive even power implies PST under every positive even power. For a symmetric three-point quadratic spectrum whose outer projection signs agree and differ from the central sign, a nonzero rational shift leaves exactly one PST exponent. We classify all adjacency powers of hypercubes, cycles, and Johnson graphs, and all adjacency squares of paths. In particular, the adjacency matrix of $P_7$ has PST from vertex $2$ to vertex $6$ only at exponent $2$.

QiT: Quantum-Inspired Transformer for Visual Recognition Task

Authors: Badri N. Patro, Vijay Agneeswaran
Type: experiment · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.17789v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces QiT, a Quantum-inspired Transformer that adapts concepts like angle encoding and periodic feature mapping from quantum mechanics into a purely classical, scalable vision model. By doing so, it tests whether quantum-motivated structural biases can improve classical performance without requiring actual quantum hardware. The results show QiT is a competitive and efficient baseline for exploring quantum-inspired inductive biases in computer vision.

Why it may be interesting. While the primary focus is on classical ML, the explicit use of periodic functions and kernel approximations derived from quantum state representations provides a novel, testable inductive bias for deep learning models.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of incorporating structural insights from quantum models into scalable classical neural network architectures for visual recognition.

Main result. The proposed model, QiT, achieves competitive performance on ImageNet-1K (78.3% top-1 accuracy) while remaining computationally efficient and avoiding the runtime costs of simulated quantum circuits.

Method. QiT integrates three quantum-inspired components into a classical Transformer: angle-inspired encoding, self-attention over periodic features, and a gated multiplicative emulation layer.

Model / system. QiT is a classical Transformer architecture designed to mimic quantum principles. It uses angle encoding to map image tokens to trigonometric features analogous to quantum rotations, and employs a gated mixer inspired by variational quantum circuits.

Key observables. ImageNet-1K top-1 accuracy (78.3%), computational cost (11.5 GFLOPs), and parameter count (45.7M).

Important parameters / regimes. The model operates entirely using differentiable tensor operations, maintaining the standard $\mathcal{O}(N^2D)$ attention complexity.

Assumptions / limitations. The model explicitly claims neither quantum computation nor quantum speedup; its performance is evaluated as a classical architecture.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the encoder block using angle encoding and Fourier features; Figure 2 contrasts the explicit cosine feature kernel used by QiT with the circuit-dependent fidelity kernel.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem, details the three classical components (encoding, attention, mixing) motivated by quantum concepts, and validates the architecture's feasibility and performance on standard image classification benchmarks.

Abstract

Quantum machine learning offers a compelling representational perspective: angle-encoded states inhabit Hilbert spaces in which periodic similarities and interactions can be expressed naturally. Realizing this perspective for visual recognition remains difficult, however, because present quantum neural networks are constrained by limited qubit counts, costly circuit simulation and measurement, noise, and unstable optimization on noisy intermediate-scale quantum devices. We investigate whether useful structural ideas from quantum models can instead be realized as scalable classical Transformer operations. We introduce QiT, a Quantum-inspired Transformer for vision tasks with three components: (i) angle-inspired encoding that maps image tokens to learned trigonometric Hilbert-space features analogous to quantum rotation-based state encoding; (ii) self-attention over these periodic features, inducing a classical cosine kernel approximated to quantum fidelity kernels; and (iii) gated multiplicative emulation, a trainable classical surrogate for interaction terms found in variational circuits. All components are differentiable tensor operations, so QiT claims neither quantum computation nor quantum speedup and retains the $\mathcal{O}(N^2D)$ attention complexity of a standard Vision Transformer. Across image-classification benchmarks, QiT is competitive with a matched classical Transformer while avoiding the severe runtime cost observed for a small simulated quantum Transformer. QiT-B reaches 78.3\% ImageNet-1K top-1 accuracy with 45.7M parameters and 11.5 GFLOPs. These results position QiT as a scalable baseline for isolating and evaluating quantum-motivated inductive biases in visual recognition.

The Expressive Power of Constrained QAOA: What You Might Have MISsed

Authors: Boris Tsvelikhovskiy, Bao Bach, Ilya Safro
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18209v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work rigorously compares two types of mixers used in constrained QAOA for solving MIS. It proves that the alternative 'Flip-or-Stay' mixer offers superior expressive power compared to the standard mixer, demonstrating a qualitative gap in the reachable quantum states. The findings are crucial for understanding the fundamental limitations and potential enhancements in quantum optimization algorithms.

Why it may be interesting. The detailed algebraic comparison of different quantum control Hamiltonians (Laplacian vs. Adjacency) provides deep insight into which physical constraints yield the most powerful quantum computational models.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the expressive power and controllability of constrained quantum optimization algorithms, specifically for the Maximum Independent Set (MIS) problem.

Main result. It establishes a qualitative expressivity separation between two mixer types (standard vs. Flip-or-Stay) and proves that for connected graphs, the free mixer generates the full unitary Lie algebra on the feasible subspace.

Method. The analysis relies heavily on comparing the dynamical Lie algebras generated by different mixer Hamiltonians (standard vs. alternative) when restricted to the feasible subspace defined by independent sets.

Model / system. The model is constrained quantum optimization dynamics applied to the MIS problem on a graph $\Gamma$. The dynamics are governed by mixers, such as the standard controlled bit-flip mixer and the alternative Flip-or-Stay mixer, acting within the feasible subspace $\mathcal{W}_F$.

Key observables. Controllability (generating the full unitary Lie algebra), expressivity separation (comparing orbit dimensions), and the variance of the loss function in the unitary-design limit.

Important parameters / regimes. The graph structure ($\Gamma$), the depth of the QAOA circuit ($p$), and the dimensionality of the feasible subspace ($|F|$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the circuit ensemble forms an exact unitary 2-design for variance calculations, and it focuses on the operator-algebraic structure rather than just approximation quality.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by defining the constrained dynamics for MIS, comparing the algebraic structures of the standard and alternative mixers, proving full controllability for the free architecture, and finally establishing the expressivity gap using graph families.

Abstract

Feasibility-preserving mixer Hamiltonians offer an attractive alternative to penalty encodings for constrained quantum optimization, yet how enforcing constraints alters the state space reachable by the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) remains largely unexplored. We address this question within the context of the Maximum Independent Set (MIS) problem. Alongside the standard controlled bit-flip mixer, we analyze the alternative $\mathit{Flip\text{-}or\text{-}Stay}$ mixer, whose restriction to the feasible subspace $W_{\mathcal{F}}$ corresponds precisely to the shifted negative Laplacian of the independent-set reconfiguration graph. Although both mixers induce identical transitions between independent sets, the diagonal modification changes the spectral structure and can substantially enlarge the set of reachable quantum states. For every connected input graph with at least two vertices, we prove that independently parameterized local controls associated with either mixer generate the full unitary Lie algebra on the feasible subspace. Standard QAOA exhibits a more nuanced algebraic structure. We prove that the dynamical Lie algebra associated with MIS-QAOA employing the conventional mixer embeds into its Flip-or-Stay counterpart. Furthermore, we construct an infinite family of graphs for which the corresponding vacuum-state dynamical group orbits exhibit strictly distinct dimensions, establishing a qualitative expressivity separation between the two architectures. For either mixer, we show that standard QAOA initialized in the empty set can prepare a state supported entirely on maximum independent sets at finite depth. For the free architectures, we also derive an exact loss-variance formula in the unitary-design limit, expressed through the number of feasible independent sets and the variance of their cardinalities.

Variational Quantum Transformer Architecture for Synthetic Language Generation

Authors: Julian Hager, Michael Kölle, Gerhard Stenzel, Tobias Rohe, Jonas Stein, Claudia Linnhoff-Popien
Type: experiment · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18565v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: QC/QI experiment 1/5

Summary. This paper introduces a variational quantum transformer designed for sequence modeling in QNLP, replacing classical layers with quantum circuits. The authors demonstrate that the architecture can learn complex grammar rules, though performance comparisons show that a classical transformer remains more accurate and stable. The work serves as a proof-of-concept for applying quantum circuits to structured data generation under near-term quantum constraints.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a concrete, near-term quantum circuit implementation for a complex machine learning task (NLP), which is highly relevant for researchers exploring quantum machine learning algorithms on current hardware.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of developing a compact, NISQ-compatible quantum architecture for performing synthetic Quantum Natural Language Processing (QNLP) sequence modeling.

Main result. While the quantum models successfully learn nontrivial grammar regularities, the classical transformer baseline remained more accurate and stable, indicating the current work is a feasibility study rather than a claim of quantum advantage.

Method. The authors propose a variational quantum transformer that replaces classical attention/feed-forward layers with variational quantum blocks, trained end-to-end using negative log-likelihood loss.

Model / system. The system is a hybrid quantum-classical model featuring an autoregressive structure. It uses angle-encoding for tokens and consists of variational quantum encoder, connector, and decoder blocks.

Key observables. Token accuracy and a comprehensive grammar score evaluating structural validity on deterministic and lexicographic grammars.

Important parameters / regimes. The architecture depth (E, D, V) and the number of trainable parameters (e.g., 354–364 for quantum vs. 700 for classical).

Assumptions / limitations. The contribution is explicitly framed as an architectural demonstration under NISQ constraints, acknowledging that the classical baseline is superior in performance and stability.

Figures summary. Figure 1 shows the mean grammar score comparison across various quantum and classical models for both deterministic and lexicographic tasks over multiple seeds.

Paper structure. The paper details the architectural design, including circuit components (encoding, encoder blocks, connector, decoder blocks), presents the experimental evaluation on controlled grammar tasks, and concludes by comparing performance against a classical baseline.

Abstract

We propose a compact NISQ-compatible quantum transformer architecture for synthetic QNLP sequence modelling. The model preserves the autoregressive next-token interface of a classical transformer, but replaces attention and feed-forward sublayers with variational quantum encoder blocks, connector circuits, decoder blocks and a direct two-qubit measurement readout. Token contexts are angle-encoded into small quantum registers, processed by parallel variational heads and encoder integration circuits and conditioned through decoder ancillae to produce a distribution over a four-token vocabulary. We evaluate several architecture variants on deterministic and lexicographic grammar-generation tasks against a compact classical transformer baseline. The quantum models are trainable end-to-end and learn nontrivial grammar structure, including perfect deterministic generation in individual runs and high lexicographic validity in the strongest variant. The classical baseline remains more accurate and stable and the quantum models are sensitive to initialization. The contribution is therefore not a claim of quantum advantage, but a concrete architecture and evaluation of transformer-inspired QNLP sequence modelling under near-term quantum constraints.

Summary. This work develops the resource theory of Quantum-State Texture (QST), a measure quantifying quantum information content. Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper advances the resource-theoretic formulation of Quantum-State Texture (QST) to provide a rigorous mathematical framework for quantifying quantum information content.

Main result. It establishes the basis-independent concept of 'set texture' and provides concrete characterizations of QST free operations for single qubits and composite systems.

Method. The authors utilize resource theory principles, focusing on the experimentally accessible 'grand sum' of density matrix elements and deriving bounds and operational interpretations for various texture monotones.

Model / system. The theory applies to general quantum states ($\varrho$) in a Hilbert space of dimension $D$, analyzing how quantum operations (CPTP maps) affect the state's texture.

Key observables. Quantum-State Texture (QST), the grand sum ($\Sigma(\varrho)$), distillable texture ($r$), and rugosity ($R(\varrho)$).

Important parameters / regimes. The dimension $D$ of the Hilbert space, and the parameters characterizing the action of free operations on the Bloch vector for single qubits.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis centers on the grand sum $\Sigma(\varrho)$ as the key experimentally accessible observable, and assumes the existence of a fixed-point resource theory framework.

Paper structure. Abstract

The recently introduced concept of quantum-state texture (QST) has found applications ranging from the identification of unknown quantum gates to the study of quantum phase transitions and criticality, and has already been experimentally investigated. Here, we advance its resource-theoretic formulation in several directions. Our approach centers on the experimentally accessible grand sum--the sum of all matrix elements of a density operator in a given basis. We clarify several points raised in the recent literature, including the relation between texture distillation and a simple grand-sum-based QST monotone. We completely characterize free operations for a single qubit and study the resulting state-conversion order; classify relevant families of free operations in arbitrary dimensions; derive bounds relating the texture of composite systems to that of their subsystems; and introduce the basis-independent concept of set texture. Together, these results strengthen and broaden the resource theory of QST, providing a foundation for its further development and applications.

Abstract

We prove that minimum-output Rényi-entropy additivity can fail by almost one bit simultaneously at every nonnegative order. Abstract

We demonstrate the possibility to activate genuine multipartite entanglement (GME), the strongest form of entanglement for multipartite states, within the framework of quantum catalysis. Abstract

We study the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm with a Grover mixer and independently sampled cost and mixing angles. Under a lattice condition on the cost values carried by the initial state, we prove a depth-independent lower bound for the variance of the loss at every depth, together with the same bound for the derivative with respect to the final mixing angle. We also establish Grover-type reachability bounds showing that the depth required to approximate a prescribed carried eigenspace is bounded below by a constant multiple of the inverse square root of its initial probability.

Abstract

Phonon angular momentum, characterized by circular or elliptical motion of atoms, plays a decisive role in diverse phenomena, ranging from the phonon Hall effect, phonon magnetic moment, Einstein-de Haas effect, Weyl phonons, and driven chiral phonons. The phonon modes with an antisymmetric Raman tensor, which carry an intrinsic phonon angular momentum in a pseudovector symmetry channel, have been overlooked in the past in the chiral phonon community. Here, we report the detection of this type of phonon mode using polarization-resolved Raman spectroscopy. This mode gets Raman activity due to the presence of Cr vacancies in Cr$_2$O$_3$ that locally break the twofold rotational symmetry perpendicular to the threefold axis. Our results provide a new way to detect the novel symmetry-forbidden $A_2$ phonon modes with an antisymmetric Raman tensor in a pseudovector symmetry channel.

Abstract

Using optical spectroscopy and density functional theory, we provide evidence that electron-electron interactions strongly affect the properties of the kagome antiferromagnet Mn$_3$Sn, since its low-energy optical interband transitions arise exclusively from the correlation-modified electronic band structure. Mn$_3$Sn possesses an optical effective mass about three times larger than that of its isostructural analog Mn$_3$Ge. The DFT+$U$ treatment in the Lichtenstein formulation, with an on-site Coulomb repulsion $U = 4$~eV and Hund's coupling $J = 0.25$~eV, accounts for the optical transitions in Mn$_3$Sn, whereas those in Mn$_3$Ge are already reproduced without Hubbard corrections ($U, J = 0$). Our findings establish a comprehensive-correlated picture of Mn$_3$Sn and Mn$_3$Ge that offers a template for other correlated topological metals.

Abstract

Can quantum entanglement continue to grow even after the entanglement entropies of subsystems have saturated? We address this question using genuine multi-entropy, which probes multi-partite entanglement structure beyond bipartite entanglement. In a two-sided BTZ black hole, this quantity grows linearly with time after every subsystem entropy of a fixed tripartition has saturated, extending the interval in which entanglement detects Einstein-Rosen bridge growth. The result refines the ER=EPR relation by identifying multi-partite structure invisible to ordinary entanglement entropies. Some spin-chain models and the spatially partitioned Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev (SYK) model exhibit distinct transients, while the late-time plateau values in holography, the SYK model, and Haar-random states share a universal behaviour.

Abstract

We develop a geometric framework for Hamiltonian engineering in finite-dimensional quantum systems using ideal control pulses. Starting from a bilinear Schrödinger equation with unbounded control amplitudes, we construct the closed pulse group and use extensions of control systems and Filippov's relaxation theorem to obtain a family of effective Hamiltonians given by the convex hull of the drift's adjoint orbit plus the Lie algebra of the pulse group. The geometry of this orbitope describes possibilities beyond group averaging. Using the isotypic decomposition of the adjoint representation, we characterize its affine hull and show that the group average lies in its interior. This yields locally accessible families of effective Hamiltonians around the invariant part of the drift. We apply the framework to recover the necessary and sufficient condition for dynamical decoupling from arbitrary interactions with a finite-dimensional bath. For connected abelian pulse groups, we describe the relevant representation decomposition through restricted roots. Finally, for qubit networks with identical pairwise couplings, we give a qualitative characterization and quantitative estimation of effective Hamiltonians that can be generated using our framework.
