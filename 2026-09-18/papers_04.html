Pathology-Free Real-Space Renormalization Group Theory on an Inverse Limit Space

Authors: Fabio Arz
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18356v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 2/5 · methods for driven-dissipative 2/5 · analog quantum simulation 1/5

Summary. This paper presents a novel, mathematically rigorous framework for Wilson's Renormalization Group theory by employing inverse limits of finite systems. It successfully defines a continuous RG map on the space of physical Hamiltonians, thereby avoiding pathologies found in previous rigorous attempts. This provides a powerful new tool to study critical phenomena and fixed points in statistical mechanics.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical statistical mechanics, the rigorous mathematical machinery developed—especially the use of functional spaces, continuity proofs, and flow analysis—is highly relevant to understanding the dynamics of open quantum systems or quantum field theories where renormalization is key.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To develop a mathematically rigorous and pathology-free framework for Wilson's Renormalization Group (RG) theory.

Main result. The authors establish a continuous RG map on a quotient space of Hamilton functions, proving the framework's mathematical soundness and providing a path to study fixed points.

Method. The core method involves using inverse limits of finite systems and defining the RG transformation on the space of physical interactions ($V_{ ext{phys}}$) to ensure mathematical consistency.

Model / system. The theory is applied to the Ising model on the hypercubic lattice ($\mathbb{Z}^d$), analyzing the flow of interactions (Hamilton functions) in the infinite volume limit.

Key observables. RG flow, fixed points (e.g., $H=0$), critical exponents, and the norm $\lVert [\hat{H}] Vert_{\hat{V}}$ used to measure convergence.

Important parameters / regimes. The decay rate of interactions ($\Phi$), the lattice dimension ($d$), and the coupling constants ($eta$).

Assumptions / limitations. The framework assumes the existence of a suitable space $V_{ ext{phys}}$ and relies on proving the continuity of the RG map $R$ on this space.

Paper structure. The paper builds from defining block-spin RGTs on finite graphs using inverse limits, establishing the map $R: V_{ ext{phys}} o V_{ ext{phys}}$, and finally analyzing the flow properties on the quotient space $\hat{V} = V_{ ext{phys}}/R$.

Abstract

It has been over fifty years since Kenneth Wilson had his Nobel-prize-winning ideas on the renormalization group. In this time frame, despite many attempts, no mathematical results have implemented Wilson's vision to a satisfactory degree. Although a number of predictions stemming from the renormalization group framework have been proven to date, these proofs usually rely on alternative ideas and do not cover the full predictive power of Wilson's renormalization group. The discovery of pathologies within rigorous implementations of renormalization group transformations have further reduced the enthusiasm of the mathematics community for this subject. This paper presents a novel framework to implement Wilson's original idea in a rigorous manner based on inverse limits of finite systems. Doing so, one avoids these pathologies and hence obtains a mathematically sound framework for Wilson's renormalization group. This opens up a completely new path to tackle some of the existing problems in rigorous renormalization group theory like the existence of non-trivial fixed points.

Scalar quasinormal and quasibound states of weakly magnetized Ernst--Schwarzschild spacetime: a confluent-Heun Wronskian approach

Summary. This work develops a unified mathematical framework to calculate the quasinormal and quasibound modes for scalar fields in a weakly magnetized black hole spacetime. It achieves this by transforming the governing differential equation into a confluent-Heun form and defining the spectral condition via a global Wronskian. The method provides a robust, horizon-to-infinity spectral condition that matches established theoretical benchmarks.

Why it may be interesting. While the core physics is general relativity/field theory, the mathematical machinery—reducing a physical boundary value problem to the zeros of a specific transcendental function (the Wronskian)—is highly relevant to open quantum systems and spectral analysis in other physical contexts.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To provide a unified and rigorous method for determining the quasinormal-mode (QNM) and quasibound-state (QBS) spectra for a massless scalar field in a weakly magnetized Ernst--Schwarzschild spacetime.

Main result. The spectral condition is unified by constructing an endpoint-selected spectral Wronskian, which successfully reproduces known weak-field benchmarks for both QNMs and QBSs.

Method. The analysis reduces the Klein-Gordon equation to a radial confluent-Heun equation, whose spectral condition is determined by setting the global spectral Wronskian to zero.

Model / system. The system is a massless scalar field propagating in a weakly magnetized Ernst--Schwarzschild spacetime. The analysis is restricted to the separated, weak-field approximation ($O(B_0^2)$) of the geometry.

Key observables. Quasinormal mode frequencies ($\omega$) and quasibound state frequencies, determined as the zeros of the spectral Wronskian.

Important parameters / regimes. Magnetic field strength ($B_0$), mass ($M$), angular number ($\ell$), and the frequency ($\Omega$).

Assumptions / limitations. The results are strictly limited to the separated $O(B^2_0)$ weak-field model, and the method relies on the global spectral Wronskian condition rather than local polynomial termination.

Figures summary. Tables compare calculated QNM frequencies against established benchmarks from the literature for varying magnetic fields and masses.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the weak-field reduction to the confluent-Heun equation, derives the global spectral Wronskian from the recurrence relation, and then applies the zero-finding condition to unify the spectral determination for both radiative (QNM) and bound (QBS) states.

Abstract

We study the quasinormal-mode and quasibound-state spectra of a massless scalar field in weakly magnetized Ernst--Schwarzschild spacetime. In the separated $O(B_0^2)$ regime, the Klein--Gordon equation reduces to a radial confluent-Heun equation. We construct an endpoint-selected spectral Wronskian directly from the canonical confluent-Heun recurrence and its regular-to-irregular connection limit. Quasinormal modes and quasibound states are then obtained as zeros of the same analytic spectral function on the radiative and bound sheets, respectively, providing a unified treatment of the two spectral sectors within the separated weak-field problem. The resulting QNM and QBS frequencies agree with independent weak-field Ernst and massive-Schwarzschild benchmarks available in the literature. We further show, for representative cases, that the frequencies selected by the local Heun-series termination relation examined here do not satisfy the endpoint-selected quasibound-state condition. The construction therefore provides a direct horizon-to-infinity spectral condition for both radiative and bound states within the weak-field Ernst problem.

Unconventional linear transverse exciton transport in valley-layer coupling two-dimensional materials

Authors: Ci Li
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18035v1

Summary. This theoretical work explores unconventional linear transverse exciton transport in valley-layer coupled 2D materials. By using an out-of-plane electric field ($E_{\perp}$), the authors demonstrate control over exciton dispersion anisotropy, which generates measurable transverse currents. This provides a versatile platform for engineering excitonic phenomena in next-generation quantum materials.

Why it may be interesting. The ability to use an external electric field ($E_{\perp}$) to engineer and control specific, symmetry-protected transport channels (like anisotropy-induced currents) in excitonic systems is highly relevant for designing quantum light sources or detectors.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates unconventional linear transverse exciton transport (LTET) in valley-layer coupling (VLC) two-dimensional (2D) materials.

Main result. The perpendicular electric field ($E_{\perp}$) acts as a powerful knob to control exciton-dispersion anisotropy, thereby inducing and tuning LTET in both monolayer and twisted bilayer structures.

Method. Theoretical analysis using effective exciton Hamiltonians and semi-classical transport theory to calculate conductivity tensor components.

Model / system. The system is VLC 2D materials, prototyped by TiSiCo, which exhibit valley coupling via crystal symmetry. The model extends to both monolayer and twisted bilayer configurations, incorporating interlayer Förster coupling.

Key observables. Transverse conductivities ($\sigma_{AS}^{ ext{tra}}, \alpha_{AS}^{ ext{tra}}$) and the competition between anisotropy-induced responses and $\mathcal{T}$-even layer Hall/Nernst counterflow.

Important parameters / regimes. Perpendicular electric field ($E_{\perp}$), twist angle ($ heta$), and temperature ($T$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis focuses on the zero-Berry-curvature limit and assumes the exciton binding energy is omitted for simplicity.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the crystal structure, exciton dispersion dependence on $E_{\perp}$, and the calculated dependence of transverse conductivities on temperature, $E_{\perp}$, and twist angle for both monolayer and bilayer systems.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the theoretical framework for LTET in VLC materials, first analyzing the monolayer response governed by anisotropy, and then extending the analysis to the twisted bilayer where multiple competing transport channels (anisotropy-driven vs. layer counterflow) coexist and are tunable.

Abstract

Valley-layer coupling (VLC) two-dimensional (2D) materials define a distinct class of quantum systems in which valleys are related by crystal, rather than time-reversal ($\mathcal{T}$) symmetry, enabling gate-controlled valley-contrasted layer polarization. Here we extend this concept to excitons. Using TiSiCo as a prototype VLC material, we show that a perpendicular electric field $E_{\perp}$ controls exciton- dispersion anisotropy and thereby generates unconventional linear transverse exciton transport in both monolayer and twisted bilayer structures. In the monolayer, this response is characterized by anisotropy-induced transverse conductivities, arising from the antisymmetric combination of diagonal elements in the conductivity tensor and strongly tunable by $E_{\perp}$. In the twisted bilayer, symmetry additionally permits transverse responses from the symmetric part of the conductivity tensor. Since intralayer excitons in different layers are connected by the Förster coupling, these symmetric and antisymmetric responses coexist and compete with the recently proposed $\mathcal{T}$-even layer Hall and Nernst exciton counterflow. This interplay is highly tunable by twisted angle, temperature, and the direction of the in-plane driving force, providing a route to disentangle distinct transverse exciton transport signals experimentally. Our results establish $E_{\perp}$ as a powerful knob for controlling exciton transport via VLC and identify VLC 2D materials as a promising platform for engineered excitonic phenomena.

Summary. It successfully maps out the physics across three quantum critical regimes, confirming the utility of ML in extracting deep physical structure from boundary data.

Why it may be interesting. The application of advanced ML techniques to solve fundamental problems in strongly correlated electron physics (strange metals) via holography provides a powerful, modern tool for theoretical condensed matter research.

Abstract

We present a physics-informed machine learning framework based on Neural Ordinary Differential Equations that solves the holographic inverse problem: reconstructing the bulk spacetime and gauge field of a charged AdS black hole directly from boundary fermionic spectral functions. Encoding the UV asymptotics, horizon regularity, and zero temperature extremality as hard constraints in the neural network architecture, our framework reliably reconstructs the extremal Reissner-Nordström AdS geometry across three quantum critical regimes set by the $U(1)$ probe charge---non-Fermi liquid, marginal Fermi liquid (strange metal), and Fermi-liquid-like states---and can jointly infer the probe charge itself to sub-percent accuracy. Relaxing the near-AdS boundary constraint uncovers a geometrical degeneracy: bulk profiles that differ throughout the radial direction but share the same near-horizon $AdS_2 \times \mathbb{R}^2$ data reproduce identical spectral functions near the Fermi surface. This isospectral non-uniqueness is precisely the bulk degeneracy expected on general holographic grounds at zero temperature, and its spontaneous emergence across independent training runs shows that the network isolates the IR CFT universality rather than overfitting a single UV completion.

Summary. This theoretical paper establishes a rigorous link between Full Eigenstate Thermalization (full ETH) in QFT and the concept of gravitational scrambling. By employing thermal analyticity and free probability theory, the authors derive quantitative bounds that are shown to be saturated by holographic CFTs. This work provides a unified mathematical framework connecting thermalization, non-commutative probability, and quantum gravity effects.

Why it may be interesting. While highly abstract, the connection established between thermalization dynamics (ETH) and fundamental algebraic structures (free cumulants, modular theory) provides powerful, non-perturbative tools applicable to understanding thermalization in complex quantum many-body systems, including those relevant to AMO physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates Full Eigenstate Thermalization (full ETH) in Quantum Field Theory and establishes a deep connection between full ETH, free cumulants, and the concept of gravitational scrambling.

Main result. The authors derive directional large-frequency bounds for full ETH functions, showing that holographic CFTs saturate these bounds at fourth order, thus linking full ETH to gravitational scrambling.

Method. The methodology combines thermal analyticity, free probability theory (using free cumulants), and advanced algebraic techniques from quantum field theory, particularly those related to modular flow and the AdS/CFT correspondence.

Model / system. The analysis is conducted in continuum Quantum Field Theories (QFTs), focusing on thermal Conformal Field Theories (CFTs) and utilizing holographic models derived from the AdS/CFT correspondence.

Key observables. Full ETH smooth functions, two- and three-point functions in thermal CFTs, fourth-order free cumulants, and the holographic eikonal scattering matrix phase shift.

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis examines behavior at low orders (two/three-point functions) and the first appearance of dynamics at fourth order, relating to the asymptotic freeness limit.

Assumptions / limitations. The derivation relies on the complex-time analyticity of regulated thermal free cumulants and assumes the semiclassical near-horizon regime for the holographic identification.

Paper structure. The paper is structured logically, first reviewing the algebraic framework for gravitational scrambling, then introducing free probability theory, deriving ETH constraints using analyticity, testing these bounds in CFTs, and concluding with the synthesis of the connection between ETH, free cumulants, and scrambling.

Abstract

Using thermal analyticity, we derive directional large-frequency bounds on the smooth functions entering full eigenstate thermalization in continuum quantum field theory. The strongest directions reproduce the scales previously identified for lattice systems, while the complete analyticity domain reveals a broader asymmetric multifrequency structure. In thermal two-dimensional conformal field theory, exact two- and three-point functions saturate the corresponding bounds. At fourth order, where genuine dynamical information first appears, we show that the holographic CFTs studied here saturate the strongest full-ETH bounds. These results establish a direct connection between full ETH, free cumulants, and gravitational scrambling, and are consistent with the emergence of asymptotic freeness realized by the gravitational scrambling algebra.

Abstract

This facilitates entanglement extraction from a catalyst many-body state while leaving it asymptotically unchanged. The resulting state is accompanied by nonlocal nonstabilizerness that diverges with the system size, consistent with a recently established equivalence between universal embezzlement and divergent nonlocal magic. Thus, without any fine tuning, a chaotic quantum evolution generates intermediate states which form a universal family of catalytic reservoirs.

Abstract

We study statistical variants of the classical largest average submatrix problem. For small submatrices (where the dimension is less than linearly proportional to the original matrix), the problem is typically well understood and believed to exhibit the statistical-computational gap (SCG). However, analytical treatment of both the information-theoretic and algorithmic aspects of the linear regime remains challenging, and no mathematically rigorous results have yet arrived anywhere close to proving or disproving SCG existence in this setting. Focusing on the linear regime, we make strong progress in several key directions: 1) We develop a generic Random Duality Theory (RDT) framework to characterize largest average submatrix values. 2) Using the plain RDT variant, we obtain closed-form upper bounds as explicit functions of dimensionality proportionality parameters. 3) We demonstrate that a lifted RDT variant strictly improves upon the plain RDT within a certain linear range of submatrix dimensions. 4) For small submatrices where the dimensional proportionality constants approach zero, we prove that our results match both the replica results from [34] (obtained via one-step replica symmetry breaking) and the sublinear results from [18,45].

Summary. This work develops a security protocol for Quantum Error Correction against malicious or flawed advice from AI agents. By introducing a trusted calibration step, the authors resolve ambiguities in syndrome measurements that otherwise allow harmful updates. The framework establishes conditional guarantees, ensuring that recovery updates are only accepted if they can be mathematically certified to improve the system's state beyond known error bounds.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a rigorous, information-theoretic framework for assessing trust in complex, adaptive quantum control loops, which is crucial for deploying quantum computation in noisy, real-world environments.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the security of Quantum Error Correction (QEC) protocols when the advice or syndrome history is potentially misleading or manipulated by an external AI agent.

Main result. Security is maintained by implementing a separate, trusted evaluator that requires certified improvement based on calibration uncertainty and drift bounds, effectively separating the 'advice' from the 'authority' of the recovery decision.

Method. The authors analyze the joint distribution of passive syndrome histories, using signed calibration measurements to resolve sign ambiguities inherent in the syndrome records, and deriving rigorous bounds on accumulated risk (infidelity).

Model / system. The primary model is the odd-distance square toric code, used to encode logical qubits. The analysis involves syndrome measurements, recovery operations, and the incorporation of calibration measurements to test the integrity of the advice.

Key observables. Passive syndrome-history distributions, terminal logical measurement outcomes ($q_Y( heta)$), and the excess infidelity ($D_u( heta)$).

Important parameters / regimes. The required improvement threshold ($\delta$), the calibration uncertainty, and the drift bound ($L_D v A$) are critical parameters for certification.

Assumptions / limitations. The security guarantees rely on the assumption that the drift rate is bounded and that the calibration measurements provide accurate, sign-sensitive information.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates how trusted calibration separates advice from authority by resolving sign ambiguities in passive history distributions. Figure 2 shows the quantitative chain involving syndrome history, calibration, and the decision process using uncertainty and drift bounds.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the ambiguity in passive syndrome records (sign symmetry) and then introduces the role of signed calibration to resolve this. It develops a formal risk assessment framework using drift bounds and finally applies this framework to show that only certified improvements are accepted, while also analyzing the robustness of these bounds.

Abstract

Can an attacker turn influence over an artificial intelligence (AI) adviser into a harmful quantum error-correction update? We identify an ambiguity in passive syndrome records that obstructs recovery selection, then show how additional calibration measurements support certified recovery updates under uncertainty and drift. In an odd-distance square toric code with error-free preparation, syndrome measurements, and recovery operations, opposite coherent $X$ rotations produce identical passive syndrome-history distributions. Yet a fixed phase correction can help at one sign and harm at the other. A terminal logical measurement on known encoded calibration states supplies the missing sign information. A separate evaluator accepts an update only when calibration uncertainty and a justified drift bound certify improvement over the current recovery, without assuming that the adviser recommends correctly. In simulated advice attacks, calibration-confidence checks reject harmful proposals while retaining beneficial updates under honest advice. We derive sufficient limits on calibration age that require improvement through deployment. In matched simulations, a validated channel-specific bound retains more beneficial updates than the general bound after accounting for evaluation time, while preventing the tested harmful activations under the stated drift assumption. A separate surface-code experiment includes stochastic circuit faults and noise changing during acquisition. Deterministic controllers achieve at least as many beneficial updates with the same observations. Violating the drift assumption permits harmful acceptance in the toric experiment. The results identify information required for recovery selection, establish conditional guarantees against harmful updates, and quantify the recovery improvements forgone through conservative acceptance.

Summary. This work uses optical measurements and DFT+DMFT calculations to characterize the electronic structure of the ferromagnetic Weyl semimetal CeAlSi. The findings confirm that the electronic properties are dominated by localized Ce 4f states, and the overall Weyl electronic structure remains robust across the magnetic phase transition. This highlights the utility of correlated electron theory in understanding topological materials.

Why it may be interesting. The combination of optical spectroscopy with DMFT calculations provides a powerful tool to probe the interplay between itinerant (Weyl) and localized (4f) electronic degrees of freedom in correlated magnetic materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the electronic states and the effect of the ferromagnetic transition on the Weyl electronic structure in the magnetic Weyl semimetal CeAlSi.

Main result. The optical conductivity spectra are successfully modeled using DFT+DMFT, suggesting that the Ce 4f states are nearly localized, and the overall spectral shape remains stable across the ferromagnetic transition temperature.

Method. The research combines experimental measurements of temperature-dependent optical conductivity ($\sigma_1(\omega)$) with advanced theoretical calculations using DFT+DMFT.

Model / system. The system is CeAlSi, a magnetic Weyl semimetal with a non-centrosymmetric tetragonal structure. The theory incorporates local Coulomb interactions for Ce 4f electrons via the Hubbard-I approximation.

Key observables. Temperature-dependent optical conductivity $\sigma_1(\omega)$, dynamic effective mass, and the location/chirality of Weyl points.

Important parameters / regimes. Ferromagnetic ordering temperature ($T_C$), Coulomb interaction parameters ($U=6.30 ext{ eV}$, $J_H=0.79 ext{ eV}$), and temperature ($10 ext{ K}$ vs $300 ext{ K}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the Hubbard-I approximation for local correlations and extrapolations for optical measurements.

Figures summary. Figures show temperature-dependent reflectivity and derived $\sigma_1(\omega)$ spectra, alongside calculated single-particle excitation spectra $A(\mathbf{k}, \omega)$ and the mapping of Weyl point distributions in both Paramagnetic and Ferromagnetic phases.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by measuring optical spectra across the magnetic transition, comparing these to DFT calculations, and refining the model using DFT+DMFT to account for strong correlations and accurately predict the electronic structure in both magnetic phases.

Abstract

To investigate electronic states during the ferromagnetic transition in a magnetic Weyl semimetal CeAlSi, we measured temperature-dependent optical conductivity [$σ_1(ω)$] spectra and compared them with DFT+DMFT band calculations. The $σ_1(ω)$ spectrum did not change significantly across the ferromagnetic ordering temperature ($T_C$), suggesting that the Ce 4f states are almost localized. DFT+DMFT calculations with almost localized Ce 4f states successfully reproduced the spectral shape and the unchanged $σ_1(ω)$ spectra across $T_C$. The dynamic effective mass evaluated from the extended Drude model is very small, which DFT+DMFT calculations also reproduce, but the scattering probability at even lower temperatures suggests ferromagnetic fluctuations. These results suggest that the interaction intensity between the Weyl fermions and Ce 4f states is very weak, as reproduced by DFT+DMFT calculations.

Benchmarking indirect quantum control schemes via higher-order quantum operations

Authors: Simon Vedl, Varun Srivastava, Riddhi Ghosh, Alexei Gilchrist
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18409v1

Summary. This work develops a formal framework using higher-order quantum operations to benchmark indirect quantum control. By casting the optimal control problem as a Semidefinite Program, the authors establish a fundamental upper bound on achievable performance. This allows researchers to systematically quantify the advantage gained by using complex, multi-step control protocols over simpler, restricted strategies.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a powerful, general mathematical framework (SDP) to quantify the true potential of complex quantum control schemes beyond simple unitary evolution, which is crucial for designing robust quantum information protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to establish a rigorous benchmark for indirect quantum control schemes by formulating them using higher-order quantum operations.

Main result. The optimal control problem is formulated as a Semidefinite Program (SDP), providing an upper bound that can systematically compare against restricted, physically motivated control protocols.

Method. The authors use the language of higher-order quantum operations and model admissible controller manipulations as deterministic superinstruments, leading to an SDP formulation.

Model / system. The model involves a target system (S) manipulated indirectly via an auxiliary controller (C) through a fixed interaction. The dynamics are analyzed using the Choi-Jamiołkowski representation and link products.

Key observables. Final state fidelity, performance metric derived from the SDP objective function.

Important parameters / regimes. Control time steps, interaction strengths (e.g., $J_{\parallel}, J_{\perp}$), and detunings ($\delta$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis considers deterministic protocols with a fixed interaction and a finite number of discrete intervention times for controller operations.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the separation of dynamics into state preparation, the process tensor (uncontrolled interaction), and the deterministic superinstrument (controlled operations).

Paper structure. The paper develops the formalism using HOQOs and superinstruments, formulates the optimization as an SDP, and illustrates the framework with a two-step qubit-purification task, comparing the benchmark to simple unitary strategies.

Abstract

Indirect quantum control aims to manipulate a target system through interventions on an auxiliary controller with which it interacts. We formulate finite-step indirect-control protocols using the language of higher-order quantum operations, representing admissible controller manipulations as deterministic superinstruments. This representation separates the uncontrolled but fixed dynamics from the controllable operations and allows the optimal control problem to be written as a semidefinite program for a chosen figure of merit. The resulting optimum gives an operational benchmark by providing the best performance achievable by a finite sequence of control operations, including protocols with classical or quantum feed-forward. More restricted and experimentally motivated control classes, such as independent unitary controls or memoryless quantum channels, can then be compared against this benchmark. We illustrate the framework with a two-step qubit-purification task, in which an initially mixed target qubit is steered toward a pure state through a fixed interaction with a controller qubit. The example shows regimes where simple unitary strategies saturate the higher-order benchmark, as well as regimes where they are provably suboptimal. This approach provides a systematic way to study the value of control resources in indirect quantum-control schemes.
