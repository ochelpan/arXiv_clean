Benchmarking Large Language Models on Repairing Qiskit Programs using Bugs4Q

Authors: Saumya Brahmbhatt, Mitali Hukkeri, Dongchan Kim, M. V. Panduranga Rao, Lei Zhang
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.09007v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

Summary. This paper evaluates how well large language models can fix bugs in quantum circuits written using Qiskit. The authors find that performance metrics are misleading because the underlying benchmark itself becomes invalid as the quantum framework updates. They conclude that robust evaluation requires explicitly pinning both the benchmark and the software version.

Why it may be interesting. It establishes rigorous standards for evaluating AI assistance in quantum software development, which is crucial for any field relying on complex quantum circuit implementation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study benchmarks Large Language Models (LLMs) on automatically repairing quantum programs written in Qiskit using the Bugs4Q dataset.

Main result. LLM performance is highly dependent on the specific version of the underlying quantum framework (Qiskit), and many apparent successes are due to environmental compatibility rather than true repair correctness.

Method. The authors evaluate four LLMs by generating multiple repair candidates for 67 known defects across six pinned versions of Qiskit, using pass@k metrics.

Model / system. The system under test is quantum computation implemented via Qiskit programs containing real, documented defects sourced from the Bugs4Q benchmark.

Key observables. Pass@10 rate, executability status across different Qiskit versions, and the proportion of successful repairs occurring on invalid benchmark entries.

Important parameters / regimes. Six pinned Qiskit releases (e.g., 0.25.0 to 2.3.1), and the number of repair candidates tested per defect (up to 10).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption challenged is that benchmark labels remain valid across software updates; correctness depends on the (benchmark, version) pair.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the pass@10 rate peaking at Qiskit 0.45.0 and declining sharply after the Qiskit 1.0 transition, while Table III shows performance restricted to valid pairs.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem of benchmarking quantum code repair, details the version-pinned evaluation protocol using Bugs4Q across multiple LLMs, presents results showing version dependency, and concludes by advocating for mandatory benchmark validation before repair assessment.

Abstract

In quantum programs, Bugs4Q is a widely used benchmark containing real quantum defects. However, its evaluation assumes that benchmark labels remain valid and that generated fixes execute in the target environment. We evaluate two Bugs4Q versions containing 67 unique real Qiskit defects, adding executable tests where missing, and re-run all entries across six pinned Qiskit releases (0.25.0, 0.45.0, 1.0.0, 1.1.1, 2.0.0, and 2.3.1). We find that quantum benchmarks can suffer from silent label inversion: entries become invalid without errors when reference fixes stop executing or buggy programs no longer reproduce failures. Thus, correctness depends on the (benchmark, version) pair rather than the benchmark alone. We evaluate four LLMs (GPT-4o-mini, GPT-5o-mini, GPT-5.4, and GPT-5.4-mini), generating up to 10 repair candidates per defect and testing them across all versions. GPT-5.4 achieves the highest pass@10 (48.8%), followed by GPT-5.4-mini (47.3%), GPT-5o-mini (30.3%), and GPT-4o-mini (22.6%). All models perform best on Qiskit 0.45.0 and decline after the Qiskit 1.0 transition. Many failures arise from deprecated or incompatible APIs rather than incorrect repairs, and 64\% of successful repairs occur on entries invalid under the target version. We release a re-validated, version-pinned Bugs4Q benchmark and show that benchmark validation must precede repair evaluation.

Summary. This work introduces a constraint-preserving Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) framework tailored for personnel rostering. Instead of using penalty terms that allow exploration of invalid states, the authors embed hard constraints directly into the mixer Hamiltonian via guarded and tight-pattern mixers. This guarantees that the quantum evolution remains confined to only feasible schedules, leading to higher quality optimization results.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a concrete, mathematically rigorous technique for enforcing hard constraints in quantum optimization algorithms, which is crucial for applying QAOA to real-world scheduling problems where infeasible states must be strictly avoided.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses how to apply the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) to solve complex, constrained combinatorial optimization problems like personnel rostering.

Main result. The authors propose a novel constraint-preserving QAOA framework that embeds hard scheduling constraints directly into the mixer Hamiltonian, guaranteeing feasible evolution by construction.

Method. They develop specific mixer constructions—the guarded-XY and tight-pattern extensions—to confine the quantum dynamics exclusively to the subspace of physically valid solutions.

Model / system. The model is a binary rostering problem formulated on a transition graph, requiring satisfaction of daily coverage and no-consecutive-duty constraints. The physics involves designing specialized mixer Hamiltonians ($\hat{H}_M$) for QAOA.

Key observables. Output distribution quality (concentration on optimal feasible schedules), feasibility probability (aiming for unit probability by construction).

Important parameters / regimes. The workload parameters ($W_d$), the size of the roster ($N$ nurses, $D$ days), and the QAOA depth/mixing angles.

Assumptions / limitations. The core assumption is that constraints can be perfectly encoded into the mixer Hamiltonian to restrict dynamics, distinguishing this from penalty-based methods.

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically illustrates the binary encoding of a personnel roster for $N=4$ nurses and $D=4$ days.

Paper structure. The paper contrasts conventional penalty-based QAOA approaches with their novel constraint-preserving method. It details the construction of guarded and tight-pattern mixers, proves feasibility preservation by construction, and validates the approach via statevector simulations against existing formulations.

Abstract

The Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) is a promising framework for combinatorial optimization, but constrained problems are commonly handled using energetic penalty terms that require calibration and allow infeasible configurations to remain dynamically accessible. We develop a constraint-preserving QAOA framework for personnel rostering in which hard scheduling constraints are embedded directly into the mixer Hamiltonian. Using a binary rostering model with daily coverage and no-consecutive-duty constraints, we formulate the dynamics from a transition-graph perspective and introduce a guarded-XY mixer that confines the evolution to the fully feasible scheduling manifold. We further distinguish feasibility preservation from feasible-transition design and propose a tight-pattern extension that introduces collective feasible exchanges in saturated workload segments where local guarded exchanges alone are insufficient. Exact statevector simulations demonstrate that, compared with Penalty-X and Coverage-XY formulations under both expectation-value and Conditional Value-at-Risk optimization, the proposed approach eliminates hard-constraint penalty calibration, guarantees feasible evolution by construction, and consistently yields higher-quality output distributions with stronger concentration on optimal feasible schedules. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first constraint-preserving QAOA formulation for personnel rostering, and the transition-graph framework is readily applicable to a broad class of constrained quantum optimization problems.

Is the Eyring Plot Misleading? A Case for Arrhenius Analysis of Activation Parameters

Authors: Titus S. van Erp
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.09341v1

Summary. This paper critically examines whether the Eyring or Arrhenius formulation is more fundamentally correct for describing reaction rates. By analyzing theoretical derivations from TST using harmonic and hard-core potentials, the author argues that an Arrhenius analysis yields parameters ($\Delta H^\ddagger, \Delta S^\ddagger$) that are more physically consistent than those derived from the standard Eyring plot due to underlying mathematical inconsistencies.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on chemical kinetics, the rigorous comparison of different theoretical frameworks (Eyring vs. Arrhenius) for extracting fundamental thermodynamic quantities ($\Delta H^\ddagger, \Delta S^\ddagger$) is highly relevant to understanding approximations and limitations in statistical mechanical models used across AMO physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper debates the relative fundamental nature of the Eyring representation versus the Arrhenius representation for calculating chemical reaction rate constants from temperature-dependent measurements.

Main result. The author argues that activation enthalpies and entropies derived from an Arrhenius fit are arguably more physically relevant than those obtained directly from the standard Eyring plot analysis due to inherent dimensional mismatches in the free energy of activation ($\Delta G^\ddagger$).

Method. The analysis compares rate expressions derived from Transition State Theory (TST), contrasting harmonic approximation results with derivations using reactive flux formalism and proposing a modified potential restraint approach.

Model / system. The study models chemical reactions using TST, comparing the standard Eyring equation ($k \propto e^{-eta \Delta G^\ddagger}$) against the Arrhenius form ($k \propto e^{eta E_a}$). It utilizes harmonic and hard-core potential approximations to analyze the temperature dependence of activation parameters.

Key observables. Rate constant ($k$), Activation Enthalpy ($\Delta H^\ddagger$), Activation Entropy ($\Delta S^\ddagger$), Gibbs Free Energy of Activation ($\Delta G^\ddagger$).

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature ($T$), Planck's constant ($h$), Boltzmann constant ($k_B$), and various potential energy surface parameters.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on harmonic approximations for potentials, assumes the validity of TST derivations, and proposes modifications (like using an infinite square-well restraint) to improve parameter consistency.

Figures summary. Figure S1 illustrates a particle confined in a water slab between two membranes, representing a hard-core system used in model calculations.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the conflict between Eyring and Arrhenius interpretations; it then details derivations using classical statistical mechanics and reactive flux expressions; finally, it proposes an alternative framework using modified potential restraints to yield consistent Arrhenius parameters.

Abstract

A common view in physical chemistry literature is that the Eyring representation, in which a linear fit of $\ln(k/T)$ versus $1/T$ is attempted, is more fundamental than the Arrhenius representation, $\ln(k)$ versus $1/T$. This perception is typically motivated by its derivation from statistical mechanics and quantum mechanics, and by the interpretation of the intercept in terms of the activation entropy $ΔS^\ddagger$, whereas the Arrhenius equation and its prefactor are often regarded as purely phenomenological. However, harmonic approximation models yield exact linearity in Arrhenius plots but not in Eyring plots, although for real experimental data both generally appear equally linear within typical experimental accuracy. Furthermore, the impression that the Eyring formulation is inherently quantum mechanical arises from the presence of the Planck constant in the prefactor, whereas this term results from normalization conventions in the partition function. This also highlights an interpretational issue in $ΔG^\ddagger$, which is based on partition functions of different dimensionality between reactant and transition state. This dimensional mismatch can be reformulated in an alternative representation that improves interpretability and reduces to an Arrhenius-type expression in which the prefactor is directly related to an entropy of activation. In this framework, both activation enthalpy and entropy obtained from an Arrhenius fit are arguably more physically relevant than the corresponding Eyring fit values.

Quantum Logic as the Logic of Contexts

Authors: Haruki Emori, Atsushi Iriki, Andrei Khrennikov, Kazunori Kondo
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.09032v1

Summary. This paper redefines quantum logic by treating classical logic as a derived, information-losing image of a richer contextual calculus built on an orthomodular lattice. Using a component-wise algebra over contexts and bit vectors, the authors rigorously demonstrate that contextuality is fundamental, making classical reasoning a specific, restricted limit case.

Why it may be interesting. While highly abstract, this work provides a rigorous algebraic framework for understanding how quantum non-classicality (contextuality) structures logical inference beyond what is captured by standard Boolean or even orthomodular logics. It offers deep structural insights into the relationship between context and truth values.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To reverse the conventional understanding of quantum logic, arguing that classical logic is a quotient of a richer contextual quantum logic rather than being its foundation.

Main result. The paper proves that classical logic emerges as an information-losing, six-to-one image (a surjective homomorphism) from the full contextual calculus defined by the free orthomodular lattice on two generators.

Method. The authors develop a context–bit-vector calculus based on decomposing the 96-element free orthomodular lattice into a direct product of a context factor and a Boolean content factor.

Model / system. The model is an abstract algebraic structure: the free orthomodular lattice $F_2$ (with 96 elements), which is mathematically decomposed as $MO_2 imes 2^4$. The 'contexts' are represented by the non-distributive factor $MO_2$, and the 'content' by the Boolean factor $2^4$.

Key observables. Contextuality (dependence on context/order), commutativity of sublogics, and the structure of the projection onto classical logic.

Important parameters / regimes. The size of the lattice (96 elements); the number of contexts (six layers derived from $MO_2$); the dimension of the Boolean content (four bits).

Assumptions / limitations. Classical logic is treated as a limit or image resulting from 'forgetting' context, and structural analogies to physical processes like measurement are used.

Figures summary. The notes reference Table I showing the six-layer stratification of $F_2$ based on contexts, and Appendix A enumerating all 96 elements organized into these layers.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the algebraic model ($F_2 \sim MO_2 imes 2^4$), defines component-wise operations in the context–bit-vector calculus, classifies the resulting six layers based on commutativity, and culminates by proving the existence of the context-forgetting projection onto classical logic.

Abstract

Quantum logic is usually presented as a non-classical departure from ordinary reasoning forced on us by quantum mechanics, with classical logic kept as the secure starting point. We argue for the opposite order of explanation in a finite and fully computable setting. The free orthomodular lattice on two generators has ninety-six elements, the direct product of a six-element non-distributive factor and a sixteen-element Boolean factor. Reading the first factor as a register of contexts and the second as Boolean content, we obtain a calculus whose elements are context--bit-vector pairs and whose operations act component by component. With this calculus we establish three results. First, we classify the six layers by commutativity, identifying the central kernel of context-neutral propositions together with a dual central layer in which all complementary contexts are present. Second, we show that orthocomplementation rearranges the layers exactly as the complementation of the small factor rearranges its elements, which makes the duality among the layers rigid rather than accidental. Third, we prove that the operation forgetting the context is a surjective homomorphism of orthocomplemented lattices whose quotient is the classical Boolean algebra, so that classical logic is a six-to-one, information-losing image of the contextual calculus.

Summary. This work investigates the source of water's anomalous heat capacity by linking it directly to structural fluctuations between low-density and high-density local arrangements. Using molecular dynamics simulations, the authors show that these structural transitions, quantified by an order parameter, provide a microscopic explanation for both the supercooled maximum and the excess ambient $C_P$. Main problem. The study aims to elucidate the structural origin responsible for water's anomalously large isobaric heat capacity ($C_P$) and its characteristic maximum upon supercooling.

Main result. The anomalous $C_P$ is primarily attributed to structural fluctuations, quantified by a second-solvation-shell intruder order parameter ($ ext{SSSI}(3)$), which maps onto an effective enthalpy scale of $\approx 4 ext{ kJ/mol}$ governing the interconversion between low- and high-density-like local structures.

Method. The research employs molecular dynamics simulations, comparing classical and path-integral approaches to separate vibrational contributions from structural (configurational) ones. A two-state mapping is used to model the structural transitions.

Abstract

Water isobaric heat capacity is anomalously large under ambient conditions and exhibits a sharp maximum upon supercooling. Using classical and path-integral molecular dynamics with accurate machine-learning interatomic potentials, we show that nuclear quantum effects primarily act by suppressing high-frequency vibrations, while the anomalous temperature dependence of the isobaric heat capacity originates from structural fluctuations, quantified by the second-solvent-shell intruder order parameter. A simple two-state mapping reveals an effective enthalpy scale of about 4 kJ/mol associated with the interconversion of low- and high-density-like local structures, providing a microscopic link between their population changes and the excess heat capacity from supercooled to ambient conditions.

Abstract

Frequency combs, evenly spaced spectral lines locked to one fundamental frequency, are well known in optics and have also been found in phononic, magnonic, ferroelectric, and cosmological systems, but have not yet been studied in oscillating chemical reactions. We also test this idea experimentally using the Belousov-Zhabotinsky reaction. Intensity signals recorded at different points in a target pattern show a shared fundamental frequency with several weakening harmonics, matching the simulated pattern closely. Together, these results show that reaction-diffusion chemistry is a new platform for generating frequency combs.
