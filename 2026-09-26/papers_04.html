Microscopic theory of the collective optical response of dilute atomic clouds at finite temperature

Authors: Tanja Schoger, Romain Pierrat, Nicolas Cherroret
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.29786v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 2/5 · correlated / nonlocal dissipation 1/5

Summary. This paper presents a microscopic theory for the optical response of dilute atomic clouds at finite temperatures. It rigorously accounts for the suppression of collective light-induced dipole-dipole interactions due to thermal motion. The key finding is the continuous crossover of the resonance shift from the quantum Lamb shift at low T to the classical Lorentz-Lorenz shift at high T.

Why it may be interesting. This work is highly relevant to quantum optics and AMO physics as it provides a quantitative, microscopic understanding of how thermal decoherence affects collective quantum light-matter interactions, bridging the gap between quantum collective effects and classical limits.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops a microscopic theory to describe how thermal motion suppresses collective light-induced dipole-dipole interactions in dilute atomic clouds.

Main result. It shows that the resonance shift continuously evolves from the low-temperature collective Lamb shift to the high-temperature classical Lorentz-Lorenz shift, providing an exact description of finite-temperature corrections.

Method. The authors use a diagrammatic approach based on the Lippmann-Schwinger equation to derive the collective contribution to the optical permittivity, comparing analytical results with coupled-dipole simulations.

Model / system. The system is a dilute atomic gas of two-level atoms treated as an effectively infinite, translationally invariant medium. Atomic motion is modeled using ballistic trajectories under thermal equilibrium.

Key observables. Optical permittivity, refractive index, extinction coefficient, and the resonance shift.

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature (T), density (n), and the ratio of thermal velocity to decay rate ($ar{v}/\Gamma$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis initially uses a scalar approximation for the optical field and assumes the ballistic approximation for atomic motion.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate light scattering, showing how increasing density leads to collective response, and how finite temperature introduces random phase shifts that suppress this collective scattering.

Paper structure. The paper builds from the general theory of the optical response using the Lippmann-Schwinger equation, derives the self-energy contributions ($\Sigma_L$ and $\Sigma_B$) via diagrammatic expansion, and then analyzes the asymptotic limits (low and high temperature) for these contributions.

Abstract

At low temperature, collective light-induced dipole-dipole interactions are known to strongly reshape the optical response of atomic clouds, revealing the breakdown of the independent-scattering picture as the density increases. While thermal motion is generally believed to progressively suppress these interactions, the microscopic mechanisms behind this suppression remain largely unexplored. Here, we develop a microscopic theory of the optical response of a dilute atomic gas at finite temperature, explicitly accounting for atomic ballistic motion during the recurrent-scattering events associated with the collective corrections. Using a diagrammatic approach for scalar light, we derive the collective contribution to the optical permittivity and characterize its behavior across the full temperature range. We find that thermal corrections scale as $\sim T$ at low temperature, while recurrent-scattering contributions are suppressed as $\sim T^{-3/2}$ at high temperature. Our predictions are confirmed by extensive coupled-dipole simulations that explicitly account for atomic motion. These simulations also reveal the inaccuracy of the modified frozen-dipole approximation commonly used to treat thermal effects. Finally, extending our theory to vector light, we provide a complete picture of how the resonance shift continuously evolves from the collective Lamb shift at low temperatures to the classical Lorentz-Lorenz shift at high temperatures.

Universal holonomic control of algebras of observables

Summary. This paper develops a geometric theory for controlling quantum systems by analyzing the parallel transport of observable algebras along closed paths. It proves that this holonomy mechanism can universally implement all necessary logical gates and sector permutations. This establishes a rigorous link between the geometry of the observable space and the computational power of the quantum system.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a deep geometric framework connecting the algebraic structure of quantum observables to the realizable unitary operations, offering a powerful tool for designing quantum gates.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the geometric problem of determining how observable algebras must be transported (parallel transported) when the algebra itself undergoes a closed path evolution in the space of observables.

Main result. It shows that the parallel transport is uniquely fixed by unitary invariance, recovering Berry–Wilczek–Zee transport, and that closed paths can realize every inner logical gate and sector permutation on positive-dimensional strata.

Method. The analysis employs geometric control theory, defining a canonical connection and calculating the holonomy group associated with the evolution of the observable algebra structure.

Model / system. The system involves quantum states described by observable algebras $A$, analyzed using the Artin–Wedderburn decomposition. The control is achieved via paths in the space of these algebras, often related to band structures derived from gapped Hamiltonians.

Key observables. Holonomy ($ ext{hol}(\gamma)$), inner logical gates, sector permutations, and the curvature $F(\mu)$ of the connection.

Important parameters / regimes. The structure of the algebra (fixed block structure $ au$), the existence of a compatible gapped Hamiltonian, and the path taken ($\gamma$).

Assumptions / limitations. The results are derived for algebras with a fixed block structure, and the adiabatic implementation relies on the existence of required control paths.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the concept of holonomy for a closed control loop, showing how the failure of the lifted frame to close results in a non-trivial holonomy element belonging to $N(A)$.

Paper structure. The paper builds from defining the geometric problem of transport on observable algebras, deriving the canonical connection, calculating the holonomy for closed loops, and finally proving that these holonomies realize universal gates under adiabatic control.

Abstract

Closed paths of observable algebras can enact logical gates. We show that algebras of fixed block structure have a unique unitarily invariant parallel transport, recovering Berry--Wilczek--Zee transport on bands. On positive-dimensional configuration spaces, the holonomy group is the special-unitary part of the normalizer: every inner logical gate and every equal-type sector permutation is attainable. Given a compatible gapped Hamiltonian and access to the required paths, inner logical gates can be implemented adiabatically by isospectral control of a band refinement.

All you need is the universal correlation detector: A unified approach to universalize communication protocols over quantum channels

Summary. This paper introduces universal correlation detection as a foundational tool for quantum communication. By developing detectors that work optimally even when the exact quantum state or channel is unknown, the authors provide a unified framework. This allows for the systematic design of capacity-achieving coding schemes for various tasks over unknown quantum channels.

Why it may be interesting. The concept of universal detection methods is highly relevant for open quantum systems and quantum optics, as many real-world scenarios involve unknown noise channels or unknown initial states, making state-agnostic protocols crucial.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The central challenge is constructing optimal quantum information protocols when the underlying quantum states or communication channels are unknown or partially known.

Main result. The authors developed universal correlation detectors that achieve optimal first-order asymptotic performance, enabling the construction of channel-independent coding schemes that achieve capacity for various quantum communication tasks.

Method. The approach involves developing universal correlation detection as a common building block, combining it with position-based decoding and convex splitting techniques.

Model / system. The work analyzes bipartite quantum states ($ ho_{AB}$), classical-quantum states ($ ho_{XB}$), and general quantum channels, focusing on the detection of correlations.

Key observables. Mutual information $I(A:B)$, total correlation $I[S]$, and the performance of quantum hypothesis testing (Type I/Type II error rates).

Important parameters / regimes. The asymptotic performance is characterized by the first-order limit of the hypothesis testing divergence, $n$ (number of copies), and the marginal state information.

Assumptions / limitations. The work addresses the need for 'state-agnostic' or 'universal' detection, moving beyond the assumption of complete knowledge about the system state.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem of unknown channels, develops the universal correlation detector, proves its optimality for both general and classical-quantum states, and finally combines this detector with decoding techniques to construct capacity-achieving coding schemes for multiple tasks.

Abstract

Constructing optimal quantum information protocols without complete knowledge of the underlying states or channels is a central challenge. We address this challenge by developing universal correlation detection as a common building block for quantum communication. Our detectors distinguish a bipartite state from the product of its marginals and attain the same first-order asymptotic performance as optimal tests constructed with complete state information. For general bipartite quantum states, knowledge of a single marginal suffices: a detector depending only on that marginal is first-order optimal for every compatible state. For classical-quantum states, the detector is fully universal and requires no prior state information. By combining these detectors with position-based decoding and convex splitting, we construct channel-independent coding schemes that achieve capacity for a range of communication tasks. These results turn universal correlation detection into a systematic tool for universal protocol design, providing a unified route to designing capacity-achieving protocols for many communication tasks over unknown quantum channels.

Differential hierarchy of the Husimi representation

Authors: Zacharie Van Herstraeten, Ulysse Chabaud
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.29388v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5

Summary. This paper develops a novel, local criterion for determining if a phase-space function is a valid Husimi representation of a quantum state. By establishing a differential hierarchy based on recursively defined Fock minors, it shows that global quantum properties like positivity and operator rank can be determined solely by checking local differential inequalities on the function. This offers a powerful geometric tool for quantum state characterization.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, purely local, and differential geometric criterion for verifying quantum state properties (like positivity and rank) directly from a phase-space function, which is highly valuable for theoretical quantum optics and open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the difficulty of using phase-space representations to verify the fundamental quantum mechanical property of density operator positivity, which typically requires global conditions.

Main result. It establishes that the condition for a phase-space function to be a valid Husimi function is equivalent to a set of local, differential inequalities forming a differential hierarchy.

Method. The authors relate the algebraic characterization of quantum operators (via displaced Fock minors) to local differential conditions on the Husimi function, leading to a recurrence relation analogous to the Toda molecule equation.

Model / system. The study concerns general quantum states ($\hat{ ho}$) and operators ($\hat{O}$) represented in phase space via the Husimi Q-function. The analysis is performed using the framework of Bargmann-entire operators and coherent states.

Key observables. The primary observables are the displaced Fock minors ($M_n(\alpha)$) and the local derivatives of the Husimi function $Q(\alpha)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The rank of the operator ($ ext{rank}(\hat{O})$) and the degree of positivity are characterized by the highest non-vanishing minor in the hierarchy.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the operator is Bargmann-entire and relies on formal identities connecting minors to local derivatives, though convergence in some reconstruction formulas is noted as a limitation.

Figures summary. The notes do not describe specific figures, but detail the mathematical structure involving recurrence relations (e.g., Equation 2, Equation 10) and determinant identities.

Paper structure. The paper builds by first defining the problem (global vs. local positivity), then introducing the algebraic tools (Fock minors, rank characterization), and finally deriving the differential hierarchy by showing that these minors satisfy a specific recurrence relation, linking the structure to integrable systems.

Abstract

Phase-space representations reveal the geometry of quantum mechanics and distinguish quantum features from classical ones. However, the positivity of a density operator is among the properties of quantum states that phase-space representations capture least directly: deciding whether a phase-space function describes a physical quantum state has so far required global conditions coupling distant points in phase space. Here we show that these conditions can in fact be made local, by considering the Husimi representation in phase space. We establish a complete set of conditions which captures when a phase-space function $f$ is the Husimi function of a quantum state, with each condition involving only the derivatives of the function $f$ evaluated at a single phase-space point. We further show that each condition has an equivalent algebraic characterization, which takes the form of the nonnegativity of a determinant of the underlying operator in a displaced Fock basis. As a result, these conditions obey a recurrence relation organizing them into a differential hierarchy, which terminates based on the rank of the operator. Our results thus show that operator rank and positivity are local properties in phase space, reinforcing the role of phase-space representations as a geometric description of quantum mechanics.

Full-frequency GW from Cayley-transformed self-energy moments

Authors: Marcus K. Allen, George H. Booth
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.29271v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces a novel method to calculate the full-frequency GW self-energy by reformulating spectral moments using a Cayley transformation. This technique ensures numerical stability and achieves significantly faster convergence compared to prior moment-based approaches. It provides a robust, grid-free route to obtaining complete spectral information for electronic systems.

Why it may be interesting. The development of highly accurate, non-perturbative spectral methods for electronic structure is crucial for understanding strongly correlated materials where simple approximations fail.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To develop a stable, compact, and systematically improvable method for calculating the full-frequency Green's function ($G_0W_0$) spectrum of electronic systems, overcoming limitations of existing GW approximations.

Main result. The use of Cayley-transformed moment constraints provides a representation that converges substantially faster and more reliably than previous monomial moment approaches, allowing for the calculation of the full spectral information.

Method. The authors reformulate the GW self-energy moments using a Cayley transformation, which maps real frequencies to the unit circle. These moments are calculated via efficient contour integration and used to construct and diagonalize a Hermitian upfolded Hamiltonian.

Model / system. The theory is applied to general electronic systems, benchmarked on molecular calculations like Chlorophyll A. The underlying physics involves calculating the $G_0W_0$ self-energy ($\Sigma(\omega)$) within the framework of many-body perturbation theory.

Key observables. Full-frequency $G_0W_0$ Green's function, quasiparticle energies, satellite features, and their spectral weights.

Important parameters / regimes. Moment order ($n$), Cayley scale ($\omega_p$), and contour integration parameters ($\sigma$).

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes the ability to compute moments via contour integration and relies on the Cayley map to ensure moment boundedness and numerical stability.

Figures summary. Figures compare the convergence of excitation energies (HOMO/LUMO) using the new Cayley moment expansion (cmGW) versus the old monomial expansion (mGW), showing superior convergence for cmGW. Another figure shows the spectral function of Chlorophyll A for various moment orders.

Paper structure. The paper reviews previous monomial moment expansions, details the calculation of the new Cayley-transformed moments via contour integration, describes implementing Dyson's equation using the moment-conserving upfolded Hamiltonian, and presents convergence results.

Abstract

The dynamical GW self-energy approximation is a key computational tool to provide the fundamental spectrum of electronic systems. We reformulate this approximation, representing the particle and hole parts of the GW self-energy through a highly compact set of Cayley-transformed moment constraints. The Cayley transformation maps real frequencies to the unit circle, keeping the moments bounded as their order increases, ensuring numerical stability and allowing resolution to be focused on an energy range of interest. We calculate these Cayley-transformed moments via an efficient O[N$^4$] scaling contour integration, and from them, construct a Hermitian upfolded Hamiltonian with a linearly scaling dimensionality with system size. A single-shot diagonalization of this effective Hamiltonian gives an explicit full-frequency G0W0 Green's function with manifestly real poles and non-negative spectral weights. This enables quasiparticle energies, satellite features, and their spectral weights to be obtained across the full G0W0 spectrum. Comparisons with exact G0W0 calculations and convergence across the GW100 test set and the larger Chlorophyll A molecule demonstrate substantially faster and more reliable convergence with moment order than an earlier monomial-moment approach. These Cayley moment representations therefore provide a stable, compact, and systematically improvable route to the complete spectral information of zero-temperature GW, without explicit frequency grids, plasmon-pole models and other common approximations, or analytic continuation.

How Not to Build Microcrypt

Authors: Aditya Gulati, Dakshita Khurana, Kabir Tomer
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.30253v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5

Summary. This paper analyzes the fundamental assumptions required for quantum cryptography, specifically targeting primitives like PRS and PRU. By introducing NP-aided shadow tomography, the authors demonstrate that many proposed constructions inadvertently imply the existence of classical one-way functions or require computational hardness equivalent to NP. This work constrains the assumptions available for building quantum-secure protocols.

Why it may be interesting. While the focus is on complexity theory, the techniques developed for state learning and distinguishing quantum states from random ones are fundamental tools in quantum information theory, relevant for understanding the limits of quantum computation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The authors investigate the feasibility of constructing quantum cryptographic primitives, such as Pseudorandom States (PRS) and Pseudorandom Unitaries (PRU), without relying on the assumption of classical one-way functions.

Main result. They prove that many existing constructions for PRS/PRU, even those designed to avoid one-way functions, are shown to either imply the existence of one-way functions or imply NP hardness.

Method. The work introduces and utilizes NP-aided shadow tomography and develops new algorithms for learning quantum unitaries, employing contradiction arguments based on posterior sampling techniques.

Model / system. The analysis focuses on computable pure-state families of quantum states and unitary transformations, analyzed through their measurement distributions and learnability by quantum computers with access to an NP oracle.

Key observables. Amplitudes and phases of quantum states, measurement distributions (classical transcripts), and the fidelity between estimated and true key states.

Important parameters / regimes. Error bounds ($\epsilon, \delta$), computational complexity classes (NP, BQP), and the structure of the underlying cryptographic primitives (OWSG, PRS, PRU).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the existence of computable and samplable pure-state One-Way State Generators (OWSGs) and the ability to perform posterior sampling estimates.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the tools (NP-aided tomography, learning algorithms) and then applying these tools to show that specific classes of cryptographic candidates fail because they imply classical one-way functions or NP hardness.

Abstract

A key challenge in quantum cryptography is to build quantum one-wayness and pseudorandomness without the use of (quantum computable) one-way functions. So far, this has turned out to be a difficult task, with only a few proposed candidates that are not directly built from one-way functions. Even within these few proposed candidates, we have lacked the techniques to analyze when proposed constructions may inadvertently yield one-way functions. In this work, we introduce efficient NP-aided shadow tomography of quantum states. We prove that collections of {\em computable} pure states can be learned via efficient NP-aided shadow tomography, where we say that a state is computable if the amplitude and phase on any computational basis term can be classically efficiently computed given the description of an efficient preparation circuit for the state. We also give new algorithms for NP-aided learning of unitaries given polynomially many queries to the unitary. By building on this, we show that many existing architectures for PRS and PRU, including some that were explicitly introduced for the purposes of avoiding one-way functions (e.g., Hamiltonian Phase States, Bostanci et. al., TQC 2025), actually do imply the existence of one-way functions or imply \(\NP\) hardness. We hope that these no-go results will inform future investigations into building PRS and PRUs from assumptions that are plausibly outside the complexity class NP.

Optimal entanglement criteria from trace invariants

Authors: Bivas Mallick, Aabhas Gulati, Ion Nechita
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.29471v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 2/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops a rigorous mathematical framework to find the best entanglement criterion based only on measurable local unitary trace invariants. By employing convex duality and solving truncated moment problems, the authors derive explicit, dimension-free separability inequalities. This advances the understanding of entanglement detection by connecting it to moment theory.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides rigorous mathematical tools (convex duality, moment problems) to establish necessary and sufficient conditions for separability based on measurable quantities (trace invariants), which is fundamental to quantum information theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses determining the optimal entanglement criterion that can be constructed using only a finite set of local unitary trace invariants, as randomized measurements only provide access to these invariants, not the full quantum state.

Main result. The authors provide a framework using convex duality to solve the associated truncated moment problem, yielding explicit, dimension-free families of separability inequalities (binomial and L1-weighted).

Method. The core methodology involves applying convex duality to relate the cone of separable states to the polar dual of a moment cone, solving the resulting moment problem via semidefinite programming relaxations.

Model / system. The focus is on bipartite quantum states $ ho$ on $\mathcal{C}_d \otimes \mathcal{C}_d$, specifically determining when they are separable ($ ho = \sum p_i \sigma_{A,i} \otimes \sigma_{B,i}$).

Key observables. Local unitary trace invariants, moment vectors derived from partial transposition and realignment matrices, and the optimal $m$-moment relaxation $\alpha(r)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The number of moments considered, $m$, and the local dimensions $d_A, d_B$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the assumption that the best criterion can be found by optimizing over a finite number of moment constraints derived from trace invariants.

Figures summary. Not specified.

Paper structure. The paper systematically introduces the cone of local unitary trace inequalities for separable states, relates this to moment cones via duality, analyzes specific criteria like PPT and realignment using moment matching, and finally solves the resulting truncated moment problem to derive optimal inequalities.

Abstract

Randomized measurement protocols give experimental access to low-degree polynomial invariants of a quantum state rather than to the state itself, which raises the question of what the best entanglement criterion is that can be built from finitely many such invariants. In this work we answer this question through convex duality. For each degree we introduce the cone of local unitary trace inequalities that are valid for separable bipartite states in all local dimensions, together with its one-matrix counterparts for Hermitian and positive semidefinite matrices. Each cone is the polar dual of a moment cone; we also determine the minimal combinatorial parametrization of the separable cone. The framework organizes the known moment criteria: the PPT criterion using the first three moments of the partial transposition is the image under an embedding of a Hankel determinant from the positive semidefinite cone to the bipartite cone, which we show to be an extremal ray, and we determine the first nontrivial separable cone completely, finding exactly four extremal rays. The framework allows us to analyze the realignment criterion and its centered (or enhanced) variant. We prove that the even singular value moments of the (centered) realignment matrix are trace invariants, associated with explicit fixed-point-free involutions. Extracting the optimal trace-norm bound from $m$ such moments thus becomes a dimension-free truncated moment problem, which we solve in closed form for $m = 2$ and relax to a semidefinite program of size $O(m)$ for arbitrary $m$. The dual solutions are polynomial minorants of $\sqrt{x}$ on $[0,1]$, and two explicit families, binomial and $L^1$-weighted, yield hierarchies of separability inequalities. Finally, we compare the criteria against one another and on PPT entangled states.

Upper critical dimension for dirty Weyl semimetal-to-metal quantum phase transitions

Authors: Yongtai Li, Bitan Roy

Summary. This paper analyzes the quantum phase transition from a semimetal to a metal in disordered Weyl semimetals. By combining numerical simulations with advanced renormalization group techniques, the authors establish that the transition is governed by critical dimensions at $d=2$ and $d=4$. The findings provide universal scaling laws for the critical exponents, offering a deep theoretical understanding of disorder effects in topological matter.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a detailed theoretical framework for understanding quantum criticality in topological materials under disorder, which has implications for understanding transport properties in disordered quantum systems relevant to AMO physics and condensed matter theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the disorder-driven semimetal-to-metal quantum phase transition (QPT) occurring in Weyl semimetals (WSMs). It aims to determine the critical dimensions governing this unconventional transition.

Main result. The analysis strongly suggests that the lower critical dimension is $d_\ell=2$ and the upper critical dimension is $d_u=4$. Furthermore, the dynamic scaling exponent is found to be $z \approx d/2$ for $d \geq 3$.

Method. The study employs a combination of numerical computations (Kernel Polynomial Method for ADOS) and advanced theoretical techniques, including the self-consistent Born approximation (SCBA) and field-theoretic renormalization group (RG) calculations ($\epsilon_d$ and $\epsilon_m$ expansions).

Model / system. The system under study is a Weyl semimetal described by a tight-binding Hamiltonian on a $d$-dimensional hypercubic lattice, subjected to random pointlike charge impurities (disorder strength $W$).

Key observables. Average Density of States (ADOS) $ ho(E)$, the order parameter $ ho(0)$, the correlation length exponent ($ u$), and the dynamic scaling exponent ($z$).

Important parameters / regimes. The spatial dimension $d$, the disorder strength $W$, and the critical disorder strength $W_c$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the validity of the SCBA and the results derived from the $\epsilon_m$ RG expansion, which is considered more reliable than the $\epsilon_d$ expansion.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the calculated ADOS $ ho(E)$ across various dimensions ($d=2$ to $6$) and show the scaling behavior of $ ho(0)$ and the determination of critical exponents ($ u, z$) as a function of dimension.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the QPT using numerical ADOS calculations, followed by rigorous theoretical analysis using RG techniques to pinpoint the critical dimensions ($d_\ell=2, d_u=4$) and derive universal scaling exponents.

Abstract

Weyl or Dirac fermions with the iconic linear energy-momentum relation and average density of states (ADOS) $ρ(E) \sim |E|^{d-1}$ at energy $E$ in $d$ spatial dimensions, constitute a unique setup to study the disorder-driven semimetal-to-metal quantum phase transition (QPT) between ballistic (realized for weak disorder) and diffusive (stabilized at stronger disorder) quasiparticles. Such a QPT takes place only for $d>2$ and falls beyond the realm of the Anderson metal-to-insulator transition. From numerically computed ADOS (using the kernel polynomial method) in dirty Weyl systems in $d=2$ to $6$, here we show that $d=2$ and $d=4$ are the lower ($d_\ell$) and upper ($d_u$) critical dimensions for such an unconventional QPT, respectively, as suggested from the solution of quasiparticle lifetime within the self-consistent Born approximation. Consequently, for $d \geq 4$ the associated correlation length exponent is found to be $ν\approx 0.5$ (within numerical accuracy). However, the dynamic scaling exponent at the quantum critical point is pinned close to $z \approx d/2$ (numerically) for any $d \geq 3$, which is shown to be an exact result from a field-theoretic renormalization group calculation. Therefore, Weyl semimetal-to-metal QPTs can be studied field theoretically around both $d_\ell$ and $d_u$.

Consistent determination of stability regimes in natural ecological communities from abundance time series

Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.30154v1

Summary. This paper introduces a statistical method to assess the stability of complex ecological communities using only long-term species abundance time series. By applying concepts from dynamical mean-field theory to stochastic Lotka-Volterra dynamics, the authors classify communities into regimes of stable coexistence, quasi-extinction, or unbounded growth. This provides a powerful, non-invasive tool for quantifying species extinction risk in ecology.

Why it may be interesting. The mathematical machinery used—analyzing steady-state distributions (Gamma law) and phase transitions based on noise and interaction parameters—is highly analogous to techniques used in non-equilibrium statistical mechanics for complex interacting systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To develop a statistical framework that infers the stability regimes of large ecological communities directly from species abundance time series, bypassing the need to measure direct species interactions.

Main result. The theory predicts three distinct stability regimes (stable coexistence, quasi-extinction, and unbounded growth), with the stability determined by the relationship between environmental stochasticity and interaction strengths.

Method. The authors use Dynamical Mean-Field Theory (DMFT) applied to stochastic Generalized Lotka-Volterra dynamics, reducing the multi-species system to an effective single-species stochastic process analyzed via multivariate regression.

Model / system. The system models community dynamics using stochastic GLV equations with random interactions and environmental noise. The analysis focuses on the steady-state probability distribution, which is predicted to follow a Gamma law.

Key observables. Species abundance time series, steady-state species abundance distribution (SAD), and the coefficient of variation (CV) of abundance.

Important parameters / regimes. The stability is governed by parameters related to environmental noise intensity ($\sigma^2_{env}$), interaction variance ($\sigma^2_{int}$), and the mean growth rate ($\mu$).

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes that the dynamics can be effectively described by the stochastic GLV model and that the system can be reduced via DMFT to an effective single-species process.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the statistical framework linking time series to stability phases, show parameter recovery using synthetic data, and present phase diagrams classifying different ecological datasets into SC, QE, or UG regimes.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the statistical framework using DMFT on stochastic GLV dynamics, deriving the Gamma-distributed steady-state solution, defining the criteria for the three stability regimes based on system parameters, and finally applying this method to analyze empirical ecological datasets.

Abstract

The stability of an ecological community is conventionally defined through species interactions, which quantify how species affect one another. Interaction strengths are notoriously difficult to measure in species-rich assemblages. Long-term monitoring of species-rich communities, however, provides species abundance time series, increasingly available across habitats and taxa but not yet connected to the stability properties of the communities they describe. Here we develop a statistical framework that infers stability regimes in large ecological communities directly from abundance time series. We study stochastic Generalized Lotka--Volterra dynamics with random interactions and environmental fluctuations using dynamical mean-field theory, reducing the multispecies system to an effective stochastic process for a representative species. The theory predicts three stability regimes (stable coexistence, intermittent dynamics close to extinction, and unbounded growth) separated by analytical boundaries, and shows that environmental stochasticity systematically destabilizes coexistence by promoting intermittent low-abundance dynamics. The resulting steady-state species abundance distribution is a Gamma law that ties the dynamical phases to the variability-based stability metrics used in empirical studies. Recasting the effective dynamics as a multivariate regression model, we infer interaction statistics, environmental variability and the characteristic timescale of the dynamics from community data, without reconstructing the interaction network. Applied to natural communities spanning a broad range of habitats and taxa, the method resolves contrasting stability regimes and yields quantitative estimates of species extinction risk.

Summary. This paper introduces MOR+EC, a novel framework that combines Model Order Reduction and Eigenvector Continuation to create compact, reusable models for calculating Green's functions. Abstract

We introduce the learned-projector quantum alternating operator ansatz (LP-QAOA), a multistage protocol that freezes optimized circuits and uses their output states to define later projector mixers. Abstract
