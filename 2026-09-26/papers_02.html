Anisotropic information geometry of entropy production

Summary. This theoretical paper develops a geometric framework to decompose entropy production in open quantum systems into three orthogonal components. It reveals that these components are governed by distinct geometric constraints, leading to rigorous, physically accessible upper and lower bounds on the total entropy production. This advances the understanding of non-equilibrium thermodynamics by linking it to information geometry.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides powerful geometric tools to quantify non-equilibrium processes, linking fundamental concepts like entropy production to measurable physical quantities and geometric constraints, which is highly relevant for understanding thermalization and dissipation in complex quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to provide a unified geometric framework to analyze the intrinsic anisotropy of entropy production in open quantum systems.

Main result. Entropy production is decomposed into three geometrically distinct contributions (temperature deviation, environment deviation, and correlations), each obeying fundamentally different bounds.

Method. The analysis uses the quantum relative entropy and applies a hierarchical projection structure to achieve an exact orthogonal decomposition of the entropy production functional.

Model / system. The system is an open quantum system composed of a system (S) coupled to an environment (E), governed by a total Hamiltonian H(t). The framework is shown to apply to classical Markovian dynamics as a limit.

Key observables. Entropy production ($\Sigma( au)$), and its three orthogonal geometric components ($D_X, D_Y, D_Z$).

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis assumes $\hbar=kB=1$. The bounds depend on system/environment dimensions and mean energy/Hamiltonian variance.

Assumptions / limitations. The decomposition relies on the orthogonality condition for quantum relative entropy, and the analysis uses the quantum speed limit to derive bounds.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the geometric decomposition of entropy production in both open quantum systems and classical Markovian systems, showing the vanishing of the correlation component in the latter.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the geometric framework using quantum relative entropy, derives the three-component orthogonal decomposition of entropy production, analyzes the distinct geometric bounds for each component, and finally combines this with dynamical speed limits to establish rigorous bounds on $\Sigma( au)$.

Abstract

We reveal that the information geometry of entropy production exhibits an intrinsic anisotropy, providing a unified origin for the inequivalent geometric constraints obeyed by its different components. Using a hierarchical projection structure of quantum relative entropy, we derive an exact orthogonal decomposition of entropy production into three distinct geometric contributions associated with the deviation of the Gibbs projection temperature from the reference temperature, the deviation of the environment from its Gibbs projection state, and system--environment correlations. We demonstrate that these contributions obey fundamentally different geometric bounds: the first reduces to a classical Kullback--Leibler divergence and admits no universal upper bound in terms of the trace distance and the system and environment dimensions alone, whereas the latter two admit rigorous distance-based bounds with dimension-dependent factors. The framework also applies to classical Markovian dynamics described solely in terms of the system state, for which the system--environment correlation contribution is absent from the decomposition. Combining this geometric decomposition with dynamical speed limits, we obtain rigorous bounds on entropy production expressed solely in terms of physically accessible quantities, such as the mean energy and Hamiltonian variance.

Summary. This paper introduces a novel, multidimensional method to characterize node importance in complex networks by analyzing the system's response to small perturbations via the Green function. It extracts three complementary metrics—Influence, Efficiency, and Distortion—which quantify different aspects of how a perturbation propagates. This approach moves beyond single structural measures by providing a detailed, physically interpretable decomposition of dynamical node behavior.

Why it may be interesting. The methodology of using linear response theory (Green functions) to extract multiple, complementary dynamical metrics (temporal, spectral, cumulative) is highly relevant to analyzing open quantum systems or complex many-body dynamics where single metrics fail to capture full system behavior.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Conventional centrality measures are insufficient because they only capture structural relations and fail to resolve the temporal and spectral organization of dynamical perturbation responses.

Main result. The framework successfully decomposes a common dynamical response into three distinct, physically interpretable characteristics: Influence, Efficiency, and Distortion.

Method. The study develops a multidimensional characterization framework by analyzing the Green function of a linearized networked system.

Model / system. The framework is applied to weighted Kuramoto–Sakaguchi dynamics on heterogeneous Barabási–Albert networks, modeling coupled oscillators on a complex network structure.

Key observables. Influence (cumulative response strength), Efficiency (temporal rate of response), and Distortion (spectral concentration), alongside a source--target contribution matrix.

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis is performed on BA networks with specific parameters (e.g., N=200, m=4) and involves time-window truncation for numerical stability.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis fundamentally relies on the linear-response assumption, which limits its applicability to strong perturbations or highly nonlinear regimes.

Figures summary. The notes mention a correlation analysis (Table I) comparing the three dynamical measures against conventional structural centralities (Degree, Betweenness, etc.).

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the need for a multidimensional characterization, then details the linear-response construction using the Green function, defines the three indicators (I, E, D) and their contribution matrix, and finally validates the approach numerically and discusses limitations.

Abstract

Conventional centrality measures provide compact descriptions of node importance, but they emphasize specific structural relations and do not directly resolve the temporal and spectral organization of dynamical perturbation responses. Building on the Green function of a linearized networked system, we develop a multidimensional framework for dynamical node characterization. From the same response function, we extract three complementary indicators---Influence, Efficiency, and Distortion---that quantify cumulative response strength, temporal rate of response, and spectral concentration, respectively, together with a contribution matrix that resolves these quantities into source--target pathways. As a numerical demonstration, we apply the framework to weighted Kuramoto--Sakaguchi dynamics on heterogeneous Barabási--Albert networks. The three indicators distinguish implanted dynamical node classes through complementary dimensions of the perturbation response, while remaining strongly coupled to conventional topological centralities. These results demonstrate how a common dynamical response can be decomposed into distinct, physically interpretable characteristics rather than represented by a single aggregate descriptor. We finally discuss the scope and limitations of the linear-response assumption and extensions to time-dependent and nonlinear regimes.

Summary. This work develops and validates Superadiabatic Dynamical Density Functional Theory (SDDFT) for modeling the relaxation dynamics of 1D hard-rod fluids. By incorporating superadiabatic effects beyond the standard adiabatic approximation, SDDFT achieves quantitative agreement with Brownian Dynamics simulations. The study highlights the critical role of non-adiabatic terms in accurately describing non-equilibrium transport in soft matter systems.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical fluids, the rigorous development of non-equilibrium dynamics using density functionals and the explicit separation of adiabatic vs. non-adiabatic (superadiabatic) contributions provides a powerful methodological template applicable to open quantum systems where memory effects are crucial.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of accurately describing the non-equilibrium relaxation dynamics of interacting fluids, specifically focusing on capturing superadiabatic effects beyond standard adiabatic approximations.

Main result. Superadiabatic Dynamical Density Functional Theory (SDDFT) shows excellent quantitative agreement with Brownian Dynamics simulations for the total current, suggesting it correctly captures non-adiabatic memory effects.

Method. The authors employ advanced theoretical frameworks like SDDFT and Power Functional Theory (PFT), comparing them against molecular dynamics simulations.

Model / system. The system studied is a one-dimensional (1D) fluid composed of hard rods, whose dynamics are modeled using the Smoluchowski equation. The theory builds upon classical DFT by incorporating higher-order correlations up to the three-particle level.

Key observables. One-body density profile ($ ho(r, t)$), total current ($j_{tot}$), adiabatic current ($j_{ad}$), and superadiabatic current ($j_{sup}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The comparison is sensitive to ensemble differences (canonical vs. grand canonical) and the degree of superadiabaticity.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary approximations involve the closure relations used in SDDFT (at the three-particle level) and the functional ansatz used in PFT. A major limitation noted is the discrepancy arising from ensemble mismatch.

Figures summary. Figures compare the time evolution of the total current ($j_{tot}$) and its components ($j_{ad}, j_{sup}$) across DDFT, SDDFT, PFT, and BD simulations, highlighting the success of SDDFT.

Paper structure. The paper systematically develops SDDFT by extending DDFT to include three-particle correlations. It then compares this result against PFT and benchmark BD simulations, analyzing the contributions of the adiabatic and superadiabatic currents.

Abstract

We study the relaxation dynamics of a one-dimensional system of hard rods at the level of one-body fields using superadiabatic dynamical density functional theory, and compare the results with Brownian dynamics simulations. The theory uses the exact equilibrium excess free energy functional and incorporates superadiabatic effects from first principles via an adiabatic closure relation at the three-particle level. The predicted superadiabatic response shows very good agreement with simulation data, provided that initial-state ensemble differences between theory and simulations remain small. Furthermore, we evaluate an approximate power functional theory based on a velocity-gradient expansion, which also yields an accurate superadiabatic response, although requiring a memory kernel extracted from simulation data.

Summary. This work characterizes quantum channels generated by Clifford interactions with an environment using tools from symplectic geometry. It develops a 'channel normal form' to classify these interactions based on their information-theoretic properties. The findings distinguish between different interaction types, providing new bounds on quantum and private capacities.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical framework connecting symplectic geometry to channel capacity provides powerful tools for understanding decoherence and information flow in open quantum systems, which is highly relevant to quantum optics and open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper characterizes quantum information transmission through channels induced by bipartite Clifford interactions with an environment, extending concepts from positive discrete quantum convolution.

Main result. It establishes a classification of these interactions, showing a key distinction: those locally equivalent to positive convolution yield entanglement-breaking channels, while others support noiseless quantum transmission.

Method. The analysis employs symplectic geometry, Weyl operators, and the development of a 'channel normal form' to analyze the channel action $ ho oxtimes_S \sigma$.

Model / system. The system models quantum channels defined by Clifford interactions involving signal input ($A$), environment input ($B$), and corresponding outputs ($A', B'$), analyzed using symplectic matrices $S \in ext{Sp}(V \oplus V)$.

Key observables. Quantum and private capacities, the cross-ratio invariant, and the modified relative entropy of magic.

Important parameters / regimes. The regularity of the interaction matrix $S$, and the structure of the environmental stabilizers.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis focuses specifically on Clifford interactions and assumes the initial independence of the signal and environment.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the channel as a two-input/two-output system, and Figure 2 shows the Clifford transport identity, relating environmental change to signal-input and two output Cliffords.

Paper structure. The paper develops the framework using symplectic geometry, proves identities for convolution and transport, establishes a transport criterion based on the cross-ratio, and finally derives capacity bounds using the modified relative entropy of magic.

Abstract

We study how Clifford interactions with a fixed environment determine quantum transmission, in a setting that extends positive discrete quantum convolution . We characterize when Clifford operations on the environment can be replaced by operations on the signal input and the two outputs, and classify the interactions under local Clifford transformations . For pure stabilizer environments, we determine the noiseless, dephasing, and completely depolarizing parts of the channel directly from the interaction and environmental stabilizers, obtaining its quantum and private capacities . This reveals a distinction between interactions: those locally equivalent to positive convolution produce entanglement-breaking channels for every pure stabilizer environment, whereas every other interaction in the class admits noiseless quantum transmission with a suitable pure stabilizer environment . For positive convolution, environmental Clifford transport also allows us to separate the stabilized coordinates of an arbitrary mixed environment . These coordinates carry only a classical label shared by the receiver and environment, so removing them preserves quantum and private capacities . The resulting channel reduction yields a private-capacity bound by the modified relative entropy of magic of the environmental state .

Abstract

Quantum heat transport governs energy exchange processes and statistical laws in non-equilibrium quantum systems, and plays a pivotal role in quantum thermodynamics. We investigate the steady-state thermal transport of a noncommuting coupled spin system. We employ the quantum dressed master equation approach within the framework of open quantum system theory to accurately analyze the non-equilibrium dynamics, ensuring the validity of transport results in the strong coupling regime. Our results demonstrate that noncommuting spin coupling serves as a significant resource for modulating the nonlinearity of the heat current. Specifically, in the weak spin-coupling regime, the system exhibits robust negative differential thermal conductance (NDTC) across various spin numbers. By deriving analytical expressions for the heat current in both the single-spin and large-spin limits, we reveal that this NDTC behavior is governed by microscopic cycle fluxes. Physically, this arises because spin excitation channels induced by the cold reservoir are suppressed under a large temperature bias, thereby blocking energy exchange cycles. Conversely, in the strong spin-coupling and large temperature bias regime, the quantum system demonstrates pronounced thermal rectification. This high rectification efficiency originates from the unidirectional saturation of the heat current, rendering the system a promising candidate for high-performance thermal diodes. Furthermore, we extend the model to a three-terminal configuration to construct a quantum thermal transistor. By manipulating the temperature of the gate reservoir, we achieve efficient modulation and amplification of heat flow between the source and drain. The heat amplification factor is shown to far exceed unity in specific operating regions, confirming significant thermal amplification.

Abstract

A common belief in Kondo physics is that a magnetic impurity can act as an efficient probe of key properties of its host bath. In altermagnets, however, this intuition fails in a symmetry-enforced way: the momentum-dependent spin splitting cancels in the local impurity spectrum, making a conventional single-impurity Kondo resonance essentially blind to the altermagnetic form factor. Here, we overcome this constraint by introducing Floquet symmetry conversion. We show that an in-plane AC field converts the hidden altermagnetic spin splitting into a locally detectable spin-dependent hybridization channel, which is further amplified by Kondo correlations into a novel altermagnetic Kondo effect. Remarkably, the Kondo splitting exhibits a nontrivial dependence on the field orientation and inherits the same crystalline form factor as the altermagnetic host, thereby serving as Kondo tomography of altermagnetic spin splitting. This field-controlled response further provides a natural route to symmetry-controlled Kondo logic gates. Our results establish a field-controllable altermagnetic Kondo effect, linking symmetry-resolved impurity spectroscopy to Kondo logic functionality.

Abstract

The topological order of the Kitaev toric code is insensitive to disorder in its couplings since all star and plaquette operators commute; consequently, the stabilizer ground-state manifold is independent of the individual coupling strengths, and the phase remains stable against weak local perturbations. This insensitivity, however, is a property of the eigenstates and not of the dynamics they generate, and we show that the disorder sensitivity of a probe prepared outside that eigenbasis can be a metrological resource. In particular, we investigate the estimation of the strength of three topological-order-breaking perturbations, the nonlinear Castelnovo-Chamon deformation, nearest-neighbor Ising interactions, and a magnetic field along horizontal edges, encoded on an initially separable probe through unitary evolution under the disordered toric code Hamiltonian. We demonstrate that the quenched average quantum Fisher information (QFI), a figure of merit for metrological precision, exceeds that of its ordered counterpart in suitable regimes of perturbation strength and encoding time. For the ordered case, we further identify optimal product probes, for which the quadratic and cubic terms of the short-time expansion vanish identically, leaving a transient quartic scaling of QFI. Interestingly, this window persists as long as the multipartite entanglement generated by the encoding continues to increase, and it survives under local dephasing, bit-flip, and amplitude damping noise acting independently on each site, with the non-unital channel being the least detrimental.

Abstract

Magic-state cultivation offers a promising alternative for lowering the cost of non-Clifford operations in fault-tolerant quantum computing (FTQC). However, realizing cultivation in practice exposes two challenges: (i) the lack of an open-source classical runtime layer between logical software and physical control, and (ii) the latency constraints on protocol-specific decisions that determine magic-state readiness. To address these challenges, we adopt an algorithm--hardware co-design approach. At the algorithm level, we develop a two-stage early-escape scheme that identifies an informative subset of detectors offline, constructs a compact decoding problem, and performs partial decoding in parallel with complete decoding at runtime, allowing high-confidence attempts to advance before full decoding completes. At the hardware level, we present MagiCFirm, a configurable runtime that combines offline-compiled microprograms with dedicated datapaths for detector construction, event processing, and protocol control, enabling end-to-end execution of magic-state cultivation. Across evaluated configurations, MagiCFirm reduces wall-clock magic-state preparation time by up to 39.3% at matched logical error rate. For a representative magic-state-bound workload, this translates to an estimated 11% reduction in overall application runtime.
