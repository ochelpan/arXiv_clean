One-shot Routing in Quantum Networks

Authors: Nadav Lavi, Nir Gutman, Ido Kaminer, Ariel Orda
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.29752v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper tackles the challenging one-shot routing problem in quantum networks, where the goal is maximizing connection fidelity from finite resources. The authors develop the FOLD heuristic, a polynomial-time algorithm that efficiently finds near-optimal routing schedules by intelligently interleaving entanglement swapping and distillation. This work is vital for designing scalable quantum communication protocols.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a concrete, computationally tractable algorithm (FOLD) for optimizing resource utilization in quantum communication networks, which is crucial for scaling up quantum internet infrastructure.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the complex 'one-shot routing' problem in quantum networks, which requires optimizing end-to-end fidelity from a fixed, limited set of entangled resources rather than maximizing the rate of resource generation.

Main result. The proposed heuristic, FOLD, runs in polynomial time and achieves near-optimal end-to-end fidelity, significantly outperforming exact scheduling methods whose complexity is super-exponential.

Method. The authors introduce the Fidelity-Optimizing Local Detours (FOLD) heuristic, which iteratively improves paths by merging detours only when distillation boosts the overall fidelity on both branches.

Model / system. The model is a quantum network connecting nodes via links characterized by Werner-state entangled pairs. The process involves jointly optimizing path selection, entanglement swapping, and distillation schedules.

Key observables. End-to-end fidelity ($F = \langle\Psi^-| ho|\Psi^- angle$), which quantifies the quality of the quantum connection.

Important parameters / regimes. The size of the network (number of nodes $n$ and edges $m$), the circuit rank $r(G)$, and the path budget $k$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the ability to evaluate fidelity for swapping and distillation operations, and the heuristic relies on bounding the number of successful merges by the graph's cycle rank.

Figures summary. Figures compare fidelity achieved by various scheduling strategies (e.g., purify-then-swap vs. optimal interleaving) and demonstrate the polynomial scaling of the FOLD heuristic compared to exponential exact solvers.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the one-shot routing formulation, contrasts it with rate-based models, develops the mathematical framework for fidelity calculation, introduces the intractable exact solution, and finally proposes and analyzes the polynomial-time FOLD heuristic.

Abstract

Distributed quantum computation requires many entangled pairs to be available simultaneously, so routing must optimize fidelity from a fixed, short-lived set of network resources rather than the rate of pairs accumulated over time. We formulate this one-shot routing problem for heterogeneous Werner-state links and jointly optimize path selection and the schedule of entanglement swapping and distillation. We show that the common purify-then-swap ordering is not optimal network-wide and that exact schedule enumeration requires $m^{Θ(m)}$ time. We introduce Fidelity-Optimizing Local Detours (FOLD), a heuristic that starts from the highest-fidelity path and merges a detour only when distillation improves on both routes and raises end-to-end fidelity. FOLD runs in polynomial time, recovers almost all of the optimal fidelity on tractable instances, and is four orders of magnitude faster than exact optimization at circuit rank $5$. On the Internet topologies where any strategy improves on shortest-path routing, FOLD achieves the highest mean end-to-end fidelity among the polynomial baselines. We further extend the formulation to links whose fidelities are known only as distributions, where uncertainty, rather than the effort spent routing, limits the fidelity delivered. Through an example of requests competing for the same links, we show that coordination becomes a condition for feasibility, and that a shared path budget controls the fidelity trade-off between them.

Optimal Two-Qubit Gate-Cutting Cost and Measures of Nonlocality

Authors: Michael Hart
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.30141v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical paper analyzes the optimal cost ($\gamma$) associated with gate-cutting, a method for decomposing nonlocal quantum gates. It establishes that $\gamma$ is mathematically linked to the operator-Schmidt spectrum and derives sharp bounding curves showing how five distinct measures of gate nonlocality constrain this cost. The work reveals that no single measure universally determines the optimal cost, providing deep insight into the structure of quantum gate resources.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous quantification of how different measures of nonlocality constrain the fundamental cost of simulating quantum gates is highly relevant for understanding resource theories in quantum computation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to determine the relationship between the optimal two-qubit gate-cutting cost ($\gamma$) and various established measures of two-qubit gate nonlocality.

Main result. The optimal cost $\gamma$ is shown to be a Rényi-1/2 functional of the operator-Schmidt spectrum, and the study derives sharp lower and upper envelopes connecting $\gamma$ to five distinct nonlocality descriptors.

Method. The analysis involves recasting $\gamma$ as a Rényi functional and deriving sharp bounds by analyzing canonical one-parameter families of two-qubit unitaries.

Model / system. The system under study is two-qubit unitary gates ($U$), which are parameterized using the Cartan decomposition, restricting the analysis to the non-negative Cartan tetrahedron.

Key observables. Optimal QPD Extent ($\gamma$), Entangling power ($e_p$), Gate typicality ($g_t$), Operator entanglement ($E_{op}$), Schmidt strength ($K_{Sch}$), and Maximum product-input concurrence ($C_{max}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The angles ($ heta_1, heta_2, heta_3$) defining the unitary gate within the Cartan parameters, and the normalized operator-Schmidt spectrum $p$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes invariance of $\gamma$ and the descriptors under local pre- and post-unitaries, allowing restriction to the diagonal unitary form $U_d$.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate $\gamma$ versus the five nonlocality descriptors ($e_p, g_t, E_{op}, K_{Sch}$), showing the derived lower and upper bounding envelopes for the optimal gate-cutting cost.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the connection between gate-cutting and the operator-Schmidt spectrum, derives the functional form of $\gamma$, and then systematically derives and compares the sharp lower and upper envelopes imposed by each of the five nonlocality descriptors.

Abstract

Circuit cutting enables quantum computations to be decomposed into smaller subcircuits at the cost of additional sampling overhead. Gate-cutting provides one such approach by replacing nonlocal gates with quasiprobability decompositions of local operations. Starting from the known optimal two-qubit gate-cutting formula, we derive complete sharp lower and upper envelopes relating the quasiprobability extent $γ$ to five established descriptors: entangling power, gate typicality, operator entanglement, Schmidt strength, and maximum product-input concurrence. We recast $γ$ as a Rényi-$1/2$ functional of the operator-Schmidt spectrum and show how the physical constraints on two-qubit spectra sharpen generic entropy bounds and select a small set of recurring extremal Cartan families. None of the five descriptors generically determines $γ$ alone, but each imposes exact constraints, revealing how substantially the optimal gate-cutting cost can vary between gates with similar nonlocal characteristics.

Quantum Channel Stein Theorem beyond Definite Causal Order

Authors: Chengkai Zhu, Xin Wang
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.30268v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work establishes a quantum channel Stein theorem proving that the asymptotic rate for distinguishing between two quantum channels is independent of the testing strategy used (parallel, adaptive, or general). The key finding is that adaptivity offers no asymptotic advantage in this context. The paper rigorously connects hypothesis testing bounds to fundamental quantum divergences.

Why it may be interesting. It provides fundamental theoretical bounds on quantum channel discrimination, showing that complex causal structures (like general testers) do not offer an asymptotic advantage over simpler, parallel testing schemes.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates whether different quantum channel testing strategies (parallel, adaptive, general/indefinite causal order) yield different asymptotic advantages when distinguishing between two quantum channels.

Main result. It proves that for any fixed error tolerance, all testing strategies achieve the same asymptotic rate, which is the regularized channel relative entropy, demonstrating that adaptivity provides no asymptotic advantage.

Method. The analysis uses hypothesis testing theory, relating the achievable rate to the regularized channel relative entropy via continuity proofs of the sandwiched Rényi divergence.

Model / system. The system involves finite-dimensional memoryless quantum channels, modeled as Completely Positive Trace-Preserving (CPTP) maps, $N$ and $M$. The testing is performed over $n$ uses of these channels.

Key observables. The asymptotic testing rate, $D_{\infty}(N \parallel M)$, and the error probabilities associated with the three tester types (parallel, adaptive, general).

Important parameters / regimes. The fixed type-I error tolerance $\epsilon \in (0, 1)$ and the number of uses $n o \infty$.

Assumptions / limitations. The proof relies on establishing the continuity of the regularized sandwiched Rényi channel divergence at order one, and using the fixed-marginal de Finetti theorem for the general tester bound.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the asymptotic equality of testing rates across different strategies (Stein Theorem), to deriving exact one-shot duality relations, and finally establishing the exact strong-converse exponent based on these results.

Abstract

Distinguishability is a central concept in information theory and extends naturally to quantum systems. This work shows that, for any two finite-dimensional memoryless quantum channels, parallel, adaptive and general testing strategies achieve the same Stein exponent at every fixed type-I error tolerance $ε\in(0,1)$, namely the regularized channel relative entropy. When this rate is finite, any larger type-II exponent forces the probability of correctly accepting the null hypothesis to decay exponentially. Thus, adaptivity provides no asymptotic advantage. The key step is proving the continuity of the regularized sandwiched Rényi channel divergence at order one. We also derive the exact strong-converse exponent for general testers.

Representation-Dependent Recoverability in Quantum Compilation

Authors: Jinze Yang, Yangyang Li, Xiu-Hao Deng
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.29210v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper rigorously analyzes how quantum information, particularly aggregate phases, degrades or becomes 'less recoverable' as it passes through multiple stages of quantum compilation. By formalizing this loss using information-theoretic channels, the authors prove that the compiler's strategy—whether to commit results early or buffer state—imposes a quantifiable resource toll. The key takeaway is the necessity of preserving high-level phase semantics until the final aggregation step to minimize computational overhead.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides fundamental, quantitative guidelines for quantum algorithm implementation, directly impacting the resource estimation and feasibility assessment for complex quantum computations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper quantifies the information-theoretic cost associated with compiling high-level quantum structures into low-level gate sequences, specifically focusing on how the choice of compilation representation affects the recoverability of aggregate quantum phases.

Main result. The authors establish a formal trade-off showing that early commitment of output information incurs a measurable 'toll' compared to methods that buffer or defer state information, suggesting that preserving phase semantics until the final aggregation step is crucial for efficiency.

Method. The work develops a budget-explicit compiler model that charges information-theoretic costs (in bits) for different compilation passes, analyzing the trade-off between committed output ($\overline{A}_p$) and serialized state capacity ($S$).

Model / system. The model is applied to quantum compilation, analyzing the process of lowering a high-level structure, such as a commuting layer of phase gates, into a sequence of elementary gates (e.g., Clifford+$T$ words).

Key observables. Committed output information ($\overline{A}_p$), serialized crossing-state capacity ($S$), and the specific information toll ($\log_2 inom{m}{k}$) paid by early commitment.

Important parameters / regimes. Number of generators ($m$), approximation tolerance ($\epsilon$), and the number of compilation rounds ($r$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on formalizing the problem via information-theoretic channels and assumes that the physical cost can be mapped to these information-theoretic bounds.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the dispersal process of quantum information across compilation rounds and detail the mathematical relationship between the two information currencies ($ ext{S}$ and $ ext{Ap}$) that must be accounted for.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the problem of representation-dependent recoverability, formalizes the two information channels ($ ext{Ap}$ and $ ext{S}$), derives the core information bound, and then demonstrates this bound using measured tolls from instrumented compilers, concluding with a design rule for optimal compilation pipelines.

Abstract

Fault-tolerant compilation can disperse high-level structure: schedules split an accumulated phase across rounds, gate synthesis replaces an angle by a Clifford+$T$ word, and randomized compiling spreads rotations over sign-randomized fragments. They preserve the computation but change how cheaply a downstream compiler can recover the aggregate phase data. We formalize this representation-dependent recoverability by charging two channels: output committed before the suffix arrives, and a serialized restart state crossing the cut. For an $r$-round accumulation of a commuting layer with $m$ generators at accuracy $ε$ ($K_ε=Θ(1/ε)$), any $p$-pass compiler correct on every valid stream with failure probability at most $δ$ obeys $\overline{A}_p+(2p-1)S\geq(1-δ)m\log_2 K_ε-h_2(δ)$, where $\overline{A}_p$ is committed output and $S$ is the crossing-state cap. The bound covers compile-time randomized compiling; a memory-capped block compiler attains it within a constant factor, and direct semantic aggregation reaches compact output with logarithmic state. The two channels are not always interchangeable. A compiler whose committed prefix is executed at the cut pays a toll the state channel never pays: the entropy $\log_2\binom{m}{k}$ of the mask naming which $k$ coordinates it commits early. Compilers that defer or buffer pay none of it. We prove both directions and, with instrumented compilers under a preregistered protocol, measure a per-generator toll matching $\log_2\binom{m}{k}/k$ that vanishes for a public mask and a buffered control. Under a disclosed fixed-total-error Clifford+$T$ and surface-code model, a materialize-first pipeline costs orders of magnitude more logical $T$ states and spacetime volume than a semantic-first one. Phase semantics should therefore be preserved until aggregation whenever the interface permits.

RLVR landscapes for iterated multiplications can be benign: Insights from spin-glass theory

Authors: Noa Rubin, Zohar Ringel
Type: both · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.28625v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work analyzes the optimization landscape of reinforcement learning for algorithmic tasks by mapping it to a spin-glass model. The key finding is that the landscape itself is generally benign, suggesting that training failures are due to traversal issues like diffusive barriers rather than rugged local minima. This has implications for understanding the fundamental limitations of deep learning optimization.

Why it may be interesting. The connection between learning dynamics (RLVR) and the physics of disordered systems (spin glasses) provides a powerful, non-standard lens for understanding optimization in complex machine learning models.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the optimization landscape of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) when applied to algorithmic tasks like iterated group multiplication.

Main result. The optimization landscape is shown to be benign, meaning local minima are not the primary obstacle; practical difficulties arise instead from diffusive barriers and gradient estimation errors.

Method. The authors map the RLVR problem onto an energy-based spin-glass model, allowing for theoretical characterization and comparison with Monte Carlo sampling dynamics.

Model / system. The system involves mapping RLVR over myopic tabular policies to a spin-glass model defined over deterministic policies. The task involves sequential token generation for algorithmic problems.

Key observables. The structure of the optimization landscape, the existence of local minima, the RLVR-spin-glass gap, and the success rate of learning complex algorithms.

Important parameters / regimes. Entropy regulator strength ($eta$), temperature ($ au$), and the degree of input correlation ($k$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis initially relies on the approximation of using myopic tabular policies, and the proof of benignity breaks down with weight tying or input correlations.

Figures summary. Figures compare MC stationary distributions against CAVI endpoints, illustrating the effect of the entropy regulator ($ au$) and showing the relationship between reward, entropy, and the spin model.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the theoretical mapping from RLVR to spin-glass models, derives variational bounds, compares the convergence dynamics of MC sampling versus RLVR updates, and finally analyzes the impact of input correlations and policy tying on the landscape's traps.

Abstract

Despite the importance of reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), the extent to which it can learn new reasoning capabilities remains debated. Here we study the optimization landscape of RLVR on algorithmic tasks, such as iterated group and quasigroup multiplication. To this end, we map entropy-regularized RLVR over myopic tabular policies onto an energy-based (spin-glass) model over deterministic policies. This mapping upper-bounds what RLVR can achieve, and lets us rigorously characterize the landscape in this tabular setting. We show, both theoretically and experimentally, that for a wide class of models and tasks with uncorrelated inputs, this landscape is benign, containing no local minima that could trap RLVR training. Rather, the practical difficulty of these tasks appears to stem, at least in part, from issues such as diffusive barriers and gradient-estimation error in traversing the landscape. These are genuine obstacles that can prevent a solution from being found, but they are distinct from the landscape itself being rugged. We show that these obstacles can often be mitigated through the choice of entropy regulator. Consistent with this theory, we find that a transformer trained from scratch, using only last-token rewards, successfully learns an algorithmic chain of thought for iterated non-Abelian group multiplications.

The Moreau-Yosida approximation of the Entanglement of Formation: basic properties and accuracy estimates

Authors: M. E. Shirokov
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.30246v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops the Moreau-Yosida approximation, $E_\lambda^F$, as a mathematically rigorous and computable substitute for the Entanglement of Formation (EoF). By leveraging convex analysis, the authors establish that $E_\lambda^F$ converges monotonically to the true EoF as the regularization parameter $\lambda o 0$. This provides powerful tools for bounding and estimating entanglement in complex quantum systems.

Why it may be interesting. Understanding the rigorous mathematical definition and computational tractability of entanglement measures like EoF is fundamental to quantifying quantum resources in quantum information theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the mathematical challenge of defining and approximating the Entanglement of Formation (EoF), a key measure of entanglement in quantum information theory.

Main result. It introduces the Moreau-Yosida approximation, $E_\lambda^F$, which is shown to monotonically converge to the true EoF as the regularization parameter $\lambda$ approaches zero.

Method. The core method involves applying Moreau-Yosida regularization, a technique from convex analysis, to the definition of EoF to create a sequence of computable, convex approximations.

Model / system. The study concerns bipartite quantum systems described by density matrices $ ho$ acting on the tensor product of two Hilbert spaces ($\mathcal{H}_A \otimes \mathcal{H}_B$).

Key observables. Entanglement of Formation ($ ext{EoF}$), the approximation $E_\lambda^F( ho)$, and the difference $| ext{EoF}( ho) - E_\lambda^F( ho)|$.

Important parameters / regimes. The regularization parameter $\lambda > 0$, and bounds related to the rank or energy of the marginal states.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the global lower semicontinuity of EoF and assumes that the approximation $E_\lambda^F$ is defined via the Moreau envelope structure.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the relative error bound $ ext{bred}(\lambda)$ and $ ext{bre}_{N,E}(\lambda)$ as functions of $\lambda$ for different dimensions and energy levels, showing the convergence rate.

Paper structure. The paper first defines the problem using convex analysis, then derives the variational expressions for $E_\lambda^F$, proves its key properties (convexity, monotonicity, subadditivity), and finally analyzes the convergence rate and conditions for exact equality between $E_\lambda^F$ and $E_F$.

Abstract

We describe a family of convex uniformly continuous functions $E^λ_F$, $λ>0$, on the set of states of a bipartite quantum system (consisting of finite-dimensional or infinite-dimensional subsystems), which monotonically increase and converge pointwise to the Entanglement of Formation (EoF) as $λ\to0$. These functions are "nonselective" entanglement monotones defined by the way close to the construction of the Moreau-Yosida regularization (the Moreau envelope) of a convex function on a convex set used in the modern convex analysis. So, we call the functions $E^λ_F$ the Moreau-Yosida approximations of the EoF and describe their equivalent definitions and basic properties. The semicontinuity bounds for the EoF (presented in [Lob.J.Math., 46(6), 2632-2658]) allow us to obtain easily computable upper bounds on the difference $E_F(ρ)-E^λ_F(ρ)$ for a given state $ρ$. These bounds give easily computable bounds on the rate of uniform convergence of the function $E^λ_F$ to the EoF as $λ\to0^+$ on the sets of states with bounded rank/energy of one of the marginal states. We also discuss sufficient conditions for the coincidence of $E_F(ρ)$ and $E^λ_F(ρ)$ at a given state $ρ$ for all $λ$ small enough and consider several classes of states for which such coincidence takes place. The conjectured selective LOCC-monotonicity of the functions $E^λ_F$ and a possible way to prove it are briefly discussed. Secondary parts of the article are written with the help of ChatGPT-5.6.

The regularized channel Rényi divergence is continuous

Summary. This paper proves the continuity of the regularized channel Rényi divergence as the parameter $\alpha$ approaches 1. This mathematical result is highly significant as it establishes the computability of the channel relative entropy and yields several major theorems in quantum information theory, such as strong converse bounds for channel discrimination.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides fundamental mathematical tools (continuity proofs, computable quantities) for quantifying distinguishability between quantum processes, which is central to quantum communication and computation theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The central problem is proving the continuity of the regularized channel Rényi divergence at the limit $\alpha=1$.

Main result. The continuity is established, leading to the computability of the channel relative entropy and proving several key theorems, including an exponentially strong converse for channel discrimination.

Method. The proof utilizes a variational formula that reduces the divergence to a single-letter optimization problem, followed by a novel spectral confinement technique to establish the limit.

Model / system. The analysis focuses on quantum channels, represented by Completely Positive Trace-Preserving (CPTP) maps, comparing divergences between two such channels, $E$ and $F$.

Key observables. Regularized channel Rényi divergence ($D_\alpha^\infty(E\|F)$), channel relative entropy ($D_\infty(E\|F)$), and variational expressions ($C_s(E, F)$).

Important parameters / regimes. The divergence parameter $\alpha$, which is analyzed as $\alpha o 1$, and the reparameterization variable $s \in [0, 1)$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the Choi matrices $J_E$ and $J_F$ are not pathologically related (e.g., $J_E \ll J_F$) for the quantities to remain bounded.

Paper structure. The paper develops by first relating the regularized divergence to variational expressions parameterized by $s$, then proving the continuity of these expressions as $s o 0$ (or $\alpha o 1$), and finally deriving the consequences for channel discrimination and AEP.

Abstract

We show that the regularized channel Rényi divergence is continuous at $α= 1$. As consequences, we establish an exponentially strong converse for channel discrimination, a subchannel-smoothed asymptotic equipartition property, a relative entropy accumulation theorem, and a new proof of the generalized quantum Stein's lemma. Our result further shows that the regularized channel relative entropy is computable. The continuity proof relies on a variational formula that expresses the regularized channel divergence as a single-letter optimization over variables of unrestricted finite dimension. We then establish continuity using a novel spectral confinement technique.

Two temperature scales in the Ising model on the $\{5,4\}$ hyperbolic lattice with free boundaries: susceptibility peak and boundary-induced order

Authors: Sebastian Jaroszewicz, Nahuel Mendez, Maria P. Beccar-Varela, Maria C. Mariani
Type: both · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.29323v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work investigates the Ising model on a hyperbolic lattice where boundary effects are dominant. The authors find that conventional methods reveal two distinct temperature scales, $T_{c2}$ and $T^*$, instead of a single critical point. They demonstrate that the true upper thermodynamic transition can only be accessed by applying a specific boundary perturbation.

Why it may be interesting. The study provides a detailed case study of how boundary conditions fundamentally alter the identification of thermodynamic critical points in lattice models, which has implications for understanding open quantum systems or finite-size quantum simulations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To study the critical behavior and phase transitions of the Ising model on a hyperbolic lattice where boundary effects are macroscopic and dominate the system's average properties.

Main result. The system exhibits two distinct temperature scales ($T_{c2}$ and $T^*$) rather than a single critical point, and the upper thermodynamic transition ($T_{pt}$) can be recovered by perturbing the boundary.

Method. Monte Carlo simulations are used extensively, employing techniques like analyzing susceptibility peaks, Binder cumulant crossings, and applying coherent fields to probe different transition regimes.

Model / system. The ferromagnetic Ising model is studied on the $\{5, 4\}$ hyperbolic lattice with free boundaries. The geometry ensures that the boundary sites constitute a fixed, macroscopic fraction of the total system volume.

Key observables. Susceptibility ($\chi$), Binder cumulant crossings, order-parameter distribution, and the magnetization measured in different spatial regions (Boundary, Bulk, Core, Total).

Important parameters / regimes. The lattice geometry ($\{5, 4\}$), the coupling constant $J/k_B$, and the identification of two distinct temperature scales: $T_{c2} \approx 1.48$ and $T^* \approx 1.67$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis must account for the boundary-dependent nature of observables, necessitating finite-size scaling based on the generation index rather than total site count.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the hyperbolic lattice structure and show susceptibility and magnetization curves for different lattice sizes, highlighting the location of the susceptibility maximum and the crossing points for Binder cumulants.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the system by first establishing the boundary-dependent nature of the averages, then identifying the lower transition scale ($T_{c2}$) via susceptibility, followed by locating a pseudocritical scale ($T^*$) using Binder cumulants, and finally recovering the upper transition ($T_{pt}$) via boundary perturbation.

Abstract

On the $\{5,4\}$ hyperbolic lattice, the outermost generation holds 73\% of the sites at all system sizes, making macroscopic averages strictly boundary-dependent. We study the ferromagnetic Ising model on this geometry with free boundaries by Monte Carlo simulation, demonstrating that conventional observables cease to identify a single critical scale. Instead, the finite lattices are organized by two distinct temperature scales. The susceptibility maximum identifies the lower transition scale at $T_{c2}=1.4782(15)J/k_{B}$ without extrapolation. However, the order-parameter distribution lacks a conventional fixed point at this scale. A distinct pseudocritical scale emerges instead at $T^{*}\simeq1.67(3)$ inside the boundary-sensitive intermediate phase, where Binder cumulant curves cross and effective exponents are compatible with mean-field criticality. Between $T_{c2}$ and $T^{*}$, the interior amplifies boundary fluctuations into induced order. Furthermore, because the volume grows exponentially with depth, finite-size scaling must be formulated in the generation index rather than the total number of sites. The lack of asymptotic power-law convergence in the scaling stretch confirms through an independent observable that $T^{*}$ is a finite-size crossover rather than a thermodynamic fixed point. Finally, by applying a coherent field to the outermost generation alone, we recover the upper thermodynamic transition at $T_{pt}=2.81(1)J/k_{B}$, demonstrating that it remains accessible under an appropriate boundary perturbation.

Walsh-Transform Realization of Dense-to-Sparse Quantum State Preparation

Authors: Emad Rezaei Fard Boosari
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.28760v1

Summary. This paper proposes a novel, efficient method for preparing quantum states representing structured classical data by employing the Walsh-Hadamard Transform (WHT) within a Dense-to-Sparse Quantum State Preparation framework. By performing the transformation classically and using the WHT's CNOT-free inverse, the authors eliminate significant gate overhead associated with direct state preparation. This demonstrates that quantum resources scale primarily with the inherent sparsity of the signal in the Walsh domain, making it highly efficient for structured inputs.

Why it may be interesting. The focus on minimizing gate complexity and circuit depth by leveraging specific transforms like WHT is highly relevant to building practical, resource-efficient quantum algorithms for data processing.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of efficiently preparing high-dimensional quantum states corresponding to structured classical data, circumventing the exponential gate overhead of direct state preparation.

Main result. By utilizing the Walsh-Hadamard Transform (WHT) within a Dense-to-Sparse Quantum State Preparation (DS-QSP) framework, the authors achieve an efficient, deterministic preparation method with no additional CNOT or circuit-depth overhead in the decompression stage.

Method. The method involves a three-stage process: transforming the classical data via WHT (classical compression), preparing the resulting sparse coefficients using a sparse QSP algorithm, and finally reconstructing the state using the CNOT-free inverse WHT.

Model / system. The model is based on quantum circuits designed for quantum state preparation (QSP) of amplitude-encoded states. The system utilizes the WHT as the reversible classical transformation, which is implemented quantum mechanically via Hadamard gates.

Key observables. Approximation Error (epsilon), total CNOT gate cost, and the trace distance between the prepared and target quantum states.

Important parameters / regimes. The required sparsity level (d) relative to the full dimension (N), and the approximation tolerance (e.g., epsilon = 10^-2).

Assumptions / limitations. The method provides an approximation due to information loss during coefficient thresholding, and its efficiency relies on the input signal having a sparse representation in the Walsh domain.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the general DS-QSP workflow, the quantum circuit structure, and benchmark results showing that structured signals require far fewer coefficients (d) for accurate reconstruction compared to white noise.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the general DS-QSP framework, then proposes and details the specific realization using the WHT. It analyzes the circuit costs, presents numerical results comparing various signal types (sparse vs. white noise), and concludes by discussing the modular nature of the approach.

Abstract

We propose a hybrid quantum state preparation method based on the Walsh--Hadamard transform within the dense-to-sparse quantum state preparation framework. The method approximately prepares structured classical data in a quantum circuit through an indirect approach. Instead of directly preparing the dense amplitude-encoded state, the method first transforms the classical data into the Walsh domain and prepares only the largest-magnitude coefficients using a sparse state-preparation algorithm. The original state can then be approximately recovered through a parallel layer of Hadamard gates. Since this reconstruction requires no CNOT gates and has circuit depth 1, the proposed method introduces no additional CNOT or circuit-depth overhead, and its quantum preparation cost is determined by the underlying sparse state-preparation algorithm. Numerical results on representative benchmark signals demonstrate that the proposed method enables accurate state preparation for signals admitting sparse or approximately sparse representations in the Walsh domain.

Summary. This work establishes that momentum-space nonsymmorphic symmetry is a general weak-coupling route to realizing Pair-Density-Wave (PDW) superconductivity in systems like the TBCB lattice. Interestingly, LR superconductivity (hopping) promotes massive Dirac (Majorana zero) modes, while in a finite-size system, their emergence in open boundary conditions is caused by the bulk gap of the anti-periodic Hamiltonian in momentum space. Importantly, the thermodynamic phase boundaries are correctly reproduced by the above unconventional correspondence. Abstract

Twisted transition-metal dichalcogenides (TMDs) have manifested a rich variety of emerging physical phenomena, yet experimental studies have so far been largely limited in their valence bands (p-doped). Abstract

Nanoparticle properties are governed by their surfaces, yet their atomic-scale surface structures remain challenging to predict. Here, we report that bimetallic nanoparticles can form a thermodynamically controlled "shell-dimer" architecture in which one metal forms an overlayer only a few atomic layers thick on another. Using Au-Rh as a model system, atomic-resolution imaging and molecular dynamics show that an ultrathin Au overlayer forms on Rh, stabilized by competition among surface, interfacial, and strain energies. Anisotropic strain limits its growth to the subnanometer scale, and changes in surface chemistry can destabilize the overlayer altogether. Across a range of bimetallic nanoparticles, we found that overlayer formation is associated with elemental immiscibility and lattice mismatch. These findings provide a basis for understanding and controlling surface structures in multimetallic nanoparticles.
