TAP states at upper deviations of the free energy: existence, localization and marginal stability in Ising spin glasses

Authors: Yan Ru Pei
Type: theory · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.06490v1

Summary. This work rigorously analyzes the large deviation behavior of the free energy in mixed p-spin Ising spin glasses. It proves that these deviations are controlled by generalized Thouless-Anderson-Palmer (TAP) critical points. The methodology provides deep insights into the mathematical structure of disordered systems, extending classical results with advanced large deviation theory.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical statistical mechanics, the mathematical tools used—such as analyzing complex energy landscapes and critical points—are highly relevant to understanding the complexity of quantum many-body systems and optimization problems in quantum information.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the upper large deviations of the free energy in mixed p-spin Ising spin glasses.

Main result. It establishes that the large deviations are governed by generalized TAP critical points, providing precise rate functions for the free energy.

Method. The analysis combines advanced techniques from large deviation theory, relating the free energy to the Parisi functional structure and utilizing comparison methods.

Model / system. The study focuses on mixed p-spin Ising spin glasses defined on the hypercube {-1, 1}^N, characterized by their Hamiltonian and associated covariance functions $\xi(t)$.

Key observables. Free energy ($N^{-1} \log Z_N$), generalized TAP free energy ($F_{\text{TAP}}$), and the rate function $\Sigma(f)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The deviation level $f$, the radius of convergence of the covariance $\xi(t)$, and the perturbation parameter $\gamma$.

Assumptions / limitations. Key assumptions include the radius of convergence of the covariance exceeding one, and the validity of the fractional moment formula (FM).

Paper structure. The paper builds by first relating $\max F_{\text{TAP}}$ to $\log Z_N$ (Theorem 1), then characterizing the structure of the maximizers via generalized TAP critical points (Theorem 2), and finally detailing the local structure using the field law and Hessian bulk (Theorem 3).

Abstract

We study upper large deviations of the free energy of mixed $p$-spin Ising spin glasses through the generalized TAP free energy $F_{\mathrm{TAP}}$ of Chen, Panchenko and Subag. For every mixture with radius of convergence greater than one, $\max F_{\mathrm{TAP}}$ and $\log Z_N$ have the same large deviations at speed $N$; the proof combines their band theorem with Ramsey's theorem. For convex mixtures with $\sum_p 2^pβ_p^2<\infty$, upper deviations at level $f$ are carried by generalized TAP critical points, which exist at the free-energy rate without Boursier's strict Plefka condition. Outside an event of smaller exponential order, near-maximizers at level $f$ lie near the contact set of a constrained Parisi obstacle, follow the Auffinger-Chen field law, and have Hessian bulk near a reflected Pastur law whose edge is nonpositive and vanishes exactly at contacts where the obstacle is flat to second order. Where every contact is of this kind, a fixed stability margin costs rate.

The superb supersinglet

Authors: Gabriele Cobucci, Armin Tavakoli, Alexander Bernal, Shishir Khandelwal
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.06183v1

Summary. This work characterizes supersinglets, a class of highly symmetric, high-dimensional multipartite entangled states. Using advanced optimization techniques, the authors prove these states are remarkably robust against noise compared to standard entangled states. They establish supersinglets as a powerful, versatile resource for quantum sensing, achieving optimal Heisenberg scaling.

Why it may be interesting. The combination of high symmetry, robust entanglement, and provable metrological advantage makes supersinglets a theoretically rich resource for quantum sensing protocols relevant to AMO physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper explores the entanglement properties and quantum information applications of fully antisymmetric states, known as supersinglets, in multipartite systems.

Main result. Supersinglets are shown to be exceptionally robust entangled states, exhibiting remarkable resilience to noise and enabling Heisenberg scaling in quantum sensing applications.

Method. The authors use convex optimization, semidefinite programming (SDP) relaxations, and the construction of entanglement witnesses to quantify robustness and certify entanglement depth.

Model / system. The focus is on multipartite systems of high-dimensional particles, specifically the fully antisymmetric state $| ext{S}_n angle$, which possesses invariance under arbitrary collective local unitaries.

Key observables. Entanglement robustness (critical visibility $v$), genuine dimensionality and depth of entanglement ($ ext{DGME}$), and Quantum Fisher Information ($F_Q$).

Important parameters / regimes. The number of particles ($n$), the local dimension ($d$), and the noise parameter ($v$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on SDP relaxations providing upper bounds on robustness, and the identification of supersinglets as a natural resource for sensing.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates a quantum circuit for recursively constructing an $n$-dimensional supersinglet; Table I compares the critical visibility bounds for supersinglets versus GHZ and cluster states.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first defining supersinglets and analyzing their noise robustness using convex optimization, then developing witness methods to certify entanglement depth, and finally applying these findings to prove their utility in high-dimensional gradient sensing.

Abstract

Multipartite systems of high-dimensional particles can host forms of quantum entanglement inaccessible to qubit systems. A particularly fascinating class is formed by the fully antisymmetric states, also known as supersinglets, which possess a remarkable symmetry: they are invariant under arbitrary collective local unitaries. Here, we explore their entanglement properties and establish their quantum information applications. Using convex optimisation, we show that central entanglement features of supersinglets are remarkably robust to noise, substantially surpassing the robustness of several paradigmatic entangled states under certain relaxations. We then develop witness methods, based on collective-spin or randomised measurements, that certify both the depth and dimensionality of their entanglement. Finally, we identify supersinglets as a natural resource for high-dimensional gradient sensing and prove that they enable Heisenberg scaling. Together, these results reveal supersinglets as an exceptionally robust and versatile class of multipartite entangled states, combining striking symmetry and strong entanglement with concrete potential for quantum sensing.

Summary. This theoretical work develops two superconducting pairing models on the Kitaev honeycomb lattice by deriving the pairing kernels microscopically rather than assuming them. It establishes deep connections between lattice geometry, symmetry selection rules, and the resulting superconducting order parameter. The findings are crucial for understanding topological superconductivity in strongly correlated quantum magnets.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous derivation of pairing symmetries from underlying lattice constraints (like bond-star geometry) provides a powerful, symmetry-enforced framework for understanding unconventional superconductivity in correlated materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper constructs and analyzes two superconducting pairing models derived microscopically on the doped Kitaev honeycomb lattice, focusing on how pairing symmetry is dictated by lattice geometry.

Main result. The models predict specific superconducting states (e.g., d+id singlet) whose topological properties, like the Chern number, are robustly linked to the lattice geometry and bond structure.

Method. The analysis uses mean-field Bogoliubov-de Gennes theory, deriving pairing kernels from symmetry selection rules (flux-sector or $C_3$ little-group) and employing Majorana fermion techniques.

Model / system. The system is superconducting pairing on the doped Kitaev honeycomb lattice, utilizing a bond-local glue mechanism. The theory involves mapping the spin-liquid ground state onto an effective electron-electron interaction.

Key observables. Superconducting gap ($\Delta$), Chern number ($C$), Thermal Hall Conductance ($\kappa_{xy}^T$), and the gap magnitude derived from bond sines.

Important parameters / regimes. Doping level, bond coupling strengths ($J_K$), and the geometry of the bond-star structure.

Assumptions / limitations. The pairing glue is assumed to be bond-local and neutral between even- and odd-parity channels; the general theorem holds on odd-fold bond stars.

Figures summary. Not specified, but the notes mention tables detailing calculated values of the flux gap ($2\Delta_F$) and spectral weights.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the background (Kitaev model solution via Majorana fermions) to deriving two specific superconducting models (Model 1 for odd-parity, Model 2 for even-parity) and concluding with a general theorem regarding parity alternation on odd-fold bond stars.

Abstract

We construct two superconducting pairing models on doped Kitaev honeycomb, whose microscopic kernels are derived rather than assumed. Both are driven by a bond-local glue neutral between the even- and odd-parity pairing channels; the chirality--parity linkage is set by bond-star geometry, not by the microscopic glue. (i) Model~1(odd-parity sector): the kernel follows from the flux-sector selection rule of the pure model, fixing its component-diagonal, nearest-neighbor separable, rank-one-per-bond form, with $w_α=J_K^2χ_α\propto r_αm_α$, where $m_α$ follows from the Hellmann--Feynman theorem and $r_α\simeq0.24$ is computed numerically. The spin-vertex state is a unitary triplet with a bond-locked anisotropic $\mathbf d$ vector; its gap is a Gram sum of bond sines, so nodal directions are fixed by geometry. (ii) Model~2(even): the kernel is fixed by the $C_3$ little-group selection rule of a zone-corner chiral phonon, up to a Cooper-channel extension whose momentum constraint we state explicitly. The charge-vertex pairing is a $d+id$ singlet: the parity decomposition $f_\pm=g_\pm+p_\pm$ projects onto $g_\pm$, so chirality is slaved to the phonon polarization, with $C=\pm4$ and $κ_{xy}/T=2κ_0$. Reversing the pump helicity reverses the Chern number, Kerr rotation, and thermal Hall sign. (iii) General theorem: on odd-fold bond stars, the angular-momentum ladder of a single-star chiral form factor alternates in parity. Every chiral state selected by such a bond-local form factor is a geometrically enforced singlet--triplet composite with universal amplitude ratios ($n=3$: $d\oplus p$; $n=5$: $g\oplus p$ or $d\oplus f$); coherent doubled-star combinations can restore parity purity. The Chern number of the mixed state follows the dominant on-shell component across a topological boundary. The spin glue drives $C=\pm2$; the charge kernel $C=\pm4$.

A solvable quantum many-body problem

Summary. This theoretical paper finds exact solutions to the Schrödinger equation for $N$-body quantum systems when the potential energy is proportional to the inverse of the interparticle distance. By proposing the wavefunction $\Psi = e^{-KR}$, the authors derive general energy and potential relations for these solvable models. This work provides significant analytical insight into the behavior of interacting quantum particles under specific, highly symmetric potential conditions.

Why it may be interesting. The exact solvability demonstrated for a specific class of potentials ($V \propto R^{-1}$) provides a powerful, analytically tractable benchmark for understanding complex many-body interactions, which is valuable for developing theoretical intuition in AMO physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper seeks to find exact solutions to the Schrödinger equation for quantum many-body systems, specifically those governed by potentials proportional to the inverse of the interparticle distance ($R^{-1}$).

Main result. It demonstrates that the wavefunction $\Psi = e^{-KR}$ is an exact eigenstate for such potentials in $D$ dimensions for $N \ge 2$, providing explicit energy and potential relations.

Method. The analysis involves solving the Schrödinger equation using relative coordinates and testing the proposed exponential form of the wavefunction $\Psi = e^{-KR}$ for various particle numbers ($N=2, 3, 4$) and dimensions.

Model / system. The model is a system of $N$ identical bosons described by a Hamiltonian involving kinetic energy and a pair potential $V(\{r_{ij}\})$. The analysis focuses on the structure of the wavefunction related to the sum of squared interparticle distances, $R^2$.

Key observables. Energy eigenvalues ($E_N$) and potential energy relations ($V_N$) are key observables, which are shown to scale predictably with the excitation parameter $K$.

Important parameters / regimes. The number of particles ($N$), the dimensionality ($D$), and the coupling constant embedded in the potential $V \propto R^{-1}$ are important parameters.

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that the potential $V$ must be proportional to $R^{-1}$ for the proposed exponential wavefunction to yield an exact solution.

Paper structure. The paper develops the argument by solving the problem for small particle numbers ($N=2, 3, 4$) and then generalizes the findings to arbitrary $N$ in three dimensions, including the explicit form for excited states.

Abstract

A many-body wavefunction exponential in R, where the square of R is the sum of the squares of the interparticle distances, is shown to be the solution of Schrodingers equation for a potential proportional to the reciprocal of R, for N particles. A set of translationally invariant rotational eigenstates of angular momentum is found.

Summary. This experimental study uses small-angle neutron scattering (SANS) to investigate the magnetic structure of TbMn6Sn6 across its spin-reorientation transition. The research rules out the formation of a bulk, long-range skyrmion lattice, instead characterizing a highly anisotropic mesoscale magnetic response. This work confirms that the magnetic correlations are dominated by stripe or domain-like textures whose characteristics strongly depend on the applied magnetic field and temperature.

Why it may be interesting. While the focus is on magnetism, the study of phase transitions (SRT) and the evolution of correlated states (mesoscale textures) under external fields provides insights into many-body dynamics and symmetry breaking, which are relevant to understanding complex quantum many-body systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to determine if local magnetic textures observed in thin specimens of TbMn6Sn6 develop into a bulk long-range-ordered skyrmion lattice across the spin-reorientation transition (SRT).

Main result. The authors found no sixfold SANS Bragg pattern indicative of a long-range skyrmion lattice; instead, the dominant response is an anisotropic twofold low-Q scattering component evolving markedly across the SRT.

Method. Small-angle neutron scattering (SANS) was used on a bulk single crystal, probing mesoscale magnetism by measuring the evolution of the low-Q scattering component under varying magnetic fields and temperatures.

Model / system. The physical system is TbMn6Sn6, a centrosymmetric ferrimagnetic kagome metal exhibiting strong magnetic anisotropy and a near-room-temperature spin-reorientation transition. The analysis compares bulk scattering data to real-space imaging results.

Key observables. Anisotropic twofold low-Q scattering component, SANS Bragg patterns (specifically the absence of sixfold peaks), and the field/temperature dependence of the scattering intensity and the inverse-q descriptor (d).

Important parameters / regimes. Spin-reorientation temperature (TSR $\sim 310$ K), applied magnetic fields ($H \parallel b$ and $H \parallel c$), and mesoscale length scales probed by SANS.

Assumptions / limitations. The observed scattering is attributed to stripe- or domain-related textures, and the analysis relies on interpreting the field-dependent contrast evolution rather than assuming a unique real-space periodicity.

Figures summary. Figures show the evolution of the low-Q signal across the SRT, comparing field dependence at different temperatures (e.g., $H \parallel b$ at $345$ K vs. $H \parallel c$ at $310$ K), and include robustness checks using high-field-reference subtraction.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the context by noting the discrepancy between real-space imaging and bulk measurements. It then presents SANS results showing the anisotropic, field-dependent low-Q scattering across the SRT, concluding that the response is mesoscale and anisotropic, consistent with domain structures but not a long-range skyrmion lattice.

Abstract

TbMn6Sn6 is a centrosymmetric ferrimagnetic kagome metal that combines topological electronic states with strong magnetic anisotropy and a near-room-temperature spin-reorientation transition (SRT). Real-space imaging studies of thin specimens have revealed helical stripes, biskyrmion-like textures, and skyrmion-bubble-like structures in the spin-reorientation regime. Whether such local textures develop into a bulk long-range-ordered skyrmion lattice remains unresolved. Here we present small-angle neutron scattering (SANS) results probing the mesoscale magnetism of a bulk TbMn6Sn6 single crystal. We observe no sixfold SANS Bragg pattern indicative of a long-range-ordered skyrmion lattice under the explored conditions. Instead, the dominant magnetic response is an anisotropic twofold low-Q scattering component whose intensity distribution and field dependence evolve markedly across the spin-reorientation regime. In zero field, a broad and horizontally elongated low-Q signal develops above TSR and weakens as the system approaches the paramagnetic state. At 345 K with H || b, the signal is diffuse and is suppressed rapidly below about 700 Oe. By contrast, at 310 K with H || c, close to TSR, the response is more concentrated and line-shaped and persists to higher fields. The contrasts between these scans likely reflect the combined effects of rapidly evolving magnetic anisotropy near TSR and the field geometry. Our results therefore establish a bulk anisotropic mesoscale magnetic response across the spin-reorientation regime, consistent with stripe- or domain-related textures reported by real-space imaging, while not uniquely identifying their real-space morphology.

Anti-symmetrization is cheap and useful

Authors: Adam Bene Watts, John Bostanci, Matthias C. Caro, Daniel Grier, Sihan Liu, Gaurav Mahajan

Summary. This work introduces efficient quantum algorithms for performing anti-symmetrization on mixed quantum states. By developing both streaming and copy-reusing procedures, the authors provide improved resource estimates for key quantum tasks. These results advance the understanding of how to prepare highly entangled, anti-symmetric quantum states for applications in quantum sampling and state purification.

Why it may be interesting. The techniques developed for state preparation and sampling are fundamental building blocks for quantum simulation and quantum computation protocols, especially those involving entanglement structure.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper focuses on developing efficient quantum algorithms to perform anti-symmetrization on unknown mixed quantum states.

Main result. They present algorithms achieving sample complexities of O(r/lambda_min) for anti-symmetrization, which are then used to improve weak/unitary Schur sampling and purity amplification protocols.

Method. The methods involve developing streaming and non-streaming quantum circuits, leveraging tools from representation theory (Schur-Weyl duality) to analyze the state preparation.

Model / system. The system involves mixed qudit states $\rho$ with rank $r$, and the goal is to project onto the anti-symmetric subspace $\Pi_{\text{anti}}^r$. The analysis uses concepts from the representation theory of the symmetric group.

Key observables. Sample complexity ($n$), gate complexity (controlled-SWAP operations, T-count), and the resulting state proportional to the anti-symmetric projector.

Important parameters / regimes. Rank of the state ($r$), minimum non-zero eigenvalue ($\lambda_{\min}$), local dimension ($d$), and desired accuracy ($\delta$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on advanced tools from representation theory (e.g., Pieri's rule) and assumes the validity of previous results regarding projector structures.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining the anti-symmetrization task and presenting initial streaming algorithms, to developing more complex, copy-reusing procedures, and finally using representation theory to rigorously analyze the resulting state structure for applications like Schur sampling.

Abstract

We consider the computational task of anti-symmetrization of an unknown mixed quantum state: Given iid copies of a rank-$r$ qudit state $ρ=\sum_{i=1}^r λ_i |\varphi_i\rangle\!\langle\varphi_i|$, prepare a copy of the state proportional to $Π_{\mathrm{anti}}^r (|\varphi_1\rangle\otimes \ldots\otimes|\varphi_r\rangle)$, with $Π_{\mathrm{anti}}^r$ the anti-symmetric subspace projector. We first give a simple algorithm for anti-symmetrization that uses $\widetilde{O}(r / λ_{\min})$ copies of $ρ$, where $λ_{\min}$ is the minimum non-zero eigenvalue of $ρ$. This algorithm works even in the streaming setting with small working memory, and it uses only simple quantum operations like controlled-SWAPs and constantly many layers of single-qubit gates. As an application of this streaming anti-symmetrization procedure, we give a reduction from gapped mixed-state one-way state generators to pure-state one-way state generators. Then, using tools from Schur-Weyl duality, we give and analyze a second (non-streaming) anti-symmetrization procedure that uses $O(r / λ_{\min})$ copies of $ρ$, which then serves as a building block in new algorithms for weak Schur sampling and unitary Schur sampling, outperforming existing approaches in some regimes of number of copies $n$ and local dimension $d$, as well as in an algorithm for optimal purity amplification for qudit states.

Certified Randomness Generation against Quantum Adversaries via Energy Constraints

Authors: Thomas A. Hahn, Efrat Gerchkovitz, Ester Yehuda, Rotem Arnon
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.05766v1

Summary. This paper develops a rigorous security analysis for Quantum Random Number Generators (QRNGs) that relies on physical energy constraints rather than abstract assumptions. By deriving a tight lower bound on the von Neumann entropy using Semidefinite Programming, the authors show a significant improvement in security guarantees compared to previous min-entropy-based methods. This establishes a key ingredient for future, practically implementable security proofs for QRNGs.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous, physically motivated framework for quantifying randomness security that moves beyond standard computational or non-signaling assumptions, which is highly relevant for quantum hardware implementation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of generating certified randomness against quantum adversaries using physical constraints, specifically energy constraints, in prepare-and-measure Quantum Random Number Generators (QRNGs).

Main result. The authors derive a new, essentially tight, lower bound on the single-round von Neumann entropy against adversaries with both classical and quantum side-information. This shows that using von Neumann entropy significantly improves security bounds compared to min-entropy in this energy-constrained model.

Method. The security analysis involves deriving lower bounds on the conditional von Neumann entropy using a hierarchy of Semidefinite Programs (SDPs) and advanced techniques like the Entropy Accumulation Theorem.

Model / system. The model is a simple prepare-and-measure QRNG where security is guaranteed by bounding the energy expectation value of the prepared states ($ ext{Tr}[\mathcal{O}\lambda ho\lambda^x] \le \omega_x$). The adversary has access to classical and quantum side-information.

Key observables. Single-round von Neumann entropy $H(A|E, \Lambda, X=0)$, and the energy expectation values ($\omega_x$).

Important parameters / regimes. The energy bounds ($\omega_x$), the correlation parameters ($E_x$), and the structure of the side-information registers ($\Lambda, E$).

Assumptions / limitations. The security proof relies on the energy constraint model, and the mathematical derivations involve approximations (like piecewise linear functions) and the assumption of measurement independence in certain steps.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the general adversarial model incorporating the energy constraint. Figure 2 compares the derived lower bounds on von Neumann entropy against those from min-entropy bounds as a function of the energy bound $\omega$.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the physical model and the security goal (von Neumann entropy bound). It then develops the mathematical machinery, utilizing SDPs and entropy theorems, to derive increasingly tight lower bounds on the entropy, culminating in the final SDP formulation for the bound.

Abstract

For quantum random number generators (QRNGs), strong security guarantees can be obtained under various assumptions. The most well-known assumptions are the non-signalling assumption (needed for device-independent (DI) QRNG) and post-quantum computational assumptions (needed for computationally bounded single-prover QRNG protocols). In this work, we study a third model: a simple prepare-and-measure QRNG whose security relies on physically motivated energy constraints. This physically motivated set-up allows for easier implementations of QRNGs and can supply better rates. So far, previous works that studied the energy-constrained model either assumed that the adversary is classical in their security proof or supplied relatively weak bounds on the certified randomness, by bounding the conditional min-entropy. In this work, we prove a new, essentially tight, lower bound on the single-round von Neumann entropy against adversaries holding both classical and quantum side-information. Our work reveals that working with the von Neumann entropy, rather than the min-entropy, in the quantum case can lead to a major improvement, nearly matching the classical bound in many cases. We further study the robustness of our entropy bound. Since the single-round von Neumann entropy is the main quantity of interest when characterizing the key rate of QRNGs, our bound establishes the main ingredient for rigorous security proofs against general quantum attacks in this framework.

Complexity separations for optimal matchgate-Clifford synthesis

Authors: Berta Casas, Diego García-Martín, Yuxuan Zhang

Summary. This paper analyzes the complexity of synthesizing target unitaries within the matchgate-Clifford group, which governs certain fermionic quantum protocols. It proves that while synthesis is efficiently solvable for simple graph structures like paths, the problem becomes NP-complete for general tree topologies. The work provides both theoretical complexity bounds and practical, high-performance compilers.

Why it may be interesting. The results provide concrete complexity boundaries for simulating fermionic quantum circuits, which is crucial for understanding the limits of quantum simulation algorithms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the optimal unitary synthesis problem: finding the minimum number of gates required to implement a target unitary within the matchgate-Clifford group.

Main result. The optimal synthesis problem is efficiently solvable in polynomial time ($O(n^2)$) for path and complete connectivity graphs, but it is proven to be NP-complete for tree connectivity graphs.

Method. The authors use algebraic methods involving potential functions ($\Phi(w)$) derived from the group structure of signed permutations, comparing the required gate count to established lower bounds.

Model / system. The system involves quantum circuits restricted to the matchgate-Clifford group, whose structure is isomorphic to the group of unit-determinant signed permutations of $2n$ Majorana modes.

Key observables. Optimal gate count ($\ell_G(w)$), potential function ($\Phi(w)$), and the underlying signed permutation matrix ($w$).

Important parameters / regimes. The connectivity graph structure (Path, Complete, Tree) dictates the computational complexity of the synthesis problem.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on restricting the synthesis problem to specific graph topologies (path, complete, tree) to determine tractability.

Figures summary. Figures compare the performance of the proposed optimal compiler against Qiskit and Tket, showing constant factor improvements for chain simulations up to $n=80$ qubits.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by establishing the problem formulation, deriving potential functions for specific graphs (path, complete), proving efficient solvability for these cases, and finally proving NP-completeness for the general tree case via reduction from token swapping.

Abstract

Clifford and matchgate circuits are canonical families of classically simulable quantum circuits. Their intersection, the matchgate-Clifford group, plays an important role in randomized fermionic protocols and in matchgate synthesis. Its adjoint action is isomorphic to the group of unit-determinant signed permutations of $2n$ Majorana modes, and we study optimal exact synthesis in this group. That is, given a target unitary and a gate set, output an $n$-qubit circuit implementing the target using the fewest operations. We show that the complexity of this problem strongly depends on the gate set. In particular, we study gate sets consisting of Majorana braids with different connectivity graphs. For path and complete graphs, we prove that the problem is classically solvable in $\mathcal{O}\left(n^2\right)$ time, and we provide explicit gate-optimal compilers. In addition, we prove that when the connectivity graph is a tree, the decision version of the optimal synthesis problem becomes NP-complete. Finally, we benchmark our optimal compiler on chains of up to $n=80$ qubits against those of \texttt{Qiskit} and \texttt{Tket}, obtaining circuits with constant factor improvements $\times2.57$ and $\times2.28$ in the total number of gates, respectively.

Abstract

We develop entanglement-of-formation potentials for feedback-assisted classical communication and one-way randomness distillation. For a finite-dimensional quantum channel without initial shared entanglement, an exact amortization theorem bounds the increase of message information plus entanglement remaining across the communicating laboratories. This proves a classical-feedback capacity bound using a mixed-convex-roof expression previously studied by Winter and Yang in the context of potential capacities. We determine the capacity of flagged mixtures of noiseless and entanglement-breaking channels, improve a published depolarizing-channel converse, and evaluate the new bound exactly for the amplitude damping channel. A state analogue gives a finite-blocklength upper bound on the net shared randomness obtainable with one-way classical communication. Amplitude-damping Choi states provide exact benchmarks, while a full-rank generalized amplitude-damping Choi state demonstrates a useful improvement where we do not evaluate the operational rate exactly. We also distinguish one-way from two-way randomness distillation: a two-round weak-measurement protocol strictly exceeds the exact one-way rate of every nontrivial qubit isotropic state. The protocol preserves quantum side information during its first decoding and extracts additional randomness in the reverse direction. This shows why the one-way state potential is not a general two-way converse. Together, these results establish a common entanglement-based approach to sharper communication and randomness bounds, while revealing how retained quantum correlations enable two-way distillation to surpass one-way limits.

Abstract

Recent work has shown that syndrome information obtained during error-corrected quantum computation can be utilized for logical error mitigation (LEM), yielding exponential reductions in runtime overhead compared to external'' LEM methods, which do not make use of syndrome data. However, these syndrome-aware LEM methods (SALEM) generally require adaptive circuit operations based on real-time processing of syndromes. Here, we introduce syndrome-based offline logical error mitigation (SOLEM), a framework that utilizes syndrome data entirely in post-processing, without any modification to the given error-corrected circuit. In this sense, SOLEM is built into'' error-corrected circuits, and is in fact simpler to implement than external LEM protocols. The basic idea of SOLEM is to use naturally occurring syndrome-conditioned logical error channels in place of deliberate circuit modifications. We develop unbiased and heuristic estimators within this framework, including offline logical versions of probabilistic error cancellation (PEC) and zero-noise extrapolation (ZNE), as well as a global-regression estimator. SOLEM's fully offline nature leads to three significant advantages. First, SOLEM can post-process syndromes with a more powerful decoder than the one used in real time. Second, multiple estimators can be constructed from the same measured data and optimized using empirical statistical errors and related performance metrics. Third, logical error characterization is needed only in post-processing, allowing characterization data to be collected during circuit execution and reducing sensitivity to hardware drift. Remarkably, SOLEM estimators can achieve these advantages while attaining a runtime overhead which is exponentially lower than that of external LEM and is comparable to that of corresponding SALEM estimators.

Abstract

In a previous work, we showed that real quantum theory (RQT) cannot be experimentally distinguished from ordinary quantum theory (QT). In this paper, we investigate whether this indistinguishability survives two natural extensions of the setting: allowing separated measurements to be represented by commuting rather than tensor-product observables, and imposing superselection rules, as in fermionic information theory. We show that it does in both cases. Our first result is a positive answer to the Tsirelson problem in RQT: commuting and tensor-product measurements generate the same finite-dimensional correlations. Our second result is a local, real quantum formulation of superselected theories. We extend the RQT framework to accommodate a representation of superselection over the reals and show, in particular, that it admits a fermionic version whose predictions are experimentally indistinguishable from those of fermionic information theory. More generally, the construction extends to all superselected QTs.

Abstract

Abstract

We give a shadow tomography algorithm that uses $O(\log(m) \log(m/δ) / \varepsilon^2)$ copies of the input state. Specifically, given a quantum state $ρ$ and observables $O_1,\ldots,O_m$, the algorithm outputs estimates of $\mathrm{tr}(O_iρ)$ for all $i\in[m]$ that are all accurate to within $\pm\varepsilon$, except with probability at most $δ$. The key idea is to work in the average-case setting: for a random state drawn from a known prior, a pretty good measurement on $O(\log(m)/\varepsilon^2)$ copies gives a constant-error guarantee on all but a small fraction of the prior. Boosting this guarantee gives an average-case shadow tomography algorithm, and the minimax principle then (non-constructively) implies the existence of a measurement with the same worst-case guarantee. We also identify an obstruction to further improvement with this approach. While pretty good measurements amplify success over the randomness of the prior, they do not amplify success over the randomness of measurement. This issue appears to be inherent, and gives rise to the remaining $\log(m)$ factor separating the complexity of the quantum problem from its classical counterpart.

Abstract

I present a short approach to discretizing continuous unitary evolution. It gives a correspondence in both directions between continuous unitary evolutions and products of unitaries in finite dimensions, and I use it to reduce discrete adiabatic theorems to continuous ones. At fine steps, holding truncated samples of a regulated Hamiltonian on a suitable finite partition gives an approximating product. Conversely, rounding the clock identifies a product of $T$ unit-step samples with a continuous evolution, up to two half-step phases. I bound its operator-norm distance from the original evolution by $O(1/T)$ through cancellation within each time step. This assumes bounded first and second derivatives, bounded spectral width, and eigenvalue differences bounded away from nonzero multiples of $2π$, uniformly in $T$. For a unitary walk with these derivative bounds, a fixed common free spectral arc supplies a Hermitian logarithm path satisfying the comparison hypotheses. Continuous simulation guarantees then transfer to the phase-corrected product, and continuous adiabatic bounds to the product itself, with an explicit additive $O(1/T)$ error and each adiabatic theorem's hypotheses retained. For positive definite quantum linear systems of condition number $κ$, this comparison and continuous adiabatic theory give a rescaled qubitized walk with $O(κ/δ)$ steps for leakage $δ$, matching the scaling previously obtained through a discrete adiabatic theorem. These products need no discrete adiabatic theorem.

Summary. This work rigorously analyzes the quantum leakage in Shamir secret sharing schemes when devices utilize correlated quantum resources. Main result. It proves that the leakage state exhibits an exponentially small trace distance between any two secrets, provided the reconstruction requires a sufficiently large fraction of the shares.

Abstract

We study leakage from Shamir secret sharing when each device uses its share to produce one qubit from an initial quantum resource independent of the secret and sharing randomness. We prove exponentially small trace distance between the leakage states of any two secrets, when reconstruction requires a sufficiently large fraction of the shares, for fixed stationary chains satisfying an upper operator mixing condition and for resources generated by fixed finite quantum memories with primitive transfer channels. These guarantees permit arbitrary extensions of the device marginal as adversary side information and hold uniformly over prime fields larger than the number of shares. For independent device--reference pairs, which provide the quantitative input to the argument, we determine the optimal exponential base when reconstruction requires all shares: $2\sqrt2/π$ for a qubit and $2/π$ for a classical bit, in both cases allowing arbitrary finite references. A comparison of selected marginals with product states converts the bounds for independent device--reference pairs into security guarantees for the correlated resources.
