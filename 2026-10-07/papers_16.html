Intrinsic spin-orbit coupling in graphene stacks

Authors: Guillermo Parra-Martinez, Alejandro Jimeno-Pozo, Ady Stern, Jose Angel Silva-Guillén, Francisco Guinea
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.05905v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 2/5 · Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work calculates the intrinsic spin-orbit coupling in stacked graphene layers, demonstrating that this effect is crucial and cannot be ignored as in single-layer graphene. By analyzing the resulting Hamiltonian using mean-field theory, the authors map out new correlated phases and propose experimental diagnostics based on magnetization to confirm the importance of interlayer SOC.

Why it may be interesting. The detailed analysis of spin-valley locking and the emergence of correlated phases due to SOC provides rich theoretical models relevant to topological quantum materials and strongly interacting electron systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To determine the physical consequences of intrinsic spin-orbit coupling (SOC) in multilayer graphene stacks, arguing that SOC effects are significant in these systems, unlike the single-layer case.

Main result. The intrinsic SOC term ($\lambda_4$) is the dominant effect in multilayer graphene, modifying the phase diagram and leading to a distinct, measurable density-dependence of magnetization in weak magnetic fields.

Method. The study employs tight-binding models, mean-field Hartree-Fock theory, and continuum approximations to analyze the effective low-energy Hamiltonian.

Model / system. The system is graphene stacks, specifically rhombohedral multilayer graphene (RMG) and $ ext{R}4 ext{G}$. The analysis incorporates the atomic SOC and derives an effective Hamiltonian containing terms like the Kane-Mele term ($\lambda_1$) and the crucial interlayer spin-flip term ($\lambda_4$).

Key observables. Phase diagrams, Fermi surface topologies (quantified by Euler characteristic $\chi_\xi$ and index $\Lambda$), and the density-dependence of magnetization ($\langle s_z angle$).

Important parameters / regimes. SOC strengths ($\lambda_1$ vs. $\lambda_4$), carrier density ($n$), and external magnetic field strength ($B$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis uses a continuum model and neglects effects due to a possible displacement field.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the derivation of SOC terms, phase diagrams showing transitions between correlated metallic states, and the topology of the Fermi surface in different regimes.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the necessity of including intrinsic SOC in multilayer systems, deriving the effective low-energy Hamiltonian, applying Hartree-Fock theory to map out the phase diagram, and finally using magnetization diagnostics to distinguish between different SOC mechanisms.

Abstract

The effect of spin-orbit coupling (SOC) in monolayer graphene has been thoroughly studied in the past. Its impact is commonly disregarded since the electronic structure of graphene is almost unaffected. Consequently, SOC has also been ignored in other graphitic systems. In this work, we calculate the intrinsic SOC in stacks of graphene layers from a model that includes all the valence orbitals of carbon as well as the intra-atomic SOC.The leading spin-orbit term in a graphene multilayer combines a Rashba-like spin-flip process with interlayer hopping. We find that this intrinsic term modifies the phase diagram and Fermi surfaces of finite rhombohedral stacks, and it leads to a distinct density-dependence of the magnetization in weak magnetic fields. This Letter shows that, unlike in the single-layer case, SOC has important consequences in multilayer graphitic systems and it should not be neglected when studying their physical properties.

Studying the evolution of exchange and dipolar interactions and spin correlation in vdW magnet CrCl3 across magnetic phases

Authors: Prem B. Karki, Ayodimeji E. Aregbesola, Karishma Prasad, Jonathan Skeels, Avani Inamdar, Pooja Kalal, Jian Wang, Kapildeb Ambal

Summary. This work uses broadband magnetic resonance spectroscopy on CrCl3 to map the temperature evolution of magnetic interactions. By analyzing linewidth narrowing and resonance shifts, the authors distinguish between temperature-dependent exchange and relatively constant dipolar interactions. The findings confirm robust short-range magnetic correlations in the material, highlighting the technique's sensitivity for quantum magnetism studies.

Why it may be interesting. The detailed spectroscopic measurements provide quantitative insights into the interplay between short-range magnetic correlations and long-range ordering in a tunable quantum magnetic material, which is crucial for designing spintronic devices.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how exchange and dipole-dipole interactions, along with spin correlations, evolve in the van der Waals magnet CrCl3 across different magnetic phases.

Main result. The research found that the exchange interaction evolves exponentially upon cooling, while the dipole-dipole interaction remains constant, indicating short-range magnetic ordering even far above the global ordering temperature.

Method. The primary method involves multifrequency broadband (1-16 GHz) magnetic resonance measurements performed on a single-crystal CrCl3 sample, supplemented by structural characterization.

Model / system. The physical system is CrCl3, a van der Waals magnet. Analysis utilizes the Anderson-Weiss model to interpret linewidth narrowing and employs the Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless (BKT) model to analyze temperature-dependent linewidth broadening.

Key observables. Magnetic resonance linewidth, exchange field ($B_{ex}$), dipolar field ($B_d$), half-field magnetic resonance transition, and the spin correlation length ($\xi$).

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature range (RT down to 50 K), microwave frequencies (1-16 GHz), and the measured inter-spin distance (2.4(3) A).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the Anderson-Weiss theory for linewidth narrowing and the BKT model for analyzing the temperature dependence of correlations.

Figures summary. Figures show broadband magnetic resonance spectra across varying temperatures and orientations, illustrating linewidth narrowing due to exchange effects and the temperature dependence of correlation length fitted to the BKT model.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first characterizing the system using broadband magnetic resonance, then analyzing the temperature dependence of the exchange and dipole interactions, and finally using the BKT model to quantify the magnetic correlation length divergence near the phase transition.

Abstract

Understanding quantum interactions in van der Waals magnets are critical to realizing their potential for next-generation magnetic, electronic, and spintronic applications. In this study, we investigated the evolution of exchange and dipole-dipole interactions in van der Waals magnet CrCl3 using multifrequency broadband (1-16 GHz) magnetic resonance on a bulk single-crystal CrCl3 sample. We found that the dipolar-broadened low-field resonance linewidth narrows due to exchange narrowing at higher resonance fields. Our findings show that the exchange interaction evolves exponentially as we lower the sample temperature from room temperature to 50 K. However, we found that the dipole-dipole interaction remains constant in the same temperature range. We have also detected the half-field magnetic resonance transition at room temperature, indicating a robust dipole-dipole interaction among spins. The measured inter-spin distance is 2.4(3) A at room temperature. The evolution of dipole interactions, the observation of half-field magnetic resonance, and the strong exchange interaction indicate short-range ordering/correlation in CrCl3 at temperatures far above the magnetic global ordering temperature. This study also demonstrates the significance and sensitivity of the multifrequency electron spin resonance technique for understanding quantum interactions and magnetic correlations in van der Waals magnetic materials.

Summary. This theoretical work develops an advanced model for magnetization dynamics in antiferromagnets by incorporating inertial damping alongside standard damping mechanisms. By analyzing the system across three magnetic phases, the authors derive the full spectrum of eigenfrequencies and propose an experimental protocol to measure the underlying dynamical coefficients.

Why it may be interesting. The detailed treatment of inertial damping and the derivation of an 'inertial Landau–Lifshitz–Gilbert equation' provides a sophisticated framework for modeling high-frequency dynamics in magnetic materials, relevant to understanding quantum coherence in spin systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper formulates and analyzes the equations of motion for uniform magnetization dynamics in multisublattice magnets, focusing on the inclusion of reactive inertia and a novel inertial damping torque.

Main result. The model successfully derives the dynamical magnetic susceptibilities and eigenfrequencies across three distinct magnetic phases (AP, CA, FP), showing how inertial damping affects the relaxation of nutational modes.

Method. The methodology involves deriving phenomenological equations of motion, linearizing them around equilibrium points, and calculating the eigenfrequencies by solving the determinant of the resulting dynamical matrix.

Model / system. The system is a general multisublattice magnet, specifically applied to a two-sublattice uniaxial antiferromagnet. Dynamics are described using Néel ($\mathbf{n}$) and magnetization ($\mathbf{m}$) vectors, constrained by fixed-length conditions.

Key observables. Dynamical magnetic susceptibilities ($\chi$), resonance frequencies ($\Omega = \omega - i\Gamma$), and linewidths of precessional and nutational modes.

Important parameters / regimes. Exchange frequency ($\omega_E$), anisotropy frequency ($\omega_A$), Zeeman frequency ($\omega_H$), and various damping coefficients (Gilbert, inertial, etc.).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes fixed magnitudes for magnetization vectors and relies on perturbative expansions in damping parameters, particularly in the exchange-dominated regime.

Figures summary. Figure 1 shows the field dependence of eigenfrequencies and quality factors across the AP, CA, and FP phases, summarizing the results in Table I.

Paper structure. The paper builds from general equations of motion for multisublattice magnets to specific derivations for the two-sublattice case. It analyzes linear response around three equilibrium phases (AP, CA, FP) by calculating dynamical susceptibilities and solving for eigenfrequencies in different approximations.

Abstract

We formulate equations of motion for uniform magnetization dynamics in multisublattice magnets, with emphasis on a recently proposed inertial damping torque. We include intra- and intersublattice contributions to Gilbert damping, reactive inertia, and inertial damping. For a two-sublattice uniaxial antiferromagnet in an easy-axis static magnetic field, we derive the dynamical magnetic susceptibilities and approximate eigenfrequencies across all field-dependent equilibrium phases. In particular, we show how inertial damping affects the relaxation of the nutational modes and propose an experimental protocol to determine the dynamical coefficients.

Important parameters / regimes. The dimensionless parameters K (in-plane anisotropy) and B (out-of-plane Zeeman field) define the phase diagram.

Figures summary. The notes mention a phase diagram (Fig. 1(b)) illustrating the crossover between different magnetic phases.

Abstract

We perform an exhaustive study of the parameter space of the chiral magnetic model with a Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction, an in-plane anisotropy term and an out-of-plane Zeeman magnetic field. We map out the phase diagram for the model and find a chiral soliton lattice hosted on a chiral soliton lattice as the ground state in a particular corner of the phase diagram. There is a smooth crossover, as opposed to a phase transition, to the skyrmion crystal lattice phase. Finally, we also discover excited composite soliton states such as an anti-skyrmion coexisting with a bimeron or an anti-bimeron.

Summary. This work investigates the electronic structure of graphene grown on SiC by combining high-resolution STEM-EELS experiments with detailed DFT calculations. The key finding is that substrate interaction induces site-specific hybridization, leading to measurable symmetry breaking in the electronic orbitals. This provides a quantitative framework for tailoring graphene interfaces for advanced electronic applications.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on solid-state physics, the concept of engineering electronic properties via controlled interface interactions and symmetry breaking is relevant to designing quantum materials platforms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to understand how the interaction between epitaxial graphene and the SiC substrate modifies its electronic structure, particularly focusing on the origin of any induced bandgap or electronic changes.

Main result. The substrate interaction causes site-specific symmetry breaking and partial sp^3 hybridization in the buffer layer, which can be spectroscopically identified by analyzing the C K-edge ELNES features.

Method. The research combines experimental measurements using STEM-EELS with theoretical calculations employing Finite-Difference Method Near-Edge Structure (FDMNES) simulations.

Model / system. The system is epitaxial graphene grown on SiC(0001). The analysis distinguishes between covalently bonded (CB) sites, which show sp^3 character, and non-covalently bonded (CNB) sites, which retain more sp^2 character.

Abstract

The epitaxial graphene grown on SiC interface has been the subject of tremendous research efforts to control its electronic transport properties in the quest for graphene electronic applications. The electronic structure of the epitaxial graphene buffer layer on SiC(0001) is investigated at the atomic scale using monochromated electron energy loss spectroscopy (EELS) in an aberration-corrected scanning transmission electron microscope (STEM). The C $K$-edge reveals spectral fine structures distinctly different from free-standing graphene layer, providing direct experimental evidence of a modified bonding environment. Ab initio calculations providing a site-resolved probe of orbital character to elucidate the origin of these spectral features: covalent bonding between one-quarter of the buffer-layer carbon atoms and the substrate silicon atoms induces partial $\mathrm{sp}^3$ hybridization, quenching the out-of-plane $π$ states and disrupting $π$-conjugation across the interfacial layer. Substrate-imposed symmetry breaking further lifts the in-plane orbital degeneracy. This orbital-engineering framework, rooted in $π$-state quenching and site-specific symmetry breaking, establishes a quantitative basis for tailoring graphene-substrate interfaces toward next-generation low-energy transistor applications.

Anisotropic Friedel oscillations and isoperimetric transport in two-dimensional free lattice Fermi seas

Authors: Guillermo R. Zemba
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.04724v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5 · non-equilibrium universality 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical work investigates how underlying lattice symmetries constrain the physics of 2D free electron gases, leading to anisotropic density patterns. It derives a universal relationship showing that electrical conductivity scales inversely with the square root of the Fermi surface's isoperimetric parameter. This framework unifies geometric topology, density oscillations, and transport properties into a single, powerful descriptor.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a powerful geometric descriptor ($\mathcal{I}$) to unify mesoscopic density physics (Friedel oscillations) with macroscopic transport (conductivity) in a way that transcends simple continuum models, suggesting new topological invariants for conductors.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper explores the physical consequences of classifying 2D Fermi seas using flat orbifolds defined by discrete lattice symmetries ($\Gamma$). It connects the geometry of the Fermi surface to measurable quantities like density modulations and electrical conductivity.

Main result. It establishes that the single-particle density matrix exhibits anisotropic Friedel oscillations dictated by lattice symmetry, and it derives a universal scaling law for conductivity: $\sigma_{xx} \propto 1/\sqrt{\mathcal{I}}$, where $\mathcal{I}$ is the isoperimetric parameter.

Method. The analysis combines concepts from geometric measure theory, chiral anomaly calculations (bosonization), and the study of single-particle density matrices on orbifold manifolds.

Model / system. The system is a non-interacting 2D free gas of fermions confined to a background with discrete lattice symmetries, modeled mathematically as a flat orbifold $\mathbb{R}^2/\Gamma$. The analysis covers both the density structure and the resulting macroscopic transport properties.

Key observables. Anisotropic Friedel oscillations in the single-particle density matrix, and the longitudinal electrical conductivity ($\sigma_{xx}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The isoperimetric parameter ($\mathcal{I}$), Fermi wavelength ($\lambda_F$), and thermal de Broglie wavelength ($\lambda_T$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on a mean-field approach for conductivity and assumes that the topological structure dictates the universal scaling laws, even when incorporating structural drag from orbifold corners.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the spatial density matrix $ ho(\mathbf{r}, 0)$ for specific lattices ($p4m, p6m$), showing anisotropic diffraction patterns, and reference power-law decay regimes.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from analyzing the isotropic case (ideal 2D gas) to the anisotropic case using flat orbifolds. It then develops the conductivity calculation by relating the Fermi surface geometry to the isoperimetric parameter, culminating in a unified formula incorporating structural drag from orbifold corners.

Abstract

Two physical consequences of the recent topological classification of 2d Fermi seas for free particles based on flat orbifolds $\mathbb{R}^2/Γ$ of discrete symmetry $Γ$, are explored. The first is an study of the spatial density behavior inside the mesoscopic window enclosed by the Fermi and (infinite) thermal lengths. It is shown that the single-particle density matrix exhibits a sharp directional anisotropy, encoding a real space diffraction pattern, {\it i.e.}, a Fraunhofer-like diagram of the Fermi sea, that reveals the point-group symmetries of the background lattice $Γ$ in the form of anisotropic Friedel oscillations. As examples, the special cases of the $p4m$ and $p6m$ crystallographic flat orbifolds are treated in some detail. The second physical consequence is the establishment of an explicit geometric correspondence between momentum-space packing and electrical transport. A mean-field expression for the longitudinal electrical conductivity $σ_{xx}$ as a function of the {\it isoperimetric parameter} $\cal{I}$ of the occupied orbifold domain by utilizing geometric measure theory is presented, satisfiying the scaling law $ σ_{xx} \propto 1/\sqrt{\cal{I}}$. The topological transport formula naturally incorporates the vanishing conductivity ($σ_{xx} \to 0$) of the previously described ``geometric insulators,'' promoting the isoperimetric parameter as an efficient descriptor for topological and geometric phases in conductors.

Summary. This work demonstrates a giant, 50-fold nonlinearity in the kinetic inductance of superconducting $ ext{NbN/Al}$ hybrid strips. Abstract

We demonstrate giant nonlinearity of the kinetic inductance $L_k$ in a superconducting NbN/Al hybrid strip. In contrast to ordinary superconductors, where the maximum observed current-induced variation of $L_k$ does not exceed tens of percent, we observe a 50-fold change in $L_k$. Formally, this leads to $L_k \sim dq/dI \to \infty$ as $I \to I^*$. We believe that our results open a new route for creating the next generation of highly sensitive detectors of electromagnetic radiation, calorimeters, parametric amplifiers, and magnetometers.

Summary. This paper theoretically demonstrates that intense, low-frequency laser fields can induce a qualitative bifurcation in the energy gap structure of crystalline solids. This phenomenon, which changes the local minimum geometry, is driven by field-modified band curvature and is spectroscopically observable as an additional feature in the interband susceptibility.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a general, non-perturbative mechanism for how strong-field dressing modifies the local geometry of the electronic spectrum, which is highly relevant for understanding ultrafast spectroscopy and light-matter interactions in solids.

Main problem. The study investigates how an intense, low-frequency laser field can qualitatively alter the local extremal structure of the interband energy gap within a crystalline solid.

Main result. The gap exhibits a bifurcation at a critical field strength, where a single minimum splits into two symmetry-related minima separated by a local maximum. This is signaled spectroscopically by the emergence of an additional van Hove feature in the interband susceptibility.

Method. The authors derive a quartic normal form for the dressed gap near a high-symmetry point and analyze the system using an instantaneous laser-dressed basis obtained from nonperturbative diagonalization of the multiband Hamiltonian.

Model / system. The theory is applied to the electronic structure of solids, using the 1D Kronig-Penney model as a concrete realization. The dynamics are governed by the time-dependent Schrödinger equation under the influence of a strong, low-frequency laser field.

Abstract

We show that an intense low-frequency laser field can qualitatively modify the local extremal structure of an interband energy gap in a crystal. Authors: Thomas Huang, Yueyao Fan, Kaichen Xie, Bolun Li, Kaijie Yang, Jenna A. Bilbrey, Eric Bylaska, Peter V. Sushko, Di Xiao, Ting Cao
Type: theory · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.04115v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

Summary. This work introduces MoiréMLIP, a machine-learned potential trained specifically on moiré materials to overcome the computational barriers of simulating large, twisted superstructures. By fine-tuning and compressing this model, the authors created a highly accurate potential capable of simulating systems up to one million atoms. This enables predictive simulations of complex phenomena, such as hierarchical polarization networks, in emergent moiré systems.

Why it may be interesting. The development of highly accurate, scalable MLIPs for moiré systems provides a powerful tool for simulating emergent electronic and structural phenomena in 2D materials, which is crucial for understanding correlated electron physics in these systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Direct ab initio modeling of large, complex moiré superstructures is computationally prohibitive due to the high dimensionality of structural degrees of freedom.

Main result. The authors developed MoiréMLIP, a fine-tuned machine-learning potential, which accurately reproduces DFT reconstructions and scales to one million atoms using knowledge distillation.

Method. They fine-tuned a universal MLIP on a specialized dataset (Moiré Kaleidoscope) and employed knowledge distillation to create a highly accurate, yet computationally efficient, student model.

Model / system. The study focuses on moiré lattices formed by twisted transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDs) like MoTe2. The model aims to capture complex structural reconstructions arising from moiré supercells.

Key observables. Force Root-Mean-Square Error (RMSE), interlayer distance differences (e.g., $\Delta d_{ ext{MM}- ext{MX}}$), and the emergent polarization network.

Important parameters / regimes. Twist angles (e.g., 7.34$^\circ$, 5.09$^\circ$, 3.48$^\circ$), system size (up to one million atoms), and force error thresholds (e.g., 6--8 meV/Å).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that fine-tuning a universal MLIP on a comprehensive dataset can overcome the limitations of existing potentials when applied to novel moiré geometries.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the workflow of dataset construction, the learning curves showing force RMSE reduction with training data volume, and the comparison of reconstruction profiles across different models.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the computational bottleneck of moiré materials, introduces the MoiréMLIP methodology via dataset curation and fine-tuning, validates its accuracy against DFT benchmarks, and finally applies the model to predict emergent physical properties like polarization in large-scale systems.

Abstract

Atomic reconstruction reshapes moiré materials across multiple scales, from local structure and polarization textures to global electronic topology, yet direct \textit{ab initio} modeling becomes prohibitive for large superstructures such as marginal-twist-angle moirés and moiré-of-moirés. We develop MoiréMLIP by fine-tuning a universal atomistic model on the density functional theory labeled Moiré Kaleidoscope dataset, which spans transition metal dichalcogenide compositions, symmetries, stackings, and twist angles. MoiréMLIP reproduces \textit{ab initio} reconstruction with force errors of 6--8\,meV/Å and transfers to smaller twist angles and unseen structures. Knowledge distillation yields MoiréMLIP-mini, which retains this accuracy while extending single-GPU inference to one million atoms. Applied to an alternate-twist MoTe$_2$ trilayer, it reveals a hierarchical polarization network spanning tens of nanometers arising from large moiré-cell distortions sharply localized along the moiré-of-moiré domain walls. These results overcome key accuracy, transferability, and scaling bottlenecks of existing atomistic models and enable predictive simulations across emergent moiré length scales.

Gate-tunable large second-order and third-order nonlinear Hall effect in topological insulator BiSbTeSe2

Authors: Fengyi Guo, Shuai Zhang, Xiubing Li, Zheng Dai, Congcong Li, Yufan Ju, Ziqi Wang, Cheng Bin, Zhe Ying, Fengqi Song
Type: experiment · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.05713v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work reports the observation of large and highly gate-tunable second- and third-order nonlinear Hall effects in $ ext{BiSbTeSe}_2$ topological insulators. By utilizing dual-gating, the researchers demonstrated that these nonlinear responses are strongly dependent on the Fermi level position relative to the Dirac point. This establishes a promising experimental platform for developing next-generation electronic devices based on topological properties.

Why it may be interesting. While the primary focus is condensed matter transport, the strong dependence of the nonlinear response on topological invariants (like BCD/BPT) provides a rich platform for studying quantum geometric effects in electronic transport, which can inform theoretical models of quantum materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to explore and engineer a strong and highly tunable Nonlinear Hall Effect (NLHE) for use in next-generation electronic devices.

Main result. Large, highly gate-tunable second-order and third-order NLHEs were observed in dual-gated $ ext{BiSbTeSe}_2$, with the second-order effect showing higher generation efficiency than previous topological insulator systems and remaining stable up to room temperature.

Method. The researchers measured the second-order ($2\omega$) and third-order ($3\omega$) nonlinear Hall voltages by applying an a.c. excitation current and using lock-in amplification, while tuning the Fermi level via dual-gating.

Model / system. The physical platform is a dual-gated topological insulator, $ ext{BiSbTeSe}_2$, where transport is dominated by topological surface states (TSSs). The measurements are performed by modulating the gate voltages to tune the Fermi level relative to the Dirac point (CNP).

Key observables. Second-order nonlinear Hall voltage ($V_{xy}^{2\omega}$), third-order nonlinear Hall voltage ($V_{xy}^{3\omega}$), and the nonlinear Hall generation efficiencies ($\eta^{(2)}$ and $\eta^{(3)}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Gate voltage ($V_{tg}, V_{bg}$), temperature (up to 300 K), and the proximity to the Charge Neutrality Point (CNP).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on scaling arguments linking the observed signals to specific physical mechanisms, such as the Berry curvature dipole (BCD) or Berry connection polarizability tensor (BPT).

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the gate-dependent mapping of $R_{xx}$ and the nonlinear Hall voltages, confirming tunability near the CNP. Temperature dependence studies show the persistence of the second-order effect up to room temperature.

Paper structure. The paper systematically measures the nonlinear response at different temperatures and gate biases, analyzes the scaling of the nonlinear coefficients with the longitudinal conductance squared, and discusses the microscopic origins of the observed effects based on theoretical models.

Abstract

The nonlinear Hall effect (NLHE) holds significant promise for next-generation electronic device applications, and it is essential to explore and engineer a strong and highly tunable NLHE. In this work, we report large second-order and third-order NLHEs in dual-gated BiSbTeSe2 devices. Both the second-order and third-order NLHE are highly gate-tunable. The second-order NLHE exhibits a higher generation efficiency than those reported in previous topological insulator systems, and it can be stably observed up to room temperature. Meanwhile, the generation efficiency of the third-order NLHE is comparable to previously reported values. The scaling analysis indicates that the second-order NLHE is consistent with contributions from skew scattering and a term compatible with either the Berry curvature dipole or side jump, whereas the third-order NLHE is consistent with contributions from the Berry connection polarizability tensor and higher-order skew scattering. Our work establishes a promising experimental platform for the development of NLHE-based devices.
