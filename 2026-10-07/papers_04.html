Summary. This paper proposes a novel method to simulate open quantum system dynamics at finite temperatures using gate-based quantum computers. By exploiting U(1) symmetry, the authors show that the complex thermal dynamics can be reconstructed classically from measurements performed only with pure ancilla states. This significantly reduces quantum circuit overhead and preparation errors compared to traditional thermal state preparation methods.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete, resource-efficient protocol for simulating thermal quantum dynamics on near-term quantum hardware by shifting the complexity from quantum state preparation to classical post-processing.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of simulating finite-temperature open-system dynamics on gate-based quantum computers, specifically minimizing the overhead and errors associated with preparing thermal ancilla states.

Main result. It demonstrates that for U(1)-symmetric interactions, finite-temperature dynamics can be reconstructed classically from measurements performed using only pure ancilla states, thereby bypassing explicit thermal state preparation.

Method. The methodology involves decomposing the dynamics into population and coherence sectors, deriving reconstruction formulas based on the symmetry constraints, and analyzing the resulting error scaling for different noise models.

Model / system. The system is an open quantum system modeled using collision maps, where the environment interaction is represented by sequential system-ancilla collisions. The dynamics are analyzed using the Bloch vector representation and exploiting U(1) symmetry.

Key observables. Population relaxation coefficients ($\gamma$), coherence variables ($w$), and preparation-induced error terms ($\Delta r_{ ext{prep}}^n$, $E_{ ext{prep}}^n$).

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature ($T$), which is treated classically in the post-processing step; noise parameters associated with the depolarizing and amplitude-damping channels.

Assumptions / limitations. The reconstruction relies critically on the U(1) symmetry of the system-ancilla interactions; error analysis uses first-order approximations in error parameters.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the workflow: generating pure-branch data on the quantum computer followed by classical reconstruction to determine finite-temperature dynamics.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the collision model and symmetry decomposition, presents the reconstruction formulae for both population and coherence sectors, and concludes with a detailed comparison of preparation-induced error scaling under various noise models.

Abstract

Simulating open-system dynamics on gate-based quantum computers offers a route to exploring dissipative quantum phenomena through programmable quantum processing. Collision models provide a direct and flexible framework by representing the environment through sequential system--ancilla collisions. At finite temperatures, however, they typically require repeated preparation of thermal ancillas, adding circuit overhead and gate errors. Here, we show that, for a broad class of $U(1)$-symmetric system--ancilla interactions, finite-temperature dynamics can instead be reconstructed from simulations with pure ancillas in orthogonal basis states. The target temperature enters only through classical post-processing of the resulting pure-branch data, allowing the same data to be reused for different temperatures without additional quantum processing. Bypassing explicit thermal-state preparation reduces ancilla-preparation overhead and preparation-induced errors.

Summary. This theoretical work develops a comprehensive framework to classify mixed-state topological orders in (3+1)D by mapping them onto braided fusion 2-categories. By modeling the effect of decoherence on pure-state systems, the authors demonstrate that these mathematical structures govern the physical realization of exotic topological phases, particularly those exhibiting intrinsic mixed-state characteristics.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a rigorous mathematical framework connecting abstract category theory (braided fusion 2-categories) to concrete physical phenomena like decoherence and emergent symmetries in higher-dimensional quantum matter.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to systematically classify and realize mixed-state topological orders (mTOs) in (3+1)D, distinguishing them from pure-state orders by analyzing the role of decoherence and higher-form symmetries.

Main result. Every braided fusion 2-category can be realized as the topological operator category of a (3+1)D mTO via a general construction involving decoherence and gauging. Intrinsically mixed-state orders (imTOs) are characterized by specific anomalous symmetries.

Method. The authors employ advanced mathematical tools, relating the data of braided fusion 2-categories to physical symmetries and anomalies, and modeling the process via decoherence channels applied to parent pure-state theories.

Model / system. The focus is on (3+1)D mixed-state topological orders, exemplified by applying decoherence channels to the fermionic $\mathbb{Z}_2$ toric code Hamiltonian. The mathematical framework utilizes braided fusion 2-categories to encode the topological structure.

Key observables. Strong higher-form symmetries, their anomalies (e.g., 't Hooft anomaly), and the failure of two-way channel connectivity to a pure state.

Important parameters / regimes. The dimensionality (3+1)D is crucial, as the authors highlight novel mechanisms absent in (2+1)D, such as gravitational anomalies.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis focuses on bosonic theories, and the classification relies on the mathematical structure of braided fusion 2-categories, which is a generalization beyond standard (2+1)D models.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the vertex and plaquette terms of the (3+1)D fermionic $\mathbb{Z}_2$ toric code Hamiltonian and define the operators used in the decoherence process.

Paper structure. The paper builds from defining the mathematical structure of braided fusion 2-categories, generalizing classification tools from (2+1)D to (3+1)D, and finally applying these tools to model the physical process of decoherence to generate mTOs.

Abstract

We construct large families of mixed-state topological orders in (3+1)D, including intrinsically mixed-state topological orders. We realize these theories by relating the data parameterizing braided fusion 2-categories to the strong higher-form symmetries and their anomalies that characterize mixed-state topological order. We focus here on bosonic theories, which include theories which admit an emergent fermion in the spectrum of line operators. Our general framework is exemplified on the lattice, where we present concrete models and identify how the data of braided fusion 2-categories are physically encoded. We then formalize this data and show that every braided fusion 2-category arises from a general construction (decoherence followed by strong and weak gauging) as the category of topological operators of a (3+1)D mixed state, with intrinsically mixed-state topological orders distinguished by the presence of strong higher-form symmetries whose anomalies cannot be realized in any (3+1)D gapped ground state. Notably, we show that (3+1)D admits a new mechanism with no (2+1)D counterpart: intrinsically mixed-state order whose topological operators braid non-degenerately, but which carries a gravitational anomaly.

Abstract

We examine the dc driven transport of particles with Yukawa interactions through an assembly of chiral active particles undergoing circular motion. The driven particles exhibit a jammed phase, a nonlinear disordered or plastic regime where there is a finite Hall effect produced by interactions with the active chiral particles, and a high drive linear regime where the driven and nondriven particles phase separate and the Hall effect nearly disappears. The chiral particles experience strong drag and Hall effects from the driven particles, and these effects vary monotonically with changing thermal fluctuations or chiral motion parameters. The different dynamical phases are correlated with features in the longitudinal and transverse transport curves, and we map out the phases as a function of active orbit size, thermal fluctuations, and particle density.

Summary. This work develops theoretical tools to quantify how fast quantum Gibbs samplers approach thermal equilibrium by analyzing the decay of relative entropy. It successfully proves exponential decay rates for several important physical models, such as Bose-Hubbard and Coulomb systems. Crucially, it also establishes a boundary case, showing that a positive spectral gap alone is insufficient to guarantee the strong convergence properties (hypercontractivity) needed for the fastest decay rates.

Why it may be interesting. It provides rigorous mathematical tools (hypercontractivity, entropy decay rates) to analyze the convergence speed of quantum thermalization processes in complex, infinite-dimensional many-body systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper derives rates of exponential relative-entropy decay for a broad class of infinite-dimensional quantum Gibbs samplers used for thermal state preparation.

Main result. Exponential entropy decay rates are established for Bose-Hubbard samplers and harmonically confined quantum Coulomb gases, while also providing a counterexample showing positive spectral gap does not guarantee finite-time hypercontractivity.

Method. The analysis heavily relies on quantum Markov semigroups, utilizing hypercontractivity estimates and modified logarithmic Sobolev inequalities to bound the relative entropy decay.

Model / system. The study covers general infinite-dimensional quantum Gibbs samplers, including full multimode Bose-Hubbard samplers, harmonically confined quantum Coulomb gases, and a periodic charging model on $\ell^2(\mathbb{Z})$.

Key observables. Relative entropy $D( ho\|\sigma)$, spectral gap ($\lambda$), and the $L^2 o L^p$ map bounds.

Important parameters / regimes. The decay rates ($\alpha_{HC}, \alpha_{KMS}$) depend on the spectral gap ($\lambda$) and the constants derived from hypercontractivity bounds.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis often assumes the existence of a positive spectral gap ($\lambda > 0$) or relies on specific structural properties of the interaction terms (e.g., finite-rank approximations).

Paper structure. The paper progresses by establishing general theoretical bounds using hypercontractivity, applying these bounds to specific physical models like Bose-Hubbard and Coulomb systems, and concluding with a counterexample demonstrating the limitations of spectral gap conditions.

Abstract

We derive rates of exponential relative-entropy decay for a broad class of infinite-dimensional quantum Gibbs samplers. A key technical ingredient is a perturbative stability theory for hypercontractive estimates and logarithmic Sobolev inequalities. For suitably chosen dissipative couplings, we obtain exponential entropy decay for full multimode Bose-Hubbard samplers with arbitrary hopping and repulsive density interactions. For harmonically confined quantum Coulomb gases and molecular systems, we prove exponential entropy decay for every fixed finite-rank low-energy truncation of the interaction. Finally, we exhibit a periodic charging model with a positive spectral gap but no finite-time $L^2$-to-$L^p$ bound for any $p>2$.

Summary. This paper extends the quantum-classical correspondence for non-Hermitian systems by developing a momentum-space path integral formulation. It rigorously compares this dual formulation to the coordinate-space version, showing necessary corrections for noncontractible orbits. The framework is successfully applied to model the spectral transition of Bloch oscillations in finite non-Hermitian lattices.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous, generalized semiclassical tool to analyze spectral transitions (real-to-complex) in non-Hermitian systems, which is highly relevant for understanding open quantum systems and parity-time symmetric physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops and compares momentum-space and coordinate-space path integral approaches to establish a quantum-classical correspondence for non-Hermitian symmetry breaking.

Main result. The correspondence is established, showing that the momentum-space formulation requires a boundary term correction for noncontractible orbits, which is naturally incorporated when analyzing Bloch oscillations in finite lattices.

Method. The authors use path integrals (both coordinate and momentum space) applied to the Green operator, deriving dual Gutzwiller trace formulas and quantization conditions.

Model / system. The analysis focuses on general non-Hermitian systems, particularly those satisfying $\eta$-pseudo-Hermiticity, and applies the framework to Bloch oscillations in a finite non-Hermitian lattice.

Key observables. Real or complex nature of individual eigenenergies; quantization conditions derived from path integrals.

Important parameters / regimes. The winding number (real-space vs. Brillouin-zone); the boundary term correction for noncontractible orbits.

Assumptions / limitations. The validity of the Saddle-Point Approximation (SPA) and the ability to analytically continue semiclassical phase-space variables to the complex domain.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the complex energy spectrum structure for the model Hamiltonian and compare numerical Bloch-oscillation energy levels against analytical quantization condition results.

Paper structure. The paper builds upon prior coordinate-space work by deriving the momentum-space dual formulation, comparing the resulting quantization conditions for contractible vs. noncontractible orbits, and concluding with a detailed application to Bloch oscillations.

Abstract

A quantum-classical correspondence for non-Hermitian symmetry breaking has recently been established using coordinate-space path integrals, providing a semiclassical understanding of spectral transitions at the level of individual eigenstates. Here we develop its dual formulation in momentum space by constructing the corresponding trace formula and quantization condition. We show that the real or complex nature of individual eigenvalues is determined by the symmetry properties of the associated semiclassical orbits, as in the coordinate-space path-integral approach. Moreover, we demonstrate that the topology of semiclassical orbits determines the natural formulation of the quantization condition: the coordinate- and momentum-space formulations are equivalent for contractible periodic orbits in phase space, whereas for noncontractible orbits, the quantization condition along the winding direction remains valid, but its dual form must be corrected by a boundary term. In particular, real-space-winding and Brillouin-zone-winding orbits naturally select coordinate- and momentum-space quantization, respectively. As a nontrivial application, we investigate the boundary-induced spectral transition of Bloch oscillations in a finite non-Hermitian lattice, where Bloch-oscillation orbits winding across the Brillouin zone preserve the relevant symmetry and yield real energy levels, whereas boundary-reflected orbits form symmetry-related pairs and give rise to complex-conjugate eigenvalues. Our work completes the quantum-classical correspondence framework for non-Hermitian symmetry breaking, extending its applicability to a broader class of non-Hermitian problems.

Summary. This work quantifies the 'magic cost' of fermionic randomness, determining that achieving quantum chaotic sampling statistics requires the input state to possess a defect ($ u$) that scales linearly with system size. The authors establish a direct, measurable link between this defect and the necessary preparation resources, providing a quantitative witness for non-Gaussianity in fermionic quantum computation.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a rigorous, quantitative measure ($ u$) linking fundamental quantum resource limitations (non-Gaussianity) to observable statistical properties (collision moments) in fermionic many-body systems, which is highly relevant for understanding quantum simulation feasibility.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the minimum non-Gaussian resource required in the input state of fermionic systems to achieve quantum sampling statistics associated with quantum chaos, especially when the evolution dynamics are passive and Gaussian.

Main result. Achieving anticoncentration or approaching Porter-Thomas statistics requires the one-body defect ($ u$) to scale linearly with system size ($ u = \Omega(N)$), establishing an extensive 'magic cost' for fermionic randomness.

Method. The analysis uses theoretical bounds derived from analyzing collision moments, two-copy interference protocols, and comparing asymptotic behavior to known random matrix statistics.

Model / system. The system models fermionic particles evolving via passive fermionic linear optics (FLO) on $2N$ fermions, analyzed specifically at half-filling. The resource is quantified by the one-body defect ($ u$).

Key observables. One-body defect ($ u$), defect density ($\gamma$), normalized collision moment ($C_N$), and the two-copy variance ($ ext{Var}(\Delta n)$).

Important parameters / regimes. System size ($N$), defect density ($\gamma$), and the ratio of the collision moment to the Porter-Thomas value (2).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the evolution is passive and that the resource is entirely contained within the input state. Hardness results rely on conjectures regarding the polynomial hierarchy.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the relationship between the collision moment ($C_N$) and defect density ($\gamma$), showing the distinct asymptotic limits for Slater inputs versus the Porter-Thomas regime, and visualizing the two-copy variance measurement.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the obstruction caused by Gaussian evolution, then deriving the necessary scaling of the defect ($ u$) to overcome it, and finally providing a quantitative link between this defect and the preparation cost via two-copy measurements.

Abstract

Sampling-based quantum advantage raises a basic resource question: how much non-Gaussianity is needed to reproduce the random-matrix statistics often associated with quantum chaos? Passive fermionic linear optics preserves Gaussian structure, and even Haar-random mode mixing fails to anticoncentrate for Slater inputs. We show that overcoming this obstruction requires an extensive input resource. For any pure half-filled input state, the one-body defect $ν$, defined as the summed occupation variance of the natural orbitals, must scale linearly with system size. For products of bounded-size blocks on $4N$ modes, this condition is also sufficient, with normalized collision moment asymptotic to $2N/ν$, compared with the Porter-Thomas value 2. For four-mode products, the full Porter-Thomas moment hierarchy is recovered precisely when the defect density tends to its maximum. Assuming average-case hardness, approximate classical sampling remains hard near maximal density, with an error tolerance that increases with the density. Two-copy number fluctuations measure $ν$ directly, and the same quantity lower-bounds the combined cost of non-Gaussian resource modes and parity-preserving local gates in deterministic exact preparation. Fermionic randomness therefore carries an extensive magic cost, with a quantitative link between sampling statistics, preparation resources, and an experimentally accessible witness.

Abstract

Spatial smearing and an ordered weak-coupling then small-separation limit specify the microscopic scope of the lifetime prediction.

Abstract

A recurring design principle in modern optimizers is to decouple update magnitude from the raw gradient norm, yet its consequences for learning-curve and resource scaling remain unclear. We isolate this mechanism by studying normalized SGD in a random-feature model with power-law teacher and data covariance. Fixed-norm updates induce an effective learning rate that grows as gradients shrink. We derive a dynamical mean-field theory (DMFT) describing the joint dependence of the loss on training time, model width and batch size. Normalization initially accelerates SGD, mapping the power-law exponent $r_{\rm SGD}<1$ to $2r_{\rm SGD}/(1-r_{\rm SGD})$, with exponential convergence at $r_{\rm SGD}=1$ and formal finite-time convergence for $r_{\rm SGD}>1$. At finite step size, however, the same feedback ultimately breaks the acceleration and leads to marginal stability. The late-time theory yields width-limited, edge-of-stochastic-stability (EoSS), and deterministic edge-of-stability (EoS) regimes. These phases determine when larger batches or wider models reduce serial training time at comparable compute. We quantify in which of these phases increased batch size or width can compensate the excess compute use per step by fewer optimization steps to target loss. Linearized ResNet experiments on CIFAR-5M support the predicted acceleration, breakdown, and resource-scaling trends. Together, these results connect normalization-induced acceleration, EoS effects, and width--batch allocation within a solvable theory.

We reconstruct this matrix, up to physically irrelevant phase choices, from a quadratic number of measurable Bargmann invariants (quantities encoding overlap magnitudes and collective phases). We access these quantities using two complementary approaches: targeted Fourier interferometers and two- and three-body photon-number correlations measured in a single random interferometer. We derive explicit error bounds that control the total-variation distance between predicted and actual outcome distributions for any subsequent experiment that is insensitive to the internal states. These guarantees account for finite sampling and deviations from purity. Crucially, the total sample complexity of the protocol is independent of the internal Hilbert-space dimension and polynomial under mild assumptions on overlaps of internal states. Our protocol uses standard interferometric measurements and turns them into a robust and operationally grounded characterization of partially distinguishable photons.

Abstract

We study how the critical behavior of holographic superconductors controls the interior geometry of charged black holes. Although the exterior condensate vanishes at criticality, the critical zero mode develops logarithmic oscillations near the Reissner--Nordström Cauchy horizon, rendering the interior critical limit nonuniform. We show that the transmitted critical-mode amplitude, together with local Cauchy-horizon quantities, fixes both the leading Einstein--Rosen collapse $Δz_{\rm ER}$ width and the phase $Θ_J$ in the subsequent Josephson regime. Thus near-critical scaling is governed by critical-mode transmission, whereas the low-temperature behavior is controlled by the infrared endpoint.

Abstract

We study the spectral gap of recently discovered families of KMS-detailed-balanced Gibbs-sampler Lindbladians for the mean-field Heisenberg model on $n$ spins.
