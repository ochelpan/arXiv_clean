Error-Transparent Code Switching Between Permutation-Invariant Codes with Transversal Clifford and Icosahedral Gates

Authors: Zachary P. Bradshaw
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.05189v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical work establishes the necessary and sufficient conditions for performing error-transparent switching between two distinct permutation-invariant quantum error-correcting codes. By linking the switch's existence to the continuous path connecting the codes through distance-three structures, the authors construct a highly accurate, fault-tolerant switching unitary. This advances the practical feasibility of modular quantum computation by ensuring robustness during code transitions.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical framework for ensuring fault-tolerant operations across different quantum codes is highly relevant to building large-scale quantum processors.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of achieving error-transparent code switching between two different permutation-invariant quantum error-correcting codes.

Main result. An error-transparent switch exists if and only if the two codes are connected by a continuous path of distance-three codes, and the constructed unitary achieves machine-precision recovery fidelity.

Method. The analysis uses algebraic constraints derived from the requirement of distance-three codes and constructs the switching unitary as a collective operator along a continuous path.

Model / system. The system involves quantum error-correcting codes on $n$ qubits, specifically permutation-invariant (PI) codes utilizing transversal Clifford and Icosahedral gates. The switching mechanism is analyzed by examining the constancy of the error geometry along the path connecting the two codes.

Key observables. Recovery infidelity per fault, error geometry (inner products between error states), and the fidelity of the recovered channel.

Important parameters / regimes. The distance of the codes (specifically distance $\ge 3$), the fault probability ($p$), and the number of qubits ($n$, up to 29).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the switch acts on a single block without ancilla codes, and the error model is restricted to single-qubit faults.

Figures summary. The notes do not provide a summary of figures, but mention explicit constructions for 13 qubits forming a circle through Clifford and Icosahedral codes.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining the problem (error-transparent switching) to establishing necessary conditions (distance-three codes, constant error geometry), deriving the existence criterion (continuous path), and finally constructing and verifying the unitary operator.

Abstract

Code switching moves an encoded qubit between two codes whose transversal gates together are universal, but the switching operation itself is usually not transversal and can spread a single fault into an uncorrectable error. We study switching between two permutation-invariant codes on the same qubits, one with transversal Clifford gates and one with transversal gates from the non-Clifford binary icosahedral group. Both are compatible with transversal $X$, $Z$, and $F = HS^\dagger$, and the encoders with this property form a small linear space. We show that its codes of distance at least three are cut out by at most two quadratic equations, and that all of them have the same error geometry in the sense that their single-qubit error states have the same inner products. It follows that a switching unitary commuting with qubit permutations and with the shared transversal gates at every instant, and tolerating one single-qubit fault at an unknown time, exists if and only if the two codes are joined by a continuous path of distance-three codes. Such a switch can be made error transparent, so that a fault at any moment reaches the target code as the same single-qubit error. For real encoders, the distance-three codes are the solutions of a single quadratic equation, and on thirteen qubits, they form an explicit circle through two Clifford codes and an icosahedral code. We construct the switching unitary along this circle as a collective operator, leaving its synthesis into physical control open, and verify that its recovery infidelity per fault is at machine precision, whereas two natural alternatives leave average infidelities of $0.13$ and $0.85$. A survey over odd qubit numbers up to $29$ lists where such switches exist.

Frame-dependent coherence of Gaussian quantum states

Authors: Nicolae Cotfas
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.05623v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces a frame-dependent measure of coherence for Gaussian quantum states, moving beyond traditional basis-dependent definitions. It derives an explicit formula for this coherence using the covariance matrix for continuous variables and establishes a rigorous connection to the discrete variable case. This provides a more robust and general tool for quantifying quantum coherence in Gaussian systems.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, basis-independent tool (frame-dependent coherence) to characterize quantum states, which is crucial for understanding how coherence measures behave when the underlying mathematical description of the state space is generalized beyond standard orthonormal bases.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops an alternative method to quantify quantum state coherence for Gaussian states by utilizing a 'tight frame' description instead of standard orthonormal bases or the Hermite-Gauss basis.

Main result. An explicit, closed-form formula for the frame-dependent coherence of a mixed Gaussian state is derived, expressed simply in terms of the determinant and trace of the state's covariance matrix.

Method. The authors generalize the $\ell_1$-norm of coherence to tight frames, deriving specific formulas for both continuous-variable (CV) and discrete-variable (DV) Gaussian states, and analyzing the convergence between the two descriptions.

Model / system. The focus is on single-mode, pure or mixed, Gaussian quantum states, which are characterized mathematically by their covariance matrix ($\sigma$) in the continuous case, and by discrete Wigner functions in the discrete case.

Key observables. Frame-dependent coherence ($ ext{CF}( ho)$), Purity ($ ext{tr} ho^2$), Covariance matrix ($\sigma$).

Important parameters / regimes. The dimension of the Hilbert space ($d$), the determinant ($\det \sigma$), and the trace ($ ext{tr} \sigma$) of the covariance matrix.

Assumptions / limitations. The derivation relies on the mathematical framework of tight frames and the assumption that the discrete system converges to the continuous limit as the dimension increases.

Figures summary. The notes mention Figure 1 showing the discrete-variable Gaussian function and its Wigner function, and Table I comparing purity and coherence values between CV and DV Gaussian states across different dimensions.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the concept of frame-dependent coherence, then derives the general formula for CVGS using the covariance matrix, and finally extends this analysis to DVGS, demonstrating the convergence between the two descriptions.

Abstract

A Hilbert space is usually described using an orthonormal basis, but a more general description can be defined using a tight frame. In the case of continuous-variable Gaussian states, the coherence is usually described using the Hermite-Gauss basis. We present an alternative description using the tight frame of all canonical coherent states. The description of Gaussian states in terms of coherent states is simpler than in terms of Hermite-Gauss basis, and this allows us to obtain an explicit formula describing the frame-dependent coherence of a mixed Gaussian state as a function of the determinant and the trace of covariance matrix. For each mixed Gaussian state, we construct a sequence of discrete-variable quantum states rapidly convergent to it (as concern the purity and coherence) when the dimension of Hilbert space becomes large enough (15 or more). In the discrete case, the coherence is measured with respect to the tight frame of all discrete coherent states.

Free entropy and quantum minimum description length

Authors: Patrick Hayden, Alexander Maloney, Jinzhao Wang, Yuxiang Yang
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.06741v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper connects the abstract mathematical concept of free entropy to a concrete physical resource: the minimum quantum memory required to store many copies of a quantum state. By showing that the physical free entropy characterizes the optimal compression cost, it gives free entropy a tangible role in quantum information theory.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a powerful, non-standard entropy measure ($\chi_{phy}$) that quantifies fundamental resource limits (memory) in quantum information processing, potentially offering insights beyond standard von Neumann entropy.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper seeks to establish a physical meaning for free entropy, a non-commutative entropy measure, by relating it to the quantum minimum description length (QMDL) for state compression.

Main result. The optimal asymptotic memory cost for compressing $n$ copies of a state is shown to be directly proportional to the physical free entropy of the state.

Method. The authors use volume calculations in the space of matrices to define a finite-dimensional physical free entropy ($\chi_{phy}$), and then compare this quantity to the known asymptotic scaling of the memory cost in quantum data compression.

Model / system. The model involves quantum states ($ ho$) with known, non-degenerate spectra, analyzed in the context of unitary orbits ($ ho_U = U ho_0U^\dagger$). The task is quantum compression of $ ho^{\otimes n}_U$.

Key observables. Physical free entropy ($\chi_{phy}( ho; \epsilon)$) and the minimum memory cost ($ ext{|M}_n|$).

Important parameters / regimes. The number of copies ($n$), the dimension ($d$), and the spectral gaps between eigenvalues.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the state has a known, strictly positive, non-degenerate spectrum, and relies on asymptotic limits ($\epsilon o 0$ or $n o \infty$).

Figures summary. Figure 1 compares the structure of Schumacher compression (requiring purification) versus the current task (where only the state itself needs recovery).

Paper structure. The paper develops the physical free entropy via volume ratios in finite dimensions, derives the memory cost for the known-spectrum compression task, and establishes the proportionality relationship between the two quantities.

Abstract

In free probability, Voiculescu's free entropy counts matrix microstates that approximate an operator. It is a non-commutative analog of the Shannon entropy, distinct from any quantum entropy of a density operator, but it has had no physical meaning in quantum theory. We define a finite-dimensional physical free entropy at finite resolution and show that it directly characterizes, up to an explicit constant, the minimal quantum memory needed to program many copies of a state with known eigenvalues and unknown eigenbasis. This task is a variant of Schumacher compression, and we call its optimal cost the quantum minimum description length. We briefly discuss how free entropy differs from quantum entropies and its potential applications in quantum physics.

Gap Amplification for Local Hamiltonians with Combinatorial Soundness

Authors: Mitali Bafna, Quynh T. Nguyen, Tina Zhang
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.04952v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces a novel, viable template for quantum locality-preserving gap amplification, a critical missing piece for proving the quantum PCP conjecture. By showing how to amplify the gap of local Hamiltonians while controlling locality, the authors provide a major theoretical advance in quantum complexity theory. This work bridges classical PCP techniques into the quantum domain using advanced concepts from quantum coding and fault-tolerant computation.

Why it may be interesting. The development of locality-preserving gap amplification is a crucial theoretical tool for quantum complexity theory, providing a pathway to tackle the quantum PCP conjecture by importing a key classical ingredient.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of translating the classical PCP theorem into the quantum setting, specifically by developing a method for locality-preserving gap amplification.

Main result. The authors present the first viable template for quantum locality-preserving gap amplification, proving that this procedure successfully amplifies the combinatorial gap of local Hamiltonians.

Method. The technique combines ideas from classical PCP proofs using high-dimensional expanders and circuit-to-Hamiltonian constructions, focusing on distributing fault-tolerant 'violation information' rather than repeating the witness.

Model / system. The work is framed around local Hamiltonian problems, which are used to model quantum constraint satisfaction problems (CSPs) and are central to the quantum PCP conjecture.

Key observables. Combinatorial energy ($\epsilon$), ground energy, and the gap of the local Hamiltonian.

Important parameters / regimes. The gap regime (low-gap vs. high-gap) dictates the polynomial scaling of the required qudit dimension and log-alphabet size.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on developing novel techniques for fault-tolerant computation and assumes the existence of asymptotically good quantum locally testable codes (qLTCs) for certain bounds.

Paper structure. The paper builds conceptually by first establishing the necessity of locality-preserving gap amplification for the quantum PCP conjecture, then presenting the template using existing tools (HDX, circuit-to-Hamiltonian), and finally detailing the resulting combinatorial soundness proof.

Abstract

The quantum PCP conjecture is one of the major open problems in quantum complexity theory. It has resisted attack in part because many primitives used in the proof of the classical PCP theorem, such as locality-preserving gap amplification and alphabet reduction, have no obvious quantum analogues due to quantum no-cloning. Locality-preserving gap amplification is a procedure that takes as input a local Hamiltonian problem instance and produces a new instance with a larger promise gap, without increasing the locality of the Hamiltonian, and instead moderately increasing its local qudit dimension. Obtaining this kind of control over the locality during gap amplification is critical to the success of many known strategies for proving the classical PCP theorem. In this work, we put forth the first known viable template for quantum locality-preserving gap amplification, and we prove that our procedure amplifies the combinatorial gap of local Hamiltonians. Our work introduces a new framework for reasoning about quantum gap amplification in terms of fault-tolerant computation, and illuminates a route toward importing one of the central ingredients in classical PCPs into the quantum setting. In particular, we build upon ideas from the recent classical PCP of Bafna--Minzer--Vyas based on high-dimensional expanders, and the circuit-to-Hamiltonian construction of Anshu--Breuckmann--Nguyen, in addition to several recent advances in quantum coding theory.

Generalized Dunkl Quantum Systems with Energy-Dependent Interactions: Exact Solvability and Thermodynamic Properties

Authors: B. Hamil, R. Boudjouraf, A. Benchikha, M. Merad
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.04322v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work develops a theoretical framework for generalized Dunkl quantum systems featuring energy-dependent interactions. It successfully derives the energy spectrum and analyzes the system's thermodynamic properties, showing that the deformation and energy-dependence parameters offer tunable control over both quantum and thermal characteristics.

Why it may be interesting. The combination of exactly solvable models (Dunkl systems) with non-standard interactions (energy-dependent potentials) provides a rich theoretical playground for studying how complex physical effects manifest in both quantum spectra and macroscopic thermal behavior.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the exact solvability and thermodynamic properties of quantum systems incorporating generalized Dunkl operators and energy-dependent interactions.

Main result. The study derives explicit analytical expressions for the energy spectrum, partition function, internal energy, and entropy, demonstrating that the generalized framework allows effective control over spectral and thermal characteristics.

Method. The analysis combines solving the generalized Dunkl Schrödinger equation for stationary states with applying the Euler-Maclaurin summation formula to calculate the canonical partition function.

Model / system. The model is a one-dimensional quantum system governed by a generalized Dunkl-Schrödinger equation, featuring an energy-dependent potential and incorporating parameters like the Wigner ($ u$) and deformation ($\gamma$) parameters.

Key observables. Energy spectrum ($E_{s,n}$), probability density ($ ho$), probability current ($J$), internal energy ($U$), entropy ($S$), and specific heat ($C_K$).

Important parameters / regimes. Wigner parameter ($ u$), deformation parameter ($\gamma$), and energy-dependence parameter ($\lambda$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis requires the condition $|\gamma| < 1$ for a real spectrum, and the thermodynamic calculations rely on the Euler-Maclaurin approximation up to $p=2$.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the variation of the energy spectrum with quantum number $n$ for varying $ u$, $\gamma$, and $\lambda$. Other figures show the influence of $ u$, $\gamma$, and $\lambda$ on $S$, $U$, and $C_K$ respectively.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the generalized Dunkl formalism and derives conservation laws. It then solves the energy spectrum for the energy-dependent harmonic oscillator. Finally, it calculates the thermodynamic properties using the partition function and analyzes the results based on the key parameters.

Abstract

This work investigates a class of exactly solvable quantum systems described by a generalized Dunkl--Schrödinger equation with energy-dependent interactions. A two-parameter extension of the Dunkl differential operator is introduced, incorporating both the conventional reflection contribution and an additional derivative-reflection coupling. The resulting formalism leads to a modified Heisenberg algebra, from which the continuity equation, conserved probability current, and generalized normalization condition are derived for energy-dependent potentials. As an application, an energy-dependent harmonic oscillator is studied in detail. Exact analytical expressions for the eigenfunctions and energy spectrum are obtained in terms of associated Laguerre polynomials. The combined effects of the Wigner parameter, the deformation parameter, and the energy-dependence parameter are shown to produce parity splitting, nonlinear energy spectra, and nonuniform level spacing while preserving exact solvability. The thermodynamic properties of the model are then examined through the canonical partition function using the Euler--Maclaurin summation formula. Analytical expressions for the partition function, internal energy, entropy, and specific heat are derived and analyzed numerically. The results demonstrate that the generalized Dunkl deformation and energy-dependent interaction provide effective control over both the spectral and thermal characteristics of the system, extending the class of exactly solvable quantum models with reflection symmetry.

Giant Conductance Enhancement in Graphene Nanoscrolls via Interlayer-Coupled States

Authors: Jia-Cheng Li, Ying-Je Lee, Yu An Cheng, Yu-Hsiang Fu, Xuan-Fu Huang, Yu-Jie Zhong, Ion Cosma Fulga, Carmine Ortix, Ching-Hao Chang

Summary. This theoretical work demonstrates that rolling graphene nanoribbons into few-turn nanoscrolls dramatically enhances their electrical conductance by nearly an order of magnitude. This effect is fundamentally linked to interlayer hopping, which reconstructs localized edge states into highly conductive one-dimensional channels. The research establishes the nanoscroll's turn number as a tunable knob for engineering quantum electronic states.

Why it may be interesting. The mechanism—where geometric curvature induces a topological transition that enhances quantum conductance—is highly relevant to understanding engineered quantum transport in low-dimensional carbon materials, which shares principles with quantum confinement in nanoscale devices.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the mechanism behind a giant enhancement in the longitudinal conductance of graphene nanoscrolls (GNSs) compared to flat graphene ribbons.

Main result. Rolling the graphene nanoribbon into a few-turn nanoscroll (1.0-1.2 turns) triggers a giant, geometry-driven conductance enhancement, which scales inversely with the number of turns.

Method. The study employs a tight-binding approach, supplemented by continuum models and comparisons with DFT calculations, using the Landauer formula to calculate quantum conductance.

Model / system. The physical system is a graphene nanoscroll, modeled as a radial superlattice formed by rolling a graphene nanoribbon. The Hamiltonian includes both intralayer and interlayer hopping terms, with the interlayer coupling being key to the observed electronic reconstruction.

Key observables. Longitudinal conductance (showing up to 1700% enhancement), Fermi energy shift, and the number of active conduction channels.

Important parameters / regimes. The nanoscroll turn number (1.0-1.2 turns), the interlayer hopping parameter (gamma_1), and the temperature scale relative to the subband spacing (k_B T vs Delta E).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes a perfectly ballistic transport regime and relies on the validity of the tight-binding and continuum models for describing the interlayer coupling.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the unit cell structure and band structures for different turn numbers, showing the evolution of the Fermi level and carrier density distribution as the ribbon rolls into a scroll.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by establishing the physical model (GNS geometry), deriving the Hamiltonian incorporating interlayer coupling, calculating the conductance using Landauer formalism, and finally analyzing how the turn number dictates the electronic structure and resulting transport properties.

Abstract

Graphene nanoscrolls form a unique radial superlattice whose geometry enables transport behaviors distinct from their flat counterparts. We theoretically demonstrate that rolling a graphene nanoribbon into a few-turn nanoscroll (1.0-1.2 turns) triggers a giant enhancement in longitudinal conductance by an order of magnitude (nearly twentyfold). We show that this effect stems from interlayer hopping between open boundaries, which not only transforms localized edge states into one-dimensional-like conducting channels and also induces a significant Fermi energy shift. Our results reveal an inverse scaling of conductance with turn number, establishing GNSs as a platform for geometry-driven quantum transport and offering a tunable approach to engineering nanoarchitectural electronic states.

Improved bounds on stabilizer extent and Clifford rank

Authors: Pulkit Sinha, Benjamin Lovitz
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.06819v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical paper establishes significantly improved quantitative bounds connecting the stabilizer rank of quantum states and operators to their respective extents and Clifford ranks. By proving these bounds, the authors derive new lower bounds for the complexity of quantum states, such as tensor powers, and provide tighter characterizations of pseudorandom quantum ensembles. This work advances the understanding of resource quantification in quantum information theory.

Why it may be interesting. The derived bounds provide concrete, improved quantitative measures of quantum state complexity, which are crucial for understanding the limits of quantum computation and simulation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to establish improved quantitative bounds relating the stabilizer rank ($\chi$) of quantum states and operators to their respective 'extent' ($\xi$) and Clifford rank ($\chi_C$).

Main result. It proves that for a state of stabilizer rank $k$, its extent is bounded by $2^{O(\sqrt{k\log(k+1)})}$, and derives lower bounds for the approximate stabilizer rank of tensor powers of non-stabilizer states.

Method. The authors use techniques involving decomposing states into stabilizer components, applying randomized projections, and relating operator bounds to state bounds via the Choi map formalism.

Model / system. The focus is on quantum states and operators acting on $n$ qubits, specifically analyzing the structure and complexity of stabilizer states and general non-stabilizer states.

Key observables. Stabilizer Rank ($\chi$), Stabilizer Extent ($\xi$), Clifford Rank ($\chi_C$), and Stabilizer Fidelity ($F_S$).

Important parameters / regimes. The rank $k$ (the maximum allowed stabilizer rank) and the number of qubits $n$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the ability to decompose states into sums of stabilizer states and the use of specific projection techniques to control the coefficients.

Paper structure. The paper builds from establishing the core quantitative bounds (Theorem 1.1 and 1.2) relating rank to extent/norm, which are then leveraged to prove lower bounds for state complexity (tensor powers, AND function) and characterize pseudorandomness.

Abstract

We prove that every pure state of stabilizer rank at most $k$ has stabilizer extent at most $2^{O(\sqrt{k\log(k+1)})}$, and establish the analogous bound for the squared Clifford coefficient norm of Clifford-rank-$k$ operators. This implies stabilizer fidelity at least $2^{-O(\sqrt{k\log(k+1)})}$, resolving the quantitative conjecture of (Mehraban-Tamasbi, STOC, 2025), and proves an $Ω(n^2/\log n)$ lower bound for the approximate stabilizer rank of tensor powers of any non-stabilizer qubit state. The latter result generalizes the best-known lower bound for tensor powers of $T$-states (Mehraban-Tamasbi, STOC, 2024) to arbitrary non-stabilizer qubit states, including magic states. As a consequence of the Clifford rank--norm inequality, we obtain an $Ω(n^2/\log n)$ lower bound for exact representations of $n$-bit AND function by quadratic phases, improving the previous best-known linear bound. Further consequences rule out pseudorandom state and unitary ensembles with approximate stabilizer and Clifford rank $O((\log n)^2/\log\log n)$, respectively, a $\log n$ improvement over prior work (Kalra-Sinha, Quantum, 2026). We also obtain tomography algorithms for states of stabilizer rank at most $k$, with $poly(n)2^{O(\sqrt{k\log(k+1)})}$ time and copy complexity, a nearly square-root improvement in the exponent over the best-known algorithm.

Indistinguishability Lifting for Keyed Oracles, Compressed Ideal Cipher, and More Applications

Authors: Ritam Bhaumik, Yu-Hsuan Huang
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.04674v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops a general theorem to lift quantum indistinguishability proofs from base oracles to complex keyed oracles, overcoming limitations in prior work regarding statefulness. It applies this theorem to construct a quantum-secure compressed ideal cipher and to extend the message length of strong pseudorandom permutations. The work advances the theoretical understanding of quantum cryptographic security proofs.

Why it may be interesting. While highly theoretical and cryptographic, the techniques used—such as polynomial methods and bounding quantum advantage—share mathematical rigor with quantum information theory, particularly in complexity analysis.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of proving quantum indistinguishability for keyed oracles that are composed of many independent instances of a base oracle, especially when the base oracles are stateful.

Main result. They establish a generic indistinguishability lifting theorem showing that if base oracles are indistinguishable, the keyed oracles are too, up to an $O(q^2)$ loss. This is applied to create an efficiently implementable compressed ideal cipher.

Method. The core method is the development of the lifting theorem, which transforms the problem of distinguishing keyed oracles into distinguishing the underlying base oracles. Applications involve modular constructions and polynomial-method arguments.

Model / system. The model is purely cryptographic, focusing on quantum query complexity, ideal cipher models, and compressed permutation oracles. It does not describe a physical Hamiltonian or quantum system.

Key observables. Distinguishing advantage ($\epsilon$), number of queries ($q$), and the security loss factor ($O(q^2)$).

Important parameters / regimes. The number of queries ($q$) and the message space size ($n$) are key parameters determining the security bounds.

Assumptions / limitations. The theorem assumes the existence of a base oracle whose indistinguishability can be established, and it applies to both statistical and computational settings.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the domain extension of a PRP from $n$-bit to $2n$-bit messages. Figure 3 illustrates the structure of the oracle $O_b$ used for lifting hardness, detailing 'read' and 'challenge' modes.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the lifting theorem, proves its optimality, and then presents two major applications: first, constructing a compressed ideal cipher; and second, providing a modular construction to double the message length of quantum-secure strong pseudorandom permutations.

Abstract

Cryptographic security proofs often involve an adversary interacting with a larger, keyed oracle that consists of (potentially exponentially) many independent instances of a smaller, base oracle. However, showing quantum indistinguishability between two such keyed oracles can be tricky, since a single query made by an adversary may involve a superposition that covers all instances of the base oracles simultaneously. In this paper, we establish a generic indistinguishability lifting theorem of the following form: if the two base oracles are indistinguishable under quantum queries, then their corresponding keyed oracles are too, up to an O(q^2) multiplicative loss in distinguishing advantage, where q is the number of queries made by the adversary. Our lifting theorem applies to both statistical and computational settings, and to oracles that are stateful as well. It is also optimal in that it matches the obvious Grover search attack for a certain (contrived) choice of oracles. As an immediate application, we extend Carolan's compressed permutation oracle to an efficiently implementable compressed ideal cipher, and use it to prove preimage resistance of the Davies-Meyer compression function in the quantum ideal cipher model. Thanks to our lifting theorem, the soundness of our compressed ideal cipher reduces to that of Carolan's oracle, and any further improvement on the latter would automatically carry over to the former. As our second application, we give a modular construction that doubles the message length of any quantum-secure strong pseudorandom permutation. Along the way, we show that an existing two-round tweakable Feistel construction is indistinguishable from a random permutation under quantum bidirectional queries. This is done via a dedicated polynomial-method argument, which may be of independent interest.

Information causality does not single out the set of quantum correlations

Authors: Marco Pompili, Mirjam Weilenmann
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.06604v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper rigorously proves that Information Causality (IC), a proposed physical constraint on non-local correlations, is not strong enough to fully characterize quantum correlations. By constructing explicit counterexamples in Bell scenarios, the authors demonstrate that correlations satisfying IC can exist that are fundamentally non-quantum. This has significant implications for understanding the limits of information-theoretic characterizations of quantum mechanics.

Why it may be interesting. This work challenges fundamental assumptions about the physical constraints governing non-local correlations, which is central to quantum information theory and device-independent quantum cryptography.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates whether the physical principle of Information Causality (IC) is sufficient to uniquely characterize the set of quantum correlations in Bell scenarios.

Main result. The authors prove that IC does not single out the set of quantum correlations by constructing specific correlation sets that satisfy IC but are demonstrably outside the set of quantum correlations.

Method. The analysis uses information-theoretic tools, specifically entropic constraints (Shannon mutual information) applied to generalized probabilistic theories (GPTs) in the context of multi-copy Bell tests.

Model / system. The study focuses on bipartite correlations in a Bell scenario, parameterized by the number of inputs ($m$) and outputs ($d$), and extends to multi-copy protocols ($n$).

Key observables. Information Causality constraints ($IC(m,d)_n$), quantum correlations ($\mathcal{Q}(m,d)$), and various information quantities like mutual information and conditional entropy.

Important parameters / regimes. The database size $n$ (number of inputs/copies) and the number of outcomes $d$ are key parameters in the Bell test setup.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is often restricted to the $(m, m, d, d)$ case for simplicity, and the results are based on constructing specific counter-example correlation sets.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the notation for $n$-dit random-access codes and show specific examples of correlation sets ($P^*$) that satisfy IC but lie outside the quantum set $\mathcal{Q}$.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first defining the IC constraint, then presenting results showing that finite-length and single-copy IC are insufficient, culminating in the construction of a general counterexample in the 3-input, 2-outcome setting.

Abstract

Information causality (IC) was introduced as a physical principle that imposes constraints on the non-local correlations between two parties (Alice and Bob) in a Bell scenario. Specifically, it states that the information that Bob can gain about an $n$-bit database held by Alice is bounded by the information she communicates to him. To date, the question whether IC for any $n \in \mathbb{N}$ singles out the set of quantum correlations remains open, meaning that it is unknown whether for any number of inputs $m$ and outputs $d$ of the two parties the set of correlations that satisfy IC exactly coincides with the quantum correlations. In this work, we answer this question in the negative. Firstly, we show that for any finite database length $n \geq 2$, there is a bipartite set of correlations that satisfies IC for all compatible protocols and that exhibits post-quantum correlations in the Bell scenario with $n+1$ inputs and $2$ outcomes per party. This result relies on constructing an entropic proof of IC for GPTs that may be of independent interest. We further show that in the Bell scenario with $n+1$ inputs and $2$ outcomes per party, our constructed sets of correlations lead to neither a superset nor a subset of the quantum correlations. Secondly, we construct a set of correlations in the Bell scenario with $3$ inputs and $2$ outputs per party that satisfies IC (for all database sizes $n \in \mathbb{N}$) and that includes post-quantum points. Consequently, all known formulations of information causality fail to fully characterize the bipartite set of quantum correlations, already in the $3$-input, $2$-outcome Bell scenario.

For $d = 2$, Alice's message is a stabilizer state and Bob's measurement is Clifford, so the protocol uses no magic, yet achieves an optimal quadratic quantum advantage. Finally, we discuss how these separations can be adapted to near-term quantum advantage experiments in which the demonstrated advantage is both unconditional and efficiently verifiable.

Abstract

Variational quantum algorithms turn choices of Hamiltonian, ansatz, and parameterization into a classical nonconvex optimization problem. We study how this objective function can be visualized and characterized in ways that help explain optimizer behavior. We distinguish two properties of the objective: the number of local minima encountered along sampled directions and the differences in quality among local-search endpoints. We then ask how a local optimizer, represented by BFGS, compares with adaptive differential evolution, represented by jSO. Rather than comparing jSO with a single local run, we allow BFGS multiple starts within the same function-evaluation budget. This gives local search repeated opportunities to explore different basins and provides a stronger baseline for asking when a global evolutionary solver is useful. We study these questions using VQE and QAOA, focusing on how frustra- tion, circuit depth, parameter tying, mixed locality, and nonlinear repa- rameterization change the Hamiltonian expectation-value objective seen by the classical optimizer. Increasing independent circuit depth raises the sampled local-minimum count without making global search more effective. Parameter tying, by contrast, produces both more repeated local structure and much larger differences in quality among local-search endpoints, and in this regime adaptive differential evolution outperforms function-evaluation-matched multistart BFGS. The comparison shows that the number of local minima alone does not determine whether global search is advantageous: the important distinction is whether different basins lead to similarly good solutions or to substantially different objective values. These results provide a practical workflow for connecting model construction, objective- function geometry, empirical diagnostics, and optimizer choice.
