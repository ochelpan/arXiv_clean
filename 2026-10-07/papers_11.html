Polynomial Separation between One-Way and Two-Way LOCC in Discrimination of Maximally Entangled States

Authors: Youngrong Lim
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.04288v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper rigorously proves a polynomial separation between the distinguishability of orthogonal maximally entangled states using one-way versus two-way LOCC. By designing a feedback protocol ($A o B o A$), the authors show that two-way communication allows distinguishing exponentially more states than one-way communication. This establishes a key resource advantage of classical feedback in quantum information processing.

Why it may be interesting. The quantitative separation between one-way and two-way communication protocols in quantum state discrimination is a fundamental result in quantum communication theory, providing concrete bounds on the power of classical feedback.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper establishes a quantitative separation between the distinguishability of orthogonal maximally entangled states (MESs) using one-way versus two-way Local Operations and Classical Communication (LOCC).

Main result. Two-way LOCC allows for the perfect discrimination of a set size that grows polynomially with the local dimension $d$, contrasting sharply with the constant bound achievable with one-way LOCC.

Method. The authors develop protocols involving sequential quantum measurements and classical feedback ($A o B o A$) to bound the number of distinguishable states, relying on advanced techniques from quantum information theory.

Model / system. The system under study consists of sets of orthogonal maximally entangled states in the tensor product space $\mathbb{C}^d \otimes \mathbb{C}^d$. The analysis compares the limitations imposed by one-way versus two-way communication channels.

Key observables. The number of pairwise orthogonal MESs ($k$) that can be perfectly distinguished, quantified by $m_{ o}(d)$ and $m_{\leftrightarrow}(d)$.

Important parameters / regimes. Local dimension $d$, the number of states $k$, and the communication structure (one-way vs. two-way LOCC).

Assumptions / limitations. The results concern the perfect distinguishability of every set of orthogonal MESs, and the analysis provides sufficient conditions and necessary lower bounds.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the two-way discrimination protocol for $k=4$, detailing the sequence of quantum systems and classical messages ($A o B o A$). Table I summarizes the worst-case thresholds for different communication classes.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the limitations of one-way LOCC (constant distinguishability) and then constructing a protocol using two-way LOCC that achieves a polynomial lower bound on the distinguishable set size, culminating in a universal copy bound.

Abstract

One-way local operations and classical communication (LOCC) can fail to distinguish four orthogonal maximally entangled states in every local dimension $d\ge4$. We show that every set of $k$ pairwise orthogonal maximally entangled states in $\mathbb C^d\otimes\mathbb C^d$ is perfectly distinguishable by an $A\to B\to A$ protocol whenever $d\ge Ck^4[\log(2k)]^ν$, for universal constants $C,ν>0$. A single feedback message therefore raises the number of candidates that can always be distinguished from a constant to $Ω\!\left(d^{1/4}/(\log d)^{ν/4}\right)$, establishing a polynomial separation between one-way and two-way LOCC. In our protocol, Alice preserves exact orthogonality while reducing overlaps between Bob's conditional subspaces. Bob reduces the candidates to a singleton or an orthogonal pair and refines his outcome so that Alice can complete the discrimination. As a corollary, nine copies suffice for perfect two-way LOCC discrimination of any orthogonal maximally entangled set in all sufficiently large local dimensions.

Protecting Quantum Computers against Untrusted Users

Authors: Shiv Akshar Yadavalli, Joel Rajakumar, Alexander Schuckert, Michael J. Gullans
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.06812v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

Summary. This paper proposes a restricted quantum computation model, $ ext{BQP}_{1}$, to safely expose quantum computing power to the public. It argues that this model prevents factoring RSA moduli by limiting the output to a single bit, thereby restricting the computational power available to classical attackers. The security proof is highly technical, resting on both number theory hardness and deep conjectures about circuit complexity.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on cryptography, the rigorous comparison between computational models ($ ext{BQP}_{1}$ vs $ ext{DQC}_1$) and the reliance on circuit complexity barriers ($ ext{P}$ vs $ ext{NC}^1$) are fundamental theoretical tools relevant to understanding the limits of quantum simulation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the critical need for a secure interface to public quantum computers that allows scientific utility while preventing the use of the machine to break public-key cryptography like RSA factoring.

Main result. It proposes the restricted model $ ext{BQP}_{1}$, showing that security relies on both classical hardness for factoring and conjectured circuit lowerbounds for rational reconstruction.

Method. The security analysis proceeds in two steps: demonstrating that one-bit readout makes standard factoring quantum stages classically simulable, and then showing that random inputs obstruct known workarounds like coherent rational reconstruction.

Model / system. The core model is the restricted computation $ ext{BQP}_{1}$, where a quantum server provides random computational-basis inputs and returns only a single designated output bit. This is compared against the one-clean-qubit model ($ ext{DQC}_1$) and the full $ ext{BQP}$ model.

Key observables. The primary observable is the ability of a classical client to efficiently factor RSA moduli using polynomially many adaptive requests.

Important parameters / regimes. The security is contingent on the system size ($n$) and the computational complexity class ($ ext{P}$ vs $ ext{NC}^1$).

Assumptions / limitations. The security proof relies on the conjecture that the proposed restrictions are sufficient, and specifically on the conjectured circuit lowerbounds for rational reconstruction.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the $ ext{BQP}_{1}$ interface, contrasting it with $ ext{DQC}_1$ and $ ext{BQP}$ by detailing the input randomization and output measurement restrictions.

Paper structure. The paper develops the $ ext{BQP}_{1}$ model, analyzes its relationship to $ ext{DQC}_1$, and builds the security argument by first showing classical simulability of factoring stages via one-bit readout, and second, by analyzing the failure of rational reconstruction under random inputs.

Abstract

Publicly accessible fault-tolerant quantum computers must preserve scientific utility while limiting cryptanalytic power. We propose the restricted model of computation, 1/2BQP_1: a quantum server provides random computational-basis inputs, revealed only after execution, and one designated output bit. This interface permits arbitrary circuits and system sizes. We conjecture that a classical client making polynomially many adaptive requests cannot efficiently factor RSA moduli. The model subsumes the well-known one-clean-qubit model DQC1, retaining its applications to infinite-temperature multi-time correlations, out-of-time-order correlators, and suitably normalized partition functions. We propose a candidate for separating 1/2BQP_1 from DQC1 based on testing classical predictions of quantum spin dynamics. We argue security in two steps. First, we show that one-bit readout makes the quantum stages of standard factoring algorithms classically simulable, even when their preparation and postprocessing are redesigned within a broad family around a single arithmetic-oracle call. This covers the quantum stages of Shor, Ekera-Hastad, Kitaev, and Regev factoring constructions. Second, we examine the workaround of coherently implementing rational reconstruction to release a factor bit. Random inputs obstruct this straightforward attack: known techniques accommodate logarithmic-depth classical circuits, but rational reconstruction has resisted such parallelization for decades. Therefore, we show that security of our protocol is built on not only classical hardness for factoring, but also conjectured circuit lowerbounds for rational reconstruction.

Quantum Complexity of Solving Linear Equations on Higher-Order Networks

Summary. This paper analyzes the quantum computational difficulty of solving linear systems arising from Hodge Laplacians on higher-order networks. By reducing an arbitrary quantum computation to this linear system problem, the authors prove that solving it is BQP-hard. This result establishes a fundamental, worst-case quantum complexity benchmark for analyzing quantum algorithms in topological physics and network science.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a rigorous, worst-case quantum complexity benchmark for analyzing quantum algorithms applied to topological or network-based physical models, which is crucial for assessing potential quantum advantage in condensed matter simulations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Determining the quantum complexity of solving linear equations derived from Hodge Laplacian systems on Higher-Order Networks (HONs).

Main result. The problem of preparing the quantum state encoding the normalized minimum-norm solution is proven to be BQP-hard, establishing a worst-case complexity foundation for quantum advantage in HON analysis.

Method. The authors use quantum complexity reduction techniques, mapping an arbitrary polynomial-time quantum computation into the task of solving the specific linear systems.

Model / system. The model involves Higher-Order Networks (HONs) analyzed via simplicial complexes, leading to linear systems governed by Hodge Laplacians ($\Delta^k$).

Key observables. The normalized minimum-norm least-squares solution of the Hodge Laplacian linear system.

Important parameters / regimes. Sparsity bounds, condition number ($\kappa$), and error tolerance ($\epsilon$).

Assumptions / limitations. The hardness results rely on the sparse-oracle model and specific parameter promises, and the complexity is worst-case.

Figures summary. Not specified (The notes mention Table 1 comparing this work to previous quantum hardness results).

Paper structure. The paper establishes the mathematical framework using Hodge Laplacians, develops the quantum reduction from general quantum circuits to the linear system problem, and proves BQP-hardness and BQP-completeness for the decision problem.

Abstract

Higher-order networks (HONs) represent interactions among groups of objects, supporting the study of collective behaviour that pairwise models can miss. In simplicial models of these networks, Hodge Laplacian linear systems provide a common mathematical framework for analysis, for example, for solving statistical ranking problems and investigating long-term stability in coupled oscillator systems. The large number of variables associated with edges, triangles, and higher-dimensional simplices can make these systems costly to solve. Recent quantum algorithms address this bottleneck. However, comparisons with particular classical algorithms do not decision output whether solving these linear system problems establishes a quantum complexity foundation for provable quantum advantage. We prove that preparing a quantum state encoding the normalized minimum-norm solution of Hodge Laplacian linear systems is $\mathsf{BQP}$-hard under the specified sparse oracle model and parameter promises. Our sequence of reductions maps an arbitrary polynomial-time bounded-error quantum computation to such linear systems. Each reduction allows efficient recovery of the normalized minimum-norm solution of the preceding linear system. Together with an efficient quantum algorithm for the associated decision problem from the approximate solution state, we show that this problem is $\mathsf{BQP}$-complete. These results provide a worst-case complexity foundation for evaluating quantum advantage in HON analysis and show that the $\mathsf{BQP}$-hardness of the quantum linear system problem (QLSP) persists even when the matrices are restricted to Hodge Laplacians.

Quantum Fluctuations in Bose--Einstein Condensates:Microscopic Correlations and Moment Bounds

Authors: Mauris Chueng, Keheng Zhu, Hexiang Wang
Type: theory · Category: quantum gases · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.06367v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper provides a rigorous mathematical analysis of quantum fluctuations in Bose-Einstein Condensates operating in the Gross-Pitaevskii regime. It tracks the evolution of higher moments of energy and fluctuations, proving that certain moments remain bounded independently of the particle number N. The results are significant for understanding the quantitative limits of mean-field approximations in interacting quantum gases.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides deep mathematical rigor to the study of quantum fluctuations in BECs, offering precise bounds and convergence results that are crucial for understanding the transition from mean-field descriptions to fully quantum regimes.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the behavior of higher moments of quantum fluctuations and the microscopic correlation energy within the context of the three-dimensional Gross-Pitaevskii regime.

Main result. It establishes that initial bounds on the third moment of correlated energy control the third moment of depletion and sixth moment of fluctuations independently of particle number N. Furthermore, it identifies the excitation-energy measure at scale N^2 for interacting systems.

Method. The analysis employs rigorous mathematical techniques, including propagating spectral moments along the exact many-body evolution in Fock space and deriving detailed error estimates for approximations.

Model / system. The system modeled is a Bose-Einstein Condensate (BEC) governed by a Hamiltonian related to the Gross-Pitaevskii equation, involving a smooth, compactly supported, nonnegative radial interaction potential v.

Key observables. Third spectral moment of correlated energy, third moment of depletion number, sixth moment of collective one-particle fluctuations, and the excitation-energy measure.

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis is highly dependent on the particle number N, with results scaling with powers of N (e.g., N^2, N^{2p-2}).

Assumptions / limitations. Assumes the interaction potential v is smooth, compactly supported, nonnegative, and radial, and that the initial state has specific uniform bounds on its correlated energy moment.

Paper structure. The paper follows a highly technical structure, progressing from setting up the Hamiltonian and initial conditions to analyzing correlation transformations, deriving energy estimates, and finally determining the behavior of higher moments and spectral measures.

Abstract

We study higher moments of quantum fluctuations and microscopic correlation energy in the three-dimensional Gross--Pitaevskii regime. For a smooth, compactly supported, nonnegative radial interaction and an $N$-independent normalized initial condensate wave function in $H^6$, we propagate the third spectral moment of a positive correlated energy along the exact many-body evolution. A uniform initial bound controls the third moment of the depletion number and sixth moments of collective one-particle fluctuations on every compact time interval, independently of $N$. For a nonzero interaction and a smooth, compactly supported initial condensate, we also identify the excitation-energy measure at scale $N^2$, weighted by the first energy. This limit holds for norm-convergent pure-state families with a uniform bound on the initial third correlated-energy moment. Its positive-energy part is a spectral measure of the relative scattering operator $-2Δ+v$, multiplied by a time-dependent dressed contact. We compute the radial density and prove strict positivity of the contact for nonzero references with finite kinetic energy and finite mean excitation number. The same evolving states have bounded physical fluctuation moments and excitation-energy moments of order $p>1$ bounded below by a positive multiple of $N^{2p-2}$, in the extended spectral sense. Inverse powers of the positive initial energy construct admissible pure and mixed families. For the norm-convergent pure-state class, we determine the scaled interaction-energy moments for $1\le p<2$ and bound the second moment by $O(N^2)$. A finite cubic approximation has norm error $O(N^{-3/16})$ on the energy form domain and $O(N^{-5/4})$ on a stronger initial domain of its generator.

Quantum Krylov Linear Solving Beyond Global Conditioning via Spectral Compression and Directional Stability

Authors: Sijia Yu, Yifan Zhou
Type: theory · Category: numerical methods · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.06620v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

Summary. This theoretical paper analyzes Quantum Krylov Linear Solving (QKS) for solving quantum linear systems. It demonstrates that the computational difficulty, measured by normalized-state complexity, is not dictated by the system's global condition number when a small, solution-relevant eigenvalue is present. The authors establish that the QKS method maintains stability and bounded resources even when the full system exhibits extreme spectral gaps.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides rigorous theoretical bounds for quantum algorithms solving linear systems, directly impacting the feasibility assessment of quantum simulation techniques used in AMO physics and quantum optics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the overestimation of difficulty in Quantum Krylov Linear Solving (QKS) due to global condition number dependence, especially when the ill-conditioning stems from a solution-relevant, vanishing eigenvalue.

Main result. It proves that the normalized-state complexity of QKS does not need to inherit the divergence of the global condition number, even when the system is severely ill-conditioned.

Method. The authors analyze the QKS process by projecting the dynamics onto a reduced, stable subspace, comparing the required resources to the full system's spectral properties.

Model / system. The model is solving a quantum linear system $Ax = |b angle$ where $A$ is a Hermitian positive-definite operator. The solution is approximated using time-evolution states within the Krylov subspace.

Key observables. Normalized-state complexity, spectral compression stability, and the comparison between full inverse amplification ($\chi_{ ext{filt}}$) and QKS stability metrics ($K_B, K_c$).

Important parameters / regimes. The small, retained eigenvalue ($\epsilon o 0$), the global condition number ($\kappa(A) \sim \epsilon^{-1}$), and the Krylov subspace dimension ($m$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the existence of a solution-relevant, compact spectral representation and relies on bounding the leakage of inactive eigenvalues into the reduced subspace.

Figures summary. The notes describe the construction of explicit separation families and the analysis of embedded systems to show the divergence of full inverse amplification while QKS parameters remain bounded.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the QKS framework, then developing the core theoretical results showing bounded complexity (Theorem 1), followed by detailed analyses using spectral compression and constructing counterexamples to separate full inverse amplification from normalized-state difficulty.

Abstract

Global condition-number dependence can substantially overestimate the difficulty of preparing normalized quantum linear-system solutions. Many existing beyond-conditioning approaches are favorable when globally ill-conditioned directions have limited relevance to the target solution. Here we study a harder regime for time-evolution quantum Krylov linear solving (QKS), where a vanishing eigenvalue remains solution-relevant and must be retained. We identify a QKS regime in which the solution-relevant spectrum is compressed into a compact reduced space where the ill-conditioning is concentrated along a soft direction aligned with the solution, so that normalization removes the divergent amplification along that direction and leaves only a bounded transverse response. Consequently, the normalized-state complexity need not inherit the divergence of the global condition number. Our contributions are threefold: (1) We show that the solution-relevant information admits a compact and stable QKS representation, with bounded subspace dimension, evolution time, and reconstruction overhead as the relevant eigenvalue vanishes. (2) We show that normalized-state difficulty is governed by whether inverse amplification changes the solution direction, rather than by the small eigenvalue alone; even an arbitrarily ill-conditioned reduced system can remain directionally stable when the amplification is solution-aligned. (3) We prove that this stability persists under finite projected-system errors and transfers to the actual QKS output, yielding an end-to-end complexity bound without inverse dependence on the retained small eigenvalue. An explicit separation family further shows that a full inverse-amplification criterion can diverge while the corresponding QKS quantities remain bounded.

Quantum n-coloring is undecidable for every n $\ge$ 3

Summary. This paper proves that determining whether a graph can be quantum n-colored is an undecidable problem for any number of colors n greater than or equal to 3. The authors achieve this by constructing a specific related graph G' and showing that the quantum 3-coloring problem can be reduced to the quantum n-coloring problem via this construction. This result significantly expands the known boundaries of decidability in quantum graph theory.

Why it may be interesting. While the topic is graph theory/computability, the underlying framework involves quantum constraints (projections, orthogonality) which touches upon quantum information theory, making it relevant to theoretical quantum computation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the decidability of the quantum n-coloring problem for an arbitrary graph G, which asks if a perfect quantum strategy exists for the n-coloring game.

Main result. The authors prove that for every fixed integer n greater than or equal to 3, the quantum n-coloring decision problem is undecidable.

Method. They establish a reduction from the known undecidable case of quantum 3-coloring to the general case using a specific graph construction: G' = (G <0xE2><0xA8><0x81> K_3) <0xE2><0xA8><0x81> K_{n-3}.

Model / system. The model is the quantum n-coloring problem, defined algebraically using projections $\{P_{u,i}\}$ on the state space $\mathbb{C}^d imes d$ that must satisfy completeness and orthogonality conditions.

Key observables. The existence of a perfect quantum strategy for the n-coloring game.

Important parameters / regimes. The number of colors, n (specifically n >= 3), and the structure of the graph G.

Assumptions / limitations. The proof relies on the algebraic properties of graph products (Cartesian product $\square$ and join $\vee$) to construct the reduction.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the background on quantum coloring, notes the known undecidability for n=3, and then proceeds by defining the graph $G'$ and proving the key equivalence (Lemma 3.1) that allows the reduction from the n=3 case to the general n case, culminating in the main theorem.

Abstract

The quantum $n$-coloring problem, given graph $G$, asks whether there exists a perfect quantum strategy for the $n$-coloring game of $G$. Previously it was known that quantum $n$-coloring is undecidable only for $n=3$. We extend this to all $n\geq 3$ with one elementary reduction. Letting $G'=(G\square K_3)\lor K_{n-3}$, we show that $G$ is quantum $3$-colorable if and only if $G'$ is quantum $n$-colorable.

Summary. This paper rigorously analyzes Quantum-State Oblivious Transfer over the quantum erasure channel, providing security guarantees for both honest and malicious adversaries. It establishes that the achievable capacity is limited by $\min\{\epsilon, 1-\epsilon\}$ in both security models. The work is significant for quantifying secure quantum communication protocols under realistic noisy channel conditions.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on quantum cryptography, the rigorous analysis of information leakage and capacity bounds over noisy channels provides theoretical tools applicable to understanding decoherence and information retention in complex quantum physical systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper studies the feasibility and capacity of Quantum-State Oblivious Transfer (OT) when the quantum channel used is the quantum erasure channel.

Main result. It establishes that the capacity for QOT in both the malicious and honest-but-curious settings is $\min\{\epsilon, 1-\epsilon\}$, and that finite-blocklength converse and achievability bounds match in both settings.

Method. The analysis uses information-theoretic security proofs, deriving finite-blocklength bounds by combining techniques like interactive hashing, random Clifford encoding, and Schmidt-rank growth arguments.

Model / system. The model involves Alice holding two quantum systems and Bob attempting to recover one chosen state via $n$ uses of the quantum erasure channel $ ext{E}_\epsilon$. Security is analyzed against both honest-but-curious and malicious adversaries.

Important parameters / regimes. The key parameters are the erasure probability $\epsilon$, the number of channel uses $n$ (blocklength), and the security error bounds ($\delta, u$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes no initial shared entanglement between the parties and relies on asymptotic approximations (e.g., normal approximation to the binomial distribution) for large blocklengths.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by defining the QOT protocol structure, establishing $\delta$-achievability conditions for both security models, deriving the finite-blocklength bounds (Theorem 1 and Theorem 2), and concluding with the matching capacity results.

Abstract

We study quantum-state oblivious transfer over the quantum erasure channel under information-theoretic security guarantees. Specifically, Alice holds two quantum systems, Bob wishes to recover one of them, and the parties have access to independent channel uses and authenticated public classical communication, with no initial shared entanglement. We consider both honest-but-curious and malicious behavior. We establish finite-blocklength converse and achievability bounds, which match in the honest-but-curious setting. The malicious setting construction combines interactive hashing with tests of whether Bob received selected channel outputs, and distributes each input across several qubits so that erasures conceal an entire input from a dishonest receiver, even when the inputs are entangled with an external reference. We also derive fixed-error asymptotic expansions and show that both malicious and honest-but-curious settings have the same capacity.

Reduced Cost Quantum Kernel Training

Summary. This paper tackles the high computational cost of training quantum kernel models for anomaly detection. The authors demonstrate that combining sample downsampling with the Nyström low-rank approximation can drastically reduce the required quantum circuit evaluations by thousands of times. This resource reduction is shown to be effective, maintaining strong anomaly detection performance while drastically lowering the estimated quantum training cost.

Why it may be interesting. While the application is ML-focused, the core techniques—analyzing resource scaling, mitigating exponential measurement requirements, and implementing low-rank approximations for quantum circuits—are highly relevant to designing practical quantum algorithms for complex physical simulations.

Main problem. The primary challenge is developing resource-efficient methods for training quantum kernel models used in machine learning, specifically for one-class anomaly detection.

Main result. By combining training-set reduction (downsampling) and low-rank approximation (Nyström method), the required quantum kernel evaluations can be reduced by orders of magnitude while retaining meaningful anomaly-discrimination performance.

Method. The authors employ quantum kernel methods, approximating the full Gram matrix construction using the Nyström method to reduce complexity from O(N^2) to O(Nm). They apply this to classical anomaly detection frameworks like QSVR.

Model / system. The work utilizes quantum feature maps acting on $n$ qubits to define a quantum kernel, $k(x, x') = |\langle\psi(x) | \psi(x') angle|^2$. The models are tested on the ULB Credit Card Fraud Detection dataset.

Key observables. Average Precision (AP) for anomaly detection, and the required number of quantum kernel evaluations (cost).

Important parameters / regimes. Training set size (e.g., 500 samples), number of qubits ($n$), and the number of landmark points ($m$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the minimum IonQ pricing model for cost estimation, and the Nyström approximation is used to replace the exact kernel matrix.

Abstract

Quantum kernel methods are promising for near-term quantum machine learning, but their training costs are driven by the quadratic scaling of Gram-matrix construction and the measurement cost of estimating individual kernel values. In this work, we investigate one-class anomaly detection as a resource-efficient operating regime for training non-variational quantum kernel models. Using the ULB Credit Card Fraud Detection dataset, we evaluate three quantum feature maps across training-set sizes and qubit counts. Power-law saturation models reveal strong diminishing returns with increasing training data, with 500-sample models retaining approximately 90--93\% of their fitted asymptotic average precision (AP). We further observe no severe exponential concentration among the evaluated feature maps over the investigated qubit range, thereby avoiding the exponentially increasing measurement requirements associated with that regime. Finally, Nyström approximation reduces kernel construction from $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ to $\mathcal{O}(Nm)$ evaluations, with QSVR retaining 94--97\% of its exact-kernel AP using only five landmarks. For ZZ-QSVR, downsampling from 4,000 to 500 training samples and applying a five-landmark approximation decreases the number of required kernel evaluations from approximately $8.0$ million to $2,485$, a reduction of more than 3,200-fold, while AP decreases from $0.812$ to $0.737$. Under the minimum IonQ pricing assumption considered here, this corresponds to an illustrative reduction in quantum kernel training cost from approximately \$1.34 billion to \$417{,}480. These results demonstrate that training-set reduction and low-rank approximation can substantially reduce the resources required to train non-variational quantum kernel anomaly detectors while retaining meaningful anomaly-discrimination performance.

Robust subspace designs and the power of a unique small quantum witness

Authors: Simon Apers, Roman Edenhofer, Benjamin Mathieu-Bloise, Partha Mukhopadhyay

Summary. This paper develops the mathematical concept of 'robust subspace designs' to quantify how well a family of subspaces avoids intersecting any given subspace. It applies this theory to quantum complexity theory, proving a quantum analogue of the Valiant-Vazirani theorem by showing that restricting quantum witnesses to a unique subspace preserves computational hardness.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical tools developed (robust subspace designs) provide powerful techniques for analyzing the structure and hardness of quantum computational models, which is central to quantum information theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper introduces and analyzes 'robust subspace designs' as a quantitative tool to establish quantum complexity containments.

Main result. It proves a quantum space-bounded variant of the Valiant-Vazirani theorem, specifically $ ext{FewQUMAL1} \subseteq ext{RLUQUMAL1}$, using these designs to isolate unique quantum witnesses.

Method. The core method involves using robust subspace designs, derived via probabilistic or explicit constructions, to iteratively reduce the kernel dimension of a matrix associated with a quantum protocol.

Model / system. The work analyzes quantum Merlin-Arthur protocols ($ ext{FewQUMAL1}$), which involve quantum witnesses, and relates them to algebraic structures like subspaces and singular values.

Key observables. The minimum non-zero eigenvalue ($\lambda_{\min}(H_x)$) of a constructed Hamiltonian $H_x$, and the dimension of the accepting witness subspace.

Important parameters / regimes. The robustness parameter ($\eta$), the acceptance gap, and the time horizon $T$ used in the Hamiltonian construction.

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically illustrates the difference between a 'Robust' and 'Non-robust' family of planes in $\mathbb{R}^3$.

Paper structure. The paper first develops the theory of robust subspace designs (probabilistic and explicit constructions). It then applies this theory to establish the quantum complexity containment $ ext{FewQUMAL1} \subseteq ext{RLUQUMAL1}$ via kernel peeling, followed by related completeness proofs.

Abstract

The concept of subspace designs was introduced by Guruswami and Xing (STOC'13), and explicit constructions were given by Guruswami and Kopparty (FOCS'13). These are families of subspaces that have small intersection with any given subspace of a fixed dimension. We introduce \emph{robust subspace designs}. Informally, these are a quantitative extension in which we demand that not too many subspaces of the family contain directions that lie close to any given subspace of a fixed dimension. We give a probabilistic construction of such a robust subspace design of polynomial size, as well as a non-trivial explicit construction of superpolynomial size. Our main application of this new concept is a quantum space-bounded variant of the Valiant-Vazirani theorem (Theor.Comput.Sci.'86), which shows that restricting $\mathsf{NP}$-complete problems to instances with at most one accepting witness preserves hardness under randomized reductions. For quantum witnesses, the analogous quantity is the dimension of an accepting witness subspace. We use our probabilistic construction of robust subspace designs to isolate a unique witness for space-bounded quantum Merlin-Arthur protocols with perfect completeness and an acceptance gap outside their perfectly accepting subspace. As further applications, we give a randomized reduction of well-conditioned nullity testing to space-bounded quantum Merlin-Arthur protocols with perfect completeness. Using a similar idea, we find that ordinary subspace designs allow us to recover the classical $\mathsf{C_= L}$ containment of Allender, Beals, and Ogihara (STOC'96) for general nullity testing through a simpler proof.

Self-assembly Monte Carlo reveals localized entanglement in giant polymer melts

Abstract

The Clifford hierarchy plays a central role in fault-tolerant quantum computation, but its higher levels remain only partially understood. Recent work on quantum higher order Fourier analysis characterizes membership in the Clifford hierarchy using quantum derivatives, but does not provide an explicit description of the individual levels. In this work, we address this problem by showing that the collection of quantum derivatives of a unitary operator forms a non-abelian \(1\)-cocycle, and conversely that every such \(1\)-cocycle arises from a unitary operator. This yields a recursive cohomological characterization of the Clifford hierarchy. We then specialize this framework to the third level and decompose the cocycle data into symplectic, affine, and phase components, each admitting a cohomological interpretation. As applications, we give a new proof that every two-qudit gate in the third level of the Clifford hierarchy is semi-Clifford, and prove for the first time that every three-qudit gate in the third level is semi-Clifford.

Abstract

We investigate converse bounds on the average decoding error probability in finite-blocklength classical-quantum channel coding. We first present a lower bound for multiple quantum hypothesis testing in terms of pairwise trace distances and derive a corresponding fidelity bound. We then obtain a spectrum-based converse that depends only on the a priori probabilities and spectra of the states. We show that the converse bound remains tight for the quantum depolarizing channel under suitable conditions. For codes with product-state outputs, this converse takes an explicit form involving products of output-state eigenvalues. We apply it to binary codes over the quantum amplitude damping channel using the input states $\vert+\rangle$ and $\vert-\rangle$. For this setting, we also discuss a normal approximation to the spectrum-based converse in the large blocklength regime. In all numerical examples considered, the spectrum-based converse is tighter than the other converse bounds at low noise levels.
