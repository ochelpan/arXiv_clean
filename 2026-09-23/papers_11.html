Summary. This research introduces microwave impedance microscopy (iMIM) as a breakthrough, contact-free electrical technique to image dark excitons in 2D semiconductors like WS2. By measuring the material's complex permittivity, the authors successfully separated the purely dielectric exciton response from background conductivity. This establishes excitons as tunable dielectric elements and opens a new pathway for nanoscale quantum material characterization.

Why it may be interesting. This work bridges solid-state physics (exciton dynamics) with microwave engineering, providing a novel, non-optical diagnostic tool for quantum materials that is highly relevant to condensed matter theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is to develop a contact-free electrical method to probe dark excitons in atomically thin semiconductors, which are typically only visible via optical spectroscopy.

Main result. The authors successfully demonstrated that photoexcited excitons contribute a measurable, purely dielectric permittivity at microwave frequencies, allowing for nanoscale imaging of dark exciton transport.

Method. The experiment utilizes laser-illuminated microwave impedance microscopy (iMIM) to measure the complex permittivity, which is complemented by first-principles DFT and GW-BSE calculations to model the exciton polarizability.

Model / system. The system is monolayer tungsten disulfide (WS2) and other 2D semiconductors. The theoretical framework involves modeling the exciton polarizability using an effective Keldysh electron-hole Hamiltonian and relating it to the macroscopic dielectric response via Clausius-Mossotti-like relations.

Key observables. The imaginary part of the tip admittance ($ ext{iMIM-Im}$) shows resonant peaks matching exciton spectra, while the real part ($ ext{iMIM-Re}$) remaining zero confirms a purely dielectric response, distinguishing it from photoconductivity.

Important parameters / regimes. The measurements are performed at gigahertz frequencies, with spatial resolution achieving sub-100 nm, and the response is tunable by laser power and temperature.

Assumptions / limitations. Key assumptions include the negligible out-of-plane polarizability compared to the in-plane component, and the use of the Clausius-Mossotti relation to link microscopic polarizability to macroscopic permittivity.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the schematic of the iMIM setup, the dependence of the effective dielectric constant on photon energy, and comparative maps showing the distinct purely dielectric response (high-purity) versus the conductive response (defect-rich) in the iMIM spectra.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the limitations of optical probing, then introducing the iMIM technique to measure the dielectric response, demonstrating the distinction between exciton-induced permittivity and conventional photoconductivity, and finally validating the findings through theoretical modeling of exciton polarizability.

Abstract

Excitons in atomically thin semiconductors are almost always probed through their optical signatures, because the short lifetimes of these transient quasiparticles are generally assumed to preclude electrical detection. Here we show that photoexcited excitons in monolayer tungsten disulfide produce a large, optically tunable permittivity at gigahertz frequencies, and that the effect provides a contact-free electrical route to imaging dark excitons at the nanoscale. Using laser-illuminated microwave impedance microscopy, we find that high-purity encapsulated flakes exhibit a purely dielectric response resonant with the exciton spectrum, whereas defect-rich samples are governed by conventional photoconductivity. Spatial mapping of diffusion and sublinear power dependence identify long-lived dark excitons as the dominant contributors, and first-principles modelling of exciton polarizability reproduces the measured susceptibility. Our results establish excitons as optically tunable dielectric elements and introduce microwave microscopy as a direct electrical probe of dark-exciton transport with sub-100 nm resolution.

Summary. This theoretical work explores how the thickness and symmetry of topological superconductor films dictate the emergence and interaction of multiple types of Majorana modes. By modeling 2M-WS2 coupled to magnetic insulators, the authors show that crystalline symmetry enables the simultaneous existence of first-, second-, and third-order Majorana modes. This control mechanism is crucial for advancing the realization of fault-tolerant quantum computation based on non-Abelian statistics.

Why it may be interesting. The explicit control over multiple, distinct types of Majorana modes (vortex, edge, corner) via crystalline symmetry and thickness offers a highly tunable platform for realizing non-Abelian statistics, which is a key goal in topological quantum computation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the thickness-dependent evolution, coexistence, and interconversion of multiple-order Majorana modes in topological superconductor films.

Main result. The research demonstrates that the proper crystalline symmetry allows for the unprecedented coexistence of first-, second-, and third-order Majorana modes in a single platform, which can be controlled by film thickness and external fields.

Method. The authors employ theoretical modeling using a properly constructed k.p model and perform numerical diagonalization of the resulting Bogoliubov-de Gennes Hamiltonian.

Model / system. The system is a $C_{2h}$-symmetric topological superconductor film (like 2M-WS2) proximity coupled with magnetic insulators. The model incorporates the full BdG Hamiltonian, including proximity-induced exchange fields and s-wave pairing.

Key observables. Zero-energy states, Majorana vortex bound states (MVBs), chiral Majorana edge modes (CMEs), and Majorana corner modes (MCMs).

Important parameters / regimes. Film thickness ($d$), proximity-induced exchange field ($h$), and the penetration depth of the exchange field ($z_h$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on projecting the bulk Hamiltonian onto specific surfaces and assumes the validity of the k.p model description.

Figures summary. Figure 1 presents a schematic of the heterostructure, a phase diagram mapping thickness and $h$ to different topological regimes, and illustrations of the coexisting Majorana modes. Figure 2 and 3 show energy spectra and wavefunctions for varying field strengths and thicknesses.

Paper structure. The paper is structured by physical regimes: the thick-film regime showing mode coexistence, the crossover regime analyzing critical field dependence on thickness, and the ultrathin-film regime exploring thickness-dependent $Z_2$ phases and electric field tuning.

Abstract

We theoretically investigate the thickness-dependent evolution of Majorana modes in $C_{2h}$-symmetric topological superconductor films (such as the recently discovered 2M-WS$_2$) proximity coupled with magnetic insulators. For sufficiently thick films, two Majorana bound states coexist as end modes at the surface and interface along a vortex line, with the interfacial mode evolving into a chiral Majorana edge mode upon increasing the proximity-induced exchange field. The intrinsic $C_{2h}$ crystalline symmetry selects two chiral Majorana modes circulating along the hinges on two of the four side surfaces of the film. When the penetration depth of the exchange field is sufficiently shallow, the two circulating modes are localized near the interface, but with qualitatively different subsequent evolutions. One of them further collapses to form two Majorana corner modes, while the other merges with the chiral Majorana mode circulating around the interface. Importantly, the corner modes are well decoupled from the interfacial chiral mode, thereby enabling an unprecedented coexistence of first-, second-, and third-order Majorana modes within a single material platform. We further show that such coexistence persists even in the ultrathin-film limit, where the electric-field-controlled two-dimensional $Z_2$ topology offers an extra advantage to readily interconvert the multiple-order Majorana modes. These findings highlight the pivotal role of the proper crystalline symmetry in enabling emergence, manipulation, and potential braiding of Majorana modes for demonstrating non-Abelian statistics and fault-tolerant quantum computation.

Summary. This work establishes generalized, necessary, and sufficient conditions for odd-frequency superconducting pairing applicable to all superconducting structures. By analyzing various physical systems like SF junctions and TMDs, the authors demonstrate that the non-zero commutator of the normal and superconducting Hamiltonians dictates the presence of this exotic pairing state. This advances the theoretical understanding of unconventional superconductivity signatures.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous derivation of a universal condition for odd-frequency pairing provides a powerful theoretical tool for understanding unconventional superconductivity, which is a major topic in condensed matter physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To derive generalized necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of odd-frequency superconducting pairing in any superconducting system.

Main result. The condition for odd-frequency pairing is found to be the non-zero commutator $[\hat{h}, \hat{\Delta}]^* eq 0$, which holds regardless of the order parameter expansion or system complexity.

Method. The authors use Green's function theory, employing Neumann series and high-frequency expansions of the Bogoliubov-de Gennes Hamiltonian to analyze the anomalous electron-hole Green's functions.

Model / system. The theory is applied to diverse superconducting systems, including bulk superconductors, superconducting-ferromagnet (SF) junctions, Josephson junctions, and TMD monolayers proximitized by conventional superconductors.

Key observables. Odd-frequency pair amplitudes ($F_{ij}(z)$), paramagnetic Meissner response, and the commutator $[\hat{h}, \hat{\Delta}]^*$.

Important parameters / regimes. Superconducting gap ($\Delta$), magnetic field ($b_x$), and various hopping/tunneling amplitudes ($t, t_s, \Delta$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on expanding the Green's functions in powers of the order parameter ($\hat{\Delta}$) and utilizing high-frequency approximations for computational tractability.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the absolute value of the pair amplitude $F_{\uparrow\uparrow}$ versus frequency $z$ for specific couplings (like $t_s$ or $b_z$) in junction models.

Paper structure. The paper first derives the general condition using Green's functions, then applies this condition systematically to model junctions (SF and Josephson) and advanced materials (TMDs) to confirm the theoretical predictions.

Abstract

Odd-frequency superconducting pairing has been predicted to arise in many systems and is known to lead to phenomena such as paramagnetic Meissner response and long-range superconducting proximity effect. Here, we provide generalized necessary and sufficient conditions for odd-frequency pairing appearing in any superconducting system, from bulk superconductors to superconducting hybrid structures. This generalizes an earlier first-order expression in multiband bulk superconductors to all superconducting systems and to all orders, in both order parameter and frequency. We then apply the derived conditions to several systems, including superconducting-ferromagnet and superconducting Josephson junctions, as well as a transition metal dichalcogenide monolayer proximitized by a conventional superconductor, where the generalized conditions are used to understand the properties of the superconducting state.

Method. The study employed magneto-transport and temperature-dependent measurements on moiré superlattices, analyzing the evolution of longitudinal and Hall resistances.

Model / system. The physical system is hexalayer rhombohedral graphene moiré superlattices encapsulated between hBN, which create a tunable moiré potential hosting flat bands that act as a Kondo lattice.

Important parameters / regimes. Carrier density ($n$), displacement electric field ($D$), and magnetic field ($B$) are key tuning parameters, with measurements focused near filling factor $ u=2$.

Abstract

Kondo effect in strongly correlated systems arises from the antiferromagnetic coupling between itinerant conduction electrons and localized magnetic moments, giving rise to a variety of exotic quantum phenomena. Two-dimensional moiré superlattice systems provide a highly tunable platform featuring topological flat bands, where Wannier orbitals are spatially confined by the periodic moiré potential and serve as localized magnetic moments, enabling the observable Kondo effect. Here we experimentally demonstrate Kondo interactions in hexalayer rhombohedral graphene moiré superlattices through magneto-transport and temperature-dependent measurements. With increasing magnetic field, the magnetoresistance exhibits an increase-decrease transition across a critical field B_c, while the Hall resistance R_xy undergoes a sign reversal near B_c. Moreover, as temperature decreases, the longitudinal resistance R_xx first increases logarithmically and then decreases following a T^2 behavior, indicating a transition to heavy fermion liquid. These behaviors can be consistently explained by the breakdown of Kondo singlets induced by either magnetic field or temperature, which liberates carriers previously bound to localized moments, thereby enhancing conductivity and altering the dominant carrier type. Furthermore, our results demonstrate that the Kondo interaction can be continuously tuned by both carrier density n and displacement electric field D, and suggest the emergence of a Kondo insulating state. Our findings provide deep insight into the Kondo effect in moiré engineered flat-band systems, paving the path for exploring exotic correlated quantum phases.

Abstract

Summary. This paper analyzes wave selection at an O(2)-equivariant Hopf bifurcation in nonlinear PDEs. By imposing the condition that the nonlinearities are variational (derived from local Hamiltonians), the authors prove that the selection depends solely on one coefficient, $\zeta_R$. This provides a powerful structural constraint linking Hamiltonian symmetries to the stability of emergent wave patterns.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical framework connecting variational principles (Hamiltonian structure) to the selection of stable wave patterns in dissipative, nonlinear PDEs is highly relevant to understanding pattern formation in complex physical systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the selection mechanism between traveling and standing waves that occurs at an O(2)-equivariant Hopf bifurcation in nonlinear partial differential equations (PDEs).

Main result. It establishes that for variational nonlinearities, the selection is governed by the sign of $\zeta_R$, predicting stable standing waves for $\zeta_R < 0$ and unstable ones for $\zeta_R > 0$.

Method. The analysis uses center-manifold reduction to derive a normal-form system, followed by detailed calculation of the cubic nonlinear coefficients ($\zeta, \xi$) using techniques involving Poisson operators and Hamiltonian structures.

Model / system. The study focuses on a class of two-component PDEs on the circle whose nonlinearities are generated by reflection-invariant local Hamiltonians via a common constant-coefficient Poisson operator.

Key observables. The real parts of the cubic normal-form coefficients, $\zeta_R$ and $\xi_R$, which determine the stability and selection between wave types.

Important parameters / regimes. The bifurcation parameter $\lambda$, the dissipation rate $\epsilon$, and the resonance conditions (e.g., 2:1 resonance).

Assumptions / limitations. The nonlinearities are assumed to be variational, meaning they are derived from a local Hamiltonian structure, which imposes strong constraints on the coefficients ($\xi_R=0$).

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by setting up the problem at the Hopf bifurcation, deriving the reduced normal form via center manifold theory, and then applying the variational constraint to simplify the coefficients and determine the resulting wave selection criteria.

Abstract

At an $O(2)$-equivariant Hopf bifurcation, the real parts $ζ_R$ and $ξ_R$ of the two cubic normal-form coefficients govern selection between traveling and standing waves. In a fourth-order regularized $p$-system, $ξ_R$ was observed to vanish and the cubic nonlinearity to shift only frequencies. We show that both observations are structural: variational nonlinearities enforce them even when the linear part is dissipative and non-Hamiltonian. We consider a class of two-component PDEs on the circle whose quadratic and cubic nonlinearities are generated by reflection-invariant local Hamiltonians of the fields and finitely many of their spatial derivatives, through a common constant-coefficient Poisson operator. Then $ξ_R=0$ for every admissible linear part, while $ζ_R$ depends only on the quadratic nonlinearity. For variational nonlinearities the direct cubic terms are purely imaginary. The mixed cascade term loses its real part because its second harmonic has zero temporal frequency and the resolvent keeps its parity, while the self-cascade at the doubled critical frequency keeps a real part. When $ζ_R\ne0$, the bifurcating traveling waves are saddles, while the standing waves are supercritical and orbitally asymptotically stable for $ζ_R<0$ and subcritical and unstable for $ζ_R>0$. When the nonlinearities are the Poisson operator applied to polynomials in the fields alone, and its symbol does not vanish at the critical and second-harmonic wavenumbers, cancellation for every admissible linear part conversely characterizes variationality. In a dissipatively regularized Boussinesq family, as dissipation tends to zero, $ζ_R$ vanishes linearly off resonance and diverges inversely at the 2:1 resonance. The Hamiltonian limit of the cubic coefficient is therefore singular.

Abstract

We study the quantum corrections to the vacuum energies and to the binding of the multi-kinks of the non-degenerate double sine-Gordon model. With normal ordering defined at the meson mass of the primary vacuum, the one-loop calculation predicts an inversion of the classical vacuum hierarchy, in which quantum fluctuations favour the central secondary vacuum above a coupling set by the classical splitting of the vacua and their meson mass ratio.
