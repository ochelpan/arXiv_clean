Metric Self-Dual Completion and Optimal Additive Hardness for Quantum and Graph-State Distance

Authors: Rafail Ostrovsky
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.22669v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical paper establishes that approximating the quantum code distance and the graph-state distance are computationally hard problems, showing an $\Omega(N)$ additive gap. The authors achieve this using a novel, deterministic technique called metric self-dual completion. This work significantly raises the known lower bounds for these critical metrics in quantum information science.

Why it may be interesting. The results provide fundamental computational complexity bounds on the distinguishability of quantum error-correcting codes and graph states, which are central to quantum information theory and quantum computation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to establish the optimal additive hardness gaps for approximating two distance problems: the quantum code distance and the graph-state distance.

Main result. It proves that both the quantum code distance and the graph-state minimum distance are NP-hard to approximate within an additive error of $\Omega(N)$, significantly improving previous lower bounds.

Method. The core technique is the 'Metric Self-Dual Completion,' a deterministic classical construction used to convert general codes into self-dual codes while exactly doubling the original coset metric, allowing the application of classical hardness reductions.

Model / system. The study focuses on quantum error-correcting codes, specifically CSS codes with identical X- and Z-check spaces, and the graph-state distance defined by a graph's adjacency matrix $\Gamma$.

Key observables. Quantum code distance ($d(Q)$) and graph-state minimum distance ($d(\Gamma)$).

Important parameters / regimes. N (number of qubits/vertices), additive error gap ($c N$), and code parameters like rate and relative distance.

Assumptions / limitations. The quantum hardness result holds for CSS codes with constant rate and constant relative distance. The graph-state result holds for balanced bipartite graphs with $\Gamma^2 = I$.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the metric self-dual completion construction, detailing its properties. It then applies this construction to derive the NP-hardness results for the quantum code distance and the graph-state distance, respectively.

Abstract

We prove that the quantum code distance is NP-hard to approximate within an additive error of $c N$, for some constant $c >0$, where $N$ is the number of qubits. Our reductions are deterministic. This improves the previous square-root additive gap to $Ω(N)$ and resolves the explicitly stated linear-gap question of Kapshikar and Kundu. Our result holds for CSS codes with identical $X$- and $Z$-check spaces, and with a constant rate and constant relative distance. For every fixed $λ>1$, there is a constant $c>0$ such that hardness still holds even when every nonidentity stabilizer has weight greater than $λ$ times the quantum distance. We also improve the hardness gap of graph state distance on $N$ vertices of Grigorescu, Jha, and Samperton from cube-root to $Ω(N)$, resolving their explicitly stated open question. Both hardness results are asymptotically optimal since both distances are at most $N$. Our graph state distance hardness result holds for balanced bipartite graphs with a binary adjacency matrix that is its own inverse (mod 2). Our main technique for both hardness bounds above is classical: we show how to convert any code $C$ of length $m$ into a self-dual code $A(C)$ of length $N=Θ(m)$ while exactly doubling the original coset metric. The conversion is deterministic and efficient. We call it the metric self-dual completion of $C$. It comes with a linear embedding $τ: \mathbb F_2^m \hookrightarrow \mathbb F_2^N$. The embedding doubles all Hamming distances between vectors in $\mathbb F_2^m$ and all pairwise distances between corresponding cosets. The embedding also guarantees that all codewords of $A(C)$ of weight at most $2m$ are exactly $τ(C)$.

Multiscale Modeling of Ion Transport in Nanopores: Fitting Implicit-Water Radial Diffusion Profiles to Explicit-Water Molecular Dynamics

Authors: Mónika Valiskó, Salman Shabbir, Eszter Molnárné Lakics, Zoltán Ható, Dezső Boda

Summary. This paper presents a multiscale methodology to model ion transport in nanopores by fitting radial diffusion profiles derived from detailed explicit-water Molecular Dynamics simulations into a reduced Nernst-Planck/LEMC framework. The approach successfully captures complex phenomena like near-wall mobility suppression and differential selectivity for $ ext{Na}^+$ versus $ ext{Ca}^{2+}$ ions. It is significant for developing accurate, efficient models for electrochemical devices operating at the nanoscale.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a rigorous method for bridging detailed molecular simulations (MD) with continuum electrochemistry models (NP equation), which is crucial for developing predictive, computationally tractable models for nanoscale devices.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops a multiscale approach to accurately model ion transport in nanopores by incorporating molecular-scale information into computationally efficient continuum models.

Main result. The derived effective diffusion coefficient profiles successfully reproduce the radial current distributions from explicit-water MD simulations, capturing crucial near-wall mobility suppression effects.

Method. A radially varying effective diffusion coefficient profile is fitted to radial conductivity profiles obtained from explicit-water Molecular Dynamics (MD) simulations, implemented within the NP+LEMC framework.

Model / system. The system is ion transport ($ ext{NaCl}$, $ ext{CaCl}_2$, mixtures) through negatively charged silica nanopores. The modeling involves comparing explicit-water MD results with reduced models based on the Nernst–Planck equation combined with Local Equilibrium Monte Carlo (LEMC).

Key observables. Radial current density profiles ($j_i(r)$), radial conductivity profiles ($\kappa_i(r)$), and the fitted radial diffusion coefficient profiles ($D_i(r)$).

Important parameters / regimes. Pore radius, surface charge density, external electric field strength, and the specific electrolyte composition ($ ext{NaCl}$ vs. $ ext{CaCl}_2$).

Assumptions / limitations. The approach assumes that the molecular-scale information from explicit-water MD can be accurately parameterized into a spatially varying effective diffusion coefficient profile for use in the reduced continuum model.

Figures summary. Figures compare radial profiles ($\sigma_i(r)$, $\mu_i(r)$, $c_i(r)$) and conductances ($G_i$) derived from different charge assignments (full vs. scaled) across various electrolyte mixtures, and show the final fitted $D_i(r)$ profiles.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the need for multiscale modeling, details the MD simulations (explicit water, specific force fields), introduces the NP+LEMC framework, and then presents the fitting procedure and comparison of results for different salt mixtures and charge models.

Abstract

We develop a multiscale approach for incorporating molecular-scale transport information into computationally efficient models of ion transport through nanopores. A radially varying effective diffusion coefficient profile is fitted to radial conductivity profiles obtained from explicit-water molecular dynamics (MD) simulations. The fitting is performed within the NP+LEMC framework, which combines the Nernst--Planck equation with Local Equilibrium Monte Carlo to account for ion correlations beyond mean-field approximation. We apply the approach to NaCl, CaCl$_2$, and their mixtures in a negatively charged silica nanopore. The resulting diffusion coefficient profiles reproduce the radial current distributions of the MD simulations, including the strong suppression of ionic mobility near the pore wall that cannot be captured by a spatially constant diffusion coefficient inside the pore. NaCl and CaCl$_2$ exhibit qualitatively different transport behavior: the former is cation selective due to enhanced near-wall Na$^+$ conduction, whereas the latter shows weak anion selectivity because strongly bound Ca$^{2+}$ ions have strongly suppressed mobility near the surface. For NaCl--CaCl$_2$ mixtures, preferential Ca$^{2+}$ binding leads to nonlinear changes in ionic conductance. The approach establishes a bridge between experimentally relevant device behavior and computationally efficient reduced models, with explicit-water MD providing the molecular-scale information that can be incorporated into the effective transport coefficients.

New lower bounds for CDS and $f$-routing

Authors: Atsuya Hasegawa, Ranitha Mataraarachchi
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.24291v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

Summary. This paper derives strong lower bounds for the entanglement cost of non-local quantum computation tasks, particularly $f$-routing, and for the shared randomness required in Conditional Disclosure of Secrets (CDS). By connecting these two problems, the authors establish bounds based on the sign rank of associated Boolean matrices, advancing the understanding of quantum information complexity.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides rigorous, information-theoretic lower bounds for fundamental quantum tasks, which can guide the design of quantum protocols and test the limits of quantum communication complexity.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper establishes lower bounds on the entanglement cost for Non-Local Quantum Computation (NLQC) tasks, specifically focusing on $f$-routing and Conditional Disclosure of Secrets (CDS).

Main result. They derive a general lower bound for one-sided-perfect $f$-routing based on the sign rank of the associated matrix, and establish a shared-randomness lower bound for robust CDS.

Method. The authors utilize techniques involving the sign rank of Boolean matrices and establish connections between the randomness complexity of CDS and the entanglement cost of $f$-routing.

Model / system. The study analyzes abstract quantum information tasks: $f$-routing (a non-local computation task) and CDS (a secure multi-party computation primitive). These tasks are modeled using quantum communication protocols involving shared entangled states.

Key observables. Entanglement cost ($\log d_E$), Shared-randomness cost ($\log q$), Sign rank ($ ext{signrank}(S_f)$), and Deterministic SMP communication complexity ($D_{\parallel}(f)$).

Important parameters / regimes. The bounds are derived for one-sided-perfect protocols, and the results are shown to be tight for specific functions like the equality function.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the connection between CDS and $f$-routing, and the use of the sign-rank method, which is most directly applicable to one-sided-perfect settings.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the setup for local vs. non-local quantum computation. Figure 2 illustrates the setup for the Conditional Disclosure of Secrets (CDS) task.

Paper structure. The paper first addresses the robust CDS lower bound using shared randomness, connecting it to deterministic communication complexity. It then tackles one-sided-perfect $f$-routing by exploiting the positivity of the low-rank matrix, yielding a bound in terms of sign rank, and finally relates these bounds across different complexity measures.

Abstract

Understanding the entanglement cost of non-local quantum computation (NLQC) is relevant to complexity theory, cryptography, quantum gravity, and related areas. A central special case is $f$-routing, motivated in part by quantum position verification. Proving lower bounds on its entanglement cost in the fully robust setting has been a major open problem in NLQC. Motivated by this problem, we establish two related lower bounds. First, we study the shared-randomness cost of robust conditional disclosure of secrets (CDS). The connection between CDS and $f$-routing established by Allerstorfer et al. (Quantum 2024) makes understanding the randomness complexity of robust CDS a natural step toward lower bounds for the fully robust routing problem. We show that the shared-randomness cost of robust CDS is lower bounded by the logarithm of deterministic SMP communication complexity, even when communication and private randomness are unrestricted. Our lower bound is tight for the equality function. Second, we consider one-sided-perfect $f$-routing, in which the protocol is exact on one input class and has constant error on the other. By exploiting the positivity of the low-rank matrix arising in the method of Asadi, Culf, and May (ITCS 2025), we derive a general lower bound on the entanglement cost in terms of sign rank. In particular, this yields a linear lower bound on the entanglement cost of routing for the inner-product function in both one-sided-perfect settings, matching the known upper bound.

On the dual character of Zn impurity in SnTe: Tuning thermoelectric and topological properties

Authors: Kacper Pryga, Bartlomiej Wiendlocha
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.24459v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical study investigates how doping SnTe with Zn affects its electronic and topological properties. The authors show that Zn introduces a resonant impurity state that boosts thermoelectric performance, while simultaneously inducing a controllable topological phase transition in the band structure. This work establishes doping as a powerful mechanism for multiscale property tuning in chalcogenide semiconductors.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on solid-state physics, the concept of tuning electronic properties (like band gaps or topological invariants) via controlled disorder or doping is relevant to understanding engineered quantum materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To clarify the microscopic role of Zn doping in SnTe and demonstrate that doping can simultaneously tune both the thermoelectric and topological properties of the material.

Main result. Zn doping introduces a resonant-like impurity state that enhances thermopower in n-type SnTe, while band structure modifications drive a controllable topological phase transition from an inverted to a trivial band ordering.

Method. The study uses first-principles calculations, specifically the Korringa-Kohn-Rostoker method within the coherent potential approximation, complemented by pseudopotential calculations.

Model / system. The physical system is Zn-doped SnTe ($ ext{SnTe}: ext{Zn}$), a lead-free analogue of PbTe, which is studied as a candidate thermoelectric material.

Key observables. Thermopower (Seebeck coefficient), resonant-like impurity state formation, L–Σ band convergence, and the transition between inverted and trivial band ordering.

Important parameters / regimes. Zn concentration, Fermi level position, and the electronic structure evolution across different doping levels.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the p-type character is primarily derived from Sn vacancies, and that the Zn substitution itself does not alter the carrier concentration if vacancies are ignored.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the evolution of the Density of States (DOS) showing the resonant 'hump' growth with Zn concentration, and band structure analysis confirming the topological phase transition.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the electronic structure using advanced computational methods to link the introduction of Zn impurities to changes in both transport properties (thermoelectric) and band topology.

Abstract

We present a first-principles study of the electronic structure and thermoelectric properties of Zn-doped SnTe using the Korringa-Kohn-Rostoker method within the coherent potential approximation, complemented by pseudopotential calculations. SnTe is a lead-free analogue of PbTe and a candidate thermoelectric material in which Zn doping has been experimentally reported to enhance the performance of $p$-type samples. We show that Zn introduces a resonant-like impurity state, located within the conduction band, which evolves strongly depending on the Zn concentration. This feature leads to a significant enhancement of the thermopower in $n$-type SnTe. In the valence band, Zn doping induces L-$Σ$ band convergence, also resulting in an increased $p$-type Seebeck coefficient over a broad concentration range and delaying the onset of the bipolar effect. We further demonstrate that the Zn-induced band-structure modifications drive a transition from an inverted to a trivial band ordering, indicating a controllable topological phase transition. Our results clarify the microscopic role of Zn in SnTe and identify doping as a mechanism for simultaneously tuning thermoelectric and topological properties.

QAOA-Based Pilot Assignment for Cell-Free Massive MIMO Systems

Authors: Xiaoyu Ma, Fang Fang, Xianbin Wang

Summary. This paper tackles the pilot assignment problem in massive MIMO systems, which is difficult to solve classically due to exponential complexity. It reformulates this optimization problem into a quantum Hamiltonian and solves it using the QAOA algorithm. The results demonstrate that the quantum approach yields near-optimal solutions, highlighting the potential of quantum computing for wireless resource management.

Why it may be interesting. While the application is in wireless communications, the core methodology—encoding a complex combinatorial optimization problem into a Hamiltonian and solving it with QAOA—is a direct application of quantum algorithms for constrained optimization.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the computationally hard problem of minimizing pilot contamination in cell-free massive MIMO systems due to unavoidable pilot reuse.

Main result. The proposed QAOA-based framework successfully encodes the optimization problem, achieving performance close to exhaustive search in simulations.

Method. The problem is reformulated as a binary quadratic optimization problem and solved using the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) with a constraint-aware mixer Hamiltonian.

Model / system. The system modeled is a cell-free massive MIMO network. The optimization involves assigning pilots ($\phi$) to UEs ($K$) to minimize the weighted pilot contamination cost $C(\phi)$.

Key observables. Normalized pilot contamination cost ($ar{C}(\phi)$) and Normalized optimality gap ($G_p$).

Important parameters / regimes. Number of UEs ($K$), number of available pilots ($ au_p$), and QAOA parameters ($\gamma, eta$).

Assumptions / limitations. The approach assumes that QAOA can effectively solve the combinatorial optimization problem, and a feasibility-preserving mixer Hamiltonian is used to enforce constraints.

Figures summary. Figure 2 shows the normalized pilot contamination cost vs. number of UEs for $p=3$. Figure 3 shows the normalized optimality gap vs. QAOA depth ($p$).

Paper structure. The paper first defines the physical problem (pilot contamination) and reformulates it into a quantum-compatible optimization problem using cost and mixer Hamiltonians. It then details the QAOA implementation and presents simulation results comparing it against classical benchmarks.

Abstract

Pilot contamination is a major challenge in cell-free massive multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) systems, where the limited number of orthogonal pilots makes pilot reuse unavoidable. Since the pilot assignment solution space grows exponentially with the number of users, finding the global optimum becomes computationally challenging on classical computers. Recent advances in quantum computing provide a promising approach for solving such large-scale combinatorial optimization problems. In this paper, we reformulate the assignment problem as a quantum-compatible optimization problem, enabling it to be directly solved by the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA). Specifically, the pilot contamination objective and assignment constraints are incorporated into the QAOA formulation. This allows the quantum search to focus on valid pilot assignments with low contamination cost. Simulation results show that the proposed method achieves performance close to exhaustive search, demonstrating the potential of quantum-assisted optimization for pilot assignment in future wireless systems.

QuWARP: A Workload-Aware Reuse Planner for simulating Quantum Circuits

Authors: Tim Littau, Rihan Hai
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.23664v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces QuWARP, a novel workload-aware planner designed to optimize quantum circuit simulations by identifying and safely reusing shared computational prefixes across related tasks. By treating simulation as a planning problem rather than isolated runs, QuWARP achieves substantial speedups while maintaining rigorous correctness guarantees through auditable legality checks.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the computational bottleneck in running quantum algorithms by treating the simulation as a resource management problem, which is highly relevant for developing scalable quantum software stacks.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary problem is the inefficiency of quantum circuit simulation when running many related tasks, as existing simulators fail to reuse shared computational state across these tasks.

Main result. QuWARP achieves significant speedups (up to 32.84x) on reuse-positive workloads by implementing a workload-level planning layer that safely identifies and materializes shared computational prefixes.

Method. The authors propose QuWARP, a planner that analyzes a sequence of related quantum tasks, identifies shared prefixes, and uses a cost-benefit analysis to decide whether to reuse a materialized boundary state or recompute the work.

Model / system. The system simulates quantum circuits using an existing framework (Qrack) and operates on both statevector and stabilizer-hybrid representations. It plans over a sequence of related tasks (e.g., VQE sweeps, QEC cycles) rather than isolated circuits.

Key observables. Speedups (2.95x–32.84x), auditable EXPLAIN-style traces detailing reuse decisions (reuse, abstain, refuse), and cost records.

Important parameters / regimes. The profitability of reuse is governed by a cost model comparing saved prefix work against materialization and reload overheads.

Assumptions / limitations. The validated reuse surface is intentionally narrow, limited to specific continuation types within the Qrack framework, and the planner is designed to be conservatively correct.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the core concept of prefix reuse in VQE sweeps. Other figures detail the planning flow, legality checks, and ablation studies demonstrating the necessity of the planner's components.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem of redundant computation in quantum simulation, proposes the QuWARP planner, details its workload-level planning mechanism (including legality checks and cost modeling), presents empirical results showing speedups, and concludes by discussing the scope and limitations of the approach.

Abstract

Quantum circuit simulation often appears as repeated-run workloads rather than isolated circuits: variational quantum eigensolver (VQE) sweeps, noisy multishot studies, and quantum error correction (QEC) cycles revisit closely related structure across many runs. Existing simulators optimise individual executions well, but they largely ignore cross-task shared state and therefore repeat work that could be reused safely. We propose QuWARP, a planner-based workload optimiser for a bounded state of the art simulator execution surface: it performs workload-level planning over related tasks, identifies shared prefixes, and chooses when to materialize exact typed boundary artifacts for later reuse across the evaluated statevector mode, and stabilizer-hybrid mode. Its planner treats continuation legality as a narrow correctness guardrail, abstains when reuse is unprofitable, and keeps each reuse, abstention, or refusal decision auditable through EXPLAIN-style traces, meaning inspectable planner reports with provenance and realized-cost summaries. Across real world quantum application workloads QuWARP delivers 2.95x-32.84x speedups over this work's main direct per-task Qrack denominator on reuse-positive workloads. These results show workload-level reuse planning improves repeated-run simulation while keeping unsupported handoffs auditable and out of the execution path.

Summary. This research investigates anomalous Hall transport in a correlated uranium Weyl semimetal, demonstrating that the transport properties do not simply follow the onset of magnetic order. Model / system. The physical system is the uranium ferromagnet UPS, modeled as a correlated uranium Weyl semimetal. The complexity arises from the U 5f electrons, which exhibit itinerant/localized duality due to strong spin-orbit coupling and correlations.

Key observables. Anomalous Hall conductivity ($\sigma_{xy}$), low-energy U 5f spectral weight evolution, magnetization ($M(T)$), and the intrinsic anomalous Hall conductivity calculated via DFT.

Abstract

Understanding how electronic correlations reshape topological states remains a central challenge in quantum materials. Here we investigate the uranium ferromagnet UPS using magnetotransport, angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy, thermodynamic measurements, and first principles calculations. Resonant photoemission reveals narrow U-$5f$ spectral weight at the Fermi level coexisting with broad incoherent states, consistent with the itinerant/localized duality characteristic of uranium $5f$ electrons. The anomalous Hall conductivity reaches approximately $4.5\times10^{2}\,Ω^{-1}\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$, yet does not simply follow the ordered magnetic moment. Temperature dependent photoemission reveals relatively little change across $T_C=118$~K, followed by a pronounced redistribution of low-energy $5f$ spectral weight below approximately 90~K. First-principles calculations identify a symmetry protected Weyl crossing with pronounced Berry curvature and yield an intrinsic anomalous Hall conductivity of approximately $9.6\times10^{2}\,Ω^{-1}\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$. These results demonstrate that magnetic order, correlated electronic reconstruction, and anomalous Hall transport develop over distinct but overlapping temperature ranges, revealing how strong correlations reshape topological transport in a uranium Weyl semimetal.

Static classical-quantum-entanglement trade-offs: an entropic converse and single-letter characterizations

Authors: Mark M. Wilde
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.23298v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper provides a comprehensive theoretical framework for characterizing the static capacity region of bipartite quantum states, defining the fundamental trade-offs between classical, quantum, and entanglement resources. It establishes a unified entropic converse and derives explicit formulas for these trade-offs, particularly for states undergoing erasure. The results advance the understanding of quantum resource quantification beyond simple asymptotic bounds.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides fundamental, rigorous bounds on how quantum resources can be converted, which is crucial for understanding the limits of quantum communication protocols and resource theories.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper determines the direct static capacity region of a bipartite quantum state, which quantifies its asymptotic conversion rates into classical communication, quantum communication, and entanglement.

Main result. It provides a unified entropic converse for this capacity region and derives specific formulas for the achievable trade-off boundaries for various state types, such as those subjected to erasure.

Method. The analysis employs advanced quantum information theory tools, including deriving a unified entropic converse using strong subadditivity and a supporting-hyperplane characterization.

Model / system. The study focuses on bipartite quantum states ($ ho_{AB}$) and the resource trade-offs between classical bits, qubits, and ebits, considering protocols involving local instruments and memory states.

Key observables. Net rates $(C, Q, E)$ (classical bits, qubits, ebits); entanglement entropy ($h$); erasure probability ($p$).

Important parameters / regimes. Blocklength ($n$); erasure probability ($p$); entanglement entropy ($h$); resource consumption/generation rates.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes finite-dimensional quantum systems and relies on deriving bounds based on local instruments and resource conservation laws.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the full erased-state region and capacity slices, showing the convex hull of achievable rates derived from explicit constructions.

Paper structure. The paper builds from establishing the general unified entropic converse for the static capacity region, deriving the supporting-hyperplane formula, and then applying these results to specific state families (e.g., erased states, dephased states) to find exact characterizations.

Abstract

The direct static capacity region of a bipartite quantum state describes its asymptotic conversion into classical communication, quantum communication, and entanglement, with all communication directed from Alice to Bob. This paper gives a unified entropic converse for this region. The proof treats generated and consumed resources simultaneously and derives all three information inequalities for one local instrument. Its main ingredients are no-signalling, the chain rule, strong subadditivity, and continuity of conditional entropy. A supporting-hyperplane characterization leads to the static capacity formula, which is helpful in determining single-letterization of the capacity region. For a pure state of entanglement entropy $h$ subjected to erasure with probability $p$, the complete region is the convex hull of $0$ and $(0,-ph,(1-p)h)$, plus the unit-resource cone. This statement holds for arbitrary collective instruments by using subset-entropy inequalities related to earlier methods used for the dynamic capacity region of the erasure channel. Complete characterizations also hold for symmetrically extendible states and locally flagged pure-state mixtures. For maximally correlated states, the optimization over local instruments admits an exact matrix formulation and an additive outer bound. Summary. This work analyzes how chains of magnetic skyrmions collectively depin from a random pinning landscape. Using simulations based on the stochastic Thiele equation, the authors show that the collective nature of the chain significantly lowers the critical depinning force compared to isolated skyrmions. This collective effect is robust and involves complex internal deformations during the transition.

Why it may be interesting. The study of depinning in disordered media, particularly the comparison between collective vs. single-particle behavior, touches upon concepts of glassy dynamics and critical phenomena relevant to many-body physics in disordered potentials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the collective depinning dynamics of interacting dipolar skyrmion chains driven through a weak, quenched random pinning landscape.

Main result. The depinning threshold is found to be significantly lower (by a factor of three) for the collective chain compared to an isolated skyrmion, and this collective effect is robustly independent of the chain length.

Method. The dynamics are simulated by numerically solving the stochastic Thiele equation, comparing results at zero temperature versus finite temperature.

Model / system. The system models dipolar skyrmion chains as an elastic polymer interacting with a quenched random pinning potential. The dynamics are governed by the stochastic Thiele equation, incorporating pair interactions and pinning forces.

Key observables. Depinning threshold (critical force), transition rounding width, effective mobility, and Hall angle.

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature (T=0 vs 300 K), chain length (N=50 to 200), and applied magnetic field (30 to 40 mT).

Assumptions / limitations. Non-bonded skyrmion forces are ignored, and the pinning potential is assumed to be weak enough to keep the skyrmion structure rigid.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate snapshots of the system, transport parameters (velocity, mobility, Hall angle) versus driving force, and the evolution of internal deformations during depinning.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theoretical model using the stochastic Thiele equation, analyzes the depinning threshold and transition rounding at different temperatures, and concludes by detailing the sequence of internal deformations observed in longer chains.

Abstract

The dynamics of interacting dipole skyrmion chains in a weak pinning landscape is studied through numerical solutions of the stochastic Thiele equation. Chains of different lengths with periodic boundary conditions are driven across quenched attractive pinning sites under different magnetic fields. A clear threshold spin current is identified as the critical depinning force. Three main results emerge: (i) for chains of 50 skyrmions or more, the depinning threshold is independent of chain length and remains close to one third of that of an isolated skyrmion. (ii) The transition rounding is only partly thermal: the width changes from w = 0.334 +/- 0.004 decades at T = 0 to 0.633 +/- 0.002 at 300 K, while the intrinsic Larkin correlation length remains about 2.6 bonds below kBT ~ V0/25. (iii) The width is nearly unchanged up to kBT ~ 0.14 V0, but increases almost fourfold by kBT ~ 0.23 V0, mainly through broadening of the creep regime. It is independent of chain length, ruling out finite-size rounding. Increasing the field from 30 to 40 mT changes the transition shape, widening its core by 26% while shortening the creep tails. For chains of about 200 skyrmions, depinning proceeds through a sequence of internal deformations - growth of the Larkin length and roughness, transient bond stretching, and subsequent recovery of mobility and Hall angle. These collective effects are relevant for skyrmion-based devices in which chains, rather than isolated textures, act as information carriers.

Detection of signals in presence of noise through Josephson junction switching currents

Authors: O. V. Pountougnigni, R. Yamapi, C. Tchawoua, V. Pierro, G. Filatrella
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.24533v1

Summary. This work investigates using Josephson junction switching currents to detect weak sinusoidal signals buried in Gaussian noise. By analyzing the switching times under controlled bias ramps, the authors quantify detection performance using the K-C index. They conclude that this switching current collection method is robust, showing that faster ramp rates enhance the signal detection capability.

Why it may be interesting. The use of nonlinear, dissipative quantum devices (JJs) as sensitive detectors for weak signals in noisy environments is highly relevant to quantum sensing and measurement theory, bridging condensed matter physics with signal processing.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of detecting a weak sinusoidal signal embedded within significant Gaussian noise using the switching currents of a Josephson junction (JJ).

Main result. The analysis demonstrates that collecting the switching currents as the bias current is ramped is a robust detection technique, with performance improving significantly for faster ramp rates.

Method. The detection efficiency is quantified using the Kumar-Carrol index (K-C index), which estimates the Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) by analyzing the average and variance of the switching times.

Model / system. The physical system is an underdamped Josephson junction modeled by a Langevin equation. The analysis involves simulating the junction's dynamics under a time-dependent bias ramp, incorporating both signal and noise terms.

Key observables. Switching currents/times ($ au_{sw}$), Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) estimated by the K-C index ($d_{KC}$), and the dependence of these metrics on the ramp rate ($v$) and driving frequency ($\omega$).

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis explores the effects of the bias ramp rate ($v$), the signal amplitude ($\gamma_{ac}$), and the driving frequency ($\omega$).

Assumptions / limitations. The K-C index is used as a proxy for SNR, and the analysis relies on the resistively shunted and capacitively junction model.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the dependence of the K-C index and average switching time on the driving frequency ($\omega$) for both coherent and incoherent strategies, and show how these metrics change as a function of the bias ramp speed ($v$).

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the governing Langevin equation for the JJ. It then details the two detection strategies (coherent vs. incoherent) and uses the K-C index to quantify performance. The core of the work analyzes how the ramp rate affects this detection capability, concluding that faster ramps are beneficial.

Abstract

Josephson junctions can be employed to reveal a sinusoidal signal in presence of Gaussian noise. To mimic realistic setups, the detection is performed linearly ramping the bias current until a switch to the finite voltage occurs; the analysis of the resulting switching currents can be exploited to decide about the presence of the harmonic drive. The signal is applied in two conditions: with an unknown initial phase (incoherent strategy) and with a known initial phase (coherent strategy). In both conditions, the analysis of the efficiency of the detection, performed through the signal-to-noise ratio, as estimated by the Kumar-Carrol index, shows that the dependence upon the Josephson junction ramp rate is beneficial, especially for relatively fast speed. One can conclude that the collection of the switching currents is a robust technique, and thus it is possible to exploit the advantages of a predetermined finite time to collect the data.
