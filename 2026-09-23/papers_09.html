Anisotropic Surface State Band Splitting and Low Energy Flat Bands in 3d Correlated Topological Kondo Insulator Candidate FeSb$_2$

Summary. This experimental study uses ARPES to probe the electronic structure of the correlated material FeSb2, a Kondo insulator candidate. Key findings include observing anisotropic splitting in surface states and identifying two distinct, low-energy flat bands. These results offer new insights into the material's correlated electronic behavior and its potential topological nature.

Why it may be interesting. The detailed mapping of surface states and the identification of sharp, low-energy flat bands in a correlated material like FeSb2 provide crucial experimental benchmarks for theoretical models attempting to describe topological superconductivity or Kondo physics in strongly correlated systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the electronic structure of FeSb2, a correlated narrow-gap semiconductor candidate for a 3d-electron Kondo insulator, focusing on the origin of its low-temperature resistance plateau and the nature of its surface states.

Main result. The research revealed clear anisotropic splitting in the surface states and the presence of two distinct low-energy flat-band-like features ($\sim 127$ meV and $\sim 70$ meV) at low temperatures.

Method. The primary method employed was systematic high-resolution laser-based Angle-Resolved Photoemission Spectroscopy (ARPES) performed at various temperatures.

Model / system. The physical system is FeSb2, which crystallizes in the orthorhombic marcasite structure and is being treated as a correlated topological Kondo insulator candidate.

Key observables. Anisotropic splitting of surface states ($eta_1, eta_2$), two flat-band features (FB1 at $\sim 127$ meV, FB2 at $\sim 70$ meV), and temperature-dependent spectral weight enhancement.

Important parameters / regimes. Low temperatures ($<6$ K, down to 1.5 K), binding energies ($\sim 127$ meV, $\sim 70$ meV), and momentum cuts along high-symmetry directions ($\Gamma–Z, \Gamma–X$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on ARPES measurements, which are highly sensitive to surface properties, and the topological origin of the states remains unresolved.

Figures summary. Figures detail the crystal structure, Fermi surface mappings showing $eta_1/eta_2$ sheets, and band structures confirming the anisotropic splitting and the momentum dependence of the two observed flat bands (FB1 and FB2).

Paper structure. The paper first characterizes the material's transport properties (resistance plateau) and then uses ARPES to map the electronic structure, detailing the anisotropic surface splitting, followed by the specific observation and characterization of the two distinct low-energy flat bands.

Abstract

FeSb$_2$ is a correlated narrow-gap semiconductor that has often been discussed as a $3d$-electron Kondo insulator candidate and exhibits a low-temperature resistance plateau with possible surface-dominated conduction. We carried out a systematic high-resolution laser-based angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES) study of FeSb$_2$ to investigate its electronic structure. The surface states around the zone center show clear anisotropic splitting. When the temperature is lowered into the resistance plateau regime ($<6\,\mathrm{K}$), the surface states remain robust, but their photoemission peaks become much sharper and gain spectral weight. Two distinct flat-band-like features are observed at low energy. One is located at $\sim$127 meV below the Fermi level, which exists only along a specific high-symmetry direction, while the other is located at $\sim$70 meV below the Fermi level and is present along all the measured momentum cuts around the zone center. These results provide new information to understand the renormalization effects, the resistance plateau, and the topological nature of FeSb$_2$.

Benchmarking higher-ranking multipoles and polarizability tensors for small molecular systems

Summary. This paper benchmarks the accuracy of various quantum chemical methods for calculating higher-order molecular properties, including quadrupole moments and polarizabilities, for small molecules. By comparing results from HF, MP2, CCSD, and numerous DFT functionals against CCSD(T) references, the authors identify the best-performing functionals for holistic accuracy. The findings provide critical guidance for developing reliable computational models for intermolecular forces.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly impacts the accuracy of intermolecular interaction models used in AMO physics, as accurate potential energy surfaces and interaction parameters rely on correctly calculated multipole expansions.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The accurate modeling of intermolecular interactions requires calculating molecular properties beyond simple dipolar terms, necessitating the inclusion of higher-ranking multipoles and polarizabilities.

Main result. The asymptotically corrected hybrid GGA B97-3-AC and PBE0-AC were found to be the best-performing density functionals for simultaneously describing dipole, quadrupole, and polarizabilities across a set of molecules.

Method. The study performs a holistic performance analysis by calculating molecular properties using various electronic structure methods (HF, MP2, CCSD, and multiple DFTs) against high-level CCSD(T) reference data.

Model / system. The work focuses on small, non-spin-polarized molecules, providing reference data for 73 different compounds. The calculations involve determining molecular dipole and quadrupole moments, as well as dipole-dipole and quadrupole-quadrupole polarizabilities.

Key observables. Molecular dipole moments, quadrupole moments, dipole-dipole polarizabilities, and quadrupole-quadrupole polarizabilities.

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis considers the impact of basis set choice, the need for asymptotic corrections (GRAC scheme), and the correlation level (HF, MP2, CCSD).

Assumptions / limitations. The paper assumes that accurate intermolecular interaction models require simultaneous high accuracy across multiple multipole moments, and it notes that the choice of finite field strength must be carefully managed.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the need for higher-order multipole calculations for intermolecular forces, presents CCSD(T) benchmarks, performs a comparative analysis of various DFTs across all four property types, and concludes with recommendations on basis sets and functional performance.

Abstract

Molecular properties govern how molecules interact with one another or external fields. Accurate molecular properties are essential for constructing intermolecular interaction models, and to achieve high accuracy we need to go beyond leading-order (dipolar) terms. This work presents CCSD(T) references for 73 small non-spin-polarized molecules for molecular dipole and quadrupole moments, and dipole-dipole and quadrupole-quadrupole polarizabilities. Using these results, we provide a holistic performance analysis of molecular properties computed with HF, MP2, CCSD, and a wide range of density-functional methods. Additionally, we investigate in detail what levels of basis sets are required to simultaneously describe all properties in the data set. The best-performing density functionals for all four properties are the asymptotically cor- rected hybrid GGAs B97-3-AC, closely followed by PBE0-AC. Surprisingly, modern meta- GGAs and range-separated functionals perform inconsistently, with large errors in the quadrupo- lar properties. These results have direct implications for methods for intermolecular interac- tions, such as symmetry-adapted perturbation theory based on density functional theory, or for the quality of properties and interactions in machine-learning datasets. Finally, for DFT methods, the Jensen aug-pcseg-2 basis set is a computationally efficient alternative to more established basis sets, but for generating correlated references, third-row elements benefit from core polarization, and double augmentation is strictly necessary for quadrupole-quadrupole polarizabilities.

Can Chemically Inspired Parameter Initialization Mitigate Barren Plateaus in Variational Quantum Eigensolvers?

Summary. This paper investigates how chemically informed parameter initializations can help Variational Quantum Eigensolvers (VQE) avoid barren plateaus (BPs) during molecular simulations. By analyzing the local gradient variance, the authors prove that starting near physically relevant states (like HF or MP2) leads to a favorable, polynomially scaling optimization landscape. This highlights the practical advantage of incorporating chemical knowledge into quantum algorithm design.

Why it may be interesting. It directly tackles a critical scaling issue in quantum chemistry algorithms, providing theoretical evidence that physical intuition (chemical initialization) can overcome generic quantum optimization barriers.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the major obstacle of barren plateaus (BPs) in Variational Quantum Eigensolvers (VQE) used for molecular electronic structure calculations.

Main result. Chemically motivated initializations (HF or MP2) mitigate local barren plateaus, showing polynomial decay of gradient variance with system size, unlike the exponential decay seen with random initializations.

Method. The authors analyzed the local gradient variance of the VQE cost function using theoretical bounds and statistical fitting techniques to compare scaling behaviors.

Model / system. The model is molecular electronic structure calculation using the UCCSD ansatz within the VQE framework. The Hamiltonian is the second-quantized electronic Hamiltonian, and the optimization is performed over variational parameters $\theta$.

Key observables. Local gradient variance ($V_q$), energy expectation value ($E_n$), and the scaling behavior (polynomial vs. exponential) of this variance with system size ($q$).

Important parameters / regimes. The initialization centers ($\theta_c$) include Hartree-Fock (HF) and MP2 amplitudes. The analysis focuses on the local optimization region rather than global behavior.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis focuses on local barren plateaus, and the derived condition for BP-freedom relies on bounding the Hessian Frobenius norm and local derivatives.

Figures summary. Figures show trajectory-resolved local gradient variance ($ ext{Var}_{\max}$) for H-chains, comparing HF/MP2 initialization to random starts, and statistical fits (BIC values) confirming power-law scaling for chemical initializations.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the VQE framework and the BP problem. It then develops theoretical conditions (Theorem 1) based on local gradient variance bounds. The core contribution is demonstrating that chemically informed initializations satisfy these conditions, which is validated through trajectory analysis and statistical model comparison.

Abstract

Recent advances in the variational quantum eigensolver (VQE) have strengthened its promise for molecular electronic-structure calculations on near-term noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) devices. However, barren plateaus (BPs) remain a major obstacle to scaling VQE, as vanishing gradients can severely obstruct optimization at large scale. While most BP studies focus on randomly initialized circuits and global gradient behavior, practical quantum chemistry VQE typically uses chemically motivated initializations and local optimization around HF- or MP2-derived states. Although chemically informed initializations are widely expected to improve VQE trainability, how they mitigate local barren plateaus remains unclear. We therefore study the occurrence of local barren plateaus in molecular UCCSD-VQE, considering both chemically motivated initialization points and iterates along the corresponding optimization trajectories. In the benchmarks considered here, chemically initialized starting points and variational trajectories show polynomial decay of local gradient variance with system size instead of the exponential BP-like decay observed for random controls, indicating that chemical initialization mitigates barren plateaus in the local optimization regions and highlights the trainability advantage of chemically motivated VQE strategies.

Casimir force in a water nanolayer confined by graphene sheets

Authors: Wijnand Broer, Jure Dobnikar
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.23609v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work calculates the Casimir force between graphene sheets separated by a nanolayer of water, finding that the confinement significantly alters the force compared to bulk predictions. The deviation is attributed to both geometric confinement and surface interface effects, which are modeled using anisotropic dielectric functions. This research is vital for understanding quantum vacuum forces in liquid-graphene nano-devices.

Why it may be interesting. The study provides quantitative predictions for how nanoscale liquid structure (water) modifies fundamental quantum vacuum forces (Casimir), which is crucial for understanding quantum electrodynamics in confined geometries.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper theoretically investigates how the presence of a nanolayer of liquid water confined between two graphene sheets modifies the Casimir force between the sheets.

Main result. Confinement reduces the Casimir force by up to 26% at small thicknesses, and the bulk model significantly underestimates the force at larger separations due to interface effects, which exhibit non-monotonic behavior.

Method. The Casimir force is calculated using the Lifshitz formula, requiring the solution of Maxwell's equations via the transfer matrix method for an anisotropic gap.

Model / system. The system consists of two graphene sheets sandwiching an anisotropic water nanolayer. The water permittivity is modeled as thickness-dependent, distinguishing between confinement and interface effects.

Key observables. Casimir force ($\Pi_{ ext{Cas}}(a)$) and Casimir free energy ($ ext{E}_{ ext{Cas}}(a)$).

Important parameters / regimes. Water gap thickness ($a$), temperature ($T=300$ K), and the anisotropic dielectric tensor components ($\epsilon_{\parallel}, \epsilon_{\perp}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The water layer is modeled as an anisotropic 3D bulk medium, and the graphene sheets are treated as 2D using the local Kubo model.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the geometry of the confined system and compare the calculated Casimir pressure under confinement versus the bulk model predictions, highlighting the magnitude of the interface effect.

Paper structure. The analysis proceeds by deriving the force using the Lifshitz formula, modeling the anisotropic permittivity of the confined water using effective medium approximations, and then comparing the results to the bulk limit to quantify confinement and interface effects.

Abstract

Casimir forces, arising from quantum and thermal fluctuations, depend strongly on the electromagnetic susceptibilities of the interacting materials. Here, we theoretically investigate the Casimir force between two graphene sheets separated by a nanometer-thin layer of liquid water. We find that, at thicknesses up to 3.4 nm, the confinement reduces the Casimir force by up to 26 % compared to the bulk case. At larger thicknesses, the bulk model underestimates the Casimir force by up to 42 % due to the interface effect. This effect does not decrease monotonically as a function of the layer thickness: it increases up to a maximum at 70 nm and it slowly declines to the bulk limit only at 2 microns. We separately assess the effect of nanoscale confinement on the electronic response of graphene, which has no effect on the Casimir force.

Characterizing local unitary equivalence of multipartite states by local unitary Bargmann invariants

Authors: Yi Shen, Lin Chen, Shao-Ming Fei
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.22902v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical paper investigates when local unitary invariants (LUBIs) are sufficient to determine if two multipartite quantum states are locally unitarily equivalent. The authors find that this equivalence holds for a specific class of states (CLC states) only if the states' single-party marginals are non-degenerate. When degeneracy occurs, the LUBIs fail to distinguish between non-equivalent states.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a precise mathematical tool (LUBIs) to distinguish between physically equivalent states (LU orbit) and states that merely share certain algebraic invariants, which is fundamental for characterizing entanglement structure.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to characterize the local unitary (LU) equivalence of multipartite quantum states using Local Unitary Bargmann Invariants (LUBIs).

Main result. The LUBIs are sufficient to guarantee LU equivalence for multipartite Completely Locally Commutative (CLC) states only when their single-party marginals are non-degenerate; otherwise, counterexamples exist.

Method. The authors use algebraic invariants (LUBIs, defined via multivariate traces) and structural properties (CLC condition) to establish necessary and sufficient conditions for state equivalence.

Model / system. The study focuses on general $n$-partite quantum states ($ ho_{A_1\cdots A_n}$), with specific attention paid to the subclass of Completely Locally Commutative (CLC) states.

Key observables. Local Unitary Bargmann Invariants (LUBIs), which are multivariate traces of the state and its single-party marginals.

Important parameters / regimes. The degeneracy status of the single-party marginals is the most critical parameter determining the validity of the LUBI criterion.

Assumptions / limitations. The sufficiency of the LUBIs is strongly dependent on the assumption that the single-party marginals are non-degenerate; the analysis is most rigorous in the CLC state setting.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining the general problem of LU equivalence to focusing on CLC states. It establishes sufficiency under non-degenerate marginals (using LUBIs) and then provides concrete counterexamples (e.g., 3-qubit systems) demonstrating failure when marginals are degenerate.

Abstract

Local unitary (LU) equivalent states are crucial for understanding the structure of quantum entanglement. We investigate the LU equivalence based on the LU Bargmann invariants (LUBIs). We consider for which states the LUBIs are sufficient to ensure the LU equivalence. We focus on a class of multipartite states with complete local commutativity, called CLC states. We first show that for multipartite CLC states, all the LUBIs are sufficient to ensure the local permutation equivalence, if their single-party marginals are diagonal and non-degenerate. By virtue of the fact that the CLC property remains unchanged under local operations, we verify that the LUBIs are also sufficient to ensure the LU equivalence of multipartite CLC states whose single-party marginals are non-degenerate. Furthermore, when the single-party marginals are degenerate, we propose concrete examples of two-qudit and three-qubit systems, showing that there are states which share all the LUBIs but are not LU equivalent.

Charge-4e Superconducting Ground State without Pair Condensation: Exact Quartet Dynamics, Rigorous Order, and a Microscopic Route

Authors: Jin-Tao Jin, Pengfei Li, Yi Zhou
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.24445v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper establishes the theoretical existence of a charge-4e superconducting ground state that maintains robust four-electron order while suppressing conventional two-electron pairing condensation. Using exact solutions and strong-coupling expansions, the authors provide rigorous proofs and microscopic derivations demonstrating this exotic superconducting phase stability.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a highly detailed, mathematically rigorous framework for stabilizing higher-order superconducting phases (charge-4e) against competing lower-order instabilities (charge-2e), which is crucial for understanding exotic superconductivity in correlated materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the theoretical challenge of establishing a superconducting ground state characterized by coherent charge-4e order while ensuring that lower-charge pairing channels (charge-2e) remain uncondensed.

Main result. The authors rigorously prove the existence of a charge-4e superconducting phase exhibiting quartet Off-Diagonal Long-Range Order (ODLRO) without charge-2e ODLRO, even deriving its stability from microscopic hopping terms.

Method. The analysis employs exact solutions for minimal parent Hamiltonians, strong-coupling perturbation theory, and rigorous mathematical bounds (like the linked-cluster argument) to diagnose order and stability.

Model / system. The study focuses on a direct charge-4e superconductor modeled using parent Hamiltonians defined on connected graphs, which are subsequently analyzed via effective low-energy Hamiltonians resembling XXZ models.

Key observables. Quartet ODLRO ($\lambda(4)_{ ext{max}}$ extensive) vs. charge-2e ODLRO ($\lambda(2)_{ ext{max}} = O(1)$), charge-4e phase stiffness ($ ho_4$), and inverse quartet compressibility.

Important parameters / regimes. Filling fraction ($ u_4$), coupling strengths ($U_0, K, t$), and lattice dimensionality ($d \ge 2$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on specific parameter regimes, such as the strong-coupling limit ($\lambda \ll 1$) for the microscopic realization, and the assumption of bipartite lattices for certain stability arguments.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the exact quartet order, the structure of the parent Hamiltonian, and the microscopic derivation path, showing eigenvalue scaling for quartet vs. pair correlations.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by first solving a minimal parent Hamiltonian exactly, establishing the quartet ODLRO. It then proves the result for a finite-range fermionic model using rigorous theorems, and finally derives a strong-coupling microscopic realization using electron hopping and pair hopping terms.

Abstract

A direct charge-\(4e\) superconductor exhibits coherent four-electron order while every charge-\(2e\) pairing channel remains uncondensed. We establish three complementary results. First, building on the \(η\)-clustering states and bipartite parent of Yoshida and Katsura, we formulate and exactly solve a minimal two-term parent on any connected graph. Its fixed-number ground states have quartet off-diagonal long-range order (ODLRO) without charge-\(2e\) ODLRO, while an exact mapping to classical hard-core exclusion dynamics yields the full fixed-sector gap and a branch of quartet-density modes. Nonzero quartet stiffness and vanishing inverse quartet compressibility identify this \(z=2\) parent as a phase-separation boundary. Second, for a finite-range fermionic family with explicit quartet transfer and sufficiently large onsite penalty, we rigorously prove quartet ODLRO without charge-\(2e\) ODLRO at half quartet filling, both at the hypercubic XY point for \(d\geq2\) and throughout a finite XXZ interval on the square lattice. Third, we derive a strong-coupling realization using only electron hopping and two-body interactions. With local gap \(U_0\), pair hopping \(K\) generates quartet motion at order \(K^2/U_0\), whereas electron hopping \(t\) first contributes at order \(t^4/U_0^3\); charge-\(2e\) excitations remain gapped at \(O(U_0)\). On bipartite lattices, positive inverse quartet compressibility opens an asymptotically controlled homogeneous \(z=1\) regime with short-ranged pair correlations, while negative curvature drives phase separation.

Circuit Hypernetworks for Quantum-Augmented Diffusion Language Models

Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces HyperQ, a novel quantum-augmented architecture for diffusion language models. It solves the computational bottleneck of large quantum circuits by restricting the circuit structure to the IQP family, allowing for efficient, classical readout calculation. The resulting model significantly boosts language understanding performance across multiple benchmarks by incorporating token-specific quantum information processing.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a concrete, computationally efficient architectural blueprint for integrating quantum circuits into deep learning models, specifically by controlling the complexity of the quantum readout to remain polynomial in the number of qubits.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the computational challenge of integrating quantum circuits into large language models while maintaining tractability as the circuit width increases.

Main result. The proposed HyperQ architecture successfully integrates quantum residual branches, achieving state-of-the-art performance on multiple benchmarks by scaling circuit width up to 64 qubits.

Method. It uses a hypernetwork to emit token-conditioned parameters for a quantum residual branch, which is structured using the two-body instantaneous-quantum-polynomial (IQP) family.

Model / system. The system is a frozen masked-diffusion language model backbone augmented by trainable quantum residual branches. The quantum computation is restricted to an IQP structure defined on a fixed sparse skeleton (ring + chord layers).

Key observables. Average benchmark scores (e.g., 54.30 at 64 qubits), and the expectation values $\langle Z_q angle$ measured for each qubit.

Important parameters / regimes. Circuit width (number of qubits, $n$), tested at 16, 32, and 64 qubits; the coupling strengths ($\omega_E$) are restricted to remain below $\pi/2$.

Assumptions / limitations. The method relies on restricting the circuit to the IQP family to ensure the readout cost scales only linearly ($\Theta(n)$) with qubit count, and that the backbone remains frozen during training.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the HyperQ pipeline, showing the insertion of the quantum residual branch into the transformer block and the emission of circuit coordinates. One figure compares the constant readout cost ($\Theta(n)$) of the fixed skeleton versus the quadratic cost ($\Theta(n^2)$) of an all-to-all skeleton.

Paper structure. The paper introduces HyperQ, details the quantum residual branch structure using a hypernetwork, explains the mathematically tractable IQP readout mechanism, and validates the approach by showing performance gains across multiple downstream language model benchmarks.

Abstract

Language models can be adapted by changing the computations applied to individual tokens. Quantum circuits offer one such approach, but evaluating wider circuits inside a large model can be computationally demanding. Here we introduce HyperQ, which adds token-conditioned quantum residual branches to a frozen masked-diffusion language model. A quantum residual branch is a module in each transformer block that reads a token's hidden state, emits the coordinates of that token's circuit, executes it, and adds the measured values back through a residual connection. The backbone remains frozen, and only the added branches are trained. Within each branch, a lightweight circuit hypernetwork emits token-specific rotation angles, coupling strengths, and measurement axes in a shared sparse circuit structure. The required expectation values have an exact classical expression whose evaluation cost grows linearly with the qubit count, enabling circuits from 16 to 64 qubits to be trained within a 1.1-billion-parameter backbone. Across downstream benchmarks, increasing circuit width raises the average score from 47.65 to 54.30. At 64 qubits, HyperQ exceeds the backbone and its low-rank-adapted counterpart by 4.71 and 3.67 points, respectively. HyperQ is fine-tuned on 20,000 prompt-response pairs, compared with 200,000 for the classical baselines. These findings support token-conditioned circuit emission as a tractable architectural approach to quantum-augmented language modelling.

Abstract

A potential path forward is Quantum Machine Learning (QML), which aims to leverage quantum computing in conjunction with classical machine learning to enhance computing efficiency and the expressiveness of models. In this paper, two different quantum classifiers - Variational Quantum Classifier (VQC) and Quantum Kernel Support Vector Machine (QSVM) - are compared with three classical classifiers as baseline classifiers - Logistic Regression, Support Vector Machine (SVM), and a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) - on the Breast Cancer Wisconsin dataset. These results indicate that for small structured datasets, classifiers based on quantum computing have not yet surpassed well-tuned classical counterparts. In some respects (e.g., benign-class recall), they perform competitively, though not on malignant-class recall, where the VQC in particular performed worse than the classical baselines, which is worth further investigation on real quantum computers.

Concatenated Composite Pulses Beyond Local Residual-Error Preservation

Authors: Masamitsu Bando
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.22728v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper develops advanced theory for designing concatenated composite pulses to maintain high fidelity against multiple systematic errors in quantum control. It shows that perfect local error cancellation is overly restrictive, deriving a global condition that must be met instead. This allows for the construction of robust quantum gates using inner pulses that only need to scale the elementary error generators by a common factor.

Why it may be interesting. It provides advanced, rigorous theoretical tools for designing high-fidelity quantum gates in physical platforms like NMR, extending the known limits of error correction beyond simple local error cancellation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of designing concatenated composite pulses that can compensate for multiple systematic control errors without relying on the strict condition of Local Residual-Error Preservation (REP).

Main result. It proves that local REP is not necessary, establishing a global necessary and sufficient condition for robustness, and provides a simple sufficient construction principle based on a common real scaling factor for inner pulses.

Method. The authors use first-order error generators derived from Taylor expansions of unitary operations to derive and analyze the global error composition rules for concatenated sequences.

Model / system. The model is based on quantum operations, specifically single-qubit gates in the context of magnetic resonance, subjected to systematic errors like Pulse Length Errors (PLE) and Off-Resonance Errors (ORE).

Key observables. Error generators ($K_x[U]$), the Global REP condition ($\sum P_{j-1}^\dagger \Delta K_{x,j} P_{j-1} = 0$), and the common proportionality factor ($q$).

Important parameters / regimes. Systematic error parameters ($\epsilon$ for PLE, $f$ for ORE), and the rotation angles/pulses used in the construction.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is restricted to first-order error terms and assumes fixed systematic error parameters across the sequence.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates fidelity maps for various concatenated constructions (e.g., CORPSE/SCROFULOUS) across the error parameter space, while Table I summarizes the cost (pulse count and total angle) for specific constructions.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first defining error generators and local REP, then deriving the necessary and sufficient global REP condition, and finally proposing and illustrating the simple sufficient condition using explicit constructions for PLE and ORE compensation.

Abstract

Residual-error preservation (REP) provides a sufficient rule for constructing concatenated composite pulses that compensate multiple systematic errors. We show that local REP is not necessary: deviations from REP can cancel across the full sequence. Using first-order error generators, we derive a necessary and sufficient condition for concatenation to retain the robustness of an outer sequence. A simple sufficient condition is that all inner pulses rescale the corresponding elementary error generators by a common real factor, not necessarily unity. We illustrate this principle using pulse-length and off-resonance errors, two systematic error models commonly considered in magnetic resonance. Short CORPSE and SCROFULOUS each compensate one error without preserving the other locally. Combining these inner pulses with suitable equal-rotation-angle outer sequences yields simultaneous first-order compensation of both errors, extending concatenated composite-pulse design beyond local REP.

Abstract

We study the self-adjoint extension of the Sierra--Rodríguez-Laguna (SR) $H=xp$-type Hamiltonian $\widehat H_{SR}$. Abstract

For two quantised oscillators prepared in their ground state, we compute the von Neumann and Rényi entropies of the reduced state and identify a frequency at which the gravitationally-induced entanglement vanishes due to cancellation between relativistic momentum squeezing and quantum-delocalisation-induced position squeezing. We further analyze the cancellation frequency and derive the approximate constraint $m_0 < \sqrt[3]{4}\, m_2$ for the spin-$2$ and spin-$0$ modes. This relation follows from demanding a stable harmonic oscillator description. Finally, we study gravitationally-induced concurrence in a non-Gaussian setup and show how quadratic gravity modifies the entanglement generated between two spatial superpositions. The concurrence can approach $\mathcal{O}(1)$ for certain choices of mass, spatial superposition, particle distance, and spin-$2$ and spin-$0$ modes. The concurrence will deviate from Newtonian gravity at certain particle separations, depending on the energy of the spin-$2$ and spin-$0$ modes. For example, spin modes as low as $0.0197$ eV become distinguishable from Newtonian gravity at a distance $d \sim 40 μ$m. This allows us to constrain the spin-$2$ and spin-$0$ masses in experiments.

Abstract

Recent discovery of reentrant superconductivity and elevated $T_c$ in Eu-substituted infinite-layer nickelates raises a critical question concerning the nature of Eu-4$f$ states and their potential interaction with Ni-$d$ electrons. Here we combine density functional theory with the dynamical mean-field theory (DFT+DMFT) calculations to investigate the valence and magnetic properties of Eu-ions in the nickelate 112 structure, explicitly treating the Coulomb repulsion in both Ni-3$d$ and Eu-4$f$ shells. We find a three-regime evolution of the Eu-4$f$ configuration driven by its Hund's rule coupling $J_{\rm H}$. The magnetic and mixed-valence Eu ionic state inferred by experiments only occurs for moderate $J_{\rm H}$, with coexisting local moments and strongly correlated $j=7/2$ quasiparticles, in contrast to the nonmagnetic state at small $J_{\rm H}$ and the fully spin-polarized divalent state at large $J_{\rm H}$. This local-itinerant duality arises from an effectively hole-doped orbital-selective Mott state induced by the intra-4$f$ charge transfer between $j=5/2$ and $7/2$ manifolds. It not only explains the origin of the mixed-valence and local moment behaviors of the Eu-ions observed experimentally, but also predicts low-energy 4$f$ quasiparticles that may hybridize with the Ni-3$d$ electrons and contribute to the elevated $T_c$. Our work provides a basis for understanding the unusual ferromagnetic and superconducting properties of Eu-substituted infinite-layer nickelates.

Abstract

We study the one-dimensional long-range self-avoiding walk in the grand-canonical ensemble where the statistical weight of a jump of length $r$ decays algebraically as $r^{-(d+σ)}$. Using large-scale Monte Carlo simulations with an efficient irreversible update scheme, we obtain high-precision estimates of the critical fugacity $z_c$, the universal Binder ratio $Q_N^c$, the correlation-length exponent $ν$, and the anomalous dimension $η$. For $σ>1$, the critical fugacity varies smoothly with $σ$, while the Binder ratio and the critical exponents remain consistent with the short-range universality class. For $σ\le 1$, by contrast, the results clearly depart from short-range behavior, identifying $σ=1$ as the boundary between long-range and short-range regimes. In the long-range Wilson-Fisher regime with $1/2 < σ\leq 1$, $η$ agrees with the long-range Gaussian-fixed-point prediction $η_{\mathrm{GFP}}=2-σ$, whereas $Q_N^c$ and $ν$ vary nontrivially with $σ$ and exhibit discontinuous jumps at $σ=1$. These findings are in good agreement with the recently proposed universality diagram in the $(d,σ)$ plane for long-range O$(n)$ models, with the self-avoiding walk corresponding to the $n\to0$ limit.
