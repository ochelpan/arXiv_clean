Avalanche Size and Interevent Time Statistics in Amorphous System

Authors: Thomas Muhren, Roberto Benzi, Mauro Sbragaglia, Jeannot Trampert, Federico Toschi

Summary. This theoretical paper analyzes the statistical mechanics of avalanches in amorphous materials using a field-theoretic model. It determines that both avalanche sizes and interevent times exhibit power-law scaling, and crucially, it derives a precise mathematical relationship connecting their respective scaling exponents. The work provides analytical predictions that successfully match numerical simulations of soft-glassy systems.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical derivation of scaling relations between two distinct statistical measures ($S$ and $t_i$) in a non-equilibrium, disordered system provides a powerful theoretical framework applicable to many complex physical processes.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the statistical properties of avalanches (size $S$ and interevent time $t_i$) in amorphous systems, with a specific focus on characterizing the interevent time statistics.

Main result. Both avalanche sizes and interevent times are found to follow power-law distributions, and their respective scaling exponents satisfy a precise theoretical relationship ($\gamma = 3 - 2\alpha$).

Method. The study employs a field theoretical model of a soft-glassy system under shear, deriving scaling laws by analyzing the evolution of the fluidity order parameter $\phi$ and its associated probability distributions.

Model / system. The system is a soft-glassy amorphous material modeled by coupled, nonlinear partial differential equations describing stress ($\Sigma$) and fluidity ($\phi$). The analysis utilizes simplified, space-independent versions of these equations.

Key observables. Avalanche size distribution $P[S] \sim S^{-5/4+\delta/2}$ and interevent time distribution $P[t_i]$, along with the correlation coefficient $r$ between $S$ and $t_i$.

Important parameters / regimes. The scaling exponent $\delta$ is a crucial parameter controlling the exponents for both $S$ and $t_i$; the critical value $\delta = 1/2$ is highlighted.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on significant simplifications, including assuming constant stress rate ($\dot{\Gamma} = 0.01$) and using approximations like the Self-Consistent Field Approximation (Hartree method) to solve the dynamics.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the stress evolution showing stress drops (avalanches), the joint probability density of $t_i$ and $S$, and the collapse of the conditional interevent time distribution $P[t_i|\lambda S_0]$ onto a Gamma distribution.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining the full dynamical model (stress and fluidity) to simplifying it into solvable forms. It then derives the power-law scaling for $P[S]$ and $P[t_i]$ separately, establishes the relationship between their exponents, and discusses the implications of the critical parameter $\delta$.

Abstract

The intermittent, stick-slip flow of amorphous systems is characterized by avalanches whose statistical properties display robust power-laws. In this paper, we study avalanche statistics in a field theoretical model of a soft-glassy system under shear. The order parameter in our model is the fluidity, which is related to the local rate of plastic events in a soft-glass. While avalanche sizes $S$ have been extensively characterized, the statistics of interevent times $t_i$ have remained largely unexplored. Here, we find that both $S$ and $t_i$ are power-law distributed and that their scaling exponents satisfy a precise relation. The model enables the analytical derivation of the scaling exponents and the reproduction of the behavior observed across a wide class of amorphous systems.

Summary. This paper analyzes collective STIRAP in interacting pairs of three-level atoms, revealing that the dynamics are governed by coupled dressed manifolds rather than a single dark state. The authors develop a framework showing that while Rydberg-Rydberg interactions shift the resonance, applying a specific frequency chirp can compensate this shift, thereby restoring efficient population transfer and enabling controlled quantum gate operations.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a sophisticated, manifold-based control scheme for interacting quantum systems, showing how tailored driving fields (chirps) can counteract intrinsic interactions to maintain coherent quantum operations like STIRAP.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates collective chirped STIRAP in interacting three-level atoms, focusing on understanding dynamics beyond simple dark-state following and compensating for Rydberg-Rydberg interaction-induced detuning.

Main result. The dynamics are governed by coherent evolution within coupled dressed manifolds, and efficient population transfer can be restored by applying a precisely calculated frequency chirp that compensates the interaction-induced energy shift.

Method. The authors use numerical simulations combined with a theoretical dressed-manifold description, projecting the full Hamiltonian onto gauge-invariant subspaces to analyze the coupled dynamics.

Model / system. The system is a pair of identical three-level ladder-type atoms, modeled by a symmetric six-state subspace. The Hamiltonian includes driving fields (Rabi frequencies) and a repulsive Rydberg-Rydberg interaction term ($V_{RR}$) shifting the doubly excited state.

Key observables. Population transfer to the doubly excited Rydberg state ($P_{33}(t)$), and the oscillatory dependence of this transfer on the peak Rabi frequency.

Important parameters / regimes. The interaction strength ($V_{RR}$), the chirp rate ($\alpha$), and the pulse timing relative to the interaction-induced resonance condition.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the rotating-wave approximation and the electric-dipole approximation, assuming the dressed-manifold structure remains robust despite the interaction shift.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the time evolution of populations, showing suppression of transfer with interaction (zero chirp) and restoration of transfer with the analytically derived chirp.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from setting up the microscopic Hamiltonian for the two-atom system to deriving the effective Hamiltonian in the symmetric subspace. It then develops the dressed-manifold picture to explain the oscillatory dynamics, culminating in the analytical derivation and numerical verification of the chirp required to counteract the interaction-induced detuning.

Abstract

We investigate collective chirped stimulated Raman adiabatic passage (STIRAP) in a pair of identical three-level atoms using a symmetric six-state model. Numerical simulations reveal a pronounced oscillatory dependence of the population transfer to the doubly excited Rydberg state on the peak Rabi frequency, indicating dynamics beyond the conventional single-dark-state description of STIRAP. To identify the underlying mechanism, we develop a dressed-manifold description based on gauge-invariant projections onto nearly degenerate instantaneous dressed manifolds. The analysis demonstrates that the wavefunction remains predominantly confined to a two-dimensional dark manifold while becoming transiently coupled to a neighboring bright manifold during the pulse-overlap interval. We further show that in the dressed-manifold representation, the Rydberg-Rydberg interaction modifies the collective resonance structure while preserving the dark-bright manifold dynamics responsible for the oscillatory transfer; an appropriately chosen frequency chirp compensates the interaction-induced detuning and restores efficient population transfer. This dressed-manifold picture provides a consistent interpretation of oscillatory collective transfer and establishes a framework for controlling adiabatic dynamics in interacting multilevel systems by linearly chirped STIRAP, opening a route toward manifold-based control of Rydberg-mediated quantum gates, correlated-state preparation, and other coherent operations in interacting atomic systems.

Intermittency in Wind-Driven Fires

Summary. This paper models wind-driven forest fires in 1D, revealing three distinct dynamical regimes governed by lightning frequency. It demonstrates that the intermediate regime exhibits intermittency, characterized by alternating periods of stable growth and chaotic, catastrophic events. The work provides quantitative scaling laws for fire sizes and gap distributions in this complex non-equilibrium system.

Why it may be interesting. The study of intermittency and the transition between different dynamical regimes (deterministic vs. chaotic) in a non-equilibrium system is highly relevant to understanding complex, self-organizing phenomena in physics, analogous to pattern formation or driven many-body systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the complex dynamics, particularly intermittency, in a one-dimensional model of wind-driven forest fires.

Main result. The model exhibits three distinct regimes controlled by the lightning rate, showing power-law scaling for gap sizes and evidence of coexisting deterministic and chaotic dynamics in the intermittent regime.

Method. The dynamics are analyzed by constructing a 1D forest-fire model and studying the time evolution of tree density and fire propagation under varying rates of tree growth and lightning strikes.

Model / system. The system is a 1D periodic ring representing a forest, where sites can be empty or occupied by a tree. Fire propagation is governed by an intensity mechanism that increases within a forest and decreases when jumping gaps. The dynamics are driven by tree growth (rate r) and random lightning strikes (rate f).

Key observables. Tree density $ ho(t)$, fire and gap size distributions, time interval distribution $Q(\Delta t)$ between catastrophes, and characteristic fire sizes.

Important parameters / regimes. Tree growth rate ($r$), lightning strike rate ($f$), and the system length ($L$). Key regimes are defined by the relative magnitudes of $f$ compared to $r/L$ and $L^{-\mu}$.

Assumptions / limitations. The model is treated as one-dimensional and periodic. The analysis relies on identifying distinct dynamical regimes based on the frequency of external forcing (lightning).

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the time evolution of tree density across the three regimes (quasi-deterministic, steady state, intermittent). They also show the distribution of fire sizes and the probability distribution of time intervals between major fire events.

Paper structure. The paper analyzes the system across three regimes defined by the lightning rate $f$: quasi-deterministic (rare lightning), steady state (frequent lightning), and the intermediate intermittent regime. The analysis focuses on characterizing the scaling laws and underlying dynamics in each regime.

Abstract

We construct a wind-driven forest-fire model in one dimension in which a fire can jump gaps between trees to ignite disjoint downwind forests. The size of a gap that a fire can jump depends on the fire intensity, which increases as the fire propagates through trees and diminishes as the fire jumps gaps. Trees grow on empty sites at rate $r$ and lightning strikes each site with rate $f$. When $f\ll r/L$, where $L$ is the system length, lightning is sufficiently rare that quasi-deterministic dynamics arises where all trees are consumed when a lightning-induced fire occurs. For $f\gg L^{-μ}$ with $μ\approx 0.8$, lightning is sufficiently frequent that a steady state is reached, but with unexpected behaviors for the forest- and gap-size distributions. Intermittency arises in between these regimes, with coexisting temporal domains of deterministic and chaotic dynamics.

Loss-Tolerant Quantum Position Verification for Metropolitan Area Networks

Authors: Wen Yu Kon, Niccolò Bigagli, Andrew Conrad, Taylor Shields, Fatih Kaleoglu, Ignatius William Primaatmaja, Alexander Craddock, RJ Pisani, Mael Flament, Jude Seeber, Omar Amer, Charles Lim, Xinhua Ling, Rob Otter, Kaushik Chakraborty, Mehdi Namazi

Summary. This paper introduces and experimentally validates a Loss-Tolerant Quantum Position Verification (LT-QPV) protocol designed for metropolitan quantum networks. By shifting entanglement preparation to the prover, the scheme achieves security independent of channel loss. The successful demonstration proves a scalable, physics-backed method for certifying spacetime coordinates for critical applications.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the practical implementation of quantum security primitives for networking, which is a key area bridging fundamental quantum information theory with applied quantum networking hardware.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To provide a physically secure 'spacetime seal' guaranteeing that a digital event occurred at an approved location and time, overcoming the spoofability of classical cryptographic methods.

Main result. The authors experimentally demonstrated a Loss-Tolerant Quantum Position Verification (LT-QPV) protocol that certifies position using commercially available hardware, achieving high security independent of channel loss.

Method. The protocol uses quantum entanglement and exploits principles like the no-cloning theorem, requiring the untrusted prover to prepare and distribute entangled qubits to a quantum verifier node.

Model / system. The system involves an untrusted prover (P), a quantum verifier ($V_{Q0}$), and a classical verifier ($V_{C1}$). It utilizes entangled photon pairs transmitted over metropolitan-scale quantum channels.

Key observables. Positional uncertainty (measured in meters), Quantum Bit Error Rate (QBER), and Prover efficiency ($\eta$).

Important parameters / regimes. The security proof is established for finite-size against QPT adversaries in the QROM model; performance was demonstrated with $N \geq 2.55 imes 10^5$ rounds.

Assumptions / limitations. The security proof assumes the verifier possesses a trusted measurement device, and the full loss-tolerance claim relies on classical and quantum communication traveling at the speed of light.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the experimental setup (P, $V_{Q0}$, $V_{C1}$), the spacetime diagram of the protocol, and performance metrics like QBER and efficiency compared to other QPV schemes.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the need for spacetime sealing, details the LT-QPV protocol, presents the security proof in the QROM, and concludes with experimental demonstrations of position certification and latency budgeting.

Abstract

A spacetime seal, a cryptographic guarantee that a digital event has occurred at an approved location and time, can augment a digital signature with location attestation for legal, financial, and regulatory use cases. In adversarial settings any purely classical realization of such a seal can be spoofed. Quantum position verification (QPV) offers a physics-based solution, exploiting the no-cloning theorem and the no-signaling principle to certify a party's spacetime coordinates. While the feasibility of QPV has been recently shown via entanglement- and coherent light-based protocols, achieving loss tolerance for these schemes substantially increases implementation complexity at metropolitan scales. Here, we introduce and experimentally demonstrate a loss-tolerant QPV (LT-QPV) protocol whose security is independent of channel loss. We prove finite-size security against quantum polynomial-time entangled adversaries in the quantum random oracle model instantiated with cryptographically secure hash functions. Implemented entirely with commercial off-the-shelf components, our system certifies position within 22 minutes of net data collection time against a restricted adversary, with a clear path to real-time certification (<1s) with upgraded hardware. Our architecture, requiring only a single quantum verifier node alongside classical infrastructure, is naturally compatible with metropolitan-area quantum networks, establishing the foundations for scalable, physics-backed spacetime certification as a deployable service.

Abstract

Bell-certified retardance polarimetry from CHSH correlators

Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.23914v1

Summary. This paper shows that the coincidence measurements used to test quantum nonlocality via CHSH inequalities can simultaneously provide a quantitative measure of sensitivity for estimating an unknown phase shift (retardance). By deriving the Fisher Information directly from these correlators, the authors establish a robust, data-driven certificate for quantum polarimetry using standard Bell-test setups.

Why it may be interesting. It bridges two distinct areas of quantum physics—nonlocality testing (Bell tests) and quantum metrology—by showing that the data set for one can quantify the sensitivity for the other, which is highly relevant for experimental quantum optics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To demonstrate that coincidence data collected during a Bell test can serve as a quantitative metrological certificate for estimating a local birefringent phase (retardance polarimetry).

Main result. The CHSH data provides a rigorous lower bound on retardance sensitivity, which is useful even when the Bell inequality is not violated. This shows that Bell violation and metrological usefulness are distinct resources.

Method. The authors derive the classical Fisher Information directly from experimentally accessible CHSH correlators and establish a lower bound relating this FI to the derivative of the CHSH parameter.

Model / system. The system involves polarization-entangled photons generated via SPDC, where one photon undergoes a local birefringent phase encoding. The analysis uses a two-outcome measurement model based on CHSH correlators.

Key observables. CHSH correlators ($E_{xy}$), the CHSH parameter ($S$), and the derived Classical Fisher Information ($F_C$).

Important parameters / regimes. The phase shift ($\phi$), visibility ($v$), and the degree of Bell violation.

Assumptions / limitations. The certification assumes unbiased single-party marginals and relies on the measurement settings being trusted (semi-device-independent).

Figures summary. Figure 1 conceptually links the CHSH correlators, the Bell parameter, and the Fisher Information. Figure 2 shows how the FI bound behaves under visibility loss, and Figure 3 illustrates the finite-data confidence bound approaching the asymptotic limit.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theoretical framework by deriving the FI bound from CHSH correlators, provides mathematical appendices detailing the derivations (e.g., using Cauchy-Schwarz), and concludes by discussing the practical implications for quantum polarimetry.

Abstract

Bell-CHSH measurements with polarization-entangled photons are commonly used as nonlocality witnesses. Here we show that the same coincidence data can also provide a quantitative metrological certificate for optical retardance sensing. For a local birefringent phase encoded on one photon, we derive the classical Fisher information directly from experimentally accessible CHSH correlators and obtain a lower bound expressed in terms of the measured correlations and the phase derivative of the CHSH parameter. In the unbiased-marginal two-qubit regime, the canonical CHSH analyzer settings are shown to be simultaneously Bell-optimal and Fisher-optimal, saturating the quantum Fisher information for retardance estimation in the ideal case. We further analyze reduced visibility, phase noise, analyzer misalignment, and finite coincidence counts, and construct conservative lower confidence bounds that can be evaluated without quantum-state tomography. The results also clarify that Bell violation and metrological usefulness are distinct resources: nonzero Fisher information can persist below the CHSH-violation threshold. The protocol therefore enables a standard Bell-test data set to provide both a nonlocality benchmark and a statistically controlled sensitivity certificate for quantum polarimetry.

Observable-targeted variational quantum simulation of Hamiltonian dynamics

Summary. This paper introduces an observable-targeted variational quantum simulation technique that focuses parameter updates on matching the evolution of specific target observables rather than the entire quantum state. By leveraging an error identity, the method significantly extends the simulation time achievable with limited measurement resources compared to standard approaches. This advancement is particularly valuable for simulating complex strongly correlated electron systems like the Hubbard model.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly improves the resource scaling of quantum simulation algorithms by making the variational optimization process physically informed, which is crucial for simulating complex, strongly correlated materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of simulating quantum Hamiltonian dynamics when only the expectation values of a few observables are needed, rather than the full quantum state.

Main result. The proposed observable-targeted variational quantum simulation method extends the median time within the target-error tolerance by up to a factor of 4.2 compared to standard VQS at equal measurement budgets.

Method. It updates variational circuit parameters by minimizing the mismatch in the equations of motion for a selected set of target observables, guided by an exact error identity.

Model / system. The method is benchmarked across several models, including six-qubit spin systems (Ising, XXZ, XY), the Fermi-Hubbard model, and molecular Hamiltonians (LiH).

Key observables. The target observable A, and the set of observables O used to construct the update dictionary (often involving repeated commutators with the Hamiltonian).

Important parameters / regimes. Measurement budgets (shot counts $S$), target error tolerance ($\epsilon$), and the number of parameters ($p$) and dictionary size ($K$).

Assumptions / limitations. The method relies on the assumption that the error can be effectively projected onto the subspace spanned by the target observables, and it uses finite measurement statistics.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the cycle of the targeted VQS: observables guide selection, the quantum processor measures data, and the classical processor determines parameter velocities to match the target observable dynamics.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theory by contrasting standard VQS (state-level objective) with the proposed observable-targeted approach (seminorm minimization). It details the mathematical formulation using the target error identity and applies the method to various physical models.

Abstract

Standard variational quantum simulation seeks to reproduce the evolution of the full quantum state, although many applications require only the expectation values of a few observables. We study a variational method for pure-state Hamiltonian dynamics that updates circuit parameters to reproduce the evolution of selected expectation values. An exact error identity guides the choice of observables, motivating a construction based on repeated commutators of the target with the Hamiltonian. For Pauli observables and Pauli-rotation circuits, the update can be estimated without ancillary qubits or controlled operations for overlap estimation. Across six-qubit spin, fermionic, and molecular benchmarks, the targeted update extends the median time within the target-error tolerance by up to a factor of $4.2$ relative to standard variational quantum simulation at equal shot budgets. These results show that directing the variational update toward the target observable can extend accurate simulation without increasing the measurement cost per time step.

Summary. This paper introduces the Otto Information Engine (OIE), an enhancement to the standard quantum heat engine using continuous Maxwell's demons. The OIE overcomes the limitations of stochastic work output and temperature constraints by ensuring deterministic operation. This allows for the extraction of work with zero fluctuations and potentially exceeding the Carnot efficiency limit.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses fundamental limits in quantum thermodynamics by engineering a mechanism (the OIE) to decouple work output from stochastic thermal fluctuations, which is a key area in non-equilibrium statistical mechanics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Standard quantum heat engines are limited by reservoir temperatures, suffer from stochastic work output, and are bounded by the Carnot efficiency.

Main result. The proposed Otto Information Engine (OIE) achieves zero work fluctuations and can surpass the standard Carnot efficiency, enabling arbitrary work extraction.

Method. The authors analyze the quantum Otto cycle, introducing continuous Maxwell's demons (CMD) and employing counterdiabatic driving techniques to control the system's trajectory deterministically.

Model / system. The model is a quantum Otto cycle implemented with a spin-1/2 working system, enhanced by two continuous Maxwell's demons (CMD1 and CMD2) to form the OIE.

Key observables. Work output ($\langle W angle$), work fluctuations ($\delta w^2$), and efficiency ($\eta$).

Important parameters / regimes. Energy level gaps ($\omega_c, \omega_h$), reservoir temperatures ($T_c, T_h$), and measurement times ($ au_c^M, au_h^M$).

Assumptions / limitations. The OIE relies on the deterministic operation enforced by the CMDs and the use of counterdiabatic Hamiltonians to eliminate quantum transitions.

Figures summary. Figure 5 compares the OHE and OIE performance (power, fluctuations, efficiency) versus driving time, showing the OIE's superior, fluctuation-free operation.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from analyzing the limitations of the standard Otto Heat Engine (OHE) to introducing the OIE via Maxwell's demons, deriving the ideal zero-fluctuation work output, and finally confirming enhanced power and zero fluctuations using Monte Carlo simulations.

Abstract

Generally, the work output of stochastic heat engines is governed by the stochastic trajectory distribution and the energy spectrum. Because the trajectory distribution depends on the thermal reservoir temperatures, the work output is not only constrained by temperature but is also susceptible to thermal fluctuations. To address these limitations, we introduce two continuous Maxwell's demons into a quantum Otto cycle, forming an Otto information engine (OIE). We demonstrate that the work output of the OIE depends solely on the energy level gap, enabling arbitrary work extraction while eliminating work fluctuations, thereby ensuring cycle-to-cycle identical work output. Furthermore, we show that the engine's efficiency can surpass the standard Carnot efficiency even after accounting for the energy cost of demon's memory erasure. Finally, we show that the OIE can deliver superior output power even when the demon's measurement time is taken into account, and the corresponding Monte Carlo simulation has been executed.

Carrier-mediated spin helices in low-dimensional systems: Josephson-interference features and correlation renormalization

Authors: Yung-Yeh Chang, Chen-Hsuan Hsu
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.24502v1

Summary. This work theoretically explores how spin helicity, mediated by itinerant carriers interacting with localized moments, establishes collective magnetic order in low-dimensional conductors. The authors predict that this helical order leaves distinct, measurable fingerprints in the Josephson current interference patterns and modifies the electronic structure of the remaining gapless carrier modes. This provides a powerful, field-tunable transport diagnostic for characterizing spin textures in correlated materials.

Why it may be interesting. The connection between collective magnetic order (helical spin texture) and measurable transport signatures (Josephson interference) in low-dimensional correlated electron systems is highly relevant to understanding exotic superconducting and magnetic phases in modern 2D materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates carrier-mediated spin helices in low-dimensional systems, focusing on how these helices manifest in Josephson interference features and how they affect carrier correlations.

Main result. The study establishes that coupled-wire carrier-localized-spin systems exhibit RKKY-driven collective helical order, which can be detected via characteristic modifications in Josephson interference patterns and reconstructed carrier-sector signatures.

Method. The analysis employs a combination of bosonization techniques, variational treatments, and theoretical modeling of anisotropic Superconductor-Normal-metal-Superconductor (SNS) Josephson junctions.

Model / system. The models include interacting one-dimensional (1D) and two-dimensional (2D) coupled-wire systems, such as those mimicking twisted bilayer WTe2. The physics is described using Luttinger liquid theory, incorporating carrier-localized spin interactions.

Key observables. Josephson interference features (central-lobe width, side-lobe asymmetry), gap reconstruction in the carrier sector, and correlation exponents ($\chi_c^n(r)$, $\chi_s^\mu{}_n(r)$).

Important parameters / regimes. Applied in-plane magnetic field, coupling strengths, and the dimensionality (1D vs. 2D) of the coupled wires.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on low-energy effective theories, bosonization, and variational approximations for gap estimation.

Figures summary. Not specified, but the text describes analyzing modifications to Josephson interference patterns and reconstructed local-density-of-states exponents.

Paper structure. The paper systematically develops the theory by first introducing the four classes of coupled 1D/2D systems, then developing the RKKY interaction and helical order, followed by analyzing the Josephson interference signatures, and finally examining the reconstructed carrier-sector observables.

Abstract

Carrier-induced helical magnetism can arise in interacting one-dimensional systems coupled to localized moments. Here, we investigate the coupled-wire sliding-Luttinger-liquid counterparts with and without valley degrees of freedom, using isolated Tomonaga-Luttinger liquids as reference limits. The carrier spin susceptibility mediates a Ruderman-Kittel-Kasuya-Yosida interaction that selects the helical ordering wave vector, while interchannel correlations lock the relative helix phases and stabilize a collective quasi-two-dimensional texture. Using a variational treatment, we estimate the helix-induced partial gap and analyze the reconstruction of the remaining gapless carrier modes. To probe the spatial texture through transport, we incorporate the helix into an anisotropic superconductor-normal-metal-superconductor Josephson junction motivated by the coupled-channel structure of twisted bilayer WTe$_2$. We exploit flux focusing near the junction interfaces to make an applied in-plane field a controlled probe of the spin helix. Relative to junctions without the helix, the helical exchange field modifies the central-lobe width and relative lobe weights. In the transverse-channel geometry, it also produces field-dependent side-lobe asymmetry and central-peak displacement, which remain negligible in the matched references for the parameters considered. The reconstructed carrier sector also exhibits modified local-density-of-states exponents, spin-relaxation behavior, and competing correlations, with distinct signatures in isolated and coupled-wire systems. Our results connect collective helical order in coupled-wire systems to carrier-sector reconstruction and identify field-tunable Josephson interferometry as a complementary transport probe of the spatial spin texture.

Abstract

Scaling analog and digital Ising machines to larger problems requires overcoming finite device capacity and the cost of communication between devices. We present cluster mean-field theory (CMFT), a framework that partitions a large interaction graph into clusters sized to fit available hardware. Each cluster performs local updates independently, while interactions across cluster boundaries enter through periodically updated effective biases computed from boundary-spin averages. To reduce the error introduced by fixed cluster boundaries, we introduce dynamic partitioning, which cycles through multiple partitions so that interactions approximated by mean fields at one stage can act through instantaneous spins at another. On three-dimensional spin glasses and planted Pegasus instances, dynamic CMFT exhibits power-law decay of residual energy over sweep budgets. This shows that solution quality continues to improve with computational effort despite the mean-field approximation. An automated graph partitioner combined with a weighted recovery ratio provides a practical heuristic for selecting partition combinations on graphs without natural cut directions. We demonstrate CMFT on four GPUs with approximately four million p-bits, reaching comparable energy densities up to 15 times faster than a single-GPU implementation of the full graph. By coupling locally evolving clusters through programmable effective biases, CMFT provides a route to extreme-scale Ising machines on both analog and digital hardware.

Abstract

We show that in this regime the diagonal shift is more than a numerical stabilizer. It acts as a statistical filter for finite-sample generalization. At a fixed wave function, SR is ridge regression from tangent features to the centered local energy. Its residual is the expressivity gap, the part of imaginary-time evolution outside the current tangent space. This gap is orthogonal to the tangent space in population, but finite batches make it act as noise that SR can overfit. We further compare MS-SR and SR in paired online training continuations, with independent endpoint energy evaluations and a separate update-cost benchmark.

Abstract

Deep learning models are powerful but opaque. As quantum machine learning matures, the field faces a defining choice: build quantum models that are equally opaque, or exploit the mathematical structure of quantum mechanics to make them inherently interpretable. We show that the latter is possible. By tracking quantum mutual information~(MI), entanglement entropy, and state fidelity through the layers of a Quantum Transformer Block (\qtb{}), a fully-coherent variational circuit with quantum analogues of both attention and feedforward, we gain direct insight into how the model processes information: which tokens it attends to, when correlations form, and why predictions fail. These proof-of-concept results, obtained on small synthetic tasks, suggest that the physics of quantum computation can provide intrinsic interpretability signals with no direct classical counterpart, motivating study of whether this advantage persists at scale.

Abstract

We investigate the coupling dependence of ballistic energy transport and the emergent spin dynamics in an integrable Hermitian three-state chain that connects a clock interaction to a highly degenerate flag limit. By constructing a regular $R$-matrix to establish a globally conserved energy current, we analytically evaluate its full variance to obtain the exact, strictly positive leading high-temperature coefficient of the thermal Drude weight and the ballistic growth rate of the energy-correlation second moment. In the strong-coupling limit, the degeneracy is lifted by virtual transitions of a delocalized third-color spectator state, which generates an effective spin-$1/2$ XXZ Hamiltonian with anisotropy $Δ= -1/2$ and fundamentally selects the all-active two-color sector as the true ground state. For periodic boundaries, this virtual spectator motion introduces a positive length-changing XXZ supercharge squared that, for $L\ge4$, strictly annihilates all states within a finite, length-independent energy interval above the ground state. Consequently, we rigorously prove that the full periodic effective theory perfectly replicates the exact low-energy XXZ spectrum, including all state multiplicities, as well as its macroscopic bulk free-energy density.
