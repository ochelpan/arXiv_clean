Summary. This theoretical study examines the dynamics of a Hopf texture in a Bose-Einstein condensate when subjected to a flow mimicking a Navier-Stokes blow-up. The authors show that the texture reorganizes through a cascade of fold catastrophes, mathematically characterized by the universal signature of a Thom $A_2$ singularity. This connects topological defect dynamics in quantum gases to advanced catastrophe theory.

Why it may be interesting. This work bridges topological defects (Hopf textures) in quantum matter (BECs) with complex, singular fluid dynamics (Navier-Stokes blow-up), providing a detailed mathematical description of how topology responds to extreme non-linear forcing.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how a pseudospin Hopf texture, existing in a two-component Bose-Einstein condensate, responds to a flow analogous to a finite-time singular blow-up solution of the Navier-Stokes equation.

Main result. The texture undergoes fold-mediated creation and annihilation of preimage pairs, with the first transition exhibiting the characteristic signature of a Thom $A_2$ catastrophe.

Method. The dynamics are analyzed using an effective Madelung–Schrödinger theory incorporating a Faxén-like advective correction, and fold events are detected by analyzing the critical set of the mapping defining the texture's preimages.

Model / system. The system is a two-component Bose-Einstein condensate hosting a pseudospin Hopf texture, coupled to the viscous Navier-Stokes dynamics of its thermal cloud. The evolution is governed by a derived effective equation (Eq. 5).

Key observables. Hopf Charge ($Q_H$), fold-mediated pair creation/annihilation, and the local signature of a Thom $A_2$ catastrophe.

Important parameters / regimes. The ratio $\Pi$ of drive to intrinsic texture response, and the characteristic length scales $\ell_*$ and $\ell_r$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on using a smooth, prescribed surrogate flow ($\mathbf{U}$) for the actual blow-up solution, and the coupling term is phenomenological.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the evolution of the drive streamlines relative to the Hopf texture at different times, and summarize the count of fold events, showing the characteristic square-root opening for the first fold.

Paper structure. The paper develops the effective evolution equation by coupling the Hopf texture dynamics to the advective flow. It then analyzes the local reorganization of the texture by tracking preimages, identifying fold catastrophes via singularity analysis, and confirming the universal square-root opening signature.

Abstract

A two-component Bose--Einstein condensate can host a pseudospin Hopf texture while its thermal cloud obeys viscous Navier--Stokes dynamics. Motivated by OpenAI's reported finite-time singular solution, we drive such a texture with a surrogate of the collapsing core, using an effective Madelung--Schrödinger theory with a Faxén-like advective correction. The texture undergoes fold-mediated creation and annihilation of preimage pairs at conserved Hopf charge with the first one-to-three transition confirmed to obey the square-root opening of a Thom $A_2$

Rank-dependent optimal resetting in multiparticle search

Authors: Ron Vatash, Eden Goldfarb, Vladimir Yu. Rudyak, Yael Roichman
Type: both · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.23542v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: non-equilibrium universality 2/5 · quantum measurements 2/5 · analog quantum simulation 1/5 · driven-dissipative phase transition 1/5

Summary. This work analyzes how stochastic resetting optimizes the arrival time sequence of multiple searchers in complex physical systems. By establishing a non-interacting theoretical baseline, the authors quantify how interactions and environmental memory modify the optimal search strategy. The findings show that the benefit of active processes increases with the rank of arrival, providing insights into collective search dynamics.

Why it may be interesting. The focus on ordered statistics and the quantitative comparison between theoretical models and complex physical systems (like active matter) provides a rich framework for understanding non-equilibrium dynamics, which is relevant to open quantum systems modeling.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how stochastic resetting optimizes the ordered arrival sequence of multiple searchers in soft-matter and biological systems, moving beyond simple first-arrival kinetics.

Main result. Optimal resetting rates ($r^*_k$) increase monotonically with arrival rank ($k$) for non-interacting systems, and the benefit of active mechanisms like chemotaxis increases significantly with the arrival rank.

Method. The authors construct an exact finite-$N$ reference for non-interacting Brownian searchers and compare this baseline against simulations of physical systems (colloids, active particles) to isolate the effects of interactions and environmental memory.

Model / system. The study models search processes in soft-matter/biological contexts using Brownian motion, active Brownian particles, and chemotactic search fields. The core mathematical tool is stochastic resetting applied to the ordered first-passage time statistics.

Key observables. Mean ordered first-passage time $\langle T_{(k)} angle$, optimal resetting rate $r^*_k$, and the ratio $S_k$ comparing chemotactic vs. control arrival times.

Important parameters / regimes. Arrival rank fraction $\phi = k/N$, critical rank fraction $\phi_c \simeq 0.412$, and system parameters like diffusion coefficients and particle concentrations.

Assumptions / limitations. The initial non-interacting reference assumes independent searchers; comparisons to physical systems are used to isolate the effects of direct interactions and environmental memory.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the dependence of $\langle T(k) angle$ and $r^*_k$ on rank, population size ($N$), and initial spatial heterogeneity ($CV$). Later figures compare chemotactic results against non-interacting controls.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the theoretical framework using non-interacting Brownian searchers to derive the rank-dependent optimal resetting rate. It then extends this analysis by comparing these theoretical results to complex physical realizations, including colloidal experiments and chemotactic simulations, to quantify the role of interactions.

Abstract

In many soft-matter and biological systems, task completion relies on the cumulative arrival of multiple searchers rather than the speed of a single pioneer. The completion kinetics are therefore set not only by the first arrival, but by the full ordered sequence of first-passage times. Here, we determine how stochastic resetting optimizes these ordered arrivals for all arrival ranks. We construct an exact finite-$N$ reference for non-interacting Brownian searchers and obtain the mean ordered first-passage time $\langle T_{(k)} \rangle$ and its optimal resetting rate $r_k^*$. For searchers with identical initial conditions, $r_k^*$ increases monotonically with arrival rank and, with increasing population size, approaches the known large-$N$ quantile limit where a finite optimum appears only above a critical rank fraction $φ_c \simeq 0.412$. Spatial heterogeneity qualitatively reorganizes this sequence, shifting its maximum from late to early ranks even without particle interactions. We then compare this baseline with Brownian colloid experiments, interacting active Brownian particles, and a collective autochemotactic search with persistent environmental memory. Across these systems, sensitivity to resetting increases strongly with arrival rank, while deviations from appropriate non-interacting references reveal the influence of direct interactions, finite return overhead, and environmental memory. Our results show that optimal resetting in multiparticle search is governed by the required completion rank and must be evaluated relative to protocol- and geometry-matched baselines.

Summary. This work analyzes the Mott transition in a modified Periodic Anderson Model using advanced theoretical techniques like slave-rotor mean-field theory and DMFT. By incorporating short-range spatial correlations via a two-site cluster extension, the authors refine the transition point prediction. Crucially, they map the resulting physics onto a generalized SSH chain, allowing for a topological characterization of the correlated insulating state.

Why it may be interesting. The connection between the Mott transition physics and the topological characterization via the SSH chain provides a powerful, structured framework for understanding correlated electron behavior beyond simple mean-field treatments.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the Mott-Hubbard metal-insulator transition (MIT) driven by strong electronic correlations in a modified periodic Anderson model.

Main result. Short-range spatial correlations shift the transition point, and the metallic phase's hopping pattern maps onto a topologically trivial generalized Su-Schrieffer-Heeger (SSH) chain.

Method. The study employs slave-rotor mean-field theory, extended to a two-site cluster, and utilizes Dynamical Mean-Field Theory (DMFT) to evaluate the self-energy.

Model / system. The system is a three-orbital modified periodic Anderson model, described by a Hamiltonian involving localized interacting f-orbitals and itinerant c/cM orbitals coupled via hybridization terms.

Key observables. The vanishing of the rotor expectation value ($\Phi$) diagnoses the Mott transition, and the quasi-particle weight $Z = |\Phi|^2$ is monitored.

Important parameters / regimes. Key parameters include the ratios $U/D$, $t_\perp/D$, and $V/D$, with the transition depending on these ratios.

Assumptions / limitations. Approximations include using slave-rotor mean-field theory and extending it to a minimal two-site cluster to account for short-range correlations.

Figures summary. Figures compare the rotor quantification ($\Phi$) between single-site and two-site results, and show the DMFT $f$-electron self-energy ($ ext{Im}\Sigma_f(\omega)$) and the corresponding auxiliary-chain hoppings for both metallic and insulating regimes.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the model and uses SRMFT to analyze the transition. It then extends the analysis using a two-site cluster and DMFT, mapping the resulting self-energy onto an auxiliary chain to explore the topological character of the transition.

Abstract

The Mott-Hubbard transition is a paradigmatic phenomenon driven by strong electronic correlations. We investigate this transition in a three-orbital modified periodic Anderson model using slave-rotor mean-field theory. A minimal two-site cluster extension is introduced to understand the minimal effects of short-range spatial correlations. In comparison to the single-site solution, short-range spatial correlations reveal a shift of the transition point. Going beyond the slave-rotor analysis, we also evaluate the dynamical self-energy and map it onto the boundary Green's function of a non-interacting semi-infinite auxiliary chain. For the metallic phase, the resulting hopping pattern corresponds to a topologically trivial generalized Su--Schrieffer--Heeger chain. This construction provides a framework for exploring the possible topological character of the Mott transition in this model.

Summary. This paper addresses the critical challenge of measuring electromagnetic coupling strengths in large superconducting quantum processors. It introduces and rigorously compares four distinct numerical techniques—including avoided level crossing and induced EMF—to extract these couplings. The consistent results across these methods validate a robust methodology for characterizing complex, multi-scale quantum hardware architectures.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides essential, rigorous tools for characterizing the coupling landscape in superconducting quantum hardware, which is a prerequisite for scaling up quantum processors and understanding decoherence mechanisms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core challenge is accurately characterizing the electromagnetic coupling strengths between qubits and the surrounding package modes in large-scale superconducting quantum processors.

Main result. The authors developed and benchmarked four distinct numerical methods—avoided level crossing, EPR, induced EMF, and impedance matrix—showing they yield consistent coupling strength estimates with high accuracy.

Method. The paper proposes and compares four distinct numerical techniques to extract coupling coefficients by analyzing the system's electromagnetic response to changes in qubit parameters or by fitting the full impedance matrix.

Model / system. The system is a large-scale planar array (e.g., $10 imes 10$) of transmon qubits housed in a metallic package. The physics is modeled using coupled LC oscillators, analyzed via full-wave 3D electromagnetic solvers.

Key observables. Coupling strength ($g$), eigenfrequencies ($\omega_m, \omega_n$), energy participation ratio ($p_{mn}$), and induced electromotive force ($V_{n, ext{open}}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis spans multiple scales, from nanometer-scale Josephson junctions to centimeter-scale packages, and often involves operating near resonance ($\omega_m/\omega_n \approx 1$).

Assumptions / limitations. The methods rely on solving linear electromagnetic models and are sensitive to numerical errors inherent in multi-scale 3D domain simulations.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the physical setup, the comparison between the four extraction methods (EPR, EMF, etc.), and the mathematical framework relating the coupling to measurable quantities.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the multi-scale coupling problem, details the four proposed numerical methods (EPR, EMF, Impedance Matrix, Avoided Crossing), benchmarks these methods on a $10 imes 10$ array, and concludes by comparing the consistency of the results.

Abstract

High-fidelity control of superconducting quantum processors requires accurate characterization of electromagnetic coupling strengths among the device's constituent elements. Accurately extracting these couplings across large-scale architectures, presently featuring hundreds of qubits, poses a challenging multi-scale modeling problem. This requires resolving scales from the nanometer-scale geometry of Josephson junctions and their leads to the centimeter-scale size of the enclosing metallic packages. We present four numerical coupling extraction methods based on the avoided level crossing, the energy participation ratio, the induced electromotive force, and the impedance matrix. These methods are tailored to work with commercially available 3D electromagnetic solvers. We benchmark these techniques on a $10\times10$ array of transmon qubits, extracting their couplings to standing package modes. Our results show that these methods yield consistent coupling strengths with a maximum relative difference of less than 5%.

Strong Symmetry from Two-Point Correlations

Authors: Han Yan
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.23591v1

Summary. This theoretical work establishes that the strong symmetry of a mixed quantum state can be fully determined by measuring only its two-point correlation functions. By analyzing the covariance matrix derived from these correlations, the authors provide an algorithm to identify the maximal symmetry subgroup and its associated character. This is highly valuable because low-order correlations are more accessible experimentally than full state tomography.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, experimentally accessible diagnostic tool for characterizing symmetry in complex quantum many-body systems, directly relevant to understanding symmetry breaking in correlated materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses how to determine the exact 'strong symmetry' of a mixed quantum state using only low-order correlation functions, circumventing the need for full-state tomography.

Main result. It proves that for continuous onsite unitary actions of connected groups, identical two-point correlations are sufficient to determine the state's strong symmetry and its associated character.

Method. The method involves analyzing the charge covariance matrix derived from one- and two-point correlation functions to identify the generators of the maximal connected strong-symmetry subgroup.

Model / system. The theory applies to general quantum many-body systems ($N$ sites) whose symmetries are governed by a connected topological group $G$ acting via onsite unitary representations.

Key observables. One- and two-point correlation functions, two-site reduced density matrices ($ ho_{ij}$), and the charge covariance matrix $\Gamma( ho)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The continuous nature of the symmetry group $G$ and the requirement of onsite unitary actions.

Assumptions / limitations. The results are rigorously proven only for continuous onsite unitary actions of connected groups; the method fails for discrete symmetries.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates that selected two-site data can establish strong symmetry, even when some pairs are unmeasured.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the theoretical equivalence between equal two-point correlations and equal pair RDMs (Theorem 1), then provides a constructive algorithm (Theorem 2) using the covariance kernel to find the maximal symmetry subgroup $H_ ho$ and its character $\chi_ ho$.

Abstract

Strong and weak symmetries and their spontaneous breaking distinguish different forms of symmetry and order in mixed states. We show that complete one- and two-point correlation functions, equivalently all two-site reduced density matrices, determine exact strong symmetry for continuous onsite unitary actions of connected groups: states with identical correlations have the same strong symmetry and character. We also present an algorithm to determine the maximal connected strong-symmetry sub-Lie group and its character from two-point correlations. Because such correlations are more accessible than full-state tomography, the algorithm is readily applicable to experiments ranging from condensed-matter systems to quantum simulators and circuits. We also identify group and representation-specific cases in which fewer correlations suffice.

Vacancy aggregation enhances NV- spin coherence in diamond: a cluster-correlation-expansion study of multi-vacancy spin baths in semiconductors

Authors: Chikara Shinei
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.24295v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: correlated / nonlocal dissipation 2/5 · QC/QI experiment 1/5 · analog quantum simulation 1/5

Summary. The study uses cluster-correlation-expansion simulations to investigate how multi-vacancy spin baths affect NV- spin coherence in diamond. It overturns previous assumptions by showing that vacancy aggregation enhances $T_2$ by increasing the spectral disorder of the bath spins. The coherence time is found to depend critically on the variety of local site orientations rather than just the total density of defects.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a detailed theoretical understanding of decoherence mechanisms in solid-state qubits, specifically showing how structural disorder (aggregation) can be beneficial rather than detrimental to coherence.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To determine if the aggregation of vacancies in a semiconductor enhances or diminishes the spin coherence time ($T_2$) of an NV- center, contrary to previous assumptions.

Main result. Vacancy aggregation enhances NV- spin coherence by 2.7–4.4 times compared to isolated vacancies, because the resulting multi-vacancy bath has a lower fraction of bath pairs sharing a transition frequency.

Method. Cluster-correlation-expansion (CCE) simulations are employed to model the spin bath interactions and calculate the coherence time.

Model / system. The system is the NV- spin qubit in diamond, coupled to a surrounding 'spin bath' composed of multi-vacancy complexes (e.g., $ ext{V}n^0$). The coherence is governed by the resonant flip-flop fraction ($P$) between bath spins.}

Key observables. Hahn-echo coherence time ($T_2$), ensemble dephasing time ($T_2^*$), and the resonant flip-flop fraction ($P_{ ext{res}}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Paramagnetic spin density, zero-field splitting ($D$), and the fine-structure splitting of the bath spins.

Assumptions / limitations. The CCE calculation uses only published electron-paramagnetic-resonance (EPR) parameters, and the coherence is limited by the fraction of bath pairs sharing a transition frequency.

Figures summary. Figures compare $T_2$ and $T_2^*$ for various vacancy species (e.g., $ ext{V}^-$ vs. multi-vacancy) across varying concentrations, illustrating the enhancement factor.

Paper structure. The paper systematically compares the coherence time derived from isolated vacancies versus those from aggregated multi-vacancy clusters, quantifying the enhancement factor based on symmetry and site multiplicity.

Abstract

Annealing a semiconductor makes its vacancies mobile; they aggregate into multi-vacancy complexes that often carry spin. Such centres are a magnetic-noise source for any spin qubit among them, in silicon and silicon carbide as well as in diamond, and they are accordingly blamed for NV- decoherence in irradiated and implanted diamond, with two coherence records credited to removing them. Cluster-correlation-expansion simulations driven by published electron-paramagnetic-resonance parameters invert that attribution. At a fixed paramagnetic spin density a multi-vacancy bath gives a Hahn-echo coherence time 2.7-4.4 times longer than a bath of isolated negative vacancies, constant over three decades of concentration. A bath dephases the qubit because its spins exchange spin projections with one another, and two can exchange only if their transition frequencies match. A fine-structure splitting shifts those frequencies. Were every defect on the same crystallographic site, all would shift alike and still match: worth only a factor 1.3. Real defects occupy symmetry-equivalent sites pointing in different directions, so neighbours land at different frequencies and stop exchanging: a further 1.9. What governs the coherence time is therefore the fraction of bath pairs sharing a transition frequency, not the zero-field splitting. Vacancy aggregation extends NV- spin coherence rather than shortening it.

Abstract

clifford_qc is a Python research toolkit for constructing quantum models, comparing variational and subspace eigensolvers, and estimating measurement resources. A common sparse Pauli algebra connects Hamiltonians, density operators, gates, and candidate-selection observables, while dedicated interfaces handle circuit programs, execution, and statistical estimates. We introduce the representation with a worked gradient example and trace a molecular calculation through reusable model preparation, an independent solve, and optional reference validation. Shared measurement caches reuse outcomes across observables on the same reference state; packed coefficient storage and streaming expose a memory-recomputation tradeoff. Committed small-system benchmarks show how these interfaces support comparisons with explicit accuracy and cost assumptions. Fully commuting measurement reduces the shots needed to certify a fixed first-order energy functional, but entangling-gate time and connectivity can offset the saving or make the protocol inadmissible under illustrative device models. Other studies identify a subspace bias floor that more shots cannot remove, an indeterminate comparison of fermionic encodings across instances, and an approximate-restriction screen whose required comparison fails before sampling. Generated tables, provenance records, and manuscript checks connect the reported claims to their inputs, although evidence labels are not yet validated uniformly. The package supports reproducible method development; these results establish neither hardware performance nor a general simulation speedup.

Summary. This theoretical work explores how various external parameters—such as strain, doping, and interlayer coupling—can control the exotic state of altermagnetism in Hubbard-model systems. By comparing Hartree-Fock and RISB calculations, the authors demonstrate that these controls can generate fully spin-polarized electronic states without requiring conventional ferromagnetism. The findings highlight a rich interplay between structural geometry and electronic correlations in determining magnetic metal behavior.

Why it may be interesting. The detailed treatment of correlation effects ($U$) and the comparison between mean-field and slave-boson methods provide valuable insights into the breakdown of simple mean-field descriptions in strongly correlated magnetic metals.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how electronic correlations, doping, strain, and interlayer coupling control the emergence and stability of altermagnetism in metallic systems.

Main result. The research establishes that correlations, strain, doping, and stacking are complementary mechanisms to generate fully spin-polarized Fermi surfaces in altermagnetic metals.

Method. The authors compare results from Hartree–Fock theory with the more advanced rotationally invariant slave-boson (RISB) approach to treat correlation effects.

Model / system. The model uses a minimal Hubbard-type Hamiltonian applied to mono- and bilayer systems, featuring hopping terms and an on-site intraorbital Hubbard interaction.

Key observables. Altermagnetic order parameter, quasiparticle (QP) weight ($Z_m$), spin-polarized Fermi surface topology, and charge order.

Important parameters / regimes. Interaction strength ($U$), doping level, uniaxial strain magnitude, and interlayer coupling strength.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the model structure (AA/AB stacking), compare HF vs. RISB results for the altermagnetic transition at half-filling, and show QP dispersions for doping away from half-filling.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the system by first establishing the model, then comparing mean-field (HF) and correlated (RISB) treatments, and finally detailing the effects of doping and strain in both monolayer and bilayer geometries.

Abstract

Altermagnets combine vanishing net magnetization with a momentum-dependent spin-split electronic structure, providing a route to spin-polarized carriers without ferromagnetism. Here, we investigate how electronic correlations, doping, uniaxial strain, and interlayer coupling control altermagnetism within a minimal Hubbard-type model for mono- and bilayer systems. Comparing Hartree--Fock theory with the rotationally invariant slave-boson (RISB) approach, we demonstrate the robustness of altermagnetic order against quasiparticle renormalization and reveal a separation between the onset of magnetic order and the loss of quasiparticle coherence at stronger coupling. In the monolayer, doping produces a pronounced particle--hole asymmetry, while its combination with uniaxial strain generates a fully spin-polarized Fermi surface close to half-filling. In the bilayer, stacking that favors ferroic alignment of the layer altermagnetic order parameters allows their momentum-dependent spin splittings to combine constructively. Weak asymmetric doping additionally induces intra-unit-cell charge order, resulting in fully spin-polarized low-energy carriers. At a bilayer filling of five electrons in four orbitals, we find a continuous paramagnet-to-altermagnet transition followed, at stronger coupling, by an evolution toward a Mott-like regime with strongly suppressed quasiparticle weight. Our results establish correlations, strain, doping, and stacking as complementary means of controlling altermagnetic metals and generating fully spin-polarized Fermi surfaces.

Abstract

Abstract

Altermagnets, characterized by vanishing net magnetization and momentum-dependent spin splitting, provide a promising platform for next-generation Josephson devices. Here, we exploit the Josephson effect in superconductor-altermagnet-superconductor junctions and show how to engineer prescribed current-phase relations by device design. Based on these programmable Josephson potentials utilizing altermagnetism, we propose a new class of transmon-like superconducting qubits that combine large anharmonicity with enhanced robustness against decoherence via coherent two-Cooper-pair tunneling. We show that in the $2φ$-junction regime, this kind of qubit provides intrinsic protection against charge noise due to parity protection. Magnetic flux can be used to precisely control the qubit and, under appropriate bias, this architecture further suppresses charge and flux noise. Our results establish altermagnets as a versatile platform for Josephson-potential engineering and open a new route toward high-performance superconducting qubits combining high coherence, large anharmonicity, and broad tunability.

Abstract

The no-signalling principle lies at the heart of Bell-type experiments involving spacelike-separated parties. Although not all no-signalling correlations can be realized within quantum theory, the no-sinalling principle itself remains fundamental even for post-quantum theories, since its violation would imply superluminal signalling. In the spirit of device independent nature of the Bell inequalities, there has recently been growing interest in causal inequalities, which identify correlations admitting no causal explanation. Such correlations have been rigorously studied within the process-matrix formalism, which is built upon a constraint analogous to spacelike separation in Bell scenarios. We refer to this constraint as the single-interaction constraint, whereby each party interacts with the environment only once. Despite considerable effort, no fundamental guiding principle is currently known that characterizes the restrictions this constraint imposes on the correlations generated by Process Matrices. In this work, we address this gap by proposing a generalized causality principle for the bipartite case. We show that for certain cases, the generalised causality principle defines a strict subset of the set of all signalling correlations. This principle thus provides a fundamental limit on quantum and even post-quantum theories subject to the single-interaction constraint. By analogy with no-signalling, whose violation excludes certain causal structures among the parties, a violation of the generalised causality principle rules out a corresponding class of causal structures(causal structures with the single interaction constraint). More broadly, our techniques provide a general framework for deriving generalised causal constraints, applicable to a wide class of indefinite-causal-order scenarios.
