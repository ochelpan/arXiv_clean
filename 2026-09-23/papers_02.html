Summary. This paper develops a method to protect a specific, low-entanglement quantum state (a cluster-state scar) embedded in a complex many-body system during continuous local measurement. By deriving rigorous bounds on the conditional cluster fidelity, the authors establish the fundamental limits on state preservation. The work is significant for quantum information theory as it provides a concrete, mathematically verified framework for measurement-assisted quantum control.

Why it may be interesting. This work is highly relevant as it tackles the fundamental problem of state preparation and preservation in noisy, open quantum systems, providing concrete, mathematically rigorous bounds for measurement-based quantum control.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the challenge of nondemolition filtering—preserving a low-entanglement eigenstate (a 'scar') embedded in a complex many-body spectrum while undergoing continuous local measurement.

Main result. They provide a rigorous construction and finite-time bounds for the conditional cluster fidelity, showing that the preservation process is limited by the initial preparation overlap.

Method. The analysis combines theoretical derivations of fidelity bounds using conditional probability and numerical diagonalization up to eleven qubits to provide finite-size evidence.

Model / system. The system is an N-qubit ring governed by a local ring Hamiltonian, targeting an exact cluster-state eigenvector. The dynamics are modeled using a Lindblad generator incorporating continuous local monitoring.

Key observables. Conditional Cluster Fidelity ($F_C(t)$), No-observed-click probability ($S(t)$), and the initial overlap ($p$).

Important parameters / regimes. The monitoring rate ($\gamma$), system size ($N$), and the initial preparation overlap ($p$).

Assumptions / limitations. The results rely on an explicit local embedding construction and are supported by formal verification (Lean 4); the evidence is limited to finite sizes.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the energy spectrum showing the cluster eigenstate's position and the adjacent-gap ratio ($\langle r angle$), and time evolution plots comparing calculated fidelity/no-click probability against theoretical bounds.

Paper structure. The paper develops the theoretical framework by constructing the Hamiltonian, deriving the fidelity bounds, performing numerical diagonalization to show spectral evidence, and finally verifying the dynamics and bounds using formal proofs.

Abstract

Identifying a low-entanglement eigenstate inside a many-body spectrum and preserving it during measurement are distinct tasks. We construct an explicit local ring Hamiltonian with an exact cluster-state eigenvector and study continuous monitoring of its stabilizer defects. For arbitrary mixed inputs, the conditional cluster fidelity is the initial target weight divided by the no-observed-click probability. A positive defect-operator gap gives finite-time bounds that hold for noncommuting Hamiltonian dynamics, nonnormal effective generators and imperfect detection. At fixed total monitoring rate, the guaranteed exponent falls inversely with system size; high conditional fidelity does not remove the preparation cost set by the initial overlap. Exact diagonalization up to eleven qubits gives finite-size evidence for a cluster-state outlier in a chaotic spectral background. Independent matrix and trajectory calculations verify the dynamics and a conservative coherent-error bound. This construction specializes established scar embedding and nondemolition verification frameworks, with explicit measurement assumptions, finite-time guarantees and resource limitations.

Summary. This paper presents a novel technique to reconstruct the phonon distribution of trapped ions, overcoming the limitations of existing methods that fail beyond the Lamb-Dicke regime. By reformulating the problem as a filter-function inversion, the authors engineer robust measurement sequences using composite pulses. This allows for the high-fidelity characterization of highly excited motional states, which is crucial for advanced quantum information protocols.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a significant methodological advance for quantum simulation and quantum information processing using trapped ions, directly addressing the practical limitations of measuring highly excited motional states.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The core challenge is accurately characterizing the phonon distribution of trapped ions, especially when the system moves beyond the standard Lamb-Dicke regime where measurement techniques become ill-conditioned.

Main result. The authors developed a robust protocol using composite pulses and multiple sideband measurements to reconstruct phonon distributions up to $n=250$, successfully characterizing highly excited motional states and measuring heating dynamics.

Method. The problem is mathematically recast as a filter-function inversion problem, $P = F^{-1}M$, where optimal filter matrices are engineered using composite pulse sequences to ensure the matrix $F$ is well-conditioned.

Model / system. The physical system is trapped ions, whose motional modes are treated as quantum harmonic oscillators. The interactions are governed by laser-driven spin-motion coupling, analyzed using the interaction-picture Hamiltonian.

Key observables. Phonon populations $P(n)$, measured spin excitation probabilities $M_j$, and the reconstruction uncertainty derived from the filter matrix $F$.

Important parameters / regimes. Lamb-Dicke parameter ($\eta$), sideband orders ($k$), pulse duration ($T$), and the maximum measurable phonon number ($n$).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary assumption is that the measurement process can be accurately modeled by a linear inversion problem, which is achieved by engineering the filter function using composite pulses to mitigate nonlinearities beyond the Lamb-Dicke regime.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the process of filter engineering using composite pulses across different sideband orders; they show the reconstruction of Fock states and the measurement of heating dynamics for high-$n$ states.

Paper structure. The paper systematically introduces the limitations of existing methods, proposes the filter-function inversion framework, details the use of composite pulses to build well-conditioned filters, and validates the protocol by reconstructing high-number Fock states and measuring decoherence.

Abstract

Characterizing the phonon distribution of trapped-ion motional states is essential for many applications in quantum information processing, but existing methods become ill-conditioned beyond the Lamb-Dicke regime. We overcome this limitation by recasting phonon-state reconstruction as a filter-function inversion problem. Using composite pulses on the carrier and multiple sidebands, we engineer well-conditioned filters in phonon space to characterize pure and mixed states, study heating dynamics, and reconstruct Fock states up to $n=250$.

Summary. The authors study spin-mixing dynamics in a spin-3/2 Fermi gas trapped in three different potentials (ISW, HOT, PTP) to test the universality of power-law decay. They find that while the decay exponent ($\alpha$) is dictated by the specific spectral structure, the decay parameter ($\gamma$) is sensitive to interaction strength and trap size. This demonstrates that power-law decay is a robust feature not limited to the harmonic trap.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a general understanding of how spectral geometry dictates non-equilibrium dynamics in many-body quantum systems, suggesting that the underlying single-particle spectrum is a key determinant of relaxation rates, which is highly relevant for understanding decoherence in quantum simulators.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To investigate the origin and robustness of universal power-law decay of spin-mixing oscillations in a spin-3/2 Fermi gas, moving beyond the highly special case of the harmonic trap.

Main result. Power-law decay persists across qualitatively distinct spectral structures (ISW, HOT, PTP), and while the decay exponent ($\alpha$) is robustly determined by the spectral structure, the decay parameter ($\gamma$) is sensitive to the overall energy scale and interaction strength.

Method. The dynamics are analyzed using the Time-Dependent Hartree-Fock (TDHF) approximation applied to a spin-3/2 Fermi gas confined in three different 1D potentials.

Model / system. The system is a spin-3/2 Fermi gas governed by a Hamiltonian including single-particle ($H_0$) and interaction ($H_I$) terms. Three traps are compared: the Infinite Square Well (ISW), the Harmonic Oscillator Trap (HOT), and the Pöschl-Teller Potential (PTP).

Key observables. Coherent spin-mixing oscillations exhibiting power-law decay, characterized by the amplitude decay $A(t) = A_0 - \gamma t^\alpha$.

Important parameters / regimes. The decay exponent ($\alpha$) and decay parameter ($\gamma$). The comparison fixes the lowest excitation energy spacing ($\Delta E_1 = 1$) to isolate spectral structure effects.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the TDHF approximation and assumes the weak-interaction regime where interaction energy is smaller than the characteristic single-particle level spacing.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the comparison of $\alpha$ and $\gamma$ across ISW, HOT, and PTP traps, showing systematic differences in $\alpha$ but similar scaling behavior for $\gamma$ when varying trap width.

Paper structure. The paper compares the spin-mixing dynamics in three distinct 1D traps (ISW, HOT, PTP) by analyzing the power-law decay of oscillations. It first establishes the persistence of power-law decay across different spectral structures, then quantifies the dependence of the decay exponent ($\alpha$) and parameter ($\gamma$) on spectral geometry and interaction strength.

Abstract

Universal power-law decay of spin-mixing oscillations has been observed in a harmonically trapped spin-$3/2$ Fermi gas, whose exactly equally spaced spectrum represents a highly special case. To gain insight into the origin of this behavior, we investigate the dynamics in traps with increasing and decreasing level spacings, represented by the infinite square well and the Pöschl--Teller potential, respectively. Despite pronounced differences in their single-particle spectra, all systems exhibit power-law decay of coherent oscillations, described by $A(t) = A_{0} - γt^α$. The exponent $α$ remains insensitive to particle number, interaction strength, and the overall energy scale, but exhibits systematic differences among traps with distinct spectral structures. In contrast, the decay parameter $γ$ is strongly influenced by both the spectral structure and the overall energy scale. These findings demonstrate that power-law decay is not restricted to the harmonic trap but persists across qualitatively distinct spectral structures. The observed variation of $α$ among different traps further suggests that the underlying single-particle spectrum plays an important role in shaping the decay dynamics.

Abstract

An imperfect many-body revival does not by itself certify the entanglement of the returning state. We give a finite-record protocol for graph-edge localizable entanglement along scar wavepackets of a graph-dressed PXP chain. Abstract

Quantum emitters such as semiconductor quantum dots can provide entangled photon pairs emitting from a system of cascaded states. However, the resulting temporal correlation between the two photons from the three-level ladder system has been shown to set a limit on the achievable two-photon interference. Post-emission synchronization shows the recovery of maximum interference visibility; in prospective quantum networks this could be achieved via deterministic quantum memories.

Abstract

We then extend this framework to a generalized TC model by introducing an additional spin $1/2$ atom, enabling us to examine the influence of collective atomic degrees of freedom. The global dynamics are investigated both with and without an $XY$ spin interaction. Our results show that, under resonance conditions, the system exhibits efficient excitation exchange between the cavity field and the atomic subsystem, resulting in regular, coherent nonclassicality dynamics and enhanced field nonclassicality generation. Conversely, an increasing longitudinal magnetic field acts primarily as an effective detuning parameter rather than a direct exchange interaction. In this off resonant regime, the atom field coupling is substantially weakened, localizing excitations within the atomic subsystem. This localization suppresses coherent energy transfer to the cavity field, leading to a pronounced reduction in field nonclassicality, while the atomic subsystem retains the majority of the quantum correlations. Notably, atomic superposition states exhibit highly irregular spin dynamics, reflecting a detuning-induced redistribution of quantum correlations. Summary. This paper analyzes pulsed thermometry in NV centers operating under conditions of near-degenerate spin transitions. The authors find that while standard thermal-echo protocols fail to yield temperature signals, a nominal spin-echo timing reveals strong, measurable temperature-dependent oscillations. This suggests that dark-state leakage provides a robust mechanism for NV thermometry suitable for low-field, mobile applications.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the limitations of standard quantum sensing protocols by modeling complex, non-ideal spin dynamics (dark-state leakage) in a highly relevant solid-state quantum system, providing a pathway for practical, field-deployable quantum thermometers.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how finite-duration, three-level spin dynamics affect pulsed thermometry protocols in Nitrogen-Vacancy (NV) ensembles, particularly when the microwave Rabi frequency is comparable to the transition splitting.

Main result. Nominal spin-echo timing produces resolvable temperature-sensitive oscillations at the common-mode detuning, which is attributed to significant dark-state leakage, suggesting a robust method for low-field thermometry.

Method. The authors developed and analyzed an ensemble model of the triplet spin dynamics, comparing conventional thermal-echo timing against nominal spin-echo timing using pulsed ODMR measurements.

Model / system. The physical system is the spin-1 triplet manifold of NV centers in diamond. The model incorporates the near-degeneracy of the $|0 angle o|+1 angle$ and $|0 angle o{|-1 angle}$ transitions, utilizing bright-dark state mixing concepts.

Key observables. Temperature-sensitive signal amplitudes, oscillations in photoluminescence (PL) varying with total evolution time, and the common-mode detuning phase accumulation.

Important parameters / regimes. Near-degeneracy of transitions, Rabi frequency comparable to transition splitting, and the common-mode detuning $\Delta_c(T)$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis contrasts the ideal drive-dominated regime with the regime where splitting and Rabi frequency are comparable; the final model captures protocol-dependent trends.

Abstract

Nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers have emerged as a highly sensitive platform for spatially resolved temperature measurements. When the $\lvert0\rangle\to\lvert+1\rangle$ and $\lvert0\rangle\to\lvert-1\rangle$ transitions are nearly degenerate and the Rabi frequency is comparable to the transition splitting, the spin dynamics encountered during pulsed protocols for temperature measurement can significantly alter the intended accumulation of temperature-dependent phase. We investigate these triplet ($\lvert0\rangle$, $\lvert\pm1\rangle$) spin dynamics and their effect on pulsed thermometry protocols under these conditions. We find that the conventional thermal-echo timing produces negligible temperature-sensitive signal amplitudes at the common-mode detuning. However, we find that the nominal spin-echo timing produces resolvable oscillations at the common-mode detuning, leading to a strong temperature-sensitive carrier signal. This departure from the expected protocol behavior is qualitatively captured by an ensemble model of the triplet dynamics, which supports significant dark-state leakage as a plausible mechanism for the temperature-sensitive response. This mixing-enabled thermal echo uses a four-phase quadrature readout that resolves the signed oscillation frequency and is validated using independent sweeps of the microwave carrier frequency and fluid temperature. We further assess the ability of a four-refocusing-pulse (LDD4B) low-field dynamical-decoupling sequence to extend the NV coherence and find that while the echo retains a sensitivity to common-mode detuning, the effective phase-accumulation interval corresponds to approximately half the total evolution time. These findings could support the use of pulsed NV thermometry in biological or mobile applications where applying magnetic bias fields is impractical and microwave power may be limited.

Abstract

Negatively charged nitrogen-vacancy (NV$^-$) centers located a few nanometers below the diamond surface are key quantum defects for nanoscale sensing of external spins. However, the creation of shallow NV$^-$ centers with high yield remains a materials challenge. Here, we demonstrate that high-angle ion implantation enhances the creation efficiency of shallow NV$^-$ centers. Our results establish geometric control of ion implantation as a simple and broadly applicable approach to engineer shallow vacancy-related quantum defects.

Abstract

Memory is an inherent feature of the dynamics of physical systems. It typically emerges when the complex structure of the system is simplified during the modeling process, obscuring a part of its underlying behavior. Consequently, the correct description of such a physical model requires knowledge of not only its present, but also its past states, leading to non-Markovian dynamics. The resulting interactions depending on the system history, however, introduce a significant layer of mathematical complexity. For this reason, when the memory time is significantly shorter than the time scales characterizing the dynamics, it is typically neglected at the cost of losing information regarding its role in the system behavior. In this dissertation, I introduce a novel methodology, namely the effective mass approach, which bridges the Markovian and non-Markovian regimes and provides an accessible framework for analyzing systems with short memory. In this method, the non-Markovian system is approximated with its Markovian counterpart, in which the short-memory effects are encapsulated within an effective mass. I then utilize this approach to demonstrate that the impact of short memory on physical dynamics can be remarkably pronounced. In particular, I show that the directed transport of a Brownian particle in an environment exhibiting temporal correlations can be reversed relative to its behavior in a memoryless medium. Moreover, I propose a setup wherein memory serves as a~control parameter for the generation of random information bits. Collectively, the results of this dissertation underscore the critical importance of short-time correlations in microscopic systems.

Summary. The authors develop a fully quantum model for optomechanics to explain vacuum-mediated heat transfer, showing that neglected higher-order interactions are responsible for the coupling. They then apply quantum thermodynamics to demonstrate that by cyclically modulating this coupling, the system can function as a quantum heat engine capable of extracting net work.

Why it may be interesting. This work bridges quantum optics, cavity QED, and non-equilibrium statistical mechanics by providing a microscopic, quantum-mechanical framework to analyze energy flow (heat and work) in a mechanical system driven by vacuum fluctuations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To provide a microscopic explanation for the vacuum-mediated heat transfer observed between mechanical membranes in cavity optomechanics, and to demonstrate the system's potential as a quantum heat engine.

Main result. Higher-order optomechanical interactions generate phonon-phonon coupling that accounts for the observed heat transfer, and the system can extract net work by cyclically modulating this coupling.

Method. The analysis uses a fully quantum model, deriving effective Hamiltonians and solving the Lindblad master equation under the adiabatic approximation to calculate work and heat currents.

Model / system. The system is a cavity optomechanical setup with two movable membranes coupled to a quantum electromagnetic field. The dynamics are governed by an effective Hamiltonian derived from higher-order interactions, leading to phonon-phonon coupling.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the adiabatic approximation ($ heta/\Gamma \ll 1$) for the heat engine cycle and assumes idealized boundary conditions for the Casimir term initially.

Abstract

In cavity optomechanics, the standard large-detuning regime is commonly described by retaining only the radiation-pressure interaction, while higher-order mirror--field interactions are assumed to be negligible. This approximation, however, fails to provide a microscopic explanation for the vacuum-mediated heat transfer observed between the mechanical membranes in the experiment of Fong, et.al.,[Nature \textbf{576}, 243 (2019)], whose physical origin has remained under active debate. Here, using a fully quantum model, we show that the neglected higher-order optomechanical interactions naturally generate phonon-phonon coupling and quantitatively account for the observed energy exchange within the standard optomechanical framework. Building on this microscopic description, we further propose a protocol in which phonon-phonon interaction drives a cyclic process enabling net work extraction. Our results establish the fundamental role of higher-order optomechanical interactions and provide a microscopic framework for describing next-generation optomechanical experiments beyond the linear approximation.
