Raman Scattering Evidence for Fluctuating Kagome-Plane Moments in $\mathrm{B} \parallel [111]$ in Pr$_2$Zr$_2$O$_7$ pyrochlore

Authors: Yuanyuan Xu, Huiyuan Man, Nan Tang, Li Xiang, Sami Muhammad, Komalavalli Thirunavukkuarasu, Dmitry Smirnov, Satoru Nakatsuji, Natalia Drichko
Type: experiment · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.24696v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5 · quantum measurements 1/5

Summary. This work uses magneto-Raman spectroscopy on $ ext{Pr}_2 ext{Zr}_2 ext{O}_7$ to probe magnetic excitations in applied fields. The results show that while some magnetic moments behave predictably via Zeeman splitting, others exhibit strong spectral broadening. This broadening is interpreted as evidence of rapid, fluctuating magnetic moments confined to the kagome planes, offering a physical explanation for discrepancies in the material's magnetization.

Why it may be interesting. The observation of dynamic moment fluctuations (motional narrowing) in a specific sublattice, contrasting with static polarization elsewhere, provides direct spectroscopic evidence for complex, fluctuating magnetic interactions beyond simple single-ion Zeeman effects.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the exotic magnetic properties of the pyrochlore $ ext{Pr}_2 ext{Zr}_2 ext{O}_7$, focusing on the nature of magnetic moment coupling and spin-orbital dynamics.

Main result. Magneto-Raman spectroscopy reveals that while moments on the triangular sublattice polarize conventionally, the moments on the kagome lattice exhibit strong broadening indicative of rapid fluctuations between Zeeman-split levels when the field is applied along [111].

Method. Magneto-Raman spectroscopy was used to probe Crystal Electric Field (CEF) excitations of $ ext{Pr}^{3+}$ ions in applied magnetic fields up to 14 T.

Model / system. The system is the $ ext{Pr}_2 ext{Zr}_2 ext{O}_7$ pyrochlore, whose magnetism involves $ ext{Pr}^{3+}$ non-Kramers ions. The analysis compares Zeeman splitting predictions with experimental spectra, modeling fluctuations using a motional-narrowing description.

Key observables. Field evolution of Raman active CEF modes; spectral broadening vs. expected Zeeman splitting; magnetization ($M$) vs. $B$ curves.

Important parameters / regimes. Magnetic fields up to 14 T; temperature of 2 K; comparison between $\mathbf{B} \parallel [100]$ and $\mathbf{B} \parallel [111]$ directions.

Assumptions / limitations. The fluctuation behavior on the kagome lattice is modeled phenomenologically by a motional-narrowing description, implying fluctuations occur on a meV timescale.

Figures summary. Figures show the magnetic field dependence of Raman spectra for both [100] and [111] fields, contrasting the expected Zeeman splitting on the triangular sublattice with the observed broadening on the kagome lattice.

Paper structure. The paper analyzes the CEF excitations under magnetic fields, first establishing the conventional Zeeman response for $\mathbf{B} \parallel [100]$, and then contrasting this with the anomalous, fluctuating response observed for $\mathbf{B} \parallel [111]$ on the kagome planes.

Abstract

The exotic magnetic properties of the pyrochlore Pr$_2$Zr$_2$O$_7$ are determined by the coupling of spin, orbital, and lattice degrees of freedom. Magnetism in this material originates from the non-Kramers Pr$^{3+}$ ion, and the exotic behavior has been discussed both in the framework of dipole-quadrupole properties of magnetic moments and disorder splitting the non-Kramers doublet. We use magneto-Raman spectroscopy to probe the crystal electric field (CEF) excitations of Pr$^{3+}$ in magnetic fields up to 14 T applied along the [100] and [111] crystallographic directions at 2 K. For $\mathrm{B}\parallel[100]$, the field evolution of the Raman active crystal field modes is quantitatively described by conventional Zeeman splitting of the Pr$^{3+}$ ground state doublet, including the thermally populated upper Zeeman branch at elevated temperature. For $\mathrm{}{B}\parallel[111]$, the spectra separate into responses from the triangular and kagome sublattices, and the CEF excitations of Pr$^{3+}$ on the triangular lattice exhibit the expected Zeeman shift consistent with full moments polarization. In contrast, the CEF excitations of Pr$^{3+}$ on the kagome lattice do not split as expected and instead broaden strongly with increasing field. This behavior is inconsistent with a static polarized configuration and is captured phenomenologically by a motional-narrowing description in which the kagome plane moments fluctuate between Zeeman-split levels on a meV timescale. This result offers a natural explanation for the reduced magnetization observed in $\mathrm{}{B}\parallel[111]$ and provides further evidence of the dipole-quadrupole properties of magnetic moments. It highlights Raman scattering as a sensitive probe of exotic spin-orbital dynamics in frustrated magnets.

Abstract

Domain walls in ferroic oxides can host emergent functionalities that differ fundamentally from those of the surrounding bulk, making their microscopic structure central to understanding and controlling macroscopic behaviour. We use scanning three-dimensional X-ray diffraction to map the spatial evolution of coupled octahedral distortion modes across ferroelastic domain walls in the hybrid improper ferroelectric Ca$_{2.15}$Sr$_{0.85}$Ti$_{2}$O$_{7}$. By combining tomographic reconstruction with symmetry-adapted analysis of the octahedral rotation and tilt and associated strain modes, we map their spatial evolution across individual walls in the bulk crystal. We find that the walls are intrinsically extended, with widths far exceeding those expected for conventional Ising-like ferroic interfaces. The wall structure is characterised by a continuous rotation of the tilt order parameter through a tetragonal intermediate. These results provide new microscopic insight into the structural mechanisms governing ferroelastic wall formation, ferroelectric switching, and emergent functionality at domain walls.

The disordered logistic map

Authors: Joseph W. Baron, Tobias Galla
Type: theory · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.24402v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: driven-dissipative phase transition 1/5 · non-equilibrium universality 1/5

Summary. This work analyzes the chaotic dynamics of the disordered logistic map, showing that disorder fundamentally alters the transition to chaos by suppressing the classic period-doubling cascade. Using mean-field theory and RMT, the authors identify new instability regimes, such as oscillatory chaos, that characterize the system's behavior in the presence of quenched disorder.

Why it may be interesting. The study of how disorder fundamentally alters universal transitions (like period-doubling) is highly relevant to understanding critical phenomena and phase transitions in complex, disordered quantum or classical many-body systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how introducing disorder affects the transition to chaos in low-dimensional dynamical systems, specifically by coupling multiple logistic maps.

Main result. Small amounts of disorder can eliminate the conventional period-doubling cascade, leading instead to a direct transition to a new high-dimensional chaotic state. New phenomena emerge that are not present in simple low-dimensional or standard high-dimensional disordered systems.

Method. The analysis employs dynamic mean-field theory, random matrix theory (RMT), and stability analysis based on the eigenvalues of the reduced Jacobian matrix.

Model / system. The primary model is the disordered logistic map, defined by coupled discrete-time maps with quenched random interaction coefficients. The analysis is extended to the disordered Hénon map.

Key observables. Largest Lyapunov Exponent (LLE), eigenvalue spectrum (checking for crossings at $\lambda=1$ or $\lambda=-1$), and the Kaplan-Yorke dimension ($D_{KY}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The control parameters include the map parameter $r$, the disorder strength $\sigma$, and the homogeneous coupling strength $\mu$.

Assumptions / limitations. Assumptions include the absence of correlations among disorder coefficients and using approximations for the eigenvalue spectrum edge derived from RMT.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate stability diagrams showing regions for different instabilities (May-Wigner, OC), and eigenvalue spectra demonstrating the bulk continuum of the reduced Jacobian matrix.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the stability boundaries by examining the eigenvalue spectrum of the coupled system. It contrasts the expected period-doubling route to chaos with transitions governed by bulk eigenvalue crossings (OC instability) or linear instability (May-Wigner instability).

Abstract

The logistic map is a quintessential model in the study of low-dimensional chaos. High-dimensional chaos, on the other hand, presents itself in disordered systems with many interacting and heterogeneously coupled components. Here, we formulate a system of many logistic maps, interacting through disordered couplings. Using a combination of dynamic mean-field theory, random matrix theory and numerical simulations, we show that even the smallest amount of disorder can remove the period-doubling cascade in the conventional logistic map. Instead we find a transition to high-dimensional chaos, marked by an oscillatory instability not previously reported for disordered systems. We also show that with sufficiently strong homogeneous coupling between the maps one recovers elements of the conventional period-doubling cascade. Our findings indicate that well-known phenomena in dynamical systems can be fragile in the face of disorder. At the same time, new phenomena emerge that are neither found in simple low-dimensional dynamics nor in high-dimensional disordered systems.

The Requirement of (at least) Complex Structure for Quantum Mechanics

Authors: M. P. Vaughan
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.23041v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5 · quantum measurements 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper argues that quantum mechanics fundamentally requires a complex structure, even if one attempts to formulate it using only real numbers. By analyzing the constraints imposed by energy conservation and state normalization on real matrix representations, the authors demonstrate that the complex algebra is mathematically necessary for time evolution. It serves as a foundational mathematical critique of 'real' quantum theories.

Why it may be interesting. This work is highly relevant as it tackles the foundational mathematical structure underpinning quantum theory, which is crucial context for developing robust quantum information protocols or analyzing open quantum systems where mathematical rigor is paramount.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the fundamental mathematical requirements for formulating quantum mechanics, specifically arguing that any such formulation, even those appearing real-valued, must inherently rely on a complex structure.

Main result. Under standard assumptions like state normalization and energy conservation, the complex structure is shown to be a minimum requirement for time evolution in quantum mechanics.

Method. The analysis involves mapping complex quantum operators and states onto equivalent real matrix representations (using $2 imes 2$ blocks) and analyzing how fundamental physical constraints (like commutation relations and time evolution) constrain these real matrices.

Model / system. The model is the general framework of quantum mechanics, applied to single qubits and bipartite quantum systems, focusing on the structure of the Hamiltonian and the time evolution operator.

Key observables. State normalization, strict energy conservation, and the commutator structure of physical observables.

Important parameters / regimes. The structure of the time evolution generator ($ ilde{\Omega}$) and the Hamiltonian ($ ilde{H}$), particularly their relationship ($ ilde{H} ilde{\Omega} - ilde{\Omega} ilde{H} = 0$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the assumptions of state normalization and strict energy conservation. It also notes that the complex structure is only a minimum requirement.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by first establishing the mapping from complex to real matrices, then applying constraints from time evolution and energy conservation to this real representation, ultimately demonstrating that these constraints force the retention of complex algebraic properties.

Abstract

It is argued that many real-valued constructions of quantum mechanics are only \emph{nominally} real in that the operators and states are restricted to impose a \emph{complex structure} on the Hilbert space. That is, complex algebra between pairs of elements representing complex numbers is preserved in these formulations. It is therefore mistaken to think of these constructions as being `real' as they are actually a representation of complex linear algebra. It is then shown that under the assumptions of state normalisation and strict energy conservation, this complex structure (at the very least) is required to allow for time evolution in quantum mechanics. This is only a \emph{minimum} requirement, as our arguments do not preclude the formulation of quantum mechanics in terms of hyper-complex entities, such as quaternions.

Summary. This work reports on the kagome metal Co3In2S2, demonstrating a unique carrier duality where its transport properties suggest high mobility while its thermodynamics point to heavy electron behavior. This exotic state arises from the simultaneous presence of topological Dirac fermions and flat bands originating from high-order Van Hove singularities at the Fermi level. The study uses ARPES and quantum oscillations to confirm this coexistence, establishing the material as a model platform for correlated physics.

Why it may be interesting. The concept of carrier duality—where different physical responses (transport vs. thermodynamics) are governed by different electronic components—is a rich topic for understanding emergent quantum states in strongly correlated materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates emergent quantum phenomena arising from the coexistence and interaction between highly mobile Dirac fermions and flat-band heavy electrons in a specific material.

Main result. The kagome metal Co3In2S2 exhibits a carrier duality where charge transport is governed by high-mobility electrons, while thermodynamic responses show heavy-electron behavior due to the interplay between Dirac fermions and high-order Van Hove singularities (HOVHSs).

Method. The research combines advanced experimental techniques, including Angle-Resolved Photoemission Spectroscopy (ARPES) and quantum oscillation measurements, with theoretical modeling using a minimal two-pocket model.

Model / system. The physical system is the kagome metal Co3In2S2. The theoretical framework involves modeling the electronic structure using a minimal two-pocket model that captures the hybridization between topological Dirac cones and flat bands derived from 4th-order Van Hove singularities (HOVHSs) at the Fermi surface.

Key observables. Carrier duality (high mobility transport vs. heavy electron thermodynamics), sublinear resistivity ($ ho_{xx} \propto T^{0.53}$), Non-Fermi-liquid (NFL) behavior ($ ho_{xx} \propto T^{1.5}$), large Sommerfeld coefficient ($\gamma$), and distinct band dispersions visible via ARPES.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the crystal structure, the heavy-electron nature via $\gamma$ vs $T$, the sublinear/NFL temperature dependence of resistivity, and ARPES results confirming the coexisting Dirac and HOVHS features.

Paper structure. The paper systematically presents evidence for the exotic electronic state: first establishing the structural and electronic features (ARPES/DFT), then quantifying the heavy-fermion nature (thermodynamics/QO), and finally synthesizing these findings to explain the observed carrier duality and anomalous transport regimes.

Abstract

The convergence of highly mobile Dirac fermions and flat-band heavy electrons offers a paradigm for emergent quantum phenomena. However, experimental realization of such intriguing state remains elusive. In this study, we report a dual carrier regime in the kagome metal Co3In2S2, wherein the charge transport is governed by high-mobility electrons while the thermodynamic responses exhibit heavy-electron behavior. This exotic duality arises from the coexistence of topological Dirac fermions and flat-band high-order (4th-order) Van Hove singularities (HOVHSs) at the Fermi surface, as revealed by magnetic-torque quantum oscillation and angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy. The interaction between Dirac and HOVHS-derived carriers can be captured by a minimal two-pocket model, manifesting as sublinear resistivity above ~100 K and field-induced non-Fermi-liquid behavior at low temperatures. Our study establishes Co3In2S2 as a model platform for exploring many-body physics at the intersection of topology and correlation effects, and provides a foundational framework for designing quantum materials hosting diverse emergent states.

Summary. This paper proposes an advanced Stochastic Series Expansion Quantum Monte Carlo method that treats the magnetic field operator as an independent class of operators. This generalization allows the field term to participate equally in Monte Carlo updates alongside the Heisenberg interactions. The new framework is validated on the 2D Heisenberg model, showing it is both accurate and computationally competitive with existing methods.

Why it may be interesting. The development of a more general update scheme for QMC methods, especially one that cleanly decouples field terms, provides a powerful, flexible tool for studying quantum magnetism and correlated electron systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To develop a more general Stochastic Series Expansion (SSE) Quantum Monte Carlo framework that treats the magnetic field operator as an independent class of operators.

Main result. The new formulation successfully incorporates the magnetic field operator on equal footing with Heisenberg interactions, yielding results in excellent agreement with conventional methods while offering enhanced flexibility.

Method. The authors utilize the SSE Quantum Monte Carlo method with loop updates, modifying the update scheme to allow the magnetic field operator to participate equally in directed-loop updates.

Model / system. The study focuses on the two-dimensional antiferromagnetic Heisenberg model subjected to an external magnetic field, whose Hamiltonian is decomposed into three types of bond operators.

Key observables. Energy density, total magnetization ($M_z$), and the integrated autocorrelation time (IAT) of the magnetization.

Important parameters / regimes. The magnetic field strength ($h$) and the inverse temperature ($eta$) are key parameters varied during simulations.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on solving directed-loop equations by minimizing the probability of a 'no-update' event, and the initial conventional method merges the field operator into the Heisenberg terms.

Figures summary. Figures compare the energy density and total magnetization versus magnetic field, and another figure compares the integrated autocorrelation time of the magnetization between the new and conventional algorithms across varying field strengths.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the generalized SSE framework, details the decomposition of the Hamiltonian into three operator classes, describes the modified diagonal and directed-loop update procedures, and validates the new method by comparing results and efficiency metrics against established techniques.

Abstract

The Stochastic Series Expansion (SSE) quantum Monte Carlo method with loop updates is among the most powerful approaches for quantum spin and boson systems. In the standard formulation, the magnetic field term is routinely absorbed into the Heisenberg interactions---a strategy that has proven highly efficient across a wide range of field strengths. In this work, we propose a more general SSE framework in which the magnetic field operator is treated as an independent operator class, enabling it to participate in Monte Carlo updates on an equal footing with all other operators. Importantly, the field operator and other interaction operators can transform into one another during the update process. We demonstrate this algorithm using the two-dimensional antiferromagnetic Heisenberg model in a magnetic field as a concrete example. The simulation results agree with those of the conventional algorithm, confirming the correctness of the new formulation. A comparison of the integrated autocorrelation time shows that the new algorithm is competitive. This flexibility not only facilitates measurements of observables associated with operators but also offers potential computational advantages for a broader class of problems.

Abstract

The recent discovery of intrinsic superconductivity in stoichiometric FeTe films has renewed interest in the Te-rich end member of the iron chalcogenides for studies of unconventional and topological superconductivity, yet its intrinsic electronic structure remains unresolved. In this work, we combine molecular beam epitaxy, angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES), electrical transport measurements, density functional theory, and embedded dynamical mean-field theory to track the electronic reconstruction of 20-unit-cell FeTe films as Te annealing progressively removes excess interstitial Fe and drives the system from an antiferromagnetic metal to a superconductor. In addition, a shallow dxz/dyz-derived hole band located about 2 meV below the Fermi level may provide an incipient-band pairing channel, while scattering between the two electron pockets at M may offer additional pairing channels. High-resolution polarization-dependent laser ARPES measurements further reveal a topological surface state whose circular dichroism is consistent with the expected orbital-angular-momentum texture of stoichiometric FeTe. These results establish the intrinsic low-energy electronic structure of superconducting FeTe and identify the electronic states most relevant to superconductivity. The coexistence of intrinsic superconductivity and a topological surface state establishes stoichiometric FeTe as a promising platform for exploring topological superconductivity and possible Majorana bound states.

Using algebraic geometry, the authors prove that the dimension increases unless the gates already span the entire unitary group $U(2n)$. This provides theoretical insight into the efficiency and completeness of quantum gate decompositions.

Why it may be interesting. This work directly addresses the complexity and structure of quantum gate sets, which is fundamental to understanding quantum computation feasibility and resource estimation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the growth rate of the dimension of the set of $n$-qubit unitaries that can be generated by multiplying a fixed number of 2-qubit quantum gates from a specified set of pairs.

Main result. The dimension of the generated set of unitaries strictly increases when an additional pair of qubits is added, provided the current set of gates does not already span the full unitary group $U(2n)$.

Method. The proof relies on algebraic geometry tools, specifically the Tarski–Seidenberg theorem, to analyze the dimension of the image of the construction map, which is treated as a real-algebraic variety.

Model / system. The system involves $n$ qubits, and the gates are constructed from a set $S$ of 2-qubit gates acting on specific pairs of qubits. The overall space is the unitary group $U(2n)$.

Key observables. The dimension of the set of achievable unitaries, $\dim(U_k)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The number of qubits ($n$), the number of gates used ($k$), and the specific pairs of qubits ($\alpha$) defining the gates.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis often assumes the existence of specific generating sets or relies on the gates being generated by one-parameter subgroups that are periodic and form real-algebraic subvarieties.

Paper structure. The paper builds on prior results regarding gate generation and focuses on proving a dimension growth property (Theorem 1.1) by showing that if the dimension stalls, the generated group must be the entire $U(2n)$, leading to a contradiction.

Abstract

In the spirit of the Brown-Susskind conjecture, proven in \cite{haf} and \cite{li}, we study the growth of the dimension of the set of $n$-qubit unitaries which can be obtained as a product of a fixed number of $2$-qubit gates from a fixed set of pairs of qubits. We show that this dimension increases strictly when one more pair is added, for at least some choice of the $2$-qubit pairs at each step.

A Sharp Noise Threshold for Shor's Quantum Factoring and Discrete Log Algorithms

Authors: Jin-Yi Cai, Ben Young
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.24316v1

Summary. This paper rigorously determines the critical noise threshold for Shor's quantum factoring and discrete log algorithms. By modeling noise as Gaussian perturbations on the QFT gates, the authors prove that the algorithms only succeed in polynomial time if the noise level falls below a specific, vanishingly small scaling with the input size. This sets a fundamental benchmark for the required precision of future quantum hardware.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides rigorous, quantitative bounds on the resilience of foundational quantum algorithms like Shor's against realistic noise models, which is crucial for assessing the feasibility of fault-tolerant quantum computation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper analyzes the asymptotic performance of Shor's quantum factoring and discrete log algorithms when subjected to noise affecting the controlled rotation gates within the Quantum Fourier Transform (QFT).

Main result. The authors establish a sharp noise threshold: the algorithms succeed in expected polynomial time if the noise level $\epsilon^{2b}$ scales as $O\left( rac{\log n}{n} ight)^{1/2!}$, and provably fail otherwise for a set of primes of positive density.

Method. The analysis uses expectation values derived from Gaussian noise models applied to the QFT circuit, comparing the expected success probability in the low-noise versus high-noise regimes.

Model / system. The model is the quantum circuit implementing the QFT, specifically involving controlled-$R_{2}^{-k}$ gates. Noise is introduced as a random relative error modeled by Gaussian random variables.

Key observables. The expected success probability $\mathbb{E}[p]$ of measuring a state $|v angle$ satisfying the required condition for the algorithm to succeed.

Important parameters / regimes. Noise magnitude parameter $\epsilon$, input length $n$ (bitlength), and the exponent $b$ related to the QFT structure.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the noise follows a Gaussian distribution and relies on the structure of the QFT circuit; it also assumes the underlying primes belong to a set of positive density (e.g., Fouvry primes).

Paper structure. The paper builds upon previous works by deriving the expected value of the success probability $E[p]$ by analyzing the noise impact on the QFT. It establishes a lower bound for $E[p]$ in the low-noise regime and an upper bound in the high-noise regime, thereby defining the sharp threshold.

Abstract

We study the asymptotic behavior of Shor's quantum factoring and discrete log algorithms when noise affects the precise controlled rotation gates in their quantum Fourier transforms. Improving the results of Cai (2024) and Cai and Young (2025), we identify a sharp and vanishingly small noise threshold. If the noise level lies below this threshold, then the two algorithms succeed in expected polynomial time. If the noise level exceeds this threshold, then the algorithms provably fail to solve their respective problems in expected polynomial time when the underlying primes belong to a set of positive density.
