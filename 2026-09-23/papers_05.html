Spin-Boson Mappings in the Formalism of $f$-Deformations

Authors: Vladimir A. Orlov, Liubov A. Markovich, Alexey V. Mikheenkov, Vladimir I. Man'ko
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.22502v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 3/5 · Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5

Summary. This paper introduces a unified algebraic approach using f-deformed oscillators to map spin operators onto bosonic variables. It demonstrates that historically separate spin-boson transformations (like HP and DM) are mathematically related realizations of a single underlying algebraic structure. This provides a systematic tool for analyzing and potentially extending known models in quantum magnetism and quantum optics.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful, unified algebraic lens through which to view spin-boson models, which are central to understanding quantum magnetism, light-matter interactions, and quantum many-body physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops a unified algebraic framework to describe spin-boson transformations, aiming to reconcile various known mappings of the su(2) algebra.

Main result. It shows that standard mappings like Holstein-Primakoff, Dyson-Maleev, and Jordan-Schwinger arise as different factorizations of a single, underlying algebraic structure defined by f-deformed oscillators.

Method. The authors use the formalism of f-deformed oscillators and employ finite-difference (Newton-series) expansions to derive exact algebraic relations on the finite physical subspace.

Model / system. The model involves spin operators obeying the su(2) algebra, which are mapped onto bosonic variables. The analysis focuses on the single-mode and two-mode realizations of this algebra.

Key observables. The bosonic realizations of spin operators (S+, S-, Sz) and the algebraic constraints required for these operators to satisfy the su(2) commutation relations.

Important parameters / regimes. The spin quantum number (2S) and the deformation parameter (alpha).

Assumptions / limitations. The derived algebraic identities are exact only on the finite physical subspace defined by the spin quantum number, not necessarily on the full infinite Fock space.

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically illustrates the general structure of spin-boson realizations of the su(2) algebra within the f-deformation formalism, showing the relationship between standard and generalized bosonic representations.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the general algebraic framework using f-deformed oscillators. It then analyzes the single-mode case, unifying HP, DM, and $\alpha$-family mappings. Subsequently, it addresses the two-mode case and concludes by deriving necessary and sufficient algebraic conditions for the su(2) algebra to be satisfied by the bosonic operators.

Abstract

We develop a unified algebraic approach to spin-boson transformations based on the formalism of $f$-deformed oscillators. In the single-mode case, we show that the Holstein-Primakoff and Dyson-Maleev transformations, together with the interpolating $α$-family, arise as different factorizations of the same algebraically determined object. The standard spin-boson mappings can thus be interpreted as realizations of a common structure, which makes it possible to clearly separate the exact algebraic content on the physical subspace from effects associated with non-Hermiticity, the choice of metric, and extensions beyond the physical subspace. In the two-mode case, the same approach yields both deformed versions of the Jordan-Schwinger transformation and new exact two-mode realizations. Our results provide a unified description of known spin-boson transformations and naturally lead to new bosonic representations.

Statistical Fluctuations as Primordial Correlators in the CMB: Finite Chemical Potential Thermodynamics

Authors: Anish Ghoshal, Anupam Mazumdar, Bartłomiej Sikorski
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.24998v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 3/5 · methods for driven-dissipative 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper explores generating primordial cosmological correlations from early-universe thermal fluctuations using a charged fluid model. It establishes that simple, isolated thermal models fail to produce the observed scale-invariant spectra. The authors overcome this by modeling the system as an open subsystem, successfully predicting realistic values for CMB observables like the spectral index and non-Gaussianity.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous application of non-equilibrium statistical mechanics (stochastic transport) to cosmology provides a deep theoretical link between microscopic thermal fluctuations and macroscopic cosmological observables, relevant for understanding system boundaries and reservoir effects.

Detailed structure

Main problem. Investigating whether primordial cosmological correlations can arise from the statistical fluctuations of an early-universe thermal system.

Main result. The analysis shows that an isolated conformal plasma cannot generate scale-invariant spectra, but an open system involving massless Dirac fermions can generate a nearly scale-invariant red curvature spectrum matching observational benchmarks.

Method. The work connects grand-canonical thermodynamics, stochastic transport theory, and gauge-invariant cosmological perturbation theory.

Model / system. The system is a charged fluid at finite chemical potential undergoing cosmological evolution, analyzed using the grand-canonical partition function and susceptibility matrix.

Key observables. Primordial power spectra ($\mathcal P_S(k)$), spectral indices ($n_s$), non-Gaussianity parameters ($f_{NL}, g_{NL}$), and the tensor-to-scalar ratio ($r/s$).

Important parameters / regimes. Finite chemical potential ($\mu$), conserved charge-to-entropy ratio, and the nature of the system (isolated vs. open subsystem).

Assumptions / limitations. The initial analysis assumes local equilibrium for deriving the covariance matrix; the solution requires considering an open subsystem exchanging energy/charge with a reservoir.

Paper structure. The paper systematically develops a framework connecting thermodynamics and cosmology, first analyzing the limitations of isolated systems, and then proposing and calculating results for an open subsystem to achieve scale-invariant predictions.

Abstract

Can primordial cosmological correlations originate from statistical fluctuations of an early-universe thermal system? We connect grand-canonical thermodynamics, stochastic transport, and gauge-invariant cosmological perturbations for a charged fluid at finite chemical potential. Energy-charge susceptibilities determine curvature, charge-isocurvature, and cross-correlation amplitudes, while transport sets when each mode leaves equilibrium. For an isolated, adiabatic conformal plasma with conserved charge-to-entropy ratio and power-law diffusion, diffusive freeze-out yields the universal spectrum $\mathcal P_S(k)\propto k^3$, with $n_{\rm iso}=4$, while equal-time local-equilibrium curvature-isocurvature covariance vanishes in the conformal source basis. Thus neither finite chemical potential nor replacing Hubble crossing by diffusion crossing alone yields approximate scale invariance. We evade this obstruction with an open subsystem of massless Dirac fermions during a sourced quasi-de Sitter phase. There, grand-canonical energy cumulants generate a nearly scale-invariant red curvature spectrum. A benchmark gives $\mathcal P_{ζ_X}(k_\star)=2.10\times10^{-9}$ and $n_s(k_\star)=0.9649$, stays within $0.30%$ of the fitted power law over $0.002\leq k/k_\star\leq4$, and predicts weak negative running and small positive intrinsic non-Gaussianity from higher-order cumulants, $f_{\rm NL}(k_\star)\simeq0.106$ and $g_{\rm NL}(k_\star)\simeq0.0158$. With a two-helicity vacuum tensor spectrum, the tensor-to-scalar ratio is $r_{t/s}(k_\star)=1.82\times10^{-4}$. This isolates the limitation of isolated conformal thermal seeding and the open-system ingredient needed to overcome it. Thermal energy-charge fluctuations thus provide a calculable source of realistic scalar correlations, while reservoir dynamics, equilibration, and later curvature-isocurvature transfer must come from a microscopic model.

Abstract

Entanglement and Bell nonlocality have different sharing constraints across subsystems of a multipartite quantum system. We examine simultaneous violations of Bell inequalities with two-outcome observables and two or three measurement settings per party. We identify configurations whose simultaneous violations can be demonstrated on IBM Quantum hardware, and derive bounds and trade-off relations for several polygamous configurations. The results connect experimental capabilities of quantum computers with mathematical methods for studying the limits of quantum correlations.

Summary. This theoretical paper rigorously analyzes retrocausal spin models to determine the conditions for 'complete detector records' when measurements are context-dependent. It derives mathematical constraints showing when detector outcomes can be independent of future settings despite past records depending on them. The work provides a detailed statistical characterization of information preservation in such non-standard quantum temporal settings.

Why it may be interesting. The deep focus on retrocausality, contextuality, and the mathematical constraints on information flow touches upon fundamental questions in quantum foundations, which often intersect with open quantum systems theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper characterizes the conditions under which detector outcome probabilities in a retrocausal spin model can remain independent of later measurement settings, even when earlier detector records depend on those settings.

Main result. It establishes necessary and sufficient conditions for passive readouts and provides a contextual embedding that recovers the isolated hidden law for specified finite adaptive quantum apparatuses.

Method. The analysis combines theoretical derivations using Fourier series, theorems based on antipodal symmetry, and numerical simulations across various contexts.

Model / system. The model is a retrocausal spin source characterized as a positive wrapped-Cauchy source, involving measurements on spin angles ($\lambda$) and outcomes ($a, b$). It analyzes the consistency of detector records across time-like measurements.

Key observables. Detector outcome probabilities $P(dr | x, a, y)$, corrected history measures $\mathrm{e}P(d\lambda, a, dr, b)$, and the signal--source-change tradeoff.

Important parameters / regimes. Source widths ($\gamma_A, \gamma_B$), coupling strengths ($g$), and the measurement settings ($x, y$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is restricted to specified statistical models, and the microscopic physical realization of the source-field interaction remains an open question.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the dependence of detector records on subsequent analyzer axes ($y$) for various parameters, showing agreement probabilities and mean outcome-seed products.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by defining record consistency, deriving conditions for passive readouts using symmetry theorems, developing corrected history measures, and finally applying these concepts to quantum instrument benchmarks and numerical simulations.

Abstract

Detector outcome probabilities can be independent of a later measurement setting even when earlier timestamps, detector seeds, or environmental records depend on that setting. We characterize complete-record preservation for a positive wrapped-Cauchy retrocausal spin source. An antipodal identity yields necessary and sufficient conditions for passive and environmental readouts, and source compensation permits a common normalized analyzer for source spins and prepared continuations. Gaussian controller and reset calculations distinguish inverse raw history weights from normalized thermal operations. A quantum instrument provides an operational benchmark, whereas a setting-dependent separable source produces setting-dependent statistics at an earlier joint probe. For two equal-width contexts, a calibrated weak probe requires a minimum source change of $\operatorname{sech}(2g)/8$ in trace distance for exact protection at every nonzero strength. The signal--source-change tradeoff is sharp for this pair of contexts. A contextual positive-history construction recovers the isolated hidden law and preserves records for a specified class of finite adaptive quantum apparatuses, subject to quantum calibration and restricted field access. Direct readout of an added likelihood field distinguishes two future axes with unit probability. The analytical results are supported by continuous-angle and quantum-trajectory simulations, deterministic quadrature, and exhaustive enumeration of a finite policy class. The analysis concerns the specified statistical models; their microscopic physical realization remains an open question.

Critical Kasner scaling from Cauchy-horizon collapse near holographic phase transitions

Authors: Ling-Long Gao, Yan Liu, Hong-Da Lyu
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.24227v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: non-equilibrium universality 3/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical work investigates how phase transitions occurring at the boundary of black holes dictate the physics deep inside the black hole, specifically across the Cauchy horizon. By analyzing the zero-mode structure, the authors show that the critical data fixes the scaling laws of the subsequent Kasner epoch. This provides a powerful mechanism for connecting external, measurable parameters to the highly non-trivial, strongly coupled physics of black hole interiors.

Why it may be interesting. The concept of transmitting boundary conditions (like critical exponents) from an external, well-defined phase transition (the bifurcation) into the deep, strongly coupled, and singular interior of a black hole provides an analogy for how external perturbations dictate the low-energy physics of complex quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to determine how critical data from an exterior phase transition can be transmitted across a black hole's Cauchy horizon to control the scaling behavior of the interior geometry.

Main result. The zero-mode data transmitted across the horizon fixes the critical exponents and amplitudes of the subsequent Kasner epoch, requiring no independent fitting to the nonlinear interior.

Method. The analysis uses matched boundary-layer techniques to connect linear zero-mode solutions near the Cauchy horizon to fully nonlinear numerical solutions describing the Einstein-Rosen bridge collapse and the subsequent Kasner regime.

Model / system. The system involves black holes (e.g., BTZ, RN-AdS branes) undergoing continuous scalar-hair bifurcations, where the dynamics are governed by coupled Einstein equations and scalar field equations.

Key observables. Critical exponents ($eta$), the exponential collapse rate ($\Gamma_{ER}$), and the scaling laws for the Kasner epoch ($1-p_t \propto |\lambda-\lambda_c|^{2eta}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The boundary control parameter $\lambda$, the critical point $\lambda_c$, and the zero-mode coefficient $B^-$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the existence of a nonextremal Cauchy horizon and relies on matching linear zero-mode solutions to nonlinear boundary layer physics.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the physical process across the black hole interior: from the asymptotic AdS boundary, through the Cauchy horizon, across the ER collapse, and into the final Kasner epoch.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the linear zero-mode behavior near the Cauchy horizon, then developing the nonlinear matching procedure across the ER bridge, and finally applying this framework to derive the scaling laws in the subsequent Kasner epoch, contrasting low-temperature vs. near-bifurcation physics.

Abstract

We study continuous scalar-hair bifurcations from stationary black holes with a nonextremal Cauchy horizon. Although the exterior deformation vanishes at criticality, the critical zero mode generically develops a logarithmic branch at the reference Cauchy horizon, producing a nonuniform critical limit. We show that the coefficient of this logarithm in the scalar perturbation, together with local Cauchy-horizon data, determines the exponential collapse rate of the Einstein-Rosen bridge. If the post-collapse geometry evolves into a Kasner regime, the boundary order-parameter scaling $O\propto|λ-λ_c|^β$ further implies $ 1-p_t\propto|λ-λ_c|^{2β}. $ Crucially, zero-mode matching fixes both the critical exponents and the associated amplitudes, without any fit to the nonlinear interior. We illustrate this mechanism in the transition from rotating BTZ to hairy rotating black holes. For scalarized RN-AdS$_4$ black branes, we quantitatively verify the predicted Kasner scaling using fully nonlinear numerical solutions. At low temperature, two charged models with identical linear critical data instead approach different infrared endpoints, yielding respectively power-law and inverse-logarithmic Kasner scaling. Thus interiors near a finite-temperature bifurcation are controlled by zero-mode transmission, whereas low temperature scaling is governed by the nonlinear infrared completion.

Entangled measurements are necessary for optimal tomography of mixed fermionic Gaussian states and of bosonic Gaussian states near the vacuum

Authors: Ron Rubin
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.23189v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 3/5

Summary. This theoretical paper establishes a fundamental resource bound for quantum state tomography of mixed Gaussian states. It proves that estimating these states using measurements on one copy at a time requires significantly more resources ($\Omega(m^3/\epsilon^2)$) than protocols utilizing entanglement across multiple copies ($\Theta(m^2/\epsilon^2)$). This separation confirms that entangled measurements are essential for achieving the optimal estimation rate.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides fundamental resource bounds for quantum state estimation, showing a clear advantage to using entangled measurements across multiple copies, which is a key concept in quantum resource theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper determines the minimum number of quantum measurements (copies) required to optimally estimate an unknown mixed Gaussian quantum state to a target trace distance $\epsilon$.

Main result. It proves a separation between single-copy measurement protocols ($\Omega(m^3/\epsilon^2)$) and the optimal collective measurement rate ($\Theta(m^2/\epsilon^2)$), demonstrating that entanglement across copies is necessary for optimal tomography.

Method. The proof uses information-theoretic bounds derived from a single-copy Fisher-information budget, combined with dimension-independent comparisons between trace distance and Gaussian parameters.

Model / system. The analysis focuses on mixed fermionic Gaussian states (free-fermion analogues) and, separately, a class of mixed passive bosonic Gaussian states.

Key observables. The state is characterized by its covariance matrix $\Gamma$, and the estimation quality is measured by the trace distance $T( ho, \sigma)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The number of modes ($m$), the target error $\epsilon$, and the scaling factors $m^2$ vs $m^3$ are central.

Assumptions / limitations. The single-copy bound assumes fresh ancillas, classical adaptivity, and no quantum memory between copies; the separation is specific to mixed Gaussian states.

Figures summary. Not specified, but the notes reference comparisons (Table 1) showing the scaling difference between single-copy and collective bounds.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the problem, derives the single-copy lower bound using information theory, and then contrasts this with the known optimal collective rate to prove the necessary resource separation.

Abstract

We prove that learning an unknown mixed fermionic Gaussian state on $m$ modes to trace distance $ε$ requires $Ω(m^3/ε^2)$ copies when measurements act on one copy at a time, even with arbitrary POVMs, fresh ancillas and classical adaptivity, but without quantum memory between copies. The bound holds for $0<ε\le1/3600$ and separates this model from the known collective rate $Θ(m^2/ε^2)$. It follows from a uniform single-copy Fisher-information budget and a dimension-independent comparison between trace distance and Gaussian parameters. On covariance matrices of operator norm at most $1-c$, we prove a Frobenius-to-trace-norm continuity bound with constant $[2c(2-c)]^{-1/2}$; matchgate shadows then attain $O(m^3/(cε^2))$ copies. This also gives explicit error certificates for thermal free-fermion states. For a class of mixed passive bosonic Gaussian states with total mean photon number at most one, a reduction to bounded-block qudit tomography gives a single-copy lower bound $Ω(m^3/(ε^2\sqrt{\log(m/ε)}))$, versus collective complexity $Θ(m^2/ε^2)$. These separations answer the mixed-state measurement-resource question posed by Chen et al. A two-mode example has optimal two-copy Fisher trace $5$, versus the separable ceiling $4$. An IBM replication gave a pre-registered witness lower bound $4.12$ at one-sided $95\%$ shot-noise confidence; its interpretation assumes the prescribed preparations and a common measurement channel and does not bound preparation systematics.

Summary. This work evaluates the performance of warm-start QAOA on a drug-response optimization problem using a trapped-ion quantum processor. The authors compare quantum results against classical heuristics and ideal theoretical bounds, finding that while quantum methods show some promise, classical search algorithms often perform comparably or better on current hardware. The study highlights the significant gap between ideal quantum performance and actual hardware realization.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a detailed, empirical benchmark of near-term quantum optimization algorithms (QAOA) on real hardware for a complex combinatorial problem, directly addressing the gap between theoretical quantum advantage and current hardware limitations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the distinction between improving feasibility and improving optimization when using warm-start Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) for solving constrained optimization problems.

Main result. While warm-start QAOA showed some improvement over random sampling, the authors argue that the performance gains are often marginal compared to classical methods like greedy search or simulated annealing, and hardware performance degrades significantly with increasing qubit count.

Method. The study compares quantum circuit outputs (QAOA) against classical baselines, including uniform feasible sampling, greedy local search, and simulated annealing, using a drug-response model.

Abstract

On a seven-compound drug-response model, warm-start quantum approximate optimization (QAOA) on IonQ Forte-1 returned valid assignments more often than random bitstrings, but this alone did not show effective optimization. Ideal QAOA raised optimum probability above uniform feasible sampling in only four of twelve reference circuits. Hardware often fell below its own noiseless circuits, while greedy search solved all hardware models within 200 objective evaluations. Expanded simulations showed a gain over feasible sampling in 28 of 35 CAMA-1 panels and none of four 647-V panels. Annealing solved all these panels in every seed. A Grover mixer preserved feasibility and improved optimum probability over feasible sampling in all ten tested models. We analyzed 25 completed tasks containing 5,300 shots from 18 circuits and 14 instances. The encodings use 6-35 qubits and at most 4,900 feasible assignments, which we enumerated to establish exact optima. Noiseless references now cover both the original six circuits and six wider circuits. At 35 qubits, with 4,900 feasible assignments, ideal feasibility was 35.85%, compared with 7 of 200 valid hardware outputs. Its ideal optimum probability was below both sampling controls. The circuits sample assignments in a model built from measured single-agent and pairwise responses.

Summary. This work proves a Lee-Yang zero-freeness theorem for various interacting fermionic models, such as the Hubbard model. This mathematical result implies that the ground-state energies of these complex quantum systems can be estimated using a provably efficient quantum algorithm. It unifies the analysis of several key models under a single rigorous theoretical framework.

Why it may be interesting. The connection between mathematical properties of the partition function zeros (Lee-Yang theory) and the existence of efficient quantum algorithms provides a deep theoretical link between statistical mechanics and quantum computation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To prove a Lee-Yang zero-freeness theorem for the partition function of a broad class of interacting fermion models, which has implications for quantum algorithms.

Main result. The theorem establishes that the partition function zeros are constrained in the complex field, implying the existence of a provably efficient quantum algorithm for estimating ground-state energies.

Method. The proof involves extending the Lee-Yang theorem from free fermions to interacting models by utilizing Schur product decompositions and analyzing the zero-free regions of the partition function in the complex field.

Model / system. The study focuses on interacting fermionic Hamiltonians, including the attractive and repulsive Hubbard models on bipartite graphs, and the interacting Hofstadter model.

Key observables. Lee-Yang zeros of the partition function Z(h), ground-state energy, and the spectral gap of the system.

Important parameters / regimes. The complex external field parameter h, the inverse temperature beta, and the hopping/interaction strengths U and A.

Assumptions / limitations. The initial class requires attractive interactions and a positive-semidefinite hopping matrix, though extensions exist for repulsive models with strong external fields.

Paper structure. Abstract

Lee-Yang theorems are a powerful tool for studying many-body systems, with applications ranging from analyzing phase transitions to proving the efficiency of certain classical and quantum algorithms. In this work, we prove a Lee-Yang zero-freeness theorem for the partition function of a broad class of interacting fermion models, implying the existence of a provably efficient quantum algorithm for estimating their ground-state energies. This class includes several well-known models such as the attractive Hubbard model, repulsive Hubbard model on bipartite graphs, and the interacting Hofstadter model. Our results also rigorously establish the nonexistence of phase transitions in these models in the presence of a nonzero local external field.

Main problem. The paper addresses the fundamental challenge of reconciling quantum mechanical predictions with classical notions of reality, specifically concerning the violation of Bell-CHSH and Leggett-Garg inequalities.

Abstract

Furthermore, generalised Macro- realism and generalised Local realism are introduced and analysed within the extended probability framework without relying on an explicit measurement. This opens up further possibilities on one of the great pursuits in physics, understanding on how a quantum system can possess classical properties.

Main problem. The paper aims to derive the Born rule within a novel quantum foundation approach, seeking to establish it under minimal, weak physical assumptions.

Abstract

The notion of a spatiotemporal quantum state extends the conventional concept of a spatial quantum state to the spatiotemporal domain. Such states are represented by unit-trace operators that encode correlations among quantum events distributed across space and time. In this work, we use the spatiotemporal Kirkwood-Dirac phase space to provide a unified characterization of spatiotemporal quantum states. Spatiotemporal states obtained from Kirkwood-Dirac distributions are generally non-Hermitian and nonnormal; whereas those constructed from Margenau-Hill distributions are Hermitian. We provide a unification via (quasi)probabilistic mixture of Kirkwwod-Dirac spatiotemporal states which encompasses almost all existing formulations of spatiotemporal quantum states. We derive recursive expressions for spatiotemporal states and elucidate the relation between Kirkwood-Dirac nonclassicality and the temporality of spatiotemporal states. We further extend the construction to many-fold correlation functions and establish its connection with out-of-time-ordered correlators (OTOCs). We also develop a more general unifying framework based on (quasi)probabilistic mixture of $s$-parametrized spatiotemporal states and establish their Petz time reversal and application in studying Kubo-Martin-Schwinger (KMS) condition in two-time setting. Finally, we apply this framework to characterize quantum entanglement in spacetime and analyze spatiotemporal entanglement using several complementary entropy measures.

Abstract

A wide range of physical and biological systems are adaptive networks, in which the dynamics of the nodes and of the edges connecting them co-evolve. Mean-field reductions of such systems typically track only the average coupling strength, and therefore cannot determine when the coupling stays effectively homogeneous and when structured connectivity emerges. Here, we present a second-order moment closure that allows us to derive mean-field equations for the coupling-weight variance in networks of heterogeneous phase oscillators with adaptive coupling, starting from uniform coupling weights. In agreement with network simulations, we find a nonlinear, non-monotonic dependence of the relative weight variance on the oscillator heterogeneity that is mediated by the phase coherence. Moreover, we find that deviations from global coupling strongly depend on an interaction between the oscillator heterogeneity and the adaptation rule. Whereas symmetric adaptation causes a strongly coupled core of coherent oscillators to emerge and creates a bistable regime that is absent without adaptation, antisymmetric adaptation leads to antisymmetric coupling within the same core, thereby destabilizing it. Our equations therefore delineate the regimes in which adaptive networks behave like globally coupled systems from those in which more complex coupling patterns form.
