A route to the thermodynamics of colloid-polymer mixtures from structural information

Authors: Vikki Anand Varma, Andrew J. Archer, Alberto Scacchi
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29124v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work presents a powerful theoretical framework to calculate the thermodynamics and phase behavior of complex fluids, such as colloid-polymer mixtures, using only structural data obtained at one state point. By decomposing the free energy into reference (excluded-volume) and tail components, the authors bypass the need to know the underlying interaction potentials. This establishes a crucial link between measurable structural properties and macroscopic phase transitions.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical fluids, the methodology of inferring macroscopic thermodynamic behavior (like phase boundaries) solely from single-point structural measurements is a powerful concept applicable to many-body quantum systems where direct potential knowledge is difficult.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops a framework to predict the thermodynamics and phase behavior of complex fluids, specifically colloid-polymer mixtures, directly from structural correlations.

Main result. It successfully demonstrates that structural information measured at a single state point is sufficient to construct the free energy functional and predict phase coexistence, bypassing the need for explicit interaction potentials.

Method. The approach constructs the excess free energy by decomposing interactions into a reference (excluded-volume) part and a tail part, using structural inputs like the radial distribution function or direct correlation function.

Model / system. The focus is on colloid-polymer mixtures and general fluid systems. The theory is built upon decomposing the total potential into a reference hard-sphere part and a remaining interaction tail, which is then analyzed using integral equation theory concepts.

Key observables. Radial distribution functions (g(r)), static structure factors (S(k)), and the pair direct correlation function (c(r)).

Important parameters / regimes. Effective hard-sphere diameter (sigma_opt), inverse temperature (beta), and the integrated attraction strength (A).

Assumptions / limitations. The method assumes radial symmetry for interactions and relies on approximations like Fundamental Measure Theory (FMT) for the reference free energy and the Ornstein-Zernike equation closures.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the predicted binodal curve for a binary mixture and show the reconstruction of pair potentials from structural correlations.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the general theory by decomposing the free energy, details the calculation routes (Thermodynamic Integration, DCF route, Virial route), and applies the framework sequentially to one-component systems before extending it to binary colloid-polymer mixtures to predict phase coexistence.

Abstract

Liquid-state theory provides a fundamental connection between microscopic structure and macroscopic thermodynamic behaviour. Here, we develop a framework for predicting thermodynamics and phase behaviour directly from structural correlations, using either radial distribution functions or static structure factors as input. The approach constructs a free-energy functional from structural information obtained at a single thermodynamic state point, without requiring explicit knowledge of the underlying interaction potentials. This circumvents a central difficulty in modelling complex fluids, for which effective interactions are often unknown or rely on approximations. We demonstrate the framework for a range of model fluids, including colloidal and colloid--polymer systems, with predictions in good agreement with molecular simulation data. The results show that structural information at a single state point can provide sufficient information to predict the broader thermodynamic response of a system. This establishes a route toward inferring phase behaviour directly from experimentally measured structure, even when the microscopic interactions are not known a priori.

Algorithmic threshold for high-dimensional projection pursuit I: general theory

Authors: Brice Huang, Mark Sellke, Nike Sun
Type: theory · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29416v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper provides a rigorous characterization of the statistical distributions that can be extracted from high-dimensional Gaussian data using only algorithms with limited computational complexity. By framing the problem as a stochastic control problem, the authors derive exact algorithmic thresholds for optimizing models like the spherical and Ising perceptrons. This work significantly advances the understanding of algorithmic limitations in high-dimensional data analysis.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical framework connecting algorithmic constraints (Lipschitz dependence) to the achievable statistical properties of high-dimensional data is highly relevant for understanding the limits of inference in complex physical systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper characterizes the set of one-dimensional empirical distributions achievable by projecting high-dimensional Gaussian data along a direction constrained by an algorithm.

Main result. The set of achievable measures is exactly characterized by a one-dimensional stochastic control problem, yielding exact algorithmic thresholds for optimizing perceptron models.

Method. The analysis employs stochastic control theory within the framework of projection pursuit, relating achievable measures to solutions of controlled Stochastic Differential Equations (SDEs).

Model / system. The study uses a null model of high-dimensional projection pursuit with data points sampled i.i.d. from a standard Gaussian in $N$ dimensions. Related physical models include spherical and Ising perceptrons.

Key observables. The achievable empirical distributions ($\mu$) of the projections, and the resulting algorithmic thresholds ($ ext{ALGIsp}, ext{ALGsphp}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The high-dimensional regime defined by $M, N o \infty$ with $M/N o \alpha \in (0, \infty)$.

Assumptions / limitations. The direction $\mathbf{x}$ must be the output of an algorithm with dimension-free Lipschitz dependence on the input data.

Paper structure. The paper develops the characterization of achievable measures using stochastic control theory, establishing necessary and sufficient conditions (Theorems 1.2 and 1.5) based on the closure of sets defined by admissible controls.

Abstract

We study a null model of high-dimensional projection pursuit: we are given $M$ points sampled i.i.d. from a standard gaussian in $N$ dimensions, where $M,N\to\infty$ with $M/N\toα\in(0,\infty)$. Our goal is to characterize the possible empirical distributions of these points' projections along a data-dependent direction $x$, which ranges over either the sphere $S_N=\sqrt{N}\mathbb{S}^{N-1}$ or cube $Σ_N=\{-1,+1\}^N$. We consider this problem in an algorithmic setting, where $x$ must be the output of an algorithm with dimension-free Lipschitz dependence on the input; this class of algorithms includes general gradient-based methods such as Langevin dynamics and approximate message passing (AMP). Our main result exactly characterizes the set of empirical distributions attainable by this class in terms of a one-dimensional stochastic control problem. As a consequence of our main result, we obtain exact algorithmic thresholds for optimizing the Hamiltonian of a spherical or Ising perceptron model with general bounded continuous activation. For the spherical problem, independent work of Montanari and Zhou (2024) characterized the empirical distributions attainable by a related two-stage AMP algorithm, also in terms of stochastic control. Our proof of hardness builds on the branching overlap gap property introduced in earlier work by the first two authors. Our main innovation is to develop stochastic control theory within the branching OGP framework, significantly expanding the settings in which it locates an exact algorithmic threshold. Notably, our methods apply even though the non-algorithmic problem of characterizing all feasible projections remains a major outstanding challenge. For the matching algorithmic result, we construct a new incremental AMP algorithm that acts on a Brownian-bridge revelation of the gaussian disorder and simulates the same family of controlled SDEs.

Bosonic codes from compact phase spaces

Authors: David Roberts, Aaron Slipper, Alireza Parhizkar, Victor V. Albert, Mohammad Hafezi
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.31156v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper establishes a rigorous mathematical boundary for bosonic quantum error correction codes based on the geometry of their phase space. It proves that for surfaces of genus two or higher, the underlying group structure is non-amenable, which mathematically forces a spectral gap that prevents the existence of any stable, normalizable code state. This contrasts sharply with the well-understood case of the torus.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a deep geometric constraint on quantum error correction, showing that the topology of the underlying physical space (the Riemann surface) dictates the fundamental feasibility of encoding quantum information.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the algebraic structure and existence of bosonic quantum error-correcting codes defined on compact Riemann surfaces of genus $g \geq 2$. The core problem is determining when such codes can support normalizable quantum states.

Main result. A fundamental no-go theorem is proven: for genus $g \geq 2$, the non-amenability of the surface group forces a strictly positive spectral gap in the stabilizer Hamiltonian, precluding the existence of any normalizable code state.

Method. The authors use tools from algebraic geometry (automorphic forms, Riemann-Roch theorem) combined with quantum information theory concepts like stabilizer codes and spectral gap analysis.

Model / system. The system models bosonic quantum error correction on a phase space defined by a compact Riemann surface $\Sigma_g$. The code space is defined by states fixed by the action of the surface's fundamental group $\Gamma$.

Key observables. Spectral Gap ($\delta$), Amenability of the stabilizer group ($\Gamma$), Dimension of the code space ($\dim H_\Gamma$).

Important parameters / regimes. Genus ($g$), Stellar Rank ($r$), The structure of the surface group $\pi_1(\Sigma_g)$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the uniformization theorem and the mathematical framework connecting quantum states to global sections of line bundles on the Riemann surface.

Figures summary. Figures compare the spectral gap ($\delta$) for $g=1$ (Torus/GKP, $\delta=0$) versus $g=2$ (Bolza surface, $\delta>0$), illustrating the physical consequence of group amenability.

Paper structure. The paper contrasts the known case of $g=1$ (amenable group, allowing approximate codes) with the general case $g \geq 2$. It develops the no-go theorem by linking the non-amenability of the surface group to a positive spectral gap, which mathematically forbids exact or approximate code states.

Abstract

We present the algebraic structure of bosonic quantum error-correcting codes on genus-two Riemann surfaces. We explicitly construct the code words as automorphic forms and analytically generate the full tower of code spaces at all weights. We prove a fundamental no-go theorem: for any genus greater than one, the stabilizer group is non-amenable, forcing a strictly positive spectral gap in the stabilizer Hamiltonian. Consequently, no normalizable quantum state can satisfy all stabilizer conditions. This sharply contrasts with standard Gottesman-Kitaev-Preskill (GKP) codes, where the amenability of the stabilizer group $\mathbb{Z}^2$ permits approximate code words with arbitrary precision.

Compensation in continuous symmetric trilayered planar ferrimagnet

Authors: Olivia Mallick, Muktish Acharyya
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.30941v1

Summary. This work uses Monte Carlo simulations to study a trilayered anisotropic XY ferrimagnet, focusing on two distinct temperature regimes: a critical transition ($T_c$) and a compensation point ($T_{comp}$). The research confirms that the system exhibits compensation—where net magnetization vanishes despite ordered sublattices—and demonstrates that increasing anisotropy stabilizes both transitions.

Why it may be interesting. The study provides detailed numerical evidence of compensation phenomena in a continuous spin model, offering insights into magnetic ordering mechanisms beyond simple mean-field treatments, which is relevant for understanding complex magnetic phase diagrams.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the compensation behavior in a trilayered anisotropic XY magnetic system, where the total magnetization vanishes even if the individual magnetic sublattices remain ordered.

Main result. The system exhibits distinct high-temperature critical behavior ($T_c$) and low-temperature compensation behavior ($T_{comp}$), both of which increase with the single-site anisotropy parameter $D$.

Method. Monte Carlo simulations using the Metropolis algorithm with random single-spin updates were employed to calculate thermodynamic properties.

Model / system. The model is a trilayered (A-B-A type) anisotropic XY magnetic system featuring intra-plane ferromagnetic and inter-plane antiferromagnetic interactions, described by a Hamiltonian including a single-site anisotropy term $D$.

Key observables. Total magnetization ($M$), magnetic susceptibility ($\chi$), specific heat ($C$), and sublattice magnetizations ($m_k$).

Important parameters / regimes. Single-site anisotropy ($D$), temperature ($T$), and system size ($L$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis distinguishes between the thermodynamic phase transition at $T_c$ and the compensation point at $T_{comp}$, noting that the latter does not show conventional critical scaling with system size.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate $M(T)$, $\chi(T)$, and $C(T)$ for varying anisotropy $D$ and system sizes $L$, showing characteristic peaks/dips at both $T_c$ and $T_{comp}$.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the magnetic response by varying anisotropy ($D$) and system size ($L$), confirming the existence of two distinct temperature regimes: a critical transition and a compensation point, and comparing these findings across different simulation parameters.

Abstract

The trilayered (A-B-A type) anisotropic (single site) XY magnetic model is considered with the intra-plane ferromagnetic and the inter-plane antiferromagnetic interactions. The equilibrium properties of such trilayered anisotropic XY system has been studied by Monte Carlo simulation with Metropolis single spin (randomly chosen) update algorithm. In a given range of interaction parameters, the system has been found to exhibit the high temperature {\it critical} (vanishing sublattice magnetisations and total magnetisation) and low temperature {\it compensation} (vanishing total magnetisation even for non-vanishing sublattice magnetisations) behaviours. The compensation temperature and the critical temperature both are found to increase with the increase of single site anisotropy. The comprehensive phase diagram is drawn. The finite size study reveals the growth of correlations near the critical temperature.

Constrained minimax approximation for quantum signal processing

Authors: Yulong Dong, James B. Larsen, Lin Lin, Rahul Sarkar
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.30937v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper tackles the mathematical challenge of constrained minimax approximation within Quantum Signal Processing (QSP). It develops and validates a nonlinear Fourier retraction technique to ensure that polynomial approximations, derived from solving large semi-infinite programs, remain physically feasible (i.e., bounded by one) while minimizing error. This provides a powerful, reliable workflow for designing practical quantum circuits.

Why it may be interesting. The work provides a robust mathematical tool for designing quantum circuits based on function approximation, which is crucial for implementing complex quantum gates or transformations in quantum computing architectures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the constrained minimax approximation problem arising in Quantum Signal Processing (QSP), which requires finding a polynomial approximation that remains bounded by one on the domain while minimizing approximation error.

Main result. The authors introduce a nonlinear Fourier retraction method that successfully converts nearly feasible polynomial approximations into strictly feasible ones, maintaining high approximation accuracy even in challenging regimes.

Method. The approach combines solving a Linear Semi-Infinite Program (LSIP) via discretization (LP) with a novel nonlinear Fourier retraction technique to enforce boundary constraints.

Model / system. The context is Quantum Signal Processing (QSP), where polynomial transformations are implemented using quantum circuits. The approximation is formulated using Chebyshev polynomials ($\Phi(x)$) to construct an $ ext{SU}(2)$ valued QSP protocol.

Key observables. Approximation error ($\lVert P - g Vert_Y$), constraint violation ($\lVert \langle \hat{c}^*, \Phi angle Vert_X - 1$), and the convergence of the retraction method.

Important parameters / regimes. The degree of the polynomial ($d$), the size of the discretization sets ($|\hat{X}|, |\hat{Y}|$), and the regime of approximation (e.g., fully-coherent regime).

Assumptions / limitations. The primary difficulty addressed is that feasibility at finite sample points does not guarantee feasibility on the entire domain, especially when the optimal approximant hits the boundary.

Figures summary. Figures demonstrate the comparison between the optimal (but potentially infeasible) solution and the retracted solution, showing the retraction method maintains accuracy and enforces feasibility, particularly for the threshold projector ($g_{TP}$).

Paper structure. The paper first defines the constrained minimax problem in QSP, analyzes initial discretization methods (Remez exchange), and then introduces and rigorously tests the nonlinear Fourier retraction method, demonstrating its superior performance over standard techniques.

Abstract

Quantum signal processing (QSP) provides a simple and efficient framework for implementing polynomial transformations using quantum circuits. Its classical design stage leads to a constrained minimax approximation problem: find a polynomial of prescribed parity that approximates a target function uniformly on a fitting set while remaining bounded in magnitude by one on the domain $[0,1]$, which can be viewed as a semi-infinite constraint. Discretization converts the problem into a linear program, but feasibility at a set of finitely many sampled points does not ensure feasibility on the whole domain, especially when an optimal approximant reaches the boundary of the feasible set. We investigate two approaches to address this difficulty. A Remez exchange method combined with active-set constraint enforcement is efficient on many tested instances, but its stability depends on the target and problem geometry. We then introduce nonlinear Fourier retraction, which uses QSP completion and phase synthesis to turn a nearly feasible polynomial into phase factors for a feasible QSP polynomial without increasing the degree. Across representative problems, retraction largely preserves approximation accuracy and remains effective on instances where the Remez heuristic is unstable. The resulting workflow connects classical minimax approximation and semi-infinite optimization with nonlinear Fourier analysis, and is implemented in the qsppack software package.

Summary. This work demonstrates that physically thinning the superconductor FeSe via exfoliation acts as a powerful tool to manipulate its electronic state. Experimentally, this process suppresses the bulk nematic order and spectroscopically confirms a symmetry switch in the superconducting pairing from s-wave to d-wave-dominant. This establishes FeSe thin flakes as a versatile platform for engineering quantum superconducting orders.

Why it may be interesting. The ability to tune superconducting pairing symmetry using purely structural/dimensional control (exfoliation) is highly relevant to understanding emergent quantum states in strongly correlated materials, providing a clean platform for studying pairing mechanisms.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how controlling the electronic structure via exfoliation can tune the competing electronic orders (nematic and superconducting) in the unconventional superconductor FeSe.

Main result. Exfoliation suppresses the bulk electronic nematic response and fundamentally switches the superconducting symmetry from bulk s-wave to a d-wave-dominant pairing state.

Method. The research employs a combination of experimental techniques, including angle-dependent Andreev reflection spectroscopy, Raman scattering, and transport measurements, complemented by theoretical modeling using the 2D-BTK model.

Model / system. The physical system is Iron Selenide (FeSe), an unconventional superconductor. The theoretical framework involves modeling the superconducting order parameter (sOP) symmetry, often using extensions of the 2D-BTK model to account for $s+d$-wave pairing.

Key observables. Superconducting symmetry (s-wave vs. d-wave), nematic order strength (probed by Raman response), superconducting gap size ($\Delta$), and critical temperature ($T_c$).

Important parameters / regimes. Flake thickness (dimensional reduction), magnetic field ($B$), temperature ($T$), and the nematic interaction strength ($g_{nem}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that exfoliation provides a clean tuning knob for electronic orders, and that Andreev reflection measurements can be accurately modeled using the 2D-BTK formalism.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the suppression of nematic signatures in thin flakes via Raman maps, the transition from coherence peaks to Zero-Bias Conductance Peaks (ZBCP) in ARS spectra, and the thickness dependence of superconducting gap components.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the problem of controlling intertwined orders, then presenting evidence from transport and Raman spectroscopy showing nematic suppression upon exfoliation, followed by definitive proof of symmetry switching using angle-dependent Andreev reflection spectroscopy, and concluding with theoretical modeling of the resulting pairing symmetry.

Abstract

Controlling intertwined electronic orders in two-dimensional superconductors offers an effective route to answering fundamental questions and engineering new quantum devices. However, tuning the balance between competing orders typically requires complex chemistry, strain, or interface engineering. Here, we show that a pristine alternative is the dimensional reduction of the unconventional superconductor FeSe. Exfoliation suppresses the bulk electronic nematic response and switches the superconducting symmetry from bulk s-wave to d-wave-dominant. Transport, electron microscopy, and Raman spectroscopy establish the substantial weakening of nematic order in thin flakes. To probe superconductivity, we perform angle-dependent Andreev reflection spectroscopy on pristine crystal edges. As the junction's orientation is rotated, the spectra evolve from zero-energy bound states to coherence peaks. The injection angle, field, and temperature dependence, along with theoretical modeling, confirm that exfoliation switches the superconducting symmetry. Our results suggest a versatile superconducting platform for engineering quantum orders and provide fresh insights into the underlying pairing mechanisms.

d'Q = TdS for Infinitesimal Irreversible Processes: On the Differential Form of the Clausius Inequality

Summary. This paper re-examines the Clausius inequality for infinitesimal irreversible processes, arguing that the correct first-order relationship is an equality, $d'Q = TdS$. This result demonstrates that entropy remains a well-defined state function even when considering quasi-static irreversible paths, providing a purely thermodynamic proof that internal entropy production is of a higher order than first order.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on classical thermodynamics, establishing the path independence of entropy for quasi-static irreversible paths using purely thermodynamic arguments provides a rigorous foundation relevant to defining entropy in non-equilibrium quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the conventional understanding of the Clausius inequality for infinitesimal irreversible processes, specifically challenging the strict inequality $d'Q < TdS$.

Main result. The correct first-order differential form for quasi-static irreversible processes is shown to be the equality $d'Q = TdS$, which implies that internal entropy production is of second order or higher.

Method. The analysis uses purely thermodynamic arguments, starting from the First Law of Thermodynamics and applying first-order approximations to the inexact differential of heat ($d'Q$) and the exact differential of entropy ($dS$).

Model / system. The study considers general thermodynamic processes, differentiating between reversible and irreversible paths under the assumption of quasi-static conditions.

Key observables. Inexact differential of heat ($d'Q$), exact differential of entropy ($dS$), and internal entropy production ($S_{prod}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature ($T$) and the assumption of quasi-static processes.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is strictly limited to quasi-static processes, and the derivation relies on first-order approximations for small temperature variations.

Paper structure. The paper builds the argument by comparing the heat exchange and entropy change for reversible and irreversible processes, deriving the equality $d'Q = TdS$ for quasi-static paths, and concluding that entropy remains a well-defined state function even for irreversible paths.

Abstract

Conventionally, the differential form of the Clausius inequality is adopted for infinitesimal irreversible processes. We show that the correct first-order form for infinitesimal quasi-static irreversible processes is the equality d'Q=TdS rather than d'Q < TdS. This provides a purely thermodynamic proof that internal entropy production is second-order or higher and confirms that entropy remains a well-defined state quantity even when quasi-static irreversible processes are included, without resorting to the Boltzmann relation.

Deterministic Minimum-Output-Entropy Nonadditivity via Haagerup's Inequality and Near-Free Permutation Representations

Authors: Guocheng Zhen, Chengkai Zhu, Ranyiliu Chen, Xin Wang
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.31081v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work provides a deterministic, polynomial-time construction demonstrating the nonadditivity of minimum-output-entropy for quantum channels. By combining advanced inequalities like Haagerup's with spectral approximation techniques, the authors establish a positive entropy gap. This result is significant because it provides a rigorous, constructive proof that translates directly into superadditivity for the one-shot Holevo quantity.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a concrete, deterministic, and algorithmic proof of a fundamental non-additivity property in quantum information theory, advancing the understanding of channel capacity beyond simple random constructions.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the deterministic realization of the quadratic certificate proving the nonadditivity of the minimum-output-entropy for quantum channels.

Main result. It constructs a deterministic, polynomial-time algorithm yielding a specific entropy gap for the channel, which is shown to translate exactly into superadditivity for the one-shot Holevo quantity.

Method. The construction combines Haagerup's length-two inequality with the deterministic spectral approximation of O'Donnell and Wu, applied to channels realized via real orthogonal Stinespring blocks.

Model / system. The system analyzed is a quantum channel $\Phi_{N'}:M_{N'-1}(\mathbb{C}) o M_K(\mathbb{C})$, whose properties are governed by its minimum-output-entropy ($H_{\min}$).

Key observables. Minimum output entropy ($H_{\min}$), the entropy gap $2H_{\min}(\Phi_{N'}) - H_{\min}(\Phi_{N'}^{\otimes 2})$, and the one-shot Holevo quantity ($\chi$).

Important parameters / regimes. Fixed integer $K \ge 2$, rational tolerance $\eta > 0$, and the asymptotic input size $N$.

Assumptions / limitations. The construction is algorithmic and asymptotic, relying on the existence of a real Stinespring realization for the channels.

Paper structure. The paper develops the deterministic construction, proves the entropy gap inequality using spectral approximation, and concludes by showing the equivalence of this gap to superadditivity for the Holevo quantity.

Abstract

We give a deterministic realization of the finite-dimensional quadratic certificate underlying Collins's mixed-unitary proof of minimum-output-entropy nonadditivity. For every fixed integer $K\ge 2$ and rational $η>0$ satisfying $\log K>2(3+η)^2$, a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm, for every sufficiently large target size $N$, outputs $K$ permutations on $N'=N+o_{K,η}(N)$ points. Restricting their permutation matrices to the nontrivial standard representation yields real orthogonal Stinespring blocks and a channel $Φ_{N'}:M_{N'-1}(\mathbb{C})\to M_K(\mathbb{C})$ such that \[ 2H_{\min}(Φ_{N'}) -H_{\min}(Φ_{N'}^{\otimes 2}) \ge \frac{\log K}{K} -2\log\left(1+\frac{(3+η)^2}{K}\right) >0. \] The construction combines Haagerup's length-two inequality with the simultaneous deterministic spectral approximation of O'Donnell and Wu. We further show that the constant $3$ is asymptotically sharp on the relevant Hermitian zero-diagonal coefficient class and that the finite spectral transfer is nearly saturated, thereby isolating the finer geometry of the full output body as the natural next level of refinement beyond the scalar-radius method. Finally, a standard covariant extension converts the same deterministic entropy gap exactly into self-tensor superadditivity of the one-shot Holevo quantity.

Emergence and suppression of phonon vortices in two-dimensional crystals: Interplay of lattice symmetry, heavy impurities, and shear

Summary. This work provides a first-principles understanding of phonon vortices in 2D crystals, demonstrating that these modes are symmetry-protected features. The authors model how these vortices are stabilized by heavy impurities but suppressed by shear strain, establishing a predictable crossover threshold. This framework offers a powerful tool for designing materials with tailored vibrational properties.

Why it may be interesting. The framework connecting vibrational topological defects (vortices) to fundamental symmetries and external perturbations (strain/defects) provides a generalizable model for understanding lattice dynamics in engineered materials, relevant to phononics and thermal transport.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the fundamental origin, emergence, and suppression mechanisms of phonon vortices in two-dimensional crystals.

Main result. Phonon vortices are shown to arise intrinsically from lattice symmetry in perfect crystals, while their existence is governed by a competition between impurity-induced resonance and shear-induced symmetry breaking.

Method. The research employs phonon eigenvalue calculations, group theory (SALC), and perturbation theory to model the effects of mass defects and strain.

Model / system. The system under study is two-dimensional crystalline materials (e.g., graphene) modeled on hexagonal or square lattices, analyzed using the phonon eigenvalue equation.

Key observables. Phonon vortex modes, frequency shifts ($\omega$), spatial coherence ($\delta$), and the strain threshold ($\gamma_c$).

Important parameters / regimes. Heavy mass impurity concentration ($\Delta M$), shear strain ($\Delta \gamma$), and lattice symmetry point groups ($C_3$, $C_6$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis often assumes the single-site resonance dominates the full mode behavior, and the initial understanding is built upon symmetry-adapted linear combinations.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the formation of vortex modes, the frequency dependence on impurity concentration, and the competition between impurity and shear effects determining a crossover strain threshold.

Paper structure. The paper develops from establishing the symmetry origin of vortices in perfect crystals, to analyzing the stabilizing effect of heavy impurities, and finally quantifying the suppression mechanism via shear deformation, culminating in a predictive strain threshold.

Abstract

Phonon vortices are vortex-like displacement fields that appear in the vibrational modes of two- dimensional materials. Here, we demonstrate that these vortices arise as symmetry-adapted linear combinations of degenerate planar phonon modes, with the superposition coefficients uniquely de- termined by the lattice point group. This symmetry principle establishes that vortices are intrinsic standing-wave solutions in perfect crystals, requiring neither impurities nor disorder. Abstract

Recent studies have shown that Bargmann invariants provide effective detectors of set imaginarity. In this paper, we investigate set imaginarity as a quantum resource in qubit systems. By exploiting the structure of Bargmann invariants, we show that the free operations for qubit set imaginarity consist precisely of common unital operations and common planarized operations. Based on this characterization, we introduce an axiomatic framework for set-imaginarity measures (SIMs). In particular, we propose two refined notions, namely unified SIMs and complete SIMs, which allow a more fine-grained quantification of set imaginarity. To make these notions concrete, we construct two qubit SIMs from the Bargmann invariants of three-state subsets. We prove that one of them is a unified SIM, while the other satisfies the stronger requirements of a complete SIM. Furthermore, we revisit the robustness of set imaginarity previously introduced in the literature. We show that, although this robustness is a valid SIM for qubit systems, it is neither a unified SIM nor a complete SIM. To overcome this limitation, we propose an improved robustness-type measure and rigorously prove that it defines a complete qubit SIM.
