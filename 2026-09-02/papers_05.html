Emergent Pair Density Wave and Incoherent Metallic State in a Strongly Correlated Doped System

Authors: Soham Maiti, Nandan Pakhira, A. Taraphder
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29742v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 3/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical work explores the complex interplay between Antiferromagnetism, d-wave Superconductivity, and Pair Density Wave formation in strongly correlated electron systems. Using a slave-rotor mean-field approach, the authors demonstrate that PDW order emerges dynamically in the coexistence region of AFM and SC. The study provides detailed spectral analysis showing a crossover from coherent to incoherent metallic behavior as charge coherence is lost.

Why it may be interesting. The explicit treatment of charge-spin separation via the rotor degree of freedom provides a powerful, non-conventional tool to analyze the interplay between magnetism, superconductivity, and charge localization in correlated materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the dynamical emergence of a Pair Density Wave (PDW) state driven by the interplay between Antiferromagnetism (AFM) and singlet d-wave Superconductivity (SC) in a strongly correlated doped system.

Main result. The authors find that PDW order emerges dynamically in the AFM-SC coexistence region, and the system undergoes a crossover from a coherent to an incoherent metallic state as charge coherence is suppressed.

Method. The analysis employs a slave-rotor mean-field framework, decomposing the electron into spinon and rotor degrees of freedom to capture strong correlation effects.

Model / system. The model is a strongly correlated electron system on a 2D square lattice, described by a Hamiltonian involving hopping, onsite repulsion (U), and terms coupling to SC and PDW order parameters.

Key observables. AFM, SC, and PDW order parameters; the rotor condensate amplitude (phi) characterizing charge coherence; and momentum-resolved spectral functions A(k, omega).

Important parameters / regimes. Doping ($\delta$), Temperature ($T$), and the onsite repulsion strength ($U$).

Assumptions / limitations. The formalism relies on slave-rotor mean-field theory, and improvements are made by using a two-site cluster formulation over a single-site approximation.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the $T-\delta$ phase diagram showing PDW emergence, the evolution of phase boundaries with increasing U, and momentum-resolved spectral functions distinguishing coherent and incoherent excitations.

Paper structure. The paper systematically develops the slave-rotor mean-field theory, calculates the free energy by diagonalizing coupled spinon and rotor Hamiltonians, and analyzes the resulting spectral functions across different ordered phases (AFM, SC, Coexistence).

Abstract

We investigate the dynamical emergence of a state driven by the interplay between antiferromagnetism (AFM) and singlet $d$-wave superconductivity (SC) within a slave-rotor mean-field framework. By decomposing the electron into charge and spin degrees of freedom, the formalism captures strong-correlation effects beyond conventional mean-field approaches. A PDW order is found to emerge dynamically in the coexistence region of AFM and SC. The AFM-SC coexistence region is significantly modified with correlation, leading to a systematic shift of the tetra-critical point. The doping and temperature evolution of the AFM, SC, and PDW order parameters, together with the rotor condensate amplitude $φ$, which characterizes charge coherence, shows a crossover from a coherent to an incoherent metal with the suppression of coherent quasi-particle spectral weight. Moreover, in the AFM + $(φ\neq 0)$ region, coherent quasi-particle bands coexist with incoherent Hubbard-like excitations, whereas only incoherent spectral features survive in the AFM + $(φ= 0)$ regime. The SC phase exhibits nodal quasi-particles consistent with $d$-wave pairing.

Enhanced Diffusion from Twist-Angle Disorder

Authors: Nicole S Ticea, Yi-Ming Wu
Type: theory · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29062v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 3/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work theoretically analyzes the impact of spatially varying twist-angle disorder on electronic transport in moiré superlattices. By modeling the disorder using a geometric constraint analogous to electromagnetism, the authors show that this specific type of disorder suppresses momentum scattering. The problem is successfully formulated using a Non-linear Sigma Model, yielding quantitative predictions for enhanced electron diffusivity.

Why it may be interesting. The explicit mapping to an NLSM and the calculation of transport coefficients in the presence of geometric constraints provide a powerful, non-trivial extension to standard theories of disorder in condensed matter.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how twist-angle disorder affects the single-particle properties and transport (diffusivity) of electrons in twisted bilayer systems.

Main result. The geometric scattering constraints imposed by the twist structure substantially suppress momentum relaxation compared to simple white noise disorder, leading to an enhanced diffusivity.

Method. The analysis employs advanced theoretical techniques, including the replica trick, mapping the problem onto a Non-linear Sigma Model (NLSM), and performing disorder averaging via Wigner transforms.

Model / system. The system is based on twisted bilayer materials (like TBLG) where the moiré potential is modulated by a spatially varying twist angle disorder. The disorder is modeled mathematically as the curl of the relative displacement field.

Key observables. Diffusivity ($D$), disorder-induced correlation function $ar{g}(r)$, and the effective action derived from the NLSM.

Important parameters / regimes. The disorder correlation length, the moiré lattice scale, and the strength of the disorder kernel (e.g., power-law vs. Lorentzian).

Assumptions / limitations. The disorder correlation length is assumed much larger than the moiré lattice scale, allowing for Wigner transform approximations and averaging over the moiré unit cell.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the spatial sampling of the twist angle disorder using various covariance kernels and compare the calculated isotropic diffusivity against a standard Anderson reference disorder, showing enhancement.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from defining the disorder Hamiltonian and using the replica trick to derive the effective disorder-averaged coupling $ar{g}(R)$. It then maps the problem to an NLSM, calculates the effective action, and finally extracts the transport properties by analyzing the small-$k$ behavior of the Green's function correlator.

Abstract

We consider the effect of twist-angle disorder on the single-particle properties of twisted bilayer systems. These materials are a natural playground for studying how tiny, uncontrolled spatial variations may become unusually consequential; a local change in the twist geometry can give rise to spatially-correlated alterations of the parent Hamiltonian, which in turn affect the coarse-grained properties of the substrate such as transport and ordering tendencies. In analogy to electrodynamics, where the magnetic field is given by the curl of the vector potential, we model the twist angle as the curl of the relative displacement between the two bilayers. We find that geometric scattering constraints imposed here by the twist structure substantially suppress momentum relaxation relative to mean-rate-matched white noise. We quantify these effects and show that the problem can be mapped onto a non-linear sigma model (NLSM).

The resource cost of magic in a code block

Authors: Jiachen Shen, Hui Zhong
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29438v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 3/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical work derives a tight upper bound on the resource cost ('magic') associated with post-selected logical measurements in quantum error-correcting codes. By analyzing the accepted effect through an outcome-by-outcome resource theory lens, the authors prove the cost decays exponentially with the code distance, $\exp[-\Omega(d \log d)]$. This result is significant because it quantifies the fundamental resource limitations for implementing complex quantum operations.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a quantitative, resource-theoretic understanding of the fundamental limitations on measurement fidelity and resource consumption in fault-tolerant quantum computation, which is crucial for designing practical quantum hardware.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to rigorously bound the resource cost, or 'magic,' associated with performing a post-selected logical measurement within a quantum code block.

Main result. The accepted magic, when multiplied by the acceptance probability, is bounded by $\exp[-\Omega(d \log d)]$, a bound that is shown to be attainable by a specific protocol.

Method. The analysis employs resource theory techniques, reading the accepted effect outcome-by-outcome against the free set of magic, and utilizes exact-component expansions over connected clusters of insertion histories.

Model / system. The system is a single code block encoding one logical qubit, analyzed using an adaptive protocol involving measurement, feed-forward, and acceptance, relevant to fault-tolerant quantum computation.

Key observables. Magic (resource cost), Acceptance Probability, Code Distance ($d$), and the resulting bound $\exp[-\Omega(d \log d)]$.

Important parameters / regimes. Code distance ($d$), the certified threshold ($D \propto d$), and the structure of the exact-recovery skeleton.

Assumptions / limitations. The resource cells must sit inside a bounded-spread exact-recovery skeleton, and the analysis relies on the structure of the accepted effect being read outcome-by-outcome.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the operations of a split-readout family, detailing syndrome measurement, classical recording, and the separation into accepted and rejected measurement fibres.

Paper structure. The paper develops bounds by first establishing an unconditional bound, then presenting the main result using an exact-component expansion. It certifies the threshold using a circuit model (stabilizer measurement followed by split readout) and concludes by showing a protocol that attains the derived exponential bound.

Abstract

We bound the magic of a post-selected logical measurement by the resource that produced it. The setting is one code block with one logical qubit and an adaptive protocol that measures, feeds forward and accepts. The witness reads the accepted effect against the free set of the resource theory of magic, outcome by outcome and not on the averaged channel, since a channel can be free while one of its outcomes measures the magic axis. Our first bound is unconditional. The accepted magic is at most a constant times the summed distance of the cells from the free set. The second is the main result. When the resource cells sit inside a bounded-spread exact-recovery skeleton, the recovery puts every insertion history below a threshold onto a single free branch, transcript by transcript, so only connected clusters reaching the threshold contribute and an exact-component expansion controls their weight. With a threshold linear in the code distance, polynomially many cells of bounded insertion degree and per-cell dilation amplitude $O(1/d)$, the accepted magic times the acceptance probability is at most $\exp[-Ω(d\log d)]$. Post-selection is disposed of before accepted transcripts are summed, so a branch of vanishing probability cannot be amplified into a magic effect. The threshold is certified from a circuit, and we run it on one exact round of stabilizer measurement followed by a split readout, which measures logical $X$ on the accepted fibre and logical $Z$ on the rejected ones. That certifies a threshold equal to the code distance for every single-layer pattern of weak $Z$-rotations, one per data qubit, so the hypotheses are met by a family and not one design. A member carries magic only if its support contains a logical $Z$ string. One member attains the exponent, again at the level of the accepted effect. Suppression is set by the threshold and not by the topology of the block.

Summary. This work investigates anomalous frequency shifts in superconducting granular aluminum resonators, finding that the frequency initially increases with temperature and power at low temperatures, contrary to standard TLS predictions. The authors propose a quasiparticle mechanism related to the granular structure to explain this anomaly. The findings are crucial for advancing superconducting circuits in quantum technology by pinpointing fundamental loss mechanisms.

Why it may be interesting. The anomalous electrodynamics observed in superconducting resonators, particularly the deviation from standard TLS predictions, touches upon non-equilibrium dynamics and quasiparticle physics relevant to open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is to identify the dominant loss mechanisms and explain the anomalous frequency shifts observed in low-loss superconducting granular aluminum (grAl) resonators.

Main result. The resonance frequency exhibits an initial positive shift with increasing temperature and power at low T, contradicting standard Two-Level System (TLS) predictions, which can partially be explained by superconducting quasiparticle effects in a granular system.

Method. The study involves characterizing the complex admittance by measuring the resonance frequency and loss rate as functions of temperature and input power in the single-photon regime.

Model / system. The physical system is a superconducting resonator made from granular aluminum (grAl). Theoretical models considered include the TLS model and an alternative model treating grAl as an array of superconducting grains with spatially non-uniform superconducting gaps.

Key observables. Resonance frequency shift ($\Delta f$), internal loss rate ($1/Q_i$), and their dependence on temperature ($T$) and readout power ($P_{in}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Low temperatures (down to $\sim 100$ mK), single-photon regime ($ar{n} \approx 1$), and the superconducting gap structure.

Assumptions / limitations. The standard TLS model is assumed to be insufficient for describing the low-temperature frequency dependence, and the quasiparticle model is used to explain the initial anomalous behavior.

Figures summary. Figures show the temperature dependence of frequency and loss, the power dependence of frequency shift, and the time dynamics of frequency following high-energy events.

Paper structure. The paper systematically presents measurements showing deviations from standard models (TLS), proposes a quasiparticle mechanism to explain the low-T anomalies, and discusses limitations regarding the loss rate and high-energy response.

Abstract

Superconducting high-kinetic inductance materials like granular aluminum (grAl) are a versatile part of the circuit quantum electrodynamics (cQED) toolbox, provided their losses at microwave frequencies are low enough. To further advance the use of grAl in quantum devices, it is indispensable to identify the dominant loss mechanisms. The standard approach pairs electromagnetic simulations of resonator geometry with measured temperature and power dependence of resonance frequency and loss rate. Most materials follow the phenomenological theory of two-level systems (TLSs) at low temperatures, resulting in an initial decrease of the resonance frequency with increasing temperature. In our work, we observe an opposite behavior at the lowest temperatures: The resonance frequency initially increases with both temperature and readout power, contradicting the standard TLS model predictions. We are able to explain a part of the data by an alternative mechanism associated with the effect of superconducting quasiparticles in a granular system with spatially-nonuniform gap. Yet, the observed change in the resonance frequency with temperature is not matched by a proportional change in the loss rate. We also observe anomalous positive frequency responses following high-energy events, characterized by several time scales. While anomalous behavior has been reported previously in grAl, the disagreement with standard models is particularly visible in our devices thanks to their exceptionally low loss rates.

Summary. This paper analyzes the resource trade-off in non-Markovian quantum cooling, balancing cooling efficiency (steps required) against the physical cost of implementation. By mapping the complex dynamics onto a collision circuit, the authors quantify the geometric cost associated with reservoir memory. They conclude that while memory can improve cooling steps, it often increases the overall resource cost, guiding better hardware-aware protocol design.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a crucial resource-based perspective on open quantum systems, directly linking the physical mechanism of memory (non-Markovianity) to quantifiable hardware implementation costs, which is vital for designing practical quantum computation protocols.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the fundamental trade-off between achieving high quantum cooling performance and the associated resource cost when implementing non-Markovian quantum cooling protocols.

Main result. A clear trade-off is established: suppressing reservoir memory reduces the required cooling steps but generally increases the overall geometric implementation cost.

Method. The authors use a pseudomode mapping to represent non-Markovian dynamics as a Markovian process implemented via a repeated collision circuit, whose cost is quantified using a geometric path length formulation.

Model / system. The system is a single-qubit undergoing algorithmic cooling via interaction with a reservoir memory, modeled using a damped Jaynes-Cummings Hamiltonian and analyzed through its pseudomode representation.

Key observables. Cooling-step count ($N_{ ext{cool}}$) and geometric implementation cost ($C_{ ext{cool}}^{ ext{geom}}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Reservoir spectral width ($\lambda$), which controls the degree of non-Markovianity ($\lambda/\gamma$ regime).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis focuses on the single-excitation sector, and the resource cost is based on the geometric path length in the collision circuit representation.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the pseudomode mapping, the gate decomposition of a collision step, the geometric path defining the cost, and plots showing $N_{ ext{cool}}$ and minimum excited-state population versus the spectral width ratio $\lambda/\gamma$.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the physical model and the pseudomode mapping. It then details the calculation of the geometric cost for the collision circuit. Finally, it analyzes the trade-off by comparing cooling performance metrics across different reservoir memory regimes.

Abstract

Quantum cooling is important for reliable quantum computation but involves a trade-off between cooling performance and implementation resources. Although reservoir memory can improve particular aspects of cooling performance, the associated resource cost, particularly for circuit implementation, remains insufficiently understood. Here, we investigate how reservoir memory affects the trade-off between cooling performance quantified by the cooling-step count and geometric implementation cost in single-qubit heat-bath algorithmic cooling. Using a pseudomode mapping, we represent the non-Markovian damped Jaynes--Cummings dynamics by a repeated collision circuit and evaluate its geometric implementation cost. Using matrix-based simulations and an implementation on the ibm_kawasaki Heron r2 processor, we identify a trade-off: suppressing reservoir memory reduces the cooling-step count but generally increases the geometric protocol cost. Our work provides a resource-based perspective on reservoir engineering for algorithmic cooling.

Duality Between Twist-Angle Disorder and Non-Hermitian Disorder

Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 2/5 · analog quantum simulation 2/5 · correlated / nonlocal dissipation 1/5 · driven-dissipative phase transition 1/5 · methods for driven-dissipative 1/5 · non-equilibrium universality 1/5

Summary. This work studies twist-angle disorder in moiré heterobilayers, showing that the disorder averaging process leads to a profound duality. This duality maps the geometric disorder onto a problem involving non-Hermitian Hamiltonians. This provides a powerful new theoretical tool to understand transport phenomena, particularly how the system can evade Anderson localization.

Why it may be interesting. The duality connects a geometrically induced disorder (twist angle) to a non-Hermitian framework, which is a key area of modern condensed matter theory relevant to open quantum systems and non-Hermitian physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the physical consequences of random variations in the twist angle within moiré heterobilayer systems.

Main result. It establishes a mathematical duality showing that twist-angle disorder is equivalent to coupling fermions to a non-Hermitian disorder, which can dynamically prevent Anderson localization.

Method. The analysis employs the replica trick and cumulant expansion to average over the disorder fields, leading to an effective action.

Model / system. The system is based on moiré heterobilayer systems (e.g., twisted bilayer WSe2/MoSe2), modeled by a continuum Hamiltonian subjected to random spatial variations in the twist angle.

Key observables. Correlation functions between replicas, localization behavior, and the effective action terms ($S^{(1)}_{ ext{dis}}, S^{(2)}_{ ext{dis}}$).

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis considers distinct length scales: Moiré lattice constant $\ll$ domain size $\ll$ system size, and the disorder correlation length $\ell_ u$.

Assumptions / limitations. The disorder is modeled by random scalar fields governed by a specific probability distribution, and the analysis relies on weak disorder approximations.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the moiré potential under constant vs. disordered twist angles, the correlation pattern $g(r, r'; \delta R)$ showing positive and negative correlations, and the corresponding patterns in tight-binding descriptions.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the model Hamiltonian with twist-angle disorder, applies the replica trick and cumulant expansion to average the disorder, derives the effective action, and finally establishes the duality with non-Hermitian disorder.

Abstract

We propose and study a model of moire heterobilayer systems where the twist angle remains uniform and well defined only within a finite range, but deforms randomly at larger distances. The local twist angle is then subject to a certain probability distribution that penalizes large fluctuations around its mean value. Within the framework of the replica trick, we show that disorder averaging produces a nontrivial correlation with alternating sign between replicas, maximized only close the boundary of each local domain and along certain directions. We show that for the low-energy moire bands, exactly the same correlation can be generated in a dual model where fermions are coupled to non-Hermitian disorder, which can evade Anderson localization dynamically. This duality provides a new perspective to investigate twist-angle disorder in moire systems, and unveils some of the essential differences between twist-angle disorder and conventional disorder.

Summary. This paper generalizes the theory of quantum evolution generators to handle complex external parameters using Wirtinger derivatives. It derives coupled evolution equations for both the quantum state and the metric of the Hilbert space bundle. The key finding is that maintaining state normalization under complex parameter changes requires a specific, coupled contribution from both the parameter's generator and its conjugate counterpart.

Why it may be interesting. This is highly relevant for theoretical physicists working on open quantum systems or quantum optics, as many physical controls (like detunings or coupling strengths) can be complex, and preserving physical quantities like normalization is crucial.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study aims to extend the formalism for deriving quantum state evolution generators from real-valued to complex-valued external parameters.

Main result. The evolution of the metric of the Hilbert space bundle under complex parameters requires a coupled contribution from both the evolution generator and its conjugate counterpart to maintain state normalization.

Method. The authors employ Wirtinger derivatives to treat the complex parameter and its conjugate as independent variables, adapting established parameter-dependent quantum evolution formalisms.

Model / system. The work analyzes quantum systems whose Hamiltonian is dependent on complex parameters, $H(z, ar{z})$. The formalism tracks the evolution of the quantum state $|\Psi angle$ and the metric $G$ of the Hilbert space bundle.

Important parameters / regimes. Complex parameters $z$ and $ar{z}$, and the Hamiltonian $H(z, ar{z})$.

Figures summary. Not specified.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the necessity of extending the formalism to complex parameters, deriving coupled evolution equations for the metric $G$ and the generators $K_z, K_{ar{z}}$. Abstract

Theoretical studies on how quantum systems are affected by external factors are often formulated through parameter changes in the system's Hamiltonian. Beyond Berry connections and phases, which focus on specific Hamiltonian eigenstates, recent studies inspired by non-Hermitian quantum formalisms provide a framework for obtaining general state evolution generators for real-valued parameters. This study extends the applicability of the evolution generator formalism to complex-valued parameters via Wirtinger derivatives. By treating a complex parameter and its conjugate as independent variables, the evolution equations are derived for both quantum states and the metric of the Hilbert space bundle. The analysis demonstrates that while state evolution with respect to a complex parameter is naturally governed by its corresponding evolution generator, metric evolution requires a coupled contribution from both the generator and its conjugate counterpart to preserve state normalization. Explicit examples are worked out to illustrate the implementation and physical consistency of the formalism.

Summary. This paper develops a thermodynamic hierarchy for quantum many-body systems, showing that the amount of extractable work depends on the allowed set of operations. The key finding is that the excess work is bounded by the difference in entropy densities between the restricted and expanded operational levels. This provides a powerful, information-theoretic tool to quantify hidden work resources in quantum matter.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous, information-theoretic framework for quantifying non-equilibrium work extraction in quantum systems, directly linking thermodynamic potential differences to quantum information measures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the thermodynamic hierarchical structure of observables and allowed operations in isolated quantum many-body systems.

Main result. The extractable work when enlarging the set of allowed operations is bounded by the difference between the entropy densities of the restricted and enlarged operational levels.

Method. The authors establish quantitative bounds by relating work extraction to the difference in entropy functionals (e.g., mutual information density, Holevo information) across different operational regimes.

Model / system. The analysis focuses on isolated quantum many-body states ($ ho$) on a lattice, considering various operational constraints such as spatial resolution, control duration, and non-local connectivity.

Key observables. Extractable Work Density, Entropy Densities ($s_R, s_E$), Mutual-Information Density, Holevo Information, Total Correlation.

Important parameters / regimes. Inverse temperature ($eta$), system size ($L$), spatial resolution ($l$), and the difference between entropy levels.

Assumptions / limitations. The framework assumes cyclic operations with no heat exchange, and later sections rely on the existence of thermodynamic limits and decay properties of interaction profiles.

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically illustrates work extraction across thermodynamic levels, bounded by the entropy difference. Figure 2 illustrates work gain across different spatial scales, relating it to entropy differences.

Paper structure. The paper develops the concept of a thermodynamic hierarchy, first establishing general bounds on work extraction based on entropy differences, and then applying this framework to specific physical extensions like increased spatial resolution or nonlocal connectivity.

Abstract

Thermodynamics is operational in the sense that the very concept of thermal equilibrium depends crucially on the choice of observables, while the amount of extractable work is defined relative to the allowed operations. Here we show that isolated quantum many-body states admit a thermodynamic hierarchical structure of observables and allowed operations, where the same state can be thermal at one level of the hierarchy and athermal at another. Enlarging the set of allowed operations therefore renders such hidden athermality a potential resource for work extraction; the resulting work gain is bounded in terms of the difference between the entropy densities of the two levels. This entropy difference takes the form of the mutual-information density. We establish the bounds for three extensions of operational access: increased spatial resolution, increased duration of control, and nonlocal connectivity, which provide access to position-state Holevo information, correlations between neighboring regions and spatially nonlocal correlations, respectively. Thus the difference between entropies at different levels of the thermodynamic hierarchy governs the bound on the work gain associated with moving to a less restricted level.

A collider spin density matrix defines a two-qubit state, while its QSE gives the geometry of conditional states accessible through local measurements. This makes the ellipsoid a direct probe of the quantum properties of fundamental particles, encoding polarization, spin correlation anisotropy, accessible-state volume, and the orientation of the dominant correlation axes. Using top-quark pair production as a benchmark process, we show how QSE observables organize the Standard Model spin state, probe entanglement, steerability, and Bell-nonlocality criteria, and use an expected precision metric to assess sensitivity to non-local correlations in the boosted central region. We show that different dimension-six operators generate distinctive QSE deformations, and provide a geometric interpretation of quantum information observables in high-energy particle physics. Quantum steering geometry therefore provides a unified framework for particle collider tomography, quantum information diagnostics, and precision searches for physics beyond the Standard Model with applications spanning the HL--LHC and future lepton, muon, flavor, and electron-ion collider programs.

The Pego Theorem for the Hilbert--Schmidt Class

Summary. This work generalizes Pego's compactness theorem to the operator setting using quantum harmonic analysis. It provides a necessary and sufficient condition for a set of quantum operators to be precompact: the set must exhibit uniform equicontinuity both in the operator domain and in its Fourier-Weyl transform domain. This has direct applications in proving the stability of quantum states in quantum information and quantum estimation theory.

Why it may be interesting. This provides a powerful, general mathematical tool (the Quantum Pego Theorem) to rigorously prove the stability and convergence (precompactness) of quantum state ensembles arising from physical measurements or thermalization processes.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper establishes an operator-theoretic version of Pego's compactness theorem within the framework of quantum harmonic analysis on general locally compact abelian phase spaces.

Main result. A bounded set of Hilbert-Schmidt operators is precompact if and only if it is uniformly equicontinuous under phase-space shifts and its Fourier-Weyl transform is uniformly equicontinuous on the dual phase space.

Method. The proof relies on characterizing precompactness via the equivalence between operator equicontinuity and the equicontinuity of the Fourier-Weyl transform, leveraging properties of unitary isomorphisms.

Model / system. The theory is applied to quantum information contexts, specifically analyzing families of thermal states and quantum state estimators in multi-mode systems.

Key observables. Quantum characteristic functions ($\chi_{ ho}$), the Hilbert-Schmidt norm ($\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{T}^2}$), and the Fourier-Weyl transform ($FU$).

Abstract

This paper establishes an operator-theoretic version of Pego's compactness theorem within the framework of quantum harmonic analysis on general locally compact abelian phase spaces. We show that a bounded set of Hilbert-Schmidt operators is precompact if and only if it is uniformly equicontinuous under phase-space shifts and its Fourier-Weyl transform is uniformly equicontinuous on the dual phase space. We provide applications to quantum physics.
