Photon-efficient quantum repeater chains via hyperentanglement-assisted purification

Authors: Vinay Kumar, Krishan Joshi, Claudio Cicconetti
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.30786v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper introduces the DAHR protocol, a novel quantum repeater chain that enhances entanglement distribution by incorporating hyperentanglement-assisted purification (DAEPP) at every segment. By deriving a new fidelity recursion, the authors prove that DAHR is significantly more resource-efficient than established methods like BBPSSW purification, especially at low operating fidelities.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a concrete, theoretically derived improvement to quantum network protocols, directly impacting the feasibility and scaling of quantum communication infrastructure.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary goal is to improve the efficiency of entanglement distribution over long distances in quantum networks by developing a superior quantum repeater chain protocol.

Main result. The proposed DAHR protocol, which integrates hyperentanglement-assisted purification (DAEPP) into the repeater chain, significantly outperforms existing methods, requiring fewer resource pairs to achieve the same fidelity.

Method. The authors derive a closed-form, end-to-end fidelity recursion by embedding the DAEPP step into the standard BDCZ entanglement swapping scheme.

Model / system. The system is modeled as a linear chain of N segments, utilizing hyperentangled photon pairs encoding independent states across multiple degrees of freedom (e.g., polarization and spatial modes).

Key observables. End-to-end fidelity ($F^N$), resource consumption (number of entangled pairs consumed per segment).

Important parameters / regimes. Operation reliability threshold, initial polar/spatial fidelities ($F_P, F_S$), and the number of purification rounds ($k$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the spatial DOF is the more robust mode ($F_S > F_P$) and uses the general Werner model for purification analysis.

Figures summary. Figure 2 compares the end-to-end fidelity ($F_N$) versus the number of segments ($N$) for raw BDCZ, BDCZ with $k$ BBPSSW rounds, and the proposed DAHR protocol, showing DAHR's superior performance.

Paper structure. The paper first reviews existing repeater limitations, then derives the DAEPP fidelity, and finally constructs the DAHR protocol by integrating DAEPP into the BDCZ recurrence relation, concluding with a strict resource lower bound comparison.

Abstract

Linear quantum repeater chains based on Werner-state purification (the BBPSSW protocol) and entanglement swapping (the BDCZ scheme) are fundamental to entanglement distribution in quantum networks. However, they operate under a stringent operation reliability threshold and rely on resource-intensive recurrence purification rounds, each consuming two entangled pairs to probabilistically produce one. In the literature, hyperentanglement has been proposed to exploit multiple degrees of freedom (DOF), such as polarisation and spatial modes, to encode independent entangled states within a single photon pair. This has led to the definition of DAEPP (DOF-Assisted Entanglement Purification Protocol), which we propose to integrate with the BDCZ scheme, resulting in a chain protocol that we call DAHR (DOF-Assisted Hyperentanglement Repeater). The DAEPP step distils the fidelity of a DOF by consuming other(s). In this work, we propose and analyse a DAHR variant which integrates DAEPP at every segment of an end-to-end path combined with BDCZ. We derive a closed-form end-to-end fidelity recursion that embeds single-segment DAEPP into the BDCZ scheme and give a strict resource lower bound for any BDCZ baseline utilising BBPSSW purification to match DAHR's per-segment effective fidelity. At a representative asymmetric operating point informed by prior experiment, we show numerically that matching DAHR's single-photon-pair performance requires two to three rounds of BBPSSW purification. Additionally, below a critical operation reliability, no amount of BBPSSW rounds matches DAHR's one DAEPP round performance.

Protected Logical Qudits in Kitaev Quantum Double Models via Stable Representations

Authors: Naihong Hu, Futao Wang
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.31011v1

Summary. This paper develops a powerful representation-theoretic method to construct fault-tolerant logical qudits within Kitaev quantum double models. By analyzing stable representations, the authors establish existence criteria and demonstrate the realization of qudits for groups like $S_n$ and $A_4$. Ultimately, they show how these structures support universal quantum computation, advancing the theory of topological quantum computation.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous mathematical link between abstract group theory (representation theory) and the physical realization of robust quantum information storage and computation, which is fundamental to quantum computing theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to develop a representation-theoretic framework to construct and characterize protected logical qudits for fault-tolerant quantum computation.

Main result. The construction successfully yields protected logical qudits for various groups, including logical qubits for $S_n$ and a logical qutrit for $A_4$, and demonstrates universal quantum computation for the $D(A_4)$ model.

Method. The methodology involves using the representation theory of Kitaev quantum double models, specifically identifying $\epsilon$-stable irreducible representations to define the protected logical subspace.

Model / system. The physical model is the Kitaev quantum double model, defined over a lattice embedded in a closed oriented surface $\Sigma$, based on a finite group $G$. The protection mechanism relies on the ground state subspace defined by local vertex and plaquette operators.

Key observables. Logical subspace dimension ($d$), logical operators ($X_L$, Hadamard), and the existence of stable representations.

Important parameters / regimes. The dimension $d$ of the logical qudit, which is determined by the structure of the stable representations.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the validity of the Kitaev quantum double model structure and the existence of specific stable representations for the chosen groups.

Figures summary. The notes mention the structure of local operators ($A_g(s), B_h(s)$) and the character tables for $A_4$ and $D(A_4)$, which characterize the underlying group structure.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the general representation-theoretic framework for protected qudits, then applying this to specific groups ($S_n, A_4$), and finally demonstrating universal quantum computation using the $D(A_4)$ structure.

Abstract

Fault-tolerant quantum computation requires robust protection of encoded quantum information. In this work, we develop a representation-theoretic framework for constructing protected logical qudits in finite-group Kitaev quantum double models. By introducing $\varepsilon$-stable irreducible representations, we establish a necessary and sufficient existence criterion and derive ribbon--projector commutation relations that yield a $d$-dimensional protected logical subspace. We apply this construction to symmetric and alternating groups, obtaining logical qubits for $S_n$ ($n\ge 3$) and a logical qutrit for $A_4$. Moreover, the family $(\mathbb Z_2)^d\rtimes\mathbb Z_d$ realizes protected logical qudits of arbitrary dimension $d\ge 2$. Finally, for $D(A_4)$, we describe a scheme for universal logical qutrit computation using ribbon-based logical operations.

Summary. This paper introduces Quantum Block Turbo Codes (QBTC) as a powerful framework for quantum error correction, extending classical turbo coding concepts to the quantum domain. It details an iterative decoding algorithm that exchanges soft information between constituent code decoders. The work demonstrates that QBTCs achieve superior error correction rates, exhibiting a measurable 'turbo decoding effect' for multiple logical qubits.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a structured, iterative decoding framework for quantum codes that mimics successful classical techniques, offering a pathway to better performance in fault-tolerant quantum computation.

Main result. QBTCs allow for flexible encoding and decoding using iterative soft-information exchange between inner and outer decoders, demonstrating a clear 'turbo decoding effect' when using larger constituent codes.

Method. The core method involves an iterative maximum likelihood decoding technique that exchanges soft information between row and column decoding blocks, analogous to classical turbo decoding.

Model / system. The system uses Quantum Error Correction Codes (QECCs), specifically stabilizer codes, which are combined in a product structure using two constituent codes, $C_1$ and $C_2$. The encoding is implemented via a unitary transformation $V$ acting on physical qubits.

Key observables. Logical error rates, syndrome measurements ($S$), and the performance comparison against minimum weight (MW) decoders.

Important parameters / regimes. Code parameters $(n_1, k_1)$ and $(n_2, k_2)$, and the depolarizing channel noise rate.

Assumptions / limitations. The decoding process must operate at the level of logical operators due to inherent degeneracy in stabilizer codes, and the M & R procedure requires careful probabilistic handling.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the encoding matrix structure, the global view of the 2D product code encoding/decoding, and detailed diagrams of the SISO inner and outer decoding blocks.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from establishing the quantum analogy to classical turbo codes, detailing the encoding/decoding using two constituent codes, formalizing the soft-information exchange via M & R procedures, and finally extending the theory to multi-layer product codes.

Abstract

In the early nineties, the introduction of turbo codes revolutionized classical error correction. The idea was mainly applied on two types of codes: convolutional turbo codes and turbo product codes. The first type of codes was adapted to quantum error correction which initiated the theory of quantum serial turbo codes. In this paper, we present a theory for quantum block turbo codes, the quantum analog of the second type of turbo codes. We describe their iterative decoding algorithm and simulate their performances on a depolarizing channel for different constituent codes.

Quantum Pessiland

Authors: Boyang Chen, Tomoyuki Morimae, Takashi Yamakawa
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29493v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This paper explores 'Quantum Pessiland,' a theoretical model where computational hardness exists, but quantum cryptography fails. By constructing specific oracles, the authors prove that the non-existence of quantum primitives like EFI pairs is implied by average-case hardness assumptions. This work provides deep insights into the necessary computational assumptions required for quantum cryptography to function.

Why it may be interesting. While highly abstract, the work pushes the boundaries of quantum complexity theory by establishing precise boundaries on what quantum computation can achieve cryptographically, which is fundamental to understanding quantum information limits.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the existence of 'Quantum Pessiland,' a theoretical setting where computational hardness (NP hard on average) holds, but no quantum cryptographic primitives (like OWFs or EFI pairs) can exist.

Main result. The authors prove the existence of such a world relative to specific oracles, demonstrating that almost no quantum cryptography exists under these conditions, and further showing no sampling-based quantum advantage.

Method. The proof relies on constructing specific quantum and classical oracles and using advanced techniques involving the Patching Lemma to show that the average-case hardness of $ ext{UP} \cap ext{coUP}$ implies the non-existence of necessary cryptographic primitives.

Model / system. The model is purely theoretical, based on quantum complexity theory, utilizing quantum oracles, quantum advice, and cryptographic primitives like auxiliary-input EFI pairs and OWPuzzs.

Key observables. Statistical distance (SD) between quantum distributions, average-case hardness of $ ext{UP} \cap ext{coUP}$, and the existence/non-existence of specific quantum primitives.

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis involves parameters related to the required accuracy of simulations, such as $r(n)$ and the number of repetitions $R$.

Assumptions / limitations. The results are conditional on the existence of specific oracles and rely on advanced techniques like the exponential moment to overcome the limitations of auxiliary inputs.

Paper structure. The paper builds upon classical Pessiland concepts, first establishing results relative to a classical oracle (Theorem 1.1), then extending to a quantum oracle (Theorem 1.2), and finally analyzing implications for sampling-based advantage and open problems in complexity theory.

Abstract

Pessiland is a world where NP is hard on average but one-way functions (OWFs) do not exist [Impagliazzo 1995]. Because almost all classical cryptographic primitives imply OWFs [Impagliazzo and Luby 1989], there is almost no classical cryptography in Pessiland. On the other hand, quantum cryptography can exist even when OWFs do not [Kretschmer 2021; Morimae and Yamakawa 2022; Ananth, Qian and Yuen 2022]. Is there a quantum analogue of Pessiland where NP is hard on average but even quantum cryptography does not exist? In this paper, we show that such a miserable world, Quantum Pessiland, exists: there is a quantum oracle relative to which $UP\cap coUP$ is hard on average against quantum polynomial-time algorithms with quantum advice, yet auxiliary-input EFI pairs do not exist. We also show that there is a classical oracle relative to which $UP\cap coUP$ is hard on average against quantum polynomial-time algorithms with quantum advice, yet classically-secure auxiliary-input one-way puzzles (OWPuzzs) do not exist. Almost all quantum cryptographic primitives imply EFI pairs or OWPuzzs, and therefore these results mean that there is almost no quantum cryptography relative to these oracles. We further show that relative to the classical oracle, SampBQP = SampBPP, and therefore there is no sampling-based quantum advantage in Quantum Pessiland. Finally, because our average-case hardness of $UP\cap coUP$ implies $P^{\#P}\not\subseteq i.o.BQP/qpoly$, our result also implies that a non-relativizing proof technique is necessary to construct OWPuzzs solely from $P^{\#P}\not\subseteq i.o.BQP/qpoly$, which gives a partial negative answer to the open problem of [Khurana and Tomer 2025].

Quantum Preconditioning For Constrained Optimization Problems

Summary. This work proposes using quantum preconditioning, derived from the QAOA algorithm, to enhance classical solvers for hard constrained optimization problems like graph bi-partitioning. By injecting problem-specific correlation information into the objective function, the method significantly speeds up the search for near-optimal solutions. This validates a powerful hybrid framework where quantum algorithms guide classical exact solvers.

Why it may be interesting. It demonstrates a concrete, actionable hybrid quantum-classical workflow, showing how quantum information (correlations) can be used to enhance the performance of established classical solvers for hard optimization problems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses constrained combinatorial optimization problems, such as balanced graph bi-partitioning, which are NP-hard.

Main result. Quantum preconditioning, derived from QAOA correlations, significantly accelerates the discovery of high-quality incumbent solutions when guiding classical Mixed-Integer Programming (MIP) solvers.

Method. A hybrid approach is used where two-point correlations from the QAOA algorithm are used to construct a modified objective function that is passed to a classical MIP solver, while the original hard constraints are maintained.

Model / system. The problem is modeled on a weighted graph G=(V, E), aiming to partition vertices into two equal sets. The quantum component uses the QAOA ansatz to generate correlation information about the solution space.

Key observables. Speed and quality of finding near-optimal incumbent solutions; the correlation matrix Z; the time to reach an epsilon-optimal threshold.

Important parameters / regimes. QAOA depth (p), the penalty parameter (rho), and the graph size (n).

Assumptions / limitations. The core assumption is that the correlation matrix Z derived from QAOA encodes meaningful structural information that guides the classical solver effectively.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the workflow showing QAOA generating Z to modify the MIP objective, and later figures compare the scaling of time to optimality using different rho selection strategies.

Paper structure. The paper first introduces the concept of quantum preconditioning for constrained optimization, then details its application to balanced graph bi-partitioning using QAOA, and finally analyzes the performance scaling and parameter selection strategies.

Abstract

We study the effect of quantum preconditioning on constrained combinatorial optimization problems, focusing on balanced graph bi-partitioning. The proposed approach uses two-point correlations between decision variables derived from the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) to construct a modified objective function that is subsequently provided to mixed-integer programming (MIP) solvers. The preconditioned MIP formulation retains the original hard constraint, and all incumbent solutions are evaluated under the original objective. Computational experiments on dense, weighted complete-graph instances show that the preconditioned problem instances reach near-optimal solutions faster, with most of the benefit already realized at the shallowest QAOA depth tested. Solver callback trajectories show this arises from earlier discovery of high-quality incumbents during the solution search. These results support a hybrid optimization framework in which quantum algorithms provide problem-specific information to guide classical exact MIP solvers.

Real-space manifestation of ferroic multipoles in altermagnetic MnF$_2$

Summary. This work uses polarized-neutron diffraction on MnF2 to overcome the challenge of observing magnetic multipoles in altermagnets. By analyzing symmetry-dependent scattering intensities, the researchers successfully reconstructed the real-space spin density. The results confirm the existence of ferroic multipolar order, providing a powerful new experimental probe for hidden magnetic interactions in quantum materials.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on solid-state magnetism, the use of advanced diffraction techniques to probe higher-order, symmetry-protected magnetic moments (multipoles) is a powerful methodology applicable to understanding complex quantum ground states in correlated materials.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The primary challenge is the direct, real-space observation of magnetic multipolar order parameters in altermagnets, which are theoretically predicted but experimentally elusive.

Main result. The study provides direct real-space evidence of ferroic multipolar order in the altermagnet MnF2, revealing both a finite fluorine ion moment and an anisotropic Mn magnetization.

Method. Polarized-neutron diffraction (PND) was employed, utilizing symmetry-selective magnetic reflections to reconstruct the three-dimensional magnetization density.

Model / system. The system studied is the prototypical altermagnet MnF2, which is characterized by specific magnetic symmetries that mandate the existence of multipolar order parameters like the magnetic triacontadipole O52.

Key observables. Reconstructed spin density, finite fluorine ion moment, anisotropic Mn magnetization, and the multipolar coefficients (e.g., P4^-2).

Important parameters / regimes. The analysis is sensitive to the magnetic field (1 T vs 6 T) and the separation of contributions from even and odd reflections.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the magnetic order does not break lattice translation symmetry, and certain chiral terms are omitted due to the crystal's symmetry.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate domain structures (Mn and F moments) under different fields, and spin density maps show the spatial distribution of magnetic moments, confirming multipolar character.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by establishing the theoretical requirement for multipolar order in altermagnets, detailing the PND methodology for separating magnetic contributions, and finally presenting the results showing the measured moments confirming the predicted multipolar order.

Abstract

Altermagnets are unconventional spin split magnets arising from the zero spin-orbit coupled limit. They host a magnetic multipolar order parameter yet direct real-space observation of these multipoles has remained elusive. Here we use polarized-neutron diffraction to reconstruct the three-dimensional magnetization density of the prototypical altermagnet MnF$_2$. By exploiting symmetry-selective magnetic reflections, we separate the dominant spherical Mn$^{2+}$ contribution from the much weaker anisotropic Mn magnetization and the covalent spin polarization of the fluorine ligands. The reconstructed spin density reveals a finite fluorine ion moment together with an anisotropic Mn magnetization consistent with the symmetry-allowed altermagnetic rank-5 magnetic multipole $O_{52}$(magnetic triacontadipole). These results provide direct real-space evidence of ferroic multipolar order in an altermagnet and establish polarized-neutron diffraction as a powerful probe of hidden magnetic multipoles in quantum materials.

Representation Learning with Quantum Signal Processing

Authors: Junqi Wang, Junyu Liu
Type: theory · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.28828v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work develops a rigorous theory for representation learning using Quantum Signal Processing (QSP) as the model. It analyzes the evolution of the quantum data geometry, proving that the dynamics simplify to integrable scalar flows in sparse data regimes but become complex and coupled in dense regimes. This provides a controlled, first-principles understanding of quantum machine learning dynamics.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical treatment of learning dynamics using quantum resources provides a powerful theoretical framework for understanding how quantum information processing can model complex, non-linear machine learning phenomena.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to establish a controlled, first-principles theory for how quantum data geometry is learned during training, moving beyond the limitations of simple frozen-kernel models.

Main result. It proves that for sparse data, the full nonlinear QSP gradient flow converges to independent, integrable scalar equations, while in the dense regime, the dynamics become coupled and deviate from simpler predictions.

Method. The authors use rigorous mathematical analysis, including calculating exact means and variances of the Quantum Neural Tangent Kernel (QNTK) and deriving convergence bounds using techniques like Gronwall's inequality.

Model / system. The core model is Quantum Signal Processing (QSP), which is used as a solvable quantum model for representation learning. The dynamics are governed by the gradient flow of a loss function, leading to the evolution of the QNTK.

Key observables. The Quantum Neural Tangent Kernel (QNTK), the loss function $L(t)$, and the convergence error $\epsilon_T$ are key observables.

Important parameters / regimes. Depth ($d$), sample density ($n$), and the ratio $w = n/\sqrt{d}$ define the different learning regimes (sparse vs. dense).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes specific scaling conditions (e.g., $n^{3/2}[1 + \log n] / \sqrt{M} o 0$ for the sparse limit) and relies on the QSP model being an adequate representation of the learning process.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the convergence of the normalized diagonal QNTK to a Beta distribution and show the QSP training trajectory deviating from scalar or frozen-kernel predictions in the dense data regime.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the QSP model, calculating kernel statistics in the large-depth limit, then deriving convergence guarantees for the full nonlinear gradient flow, and finally comparing the full dynamics against simplified scalar and frozen-kernel approximations across sparse and dense regimes.

Abstract

Representation learning begins when training changes the features that define similarity between data. A frozen-kernel model only reweights a fixed geometry. We establish quantum signal processing (QSP) as a solvable quantum model of the representation-learning regime. At arbitrary depth, we compute the exact mean and variance of its quantum neural tangent kernel, revealing an input-dependent angular geometry whose diagonal remains non-self-averaging even when the underlying unitary approaches Haar randomness. We also prove a sparse-data guarantee for the full nonlinear gradient flow without freezing or ensemble-averaging the kernel: the realized dynamics converges to an integrable scalar flow with a time-dependent kernel closure and explicit convergence times. A finite-depth speed limit holds for every data set and trajectory. At higher data density, numerical results show coupled evolution beyond both the scalar and frozen-kernel descriptions. These results give a controlled theory of learned quantum data geometry with provable training dynamics beyond the frozen limit.

Rényi Entanglement of Purification Is Non-additive

Summary. This paper rigorously analyzes the additivity of the Rényi Entanglement of Purification for classical two-qubit states. It establishes that the measure is non-additive for Rényi orders between 0 and 1, but it becomes additive for orders greater than or equal to 2. The open interval $1 \le \alpha < 2$ remains a key open problem.

Why it may be interesting. The study provides precise mathematical boundaries ($\alpha=1$ and $\alpha=2$) for when fundamental entanglement measures exhibit simple scaling properties, which is crucial for understanding resource theories in quantum information.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the additivity property of the Rényi Entanglement of Purification ($ ext{EoP}$), a measure of total correlations, for classical states on two qubits.

Main result. The $ ext{EoP}$ is proven to be non-additive for Rényi orders $\alpha \in [0, 1)$ but is proven to be additive for $\alpha \ge 2$.

Method. The authors use analytical proofs involving comparing the two-copy case to the product of single-copy values, employing concavity arguments and norm inequalities.

Model / system. The study focuses on classical two-qubit states, $ ho_P = \sum p_{ij}|ij angle\langle ij|$, and their tensor products, analyzing the entanglement measure $ ext{E}_\alpha^P( ho_P)$.

Key observables. Rényi Entanglement of Purification ($ ext{E}\alpha^P$), Rényi entropy ($ ext{S}\alpha$), and the nonadditive fraction.

Important parameters / regimes. The Rényi order $\alpha$, specifically distinguishing regimes $0 \le \alpha < 1$, $1 \le \alpha < 2$, and $\alpha \ge 2$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the canonical purification being optimal for one copy, and the additivity for $\alpha \ge 2$ is derived from a norm inequality.

Figures summary. Figure 1 shows the nonadditive fraction of violating states for $0 < \alpha < 1$. Figure 2 summarizes the phase diagram, showing nonadditivity for $\alpha < 1$ and additivity for $\alpha \ge 2$.

Paper structure. The paper first solves the one-copy optimization exactly. It then proves nonadditivity for $\alpha < 1$ using specific state constructions. Subsequently, it proves additivity for $\alpha \ge 2$ using norm inequalities, leaving the interval $1 \le \alpha < 2$ open.

Abstract

Entanglement of purification is a fundamental measure of total correlations whose additivity remains unresolved. We study its additivity for classical states on two qubits at different Rényi orders. For every $α\in[0,1)$, we prove nonadditivity within this family, witnessed by two copies of a single state. We first solve the one-copy optimization exactly for the entire family at every Rényi order. We then restrict the two-copy optimization to a natural finite set of purifications and exhibit one whose entropy is strictly below the product value. In contrast, for $α\in[2,\infty]$ we prove additivity under tensor products within this family. The interval $α\in[1,2)$, including the von Neumann case $α=1$, remains open, and we conjecture additivity there throughout the same family.

Summary. This paper solves the inverse eigenvalue problem for the partial transpose of two-qubit states, determining the exact conditions under which a given spectrum is physically realizable. It establishes necessary and sufficient conditions using simple algebraic inequalities ($q \leq y$ and $qy \leq xz$) and provides methods to calculate exact bounds on entanglement measures like negativity using only low-order moments.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides fundamental, quantitative constraints on the structure of entanglement and non-separability (via the partial transpose) for small quantum systems, which is crucial for understanding quantum resource theories.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper solves the inverse eigenvalue problem for the partial transpose ($ ho^{\Gamma_B}$) of two-qubit quantum states, aiming to characterize all possible spectra that can arise from physical states.

Main result. For two qubits, the necessary and sufficient conditions for a spectrum $\lambda = (x, y, z, -q)$ to be realized by an NPT state are $q \leq y$ and $qy \leq xz$. Abstract

Single electron spin qubits trapped in SiMOS quantum dots are a promising technology for scaling to thousand- or million-qubit systems due to their compatibility with mature CMOS manufacturing processes. A readout system that combines a single electron transistor with a custom cryogenic CMOS amplification and digitization chain offers key advantages by avoiding the use of bulky RF components or room-temperature interconnects. We present design techniques and simulation results for an optimized qubit-SET cryoCMOS interface, culminating in the design of the QNDR1 ASIC, the first cryogenic readout ASIC designed under the Quandarum project, which targets the development of a many-channel spin qubit based detector for use in high energy physics.

Abstract

A detailed study of emission wavelength tailoring of GaSb-based QDs formed by InGaSb-filling of droplet-etched nanoholes in AlGaSb is presented. The study shows that the emission wavelength can be modified from 1.48 um to the center of the telecom C-band at 1.55 mm by independently varying the QD composition and size. More specifically, the optical transition energy shifts linearly as a function of In-content of the QD material at a rate of -4.4 meV/In-percentage, and with the number of monolayers (ML) of material used for filling the nanoholes, at -2.0 meV/ML. These experimentally observed energy shifts are well predicted by simulations yielding rates of -4.3 meV/In-percentage and -2.1 meV/ML, respectively. For the simulation, a uniform In composition, low intermixing, and microscopically measured QD geometry is considered. Additionally, excellent ensemble QD uniformity, with unprecedented inhomogeneous broadening well-below 7 meV across all samples is demonstrated. Finally, photoluminescence of single-QDs reveals narrow excitonic emission lines of 13.8+/-6.7 ueV and low fine-structure splitting values reaching <10 ueV. These results identify GaSb-based LDE QDs as a tunable telecom platform for scaling quantum-photonic applications over long-haul optical fiber networks.
