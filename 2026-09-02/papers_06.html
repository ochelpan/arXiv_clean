Determinant Quantum-Quantum Monte Carlo: Coherent Auxiliary-Field Sampling

Authors: Xuepeng Wang, Sagnik Banerjee, Debanjan Chowdhury
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.28742v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 2/5

Summary. This paper presents DQ2MC, a quantum algorithm designed to simulate strongly correlated electron systems by quantum mechanically implementing the sampling core of classical DQMC. It achieves this by synthesizing amplitudes directly from the action matrix, thus bypassing the exponential storage and sampling bottlenecks. The resulting hybrid protocol offers a path to quantum simulation that retains classical acceleration techniques while improving scaling over classical methods.

Why it may be interesting. The development of quantum algorithms for simulating interacting fermions directly impacts the field of quantum simulation, offering a potential quantum advantage over classical Monte Carlo methods for materials science.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the computational bottleneck in simulating strongly correlated lattice fermions, such as those found in the Hubbard model, where interactions are comparable to kinetic energy.

Main result. They introduce Determinant Quantum-Quantum Monte Carlo (DQ2MC), a quantum algorithm that lifts the auxiliary-field sampling onto a quantum computer, avoiding the enumeration of exponentially many weights.

Method. The approach involves adapting the classical Determinant Quantum-Monte Carlo (DQMC) method by implementing its core sampling and averaging steps using quantum circuits, leading to both fully-quantum and hybrid quantum-classical protocols.

Model / system. The focus is on strongly correlated lattice fermions, exemplified by the Hubbard model, relevant to high-temperature superconductivity and Mott insulators. The simulation relies on rewriting the partition function via the Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation.

Key observables. Thermal expectation values of operators, such as the imaginary-time susceptibility $\chi_O(q, i\Omega_n)$.

Important parameters / regimes. The scaling is analyzed concerning the spatial volume ($N_{st}$), the imaginary time ($eta$), and the overhead associated with the fermion sign problem.

Assumptions / limitations. The hybrid protocol assumes that only classical information passes between quantum updates, and the full-quantum protocol assumes the one-body vertices can be implemented as controlled unitaries.

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically compares classical DQMC (Markov chain) with DQ2MC (coherent superposition) and illustrates the measurement process using a Hadamard test estimator.

Paper structure. The paper reviews classical DQMC, then details the fully-quantum protocol (III), develops the hybrid quantum-classical protocol (IV), and discusses extensions.

Abstract

We introduce determinant quantum-quantum Monte Carlo (DQ$^2$MC), a quantum algorithm that lifts the auxiliary-field sampling and averaging at the operational core of determinant quantum Monte Carlo onto a quantum computer. A determinant oracle synthesizes the DQMC amplitudes directly from a block encoding of the single-particle action matrix via quantum singular value transformations, so that the exponentially many Hubbard-Stratonovich weights are never enumerated, precomputed, or stored. Since the fermions are free for fixed auxiliary fields, the construction operates entirely at the single-particle level, requiring $O(\log N_{\mathrm{st}})$ system qubits and no Jordan-Wigner or Bravyi-Kitaev encoding, where $N_{\mathrm{st}}$ is the space-time volume. A full-quantum protocol makes observables interference amplitudes, eliminating the Markov chain and its autocorrelation time altogether; a hybrid quantum-classical protocol retains a constant-size active block of qubits and replaces the Metropolis-Hastings acceptance step with an exact heat-bath draw, so that cluster updates of any size are rejection-free, and passes only classical information between updates, admitting parallel tempering and distributed execution across quantum processors. The circuit-depth scales more favorably with spatial volume than classical DQMC, at the price of a post-selection overhead determined exactly by the largest target probability --- polynomial for smooth distributions, exponential for sharply peaked ones. Finally, the reweighting estimator underlying the fermion sign problem maps exactly onto a quantum weak value, placing the exponential cost of sign-problematic DQMC in precise correspondence with the post-selection overhead of weak-value extraction.

Quantum information in neutron-proton scattering from the $M$ matrix

Summary. This theoretical paper analyzes quantum information in neutron-proton scattering using the spin-space M-matrix. By comparing results from four different input state classes—ranging from classical mixtures to highly entangled states—the authors show that output spin correlations depend critically on the input state's coherence and entanglement. This provides a new, ensemble-based understanding of quantum correlations in nuclear reactions.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a rigorous, ensemble-based description of how quantum correlations are generated in a fundamental scattering process, linking fundamental quantum information theory metrics to nuclear physics observables.

Main problem. The study investigates the quantum-information aspects of neutron-proton scattering, specifically how input quantum state properties influence the output spin correlations.

Main result. Outgoing spin correlations are jointly governed by scattering kinematics and the structure of the incoming quantum ensemble; stronger input coherence/entanglement leads to larger quantum information measures.

Method. The authors calculate ensemble-averaged output quantum measures (Mutual Information, Negativity, Quantum Discord) using the spin-space M-matrix framework across four distinct classes of input states.

Model / system. The physical system is neutron-proton (np) scattering. The analysis uses the spin-space M-matrix framework to map input spin states ($ ho_{ ext{in}}$) to output states ($ ho_{ ext{out}}$). Input states range from classical mixed states to general entangled pure states.

Key observables. Ensemble-averaged Mutual Information, Reduced-state Linear Entropy, Negativity, and Geometric Quantum Discord.

Abstract

We study quantum-information aspects of neutron--proton scattering in the spin-space $M$-matrix framework. Four representative classes of input states are considered, namely diagonal mixed states, separable pure states, general two-qubit pure states, and a special Schmidt-like entangled subclass. For each class, ensemble-averaged output mutual information, reduced-state linear entropy, negativity, and geometric quantum discord are calculated in the relative momentum-- scattering angle plane. The results show that the outgoing spin correlations are governed jointly by scattering kinematics and by the structure of the incoming quantum ensemble. Input states with stronger intrinsic coherence or entanglement give larger maxima and higher minima in the mutual information, negativity, and geometric quantum discord. The enhanced regions of the mutual information and geometric discord depend on the input states, while the negativity maximum remains concentrated in the high-momentum backward-scattering region. These results extend earlier studies based on product-state entanglement power and provide an ensemble-based description of how spin correlations in neutron--proton scattering arise from the interplay between input-state structure and scattering dynamics.

Summary. This paper unifies multiple mathematical descriptions of quantum non-local games—including those based on projection lattices, hypergraphs, and rule operators—into a single operator-algebraic framework. By identifying the 'winning transformation space' as the central object, the authors show that these disparate models describe the same underlying mathematical structure. This provides a powerful, consistent theoretical tool for analyzing quantum correlations and non-locality.

Why it may be interesting. While the topic is abstract quantum information theory, the rigorous mathematical framework connecting different physical models (like graph homomorphisms) to a single algebraic structure is highly relevant for developing general theoretical tools in quantum physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper aims to systematically compare various mathematical formalisms used to describe quantum non-local games and establish a unified operator-algebraic framework for them.

Main result. The winning strategies across diverse game models (projection-lattice, hypergraph, rule operators) are unified by the concept of an operator bimodule, with projection-lattice games capturing the reflexive part of this structure.

Method. The authors use operator algebra techniques, defining the 'winning transformation space' as the central object of comparison across different game definitions.

Model / system. The core model is the quantum non-local game, involving separated players and a referee, formalized using input/output von Neumann algebras and projection test structures.

Key observables. The winning transformation space (an operator bimodule), the game value (probability), and the property of 'perfectness' of a strategy.

Important parameters / regimes. The structure of the input/output von Neumann algebras ($X, Y, A, B$) and the referee algebra ($\mathcal{R}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis extends to arbitrary, possibly infinite-dimensional, referee von Neumann algebras ($\mathcal{R}$), and relies on comparing the 'reflexive' parts of the various formalisms.

Figures summary. Figure 1 summarizes the main quantum-game formalisms compared, including Projection-lattice, Rule operator, and Hypergraph quantum games.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by defining the prototypical projection-test game, then systematically comparing its winning space to those derived from projection-lattice games, hypergraph games, and rule operators, culminating in comparisons of concurrency conditions.

Abstract

Quantum non-local games with quantum inputs or outputs have been formulated in several different languages, including rank-one and quantum XOR games, support maps between projection lattices, probabilistic quantum hypergraphs, and Frobenius-algebraic rule operators on finite quantum sets. We give a unified operator-algebraic comparison of these models by assigning to each rule its winning transformation space: the operator space of transformations accepted with certainty. We introduce \(\mathcal R\)-projection-test quantum games with finite-dimensional input and output von Neumann algebras and an arbitrary, possibly infinite-dimensional, referee von Neumann algebra \(\mathcal R\). Their winning transformation spaces are precisely operator spaces with a natural bimodule structure, equivalently rectangular quantum relations. We compare this formalism with projection-lattice games, hypergraph quantum games, and the graphical rule-operator definition. Projection-lattice games capture exactly the reflexive winning bimodules. Hypergraph quantum games admit value-preserving projection-test realisations, and, after passing to perfect transformations, describe the same reflexive part as projection-lattice games. Using Daws' technique, we also translate rule operators to projections in tensor products of finite-dimensional von Neumann algebras. This identification places graphical rules in the same operator-bimodule framework and preserves both values and perfectness. Finally, we compare concurrency with the synchronicity conditions of Goldberg and of Bochniak--Kasprzak--Sołtan. The framework is illustrated by classical, rank-one, quantum XOR, colouring, and quantum graph homomorphism and isomorphism games.

Abstract

We study the topological structure of the quantum phase diagram of gapped systems by identifying the noncontractibility of loops, so-called $S^1$-families, within the gapped phase diagram in which many-body Hamiltonians can have nontrivial ground-state degeneracy. We manifest the role of symmetries in such $S^1$-family classifications by the exotic symmetry interplay: (i) nontrivial mixed anomalies, (ii) semi-direct product relation between the spontaneously broken and the unbroken symmetries, and (iii) symmetry with noninvertible operators. We find that such structures lead to the novel $S^1$-family classifications inaccessible by earlier classifications based on ``independent'' symmetries. Furthermore, we construct lattice realizations of these $S^1$-families and explicitly demonstrate their novel algebraic structures.

Summary. This theoretical work proves that the quantum supremum of the $I_{3322}$ Bell inequality cannot be reached using any finite-dimensional quantum strategy. The authors show that while the supremum is approached by a specific sequence of strategies, achieving it requires an infinitely large local dimension. This highlights a non-closure property for quantum correlations in this specific scenario.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a rigorous mathematical boundary on the correlations achievable in quantum information protocols, demonstrating a fundamental gap between the supremum and any finite-dimensional realization.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates whether the quantum supremum of the $I_{3322}$ Bell inequality can be attained using strategies restricted to finite local Hilbert space dimensions.

Main result. The authors prove that the quantum supremum is approached by a specific family of strategies (Pál–Vértesi) but is never actually attained in any finite dimension.

Method. The proof uses symmetry arguments on the Bell functional, mapping strategies to finite probability matrices, and employing contradiction to show that optimality requires an unnormalizable state.

Model / system. The system is defined by the $I_{3322}$ Bell inequality, which is a specific quantum correlation test involving four measurement settings (3, 3, 2, 2). The analysis focuses on the limitations imposed by finite local dimensions.

Key observables. The Bell value $I_{3322}$, the supremum $eta_{PV}$, and the upper-bound functional $\Phi( heta)$ derived from spectral-weight matrices.

Important parameters / regimes. The local Hilbert-space dimension ($n$), which must be unbounded to approach the supremum.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on reducing the Bell value to an upper bound $\Phi( heta)$ dependent on the spectral-weight matrix $ heta$, and formalizing the proof in Lean 4.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the Pál–Vértesi construction pattern for measurements, and Figure 2 shows how the spectral-weight matrix entries are reproduced by the PV chains.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the equality between the quantum supremum and the limit derived from the PV family, then uses contradiction based on optimality conditions to prove non-attainment in finite dimensions, concluding that the set of finite-dimensional correlations is not closed.

Abstract

In 2010, Pál and Vértesi found a family of finite-dimensional strategies for the $I_{3322}$ Bell inequality whose optimized values appeared to converge as the local Hilbert-space dimension grew. They conjectured that this limit is the supremum over all finite-dimensional quantum strategies, but that no finite-dimensional strategy attains it. We prove both claims. The proof uses the symmetry of the Bell functional to associate every strategy with a finite matrix of probabilities, one for each pair of spectral subspaces of Alice and Bob. This matrix gives an upper bound on the Bell value, and finite-dimensional strategies built from the repeating structure found by Pál and Vértesi approach it as the dimension grows. If the bound were attained exactly in finite dimension, the optimality conditions would then require a state that cannot be normalized. Consequently, the set of finite-dimensional quantum correlations is not closed in the $(3,3,2,2)$ scenario, the smallest Bell scenario where this can happen. Moreover, approaching the supremum requires unbounded local dimension. The core of the proof was formalized in Lean~4.

Abstract

Diffusion processes involving membranes are of fundamental importance in both science and technology, since membranes serve as selective interfaces that regulate the transport of mass, charge, and information across multiple length and time scales. Additionally, both the tetrahedral order parameter and the HB lifetime are only locally sensitive to the action of the membranes.

Summary. This paper introduces a novel, exact method using modular annihilators to construct parent Hamiltonians whose ground state corresponds to a purified Gibbs state. It achieves optimal spectral properties for free-fermion models and provides a systematic approximation scheme for complex interacting systems. This advances the theoretical understanding of how finite-temperature dynamics can be mapped onto ground-state problems.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides rigorous, non-perturbative methods to connect thermal equilibrium states (Gibbs states) to ground-state physics via parent Hamiltonians, which is crucial for understanding open quantum systems dynamics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper develops an exact, finite Sum-of-Squares (SoS) construction for the parent Hamiltonians associated with purified Gibbs states, bypassing continuous time integrals.

Main result. The modular-annihilator construction achieves the optimal relative spectral gap of 1 for the parent Hamiltonian, and the optimal free-fermion choice yields rapid mixing independent of inverse temperature.

Method. The core method involves using modular annihilators derived from a finite set of Hermitian generators to construct the parent Hamiltonian $M_H$ such that the purified Gibbs state is its unique ground state.

Model / system. The study focuses on purified Gibbs states ($ ho_eta$), which bridge finite-temperature physics and ground-state methods. The analysis covers free-fermion models (using Majorana operators) and general interacting systems.

Key observables. Spectral gap of the parent Hamiltonian ($ ext{Gap}(M_H)$), mixing time, and the relative spectral gap $r(\{J_a\})$.

Important parameters / regimes. Inverse temperature ($eta$), interaction strength, and the dimension of the Krylov approximation.

Abstract

Purified Gibbs states provide a bridge between finite-temperature physics, dissipative dynamics, and ground-state methods. In this work, we study the exact finite sum-of-squares (SoS) construction of their parent Hamiltonians and the associated Lindbladian based on modular annihilators. Given a finite set of Hermitian generators, the corresponding modular annihilators yield a frustration-free SoS representation without continuous time integrals or an explicit decomposition into Bohr-frequency sectors. The purified Gibbs state remains a common zero mode while the freedom to choose and combine the generators can be used to optimize the spectral properties of the parent Hamiltonian. For free-fermion Hamiltonians, modular transformations act linearly on Majorana operators, leading to an analytically solvable family of parent Hamiltonians parameterized by a real symmetric coefficient matrix \(S\). For the scalar-functional subclass $S=f(h)$, we show that, at fixed operator norm, the choice $S_{\mathrm{opt}}\propto 1/\sqrt{\cosh(2βh)}$ has mixing time upper bound $2\log(2N/ε)$ for any $β$, which exhibits rapid mixing and is irrelevant to the inverse temperature $β$. For interacting systems, where the modularly dressed generators are not available in closed form, we introduce a Krylov--Lanczos approximation scheme and bound the resulting ground-state error in terms of the modular-approximation error and the parent-Hamiltonian gap. Numerical results illustrate the free-fermion spectral advantage and show how the accuracy of the interacting construction depends on temperature, interaction strength, and Krylov dimension.

Abstract

We investigate the redistribution of continuous-variable Gaussian quantum discord under the Unruh effect for both one and two uniformly accelerated observers. The discord shared between the inertial mode and the accessible accelerated mode, as well as that between the two accessible accelerated modes, decays monotonically with acceleration and vanishes in the limit $a\to\infty$, while the Unruh effect generates discord in all causally disconnected Rindler mode pairs. The initial quantum correlation is therefore redistributed across Rindler partitions rather than destroyed. We further characterize how the squeezing parameter $s$ and field frequency $ω$ control the discord in different mode-pair classes. For causally connected pairs, $s$ and $ω$ exert comparable, interchangeable effects; for cross-region pairs, the squeezing parameter dominates; and for intra-observer cross-region pairs, the field frequency is more influential. In the two-observer case, the $A_{II}$-$B_{II}$ pair exhibits non-monotonic behavior with a peak whose position shifts to larger $a$ as $ω$ increases, and both parameters contribute only weakly except near an optimal $\fracω{s}$ ratio.

Abstract

In a slowly varying aperiodic potential $h_i=h\cos(2πβi^n+φ)$ with random power-law density interactions $V_{ij}/|i-j|^α$, the resonant-object ensemble changes across $α=2n$, wherever Hartree fragmentation is operative, from isolated two-level bonds to a mixture of fragmented multi-site clusters and isolated wing bonds that survive at finite density, while the pair-resonance scaling $x_{\rm pair}^{4-2n-α}\ln x_{\rm pair}\propto h^2/(Vt_0)$ is unchanged~\cite{letter}. Here we develop the microscopic resonance theory underlying these results, together with its domain of validity. We derive the exact phase-averaged resonance statistics --- the supply $N_0\propto L^{2-n}/h$, the correlated common-phase comb, and the closed-form Hartree variance --- proving that the interaction leaves the leading supply law unchanged. Exact construction of the resonant Fock-space graph at $L\le18$ shows that the order-one forward-branching scale $h_{\rm FB}\propto L^{2-n}$ carries no giant component: one-step branching and connectivity are inequivalent. We separate the fixed-pair matching law from the shell-averaged $q\ln(1/q)$ law of the bond ensemble and develop the comb into a mesoscopic shell theory; we map the fragmentation domain, with its support threshold $V_*(α)$ and the boundary-healing recursion; and we treat the marginal case $α=2$, where shell and matching marginalities compound into a double logarithm. Three long-range thresholds emerge with distinct meanings --- $α=1/2$ (the exact variance threshold of the random Fock-space energy and the square-summability boundary of the leading LIOM-dressing estimate), $α=2n$ (change of the local resonant objects), and $α=2$ (marginality of the long-range shell sum) --- none of which is, by itself, a localization transition.
