Summary. This work analyzes a two-bead model coupled to two baths at different temperatures to study nonequilibrium steady states. It establishes that kinetic temperatures are the fundamental variables governing the system's thermodynamics, unifying energy, heat transport, and entropy production. The findings highlight a crucial distinction between path-dependent energy observables and robust, temperature-governed transport measures.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical framework connecting energy, transport, and entropy via kinetic temperatures provides a powerful, generalizable tool for analyzing non-equilibrium dynamics in complex physical systems, relevant to open quantum systems modeling.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the characterization of nonequilibrium steady states (NESS) in coupled systems subjected to thermal baths at different temperatures, focusing on the role of inertia.

Main result. Kinetic temperatures are shown to be the fundamental organizing variables that unify energy, heat transport, and entropy production in the system.

Method. The analysis employs a covariance-matrix approach combined with numerical simulations of underdamped Langevin dynamics to solve for steady-state properties.

Model / system. The model is a bead-spring system consisting of two beads with arbitrary masses, coupled by a spring, and interacting independently with two thermal baths at distinct temperatures ($T_1$ and $T_2$).

Key observables. Steady-state probability distributions, heat transport ($\dot{Q}$), entropy production ($\Delta S_{tot}/ au$), and kinetic temperatures ($T_{kin}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Bead masses ($m_1, m_2$), spring constant ($\kappa$), and bath temperatures ($T_1, T_2$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is performed within a linear Langevin framework, and the results are shown to be robust for transport/irreversibility measures even when energy observables show path dependence in singular mass limits.

Figures summary. Figure 1 schematically shows the two beads coupled to two baths. Figure 2 compares the marginal position distribution derived theoretically versus numerically. Figure 3 shows the analytical marginal position distribution for varying bead masses.

Paper structure. The paper develops the dynamics using Langevin equations, analyzes the steady-state covariance matrix, derives key thermodynamic quantities like heat flux and entropy production, and culminates in establishing kinetic temperatures as the universal variables governing the system's thermodynamics.

Abstract

We investigate a nonequilibrium steady-state model consisting of two coupled beads with arbitrary masses in contact with two thermal baths at different temperatures. Using a covariance-matrix approach together with numerical simulations of the underdamped Langevin dynamics, we characterize steady-state probability distributions, heat transport, and entropy production. We show that irreversibility measures such as entropy production and heat current are invariant under an exchange of the bead masses, whereas energy-storage observables depend explicitly on the mass arrangement in a symmetrical set up. This reveals a fundamental distinction: energy observables exhibit path dependence in singular mass limits, while transport and irreversibility remain well defined. We show that kinetic temperatures provide the natural variables governing the thermodynamics of the system: their difference controls transport and entropy production, while their sum determines the mean energy via a model specific generalized equipartition relation. In the infinite-mass limit, only constitutive relations expressed in terms of kinetic temperatures remain meaningful. Thus, energy, transport, and irreversibility are unified through kinetic temperatures as the organizing variables. We also derive an effective temperature that defines an equilibrium-like canonical distribution. Finally, we analyze the notion of ergodicity and show that the time-averaged observables converge significantly faster than the ensemble averages.

Summary. This work develops a theoretical framework to quantify how phase noise degrades the quantum interference signatures in Gaussian Boson Sampling. By modeling phase diffusion, the authors show that interference is suppressed, causing the output statistics to approach classical limits. This establishes a crucial diagnostic tool for assessing the impact of realistic noise sources on quantum photonic experiments.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a concrete, quantitative link between experimentally measurable phase fluctuations (dephasing) and the loss of quantum advantage in photonic quantum computation protocols like GBS.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how phase noise (phase diffusion) systematically suppresses the many-body quantum interference effects inherent in Gaussian Boson Sampling (GBS).

Main result. Phase noise selectively suppresses the interference contribution, driving the output statistics toward a classical dimer-model regime, while the incoherent (Hafnian) part remains relatively unaffected.

Method. The analysis uses a Heisenberg-picture tensor-network formulation to derive Fock-space expressions for output probabilities, followed by modeling the effect of phase fluctuations using an effective dephasing model based on Wiener processes.

Model / system. The system is Gaussian Boson Sampling (GBS), which uses squeezed-vacuum states. The analysis models the evolution and probability amplitude using tensor networks, focusing on the interference structure derived from perfect matchings.

Key observables. Output probability $|\langle n|U|G angle|^2$, interference sectors $I_k(ar{i})$, and the coherence factor $w_k$.

Important parameters / regimes. Phase fluctuation timescale vs. detection window $T$, photon number $m$, and the mismatch index $k$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis focuses only on phase fluctuations, neglecting amplitude fluctuations, and employs an effective dephasing model based on jointly Gaussian Wiener processes.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the coherence factor $w_k$ and its Coefficient of Variation (CV) versus the mismatch index $k$, showing the crossover from coherent to fluctuation-dominated regimes.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the Fock-space framework for GBS probabilities, then develops an effective dephasing model using Wiener processes to calculate the suppression factor $w_k$. Finally, it compares GBS to Shot-by-Shot Boson Sampling (SBS) and analyzes the effect of uniform losses.

Abstract

We develop a Heisenberg-picture tensor-network formulation of collision-free Gaussian Boson Sampling, providing a direct Fock-space expression for output probabilities in terms of experimentally accessible quantities. The resulting representation naturally recovers the Hafnian structure while revealing the decomposition of GBS probability into a phase-insensitive contribution and a hierarchy of interference sectors associated with pairs of perfect matchings. As an application, we investigate phase diffusion and show how it progressively suppresses many-body interference, driving the output statistics toward a classical dimer-model regime. Our results establish a transparent framework for connecting experimentally characterized phase fluctuations with the loss of quantum interference in photonic quantum sampling experiments.

Thresholdless dynamical instability in transverse $\mathcal{PT}$-symmetric scattering systems: The hidden role of bound states in the continuum

Summary. This paper reveals a thresholdless dynamical instability in transverse PT-symmetric scattering systems, showing that the presence of a BIC coupled to the continuum leads to exponential energy growth under anti-Hermitian perturbations. The instability is governed by the spatial overlap between the gain/loss potential and the BIC state, providing a crucial design principle for stable non-Hermitian devices.

Why it may be interesting. This work is highly relevant to AMO physics and open quantum systems as it provides a rigorous stability criterion for engineered non-Hermitian systems, directly impacting the design of stable quantum optical devices.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the dynamical stability of transverse parity-time (PT)-symmetric scattering systems, addressing the gap in knowledge regarding their dynamic stability despite extensive study of their stationary properties.

Main result. The authors demonstrate that an anti-Hermitian PT-symmetric perturbation can drive the system into a time-growing bound state (TGBS) for arbitrarily small gain-loss strength, establishing a microscopic instability criterion.

Method. The analysis relies on tracking the poles of the S-matrix in the complex wave-number plane, using perturbation theory (Feshbach projection) to analyze the shift of Bound States in the Continuum (BICs).

Model / system. The study uses transverse PT-symmetric scattering systems, modeled via tight-binding approaches, specifically examining three-site side-coupled and four-site rhombic models with engineered gain/loss potentials.

Key observables. Pole trajectories in the complex k-plane, the imaginary part of the energy ($ ext{Im } E$), and the time evolution of a localized wave packet showing exponential growth.

Important parameters / regimes. Gain-loss strength ($\gamma$), the golden-rule coupling ($\Gamma_V$), and the nature of the perturbation (Hermitian vs. anti-Hermitian).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis often assumes the existence of a single, non-degenerate BIC for simplicity, and the instability mechanism is linked to the spatial overlap between the gain-loss potential and the BIC wave function.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the pole coalescence (BIC), the shift of poles under Hermitian perturbation (lower half-plane), and the upward shift into the first quadrant under PT-symmetric perturbation, accompanied by time evolution plots showing exponential growth.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the theoretical framework by noting the gap in dynamical stability studies. It then analyzes the effect of perturbations on the BIC pole structure, contrasting Hermitian and anti-Hermitian cases. Finally, it confirms the instability mechanism using specific lattice models (three-site and four-site rhombic) and derives a design principle.

Abstract

The stationary scattering properties of transverse parity-time ($\mathcal{PT}$) symmetric systems have been extensively studied, yet their dynamical stability, a prerequisite for any stationary description, remains largely unexplored. Here we uncover a thresholdless dynamical instability in such systems, driven by symmetry-protected bound states in the continuum (BICs). Without gain and loss, the up-down mirror symmetry of the transverse geometry generically protects a BIC, which manifests as the coalescence of an $S$-matrix pole and zero on the real axis of the complex wave-number plane. A Hermitian symmetry-breaking perturbation shifts the poles into the lower half-plane, converting the BIC into a resonance with a Fermi-golden-rule decay width. An anti-Hermitian $\mathcal{PT}$-symmetric perturbation instead reverses the sign of the second-order energy shift, driving the poles into the upper half-plane and producing a time-growing bound state with $\operatorname{Im}E=γ^{2}Γ_{V}/2+O(γ^{3})$, where $γ$ is the gain-loss strength and $Γ_{V}$ is the golden-rule coupling of the BIC to the continuum. The instability therefore sets in at arbitrarily small $γ$ whenever $Γ_{V}>0$. We confirm this mechanism in a three-site side-coupled model that is unstable despite possessing a unitary scattering matrix, and in a four-site rhombic model where transverse and longitudinal gain-loss placements yield vanishing and finite thresholds, respectively. These results establish a microscopic stability criterion and a design principle for stable $\mathcal{PT}$-symmetric scattering devices.

Summary. This theoretical work analyzes how time-delayed feedback fundamentally alters thermal escape dynamics, replacing the standard exponential Arrhenius law with a logarithmic dependence on the barrier height. By inducing a deterministic 'slingshot' instability, the delay acts as a tunable control parameter, enabling escape significantly faster than predicted by classical theories.

Why it may be interesting. The replacement of exponential scaling with a logarithmic dependence is a profound non-equilibrium effect, providing a new, controllable mechanism for accelerating processes relevant to quantum tunneling or barrier crossing in open quantum systems.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how time-delayed feedback modifies the standard Arrhenius exponential scaling governing thermally activated escape over an energy barrier.

Main result. The mean escape time transitions from exponential to a logarithmic dependence on the barrier height, $\langle T angle \propto \ln(\Delta U/D)$, due to a deterministic 'slingshot' escape mechanism.

Method. The analysis involves solving the non-Markovian Langevin equation, identifying a Hopf bifurcation point, and deriving the escape time by analyzing the two-stage process of noise-seeded diffusion followed by deterministic amplification.

Model / system. The system is an overdamped particle diffusing in a confining potential $U(x)$, subjected to a time-delayed conservative force: $\gamma \dot{x}(t) = -U'(x(t- au)) + \sqrt{2D} \eta(t)$.

Key observables. Mean escape time $\langle T angle$, which exhibits a Gumbel distribution for its fluctuations.

Important parameters / regimes. Dimensionless delay $s = au/ au_{ ext{relax}}$, barrier-to-noise ratio $\Delta U/D$, and the critical delay $s_c = \pi/2$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the system is overdamped and the delay only affects the conservative force; the logarithmic law is derived for the weak-noise regime ($\Delta U/D \gg 1$).

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the contrast between Arrhenius and Slingshot escape trajectories, confirm the universal logarithmic slope $A(s)$ across different potentials, and show the Gumbel distribution of the escape-time histogram.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the non-Markovian dynamics, identifies the instability threshold ($s_c$), derives the logarithmic escape law $\langle T angle \propto \ln(\Delta U/D)$ by analyzing the envelope crossing, and discusses the implications for tunable control of activated processes.

Abstract

Thermal escape is governed by the Arrhenius law, where the mean escape time scales exponentially with the barrier height. We show that the non-Markovianity induced by time-delayed feedback in the confining force removes this exponential scaling. Beyond a threshold set by the curvature of the minimum, the delay destabilizes the well, and the thermal noise seeds an instability that is subsequently amplified deterministically to the boundary leading to \textit{slingshot} escape trajectories. The escape time becomes logarithmic in the barrier, its fluctuations follow a Gumbel law, and an optimal delay enables escape faster than free diffusion. Our results propose time delay as a tunable and experimentally feasible control parameter for accelerating activated processes.

Summary. This work explores how engineered cross-correlations in the environment reservoirs can enhance the power output of a quantum Otto engine operating in finite time. By using advanced non-Markovian quantum dynamics techniques, the authors demonstrate that these off-diagonal spectral correlations provide a novel control knob for quantum thermal machines. This establishes a new resource for controlling transient energy conversion processes.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a concrete, controllable mechanism (off-diagonal reservoir spectra) to engineer quantum thermal machines beyond simple temperature or coupling strength variations, which is highly relevant for open quantum systems theory.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates how longitudinal-transverse cross-spectral reservoir correlations affect the thermodynamic performance of a quantum Otto engine operating in finite time.

Main result. Cross-spectral correlations act as an additional resource, capable of enhancing the output power of the engine, although they do not alter the limit-cycle efficiency.

Method. The dynamics are derived using the second-order time-convolutionless (TCL2) framework, which retains non-Markovian, finite-time reservoir memory effects.

Model / system. The system is a two-level working medium coupled to two bosonic reservoirs (hot and cold) forming a quantum Otto engine. The coupling channels are characterized by a spectral-density matrix including cross-correlation terms.

Key observables. Output power, cycle work, population imbalance ($\langle\sigma_z angle$), and efficiency ($\eta$).

Important parameters / regimes. Cross-spectral correlation strength, cross-spectral phase, characteristic frequency scale, and cycle duration ($ au_{cyc}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis avoids the Markov limit to capture finite reservoir memory effects, and the limit-cycle efficiency is fixed at the classical Otto value for the considered unitary strokes.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the finite-time quantum Otto cycle sequence and show the time evolution of the population imbalance ($\langle\sigma_z angle$) over successive cycles, demonstrating sensitivity to cross-correlation strength.

Paper structure. The paper establishes the theoretical framework by analyzing the reservoir coupling via spectral density matrices, derives the non-Markovian dynamics using TCL2, and then applies this to the Otto cycle to quantify the enhancement of power via cross-correlations.

Abstract

We investigate the thermodynamic consequences of longitudinal-transverse cross-spectral reservoir correlations in a finite-time quantum Otto engine with a two-level working medium. Each reservoir couples through excitation-relaxation and dephasing channels whose fluctuations are characterized by a Hermitian positive-semidefinite spectral-density matrix, with the off-diagonal elements encoding their cross correlations. The finite-time isochoric dynamics is derived within the second-order time-convolutionless framework, without imposing the Markov limit at the outset, so that finite reservoir-memory effects can enter through time-dependent dissipative and reservoir-induced coherent contributions. The resulting dynamics is then recast in Bloch-vector form to construct the stroke-resolved cycle dynamics. At fixed auto-spectral densities, cross-spectral correlations modify the populations and coherences of the working medium and thereby its thermodynamic performance. Increasing the correlation strength can enhance the output power, with the enhancement controlled by the cross-spectral phase and characteristic frequency scale. The correlations also reshape the transient cycle-to-cycle evolution and the approach to periodic operation, while the limit-cycle efficiency remains fixed at the Otto value for the population-preserving unitary strokes considered here. These results establish off-diagonal reservoir spectra as an additional resource for controlling finite-time quantum thermal machines.

Summary. This work develops an extension of the generalized Bethe ansatz to handle quantum field theories with time-dependent interactions at both the bulk and the boundaries. By enforcing integrability, the system's dynamics are constrained to satisfy boundary quantum Knizhnik-Zamolodchikov (BqKZ) equations. This provides an exact, powerful method for determining the time-dependent wavefunction in complex, open quantum systems.

Why it may be interesting. The connection between integrable quantum field theories, time-dependent boundary conditions, and the resulting matrix difference equations (BqKZ) provides powerful, exact tools for analyzing open quantum systems dynamics, which is highly relevant to modern AMO and open quantum system research.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper extends the generalized Bethe ansatz framework to find exact solutions for quantum field theories with both time-dependent bulk and explicitly time-dependent boundary coupling strengths while maintaining integrability.

Main result. The consistency of the wavefunction requires the system to satisfy boundary quantum Knizhnik-Zamolodchikov (BqKZ) equations, which govern the exact time-dependent wavefunction.

Method. The authors employ the generalized Bethe ansatz, requiring the bulk and boundary S-matrices to satisfy the reflection equation to ensure integrability.

Model / system. The analysis focuses on the SU(2) Gross-Neveu model, described by a time-dependent Schrödinger equation for fermions confined between boundaries (open boundary conditions).

Key observables. The exact many-body wavefunction, the conserved total number of fermions (N), and the dynamical invariants ($\chi_L, \chi_R$).

Important parameters / regimes. Time-dependent coupling strengths $g(t)$ and boundary coupling functions $B(t)$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis is restricted to the case of diagonal boundaries, which breaks $Z_2$ spin flip and $SU(2)$ spin rotation symmetry but preserves $U(1)$ symmetry.

Paper structure. The structure progresses from establishing the generalized Bethe ansatz for time-dependent bulk interactions to incorporating time-dependent boundary conditions. This leads to deriving the necessary reflection equations (for the boundaries) and subsequently constructing the matrix difference equations known as the BqKZ equations.

Abstract

The generalized Bethe ansatz framework formulated in [P. R. Pasnoori, Phys. Rev. B 112, L060409 (2025)] provides a unified framework to find exact solutions to quantum many-body systems with time-dependent coupling strengths with periodic boundary conditions. In this work we extend this framework to the case of open boundary conditions where in addition to the time-dependent interactions in the bulk, the boundary conditions are explicitly time-dependent. We show that for integrable time-dependent bulk coupling strengths, the generalized Bethe ansatz framework provides the time-dependent boundary conditions compatible with integrability and reduces the time-dependent Schrodinger equation to a set of matrix difference equations called the boundary quantum Knizhnik-Zamolodchikov (BqKZ) equations. The solution to the BqKZ equations provides the explicit form of the exact wavefunction. We further show that the RG invariants of the corresponding static model identify with the dynamical invariants in the time-dependent model.

Summary. This work analyzes a non-Hermitian spin model to understand how its long-time dynamics are dictated by the eigenvalue with the largest imaginary part. The authors find that disorder can induce a sharp transition where the system's state switches from a simple, low-entanglement outlier to a complex, high-entanglement bulk state. This connects spectral theory to quantum resource diagnostics.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a concrete, analytically solvable model to study non-Hermitian dynamics, linking spectral transitions (outlier vs. bulk) directly to changes in quantum information measures like entanglement and magic.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the long-time state selection in non-Hermitian many-body systems, which is governed by the eigenvalue with the largest imaginary part.

Main result. For certain types of disorder (anti-Hermitian and mixed), the dominant eigenstate undergoes a sharp transition from a low-entanglement outlier state to a high-entanglement bulk state when the bulk spectral edge overtakes the outlier.

Method. The authors construct an analytically tractable non-Hermitian spin ensemble and analyze the spectral properties by comparing the dominant eigenvalue to the spectral bulk edge.

Model / system. The system is an all-to-all non-Hermitian spin ensemble of L spins with k-local interactions, defined by a Hamiltonian decomposed into Hermitian and anti-Hermitian parts.

Key observables. Entanglement Entropy, Magic (Nonstabilizerness), and the imaginary part of the dominant eigenvalue.

Important parameters / regimes. Disorder strength ($\sigma_R, \sigma_I$), locality ($k$), and the ratio of $k$ to $\sqrt{L}$ (determining crossover vs. sharp transition).

Assumptions / limitations. The long-time state is determined solely by the eigenvalue possessing the largest imaginary part.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the spectral landscape, distinguishing between spectral outliers and the spectral bulk, and showing the relative positions of the dominant eigenvalue in different disorder regimes.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by defining the model and diagnostics, analyzing the deterministic limit, establishing the dominance criterion based on the largest imaginary part, analyzing the three disorder cases, and finally mapping the non-Hermitian evolution to measurement-induced quantum trajectories.

Abstract

The long-time state of a non-Hermitian system is determined by the eigenvalue with the largest imaginary part. In interacting many-body systems this eigenvalue usually cannot be tracked analytically, and the character of the state it selects is unknown. We construct a non-Hermitian spin ensemble of $L$ spins with exactly $k$-local all-to-all interactions, in which this dominant eigenvalue can be tracked analytically from the clean limit into the disordered regime. Disorder produces a competition between an isolated spectral outlier and the edge of a many-body spectral bulk. We study three cases: purely anti-Hermitian disorder, purely Hermitian disorder, and mixed disorder of equal strength. For purely anti-Hermitian and mixed disorder, we show that when the bulk overtakes the outlier in imaginary part, the dominant eigenstate switches from an outlier state with low entanglement and magic (nonstabilizerness) to a bulk state with substantially larger entanglement and magic, with both changing at the same threshold. For $k \gg \sqrt{L}$ the bulk has a sharp spectral edge and the transition thresholds follow in closed form, while for $k \ll \sqrt{L}$ spectral tails broaden the transition into a crossover. Purely Hermitian disorder provides a contrasting case with no outlier-to-bulk switching. Finally, we map the non-Hermitian evolution exactly onto postselected trajectories of a monitored quantum system, connecting this spectral mechanism to measurement-induced transitions.

Abstract

We reformulate the relaxation problem in statistical mechanics by making explicit what are the \emph{operational} objects subject to relaxation: the local time statistics of the recorded signal $Z(t)$. These local time statistics are simply the estimated histograms of observations $\{Z(t_i)\}_{i=1}^M$ performed at uniformly random times $\{t_i\}_{i=1}^M$ by a clockless observer. The subject of prediction is a belief about a future fresh out-of-sample reading of a measurement outcome whose distribution is inferred from the mathematical model believed to be true. For finite bounded systems of $N\ge 1$ degrees of freedom global irreversible relaxation of predictions can occur but special initial conditions exist. Abstract

We identify a memory effect produced by phonon scattering in a superfluid: a localized scattering event generates a far-field pressure pulse with vanishing time integral but a nonzero first temporal moment, corresponding to a permanent shift in a prepotential for the far-field velocity. In superfluid $^4$He, scattering macroscopic phonon wave packets produces a signal whose estimated magnitude may lie within experimental reach, thereby providing a laboratory analogue of electromagnetic and gravitational memory.
