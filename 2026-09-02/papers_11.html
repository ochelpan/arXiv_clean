Moiré-induced lattice reconstruction at buried atomic interfaces

Authors: Nicholas Clark, Frederick Allars, Isaac Soltero, Wendong Wang, David Hopkinson, Hugo de Latour, James G. McHugh, William Talbott, Sam Sullivan-Allsop, Rongsheng Cai, Astrid Weston, Xiao Li, Gareth Tainton, Casey K. Cheung, Francisco Selles, Alex Summerfield, Andrey Kretinin, Christopher S. Allen, Vladimir Falko, Sarah J. Haigh, Roman Gorbachev
Type: both · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.30531v1

Summary. This work pioneers the use of multi-slice electron ptychography to map the 3D atomic structure at buried, twisted van der Waals interfaces in TMD crystals. Researchers observed significant, twist-induced lattice reconstruction at these interfaces, which was stronger than predicted by theory. By combining these high-resolution measurements with continuum modeling, the study provides a structural foundation for understanding the emergent electronic properties of moiré systems.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on solid-state structure, the ability to map strain and structural phase transitions at interfaces using highly sensitive, non-invasive techniques is relevant to understanding emergent quantum properties in moiré heterostructures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the structural changes, specifically lattice reconstruction, that occur at buried van der Waals heterointerfaces between multilayer crystals.

Main result. Strong, twist-induced in-plane lattice reconstruction was observed at the buried interface, exceeding theoretical predictions, and the technique allowed mapping the decay of this reconstruction into the bulk material.

Method. The research combines advanced experimental techniques, namely multi-slice electron ptychography, with detailed theoretical modeling based on continuum mechanics and energy minimization.

Model / system. The physical system involves marginally twisted rhombohedral interfaces between thick transition-metal dichalcogenide (TMD) crystals like $ ext{MoS}_2$. The theoretical model incorporates adhesion, in-plane elastic, and bending energies to predict layer-resolved atomic relaxation.

Key observables. Three-dimensional atomic structure, twist-induced lattice reconstruction field, dislocation network, strain tensor evolution, and root-mean-squared (rms) displacement decay.

Important parameters / regimes. Position precision of $\sim 3 ext{ pm}$, depth resolution of $\sim 1 ext{ nm}$, and twist angles ($ heta$) ranging from $0.2^\circ$ to $4^\circ$.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the outer layers suppress out-of-plane bending, enhancing in-plane reconstruction, and that the reconstruction effect decays predictably away from the interface.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate depth-dependent atomic structures via ptychography, showing reconstructed domains ($ ext{MX}', ext{XM}'$) at the interface, and displacement maps showing the decay of reconstruction away from the buried junction.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the experimental capability using ptychography to image buried interfaces. It then presents the structural findings, detailing the observed reconstruction patterns and the decay profile of these distortions as a function of depth and twist angle. Finally, it compares these findings against continuum mechanical models.

Abstract

Atomic reconstruction at twisted two-dimensional interfaces governs many of their emergent optical, electronic, and mechanical properties, including sliding ferroelectricity. Despite recent progress in understanding lattice reconstruction in suspended twisted bilayers, structural changes at van der Waals heterointerfaces between multilayer crystals remain largely unexplored. Here we use multi-slice electron ptychography to non-invasively recover the three-dimensional atomic structure at marginally twisted rhombohedral interfaces between thick transition-metal dichalcogenide crystals. With a position precision of ~3 pm and a depth resolution ~1 nm, we resolve the twist-induced lattice reconstruction field per layer, and the resulting dislocation network at the buried interface. Despite the bulk nature, we observe markedly strong in-plane interfacial reconstruction due to suppression of the out-of-plane bending by outer layers, exceeding predictions from our three-dimensional modelling. Furthermore, we extract the strain tensor evolution during the decay of the reconstruction into the bulk, providing a structural foundation for understanding multi-layer moiré systems.

Switchable Magnetoelectric Transport in Graphene via a Van der Waals Multiferroic

Authors: Miuko Tanaka, Shunta Aoki, Ikoi Sato, Hao Ou, Itishree Pradhan, Ngoc Han Tu, Yangsong Chen, Tomohiro Ishii, Kenji Watanabe, Takashi Taniguchi, Michihisa Yamamoto, Masayuki Hashisaka, Jiang Pu, Naoki Ogawa, Toshiya Ideue

Summary. This work reports on achieving gate-switchable magnetoelectric transport using a graphene/multiferroic heterostructure. By measuring the shift of the graphene's charge neutrality point under applied magnetic fields, the authors provide direct evidence of the magnetoelectric effect at the interface. This establishes a novel platform for engineering functional 2D van der Waals electronic devices.

Why it may be interesting. While primarily condensed matter/spintronics, the study of coupled order parameters (electric and magnetic) and their control via external fields (E and B) touches upon concepts relevant to open quantum systems and engineered quantum interfaces.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The central goal is to develop next-generation electronics and spintronics by achieving electric and magnetic control of transport properties at atomic interfaces.

Main result. The study demonstrates the first realization of gate-switchable magnetoelectric transport in a van der Waals heterostructure, showing that the magnetic field can modulate the transport peak in a polarization-dependent manner.

Method. Transport measurements (resistance/capacitance vs. gate voltage and magnetic field) were performed on graphene/multiferroic heterostructures.

Model / system. The system is a van der Waals heterostructure combining graphene with the multiferroic material $ ext{CuCrP}_2 ext{S}_6$ (CCPS). The physics relies on the coupling between the electric polarization (ferroelectric switching) and the magnetic state (magnetoelectric effect) at the interface.

Key observables. Hysteresis in the charge-neutrality resistance peak, shift of the charge neutrality point ($ ext{V}_{ ext{G,CNP}}$) under in-plane magnetic fields, and reversible switching of transport via electric field control.

Important parameters / regimes. Low temperatures ($T=2 ext{ K}$), in-plane magnetic fields ($B$), and gate voltages ($V_G$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the graphene carrier density is modulated by both the electrostatic gate voltage and the surface polarization of the multiferroic layer.

Figures summary. Figures show $ ext{CNP}$ shifts vs. $V_G$ demonstrating polarization hysteresis, and $ ext{CNP}$ shifts vs. $B$ confirming the magnetoelectric effect. Time-dependent measurements confirm the nature of the switching mechanism.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by establishing the physical platform, demonstrating polarization switching via gate voltage sweeps, quantifying the magnetoelectric coupling using magnetic field sweeps, and finally confirming the nature of the switching mechanism through time-dependent measurements.

Abstract

Electric and magnetic control of transport properties at atomic interfaces is central to the development of next generation electronics and spintronics. Van der Waals multiferroics materials that simultaneously host dielectric and magnetic orders down to the monolayer limit offer a promising platform for such interfacial control, yet the realization of electronic functionalities that exploit the unique attributes of van der Waals multiferroics has largely remained elusive. Here, we realize a van der Waals heterostructure comprising graphene and the multiferroic CuCrP2S6, enabling gate-switchable magnetoelectric transport in graphene, mediated by the multiferroic layer. The charge-neutrality resistance peak of graphene exhibits pronounced hysteresis arising from polarization flip in the multiferroic state. Application of an in-plane magnetic field shifts this peak in a polarization-dependent manner, revealing magnetic-field-induced polarization modulation a direct signature of the magnetoelectric effect. Furthermore, cooling the device under an applied electric field enables domain control of the multiferroic order, allowing reversible switching of the interfacial magnetoelectric transport. These results provide the first demonstration of interfacial magnetoelectric transport in a vdW heterostructure, and establish a pathway for engineering two-dimensional van der Waals interfaces for functional device applications.

Breakdown of Charge-Conjugation Symmetry of Disclinations in 2D Crystals

Authors: Ruslan Yamaletdinov, Mikhail I. Katsnelson, Oleg V. Yazyev
Type: theory · Category: other · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.30921v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Frenkel-Kontorova 1/5 · analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This work explores the physics of topological defects (disclinations) in 2D crystals like graphene. Using simulations, the authors reveal that the elastic behavior of these defects is not symmetric with respect to the sign of their topological charge. Specifically, they find that negative defects interact attractively, contrasting sharply with the repulsive interactions observed for positive defects.

Why it may be interesting. While the topic is condensed matter physics, the study of topological defects and symmetry breaking in low-dimensional systems shares conceptual links with defect physics in quantum materials or engineered quantum structures.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the elastic properties, energetics, and interactions of topological defects called disclinations in 2D crystalline membranes.

Main result. The paper finds a fundamental breakdown of charge-conjugation symmetry: positive disclinations repel, while negative disclinations exhibit a robust long-range attraction.

Method. The research combines atomistic simulations with continuum elasticity theory to calculate the energy and morphology of disclination configurations.

Model / system. The system is a free-standing 2D crystal, specifically graphene, containing topological defects (disclinations) characterized by a topological charge $s$. The analysis compares theoretical continuum predictions with atomistic simulation results.

Key observables. Interaction energy ($E_{exc}$), energy scaling with charge $|s|$, equilibrium shape, and adhesion energy in folded membranes.

Important parameters / regimes. Topological charge ($s$), bending rigidity ($\kappa$), and separation distance ($\Delta r$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on continuum models, such as the saddle ansatz, and uses approximations like the four-cone configuration to model negative curvature defects.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the construction of positive and negative disclinations, compare energy fits showing deviations from expected scaling for negative charges, and show how self-adhesion stabilizes negative curvature defects.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the theoretical framework using continuum elasticity, then uses atomistic simulations to test the theory for positive and negative charges, culminating in a detailed comparison of interaction forces and the role of self-adhesion.

Abstract

Disclinations are elementary topological defects in two-dimensional (2D) crystalline membranes, yet their elastic properties remain largely unexplored. Using atomistic simulations, we address the energetics, morphology, and interactions of disclinations in free-standing graphene, a prototypical 2D crystal. We find that while disclinations with positive topological charges follow an expected behavior, negative disclinations exhibit sublinear energy scaling with charge as well as equilibrium shape that deviates sharply from the conventional saddle ansatz. This breakdown of charge-conjugation symmetry leads to qualitatively distinct interactions: positive disclinations repel, whereas negative disclinations display a robust long-range attraction. These trends are shown to be further amplified by self-adhesion in folded membranes. Our results uncover a fundamentally different energetic landscape for negative curvature defects and provide a basis for understanding the stability, self-folding behavior, and defect-driven morphology of graphene and other 2D membranes.

Controlling the Coupling Strengths in Nanophotonic Networks using Modified Yagi-Uda Nanoplasmonic Antennas

Authors: Vidar Flodgren, Anders Mikkelsen
Type: theory · Category: disordered systems and neural networks · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29837v1

Summary. This paper proposes using modified Yagi-Uda nanoplasmonic antennas integrated with III-V nanowires to create compact optical interconnects for neural networks. By precisely controlling the antenna geometry, the authors demonstrate the ability to engineer highly variable coupling strengths, enabling the realization of complex artificial synaptic weight matrices on a chip.

Why it may be interesting. While the core physics is classical electromagnetism, the application directly models artificial synapses and network connectivity, linking advanced photonics to computational models relevant to neuromorphic computing research.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the need for compact alternatives to traditional waveguides for implementing optical neural networks on a chip.

Main result. By using modified Yagi-Uda nanoplasmonic antennas, the authors demonstrated the ability to achieve highly variable and complex weight distributions between connected nodes in artificial neural network layers.

Method. The study relies on simulating the interaction of light with nanoplasmonic structures using Finite Difference Time Domain (FDTD) methods.

Model / system. The physical system combines III-V nanowire emitters/receivers with nanoplasmonic metal structures, specifically modified Yagi-Uda antennas. This setup is designed to mimic the connectivity and weight calculation of artificial neural network layers.

Key observables. Power absorption ($ ext{Pabs}$) on the receiver, forward/backward scattering cross-section, and the complex weight distribution between nodes.

Important parameters / regimes. Nanowire dimensions ($L=3 \mu ext{m}, r=88 ext{ nm}$), antenna director dimensions (e.g., $[D_x, D_y, D_z] = [485, 350, 60]$ nm), and director separation (750 nm).

Assumptions / limitations. The underlying substrate is $ ext{SiO}_2$ on $ ext{Si}$, and the nanowires are assumed to function as building blocks capable of non-linear evaluation.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the basic concept, showing symmetric and modified Yagi-Uda designs, and detail the forward/backward scattering cross-section and power absorption fraction as a function of antenna director parameters.

Paper structure. The paper introduces the problem of compact on-chip optical communication, proposes the nanoplasmonic/Yagi-Uda antenna solution, details the simulation methodology (FDTD), presents results showing directional control and weight tunability, and concludes by discussing the potential for complex network realization.

Abstract

On-chip communication in optical neural networks is commonly done via waveguides which results in large system footprints. A compact alternative is to broadcast light signals between nano-optoelectronic components in free space and tailor the light field distribution using sub-wavelength nanophotonics. We propose and simulate nanoplasmonic metal structures in combination with III-V nanowire emitters and receivers to create an optical network in which the shape and position of these nanostructures create varying weights between the nano-optoelectronic nodes. Using Finite Difference Time Domain modelling, we investigate systems of experimentally verified nanowire optoelectronics combined with nanoplasmonic structures that can be made in the same fabrication step as electrical contacts powering the nanowires. We investigate both individual nanowire/antenna devices as well as networks corresponding to two layers in a neural network. We show that directed communication from a nanowire node can be significantly altered using Yagi-Uda antennas. Modifying the antenna with an asymmetric director component enables the directing of light several different angular directions simultaneously. We find that highly variable complex weight distribution between the connections in two seven node layers can be achieved depending on the combined geometry of the antenna components. The possible weight distributions in compact layers of nanowire nodes could be used for creating a variety of neural networks with complex connectivity. The concepts can be generalized to other types of nanoscale emitter/receiver systems.

Interplay of Excitonic Charge Density Wave and Superconductivity in Transition Metal Dichalcogenides

Summary. This theoretical work models the competition between excitonic Charge Density Waves and Superconductivity in TMDs using a minimal mean-field Hamiltonian. By analyzing the phase diagram and spectral properties, the authors demonstrate that tuning parameters drive a transition from CDW dominance to SC emergence. The findings provide crucial insights into the intertwined nature of these two correlated electronic orders.

Why it may be interesting. The competition between CDW and SC is a canonical problem in strongly correlated electron systems, and the use of excitonic pairing mechanisms provides a rich theoretical framework relevant to understanding unconventional superconductivity.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the complex interplay and competition between excitonic Charge Density Wave (CDW) order and conventional Superconductivity (SC) in Transition Metal Dichalcogenides (TMDs).

Main result. The phase diagram shows a generic transition where CDW order wanes and SC emerges as tuning parameters change, with the CDW nature evolving from commensurate to incommensurate.

Method. The study employs a minimal mean-field model Hamiltonian incorporating both excitonic CDW pairing and intra-band SC attraction, analyzed via self-consistent calculations.

Model / system. The system is modeled on a two-dimensional triangular lattice using a two-band tight-binding Hamiltonian for the single-particle part. Interactions include excitonic CDW pairing (particle-hole) and conventional SC pairing (intra-band).

Key observables. CDW and SC order parameters ($\chi_q, \Delta$), spectral function $A(k, \omega)$, and the average Density of States (DOS) $N(\omega)$.

Important parameters / regimes. Tuning parameters include carrier density ($ ho$), and interaction strengths ($W$ for CDW, $U$ for SC).

Assumptions / limitations. The CDW is assumed to have an excitonic origin (inter-band pairing), and SC is assumed to be conventional (intra-band pairing). The model is a minimal toy model.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the phase diagram showing C-CDW to NC-CDW crossover, band structures, and the evolution of the DOS revealing CDW gaps, SC kinks, and their coexistence.

Paper structure. The paper develops the mean-field Hamiltonian, analyzes the ground state energy minimization with respect to the modulation wave vector Q, and then calculates the resulting spectral properties (DOS, FS) across varying carrier densities to map the phase diagram.

Abstract

Motivated by the unique characteristics of the phase diagram of 1T-TiSe2, we investigated the complex interplay of excitonic charge density wave (CDW) and superconductivity (SC) on a two-dimensional triangular lattice, each site accommodating two orbitals. In response to various tuning parameters, such as intercalation, pressure, substitution, and gating, these materials exhibit a generic and gradual waning of CDW, followed by the emergence of SC. Intriguingly, the nature of CDW changes from commensurate to incommensurate with the appearance of SC. Setting up a minimal model based on observed excitonic CDW and electron and hole pockets in the Fermi surface, and analyzing it within a simple mean-field framework, we can comprehend some salient experimental features. We also found that the qualitative phase diagram remains insensitive to details of the band structure. These results offer crucial insights into the nature of the interplay between CDW and SC in transition-metal dichalcogenides.

Summary. This theoretical work analyzes the electronic structure of vortices situated at twin boundaries in a nematic superconductor using the Bogoliubov-de Gennes theory. The research finds that the boundary induces fractional vortices and novel s +/- id wave components in the superconducting order parameter. This provides crucial insights into how structural defects interact with complex pairing symmetries in correlated superconducting materials.

Why it may be interesting. The study of exotic superconducting phases, such as those exhibiting nematicity and complex pairing symmetries (like s +/- id), is highly relevant to understanding unconventional superconductivity in materials like iron-based superconductors, which often involve strong electronic correlations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the local electronic states of vortices when they are trapped at twin boundaries within a nematic superconductor.

Main result. The trapping of a single vortex at a twin boundary results in a bound pair of fractional vortices accompanied by weakly-induced s +/- id wave components, even though these components are absent in zero magnetic field.

Method. The authors employed the Bogoliubov-de Gennes (BdG) theory using a tight-binding model to calculate the local density of states (LDOS) and pair potentials.

Model / system. The system is a nematic superconductor, modeled on a square lattice with nematicity introduced via anisotropy in transfer integrals and attractive interactions. The superconductivity is described by an s +/- d wave pairing potential.

Key observables. Local density of states (LDOS), pair potential components (s and d wave), and the vortex core structure.

Important parameters / regimes. Nematic anisotropy, twin boundary structure, and the superconducting gap parameters ($\Delta_s, \Delta_d$).

Assumptions / limitations. The model uses a simple tight-binding approximation and assumes spin-singlet pairing; it also notes that the model might fail to capture the full complexity of time-reversal symmetry breaking at the boundary.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the spatial structure of the pair potential components and the LDOS spectra, comparing vortex states with and without twin boundaries.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the theoretical framework (BdG theory, tight-binding model) and then analyzing the electronic states in different regimes: first, the vortex state without boundaries, followed by the analysis of the twin boundary state, culminating in the combined vortex-at-twin-boundary case.

Abstract

Local electronic states of vortices in an s $\pm$ d wave nematic superconductor are studied both in the absence and presence of twin boundaries. The Bogoliubov-de Gennes theory for a tight-binding model is used with its nematicity represented by the anisotropy in the transfer integrals and attractive interactions between the nearest-neighbor sites. We evaluate s and d wave components of the pair potentials and the local density of states, and analyze the effects of nematicity on the vortex core structures with/without twin boundaries. We find that a single vortex trapped at the twin boundary is composed of a bound pair of fractional vortices accompanied by weakly-induced s $\pm$ id wave components, despite that such s $\pm$ id wave components do not appear in a zero magnetic field. The calculated spatial structures of the local electronic states are compared with the vortex image measured by STM experiments in an iron-based superconductor FeSe.

Fractional Impurity Entropy from Potential Scattering in a Free Electron Gas

Authors: Syeda Neha Zaidi, Jukka I. Väyrynen
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29007v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: analog quantum simulation 1/5

↔ Scroll figures horizontally

Summary. This theoretical work shows that a simple system of free electrons scattering off an impurity can exhibit a universal fractional entropy change. This effect arises purely from the shift in allowed single-particle momenta from integer to half-integer quantization. The result suggests that such entropy signatures are not exclusively markers for strong correlations or topological order.

Why it may be interesting. The finding suggests that signatures previously thought to require exotic physics (like non-Abelian anyons) can arise from simple boundary conditions or level quantization shifts, broadening the scope of what constitutes an 'entropic signature'.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To demonstrate that fractional entropy changes can occur in simple, non-interacting systems (free electrons) without invoking strong electron correlations or topological order.

Main result. The system exhibits a universal entropy change of $- rac{1}{2}\ln 2$ when the impurity strength is varied, which is attributed to a shift in single-particle momentum quantization from integer to half-integer values.

Method. The entropy is calculated by analyzing the impurity partition function, $Z_{ ext{imp}}(\alpha) = Z(\alpha) / Z(0)$, and taking its derivative with respect to temperature.

Model / system. The model is a free electron gas confined to a one-dimensional ring of circumference L, scattering off a repulsive point-like impurity of strength $\alpha$. The analysis compares spectra quantized by odd versus even integers.

Key observables. Impurity entropy change ($S_{ ext{imp}}$) and the boundary entropy change ($\Delta S$).

Important parameters / regimes. Temperature ($T$), impurity strength ($\alpha$), and the ratio of energy scales ($\epsilon_\alpha/T$ and $\epsilon_0/T$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on asymptotic limits (weak/strong scattering) and approximations like Taylor expansions and integral conversions.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the shift of allowed momenta from integer to half-integer quantization, and numerically evaluate the entropy change as a function of the impurity strength ratio.

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the physical model and calculates the entropy change in the presence of an impurity. It then generalizes the analysis by comparing spectra quantized by odd versus even integers to derive a universal boundary entropy change, $\Delta S$.

Abstract

Fractional changes in entropy are often associated with strong electron correlations or non-Abelian anyons in topological systems. We show that neither condition is necessary -- a system of free electrons on a ring scattering off a repulsive point-like impurity undergoes a $k_B\ln\sqrt 2$ entropy change as the impurity strength is increased. This effect arises from a shift of allowed single-particle momenta from integer quantization to half-integer quantization, induced by the impurity. We show that this fractional entropy change is universal across a broad class of single-particle dispersions, demonstrating that such signatures can arise without strong correlations or topological order.

Summary. This work explores how centrosymmetric thin films can host measurable phonon angular momentum (AM) responses despite symmetry dictating zero total AM. By analyzing layer-resolved AM, the authors show that compensating local AMs create a finite, measurable dipole moment across the slab. This establishes these materials as promising platforms for studying hidden, multipolar phonon thermal responses.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on condensed matter physics, the concept of symmetry-protected, hidden, and tunable multipolar responses in lattice vibrations (phonons) shares conceptual parallels with engineered topological responses in quantum materials, making it relevant to understanding emergent collective excitations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the existence and physical consequences of 'hidden' layer-resolved phonon angular momentum (AM) in globally centrosymmetric thin films, despite the total phonon AM vanishing due to symmetry.

Main result. The compensation of local, opposite phonon AM contributions across inversion-partner layers results in a finite, measurable real-space phonon AM dipole moment and layer-resolved thermal responses, which can be tuned by strain or substrate interfaces.

Method. The research employs first-principles calculations to analyze phonon dispersions and calculate layer-resolved phonon AM textures and associated thermal response tensors.

Model / system. The primary systems are centrosymmetric thin films, including BaAgAs, SrAgAs, and van der Waals materials like hBN and WSe2. The analysis compares bulk/monolayer structures to finite multilayer slabs and heterostructures.

Key observables. Layer-resolved phonon AM ($\mathbf{L}_{\ell}$), total slab phonon AM ($\mathbf{L}_{ ext{slab}}$), layer-resolved thermal Edelstein-like response ($\alpha^{\ell}_{ij}$), and the real-space phonon AM dipole response ($eta^{ ext{slab}}_{ij}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Uniaxial strain, temperature gradient, and the structural termination/interface (e.g., hBN substrate).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the constant phonon relaxation-time ($ au$) approximation.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the vanishing total phonon AM in bulk/monolayer structures but show finite, opposite AM in individual layers (e.g., Fig. 2(c)–(f)). They also demonstrate finite layer-resolved responses ($\alpha^{\ell}_{ij}$) and non-zero slab dipole moments ($eta^{ ext{slab}}_{ij}$) in finite slabs or heterostructures (e.g., Fig. 5, Fig. 6).

Paper structure. The paper progresses by establishing the symmetry constraint ($\mathbf{L}_{ ext{slab}}=0$) in centrosymmetric bulk materials, then demonstrating how finite slab geometry or heterostructuring breaks this constraint locally, activating finite layer AM and measurable dipolar thermal responses.

Abstract

In nonmagnetic centrosymmetric materials, $\mathcal{PT}$ symmetry enforces vanishing total phonon angular momentum (phonon AM) at every wave vector, making them appear unsuitable as hosts of axial phonons. Here, using first-principles calculations for BaAgAs and representative van der Waal's layered materials, we show that centrosymmetric slabs can still host large hidden layer-resolved phonon AM with both chiral and cycloidal modes. This hidden phonon AM texture produces layer resolved phonon thermal Edelstein-like responses and a real-space phonon AM dipole response, both tunable by strain. Our results establish centrosymmetric layered materials as an exciting platform for hidden axial phonon and multipolar phonon thermal response.

Machine learning reveals common features of unconventional superconductors with high transition temperatures

Authors: Haosheng Xu, Dongheng Qian, Yijun Yu, Jing Wang

Summary. This work uses machine learning to bypass the need for a fundamental theory of high-Tc superconductivity. By analyzing structural and chemical features of known materials, the authors identified key empirical signatures—specifically large electronegativity deviation and intermediate valence electron counts—that characterize these complex superconductors. This data-driven prior allows for the prediction and prioritization of entirely new candidate materials.

Why it may be interesting. While focused on solid-state materials, the use of ML to extract robust, generalizable descriptors from complex electronic and structural data mirrors approaches used in modeling strongly correlated quantum systems where first-principles calculations are intractable.

Detailed structure

Main problem. To find a common, data-driven materials-space signature for high-Tc unconventional superconductors, as no broadly applicable predictive theory currently guides their discovery.

Main result. The analysis revealed that high-Tc unconventional superconductors cluster in a materials space characterized by large electronegativity deviation and intermediate mean valence-electron numbers.

Method. The study employed interpretable machine learning, integrating compositional, structural, and latent electronic representations to perform unsupervised clustering and supervised $T_c$ prediction.

Model / system. The focus is on unconventional superconductors, particularly cuprate and iron-based families, analyzed using a unified feature vector combining 120+ dimensions from elemental properties, crystal structure, and ALIGNN-derived embeddings.

Key observables. Critical temperature ($T_c$), average deviation of electronegativity, and mean number of valence electrons.

Important parameters / regimes. Intermediate mean valence-electron number ($\approx 7-8$); large electronegativity deviation.

Assumptions / limitations. The approach avoids assuming a particular microscopic pairing mechanism, relying instead on empirical correlations within the materials space.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the ML workflow, the clustering of superconducting materials in the feature space (PC1-PC2 plane), and the correlation of $T_c$ with the key descriptors.

Paper structure. The paper follows a workflow: 1) Constructing a high-dimensional feature vector; 2) Curating the dataset; 3) Performing unsupervised analysis (PCA) to find common regions; and 4) Using supervised learning to confirm feature importance and prioritize new candidates.

Abstract

Superconductors with high critical temperatures that emerges beyond the phonon-mediated regime are usually considered unconventional in nature, yet unlike conventional superconductors, no broadly applicable predictive theory currently guides their discovery. Here, we use interpretable machine learning to uncover a common materials-space signature of high-$T_{\mathrm{c}}$ unconventional superconductors and develop a data-driven strategy for materials discovery. We construct a unified feature representation for each material by integrating compositional statistics, structural information, and latent representations from trained property-prediction models, followed by structure-aware filtering of an experimentally established superconducting dataset. Without using transition-temperature information, unsupervised analysis shows that cuprate and iron-based superconductors occupy a common region of materials space, characterized primarily by large electronegativity deviation and intermediate mean valence-electron number. A supervised $T_{\text{c}}$ model independently identifies the same descriptors as dominant features, providing complementary evidence for their relevance. Using this empirical materials-space prior together with the $T_{\text{c}}$ model, we prioritize candidate materials, recover recently discovered nickelate superconductors, and identify chemically distinct candidates for future investigation.

Summary. This review synthesizes decades of research on superconductivity and magnetism in $ ext{Bi-Ni}$ compounds and bilayers. It contrasts findings suggesting conventional $s$-wave superconductivity with strong evidence for unconventional, time-reversal symmetry breaking states. The work highlights the critical role of dimensionality and interfacial effects in modifying these fundamental electronic properties.

Why it may be interesting. The interplay between superconductivity and magnetism, especially when mediated by strong spin-orbit coupling and dimensionality effects, is a core topic in strongly correlated electron physics, relevant to understanding exotic pairing symmetries.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the complex interplay between unconventional superconductivity and magnetism in the Bi-Ni system, specifically addressing the nature of magnetic anomalies in $ ext{NiBi}_3$ and the superconducting state in $ ext{Bi/Ni}$ bilayers.

Main result. The research presents conflicting evidence: some measurements suggest intrinsic time-reversal symmetry breaking and triplet pairing in the bilayer, while other studies point towards conventional $s$-wave superconductivity in the bulk $ ext{NiBi}_3$, highlighting a major unresolved conflict in the field.

Method. The study synthesizes results from multiple techniques, including magnetotransport measurements (MR, anomalous Hall effect), magnetometry ($ ext{MOKE}$, $\mu ext{SR}$), and spectroscopic probes (Andreev spectroscopy).

Model / system. The focus is on two physical systems: the $ ext{NiBi}_3$ intermetallic compound (bulk and nanostructured) and the $ ext{Bi/Ni}$ heterostructure bilayer. The physics involves strong spin-orbit coupling ($ ext{SOC}$) and dimensionality effects.

Key observables. Superconducting critical temperature ($T_c$), magnetic hysteresis loops, nonreciprocal transport signals, and the deviation of the upper critical field ($ ext{H}_{c2}$) from the Pauli limit.

Important parameters / regimes. Low temperatures ($\sim 4 ext{ K}$), high magnetic fields ($ ext{up to } 60 ext{ T}$), and the ratio of in-plane vs. out-of-plane magnetic field responses.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis often compares experimental results against theoretical models, such as BCS theory for superconductivity and various limits ($ ext{B}{ ext{P}}$, $ ext{B}^*$) for magnetic field effects.}

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the orthorhombic structure of $ ext{NiBi}_3$, comparing magnetic measurements (ESR, moment vs. field) in bulk versus nanostructured samples, and showing the superconducting response to magnetic fields in both $ ext{NiBi}_3$ and $ ext{Bi/Ni}$ bilayers.

Paper structure. The review systematically examines the electronic properties of the two $ ext{Bi-Ni}$ systems, detailing experimental findings on magnetism (bulk vs. confined) and superconductivity (conventional vs. unconventional/topological), culminating in a discussion of open questions.

Abstract

Unconventional superconductivity with ingredients of magnetism such as time reversal breaking, triplet pairing and proximity effects has been a long time pursued topic in physics and material science as a new source of innovative phenomena and effects to drive further scientific advances and technologies. The Bi-Ni system has emerged as a convenient candidate for the investigation of such phenomenology. Strictly speaking, the term Bi-Ni system refers to two different schemes, first to the NiBi$_{3}$ intermetallic compound, a 4 K superconductor in which a complex magnetism seems to govern electrical properties in the normal phase, but without implication in the superconducting properties. The second Bi-Ni system is not quite a material but a heterostructure formed by a bilayer of Bi and Ni. Here the superconducting phase has to steal the spotlight by showing strong evidence of time-reversal breaking and triplet pairing featuring a complex unconventional state. The ease of preparing the samples, compared to other candidates for such unconventionality, usually Uranium-based compounds, has accelerated the research in this system. Here, we went through a detailed review of the main results about the electronic properties of these two systems as well as a discussion of the main open questions to be solved in order to reach the complete understanding in this topic.

Supermoiré Reconstruction and Topological Mosaics in Twisted Trilayer WSe$_2$ and MoTe$_2$

Authors: Hai Meng, Yang Xu, Fengcheng Wu
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.30813v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks

Summary. This work investigates twisted trilayer TMDs, revealing that the moiré superlattice reconstructs into domains with distinct topological properties due to broken symmetry. By calculating band structures and Chern numbers, the authors demonstrate that these systems form a 'topological mosaic,' offering a platform to study correlated and topological physics in engineered quantum materials.

Why it may be interesting. The concept of topological mosaics arising from structural reconstruction in moiré heterostructures is highly relevant to engineering topological electronic states, which is a key area in correlated electron physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates the lattice relaxation and electronic band structure of twisted trilayer Transition Metal Dichalcogenides (TMDs) to understand the formation and topological properties of supermoiré lattices.

Main result. The supermoiré structure forms a mosaic of topologically inequivalent domains, and the topmost valence bands exhibit domain-dependent valley Chern numbers, which is distinct from simpler bilayer systems.

Method. The research employs a combination of machine-learning force fields for structural relaxation and the Truncated Atomic Plane Wave (TAPW) method for calculating electronic band structures and topological invariants.

Model / system. The physical system is helical and alternating twisted trilayer WSe2 and MoTe2. The moiré superlattice is formed by the interference of two adjacent bilayer moiré lattices, leading to distinct domain types.

Key observables. Domain-dependent valley Chern numbers ($C_K, C_{K'}$), lattice relaxation patterns, and the energy difference ($\Delta E$) between different domain types.

Important parameters / regimes. Twist angles ($ heta_{12}, heta_{23}$), which define the helical or alternating configuration of the layers.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on MLFFs trained on a large dataset, and for the alternating case, small lattice constant adjustments were sometimes necessary to achieve a commensurate structure.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the spatial patterns of relaxed TMDs for both helical and alternating twists, showing domain types. Band structure plots display the moiré Brillouin zone and the energy dispersion of the topmost valence bands, highlighting non-zero Chern numbers in specific domains.

Paper structure. The paper proceeds by first analyzing the structural relaxation of the supermoiré lattice, identifying domain types based on symmetry breaking. Subsequently, it calculates the electronic band structure and topological invariants (Chern numbers) for both helical and alternating configurations, comparing the results across different TMD materials.

Abstract

We investigate lattice relaxation and band structures of helical and alternating twisted trilayer WSe$_2$ and MoTe$_2$ using machine-learning force fields and large-scale ab initio calculations. Interference between the two bilayer moiré lattices generates a supermoiré lattice that, upon relaxation, reconstructs into a few dominant domain types with locally commensurate bilayer moiré lattices. Because the systems lack $C_{2z}$ symmetry, domains otherwise related by this symmetry become energetically and topologically distinct, unlike in twisted trilayer graphene. Band structure calculations show that the topmost valence bands originate from the $K$ ($K'$) valleys and carry domain-dependent valley Chern numbers. The resulting supermoiré lattice hosts a mosaic of topologically inequivalent domains, offering a platform for exploring correlated and topological physics.
