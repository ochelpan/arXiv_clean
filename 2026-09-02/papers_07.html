Ancilla-mediated fixed-point quantum search using Grover iterations

Authors: Yash Prabhat, Snigdha Thakur, Ankur Raina
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29839v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: quantum measurements 1/5

Summary. This work presents an ancilla-mediated fixed-point quantum search algorithm designed to overcome the instability of standard Grover iterations when the number of solutions is unknown. By mapping the solution amplitude to an ancilla qubit using real-plane reflections, the method achieves high, guaranteed success probabilities and maintains a favorable query complexity of $O(\sqrt{N/M})$. This provides a robust alternative to standard amplitude amplification for quantum search tasks.

Why it may be interesting. The robust, deterministic nature of the fixed-point mechanism, which avoids phase gates and maintains a geometric structure, offers a powerful algorithmic tool for quantum computation that is less sensitive to parameter estimation errors.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper addresses the 'soufflé problem' in Grover's search algorithm, where success probability degrades due to the need for precise iteration counts, especially when the number of solutions ($M$) is unknown.

Main result. They introduce an ancilla-mediated fixed-point quantum search that achieves robust convergence with a guaranteed success probability of at least 92.6% and a query complexity of $O(\pi \sqrt{N/M})$.

Method. The method utilizes a dedicated ancilla qubit to map the solution amplitude, employing Grover's real-plane reflections to construct a fixed-point operator $\hat{F}( heta)$ that deterministically guides the search.

Model / system. The model is the quantum search algorithm applied to unstructured datasets of size $N$ with $M$ solutions. The search operates within a four-dimensional invariant subspace $V$ spanned by states involving the ancilla qubit.

Key observables. Success Probability ($\ge 92.6\%$), Query Complexity ($O(\pi \sqrt{N/M})$ worst-case, $O(\sqrt{N/M})$ expected).

Important parameters / regimes. Dataset size $N$, number of solutions $M$, and the angle $ heta$ defining the initial state.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes the state remains confined to the four-dimensional invariant subspace $V$.

Figures summary. Figures compare the probability of convergence and the required number of oracle calls for the fixed-point algorithm versus standard Grover search as a function of $M$ for large $N$.

Paper structure. The paper develops the fixed-point operator $\hat{F}( heta)$ using real-plane reflections, analyzes its action on the invariant subspace $V$, and derives the convergence probability and query complexity bounds by comparing it to standard amplitude amplification.

Abstract

Grover's quantum search algorithm provides a fundamental quadratic speedup for unstructured datasets, reducing query complexity from $\mathcal{O}(N)$ to $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{N})$. However, the algorithm's reliance on precise iteration counts leads to the ``soufflé problem,'' where over-rotation results in a sharp decline in success probability. This limitation is particularly restrictive when the number of solution states, $M$, is unknown. In this work, we present an ancilla-mediated fixed-point quantum search algorithm that achieves robust convergence by mapping the solution amplitude to a dedicated ancilla qubit. Unlike existing phase-matching fixed-point methods, our approach utilizes Grover's real-plane reflections, thereby maintaining the intuitive geometric architecture of the original algorithm. We demonstrate that this method achieves a success probability of at least $92.6\%$ with a query complexity of approximately $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{N/M})$, effectively bridging the gap between standard amplitude amplification and robust fixed-point convergence.

Branching stochastic mechanics. I. Clustering and connected correlations within a branching-process representation of the Schrödinger equation

Authors: Eric Dumonteil, Benoît Bischoff, Alain Letourneau, Loïc Thulliez, Corentin Doutre
Type: theory · Category: statistical mechanics · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29807v1

Summary. This paper develops Branching Stochastic Mechanics (BSM) to analyze the quantum state via a branching-process representation of the Schrödinger equation. It shows that by analyzing connected correlations beyond the mean density, one can mathematically demonstrate the coexistence of an extended background density and a finite correlation range. This suggests a mechanism for particle-like organization within quantum systems.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a powerful statistical framework (BSM) to analyze quantum correlations beyond mean-field approximations, directly addressing how local structure (finite correlation range) can emerge from global, extended quantum states.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates whether finite-range correlations can be hidden within the statistics of an extended quantum state whose low-order moments are fixed by the mean.

Main result. The analysis suggests that an extended Born density and a finite correlation range can coexist within a single stochastic kernel, implying particle-like organization.

Method. The Schrödinger equation is reformulated using the Schrödinger-Nagasawa transform and promoted to a Branching Stochastic Mechanics (BSM) framework, which retains higher statistical orders (genealogy) beyond mean dynamics.

Model / system. The model is quantum mechanics governed by the Schrödinger equation. The analysis uses the SN transform to define forward and backward diffusion fields, which are then treated as branching superprocesses.

Key observables. Born Density ($ ho$), Connected Kernel ($C_{FB}$), BSM Paired Density ($ ho_{BSM}$), and the correlation screening length ($\xi_{FB}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Critical dimension $d_c=2$ in free space; the effective relative diffusivity ($D_{eff}$) and relaxation rate ($\mu_{FB}$).

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis assumes that the stochastic lift (BSM) captures the necessary higher statistical structure, and that the connected moments accurately describe the inherited dependence.

Figures summary. Not specified (Notes describe mathematical structures and diagnostics rather than figure content).

Paper structure. The paper first establishes the equivalence between the SN transform and Schrödinger dynamics. It then develops the BSM framework, analyzing the connected sector's clustering properties. Finally, it derives the pair equation, showing how the diagonal matching condition and the finite screening length characterize the coexistence of extended density and finite correlations.

Abstract

Can finite-range correlations hide in the statistics of an extended quantum state? The Schrödinger-Nagasawa transform represents the wave function by positive forward and backward diffusion fields whose product is the Born density. We promote them to branching superprocesses, \(Φ_F\) and \(Φ_B\): diffusion samples stochastic paths, whereas Bohm/Fisher-controlled branching generates genealogies of alternative continuations. With rates evaluated on the prescribed Born density, their means reproduce Schrödinger dynamics exactly. The connected sector exhibits supercritical, critical, and subcritical clustering in confinement and has critical dimension \(d_c=2\) in free space. Stationary eigenmodes remain extended; subcritical clusters acquire the reduced de~Broglie scale. We then let the branching rate respond to the fluctuating product \(Φ_FΦ_B\). In the reciprocal basis, the fields equal a smooth reference \(R\) plus centered fluctuations \(ψ_F,ψ_B\), defining the signed kernel \(C_{\rm FB}(x,y)=\mathbb E_ω[ψ_F(x)ψ_B(y)]\). On the anticorrelated branch, \(ρ_{\rm BSM}(x)=-C_{\rm FB}(x,x)\) is the positive paired density. Stationary recovery requires its diagonal to match the Born profile, while off-diagonal decay defines the correlation range. The pair equation splits into collective and relative sectors: spectral cancellation selects the Born collective mode, while suppression of the leading density fluctuation selects the anticorrelated source channel. With relative diffusivity \(D_{\rm eff}\) and positive relaxation rate \(μ_{\rm FB}\), correlations have screening length \(ξ_{\rm FB}=\sqrt{D_{\rm eff}/μ_{\rm FB}}\). Thus an extended Born density and a finite correlation range can coexist in one stochastic kernel, suggesting particle-like organization.

Summary. By separating charge fluctuations, the authors achieve consistent calculations for aqueous and molten salt systems, revealing ion-specific thermodynamic effects and structural reorganization mechanisms.

Main problem. The primary challenge addressed is the accurate computation of chemical potentials in complex, charged multi-component mixtures, which is crucial for understanding electrolyte thermodynamics but difficult using standard atomistic simulation techniques.

Key observables. Chemical potentials ($\mu_\alpha$), partial static structure factors ($S_{\alphaeta}(k)$), and the charge-density structure factor ($S_{ZZ}(k)$).

Important parameters / regimes. Small wavevector limit ($k o 0$), ionic strength, and the specific composition of the salt mixture.

Assumptions / limitations. The theory assumes that the thermodynamically relevant neutral composition fluctuations can be separated from the forbidden macroscopic charge fluctuations, and that the structure factor matrix has a finite limit at zero wavevector.

Abstract

The chemical potentials of charged multi-component mixtures are central to electrolyte thermo- dynamics, but remain difficult to compute from atomistic simulations. The S0 method enables computing chemical potentials of mixtures from equilibrium molecular dynamics simulations. Here we extend the S0 method to charged mixtures by combining the composition-space framework developed in Part I: Neutral Multi-component Mixtures with a Coulombic treatment of the small- wavenumber limits of static structure factors. This approach separates thermodynamically relevant neutral composition fluctuations from forbidden macroscopic charge fluctuations, and further ac- counts for charge-neutrality constraints. We use the method to compute the chemical potentials of multiple-halide aqueous salt solutions, elucidating the ion-specific thermodynamic effects. We also calculate the mixing free energies of molten salt mixtures, demonstrating the importance of correctly describing long-wavelength electrostatic correlations.

Convex conservation of von Neumann entropy implies unitarity or antiunitarity

Authors: Nicolas G. Underwood, Jonte R. Hance

Summary. This theoretical paper establishes that for finite quantum systems, any transformation that preserves the convex structure of density matrices and conserves von Neumann entropy must be either unitary or antiunitary. It proposes that entropy conservation can serve as a fundamental physical principle, potentially replacing the traditional requirement of reversibility (unitarity) in quantum mechanics.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a fundamental information-theoretic constraint on quantum dynamics, suggesting that entropy conservation might be a more general physical principle than strict unitarity for describing time evolution.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates whether the conservation of von Neumann entropy can replace the requirement of invertibility in establishing that quantum transformations must be unitary or antiunitary.

Main result. The authors prove that any convex transformation on the space of density matrices that conserves von Neumann entropy must be either unitary or antiunitary for finite Hilbert spaces.

Method. The proof involves analyzing the constraints imposed by entropy conservation on the transformation's action on pure and mixed states, using spectral properties and linear algebra.

Model / system. The system is described by quantum states represented by density matrices $\rho$ on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space $H$. The analysis concerns general transformations $A: D(H) \to D(H)$.

Key observables. Von Neumann Entropy $S[\rho] = -\text{tr}(\rho \log \rho)$, and the density matrix $\rho$ itself.

Important parameters / regimes. The dimension $N$ of the finite Hilbert space.

Assumptions / limitations. The transformation $A$ is assumed to be convex-structure preserving and conserve von Neumann entropy. The theorem is proven only for finite Hilbert spaces.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the function $-p \log p$ for entropy calculation, while Figure 2 shows the entropy function $S$ as a function of eigenvalues, highlighting its symmetry and extrema.

Paper structure. The paper builds from Kadison's theorem, proposing entropy conservation as a replacement for invertibility. It analyzes the transformation's action on basis elements (propositions 0-7), showing that entropy conservation forces the map to be unitary or antiunitary.

Abstract

On the space of density matrices $D(H)$, Kadison's theorem establishes that all invertible convex transformations $K:D(H)\to D(H)$ are unitarity or antiunitary. For a finite Hilbert space, we propose a similar theorem which replaces the invertibility requirement with that of entropy conservation. Thus, we argue, the supposition of reversibility in quantum mechanics may be replaced with a principle of entropy conservation.

Doping-driven evolution of pairing symmetry in pressurized La$_3$Ni$_2$O$_7$

Authors: Hai-Yang Zhang, Yu-Jie Bai, Fan-Jie Kong
Type: theory · Category: strongly correlated electrons · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29091v1
Analysis basis: full PDF text, analyzed in chunks Topic relevance: Keldysh / 2PI / non-Gaussian methods 1/5

Summary. This theoretical study maps the doping-driven evolution of superconductivity in pressurized La3Ni2O7 using a bilayer two-orbital model. It finds that while hole doping induces a symmetry change to $d_{xy}$-wave, electron doping maintains $s_{\pm}$-wave pairing. Crucially, the research concludes that the $\gamma$ Fermi pocket is not essential for superconductivity, suggesting robust pairing driven by $\alpha-eta$ nesting.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides a detailed theoretical mechanism linking Fermi surface topology (pocket expansion/contraction) and magnetic fluctuations (odd/even modes) to the specific symmetry of the superconducting pairing, which is a core topic in strongly correlated electron physics.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The study investigates how the superconducting pairing symmetry evolves as a function of doping in the pressurized bilayer nickelate superconductor, La3Ni2O7.

Main result. Hole doping drives a transition from $s_{\pm}$-wave to $d_{xy}$-wave pairing, while electron doping maintains $s_{\pm}$-wave pairing, suggesting the $\gamma$ pocket is not essential for superconductivity.

Method. The pairing interaction is calculated using the Random-Phase Approximation (RPA) applied to a minimal bilayer-two-orbital Hamiltonian, utilizing spin and charge susceptibilities.

Model / system. The system is pressurized La3Ni2O7, modeled by a minimal bilayer-two-orbital Hamiltonian including hopping integrals and onsite interactions. The analysis tracks the superconducting pairing symmetry across different doping levels (hole vs. electron doping).

Key observables. Superconducting pairing symmetry ($s_{\pm}$-wave, $d_{xy}$-wave), gap function sign reversal, and the doping dependence of pairing interactions ($\Gamma_{\alphaeta}, \Gamma_{eta\gamma}, \Gamma_{\gamma\gamma}$).

Important parameters / regimes. Doping level (hole vs. electron), magnetic excitation modes (odd vs. even), and the presence/absence of the $\gamma$ Fermi pocket.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the Random-Phase Approximation (RPA) applied to the minimal bilayer-two-orbital model.

Figures summary. Figures illustrate the leading pairing states at different doping levels, showing the $ heta$-dependence of pairing interactions and the resulting gap distributions for both hole- and electron-doped regimes.

Paper structure. The paper systematically analyzes the pairing interaction using RPA, first establishing the $s_{\pm}$-wave state in the undoped limit via magnetic odd modes. It then examines the doping dependence, showing the $s_{\pm} o d_{xy}$ transition upon hole doping, and the persistence of $s_{\pm}$ pairing in the electron-doped regime, arguing against the necessity of the $\gamma$ pocket.

Abstract

We investigate the superconducting pairing symmetry and its doping evolution in pressurized La$_3$Ni$_2$O$_7$. For the undoped compound, the most favorable pairing state is found to be $s_{\pm}$-wave, characterized by sign reversal of the gap functions between the Fermi pockets. A detailed analysis of the pairing interactions reveals that this unconventional state originates from repulsive interactions mediated by the magnetic odd modes of the bilayer nickelate. Upon hole doping, the $γ$ Fermi pocket expands, which amplifies the intrapocket repulsions on this pocket. These repulsions, driven by the magnetic even modes, gradually dominates the pairing interactions and ultimately drive a transition in pairing symmetry from $s_{\pm}$-wave to $d_{xy}$-wave in the heavily hole-doped regime. In stark contrast, the $s_{\pm}$-wave pairing persists under electron doping, even deep into the heavily electron-doped regime where a Lifshitz transition occurs. This finding suggests that the $γ$ Fermi pocket is not essential for the emergence of superconductivity in bilayer nickelates. In fact, the $s_{\pm}$-wave pairing becomes more robust in the absence of the $γ$ pocket, as spin fluctuations are strongly enhanced by the favorable nesting between the $α$ and $β$ pockets --- a condition guaranteed by Luttinger's theorem and the Fermi surface topology. We believe that exploring the doping evolution of superconducting pairing will open a new realm for testing the pairing mechanism in pressurized La$_3$Ni$_2$O$_7$.

Frame phase retrievability and state distinguishability of quantum channels

Summary. This survey characterizes phase retrievability for quantum channels, determining when the output measurement can uniquely distinguish all pure input states. It establishes that this property is equivalent to the channel being injective on pure states, developing advanced mathematical criteria using frame theory and channel decomposition to quantify this information preservation.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides rigorous mathematical tools to quantify how much information a quantum process (channel) loses, which is fundamental for understanding quantum error correction, quantum communication capacity, and the limits of quantum state estimation.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper investigates the conditions under which a quantum channel can retain enough information to uniquely identify pure input quantum states, a concept termed 'phase retrievability'.

Main result. A quantum channel is phase retrievable if and only if it is injective when restricted to the set of pure states. The authors develop quantitative criteria, such as the index $\eta_2(\Phi; M)$, to characterize this property.

Method. The analysis combines concepts from frame theory (phase retrieval from intensities) with quantum information theory, utilizing the adjoint channel ($\Phi^*$) and structural criteria involving Choi ranks and relative spectra.

Model / system. The study focuses on general quantum channels $\Phi: B(HA) o B(HB)$ acting on systems described by density matrices or unit vectors in Hilbert spaces. Specific examples include depolarizing or amplitude damping channels.

Key observables. Phase retrievability index ($ ext{pr}(\Phi)$), the measure $\eta_2(\Phi; M)$, and the injectivity of the channel on pure states.

Important parameters / regimes. The dimension of the input/output spaces, the structure of the Kraus operators $\{A_i\}$, and the number of uses ($q$) for twirling channels.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis often distinguishes between 'injective identification' (phase retrievability) and stronger concepts like full tomography or perfect one-shot discrimination.

Figures summary. Not specified in the notes, but the structure implies theorems and criteria are presented, likely involving diagrams or mathematical derivations related to channel decomposition and frame properties.

Paper structure. The paper progresses from general phase retrieval concepts for vectors to the application of these ideas to quantum channels. It develops necessary and sufficient conditions using various mathematical tools (Choi rank, spectra) and concludes by applying these findings to structured channels like twirling channels.

Abstract

This survey introduces the role of frame phase retrieval in pure-state identification and information preservation by quantum channels. For unit vectors, the lift $x\mapsto xx^*$ identifies vectors that differ only by a global phase with the same rank-one quantum state, converts frame intensities into linear functionals of the lifted state, and makes the adjoint pullback of output observables an operator-valued measurement on the input system. From this viewpoint, a channel is phase retrievable exactly when it is injective on pure states. We develop this correspondence through Choi-rank-two linear combinations of Kraus operators, higher-rank relative spectra, structural obstructions, and constructions with prescribed Choi rank. We distinguish injective identification from full tomography, perfect one-shot discrimination, zero-error classical communication, and exact quantum correction. Twirling channels then provide a structured setting in which commutants, irreducible dimensions, multiplicities, orbit-frame orthogonality, coding indices, and phase-retrievable subspaces can be read from group representations.

Authors: Biman Bagchi

Summary. This theoretical work uses density-functional theory and bifurcation analysis to investigate the transition from liquid states to ordered or disordered packed states in hard-particle systems. It establishes that a specific condition—the vanishing of the grand-potential curvature at a finite wavevector—marks a thermodynamic marginality. This marginality successfully predicts known terminal packing limits across different dimensions, raising fundamental questions about the relationship between liquid correlations and jamming.

Why it may be interesting. The rigorous mathematical framework used to analyze the transition from liquid to solid/jammed states, particularly the identification of a 'thermodynamic marginality' distinct from simple phase boundaries, provides powerful tools applicable to understanding complex many-body systems far from equilibrium.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper seeks to reconcile the theoretical description of liquid freezing (crystallization) with the empirical observation of terminal disordered packing (like random close packing) in various dimensions.

Main result. The analysis shows that the vanishing of the grand-potential curvature at a finite wavevector marks a thermodynamic marginality, which yields exact terminal packing in 1D and values near RCP in 2D and 3D.

Method. The study employs density-functional theory (DFT) and analyzes the nonlinear Ramakrishnan--Yussouff bifurcation framework to distinguish between crystalline folding, thermodynamic coexistence, and terminal marginality.

Model / system. The system under study consists of hard spheres, hard rods, and hard disks in $d=1, 2, 3$ dimensions, analyzed using mean-field descriptions of liquid structure.

Key observables. Packing fractions ($\eta$), structure factor $S(k)$, grand-potential curvature ($K_{RY}(G)$), and the coupling constant $\lambda_G$.

Important parameters / regimes. Dimensionality ($d=1, 2, 3$), the terminal packing condition ($\lambda_T^G = 1$), and the crystalline close packing fraction.

Assumptions / limitations. The analysis relies on the DFT framework and the nonlinear diffusion theory of freezing, distinguishing its findings from mechanical jamming or simple phase transitions.

Figures summary. The notes do not provide a summary of figures, but the analysis compares results across different dimensions and structural inputs.

Paper structure. The paper progresses by first establishing the DFT framework for freezing, then applying bifurcation analysis to distinguish different transition types, and finally comparing the resulting marginality condition across 1D, 2D, and 3D systems to identify a universal feature.

Abstract

A density-functional theory developed for equilibrium freezing also produces a high-density endpoint close to terminal packing. We revisit the nonlinear Ramakrishnan--Yussouff bifurcation framework and distinguish the first crystalline fold, thermodynamic coexistence, and terminal marginality. At the terminal point, the grand-potential curvature vanishes along the principal finite-wavevector density mode, exactly when the nonlinear self-consistency equation loses stiffness. The construction gives exact close packing for one-dimensional hard rods, a terminal hard-disk packing fraction near 0.8407, and a hard-sphere value near 0.629, close to random close packing. These results identify a finite-wavevector thermodynamic marginality of a metastable liquid continuation, rather than equilibrium freezing or mechanical jamming, and expose a persistent puzzle: why should liquid-state correlations encode the approach to terminal disordered packing across different physical dimensions and systems?

Marginal spectral distributions on regular bipartite unitary orbits

Authors: Lin Zhang
Type: theory · Category: quantum information and computing · PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.30370v1

Summary. This theoretical paper calculates the probability distribution of local marginal states ($ ho_A, ho_B$) given a fixed global spectrum $ ho_{AB}$ by averaging over random unitaries. It employs advanced mathematical machinery, notably the Harish-Chandra-Itzykson-Zuber integral, to derive explicit, piecewise polynomial formulas for these joint densities. The results provide deep insights into the geometric constraints (compatibility regions) governing local observables in quantum systems.

Why it may be interesting. This work provides rigorous mathematical tools (HCIZ integrals, Duistermaat-Heckman theory) to calculate entanglement-related quantities (marginal state distributions) for quantum systems, which is crucial for understanding quantum correlations.

Detailed structure

Main problem. The paper studies the probability distributions induced on the spectra of the two marginal states ($ ho_A, ho_B$) when the global bipartite density matrix ($ ho_{AB}$) has a fixed spectrum and its eigenbasis is randomized by the Haar measure.

Main result. Explicit formulas, including alternating-spline and truncated-power forms, are derived for the joint density of the marginal Bloch radii in the two-qubit case, whose support is constrained by the Bravyi-Klyachko compatibility region.

Method. The analysis relies on calculating the joint characteristic function using Harish-Chandra-Itzykson-Zuber (HCIZ) integrals, connecting the problem to Duistermaat-Heckman measures and piecewise polynomial structures.

Model / system. The system is a bipartite quantum state $ ho_{AB}$ with subsystems $A$ (dimension $m$) and $B$ (dimension $n$). The analysis is performed by fixing the global spectrum $\lambda$ and averaging over random unitaries.

Key observables. Joint density of the two marginal Bloch radii ($p_\lambda(a, b)$), and the joint density of the largest and smallest eigenvalues of one marginal (in the qubit-qutrit case).

Important parameters / regimes. The fixed global spectrum $\lambda = (\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_N)$ and the subsystem dimensions $m$ and $n$.

Assumptions / limitations. The global spectrum is assumed to be regular (non-degenerate), with degenerate cases handled via confluent limits of the HCIZ integral.

Figures summary. Figure 1 illustrates the support of the joint density $G(x, y)$, and Figure 2 shows the explicit two-qubit Bloch-radius density $p_\lambda(a, b)$ for a specific spectrum $\lambda$.

Paper structure. The paper develops the general framework using HCIZ integrals, derives specific formulas for the two-qubit case (Bloch radii), and extends the methodology to higher dimensions (qubit-qutrit) using spline and power formulas.

Abstract

Fix the spectrum of a bipartite density matrix and randomize its eigenbasis according to Haar measure. We study the probability distributions induced on the spectra of the two marginal states. For arbitrary subsystem dimensions $m$ and $n$, the joint characteristic function of the reduced density matrices is expressed as a Harish-Chandra-Itzykson-Zuber integral whose external eigenvalues are the pairwise sums $x_i + y_j$. Repeated external eigenvalues are handled by confluent determinant limits. In the two-qubit case, we derive an explicit alternating-spline formula for the joint density of the two marginal Bloch radii. Its support is the Bravyi-Klyachko compatibility region. We also obtain a compact truncated-power formula for the Bloch-radius density of either individual qubit marginal. In the qubit-qutrit case, we derive a truncated-power formula for the qubit Bloch-radius density and a bivariate spline formula for the joint density of the largest and smallest eigenvalues of the qutrit marginal. The latter two variables determine the full qutrit spectrum because the trace is fixed. The derivations combine confluent HCIZ integrals, distributional Fourier inversion, orbital measures, and the SU(2) and SU(3) derivative principles. The resulting densities are piece-wise polynomial on chambers determined by subset sums of the fixed global eigenvalues, in agreement with the Duistermaat-Heckman description of projected coadjoint-orbit measures.

PauLie: Fast Classification of Pauli Dynamical Lie Algebras

Summary. This paper presents PauLie, a novel, efficient framework for classifying the isomorphism type of Dynamical Lie Algebras generated by Pauli strings. By utilizing graph-theoretic reduction on the anticommutation graph, it bypasses the exponential complexity of traditional Lie closure methods. This tool is vital for analyzing quantum control, understanding system symmetries, and diagnosing issues like barren plateaus in quantum algorithms.

Why it may be interesting. It provides a powerful, polynomial-time tool to analyze the underlying algebraic structure of quantum dynamics generated by Pauli operators, which is critical for understanding the limitations and symmetries of variational quantum algorithms.

Main problem. The primary challenge is efficiently determining the isomorphism type of the Dynamical Lie Algebra (DLA) generated by arbitrary Pauli strings, as explicit computation scales exponentially with the number of qubits.

Main result. The authors introduce PauLie, an open-source framework that classifies the DLA isomorphism type in polynomial time, $O(n|G| \max(n, |G|))$.

Method. The method uses a graph-theoretic reduction algorithm based on the anticommutation relations of the generators, systematically applying graph contractions to map the structure to a canonical graph (Type A or B).

Model / system. The system involves quantum evolution governed by Hamiltonians that are sums of Pauli strings. The DLA dictates the system's controllability and expressibility.

Key observables. The canonical graph structure (Type A, B1, B2, B3) serves as the classification invariant, which determines the DLA's algebraic isomorphism type.

Important parameters / regimes. The complexity scales polynomially with the number of qubits ($n$) and the number of generators ($|G|$).

Abstract

The dynamical Lie algebra (DLA) governs the controllability, expressibility, and simulation complexity of a quantum system. Explicitly computing it has been a major computational bottleneck: brute-force Lie closure scales exponentially in the number of qubits $n$. Many applications, however, consult only the isomorphism type of the DLA. We introduce PauLie, an open-source framework that decides this isomorphism type for DLAs generated by arbitrary Pauli strings, building on the anticommutation-graph reduction of Aguilar et al. PauLie runs in $O(n|\mathcal{G}|\max(n,|\mathcal{G}|))$ time, where $|\mathcal{G}|$ is the number of generators, turning DLA classification into a routine preprocessing step. We demonstrate its use as a structural oracle for routing Lie-algebraic simulation and Cartan decomposition, diagnosing barren plateaus in variational quantum algorithms, and engineering universal Pauli string generator sets with optimal generation rate.

Abstract

Generative models have become central across science and industry, from image and text synthesis to the design of molecules and materials. Quantum generative models are considered one of the most promising applications for quantum computers, since a quantum circuit naturally produces samples from the distribution it encodes, and for suitable circuits that distribution is believed to be hard for any classical computer to reproduce. A leading strategy trains these models on a classical computer and reserves the quantum device for generating samples at deployment. This is possible when the training loss can be evaluated on a classical computer. A prime example is the maximum mean discrepancy (MMD$^2$), a moment-matching loss that compares the model and the data through their Pauli-$Z$ correlations. Research so far has asked whether such models can be trained and whether their sampling is hard; whether minimizing such an objective yields a model that generalizes, rather than one that merely reproduces the training statistics, remains poorly understood. We benchmark a broad set of quantum and classical generative models by direct sampling and show that models trained with a moment-matching loss generally show worse generalization than the likelihood-trained models. We show this on two application-inspired datasets: first a cardinality-constrained dataset at up to $30$ qubits and second a dataset of genomic single-nucleotide variants, whose valid set is the observed data. These results indicate that a converged moment-matching loss is not a reliable measure of generalization, and that train-classical, deploy-quantum workflows will need approaches that target generalization directly, leaving open whether better training objectives suffice or whether the model architectures themselves must change.

Abstract

Since the seminal work of Selinger and Valiron, the standard design for quantum lambda calculi has kept the quantum state outside the program: terms manipulate pointers to an external register. This is largely due to the difficulty of eliminating tensor products. For example, the calculus $λ_ρ^\circ$ embeds density matrices directly within terms, where terms carry the entire computation state, a feature particularly appealing for program verification. However, lacking a tensor elimination construct, it can neither access the individual qubits of a compound state nor discard them. Borgna showed that this inability to discard qubits makes the calculus strictly less expressive than the quantum lambda calculus of Selinger and Valiron. In this paper we show that tensor elimination is possible in this setting. The key observation is that the Pauli decomposition, combined with the spectral decomposition of the Pauli matrices, allows any $n$-qubit density matrix to be expressed as a real linear combination of tensor products of single-qubit density matrices. Exploiting this fact, we extend $λ_ρ^\circ$ with a construct $\mathrm{let}\ x^{\otimes n} = ρ \mathrm{in}\ t$, which binds each $x_i$ to a single-qubit density matrix arising from the decomposition of $ρ$. We equip the extended calculus with a rewrite system, a type system, and a denotational semantics, and prove Subject Reduction, Progress, Strong Normalisation, Soundness, and Adequacy. The new construct also recovers the missing ability to discard qubits, thereby restoring expressiveness. Moreover, we show that this is achieved in a physically principled way: a variable unused in $t$ is interpreted exactly as being partial-traced out, as dictated by the no-deleting theorem. We illustrate the resulting compositionality through quantum teleportation and the three-qubit bit-flip code.
